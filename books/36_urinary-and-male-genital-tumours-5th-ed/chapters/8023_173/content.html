8.0.2.3 Adenocarcinoma of the collecting ducts and rete testis

Definition

Adenocarcinoma of the collecting ducts and rete testis is a malignant gland-forming tumour originating in the rete epithelium.

ICD-O coding

8140/3 Adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C84 & XH74S1 Malignant neoplasms of other specified male genital organs & Adenocarcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumour is centred in the rete testis.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a mass and/or pain. The tumour can be associated with hydrocoele {17853037}. Local spread and metastasis to para-aortic and iliac lymph nodes are common.

Epidemiology

The tumour is exceedingly rare. It occurs in a wide age range, although most reported cases are in adults.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are centred in the testicular hilum and have solid and cystic cut surfaces {30676333}. They are typically large, with reported diameters of 20–90 mm {32619438}; extension to spermatic cord and overlying skin with satellite nodules is common.

Histopathology

Histologically, the tumours show intrarete and invasive growth, with a range of patterns such as papillary, solid, glandular, cribriform, and glomeruloid {30676333}. Tumour cells are cuboidal to columnar with nuclear pseudostratification and noticeable cytological atypia. The cytoplasm varies from eosinophilic to clear. Desmoplasia and necrosis are common. Evidence of a transition from normal to neoplastic rete epithelium is a useful diagnostic clue if present; even so, large masses can obliterate the normal anatomy and this transition is not documentable. Immunohistochemistry is positive for CK7, AE1/AE3, EMA, vimentin, PAX8, and BerEP4; there are no specific lineage-associated (rete epithelium) markers {30676333}. The differential diagnosis includes mesothelioma, ovarian-type tumours, metastatic adenocarcinoma, epididymal carcinoma, and malignant Sertoli cell tumour. Supportive immunohistochemistry ruling out other lineage differentiation tumours such as metastatic adenocarcinoma, germ cell tumours, Sertoli cell tumour, or mesothelioma can help in the accurate diagnosis of rete testis carcinoma {30676333}.

Cytology

No published reports

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour centred in the testicular hilum; exclusion of other testicular or paratesticular tumours; exclusion of metastases.

Desirable: evidence of transition from normal to neoplastic rete epithelium.

Staging

There is no American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for these tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours have a poor prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate of approximately 15% {30676333; 3287961; 24409217}. Tumour diameter > 50 mm at presentation is a poor prognostic factor {7571213}.
