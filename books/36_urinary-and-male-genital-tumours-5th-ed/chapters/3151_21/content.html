3.1.5.1 Collecting duct carcinoma

Definition

Collecting duct carcinoma (CDC) is a high-grade, predominantly tubular, renal medulla–based adenocarcinoma presumed to arise from the renal collecting ducts of Bellini.

ICD-O coding

8319/3 Collecting duct carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 & XH4SQ4 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis & Collecting duct carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: Bellini duct carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CDC arises centrally and is usually based in the medulla of either kidney.

Clinical features

Two thirds of patients present with haematuria, back pain, fatigue, or weight loss. Approximately 40% present with metastasis, including to the lungs, liver, bone, adrenal glands, and brain {19836761; 23434943; 16753362; 22895263}.

Epidemiology

CDC is rare, accounting for < 1% of renal malignancies. It occurs across a wide patient age range (13–87 years), with the median patient age in published studies ranging from 43 to 63 years. The M:F ratio is 2:1 {19836761; 23434943; 16753362}. The true incidence is difficult to ascertain, given that it overlaps clinicopathologically with fumarate hydratase–deficient renal cell carcinoma (RCC), papillary RCC, mucinous tubular spindle cell carcinoma with high-grade transformation, and high-grade unclassified RCC.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Available cytogenetic data and recent molecular data show complex chromosomal aberrations, especially copy-number losses or loss of heterozygosity involving multiple chromosomes such as 1p, 6, 8, 9, 14, and 22 {24167600; 27144525}. These reported chromosomal alterations appear to differ from those of other established RCC types and upper tract urothelial carcinoma. Recurrent molecular alterations include frequent CDKN2A loss {27144525}, as well as NF2 and SETD2 mutations {26149668; 27713405; 27484008}.

Macroscopic appearance

CDCs are typically grey-white and firm, with haemorrhage and necrosis. The tumours have an irregular, poorly defined contour or are multinodular, extending into the cortex and renal sinus. Satellite nodules are common. The range of reported tumour sizes is broad, with a mean of 70 mm {22895263; 29309300}.

Histopathology

CDC typically shows tubular and tubulopapillary architectural patterns with irregular, infiltrating glands and associated desmoplastic stroma. Solid nested or sheet-like, trabecular, cord-like individual cells, as well as microcystic patterns, may also be present; a subset show an infiltrative nodular papillary component. The tumour edge is poorly defined, with tumour cells infiltrating between tubules and glomeruli of the adjacent renal parenchyma, which may show dysplasia or atypia within uninvolved tubules. There is often a prominent inflammatory reaction including a neutrophil infiltrate within and around the tumour. Lymphovascular invasion and regional lymph node involvement are common at nephrectomy.

The cells exhibit high-grade nuclear features, usually with eosinophilic cytoplasm, but they can be clear or amphophilic. Cells lining tubules are often cuboidal, although columnar and hobnail cells are common. Nuclei are large, vesicular, and highly pleomorphic, with prominent nucleoli. Mitoses are numerous and often abnormal. Apoptotic cells and necrosis are common. Sarcomatoid differentiation and rhabdoid cells are not uncommon. Intracellular or intraluminal mucin may be present {22895263}.

The presence of any component of the tumour showing the classic appearance of clear cell RCC, papillary RCC, or mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma supports a diagnosis of the corresponding entity with high-grade features or transformation. Metastasis of other types of carcinoma to the kidney should be excluded {33664427}.

Immunohistochemistry

The diagnosis of CDC requires careful exclusion of urothelial carcinoma and metastasis, particularly when limited tissue is available. CDCs are typically positive for high-molecular-weight cytokeratins (34βE12), low-molecular-weight cytokeratins (e.g. CK19, CK7), and PAX8, and they have retained expression of SMARCB1 (also known as INI1 or BAF47) and fumarate hydratase. A panel of immunostains such as PAX8, 34βE12, p63, GATA3, SMARCB1, OCT3/4, and fumarate hydratase is useful for the diagnosis, with careful sampling of the tumour and the adjacent renal pelvis and exclusion of metastasis {25025368; 29309300}. In one large multi-institutional cohort, 25% of cases diagnosed previously as CDC were reclassified as fumarate hydratase–deficient RCC, using immunostaining for fumarate hydratase and 2-succinocysteine {29309300}; a similar rate of CDC reclassification was found in recent sequencing studies demonstrating fumarate hydratase and SMARCB1 mutations {26149668}. Other entities that should be considered in the differential diagnosis include metastatic carcinoma (most commonly lung) {25406592; 26053895}, urothelial carcinoma (including BK polyomavirus–associated upper tract urothelial carcinoma showing prominent glandular differentiation) {29765141; 32047230}, and mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma with high-grade transformation {32879414}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Specific, defining molecular alterations for CDC remain elusive. CDC diagnosis is supported molecularly by the retained expression of SMARCB1, fumarate hydratase, and SDHB, or an absence of pathogenic alterations in the genes encoding these proteins, as well as an absence of cytogenetic or chromosomal copy-number alterations characteristic for papillary RCC or mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma {33664427}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: RCC with medullary involvement, predominantly tubular morphology, desmoplastic stromal reaction, high-grade cytology, and infiltrative growth pattern.

Desirable: exclusion of other types of RCC, urothelial carcinoma with divergent differentiation, and metastasis from other organs.

Staging

Staging follows the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

CDCs are highly aggressive tumours, and the majority are associated with a poor outcome. Approximately two thirds of patients die within 2 years of diagnosis {19836761}. Patients show very limited responses to immunotherapy and chemotherapy {17437788}.
