8.0.1.4 Mucinous cystadenoma

Definition

Mucinous cystadenoma is a benign cystic tumour lined by a non-stratified columnar epithelium with intracellular mucin.

ICD-O coding

8470/0 Mucinous cystadenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F34 & XH6H73 Benign neoplasm of male genital organs & Mucinous cystadenoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Testis, paratestis, and spermatic cord

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a painless enlargement of the testis. Occasionally, pain or heaviness is reported. Tumours can also be an incidental finding {12445137; 15737039; 26600891}.

Epidemiology

Mucinous cystadenoma is extremely rare in the testis and paratestis. The few reported cases have occurred in patients over a wide age range (11–78 years) {12960806; 23044077}.

Etiology

The histogenesis is debated; proposed hypotheses include an origin from a Müllerian remnant or Müllerian metaplasia of the mesothelium.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The reported cases are unilateral cystic masses, usually unilocular or occasionally multilocular, containing mucoid or watery fluid {23044077; 15201756}. The tumours are well demarcated and have a fibrotic wall. Reported tumour sizes range from 10 to 90 mm {12960806; 12445137}.

Histopathology

The cystic cavities are lined by a single layer of columnar epithelium that resembles intestinal, gastric, or cervical mucosa, without the cellular atypia and architectural complexity seen in their ovarian counterparts {28189139; 12960806}. Scattered goblet cells are often present in the intestinal epithelium {28189139}. No stromal invasion or necrosis is seen. Mucin extravasation and a mixed inflammatory infiltrate of lymphocytes, plasma cells, and histiocytes can be identified {28189139}.

The few reported cases with immunohistochemistry analysis showed CK20 and CDX2 positivity in the columnar cells, with variable expression of CK7. Scattered chromogranin-positive neuroendocrine cells can be identified within the epithelium. The goblet cells are MUC2-positive {15737039; 28189139}.

Primary mucinous cystadenoma needs to be distinguished from mucinous differentiation in a testicular germ cell tumour. Teratomatous elements should be absent. Postpubertal teratomas typically show gain of chromosome 12p. Therefore, an absence of 12p chromosomal gain on molecular testing and a lack of background germ cell neoplasia in situ can be used to exclude mucinous differentiation in postpubertal teratomas {27796764; 23598964}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cystic tumour lined by a mucinous columnar epithelium with no atypia or architectural complexity; no invasive or intratubular germ cell neoplasia identified in the background testis; extensive sampling is required.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The few reported cases showed benign behaviour {28189139}.
