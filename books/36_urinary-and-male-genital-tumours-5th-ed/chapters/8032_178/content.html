8.0.3.2 Well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour

Definition

Well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour (WDPMT) is a neoplasm arising from the tunica vaginalis, exhibiting a papillary architecture in which the papillae are lined by a single layer of bland cuboidal cells without invasion.

ICD-O coding

9052/0 Well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour

ICD-11 coding

2E8Y & XH67N8 Benign neoplasm of mesothelial tissue, other specified organs & Well-differentiated papillary mesothelioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: well-differentiated papillary mesothelioma; benign papillary mesothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

WDPMT arises from the tunica vaginalis, which is the double layer of mesothelium that lines the testis (the outer surface of the tunica albuginea or epiorchium, and the inner layer of the scrotum or periorchium).

Clinical features

Most cases are associated with a hydrocoele; tumours rarely occur as a scrotal mass {29787369}.

Epidemiology

WDPMT of the testicular adnexa is a very rare tumour, less frequent than its counterpart in the pleura and in the peritoneum. The patient age range is 18–70 years. Some cases have been related to asbestos exposure {11688466; 27067374}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Because of its rarity, the pathogenesis of WDPMT has not yet been established.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours arise within the hydrocoele sac as solitary papillary nodular lesions with a bulging cut surface. Multifocality is rarely reported. They range from 3 to 30 mm in size {20526287; 29787369}.

Histopathology

WDPMTs display papillary structures with a stromal formation, lined by a single row of cuboidal cells that have bland nuclear features and exceptional to absent mitosis {20526287}. Nuclear pseudoinclusions are usually present. Absent or minimal stromal invasion is the key to diagnosis {24618613}. Other, more complex, patterns have been described, but whether they can be characterized as WDPMT is debatable {20526287; 21867523}. There is a consensus that WDPMT is a benign lesion and should be exclusively composed of papillary structures lined by bland mesothelial cells {21867523; 29787369}. Tumours with a complex architecture and increased mitotic activity would be better categorized as mesothelioma of uncertain malignant potential {20526287}. WDPMT is positive for mesothelial markers including calretinin, WT1, HBME1, and D2-40. Pancytokeratin, AE1/AE3, CK7, and CAM5.2 are uniformly positive. BerEP4 shows focal (< 20%) expression and MOC31 shows variable expression. B72.3, CEA, and CD15 are negative {29787369; 21867523; 16390245}. Unlike mesothelioma, WDPMT retains BAP1 expression, and there is no homozygous deletion of CDKN2A {30171198; 29763720}. Differential diagnoses include all mesothelial proliferations mimicking WDPMT (reactive mesothelial hyperplasia, epithelioid mesothelioma) and ovarian-type serous tumours of the testis.

Cytology

The diagnosis cannot be established from cytology samples.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular alterations that are observed in mesothelioma are not observed in WDPMT: the tumour is characterized by an absence of homozygous deletion of CDKN2A (P16), and BAP1 is retained. There is also an absence of BRAF p.V600E mutations {29763720}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: solitary papillary neoplasms with bland cytomorphological features; positive mesothelial markers; retained BAP1 expression; absence of homozygous deletion of CDKN2A (P16).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Because of the limited numbers of cases and the lack of long-term follow-up in many cases, there is a hesitancy to categorize these tumours as benign despite them all showing indolent behaviour to date. Local excision is curative. Orchiectomy is required for larger tumours. Close follow-up is recommended {27067374}.
