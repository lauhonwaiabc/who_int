3.1.4.3 Other oncocytic tumours of the kidney

Definition

Other oncocytic tumours of the kidney are a heterogeneous group of oncocytic tumours not classifiable as oncocytoma, chromophobe renal cell carcinoma (ChRCC), or other tumour types with eosinophilic features.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F98 Neoplasms of unknown behaviour of urinary organs

Related terminology

Acceptable: oncocytic tumour NOS.

Subtype(s)

Because this is a heterogeneous tumour group, it is likely that new subtypes of renal neoplasia will emerge from this group.

Localization

The tumours develop in the renal cortex. They may be solitary, multifocal, or bilateral.

Clinical features

Most of these tumours are low-grade and often discovered incidentally on imaging.

Epidemiology

There are limited data, but these tumours may be overrepresented in younger patients or in a hereditary setting.

Etiology

Most of these tumours are sporadic but some are associated with tuberous sclerosis or Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome.

Pathogenesis

Limited data are currently available.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours are typically small and well circumscribed, with a grey, tan to brown, solid cut surface.

Histopathology

This is a heterogeneous group of renal tumours with eosinophilic/oncocytic cytoplasm that show overlapping or intermediate (borderline) features between oncocytoma and ChRCC and elude definitive classification. Importantly, these tumours are typically indolent, so it is important to distinguish such low-grade tumours from the high-grade unclassified RCCs that typically behave aggressively {28833389}. In the setting of Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome, tumours with such intermediate features (hybrid oncocytic tumours) also exist and they are typically multifocal and bilateral. Microscopically, these Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome–related tumours demonstrate intermediate features between oncocytoma and ChRCC, and they are also characterized by clusters and individual cells with clear cytoplasm, often exhibiting a checkerboard mosaic pattern.

When considering solitary tumours with intermediate (borderline) features that could not be further subtyped, it is important to use strict diagnostic criteria to rule out oncocytoma and ChRCC, as well as to exclude other well-defined mimics (for example, epithelioid angiomyolipoma, succinate dehydrogenase–deficient RCC, fumarate hydratase–deficient RCC, oncocytoma-like epithelioid angiomyolipoma, MiT family gene–rearranged RCC, and eosinophilic solid and cystic RCC) {28898443; 28833389; 33664427}. The remaining eosinophilic/oncocytic tumours with borderline features may be designated “oncocytic renal neoplasms of low malignant potential NOS”, a category that should be restricted to solitary, sporadic tumours with intermediate features {33664427}. Because this is a heterogeneous group and it does not represent a specific entity, it should be considered primarily a clinical management tumour category, rather than a specific diagnosis or entity.

Recent evidence also indicates that within this broader category there are two emerging entities: eosinophilic vacuolated tumour (EVT) {30232607; 30303819; 32091432; 31002177; 31936678; 33526874} and low-grade oncocytic tumour (LOT) {30895640; 32091432; 31936678; 33526874; 32918598; 32812119; 32299640}, both of which can be distinguished by their clinical, morphological, immunohistochemical, and molecular features, summarized in #24565Table 2.06 . EVT is characterized by a solid growth of eosinophilic cells with cytoplasmic vacuolation, prominent nucleoli, and only very focal (if any) CK7 expression, whereas LOT is a neoplasm with bland low-grade nuclei, diffuse strong CK7 labelling, and negative KIT (CD117) labelling. Further work is needed to fully characterize these entities.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

This is a heterogeneous category with limited data currently available. Some emerging subtypes are associated with mutations in the mTOR pathway genes (TSC1, TSC2, MTOR) {32091432} (e.g. in EVT and LOT) or in FLCN (e.g. in hybrid oncocytic tumours in Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: eosinophilic/oncocytic tumours not fulfilling all criteria of oncocytoma or ChRCC.

Desirable: detection of mutations in the mTOR pathway.

Staging

These tumours are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Limited data indicate that adverse behaviour, including recurrence or progression, is exceptional in the broader category of oncocytic renal neoplasms of low malignant potential {30232607; 30303819; 30895640; 32091432; 33526874}.
