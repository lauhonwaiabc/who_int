11.0.1.3 Plasmacytoma

Definition

Solitary plasmacytoma is a localized plasma cell neoplasm.

ICD-O coding

9734/3 Plasmacytoma, extramedullary

ICD-11 coding

2A83.2 & XH4BL1 Solitary plasmacytoma & Plasmacytoma, NOS

2A83.2 & XH0N40 Solitary plasmacytoma & Plasmacytoma, extramedullary

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Solitary plasmacytoma of bone; extramedullary plasmacytoma

Localization

Extramedullary plasmacytoma may rarely involve the genitourinary tract including the testis {16304406; 31240468; 32514930; 9158685}, epididymis {9158685}, bladder {29390408; 20923037}, kidney {28178171; 10357398; 20814135}, and ureter {20173384}. Genitourinary plasmacytoma can occur in isolation (solitary) as the first presentation of plasma cell myeloma (PCM), in patients with known PCM {9158685; 16178900; 20679613; 15814337; 20173384; 29390408}, or as a relapse of treated PCM {31240468; 32514930; 12353308; 29214871}.

Clinical features

Patients with genitourinary plasmacytoma may present with a mass lesion {9158685; 16178900; 20679613; 31012139} or haematuria {15814337; 20173384}, or they may present without symptoms {29390408}. Rare allograft recipients have developed plasmacytoma-like posttransplant lymphoproliferative disorders involving the kidney or urinary bladder {24034051; 15814337}.

Epidemiology

Plasmacytoma accounts for 2–5% of all plasma cell neoplasms {6668499; 12057071; 19016727}. Solitary plasmacytoma of bone is more common than extramedullary plasmacytoma {10580871}. The median patient age at diagnosis is 62 years, and the M:F ratio is 2.64:1 {19016727}. Approximately 0.9% of all plasmacytomas are not associated with PCM {28841208}, and 3.7% of all isolated extramedullary plasmacytomas arise in the urinary tract or male genital tract {10357398}. Testicular plasmacytoma is more common than other genitourinary plasmacytomas; however, it is usually associated with PCM {12353308; 9158685}, occasionally associated with other extramedullary plasmacytomas {9158685; 18946760; 29214871}, and rarely solitary {10357398; 29214871}.

Etiology

The etiology is largely unknown. Plasmacytoma is rarely related to immunosuppression {24034051; 15814337}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Testicular plasmacytomas are hard and well demarcated from the normal parenchyma {9158685}.

Histopathology

Biopsy shows sheets of mature or atypical plasma cells {9158685; 16178900; 20679613}. Neoplastic cells typically express CD38, CD138, IRF4 (MUM1), and monotypic kappa or lambda light chain, with a low proliferation index. Some cases express CD56, cyclin D1, KIT (CD117), and CD19, with variable expression of CD45 and CD20 {29667156; 28824230; 29338789}. The differential diagnosis for genitourinary tract plasmacytoma is broad (see #21089Table 11.08) {19297008; 19377442; 19961257; 23958546}.

Cytology

FNA at extramedullary sites usually yields cellular smears composed of numerous plasma cells with binucleated and multinucleated forms {19416310}. Flow cytometry on an aspirate from an extramedullary plasmacytoma may aid in the confirmation of the diagnosis {30788667}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There is a clonal IGH gene rearrangement in solitary plasmacytoma; other data are limited for genitourinary plasmacytoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mass lesion composed of clonal plasma cells; absence of clonal B cells; exclusion of multiple myeloma and MALT lymphoma.

Staging

There is no staging system for plasmacytoma; however, patients should be evaluated for PCM with complete blood count, serum creatinine and calcium, serum and urine protein electrophoresis, serum free light chains, radiological evaluation {25439696; 29338789}, and bone marrow aspiration/biopsy with flow cytometry and/or immunohistochemistry.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for isolated genitourinary plasmacytoma appears to be favourable {20814135; 29214871}. Patients with PCM have a poor prognosis {9158685; 12353308}. Radiation therapy is the standard treatment for solitary plasmacytoma of bone and extramedullary plasmacytoma {29338789; 12057071; 19016727; 10580871; 29667156}. Genitourinary plasmacytomas are sometimes treated with resection in addition to other therapies {9158685; 29214871}.
