3.1.6.2 Acquired cystic disease-associated renal cell carcinoma

Definition

Acquired cystic disease–associated renal cell carcinoma (ACD-RCC) is a malignant epithelial neoplasm derived from renal tubules that develops in patients with acquired cystic disease.

ICD-O coding

8316/3 Acquired cystic disease–associated renal cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 & XH0RU3 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis & Acquired cystic disease–associated renal cell carcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is no predilection for laterality. Tumours are often multiple and bilateral {16434887; 29851703}.

Clinical features

This tumour occurs exclusively in patients with acquired cystic disease, usually after long-term dialysis. The vast majority of tumours occur in men {30187581; 32271189; 32604168}. Most patients are asymptomatic, with the tumours found at an early stage incidentally by imaging {21726517}.

Epidemiology

The risk of renal tumour development in patients with acquired cystic disease is > 100 times as high as that in the general population {12028461}.

Etiology

Both haemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis, particularly long-term dialysis, are strongly associated with acquired cystic disease and subsequent ACD-RCC {16434887; 30187581; 20864552}.

Pathogenesis

Causes of ACD-RCC development are possibly multifactorial, such as oxalate accumulation, uraemia, and oxidative stress {17451187; 15767796; 20459539}. Cysts lined by eosinophilic vacuolated single or multilayered epithelium have been proposed as possible precursor lesions {24775605; 30001236}.

Macroscopic appearance

ACD-RCC is identified in acquired cysts, either as an intracystic mass or not in association with the cysts. The tumours have a brown to yellow-tan cut surface, sometimes with haemorrhage or necrosis {16434887; 30066367}.

Histopathology

Architectural variability is common, including tubular, papillary, tubulopapillary, microcystic, and solid patterns. The cells have abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and large nuclei with conspicuous nucleoli. Most characteristically, the tumour cells have intracytoplasmic or intercytoplasmic lumina or holes, imparting a cribriform (sieve-like) appearance. Intratumoural oxalate crystal deposition is common {16434887; 29851703; 32271189; 32604168}. Metastases often show a prominent cystic growth pattern {29851703; 32271189}. Immunohistochemically, PAX8, CD10, RCCm, and AMACR are positive. Most ACD-RCCs are negative for GATA3, CK7, and KIT {29851703; 32271189}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential genetic alterations have not been identified, although recurrent KMT2C or TSC2 gene mutations in ACD-RCC have been reported {32604168}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sieve-like, papillary, and variable architecture in the setting of acquired cystic disease.

Desirable: intratumoural oxalate.

Staging

The staging follows the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) criteria for clear cell renal cell carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Most ACD-RCCs usually follow an indolent clinical course, although aggressive behaviour is documented, including recurrence or distant metastasis to multiple sites {16434887; 29851703; 32271189}. Rhabdoid and sarcomatoid features have been reported in aggressive cases of ACD-RCC {16434887; 29851703}.
