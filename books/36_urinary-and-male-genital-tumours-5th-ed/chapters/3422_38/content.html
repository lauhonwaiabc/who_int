3.4.2.2 Ossifying renal tumour of infancy

Definition

Ossifying renal tumour of infancy (ORTI) is an intracalyceal tumour composed of a combination of osteoid, osteoblastic, and spindle cells.

ICD-O coding

8967/0 Ossifying renal tumour of infancy

ICD-11 coding

2F35 & XH3SR2 Benign neoplasm of urinary organs & Ossifying renal tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Renal pelvis

Clinical features

ORTI has no specific clinical presentation, but most patients present with gross haematuria and a calcified mass protruding into the renal pelvis on imaging {8597860; 24407230}. No association with any other renal tumour type or syndrome has been published.

Epidemiology

These tumours are extremely rare, with only about 25 cases published in the literature. There is a clear male predominance (M:F ratio: 7:1), and patients are aged between 6 days and 2.5 years {27633911; 30425880}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are well circumscribed and measure 10–60 mm in diameter. Almost all tumours show signs of calcification on imaging studies, and this is reflected in the gross appearance.

Histopathology

ORTIs are composed of two cell types: osteoblast-like cells that are located between and around areas of osteoid, and a spindle cell (blastema-like) component, mostly at the periphery of the tumour {8597860}. Osteoblast-like cells and spindle cells are both bland, without features of malignancy. Immunohistochemistry shows that the osteoblast-like cells are almost always positive for EMA and vimentin {27633911}. SATB2 and WT1 have also been reported to be positive {27633911; 29187024}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Five cases of ORTI with clonal trisomy 4 have now been reported (in two different papers), so clonal trisomy 4 seems a relevant and recurrent molecular abnormality {22976287; 27633911}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the typical histological combination of osteoid, osteoblastic cells, and spindle cells.

Staging

All tumours are limited to the kidney and regarded as benign lesions. No particular staging protocol applies.

Prognosis and prediction

This is a benign tumour for which conservative surgical management is sufficient. Outcome has been uniformly favourable {8597860; 9589480}.
