11.0.2.1 Juvenile xanthogranuloma

Definition

Juvenile xanthogranuloma (JXG) is a proliferation of non–Langerhans cell histiocytes with a favourable outcome except in uncommon cases with systemic dissemination.

ICD-O coding

9749/1 Juvenile xanthogranuloma

ICD-11 coding

2B31.0 Juvenile xanthogranuloma

Related terminology

Not recommended: xanthogranuloma; solitary xanthogranuloma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Solitary JXG in the genitourinary tract is extremely rare, with only a few cases reported in the testis {19968486; 24582118}, one in association with cutaneous involvement {3923211}. Renal JXG is found in the context of disseminated disease {19515049}. Rarely, the penis is the primary site {32338090}.

Clinical features

Testicular JXG mostly arises as a mass in infants aged 2.5–13 months. It may be associated with pain. One patient presented with obstructive uropathy due to ureteric compression by retroperitoneal JXG in an undescended testis {19968486}. Disseminated JXG shows multivisceral involvement {19515049; 16462582}. A single case in the penile shaft, in a young adult, has been described recently {32338090}.

Epidemiology

No distinct epidemiological findings for testicular cases are known, owing to their rarity. At other sites, JXG has been associated with neurofibromatosis type 1 {15078345}, leukaemia {20848640}, Langerhans cell histiocytosis {30430550}, and Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome {23152840}. Occurrence in monozygotic twins suggests a genetic predisposition {21091658}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There are no molecular studies on JXG of the genitourinary tract. Somatic mutations have been reported in cutaneous JXG, including PIK3CD mutations in 3 cases {26637772}, as well as BRAF mutations in CNS lesions {31685033} and after Langerhans cell histiocytosis {30430550}, suggesting a pathogenetic role of MAPK/ERK pathway activation. JXG family neoplasms may have ERK pathway mutations {26966089}.

Macroscopic appearance

JXGs appear as pale-yellow to tan-white masses with occasional fibrous bands {19968486}. In the penis they may be solid or cystic and may grossly resemble an epidermal inclusion cyst {32338090}.

Histopathology

JXG is an unencapsulated, well-demarcated, histiocytic proliferation intermixed with variable numbers of lymphocytes, eosinophils, plasma cells, neutrophils, and mast cells. The lesion involves the testicular interstitium, with partial effacement of seminiferous tubules, and it may extend to the epididymis {19968486}. The features vary in relation to the temporal evolution. Early lesions show a predominance of small oval to spindle-shaped histiocytes with eosinophilic cytoplasm and a minor degree of lipidization. Large xanthomatous histiocytes and multinucleated Touton giant cells with foamy cytoplasm and a ring-like arrangement of nuclei are more frequent in older JXGs {12717244; 24582118}. Fibrosis may be present {12717244; 24582118}.

JXGs are positive for CD68, CD163, factor XIIIa, CD4, and fascin, and they are negative for CD1a, CD207 (langerin), and S100. JXG with predominant spindle cells and rare Touton giant cells can mimic a sex cord stromal tumour {24582118}. Appropriate immunohistochemical markers can help in differentiating these entities; CD4, CD163, and factor XIIIa are positive in JXG, whereas inhibin, SF1, and calretinin are positive in sex cord stromal tumour {26220162}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an interstitial histiocytic lesion with variably prominent mixed inflammatory infiltrate; positivity for CD4, CD68, and CD163; negativity for CD1a and CD207 (langerin).

Desirable: presence of Touton giant cells.

Staging

There is no staging system for JXG.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for solitary testicular JXG is excellent. Disseminated JXG has an overall mortality rate of 5–10% {12717244; 26637774}. BRAF p.V600E–mutated JXGs have been demonstrated to have a more aggressive course in a primary CNS location, but similar studies have not been done in genitourinary system JXGs {31685033}.
