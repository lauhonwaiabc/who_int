3.1.2.3 Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma

Definition

Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma is a renal epithelial neoplasm characterized by tubules merging with bland spindle cells and myxoid stroma.

ICD-O coding

8480/3 Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 & XH5EQ2 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis & Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: low-grade collecting duct carcinoma; low-grade tubular-mucinous renal neoplasm.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma generally occurs in the renal cortex, but it may involve or arise within the renal medulla.

Clinical features

Most patients are asymptomatic with an incidental renal mass {11381369; 25395040; 31151928}.

Epidemiology

Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma accounts for < 1% of all renal neoplasms. There is a female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:3–4) and a broad age range (13–84 years; median age in the sixth decade of life) {14660100; 25395040; 31151928}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There are recurrent copy-number alterations and multiple chromosomal losses frequently involving chromosomes 1, 4, 6, 8, 9, 13, 14, 15, and 22 {12429795; 27604489; 29462091; 26009022; 28656700; 32879414}. Biallelic dysregulation of the Hippo pathway tumour suppressor genes is common {27604489}. CDKN2A or CDKN2B deletion and additional complex genomic abnormalities may be present in high-grade tumours {32879414}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are mostly well-circumscribed masses with a solid, grey-white, tan, or yellow cut surface.

Histopathology

Tumours classically consist of tightly packed, elongated, and anastomosing tubules lined by low-grade cuboidal cells merging with bland spindle cells in a myxoid stroma containing basophilic extracellular mucin. Various ratios of the elements result in spindle cell–dominant, epithelial-dominant, or mucin-poor morphology {17122511}. Tubules with tufting or small papillae and foci of foamy macrophages may occur, which can lead to morphological overlap with papillary renal cell carcinoma. Cells are low-grade with rare mitoses, and they may show clear cytoplasm or oncocytic changes. Immunohistochemically, tumours express PAX8, CK7, AMACR, and (rarely) CD10 {16330937; 17240302}.

Tumours with high-grade transformation have high-grade nuclei, necrosis, increased mitoses, vascular invasion, and solid or infiltrative growth {18830937; 28656700; 32879414}. An expansile, high-grade spindle cell element is defined as sarcomatoid transformation {18941398}. Areas with classic histology may be present.

Cytology

FNA is typically cellular with aggregates of low-grade, uniform, oval to spindle cells intermixed with abundant metachromatic myxoid matrix {17703454; 19626627}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There is multiple chromosomal loss by copy-number analysis, and VSTM2A RNA expression by in situ hybridization is an emerging diagnostic marker {30285995; 33239504}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: anastomosing tubules lined by low-grade cuboidal cells merging with bland spindle cells in a stroma with basophilic extracellular mucin.

Desirable: chromosomal loss by copy-number analyses in challenging cases or small biopsies.

Staging

Staging follows the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours with high-grade transformation are often associated with distant metastasis and death. Rare cases with classic morphology can also develop metastases {21194728; 21684183; 25395040; 31151928; 32675442}.
