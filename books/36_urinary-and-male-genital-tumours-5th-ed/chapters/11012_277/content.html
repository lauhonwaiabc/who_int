11.0.1.2 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a neoplasm of medium or large B-lymphoid cells whose nuclei are the same size as, or larger than, those of normal macrophages, or more than twice the size of those of normal lymphocytes, with a diffuse growth pattern.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

DLBCL may occur at any location in the genitourinary system; the kidney is the most common site {19377442; 29187485}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms are location-dependent and include haematuria, urinary obstruction, dysuria, back pain, abdominal pain, and scrotal swelling {19377442; 25268190}. Some patients are asymptomatic.

Epidemiology

DLBCL is the most common lymphoma of the genitourinary system, accounting for < 5% of extranodal lymphomas and < 1% of all genitourinary tumours {29187485}. High-grade primary bladder lymphomas (most of which are DLBCL) represent roughly 20% of the primary lymphomas of the urinary bladder, which themselves account for just 0.2% of all extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphomas {25837971}. There is a slight male predominance in the occurrence of primary genitourinary lymphomas {19377442; 29187485}.

Etiology

The etiology is mostly unknown {29984868; 18318740; 27447126; 28381751}.

Pathogenesis

DLBCL is usually a de novo malignancy. Less often, it arises as a transformation from an indolent B-cell lymphoma. Primary genetic abnormalities that drive the malignant clone include rearrangements in BCL2 (most frequently), BCL6, and MYC. The precise subclassification of DLBCL based on genomics is an ongoing effort. Immune escape is a major pathway in the pathogenesis of primary testicular DLBCL, with tumours showing genomic alterations in CIITA, B2M, and HLA loci (all relevant to antigen presentation). They also show structural rearrangements of CD274 (PDL1) and PDCD1LG2 (PDL2) {10676951; 29289361; 29641966; 29713087; 28985567; 32367674}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are single or multiple masses that are pink, pale, or tan in colour and firm, solid, fleshy, or lobulated, with a bulging cut surface. They displace adjacent structures, and focal necrosis can be present {19377442; 21970494; 24441246}.

Histopathology

DLBCL is a heterogeneous group of aggressive lymphomas with variability in cytomorphological features, tumour microenvironment, immunophenotype, and genetics {26980727}. Typically, there are sheets of large nucleolated lymphoid cells admixed with variable numbers of small reactive lymphocytes, histiocytes, and other inflammatory cells. The lymphoma cells can have features of centroblasts or immunoblasts, or they can be anaplastic with varying degrees of pleomorphism. Ki-67 expression is usually > 80% {18556402; 22642745}. For differential diagnosis, see #20862Table 11.05 ( p. XXX); for immunohistochemistry, see #20865Table 11.06 .

Cytology

In urine, lymphoma cells are large, with an increased N:C ratio in a necrotic/apoptotic background. They are round to oval and have centrally located nuclei with prominent nucleoli {15349991}. Urine flow cytometry may be used as an ancillary technique for diagnosis {11908726}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular pathology is not essential for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: appropriate morphology; expression of CD20 and/or other B-cell antigens.

Desirable: absence of CD3, cyclin D1, and TdT expression; CD10, BCL2, BCL6, MYC, and Ki-67 for subtyping and prognosticating, according to #20865Table 11.06 .

Staging

The Lugano system is used to stage lymphomas and has been adopted by the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

See #20867Table 11.07 {14504078; 26373676; 32054654; 29954940; 22851565}.
