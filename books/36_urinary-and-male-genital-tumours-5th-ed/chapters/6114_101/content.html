6.1.1.4 Prostatic ductal adenocarcinoma

Definition

Ductal adenocarcinomas are composed of papillary structures and/or complex and cribriform glands lined by tall columnar pseudostratified cells.

ICD-O coding

8500/3 Ductal adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C82.0 & XH7KH3 Adenocarcinoma of prostate & Infiltrating duct carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: ductal carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours arise most commonly in the peripheral zone but also in the transition zone, especially around the prostatic urethra {23443941} (including the anterior prostate) {31711982}.

Clinical features

Patients with ductal adenocarcinomas of the prostate may present in an identical manner to patients with acinar prostatic adenocarcinoma. However, the periurethral tumours may project into the urethra, leading to symptoms and signs of bladder outflow obstruction and gross haematuria {24187500}. Serum PSA levels are highly variable and may be lower than in acinar adenocarcinoma. When the tumours manifest as metastases, the diagnosis may be challenging because they mimic other malignancies. Ductal adenocarcinoma shows a predilection for unusual visceral metastases, including spread to the penis and testis {25025445; 12173328; 11939729}. Tumours are often diagnosed identically to acinar adenocarcinoma, on needle biopsy after raised PSA or abnormal MRI {10584700}; however, if they are periurethral, they may be diagnosed after transurethral resection.

Epidemiology

In the majority of cases, this subtype is mixed with acinar adenocarcinoma. However, its distinctive clinical behaviour and metastatic pattern have led it to be considered more than a subtype of acinar adenocarcinoma. Although it is present in 2.6% of all prostatic adenocarcinomas {23443941}, its pure form accounts for only 0.2–0.4% {4091189; 2416422}.

Etiology

See the Etiology subsection of section Prostatic acinar adenocarcinoma .

Pathogenesis

Because it was originally thought to be derived from Müllerian duct remnants {5116310}, ductal adenocarcinoma of the prostate was considered to be entirely separate from acinar adenocarcinoma. However, since the recognition of the similar immunohistochemical {26778368} and molecular {28457729} profiles of prostatic ductal adenocarcinoma and acinar adenocarcinoma, as well as frequent mixed disease, ductal adenocarcinoma is now thought to derive from prostatic glandular cells. It is often associated with high-grade prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia and is clonally similar to acinar adenocarcinoma {19151660; 29229583}; therefore, it might represent a divergent differentiation from a common precursor lesion {3510419}.

Genomic studies have established that ductal adenocarcinomas are typically clonally related to concurrent foci of acinar adenocarcinoma arising in the same patient, because of shared ERG rearrangements {19151660} and other molecular alterations {29229583}. However, there has been some disagreement about whether the presence or frequency of specific molecular alterations distinguishes ductal adenocarcinoma from high-grade acinar carcinoma. Some studies have reported that ERG fusions are significantly less common in ductal adenocarcinoma than in isolated acinar adenocarcinoma {19151660; 26178158; 31123724}, whereas others have found that they arise at a similar frequency in the two tumour types {19465903; 28699202}. This discordance may be due to some level of interobserver variability in distinguishing ductal and high-grade acinar adenocarcinoma morphologically {24467262}, as well as the frequent admixture of ductal and acinar adenocarcinoma in the same prostate. Compared with primary acinar adenocarcinoma, ductal adenocarcinoma may be relatively enriched for germline or somatic pathogenic alterations in genes regulating DNA repair, including homologous recombination and mismatch repair gene mutations {27756888; 31123724}; these findings are similar to those in metastatic {26000489} and other aggressive histological subtypes of localized prostate cancer {25154392; 31650100}. Finally, mutations in the WNT signalling pathway (such as activating CTNNB1 and inactivating APC mutations) may be more common in ductal adenocarcinoma than in acinar adenocarcinoma, and they are frequently mutually exclusive with alterations activating PI3K signalling (such as PTEN deletions and PIK3CA mutations) {29229583; 28457729; 32758244}.

Macroscopic appearance

The centrally located ductal adenocarcinomas often protrude into the prostatic urethra as exophytic masses arising from ducts at, or near, the verumontanum {4091189; 2416422}. Radical prostatectomy series have found a predominance of peripherally located tumours, and no differences in gross appearance between the typical ductal adenocarcinoma and acinar carcinomas have been reported {2004331; 20004936; 23443941}. Rarely, ductal adenocarcinomas may have a multilocular cystic appearance macroscopically, with the individual locules ranging from 5 to 77 mm in diameter {26900818; 27631516; 28325365}.

Histopathology

Although variations in the morphology of ductal carcinoma are seen, no well-defined subtypes are recognized {20438402}. A variety of architectural appearances may be seen in ductal adenocarcinoma, of which the papillary and large cribriform patterns are the most characteristic. The papillae typically have fibrovascular cores, whereas in the cribriform glands the lumina tend to be slit-like. Combinations of two or more growth patterns are common, and solid foci, comedocarcinoma, and invasive single non-complex glands may also be present in association with a papillary or cribriform component {2004331; 2416422; 23443941}. The epithelium is pseudostratified and composed of tall columnar cells with abundant amphophilic or pale cytoplasm. The nuclei are generally elongated and enlarged, with prominent nucleoli and frequent mitotic figures, although lower-grade examples have been reported {10584700; 24467262}. The stroma is often desmoplastic, in contrast to that seen in typical acinar adenocarcinoma, and an intraductal growth of carcinoma is frequently seen {23443941}. Only a minority of cases are pure, consisting entirely of ductal carcinoma; in most cases there is also a component of acinar adenocarcinoma {2416422; 10584700; 20004936; 23443941}. In radical prostatectomies, the term “ductal adenocarcinoma” is arbitrarily reserved for those cases with > 50% ductal morphology (and the percentage is reported), whereas in biopsies, the term “adenocarcinoma with ductal features” is recommended even when an apparent pure ductal pattern is seen.

Ductal adenocarcinomas are graded using the Gleason system. To take into account the more complex architecture and poorer prognosis of ductal adenocarcinoma than Gleason score 6 acinar adenocarcinoma, the International Society of Urological Pathology (ISUP) 2005 Consensus Conference recommended that all ductal adenocarcinomas should be assigned pattern 4 except for those with comedonecrosis, which are included in pattern 5 {16096414}.

Ductal adenocarcinomas are generally positive for prostatic markers including PSA, PAP, PSMA, NKX3-1, and AMACR {8818689; 25059847}, and there are no immunohistochemical stains that differentiate them from acinar adenocarcinomas. Some ductal carcinomas (23–60%) display patchy CK20 positivity {18620992; 25059847}. The distinction of ductal carcinoma from acinar adenocarcinoma, intraductal carcinoma of the prostate, and high-grade prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia is best made using routine microscopy and basal cell markers. The most diagnostically useful features are the presence of papillae with fibrovascular cores, stratification of nuclei, tall columnar epithelium, and nuclear elongation in typical ductal adenocarcinomas {24467262}. The other differential diagnoses in prostatic specimens include urothelial carcinoma in situ involving ducts and acini, or (in the invasive form) extension from a colorectal, bladder, or urethral primary with enteric morphology.

Ductal features such as papillary structures and tall columnar cell morphology are often preserved in metastatic sites {2845546}. Carcinomas originating in other organs (particularly urothelial carcinoma and adenocarcinomas of the lung, colorectum, and urinary bladder) may be similar in histological appearance to ductal adenocarcinoma but can be distinguished by immunohistochemical panels. Usually positive are PSA, PAP, PSMA, P501S, and NKX3-1 in prostatic ductal carcinoma; GATA3 and p63 in urothelial carcinoma; and thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) in lung adenocarcinoma {18620992; 25059847; 25029122}. CDX2 and villin do not reliably distinguish ductal adenocarcinoma from colorectal and bladder adenocarcinoma {16949907; 25059847}.

Tumour invasion and spread

Ductal adenocarcinoma has a propensity to spread along prostatic ducts and therefore intraductal carcinoma is present in as many as 90% of cases {2845546; 9130990; 23443941}. In radical prostatectomy specimens, ductal adenocarcinomas often are large tumours {31711982} with an increased risk of advanced disease (extraprostatic extension, seminal vesicle invasion) correlating with the proportion of ductal adenocarcinoma component {23443941; 21383610}. Like conventional acinar adenocarcinoma, ductal adenocarcinoma spreads preferentially to regional lymph nodes and to the bones {32453443}. A distinctive feature of ductal adenocarcinoma is its propensity to metastasize to the lungs and liver {32453443} as well as (more rarely) to other sites like the brain, skin, penis, or testis {32453443; 25025445; 23666549; 12173328}. Ductal features, such as tall columnar tumour cell morphology and papillary formations, are often preserved at the site of metastasis {2845546}. As a consequence, penile metastasis of ductal adenocarcinoma can masquerade as urothelial carcinoma owing to its papillary features {25025445}.

Cytology

Due to their frequent central location and exophytic growth pattern, ductal adenocarcinomas may rarely be seen in urine cytology samples, demonstrating sheets or crowded groups of columnar epithelial cells with hyperchromatic, palisading, or pseudostratified nuclei; finely granular chromatin; and inconspicuous nucleoli {23407018; 31043286}. Some gland formation or delicate papillary architecture may be observed.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

See the Diagnostic molecular pathology subsection of section Prostatic acinar adenocarcinoma .

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of glandular structures with papillary and/or complex cribriform morphology lined by tall columnar pseudostratified cells; often (but not always) high-grade nuclear atypia; in radical prostatectomy: > 50% histology (and the percentage reported); in needle biopsy: even if pure, use the terminology “adenocarcinoma of prostate with ductal features”.

Desirable: in urethral location and metastatic sites: immunohistochemical confirmation of prostatic epithelial origin.

Staging

Ductal adenocarcinomas of the prostate are staged as conventional acinar adenocarcinomas according to the 2017 TNM classification [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]].

Prognosis and prediction

Ductal adenocarcinoma is an aggressive subtype of prostate cancer often understaged at the time of biopsy diagnosis. The presence of any proportion of ductal adenocarcinoma in a needle biopsy carries a greater risk of biochemical failure after subsequent radical prostatectomy {10584700} and a greater risk of metastatic disease than high-risk acinar adenocarcinomas treated by radiotherapy {33279304}. Patients with mixed and pure ductal adenocarcinoma have worse metastasis-free survival after radical prostatectomy than patients with acinar prostate cancer matched for grade, stage, and nodal status {33279304}. Ductal adenocarcinomas with the cribriform pattern are more often associated with advanced pathological stage than those without this feature at prostatectomy {30772651}. Compared with high-risk conventional adenocarcinomas, ductal adenocarcinomas are less responsive to androgen-deprivation therapy administered after failed local therapy {33279304}.
