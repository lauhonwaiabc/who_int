3.1.6.3 Eosinophilic solid and cystic renal cell carcinoma

Definition

Eosinophilic solid and cystic renal cell carcinoma (ESC-RCC) is characterized by solid and cystic architecture, eosinophilic cytoplasm, and coarsely granular, basophilic cytoplasmic stippling, as well as frequent focal to diffuse immunoreactivity for CK20. There is usually alteration of TSC genes.

ICD-O coding

8311/3 Eosinophilic solid and cystic renal cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours arise in the kidney cortex.

Clinical features

ESC-RCC commonly occurs asymptomatically and is identified incidentally as a solid and cystic renal mass on imaging {29391189}.

Epidemiology

ESC-RCC was originally described as granular eosinophilic-macrocystic renal cell carcinoma in patients with tuberous sclerosis {25093518; 19403547; 24832166} and is now considered its somatic counterpart. Sporadic cases were subsequently described predominantly in women {26414221; 28786877} but, more recently, cases in children and men have been reported {28898443}. Some cases previously reported as “oncocytoid renal cell carcinoma after neuroblastoma” have also proved to be this entity {10403299; 26975037; 29975249}. The reported age range is 14–75 years.

Etiology

Some cases are associated with tuberous sclerosis, but many are sporadic.

Pathogenesis

Recently, biallelic somatic mutations in the TSC1 or TSC2 genes have been identified in a great majority of ESC-RCCs {29975249; 29668487; 29941307}. This results in upregulation of the mTOR pathway, which regulates cellular metabolism, proliferation, and survival.

Macroscopic appearance

ESC-RCCs are typically well-circumscribed, tan, solid, and cystic, with reported sizes ranging from < 10 to 135 mm. Most are organ-confined and small (< 50 mm) (pT1), although multifocal cases have been reported {29975249}.

Histopathology

ESC-RCCs are typically unencapsulated. Although solid and cystic architecture is typical, some tumours are completely solid, and rare cases are extensively papillary {29975249}. The neoplastic cells are polygonal and have voluminous eosinophilic cytoplasm with basophilic cytoplasmic stippling. The stippling may include eosinophilic to purple cytoplasmic granules surrounded by a clear rim, reminiscent of leishmaniasis. The cysts are typically lined by a single layer of hobnail cells that are often multinucleated {32251007}. Prominent cytoplasmic vacuolization (consisting of either large or small vacuoles) may result in partial cytoplasmic clearing. The nuclei are round to oval with minimal pleomorphism and variably prominent nucleoli. Clusters of foamy histiocytes and lymphocytes are often found admixed with the neoplastic cells, as well as psammoma bodies. Ultrastructural examination shows that the stippling corresponds to aggregated rough endoplasmic reticulum {26414221}.

Immunohistochemistry

The carcinoma cells typically label for PAX8. CK20 immunoreactivity is characteristically patchy, ranging from isolated positive cells to more extensive immunoreactivity (in 30–40% of the carcinoma cells), but some cases are negative. Similarly, cathepsin K is usually positive in a patchy fashion, although occasional tumours are negative and some are diffusely positive. CK7 and KIT (CD117) are usually negative. CK7 can show focal staining, but it is almost always less than a paired CK20-stained section. Occasional tumours are immunoreactive for melan-A {29975249; 29885406}. Cytoplasmic stippling, psammoma bodies, frequent cathepsin K immunoreactivity, and occasional immunoreactivity for melan-A raise the differential diagnosis of MiT family translocation renal cell carcinoma. The binucleated and solid areas of these neoplasms, as well as cytoplasmic vacuolization, can resemble chromophobe renal cell carcinoma {29975249; 28786877}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of TSC1 or TSC2 gene alterations may be helpful {29975249; 29668487; 29941307}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: solid and cystic architecture; voluminous eosinophilic cytoplasm with coarse basophilic stippling.

Desirable: CK20 immunoreactivity; mutations in TSC1 or TSC2.

Staging

Staging of ESC-RCCs follows the same Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system as used for other subtypes of RCC.

Prognosis and prediction

Albeit with limited follow-up data, the majority of ESC-RCCs appear to have been cured by resection. Rare cases with lymph node or haematogenous metastases have been reported {29975249; 29265482; 29885406}. One patient with haematogenous liver metastases at diagnosis had a complete response to mTOR pathway inhibitors and remains disease-free 10 years after diagnosis {29975249}.
