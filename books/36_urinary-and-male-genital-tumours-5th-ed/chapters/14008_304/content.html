14.0.0.8 Hereditary phaeochromocytoma-paraganglioma syndromes

Definition

Hereditary phaeochromocytoma–paraganglioma syndromes are inherited tumour syndromes characterized by the presence of paragangliomas and/or phaeochromocytomas (PPGLs).

Localization

Paragangliomas arise from paraganglia in association with the sympathetic or parasympathetic autonomic nervous system. Sympathetic paragangliomas arise from paravertebral and prevertebral sympathetic chains and sympathetic nerves innervating the thorax, pelvis, and retroperitoneum. Phaeochromocytoma is an intra-adrenal sympathetic paraganglioma arising from the medulla. Parasympathetic paragangliomas often occur along parasympathetic nerves in the head and neck.

Familial PPGLs are often multifocal. Mutations in the SDH genes (SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD, and SDHAF2) account for half of all hereditary PPGLs {29239034} and are discussed separately (see section Succinate dehydrogenase–deficient tumour syndromes).

In multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN2), the adrenal medulla is most often involved, and phaeochromocytomas are often multifocal and bilateral {6103678}. In a review of 6 studies comprising 236 individuals with von Hippel–Lindau syndrome (VHL)-related PPGLs, 90% of the patients had phaeochromocytomas, 44% had bilateral phaeochromocytomas, and 19% had paragangliomas {22041710}.

In patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) and PPGLs, 95% of the tumours were phaeochromocytomas, 14% were bilateral phaeochromocytomas, and 6% were sympathetic paragangliomas {22041710}. Hereditary paragangliomas can occur in the genitourinary system {26457353; 17875164}.

Clinical features

Patients with PPGLs may present with symptoms of catecholamine production. Parasympathetic paragangliomas are usually non-secreting. Hypertension, tachycardia, sweating, and anxiety may occur. Micturition-associated hypertension, syncope, haematuria, palpitations, and headache can occur with urinary bladder paragangliomas {4106848}.

PPGLs in MEN2 and NF1 are associated with norepinephrine and epinephrine secretion, whereas those in VHL produce norepinephrine {21862435}.

Phaeochromocytomas occur in about 50% of patients with MEN2A, often a decade after medullary thyroid carcinoma {22584709}. PPGLs occur in as many as 26% of patients with VHL {22041710}. In children, 40% of phaeochromocytomas are related to VHL {20833332}. In patients with NF1, PPGLs are identified in 0.1–5.7% of cases clinically and in 3.3–13.0% of cases at autopsy {10524872}.

Epidemiology

At least 40% of adult PPGLs are associated with pathogenic germline variants {29239044} that lead to multifocal PPGLs (frequently extra-adrenal) and a young age at disease onset {12000816}.

Pathogenesis

The discovery of pathogenic germline variants in PPGLs is an ever-changing field and currently encompasses at least 23 mutually exclusive susceptibility genes, including RET, NF1, TMEM127, MAX, KIF1B, VHL, FH, MDH2, EPAS1 (HIF2A), SDH genes (SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD, and SDHAF2), EGLN2 (PHD1), EGLN1 (PHD2), GOT2, SLC25A11, BAP1, MEN1, KMT2D (MLL2), DLST, IDH3B, and DNMT3A {25800244; 14974914; 23778871; 26032282; 25766404; 29431636; 28720665; 29740169; 22452945; 18726616; 20959442; 29977594; 23090011; 30929736}.

Macroscopic appearance

PPGLs are circumscribed and usually unencapsulated, with a pink or grey-tan cut surface.

Histopathology

Hereditary PPGLs are histologically similar to sporadic tumours, but inherited tumours can have unique features. Phaeochromocytomas in MEN2 can be multiple or single, and they are often associated with diffuse medullary hyperplasia or small nodules and abundant hyaline globules. Expansion of the adrenal medulla (previously termed “adrenal medullary hyperplasia” but now recognized to be small neoplasms) may be seen adjacent to the phaeochromocytomas driven by SDH genes, RET, TMEM127, and MAX {23478233; 21172883; 27838885}.

VHL-related tumours may have a thick vascular capsule and oedematous stroma. The cells are small to medium-sized, lack nuclear atypia and mitoses, have clear or amphophilic cytoplasm, and lack hyaline globules {12114747}. NF1-related and MAX mutation–related PPGLs may be conventional but are often composite-type. NF1-related tumours usually lack the marked vascularity of VHL tumours. SDH tumours may have a distinct pseudorosette pattern and circular nests of monotonous epithelioid cells with vacuolated cytoplasm, and they generally lack sheet-like architecture, spindling, and densely granular cytoplasm {24659481; 31789631}.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells are positive for neuroendocrine markers (chromogranin A, synaptophysin, INSM1), tyrosine hydroxylase, and DBH, and they are generally negative for keratin (with exceptions) {26386079; 31442521}. Intratumoural sustentacular cells are positive for S100 and SOX10. Loss of SDHB immunoreactivity occurs with mutation in any SDH subunit, whereas loss of SDHA predicts SDHA mutation {21752896; 19576851}. CAIX may be positive in VHL tumours {23257898}. Fumarate hydratase and 2-succinocysteine (2SC) immunohistochemistry help identify FH mutation (see section Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma syndrome); however, experience with MAX immunohistochemistry as a marker of MAX mutation has been mixed {24334767; 27857127; 29079178; 29779206}.

Cytology

Aspiration biopsy of PPGLs is not advised because of potential bleeding or catecholamine release with the risk of hypertensive crisis {19958944}. If the patient is pre-medicated, confirmation of the paraganglioma can be made along with molecular investigation to help evaluate for germline susceptibility {29575826}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

All patients should undergo germline screening, taking into consideration the clinical and pathological findings.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Pathological

Essential: morphological and immunohistochemical harbingers of germline disease.

Desirable: use of SDHB and other biomarkers relevant to tumour pathogenesis.

Clinical

Essential: a family or personal history of PPGLs or PPGL syndromes.

Desirable: functional imaging and germline testing; measurement of all three metabolites of parent catecholamines (e.g. 3-methoxytyramine, metanephrine, and normetanephrine).

Staging

The eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system is used. A multidisciplinary international pathology reporting template was introduced in 2014 {24476517}, and a recent international pathology-standardized reporting guide was introduced by the International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting (ICCR) {32407815}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is similar for hereditary and sporadic PPGLs {16372223}, although recurrences may be more common in familial disease {15644401}. The prognosis may depend on the syndrome. SDHB-PPGLs have higher morbidity and mortality than PPGLs driven by other SDH genes {22041710}, whereas in MEN2, phaeochromocytomas account for fewer deaths than medullary thyroid carcinomas {24030942}.

High proliferation, comedonecrosis, diffuse growth, and tumour cellularity have been the most commonly accepted adverse findings {32407815}. However, no single histological finding or biomarker can reliably predict metastatic disease {30217041; 24476517}. In practice, large tumour size, extra-adrenal location, molecular clusters, and biochemical status are considered in the risk stratification.
