3.1.7.3 ELOC (formerly TCEB1)-mutated renal cell carcinoma

Definition

ELOC-mutated renal cell carcinomas (RCCs) are RCCs that harbour mutations in the ELOC (TCEB1) gene at 8q21.11.

ICD-O coding

8311/3 ELOC (formerly TCEB1)-mutated renal cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours arise in the kidney cortex.

Clinical features

These neoplasms appear as renal masses, often as incidental findings detected in imaging studies for other conditions such as haematuria {23797736; 25676555; 31813809; 30986793; 27173781}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 20 cases have been reported. The majority (> 90%) have occurred in male patients with a median age of 60 years {25676555; 27173781; 30986793}.

Etiology

ELOC mutations almost exclusively involve VHL protein binding site residue Y79, resulting in non-binding with the VHL complex and consequent HIF1α accumulation and overexpression {23797736; 25676555}.

Pathogenesis

ELOC encodes elongin C, which is part of the VHL complex that ubiquitinates and thereby inactivates HIF1α. In these neoplasms, inactivation of ELOC by mutation accompanied by the loss of the other allele (typically by the loss of the entire chromosome 8) abrogates the function of elongin C; this is consistent with its role as a tumour suppressor gene {25676555}. The loss of function of elongin C prevents the degradation of HIF1α and results in its accumulation {25676555; 31813809}.

Macroscopic appearance

These neoplasms are typically small (pT1) and well-delineated renal tumours.

Histopathology

These neoplasms have a nodular appearance at low power, created by thick transecting fibromuscular bands. The architecture is that of branching infolding tubules and well-formed papillae. The neoplastic cells have voluminous clear cytoplasm and prominent cell borders. The neoplastic cells are consistently immunoreactive for CK7 (with labelling ranging from patchy to diffuse), CAIX (typically in a complete membranous fashion), and CD10. The high-molecular-weight cytokeratin marker 34βE12 is usually negative or very focal.

These tumours have a broad morphological spectrum, but the main differential diagnoses are clear cell RCC and clear cell papillary tumours. Unlike most clear cell RCCs, ELOC-mutated RCCs label for CK7 and do not show VHL gene inactivation. Unlike clear cell papillary renal cell tumour or the related renal angiomyoadenomatous tumour, ELOC-mutated RCCs do not demonstrate prominent subnuclear vacuolization. ELOC-mutated RCCs overlap morphologically with a subset of RCCs associated with mutations in genes in the mTOR pathway. Some have grouped these neoplasms together as “renal cell carcinoma with leiomyomatous stroma” {31850909}.

Without molecular corroboration, one can reasonably refer to neoplasms meeting morphological criteria as clear cell RCCs with prominent fibromuscular septations and CK7 positivity, and give a differential diagnosis {31813809}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

These neoplasms are defined by ELOC mutations.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of ELOC mutation is required to definitively diagnose this neoplasm.

Staging

ELOC-mutated RCC is staged using guidelines for adult RCC.

Prognosis and prediction

The majority of these neoplasms have an indolent course. Recently, 2 patients were reported to develop disseminated disease, but their tumours demonstrated high nuclear grade {31813809}.
