3.1.1.2 Multilocular cystic renal neoplasm of low malignant potential

Definition

Multilocular cystic renal neoplasm of low malignant potential (MCNLMP) is an indolent, exclusively cystic, multiloculated renal tumour with low-grade clear cell lining.

ICD-O coding

8316/1 Multilocular cystic renal neoplasm of low malignant potential

ICD-11 coding

2F35 & XH7PR9 Benign neoplasm of urinary organs & Multilocular cystic renal neoplasm of low malignant potential

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MCNLMPs are unilateral solitary tumours with no side preference {27108004; 16393684}, although multifocal tumours have been reported in 2–5% cases {27108004; 29770853}.

Clinical features

MCNLMP tumours are asymptomatic, and as many as 90% are incidentally detected radiologically {22763870; 17846788}. Ultrasonography reveals well-defined multilocular cysts, although enhanced CT or MRI may be required for accurate Bosniak grading. The detection of small, solid, expansive nodules and tumour necrosis is incompatible with MCNLMP {25381150; 27108004} [[Paner GP, Cimadamore A, Darrell CM, et al. Surgical pathology of cystic renal cell carcinomas: Is there an overestimation of malignancy? Diagn Histopathol. 2020 Jul;26(7):320–9. Doi:10.1016/j.mpdhp.2020.04.003.]]. As many as 20% of patients present with symptoms such as hypertension, haematuria, a palpable mass, or flank or abdominal pain, but not the classic renal cell carcinoma (RCC) triad of gross haematuria, pain, and a palpable mass {17846788; 27108004}.

Epidemiology

MCNLMPs represent 0.5–2.5% of all renal tumours and ~10% of all cystic tumours {25381150; 27108004; 31356826} [[Paner GP, Cimadamore A, Darrell CM, et al. Surgical pathology of cystic renal cell carcinomas: Is there an overestimation of malignancy? Diagn Histopathol. 2020 Jul;26(7):320–9. Doi:10.1016/j.mpdhp.2020.04.003.]]. Median patient age is 55 years (range: 18–84 years) with a male predominance (M:F ratio: 1.8:1) {27341751; 25381150; 27108004; 16393684; 29770853}.

Etiology

Like clear cell RCC (CCRCC), MCNLMPs contain VHL gene alterations {22763870}. A common etiology of these two entities is also supported by the presence of concurrent CCRCC in 5–13% of sporadic MCNLMP cases, and by the development of both tumours in von Hippel–Lindau syndrome {27108004; 25381150; 29770853}.

Pathogenesis

Sixty per cent of patients with VHL germline mutations develop renal cysts due to abnormal microtubule formation and degeneration of primary cilia, which could further progress to MCNLMP via a cyst-dependent pathway {20418675; 25387056}. A next-generation sequencing study of 88 genes showed 35 overlapping genes detected both in MCNLMP and in cystic CCRCC, with a lower mutation frequency in MCNLMP and six differentially expressed genes {31098421}.

Macroscopic appearance

MCNLMPs are well-demarcated masses with a fibrous pseudocapsule, composed entirely of variably sized cysts filled with clear, gelatinous, serous, or haemorrhagic fluid. The cysts have thin septa devoid of solid nodules or areas of necrosis. Tumour size ranges from 17 to 110 mm (mean: 41 mm) {17846788}. Pathological substaging is distributed as follows: pT1 in 90% of cases; pT2 in 8–10%; and pT3 in 1–3% {17846788; 27108004; 22763870; 16393684; 29770853}.

Histopathology

Cyst lining consists of one or a few layers of optically clear, bland-looking cells {31356826; 22763870}. Randomly distributed nuclei are either WHO grade 1 (~70% of cases) or 2 (~30%). Clear cell clusters may be present within the fibrous capsule or thin septations; however, they should not alter their contours by expansile growth or exceed a 20× (1 mm) microscopic field of view {29770853; 22982885; 31356826}. The presence of necrosis, frequent or atypical mitoses, lymphovascular invasion, or rhabdoid or sarcomatoid dedifferentiation is incompatible with a diagnosis of MCNLMP. The immunoprofile is similar to that of CCRCC because of the diffuse reactivity for CAIX, EMA, CAM5.2, PAX8, and PAX2 {29292824; 22982885}.

Cytology

The cytology of MCNLMP is not relevant, because obtaining diagnostic cells is challenging.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Loss of the entire chromosome 3 or its short arm (3p) harbouring the VHL locus is detected by FISH in 77% of MCNLMP cases {20348877; 29770853}. Inactivating VHL mutations are detected in 25–40% of cases {29292824; 21151099}. Although the rate of biallelic VHL inactivation in MCNLMP is not known, it is likely to be significantly lower than the 92% rate reported for CCRCC {25387056}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pure cystic multiloculated architecture with low-grade clear cells lacking solid growth.

Desirable: an absence of papillary projections, nuclear alignment, and prominent regression features.

Note: An unequivocal diagnosis of MCNLMP should not be made on needle biopsy alone, because this method provides limited sampling and the features of MCNLMP overlap with those of cystic CCRCC and even solid CCRCC with a focal cystic/degenerative component. Additionally, clear cell papillary renal cell tumour can manifest as a nearly entirely cystic tumour and mimic MCNLMP.

Staging

Excellent outcomes regardless of tumour size, stage, and nuclear grade indicate that the current staging systems may not be suitable for this entity {27108004}.

Prognosis and prediction

MCNLMPs are benign, according to an analysis of > 500 published cases. There are no reports of progression, metastases, or cancer-related death with long-term follow-up {27341751; 25381150; 27108004; 16393684; 29770853; 31356826}. Recurrence has been documented in 2 cases {17846788; 31356826}.
