3.1.4.2 Chromophobe renal cell carcinoma

Definition

Chromophobe renal cell carcinoma (ChRCC) is characterized by large pale and/or smaller eosinophilic tumour cells with wrinkled nuclei and perinuclear haloes.

ICD-O coding

8317/3 Chromophobe cell renal carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 & XH6153 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis & Renal cell carcinoma, chromophobe type

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ChRCC develops in the renal cortex and can be multifocal or bilateral.

Clinical features

Most ChRCCs are discovered incidentally in asymptomatic patients. Some ChRCCs are associated with Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome, which is an autosomal dominant cancer predisposition syndrome harbouring FLCN germline mutation. Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome is characterized by fibrofolliculomas, a predisposition of bilateral multifocal renal tumours, and pulmonary cysts that can cause spontaneous pneumothorax {11927500} (see section Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome).

Epidemiology

ChRCCs account for 5–7 % of renal cell carcinomas (RCCs) {18813125; 21602658; 15833508; 22367296}. The median age is 59 years (range: 17–92 years) {18813125; 22367296; 31326335; 31760491}.

Etiology

Most tumours are sporadic. Hereditary rare forms include Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome with mutations in FLCN, and Cowden syndrome with mutations in PTEN {12204536; 12459621; 23764071; 33276850}. Like oncocytoma, ChRCC is thought to arise from the intercalated cells of the renal cortex.

Pathogenesis

ChRCCs show losses of chromosomes 1, 2, 6, 10, 13, 17, 21, and Y {15467713; 13825270; 8674042; 7519827}. Eosinophilic tumour may show fewer or no genetic alterations {25155756}. There are frequent somatic mitochondrial DNA mutations {1381433; 12353267; 25155756} but these are not specific for ChRCC. Comprehensive genomic analysis of ChRCC demonstrated a low somatic mutation rate with mutations in TP53, PTEN, TERT promoter, FOXI1, RHCG, LINC01187, and CYCLOPS genes {25155756; 32299640; 29925010; 29353061; 31200327}.

Macroscopic appearance

Often ChRCCs are large, with a mean size of 80 mm (10–300 mm). They are mostly circumscribed and unencapsulated. Most classic ChRCCs have a grey or light-tan cut surface. The intensity of brown or mahogany colour correlates with the quantity of eosinophilic cells. A central scar is present in 15% of tumours. Gross haemorrhage and necrosis are present in 25% of cases, and involvement of veins is rarely seen. A cystic pattern is rare, but it can occur {9845733; 15756595; 27631338}.

Histopathology

The tumour cells are arranged in solid sheets, separated by often hyalinized vascular septa. Some tumours show variable nested, trabecular, alveolar, or even papillary patterns {18813125; 21602658; 22367296; 31918172}, or a microcystic or cystic morphology {9845733; 15756595; 27631338}. Sarcomatoid changes occur in 2–8% of tumours and are associated with a poor prognosis {18813125; 31760491; 9331291; 31036466}. ChRCC has patchy or no fibromuscular pseudocapsule, and it leads to the entrapment of benign renal tubules, which in occasional cases can give rise to a papillary tumour {25838331; 31444626}.

Classic ChRCCs show a predominance of pale cells with clear cytoplasm. The cell membrane is prominent and has a plant cell–like morphology. The cytoplasm is translucent and finely reticulated. Some larger cells with voluminous clear to foamy cytoplasm are often present. Hyperchromatic nuclei and the presence of perinuclear cytoplasmic clarity (perinuclear haloes) are characteristic. Another cell type is characterized by densely eosinophilic and granular cytoplasm. Pale and eosinophilic cells can be randomly juxtaposed. The nuclei often have a distinctive irregular wrinkled (raisinoid) appearance, and binucleation is common. In the eosinophilic pattern of ChRCC, eosinophilic cells are the predominant cell type. Hale colloidal iron staining is variable, but cells often show diffuse cytoplasmic staining in classic ChRCC.

Ultrastructurally, ChRCC is characterized by numerous cytoplasmic microvesicles, especially in the pale cells, while mitochondria are abundant in the eosinophilic cells {9273970; 7681259; 10976699}.

Immunohistochemistry

CK7 is frequently diffusely expressed in > 75% of ChRCCs. Occasionally, there are only a few clusters of weak cells with membranous staining, which should be not present in oncocytomas {17895753; 12047768; 25025368; 17683191}. ChRCCs are usually positive for KIT (CD117), parvalbumin, and Ksp-cadherin, and they show frequent expression of MOC31, claudin-7, and EpCAM {17895753; 15105658; 11504835; 15613858}. Cathepsin K is positive in ChRCCs, although less strongly than in oncocytomas or distal tubules {33764165}. CAIX (CA9) is generally negative. Vimentin is negative, but it can be expressed in areas with sarcomatoid differentiation. CD10 is usually negative but may show focal positivity {15897741; 15286660}. SDHB is positive in ChRCC.

Differential diagnosis

The differential includes oncocytoma, SDHB-deficient RCC, oncocytic renal carcinoma NOS, MiT family translocation RCC, and oncocytic angiomyolipoma. Oncocytomas may strongly resemble eosinophilic ChRCCs, but they should not have groups of CK7-positive cells or mitotic activity. Some chromophobe tumours demonstrate a mixture of cells with morphological and immunophenotypic features that overlap with those of renal oncocytoma and ChRCC. Overlapping histology between oncocytoma and ChRCC may reflect intratumoural heterogeneity rather than a distinct subtype. The distinction between oncocytoma and ChRCC can be challenging in small biopsies, and it may pose diagnostic difficulties even in the resection specimen {33100748}. Hybrid oncocytic/chromophobe tumours were initially described in patients with Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome, but they can also occur in renal oncocytosis or sporadically. Although the morphological criteria for this diagnosis are not exactly defined, these tumours are currently best regarded as a subset of ChRCC.

Cytology

Cytological findings in ChRCC include cells with well-defined borders and thickened cell membranes; irregular nuclear features with cytoplasmic nuclear inclusions, nuclear grooves, and multinucleation; and abandoned eosinophilic granular cytoplasm.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In situ hybridization detecting the typical monosomy of several chromosomes can be helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large pale cells with prominent cell membranes and perinuclear haloes; groups of CK7-positive cells.

Desirable: absence of vimentin expression (except in sarcomatoid differentiation).

Staging

Staging follows the same Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems as used for other subtypes of RCC.

Prognosis and prediction

ChRCC has a favourable prognosis compared with clear cell RCC or papillary RCC, with a 5-year survival rate of 78–100% {18813125; 14699037; 10931460; 21602658; 20679882}. Tumour stage, sarcomatoid change, and tumour necrosis independently predict outcome {18813125; 22367296; 9331291; 31760491; 31036466}. Histological grading is not recommended for ChRCC. There are currently proposals for a two-tiered, a three-tiered, and a four-tiered grading system {20679882; 22367296; 31760491; 33172723}.
