3.1.2.1 Renal papillary adenoma

Definition

Renal papillary adenomas are unencapsulated tumours with papillary, tubular, or tubulopapillary configurations of low histological grade and a diameter of ≤ 15 mm.

ICD-O coding

8260/0 Papillary adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F35 & XH09B0 Benign neoplasm of urinary organs & Papillary adenoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: tubulopapillary adenoma.

Not recommended: renal adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Papillary adenomas usually occur in the renal cortex, particularly in the capsular or subcapsular area.

Clinical features

Virtually all papillary adenomas are clinically silent and discovered as incidental findings.

Epidemiology

Detailed sectioning of autopsy kidneys has detected papillary adenomas in 10% of individuals aged 21–40 years and in 40% of those aged 70–90 years {9503505}. They are more frequent in elderly patients, patients on long-term haemodialysis, and patients with renal vascular disease or glomerulosclerosis {30418186}. They have been reported in 33% of patients with acquired cystic disease and in 14% of patients undergoing transplantation for end-stage renal disease {12028461}.

Etiology

Papillary adenomas have been reported in kidneys bearing renal cell carcinoma {1942266}, and they are common in the kidneys of patients with hereditary papillary renal carcinoma {10647647}. This lesion may occasionally be found entrapped in oncocytic tumours {31444626}.

Pathogenesis

Whole-exome sequencing has demonstrated that KMT2C mutations may be an important early event in the evolution of papillary adenoma {31381885}. Papillary adenomas frequently show trisomy 7 and 17 in addition to loss of the Y chromosome {14559990; 10398084}.

Macroscopic appearance

Papillary adenomas arise in the renal cortex and are often subcapsular; larger adenomas are often wedged-shaped, with the base at the cortical surface. In cases of numerous papillary adenomas, the term “renal adenomatosis” is applied.

Histopathology

Papillary adenomas show papillary, tubular, or tubulopapillary architecture. Adenoma cells have predominantly pale and scant cytoplasm, round to oval nuclei with stippled to clumped chromatin, minimal nuclear pleomorphism, inconspicuous nucleoli, and absent or very rare mitosis. Foamy macrophages and psammoma bodies may be present. Papillary adenomas are unencapsulated and blend with adjacent benign tubular epithelium {13628880}.

Most adenomas show expression of CK7, CD10, and AMACR {7531836}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: size ≤ 15 mm; papillary, tubular, or tubulopapillary architecture; absence of a fibrous capsule; low histological grade (grade 1–2).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Until 2015, renal adenomas were defined as tumours measuring ≤ 5 mm. The decision to increase the size cut-off point of papillary adenomas to 15 mm was based on evidence that unencapsulated low-grade (grade 1–2) tumours of this size have virtually no capacity to metastasize {21783319}. The presence of papillary adenoma in a donor kidney is not a contraindication for renal transplantation {25055966}.
