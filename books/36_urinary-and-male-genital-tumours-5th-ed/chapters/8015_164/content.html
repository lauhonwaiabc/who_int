8.0.1.5 Mucinous borderline tumour

Definition

Mucinous borderline tumour is a non-invasive neoplasm with gastrointestinal-type or endocervical-type epithelium, characterized by complex architecture and cytological atypia.

ICD-O coding

8472/1 Mucinous borderline tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F77 & XH2FF0 Neoplasm of uncertain behaviour of male genital organs & Mucinous cystic tumour of borderline malignancy

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Testis and paratestis

Clinical features

Patients usually present with painless or occasionally painful swelling of the testis {25821618; 30287624}.

Epidemiology

Intraparenchymal testicular or paratesticular mucinous borderline tumours are very rare. Cases have been reported in middle-aged and elderly men (age range: 42–69 years) {12960806}.

Etiology

The tumours may derive from mesothelium by a process of Müllerian metaplasia, or from Müllerian rests.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The rare cases reported are unilateral cystic and solid tumours measuring 13–145 mm {12960806; 25821618}. The external surface is described as smooth, whereas the cut surface can show fibrotic, haemorrhagic, and mucoid areas {22919149; 25821618}.

Histopathology

The tumour is mostly characterized by cystic areas with papillary projections lined by gastrointestinal-type epithelium, usually showing mild to moderate atypia {20694089}. Occasionally, severe atypia is identified. Scattered goblet cells can be present. Complex papillary architecture and stratification of the nuclei can be seen. There is often a surrounding fibrotic wall with inflammation and occasionally dystrophic calcification or metaplastic ossification. Extravasation of mucin into the surrounding tissue can be seen {12960806; 22919149; 9762558; 20694089}. Severe cytological and architectural atypia warrants a diagnosis of intraepithelial carcinoma {12960806}. Occasional borderline tumours can show microinvasion. This is defined as small foci of stromal invasion, each measuring < 5 mm in linear extent and showing the same degree of cellular atypia as the borderline tumour {12960806}. The few reported cases showed CK20 positivity with variable expression of CK7 and CDX2 {22919149; 25821618; 10086514}.

Primary mucinous borderline tumour of the testicular adnexa should be distinguished from metastasis from a distant primary tumour and also from a somatic tumour arising from a testicular germ cell tumour. Because metastasis from a gastrointestinal primary can show a similar immunohistochemical phenotype to that of a primary testicular mucinous tumour, imaging and/or endoscopy is often required to help differentiate between these diagnoses {30287624}. A lack of 12p gain (which is typically seen in postpubertal teratoma) and an absence of background germ cell neoplasia in situ can be used to exclude postpubertal monodermal teratoma {30287624}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a non-invasive mucinous tumour with cellular atypia and architectural complexity; tumours with invasive foci are classified as microinvasive mucinous borderline tumour when each focus measures < 5 mm in linear extent; the background testis has no invasive or intratubular germ cell neoplasia.

Staging

There is no American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for these tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Mucinous borderline tumours are reported to have an excellent prognosis, even when foci of microinvasion or intraepithelial carcinoma are present. No malignant behaviour has been reported in the well-documented cases {12960806; 20694089}.
