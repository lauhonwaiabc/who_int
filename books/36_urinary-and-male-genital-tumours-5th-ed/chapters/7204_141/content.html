7.2.0.4 Mixed teratoma and yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type

Definition

Mixed teratoma and yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type, is a testicular germ cell tumour that combines teratoma and yolk sac tumour (YST) and predominantly occurs in children aged < 6 years. There is no association with germ cell neoplasia in situ.

ICD-O coding

9085/3 Mixed teratoma and yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type

ICD-11 coding

2C80.Y & XH2PS1 Other specified malignant neoplasms of testis & Mixed germ cell tumour

Related terminology

Not recommended: germ cell tumour type 1.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Testis

Clinical features

See section Teratoma, prepubertal-type and section Yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type .

Epidemiology

These tumours occur very rarely in the testis. Therefore, their real incidence is unknown, but case studies suggest that pure prepubertal-type YSTs are at least 10 times as frequent as prepubertal-type mixed tumours {14634440; 15247758; 23707551}. Mixed tumours have also been reported in adults, with 2 well-supported cases, 1 in a patient with Down syndrome {25427839}. See also section Teratoma, prepubertal-type and section Yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type .

Etiology

See section Teratoma, prepubertal-type and section Yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type .

Pathogenesis

The current hypothesis is that YST develops in a teratoma. In sacrococcygeal teratomas, the presence of immature elements is assumed to be associated with an increased risk of progression towards YST {16410129; 9737245}. Although progression in an early, small, immature teratoma may result in a pure YST by overgrowth, progression in a fully developed teratoma may manifest as a mixed tumour. See also section Teratoma, prepubertal-type and section Yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type .

Macroscopic appearance

Most cases have the same macroscopic appearance as prepubertal-type teratoma, because the YST foci are usually small.

Histopathology

YST foci in teratomas are usually small and associated with immature elements, making them easy to overlook on microscopic examination {9737245; 15613854}. Immunohistochemical staining for AFP or SALL4 can help highlight YST components, although weak to moderate expression can also be demonstrated in immature teratomatous elements {19428066; 24525512; 32222813}. The tumours are not associated with germ cell neoplasia in situ.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

These tumours lack 12p abnormalities. See also section Teratoma, prepubertal-type and section Yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type .

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a combination of teratoma and YST elements in the absence of germ cell neoplasia in situ.

Desirable: immunohistochemical staining for YST (e.g. AFP); absence of 12p abnormalities.

Staging

See section Yolk sac tumour, prepubertal-type .

Prognosis and prediction

In the absence of metastasis and with adequate AFP normalization after surgery, adjuvant therapy is not required and the patient should continue to be followed up. In the rare case of systemic disease, further management is based on YST therapy.
