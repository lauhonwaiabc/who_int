14.0.0.7 BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome

Definition

BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndrome characterized by heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in the BAP1 (BRCA1 associated protein 1) tumour suppressor gene.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

614327 Tumour predisposition syndrome; TPDS

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is associated with increased risks of a range of benign and malignant tumours including BAP1-inactivated melanocytic tumours / atypical Spitz tumours; uveal melanoma; mesothelioma; cutaneous melanoma; renal cell carcinoma (RCC), particularly clear cell RCC (CCRCC); basal cell carcinoma; hepatocellular carcinoma; cholangiocarcinoma; meningioma; and possibly other malignancies {21874000; 21941004; 21874003; 23709298; 23684012; 30517737}.

Clinical features

Because of the limited number of families reported to date, the penetrance, frequency, and natural history are yet to be determined. However, an earlier age of onset was observed for BAP1-related cancers than for corresponding cancers in the general population. As many as 85% of patients will develop malignancy by 65 years of age {26096145; 30517737}. For RCC, there is a wide range in age of onset (36–72 years), but the median is lower than that for sporadic RCC (47 years vs 64 years, respectively) {26096145}.

Epidemiology

BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is rare. It was first reported in 2011, with increased risks of melanoma and mesothelioma {21874000; 21874003}, and in 2013, germline BAP1 mutations were found in families with multiple cases of RCC {23709298; 23684012}. To date, > 200 families have been described, with nearly all individuals developing ≥ 1 tumour during their lifetime {26096145; 30517737; 32690542}. RCC was reported in 18 (10%) of 174 probands and their relatives with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome {26096145}. In sporadic CCRCCs, BAP1 alterations occur at a frequency of 10–15% {22683710; 24382589; 24076305}.

Etiology

All reported cases have been diagnosed in individuals who have inherited a BAP1 pathogenic variant from a parent.

Pathogenesis

BAP1 is located on 3p21.1 and encodes a protein that interacts with ASXL1 to form the polycomb group repressive deubiquitinase complex. This complex plays a key role in chromatin modification by mediating the nuclear deubiquitination of H2A and HCFC1 {22683710; 32690542}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of BAP1-related tumours is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Owing to the small number of cases reported, the phenotype of BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome–associated RCC is still not fully elucidated. CCRCCs are most commonly described, but there are at least two published cases with non-clear cell subtypes {29978187; 31326218}. Multifocal CCRCC and atypical cysts have been reported {23709298; 23684012}. Tumours show loss of BAP1 immunoreactivity in the tumour nuclei, confirming a two-hit inactivation of BAP1 {23709298; 23333114}. Somatic BAP1 mutations in sporadic CCRCC are associated with high tumour grade and decreased survival, but whether this is the case in the context of BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is an open question {23333114; 22683710; 24382589; 23684012}.

Cytology

The cytological features are similar to those of sporadic renal tumours.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of a germline heterozygous pathogenic variant in BAP1 by molecular genetic testing is required to establish a diagnosis of BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of a germline pathogenic variant in BAP1.

Note: No phenotypic diagnostic criteria are associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome. Although there are no formal criteria, testing for BAP1 mutations should be considered in patients with (1) two or more confirmed BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome–associated tumours or (2) one confirmed BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome–associated tumour and a first- or second-degree relative with a confirmed BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome–associated tumour {29413689; 30517737; 26096145; 27748099}. Loss of BAP1 by immunohistochemistry in these tumours can be used to further screen for individuals who need germline testing {23709298; 30517737}.

Staging

Staging is similar to that for sporadic renal tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Sporadic BAP1-deficient CCRCCs are more aggressive than corresponding tumours lacking such mutations {23333114; 22683710}. Similarly, among the tumours of 292 patients with an inherited kidney cancer syndrome, BAP1-deficient RCCs had the fastest median growth rates {32083993}. There are no consensus screening guidelines for rare RCC syndromes. Screening for uveal and cutaneous melanoma, mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, and RCC has been proposed for carriers of BAP1 germline pathogenic variants. The recommendations include eye and skin examinations starting in adolescence, as well as chest and abdominal imaging to screen for mesothelioma and RCC beginning at the age of 30 years {29413689; 26096145}, to enable the early detection of potentially aggressive renal tumours.
