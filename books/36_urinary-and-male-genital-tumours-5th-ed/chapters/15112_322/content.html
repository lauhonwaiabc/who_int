15.1.1.2 Adenocarcinoma of the seminal vesicle

Definition

Adenocarcinomas of the seminal vesicle are malignant gland-forming tumours originating from seminal vesicle epithelium. Diagnosis requires clinicopathological exclusion of secondary carcinomas.

ICD-O coding

8140/3 Adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C84 & XH74S1 Malignant neoplasms of other specified male genital organs & Adenocarcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Seminal vesicles, but may invade adjacent structures

Clinical features

Approximately 60 cases of primary adenocarcinoma of the seminal vesicles have been reported {3191369; 6471181; 12394673; 31961000; 31852695; 17445673; 28506732}. Patients often present with urinary obstruction and haematuria, and rarely with haematospermia {12394673; 23283099}. Ultrasonography, PET-CT, and MRI may be helpful to define the extent of the lesion and exclude local spread from tumours in adjacent organs {22040796}.

Epidemiology

These are rare tumours that mainly occur in men aged 40–70 years {3191369; 6471181; 12394673; 31961000; 31852695; 17445673; 28506732}. The mean age at diagnosis is 62 years (range: 19–90 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The tumour originates from the epithelium of the seminal vesicle, which is a Wolffian duct derivative. Seminal vesicle intraepithelial neoplasia has been proposed as a putative precursor {24631410}.

Macroscopic appearance

Seminal vesicle adenocarcinoma often occurs as a large cystic or solid mass (30–50 mm in diameter), and it may be bilateral {22723505}.

Histopathology

The tumours have a glandular differentiation, often with trabecular and papillary growth patterns. There may be a papillary clear cell pattern with occasional hobnail tumour cells. Goblet cells and mucin secretion may be seen. The seminal vesicle epithelium stains positively for PAX2, PAX8, 34βE12, and p63, and negatively for AMACR, PSA, PAP, NKX3-1, and P501S {17445673; 21934480; 33126153}. The tumour cells are positive for PAX8, CK7, CEA, and CA125, and negative for PSA, PAP, AR, p63, GATA3, WT1, calretinin, and CDX2 {23283099; 17445672; 10658909}. CK20 is either positive or negative {17445672}. AMACR stains are focally positive {28506732} or negative {31961000}. Identification of mucin production is helpful as well {6471181}.

The diagnosis of this entity is complicated by the frequent invasion of adenocarcinomas from surrounding organs, most often the prostate and less often the bladder or rectum {17445673; 23283099}. Such invasion must be excluded {13381470}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: seminal vesicle location (or for large tumours, seminal vesicle epicentre); adenocarcinoma, often with papillary-glandular and trabecular growth patterns; exclusion of metastasis and local spread clinically and pathologically; supportive immunohistochemistry (see above).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is poor, with survival usually < 3 years from diagnosis {29550197}. Complete surgical resection together with chemotherapy can occasionally offer a cure {31852695; 22723505; 29021144; 24113594; 30313047}. Penile and lung metastases have been reported {17445672}. Some tumours may be responsive to adjuvant hormone treatments {6471181; 12394673; 24113594; 3188318}. Serum CA125 level may be a useful biomarker for disease recurrence.
