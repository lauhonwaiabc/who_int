3.1.7.2 TFEB-altered renal cell carcinomas

Definition

TFEB-rearranged renal cell carcinomas (RCCs) harbour fusions involving the gene encoding the TFEB transcription factor, typically via a t(6;11)(p21;q12) translocation resulting in a MALAT1::TFEB fusion. TFEB-amplified RCCs demonstrate amplification of the 6p21 locus harbouring TFEB, resulting in TFEB overexpression and its downstream effects.

ICD-O coding

8311/3 TFEB-altered renal cell carcinomas

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis

Related terminology

Acceptable: t(6;11) renal cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Kidney

Clinical features

The clinical presentation of a TFEB-altered RCC as a renal mass is not specific.

Epidemiology

TFEB-rearranged RCCs are less common than TFE3-rearranged RCCs; only about 100 cases have been reported in the global literature. The mean and median patient age is 31 years {24618616}. TFEB-amplified RCCs occur in older patients (median age: 65 years); approximately 50 TFEB-amplified RCCs have been documented in the literature {27565001; 28840857; 28009604; 28338654}.

Etiology

Past exposure to cytotoxic chemotherapy is a risk factor for TFEB-rearranged RCC.

Pathogenesis

TFEB gene fusion or amplification in RCC results in the overexpression of native TFEB {27565001; 28840857; 28338654; 28009604}. Overexpressed native TFEB acts as an aberrant transcription factor that activates the expression of multiple downstream targets, including those normally activated by the related MiT family transcription factor MITF (such as melanocytic markers and cathepsin K) {12837690; 17283122; 31600176}. Recent work showed that TFEB gene expression is increased in TFEB-amplified RCC, although not as much as in TFEB-rearranged RCC, raising the possibility that other genes at the 6p21.1 locus, such as VEGFA, CCND3, or others, may be responsible for the aggressive behaviour {31600176}.

Macroscopic appearance

TFEB-rearranged RCCs and TFEB-amplified RCCs do not have a distinctive gross appearance.

Histopathology

The most distinctive pattern of the TFEB-rearranged RCC is that of a biphasic neoplasm, comprising nests of larger epithelioid cells and smaller cells clustered around basement membrane material {11395386; 22892601; 15644781}. The TFEB-rearranged RCC characteristically entraps single native renal tubules at their periphery. TFEB-rearranged RCC can overlap morphologically with TFE3-rearranged RCC. When the population of smaller cells is small or absent, the differential diagnosis of the TFEB-rearranged renal cell carcinoma is broader and includes clear cell RCC, oncocytic renal neoplasms, and epithelioid angiomyolipoma {29052596}.

The morphology of the TFEB-amplified RCC is less distinctive than that of the TFEB-rearranged RCC, and it frequently demonstrates high-grade, poorly differentiated RCC morphology and frequent oncocytic and papillary features.

In contrast to most RCCs, TFEB-rearranged RCCs underexpress epithelial markers such as cytokeratins and EMA, but they do consistently express PAX8 and other renal tubular markers {24618616; 20679884}. TFEB-rearranged RCCs consistently express the melanoma markers melan-A and HMB45 and the cysteine protease cathepsin K.

Nuclear immunoreactivity for TFEB protein is highly specific for the TFEB-rearranged RCC {15644781}, but a TFEB break-apart FISH assay is less affected by variable fixation than is immunohistochemistry, so it is the preferred diagnostic test for establishing the diagnosis in formalin-fixed, paraffin embedded material {22892601}. Aberrant melanocytic marker expression and cathepsin K labelling or TFEB overexpression are less consistently seen in TFEB-amplified RCC (present in about half of all cases).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The t(6;11) translocation fuses TFEB with MALAT1 (Alpha), an untranslated gene of unknown function, resulting in the overexpression of native TFEB {11244503; 12719541}. Alterative TFEB fusion partners have also been reported, including COL21A1, CADM2, EWSR1, CLTC, PPP1R10, ACTB, NEAT1, and KHDRBS2 {26536169; 31764220; 33208882}. Precise diagnostic criteria for TFEB-amplified RCC remain to be defined {28840857}. Although the initial study required high-level amplification and downstream effects of TFEB amplification (aberrant melanocytic marker expression) to make this diagnosis {27565001}, other studies have included low-level TFEB-amplified cases {28338654}; the significance of these cases is currently unclear.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: TFEB rearrangement or amplification identified by break-apart FISH, or TFEB gene fusion identified by RNA sequencing.

Staging

TFEB-altered RCCs are staged using guidelines for adult RCC.

Prognosis and prediction

TFEB-rearranged RCCs are more indolent than TFE3-rearranged RCCs, with < 10% of cases resulting in patient death {26536169}. Of the approximately 60 evaluable cases in the published literature, only 11 have developed metastases, leading to patient death in 4 cases. Most neoplasms have come to clinical attention at low stage (pT1 or pT2) and have had benign follow-up. Necrosis has been found to correlate with aggressive behaviour {31764220}. TFEB-rearranged RCCs have the potential to metastasize late – as long as 20 or 30 years after diagnosis. TFEB-amplified RCCs appear to be more aggressive, frequently presenting at an advanced local stage or with metastatic disease {27565001; 28840857; 28009604; 28338654}.
