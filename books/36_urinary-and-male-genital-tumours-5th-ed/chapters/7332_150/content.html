7.3.3.2 Juvenile granulosa cell tumour

Definition

Juvenile granulosa cell tumour (JGCT) is a sex cord tumour composed of primitive-appearing granulosa cells growing in solid and follicular patterns.

ICD-O coding

8622/0 Juvenile granulosa cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F34 & XH2U25 Benign neoplasm of male genital organs & Granulosa cell tumour of the testis, juvenile

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Testis

Clinical features

The usual presentation is a testicular mass; very rarely, there is gynaecomastia or premature puberty {32719989}. JGCTs are occasionally associated with karyotypic abnormalities {27626837; 25440720} or cryptorchidism {32719989}.

Epidemiology

JGCTs predominantly occur in the first 6 months of life, and very rarely beyond the first year {32719989} or in adults {17406141; 18313118}. They account for 6–7% of all prepubertal tumours of the testis and < 0.5% of all sex cord stromal tumours of the testis {3976984; 9134041; 20592370; 20850634; 32719989}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The median tumour size is 20 mm (range: 5–50 mm). The cut surface is often partly cystic, containing watery or mucoid fluid, with tan-white/yellow solid areas {32719989; 26076062; 3976984}.

Histopathology

A lobulated growth pattern with macrocystic and microcystic areas is characteristic. The cysts are filled with eosinophilic or basophilic fluid. A solid component is also usual; papillary, basaloid, and spindle cell patterns rarely occur. The tumour cells are polygonal with vacuolated or eosinophilic cytoplasm; they have ovoid nuclei without grooves and with small nucleoli. Fibrous or fibromyxoid stroma is frequent. Mitotic activity (> 5 mitoses/mm2) and apoptosis may be prominent. Regressive changes (hyalinization) may be seen. Rete testis involvement, entrapment of seminiferous tubules, and lymphovascular invasion can occur {26076062; 3976984}.

JGCTs commonly express SF1, calretinin, inhibin, SOX9, WT1, and FOXL2, as well as (focally) low-molecular-weight cytokeratins {26076062; 18721930}.

Differential diagnoses include yolk sac tumour (YST) and other sex cord stromal tumours. The nuclear features distinguish the JGCT from the adult granulosa cell tumour. Unlike JGCTs, only one fifth of prepubertal YSTs arise in patients aged < 1 year {25828390}. Microscopically, follicular elements are not a feature of YST. Immunohistochemistry is usually straightforward; unlike YSTs, JGCTs are negative for SALL4, AFP, and glypican-3 (GPC3) {26076062}. The differentiation from other sex cord stromal tumours may be difficult, but the presence of follicles and a younger age favour a diagnosis of JGCT.

Cytology

There are no reports in the literature.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FOXL2 mutations have not yet been systematically investigated; one investigated case was FOXL2-wildtype {22742556; 19516027}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a partially macrocystic or microcystic tumour composed of primitive granulosa cells; diffuse or lobular growth; follicles of variable size and shape.

Desirable: manifestation in neonates and infants in the first year of life; typical sex cord stromal tumour immunophenotype.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

JGCTs show benign behaviour, even after testis-sparing surgery {32719989}.
