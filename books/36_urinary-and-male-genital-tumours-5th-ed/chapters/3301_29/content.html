3.3.0.1 Mixed epithelial and stromal tumour of the kidney

Definition

This tumour type encompasses mixed epithelial and stromal tumour (MEST) and adult cystic nephroma, which are biphasic renal tumours composed of epithelial cysts lined by benign epithelium (usually of renal cell type) and a proliferation of cytologically benign–appearing spindle-shaped cells.

ICD-O coding

8959/0 Mixed epithelial and stromal tumour

8959/0 Adult cystic nephroma

ICD-11 coding

2C90.Y & XH0533 Other specified malignant neoplasms of kidney, except renal pelvis & Mixed epithelial and stromal tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: renal epithelial and stromal tumour.

Not recommended: cystic hamartoma of renal pelvis; leiomyomatous renal hamartoma.

Subtype(s)

Adult cystic nephroma

Localization

These are unilateral tumours, usually arising in the renal medulla {17414095; 15322873}.

Clinical features

Adult cystic nephromas and MESTs frequently occur in middle-aged women (M:F ratio: 1:7) {27831959; 27635943}. For the rare tumours that occur in men, there is often a history of hormone therapy {17414095; 10895818; 15322873}. Both tumours are mainly asymptomatic. When symptoms are present, haematuria and abdominal pain are the most common. On radiological studies, cystic nephroma is commonly a Bosniak III lesion, and as many as 70% of MESTs have a solid enhancing component {18313107}.

Epidemiology

These are rare tumours that occur overwhelmingly in women.

Etiology

Hormonal imbalance or other hormonal factors have been hypothesized as potential etiological factors.

Pathogenesis

One pathogenetic theory is that the presence of periductal fetal mesenchyme around the epithelial component in the kidney (in the renal medulla) becomes sensitive to female hormone stimulation and undergoes proliferation {10895818}. Gene expression profiling has demonstrated that MEST and cystic nephroma are different from other renal epithelial tumours {18971776}. The epithelial and stromal components both appear to be clonal {21753695}. Abnormalities of chromosomes 8, 11, and 17 are lacking, unlike in congenital cellular mesoblastic nephroma {11381370}. DICER1 mutation has not been found in MEST or adult cystic nephroma {26509905}, supporting the notion that these are unrelated to paediatric cystic nephroma.

Macroscopic appearance

MESTs and cystic nephromas are well-demarcated and typically unencapsulated tumours. Cystic nephromas are entirely cystic, whereas MESTs are solid and cystic or entirely solid. The solid areas have a white and firm cut surface {27831959; 27635943}.

Histopathology

Adult cystic nephromas are entirely cystic, whereas MESTs contain a variable solid component {16546041; 16390243; 17414095; 18971776}. Cystic nephromas and MESTs both show variably sized cysts and glands separated by stroma.

The epithelium lining the cysts in cystic nephroma is usually flat, cuboidal, or hobnail-shaped; rarely, cells with clear cytoplasm have been observed {27831959}. The septal stroma is of variable thickness, usually thin, and consists of fibrous tissue ranging from hypocellular to hypercellular, sometimes resembling ovarian stroma. Stromal calcifications are common. Stromal luteinization and nodules of hyalinized stroma with sharp contours resembling ovarian corpora albicantia can occur {15322873; 17414095; 27831959}. Scattered mitoses are seldom encountered, but atypia and necrosis are lacking.

The epithelial component in MEST is variable in architecture and in cell type. The glands are usually scattered in the stroma or closely clustered. Small cysts containing eosinophilic material reminiscent of thyroid follicles, complex branching tubules, phyllodes-type architecture, and papillary structures have been reported {27635943; 17414095}. The epithelium can be flat, cuboidal, or hobnail-shaped, but also urothelial, ciliated, or with clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm. Mucinous goblet cells and endometrioid differentiation have been described {15322873; 16025280}.

The septal stroma consists of fibrous tissue ranging from hypocellular to hypercellular. Smooth muscle stroma and fat are common. Myxoid and oedematous changes can occur {17414095; 27635943}. Scattered mitoses are seldom encountered, but there is no atypia or necrosis. Malignant transformation can rarely occur, usually manifesting as sarcomas arising from the stroma {11764393; 19133001; 22989206; 23304819; 18261632; 17707262}.

Immunohistochemistry

The stromal component is typically positive for actin, desmin, CD10, ER, and PR {17414095; 10895818; 18971776; 27831959; 27635943} and frequently the luteinized stromal cells express inhibin and calretinin {17414095; 17213356; 27831959; 27635943}. Cathepsin K is negative. The epithelial component is PAX2/8-positive {21836481}. Immunohistochemistry is helpful in the exclusion of renal synovial sarcoma, clear cell papillary tumour, and tubulocystic renal cell carcinoma.

Cytology

Cytological diagnosis may be difficult because aspiration specimens may contain occasional groups of cohesive renal epithelial cells, raising the differential diagnosis of renal cell carcinoma {23444187}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular pathology is rarely necessary for diagnostic purposes. If the distinction from paediatric cystic nephroma is necessary, an absence of DICER1 mutation may be helpful {26509905}. Renal synovial sarcoma may have entrapped cystic tubules, mimicking MEST {24061517; 10935649; 21836481}, in which case an absence of SS18 rearrangement may be helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Cystic nephroma

Essential: a multilocular cystic tumour made up of cysts lined by flat, cuboidal, or hobnail-shaped cells and separated by thin septa with benign-appearing spindle-shaped cells.

Desirable: immunohistochemical labelling for PR and/or ER and frequently for inhibin {27831959}.

MEST

Essential: a complex solid and cystic tumour composed of varied epithelial elements embedded in a variably cellular heterogeneous spindle-shaped cell stroma.

Desirable: immunohistochemical labelling for PR and/or ER but not for cathepsin K {27635943}.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours with typical features are benign; however, putative malignant transformation of MEST (typically with sarcomatous morphology) has been described, in which behaviour has been aggressive {18261632; 14987239; 17707262; 22989206; 11764393}.
