8.0.1.7 Endometrioid tumours

Definition

Endometrioid tumours are tumours with endometrioid epithelial differentiation showing a spectrum from benign (adenoma) to malignant (adenocarcinoma), including atypical epithelial proliferation (borderline tumour), and tumours with endometrial stromal differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8380/1 Endometrioid tumour, borderline

8380/3 Endometrioid adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F77 & XH5DQ2 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of male genital organs & Endometrioid adenoma, borderline malignancy

2F77 & XH0SD2 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of male genital organs & Endometrioid adenocarcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intratesticular or paratesticular (there is 1 reported case from the appendix testis) {2227840}

Clinical features

The mean patient age is 68 years (range: 59–82 years). Patients present with scrotal swelling {30921621}. Endometriosis may occur as a tumour-like lesion {3739969} or as an incidental finding {23964911}.

Epidemiology

These are very rare tumours, with only 4 endometrioid epithelial tumours reported to date: 1 borderline tumour {24623337} and 3 carcinomas {2227840; 21474549; 3739969}. Tumour-like endometriosis {3739969; 22104297} and 1 low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma of the paratestis {29394168} have also been reported. In addition, 1 seromucinous borderline tumour was reported, similar to that encountered within the morphological pattern of endometrioid tumours {27597522}. It is possible that in the past, endometrioid tumours were not designated as such {32619438}.

Etiology

An association with estrogen therapy for prostate carcinoma was observed {3739969; 22104297}. An association with endometriosis and teratoma has also been hypothesized {30921621}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of epithelial tumours has not been studied. For the case of low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma, a JAZF1::SUZ12 gene fusion was reported.

Macroscopic appearance

The epithelial tumours reported were mostly solid and/or cystic and have measured 30–90 mm {30921621}. The single endometrial stromal sarcoma reported was a firm and multinodular lesion measuring 20 mm {29394168}.

Histopathology

The histological pattern varies according to the biological category. Tumour-like endometriosis exhibits well-formed endometrioid glands and stroma surrounded by bundles of smooth muscle {3739969}. The borderline endometrioid tumour is cystic with a papillary architecture {24623337}. The adenocarcinomas show a glandular, papillary, cribriform, or solid growth pattern consistent with a stratified columnar epithelium with variable degrees of atypia {2227840; 21474549; 3739969}. Squamous differentiation with focal keratinization has also been reported {3739969}.

By immunohistochemistry, endometrioid tumours are positive for CK7, PAX8, ER, and PR {30921621; 21934480} and negative for CK20, CDX2, and glypican-3 (GPC3) {16512598}. The differential diagnosis includes other types of testicular ovarian epithelial–type tumours {30921621}, adenocarcinoma of the rete testis {30676333}, metastatic carcinomas from the upper gastrointestinal tract and colon {18769334; 21069550}, and endometrioid-type yolk sac tumour (positive for CDX2 and GPC3) {25145708}.

Low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma resembles its uterine counterpart, consisting of proliferative phase–type endometrial stromal cells and exhibiting a multinodular infiltrative growth pattern {29394168}. It is immunoreactive for CD10, IFITM1, vimentin, ER, and PR {28459169} [[Lin MS, Ayala AG, Ro JY. Ovarian-type tumors (Mullerian tumors) of the testis: clinicopathological findings with recent advances. Ann Urol Oncol. 2019;2(1):36–45. doi:10.32948/auo.2019.03.07.]].

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour with endometrioid-type morphology; borderline tumours exhibit atypical epithelial proliferation; adenocarcinomas show a confluent (maze-like) or complex papillary growth pattern similar to that of corresponding tumours of the female reproductive organs; endometrial stromal sarcomas show tumour cells resembling endometrial stroma cells with an infiltrative growth pattern; exclusion of metastasis or spread from adjacent organs.

Desirable: CK7, ER, and PAX8 positivity for epithelial tumours; CD10 and IFITM1 positivity for low-grade endometrial stromal sarcomas.

Staging

There is no American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for these tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

For the few endometrioid tumours described, including carcinomas, no metastatic events were reported {2227840; 21474549; 3739969}.
