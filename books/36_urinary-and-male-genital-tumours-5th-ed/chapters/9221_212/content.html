9.2.2.1 Penile adenosquamous and mucoepidermoid carcinomas

Definition

Penile adenosquamous carcinomas (ASCs) and mucoepidermoid carcinomas (MECs) are invasive squamous cell carcinomas (SCCs) with glandular and/or mucinous features.

ICD-O coding

8560/3 Adenosquamous carcinoma

8430/3 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C81.Y & XH7873 Other specified malignant neoplasm of penis & Adenosquamous carcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These rarely reported tumours are said to affect the glans penis, usually around the urethral meatus. Tumours may also extend to the coronal sulcus and foreskin. They are less common on ventral penile shaft skin {8554104; 27696488}.

Clinical features

Patients are usually in their sixth decade of life and present with a penile mass.

Epidemiology

To date, < 15 cases have been reported as ASC or MEC {29144557}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

It is hypothesized that the glandular/mucin-producing tumour cells are from mucinous glands around the meatus. Rarely, metaplastic goblet cells may be present in the foreskin.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours are large, firm, erythematous masses involving the glans penis, sometimes with ulceration. They are white-grey and deeply infiltrative, most often replacing the glans penis and sometimes extending to the coronal sulcus and foreskin.

Histopathology

ASCs are considered to be gland-forming SCCs, whereas MECs are solid tumours without overt gland formation. At present, both entities are placed under the rubric of ASC.

The SCC component is predominant, with morphological features of usual-type grade 1–2 SCC. Adjacent penile intraepithelial neoplasia may be present. The glandular component is often in the perimeatal region, with columnar or cuboidal cells, intracellular mucin, and sometimes intraluminal mucin. The glandular component stains for CEA and high-molecular-weight cytokeratin (34βE12) {16924331}.

The term “MEC” is more appropriate for tumours with only mucin-producing cells, without glandular structures {29144557}. Mucin stains, CK7, EMA (MUC1), MUC5AC, and MUC6 are positive in the mucinous component. CK5, CK14, p40, p16, and p63 are negative in the mucinous cells.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: SCC with gland formation and/or mucin-producing cells positive for CK7, CEA, and mucin stains and negative for p16.

Staging

Staging follows the same Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) system as staging of SCC of the penis.

Prognosis and prediction

Lymph node involvement has been reported in about 50% of patients at the time of diagnosis {25624963}. The mortality rate is reported to be low, but this is based on data from limited follow-up periods. Aggressive behaviour has been reported in some cases of MEC {25624963}.
