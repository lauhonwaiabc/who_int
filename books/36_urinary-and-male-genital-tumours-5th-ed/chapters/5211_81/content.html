5.2.1.1 Verrucous carcinoma of the bladder

Definition

Verrucous carcinoma is a subtype of exophytic squamous cell carcinoma with multiple filiform projections covered by well-differentiated squamous epithelium, with an invasive front with a pushing margin.

ICD-O coding

8051/3 Verrucous carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C9Y & XH5PM0 Other specified malignant neoplasms of urinary tract & Verrucous carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most cases of verrucous carcinoma occur in the bladder {30565306; 2033707}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with haematuria, dysuria, urinary frequency, or obstructive symptoms {30565306; 15257362; 6415887}. Urinary excretion of cellular debris has also been reported {8351470}.

Epidemiology

Verrucous carcinoma most commonly occurs in areas where schistosomiasis is endemic {8351470; 30565306}, although it has also been reported in patients from other areas {10738227}. It is seen more frequently in men, often in the fifth decade of life {30565306; 8351470}.

Etiology

Verrucous carcinoma of the bladder is most often seen in the setting of schistosomiasis, with a reported incidence of 5000 cases per 100 000 person-years in endemic areas {7306921; 30565306}. Rare sporadic cases from areas without endemic schistosomiasis have been reported {15257362; 10738227}.

Pathogenesis

Most verrucous carcinoma occurs in patients with schistosomiasis {7306921}, although a few cases unrelated to schistosomiasis have also been reported {16819332}. HPV infection is not associated with carcinogenesis {16819332; 15257362}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, these tumours appear as exophytic, fungating lesions that are typically solitary.

Histopathology

These tumours have the same histological features as those that arise in other areas of the body, including warty gross features, large hyperkeratotic papillae, and acanthosis with minimal cytological atypia. They have a broad, pushing, invasive front, with tongue-like downward projections. Mitoses are rare. This is different from the classic small, irregular invasive tumour nests with paradoxical maturation seen in typical squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder {30565306; 2033707}.

The diagnosis of verrucous carcinoma requires examination of the entire lesion to rule out the presence of conventional invasive squamous carcinomas.

Focal cytoplasmic and membranous positivity for EGFR and TGF-α have been reported {9828815}, as has increased p53 expression {10738227}.

Cytology

Not available

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a warty mass on gross examination; histology shows verrucous or papillary growth, minimal nuclear and architectural atypia, and rounded and pushing deep borders.

Staging

Staging is based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system. Most cases are categorized as stage I.

Prognosis and prediction

Verrucous carcinoma has classically been considered a clinically indolent tumour. Most cases have been reported to have an excellent clinical prognosis. Rarely, it may be locally aggressive; extravesical extension and transformation to invasive squamous cell carcinoma have been reported {7815602; 8351470}.
