8.0.1.6 Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma

Definition

Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma is an invasive tumour characterized by glands with cribriform, papillary, and solid patterns of mucin-producing cells.

ICD-O coding

8470/3 Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C8Z & XH1390 Malignant neoplasm of male genital organs, unspecified & Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Testis and paratestis

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a painful or painless enlargement of the testis, occasionally with erectile dysfunction or ulceration {22550498; 21396680}.

Epidemiology

Primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the testicular adnexa is very rare. The few reported cases have been in middle-aged or elderly men (age range: 42–81 years) [[Rao NB, Sudhakar G, Swathi VR, et al. Mucinous adenocarcinoma of testis-a rare case report. J Evol Med Dent Sci. 2015 Sep 17;4(75):13144–8. doi:10.14260/jemds/2015/1892.]] [[Di Franco C, Porru D, Viglio A, et al. Primary paratesticular mucinous “ovarian-type” adenocarcinoma: a rare case of scrotal tumor in a patient with history of bilateral cryptorchidism. World J Nephrol Urol. 2015 Dec;4(4):260–3. doi:10.14740/wjnu235w.]].

Etiology

The histogenesis is debated. Hypotheses are Müllerian metaplasia of the tunica vaginalis or a failure in the total regression of Müllerian rests.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma is typically unilateral. There are occasional case reports describing bilateral tumours {1456884}. Macroscopically the tumour is a solid or partially cystic mass with a mucoid cut surface {22550498}. Foci of haemorrhage and necrosis can be present {15875745}. The reported cases measure 10–90 mm {12960806; 15875745}.

Histopathology

Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma is an invasive tumour composed of cuboidal to columnar cells with mild to severe cytological atypia, forming back-to-back cribriform glands and papillary structures {32028839}. Invasion measures ≥ 5 mm in linear extent. Goblet cells can be identified. Pools of mucin containing atypical epithelial cells can be present. Carcinoma can be mixed with background borderline and benign areas {15875745; 21396680}. Unlike ovarian mucinous carcinomas, testicular and paratesticular mucinous cystadenocarcinomas are typically positive for CK20, CDX2, and MUC2, and they show various degrees of CK7 positivity, in keeping with an intestinal phenotype {15875745; 21396680; 32028839}.

Primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the testicular adnexa should be distinguished from metastatic adenocarcinoma, mesothelioma, and somatic malignancy in a germ cell tumour. Metastatic adenocarcinoma to the testis from a gastrointestinal primary tumour shows a similar immunophenotype to that of a primary testicular tumour {18769334}. Consequently, appropriate radiological and/or endoscopic investigations may be necessary to differentiate a primary tumour from a metastasis. Mesothelioma arising from the tunica vaginalis can also show mucinous differentiation and can be distinguished from primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma by its expression of mesothelial markers such as CK5/6, calretinin, and WT1 {29577057}. Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma can also arise from somatic elements of a germ cell tumour {30820020}. Differentiating these tumours from primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the testicular adnexa requires exclusion of germ cell neoplasia in situ or exclusion of 12p gain (typically seen in postpubertal teratoma) by molecular testing. Negative serum germ cell tumour markers and the fact that testicular germ cell tumours tend to occur at a younger age may also help to distinguish these tumours.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an invasive mucinous carcinoma with atypia and architectural complexity; invasion measuring ≥ 5 mm in linear extent; no invasive or intratubular germ cell neoplasia identified in the background testis.

Staging

There is no American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for these tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Mucinous cystadenocarcinomas can show distant metastatic potential, with metastatic disease having a poor prognosis {22550498; 12960806}.
