8.0.1.3 Serous cystadenocarcinoma

Definition

Serous cystadenocarcinoma is an invasive epithelial neoplasm with serous differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8441/3 Serous cystadenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C80.Y & XH7A08 Other specified malignant neoplasms of testis & Serous carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Paratestis or testis

Clinical features

The presenting manifestation is testicular enlargement due to a paratesticular mass or a lesion located deeper in the testicular parenchyma.

Epidemiology

These are uncommon tumours that occur over a wide age range, including in children (range: 7–67 years) {28579069}.

Etiology

The origin is speculative; the tumours are hypothesized to originate from Müllerian metaplasia of the tunica vaginalis or from Müllerian rests in the paratesticular soft tissue, appendix testis, and scrotum {15663695}. Müllerian rests, which also exist in the female genital tract, may provide the cells of origin for low-grade serous neoplasia in both sexes {25491441}; this may also explain the paucity or absence in the testis of high-grade serous carcinoma arising from tubal-type epithelium [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Female genital tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 4). https://publications.iarc.fr/592.]].

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Serous cystadenocarcinomas are solid or cystic with excrescences. The colour is variable (greyish white, yellow, or tan) {7503358; 32619438}. Tumour diameter ranges from 15 to 115 mm.

Histopathology

Serous cystadenocarcinoma shows infiltrating and well-formed papillary, tubular, or cribriform structures lined by a serous-type epithelium. The tumour cells lie within a characteristic desmoplastic stroma with associated psammoma bodies. Unlike in the ovary, where high-grade tumours represent the most common serous types, in the testis the majority are consistent with low-grade serous carcinoma {25491441} and the surrounding areas may contain remnants of a serous borderline tumour {32619438; 15842644; 20133965}. Low-grade serous carcinoma of the testis shows occasional prominent nucleoli {28579069} and low-grade morphology {28543997; 33102004}. Uncommon cases with high-grade atypia have been also reported {25308020; 28764180}.

Immunohistochemistry is positive for PAX8, AE1/AE3, CK8/18, CK7, ER, EMA, WT1, BerEP4, CA125, and CK5/6. Immunohistochemistry is negative for CK20, inhibin, calretinin, PLAP, CD30, OCT4, and AFP {28579069; 28543997; 33102004}.

Mesothelioma, adenocarcinoma of the rete testis, and adenocarcinoma of the epididymis should be considered in the differential diagnosis.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an invasive serous tumour.

Desirable: positive immunoreactivity for PAX8 and negativity for calretinin.

Staging

There is no American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for these tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

The majority of reported serous cystadenocarcinomas are low-grade {25491441}; however, these tumours can metastasize. The prognosis of patients with metastatic disease is unfavourable {7503358; 28764180}. There have been no studies such as those for the ovarian counterpart that could link a more aggressive clinical course with morphological and genetic findings {25491441}.
