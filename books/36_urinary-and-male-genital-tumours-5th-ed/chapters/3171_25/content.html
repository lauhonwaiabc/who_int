3.1.7.1 TFE3-rearranged renal cell carcinomas

Definition

TFE3-rearranged renal cell carcinomas (RCCs) harbour gene fusions involving TFE3 with one of many different partner genes.

ICD-O coding

8311/3 TFE3-rearranged renal cell carcinomas

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis

Related terminology

Acceptable: Xp11 translocation renal cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Kidney

Clinical features

The clinical presentation of a TFE3-rearranged RCC as a renal mass is not specific, although occasional cases are heavily calcified on imaging, which can mimic renal lithiasis {17667536}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 40% of paediatric RCCs and 1.6–4% of adult RCCs are TFE3-rearranged RCC {19228722}.

Etiology

Prior exposure to cytotoxic chemotherapy is a risk factor for MiT family translocation RCC {16575003}.

Pathogenesis

Overexpressed TFE3 fusion proteins act as aberrant transcription factors that activate the expression of multiple downstream targets, including those normally activated by the related MiT family transcription factor MITF, such as melanocytic markers or cathepsin K {12837690; 17283122; 23288701}.

Macroscopic appearance

TFE3-rearranged RCCs do not have a distinctive gross appearance.

Histopathology

The usual histological pattern of the TFE3-rearranged RCC is that of a papillary neoplasm composed of epithelioid clear cells with abundant psammoma bodies {11438465; 12459622}. TFE3-rearranged RCCs can also mimic other renal neoplasms, including clear cell RCC, papillary RCC, clear cell papillary renal cell tumour, multilocular cystic renal cell neoplasm of low malignant potential, oncocytoma, and epithelioid angiomyolipoma {17667536; 23715164; 28840857}. Occasional TFE3-rearranged RCCs contain melanin pigment, creating an overlap with TFE3-rearranged pigmented perivascular epithelioid cell tumours (PEComas) {19065101; 20871214}. Unlike typical PEComas, TFE3-rearranged PEComas typically affect young patients, they almost always have an exclusively epithelioid clear cell morphology, they have minimal to no immunoreactivity for muscle markers, and they do not demonstrate inactivation of TSC1 or TSC2 genes or a clinical association with tuberous sclerosis.

In contrast to most RCCs, TFE3-rearranged RCCs underexpress epithelial markers such as cytokeratins and EMA, but they do consistently express PAX8 {24618616; 20679884}. Occasional TFE3-rearranged RCCs express melanocytic markers (melan-A and HMB45) and approximately 60% label for cathepsin K {19396149; 21602817}. Strong nuclear TFE3 immunoreactivity using an antibody against the C-terminal portion of TFE3 is highly sensitive and specific for TFE3-rearranged RCCs {12766578}, but TFE3 break-apart FISH assays have generally proved to be more useful because they suffer less from fixation issues {23715164; 20421778; 28840857}. The key exceptions are TFE3-rearranged RCCs resulting from paracentric inversions involving Xp11. These are often challenging to detect by conventional TFE3 break-apart FISH, giving a false negative result. Such fusions include RBM10::TFE3, GRIPAP1::TFE3, RBMX::TFE3, and NONO::TFE3. The diagnosis can be suspected when a neoplasm demonstrates other typical features of TFE3-rearranged RCC (such as cathepsin K immunoreactivity and characteristic morphology) along with strong diffuse nuclear TFE3 labelling by immunohistochemistry. The diagnosis can then be confirmed by RNA sequencing, RT-PCR, or FISH using specialized fosmid break-apart probes {28296677; 30622287; 31408245; 28106924}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

TFE3-rearranged RCCs harbour fusions of the TFE3 transcription factor gene (on chromosome Xp11.23) with one of multiple reported genes, including ASPSCR1 (ASPL), PRCC, NONO (P54NRB), SFPQ (PSF), RBM10, MED15, CLTC, DVL2, PARP14, KAT6A, NEAT1, MATR3, FUBP1, and EWSR1 {11438465; 12459622; 9393982; 26975036; 26032162; 30622287; 30552521; 29713041}. The three most common fusions identified in TFE3-rearranged RCCs involve PRCC, ASPSCR1 (the same gene fusion found in alveolar soft part sarcoma) {12917640}, and SFPQ. The SFPQ::TFE3 fusion is also commonly found in the TFE3-rearranged PEComas and melanotic TFE3-rearranged renal cancers {26274027; 26975036}. Overexpressed TFE3 fusion proteins act as aberrant transcription factors that activate the expression of multiple downstream targets, including those normally activated by the related MiT family transcription factor MITF (such as melanocytic markers and cathepsin K) {12837690; 17283122; 23288701}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: strong nuclear labelling for TFE3 by immunohistochemistry in a clean background, or TFE3 rearrangement identified by break-apart FISH, or TFE3 gene fusion identified by RNA sequencing.

Desirable: RCC with a mixed histological pattern, including clear cells, papillary architecture, and psammoma bodies.

Staging

TFE3-rearranged RCCs are staged using guidelines for adult RCC.

Prognosis and prediction

The survival of patients with Xp11 translocation RCCs is similar to that of patients with clear cell RCCs and significantly worse than that of patients with papillary RCCs {22498819}. In a multivariate analysis, only distant metastasis and older age at diagnosis independently predicted death {24309327}. Paediatric patients have a lower burden of genomic events than adult patients, which may explain their better prognosis {32220885}.
