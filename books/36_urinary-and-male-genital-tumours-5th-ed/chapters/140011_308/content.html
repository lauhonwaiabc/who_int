14.0.0.11 Hereditary tumour syndromes associated with homologous recombination pathway mutations

Definition

These are cancer syndromes associated with germline mutations of genes coding for homologous recombination (HR) pathways, including BRCA1, BRCA2, CHEK2, and ATM.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

None

ICD-11 coding

2C65 Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary homologous recombination cancer syndromes.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Germline mutations of HR pathway genes are associated with an increased risk of various cancer types: BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations are associated with cancers of the breast, ovary, pancreas, prostate, and urothelium, as well as with melanoma; CHEK2 mutations with cancers of the breast, ovary, colon, thyroid {26884562}, prostate, and urothelium; and ATM mutations with cancers of the breast (heterozygous), ovary {30128536}, digestive tract {23561644; 22585167}, and prostate.

Clinical features

The clinical features are dependent on the presentation of the tumour; there are no highly specific phenotypes, although HR-deficient tumours may behave more aggressively. Mutations in HR pathway genes, either germline or somatic, are enriched in men with more aggressive or metastatic prostate cancer {27169997; 27433846} and in patients with high-risk urothelial carcinoma {31844177}.

Epidemiology

The HR pathway underlies the ability of cells to repair double-stranded DNA breaks by harnessing a group of proteins derived from genes including BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, RAD51, ATM, and CHEK2. Pathogenic mutations in HR genes lead to a state of HR deficiency and are linked with hereditary cancer syndromes, such as the hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome. Prostate cancer has also been observed to aggregate with other types of familial cancer, in particular with breast and ovarian cancers {15948149}.

A family history of prostate cancer, especially in a first-degree relative, has been associated with a twofold to threefold increased risk of prostate cancer {22073129}, which further increases with the number of affected relatives. This risk is higher in those with affected brothers (rather than fathers and/or sons), and inversely related to age at diagnosis among affected relatives {11895872}. More specifically, an increased risk of prostate cancer has been identified in association with mutations in HR pathway genes including BRCA2 {16141007}, ATM {28657667}, CHEK2 {12533788}, and BRCA1 {22516946}, among others.

The Cancer Genome Atlas (TGCA) project described seven subsets of prostate cancer defined by assessing the molecular features of 333 cases of primary prostate cancer {26544944}. One distinct group was related to DNA repair pathways, which were altered in 19% of patients; most of these had defects in the HR repair pathway, including BRCA1 (1%), BRCA2 (3%), and ATM (4%). The frequency of HR defects is greater in advanced prostate cancer than in localized prostate cancer. In advanced disease, assessment of the germline mutation landscape of DNA damage repair genes in 692 men with metastatic prostate cancer identified high overall incidences of germline alterations (11.8%), including (but not limited to) those in BRCA2 (44%), ATM (13%), CHEK2 (12%), and BRCA1 (7%) {27433846}. This incidence is significantly higher than that among men with localized prostate cancer, where BRCA2 mutation has been identified in 1.2–1.8% {20215531}. BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations also increase the risk of male breast cancer. In high-risk families, BRCA2 mutations account for 60–76% of male breast cancers and BRCA1 mutations for 10–16% {24131976}.

CHEK2 is an HR pathway gene of moderate penetrance, playing a role in the regulation of TP53 and linked to a wildtype TP53 Li–Fraumeni syndrome. Dong et al. identified a prevalence of 4.8% for CHEK2 germline mutations in patients with prostate cancer, compared with 1.4% in an unaffected population, suggesting that CHEK2 mutations are associated with an increased risk of prostate cancer. The most common mutation in CHEK2, c.1100delC (p.T367fs), had a significantly higher carrier rate (1.28%) in patients of European American origin with lethal prostate cancer than in their counterparts with low-risk prostate cancer (0.16%) {29520813}. CHEK2 mutation prevalence has been estimated at 1–2.3% in urothelial cancer {31844177; 31794323}. A case–control study supported an association between the presence of variants in CHEK2 and the risk of bladder cancer, because founder CHEK2 variants were detected significantly more in patients with urothelial cancer (10.6%) than in control participants (5.9%). Breast cancers resulting from an underlying CHEK2 germline mutation account for 4–9% of all breast cancer cases in men {28008555; 11967536; 15122511}.

Homozygous germline mutations in the ATM gene result in ataxia telangiectasia, characterized by a variety of manifestations including an increased risk for several cancers. Heterozygous individuals have an increased risk of malignancies including breast cancer {1961222; 1548942}, ovarian cancer {30128536}, digestive system cancers {23561644; 22585167}, early onset prostate cancer {29659569}, and metastatic prostate cancer {27433846}.

Etiology

HR-deficient tumours are caused by germline mutations in the BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, and CHEK2 genes.

Pathogenesis

HR operates during the S and G2 phases of the cell cycle and involves proteins including BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, and CHK2. Cells with non-functioning HR due to pathogenic germline or acquired mutations in HR genes rely on other repair pathways that are less precise and more error-prone, like non-homologous end-joining {17088286}, leading to the accumulation of additional mutations and chromosomal instability, and increasing the risk of malignant transformation.

Macroscopic appearance

Distinctive macroscopic appearances have not been described for HR-deficient tumours.

Histopathology

Prostate cancer cases associated with BRCA1 and BRCA2 alterations more often have a higher grade (Gleason score ≥ 8), higher stage, and greater incidence of lymph node metastases than sporadic prostate cancer cases {23569316}. Although small series have suggested that tumours in men with germline HR mutations are more likely than sporadic tumours to display ductal or intraductal histology {29368341; 31123724}, recently reported studies with more robust control groups matched for prostate cancer grade did not find statistically significant differences in histology.

Cytology

The cytology is not known to be at variance with that of sporadic HR-deficient urological cancers.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Germline BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, and CHEK2 mutations are most often identified by next-generation sequencing performed on patient blood samples. Increasingly, tumour samples are sequenced for mutations in HR-associated genes. The finding of an HR mutation in tumour tissue may indicate either germline or somatic origin, necessitating genetic counselling and consideration of germline assessment.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pathogenic or probably pathogenic germline mutations in BRCA1, BRCA2, CHEK2, or ATM in the appropriate clinical scenario.

Staging

Staging for cancers associated with HR pathway mutation is identical to that for sporadic tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Tumour HR pathway mutations, whether germline or somatic, have therapeutic relevance as predictors of favourable response to poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibition {26510020} and platinum-based chemotherapy {26724258; 28608931}.
