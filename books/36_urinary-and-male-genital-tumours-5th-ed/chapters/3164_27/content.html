3.1.6.4 Renal cell carcinoma NOS

Definition

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC)-NOS is not a distinct subtype of RCC but a tumour that cannot be readily classified into one of the known RCC subtypes.

ICD-O coding

8312/3 Renal cell carcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C90.0 & XH05V6 Renal cell carcinoma of kidney, except renal pelvis & Renal cell carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: renal cell carcinoma not elsewhere classified; renal cell carcinoma, unclassified.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Any location

Clinical features

Clinical findings for RCC-NOS are similar to those for other renal tumours. These tumours tend to come to clinical attention at an advanced stage {20223503}.

Epidemiology

RCC-NOS accounts for < 5% of RCCs. As new subtypes of RCC are reported, RCC-NOS accounts for a decreasing proportion of RCCs {28898443; 33239504}.

Etiology

There is no known or shared etiology.

Pathogenesis

There are no characteristic unifying pathogenic features. Mutations in NF2, SETD2, BAP1, and MTOR are found in some tumours {27713405}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours are typically high-grade, large, and with local spread {20223503; 28833389; 17822461; 22404824}. Entirely sarcomatoid tumours have been placed in this group, and these tumours share features with other subtypes with sarcomatoid differentiation.

Histopathology

RCC-NOS tumours are renal tumours with features that do not readily allow classification into an established RCC subtype. Invasive urothelial carcinoma and metastatic carcinoma from other sites must be ruled out before this diagnosis is made. Some tumours may be entirely sarcomatoid. Tumours with pure sarcomatoid morphology and no recognizable epithelial component could be placed in this category, but extensive sampling is necessary to exclude a recognized subtype. Sarcomatoid carcinoma is not recognized as a separate entity. As a result of the inclusion of unclassified high-grade and sarcomatoid tumours, necrosis, lymphatic and vascular invasion, and local spread are common. Historically, RCC-NOS has included low-grade and high-grade oncocytic neoplasms, but many of these tumours can now be classified as SDHB-deficient RCC and eosinophilic solid and cystic RCC. This fifth-edition WHO classification of renal tumours has introduced a grouping called “other oncocytic tumours of the kidney” for low-grade oncocytic tumours and vacuolated oncocytic tumours {28898443; 28833389}. These low-grade and indolent oncocytic tumours should not be placed into the RCC-NOS group because RCC-NOS tumours are typically very aggressive {32619439}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no diagnostic molecular pathological features, but other RCC subtypes with specific molecular pathological features should be excluded {33239504}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: there are no defined diagnostic criteria for RCC-NOS; the tumour should not possess diagnostic features of other renal epithelial neoplasms or metastases.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth-edition Union of International Cancer Control (UICC) system, as appropriate.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours tend to have an aggressive clinical course, often being high-grade and high-stage, and entirely sarcomatoid tumours may be included. Overall, the prognosis remains related to the pathological features associated with outcome in other RCC subtypes, such as stage, grade, and sarcomatoid component {22404824}.
