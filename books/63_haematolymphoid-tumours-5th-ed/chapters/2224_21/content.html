2.2.2.4 Mast cell sarcoma

Definition

Mast cell sarcoma (MCS) is a rare and clinically aggressive form of mastocytosis characterized by the presence of a locally destructive solid tumour comprising highly atypical mast cells.

ICD-O coding

9740/3 Mast cell sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2A21.2 Mast cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant mast cell tumour; malignant mastocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Any organ or system may be involved, although the bones and gastrointestinal tract are most often affected, followed by the lymph nodes, skin, spleen, and liver {27602777}.

Clinical features

MCS can present de novo (classic MCS), or as a transformation of a previous mast cell neoplasm (MCS-like progression) {18055976; 22498828; 22661384}. More rarely, the disease is diagnosed concomitantly with systemic involvement by neoplastic mast cells, often fulfilling criteria for mast cell leukaemia {32242922}.

The clinical presentation varies depending on the location of the mass. As many as 30% of the patients present with symptoms of mast cell activation syndrome, including flushing, fever, malaise, diarrhoea, and tachycardia {27602777}.

Epidemiology

MCS is exceedingly rare. Most patients are adults, but children can also be affected. The median age of adults with MCS is 55 years (range: 1–77 years), and there is a slight female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:1.3) {27602777}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. MCS is almost exclusively sporadic, and only one case of MCS diagnosed in the context of familial cutaneous mastocytosis has been reported {23129735}.

Pathogenesis

KIT mutations are rarely demonstrated in MCS, as compared with systemic mastocytosis. KIT is wildtype in 50% of the reported cases and the canonical KIT p.D816V mutation is found in only 21% of patients. Other KIT mutations have been described in single cases {32242922; 23196796}. The paucity of KIT p.D816V mutations suggests that MCS is molecularly distinct from systemic mastocytosis.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

MCS manifests as a localized tumour of immature and often highly atypical cells showing an infiltrative and destructive growth pattern {3088063; 10430280; 12826896}. An increase in eosinophils is frequently observed. Neoplastic cells can only be appreciated as mast cells when special stains, including immunohistochemistry for mast cell tryptase and KIT (CD117), are applied. Otherwise, MCS may be misdiagnosed as undifferentiated, pleomorphic, or round cell soft tissue sarcoma, or even carcinoma {24141335; 27602777; 32242922}. Variable expression of CD2, CD25, and CD30 has been reported {32242922}. Neoplastic mast cells may also express CD68, CD45, CD43, and other nonspecific antigens, possibly causing differential diagnostic problems {23196796; 27602777; 32242922}.

MCS needs to be differentiated from extracutaneous mastocytoma, an extremely rare localized and benign tumour consisting of mature mast cells without histopathological evidence of invasive growth or atypia. Most reported cases of extracutaneous mastocytoma have been in the lung {14278896; 4162687; 2452007; 26591626; 34233110}.

Cytology

Given the localized nature of MCS and the sites involved, cytology of the primary lesion is not usually obtained. In cases with bone marrow infiltration, the cytological features are not specific and share features with other subtypes of mastocytosis {27602777}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No systematic molecular analyses have been performed in MCS. Whole KIT sequencing is recommended, given the low frequency of MCSs harbouring the typical imatinib-resistant KIT p.D816V mutation {32242922}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a localized infiltrative lesion composed of highly atypical mast cells expressing KIT (CD117) and tryptase; criteria for systemic mastocytosis are not fulfilled in cases of classic MCS.

Desirable: immunohistochemical expression of CD2, CD25, and/or CD30.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for patients with MCS is dismal. MCS usually shows locally destructive growth and progression to aggressive systemic mastocytosis or mast cell leukaemia in a short time {3088063; 12826896; 23129735; 27602777}. Life expectancy from diagnosis ranges from a few months to several years. The terminal phase of MCS is often indistinguishable from mast cell leukaemia {32242922}.
