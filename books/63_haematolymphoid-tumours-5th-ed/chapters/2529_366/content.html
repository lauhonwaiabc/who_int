2.5.2.9 Acute undifferentiated leukaemia

Definition

Acute undifferentiated leukaemia (AUL) is an acute leukaemia that expresses haematopoietic stem cell markers but no markers that permit lineage assignment.

ICD-O coding

9801/3 Acute undifferentiated leukaemia

ICD-11 coding

2B33.0 Acute leukaemia, not elsewhere classified

Related terminology

Not recommended: stem cell acute leukaemia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved.

Clinical features

Patients present with clinical features similar to those of other acute leukaemias, typically secondary to cytopenias {29616841; 31000771; 34540694}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of AUL has decreased over time {31982153}, probably owing to improved classification based on molecular findings and techniques for detecting lineage markers. AUL is estimated to account for 1–2% of acute leukaemias {29616841; 31982153}; it occurs more commonly in adults, with an M:F ratio of about 1.5:1 {29616841; 30209392; 31817495}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Approximately 40% of cases have an abnormal karyotype (often a non-complex karyotype). Recurrent mutations include those in PHF6, RUNX1, SRSF2, and ASXL1 (each ~40%), and in BCOR (~25%) {29616841; 30209392; 31000771; 34540694}. Gene fusions such as SET::NUP214 are also detected in a subset of cases {30209392}. The molecular profile in AUL with RUNX1 and ASXL1 mutations suggests that AUL may be genetically closer to acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) than to lymphoblastic leukaemia, whereas PHF6 mutation supports the notion that AUL may represent a biological continuum of T/myeloid mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia and early T-precursor lymphoblastic leukaemia {30209392; 30530780}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Blasts in the blood and bone marrow are usually immature and have no morphological features of differentiation.

Immunophenotype

Blasts in AUL express no lineage-specific markers and no more than one marker for any given lineage. By definition, they lack cCD3, myeloperoxidase, B-cell markers (e.g. cCD22, CD79a, or strong CD19), and markers of other unusual lineages (including NK cells, megakaryocytes, basophils, or plasmacytoid dendritic cells). Typically, blasts express one myeloid-associated marker (e.g. CD13, CD33, or KIT [CD117]), with two thirds of cases expressing one myeloid marker and one third of cases expressing no myeloid markers. CD34, HLA-DR, and TdT are frequently expressed, whereas CD7 and CD56 are uncommonly expressed {29616841; 31000771; 34540694}.

Cytochemistry

Blasts are negative for myeloperoxidase and Sudan Black B by definition. Very rarely, blasts may show a positive reaction with nonspecific esterase {31000771}.

Differential diagnosis

AUL must be distinguished from AML with minimal differentiation in which ≥ 2 myeloid-associated markers (e.g. CD13, CD33, and CD117) are expressed. AUL must also be distinguished from, rare mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL) subtypes and non-haematopoietic malignancies.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetic analysis and molecular assessment are needed to identify the genetic abnormalities, such as mutations (e.g. PHF6, RUNX1, and SRSF2) and fusion transcripts (e.g. SET::NUP214).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood, lacking expression of immunophenotypic lineage-specific markers; blasts do not express more than one lineage-associated marker of any given lineage; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with defined genetic alterations or rare mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia subtypes.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical management of AUL remains a challenge because of its rarity and lack of therapeutic guidelines. Overall survival has improved with contemporary treatment and differs between paediatric and adult populations {31982153}.
