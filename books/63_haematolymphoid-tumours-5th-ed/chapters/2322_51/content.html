2.3.2.2 Acute myeloid leukaemia with minimal differentiation

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with minimal differentiation is an acute leukaemia with immunophenotypic but not morphological or cytochemical evidence of myeloid differentiation that lacks defining genetic abnormalities.

ICD-O coding

9872/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with minimal differentiation

ICD-11 coding

2A60.30 Acute myeloid leukaemia with minimal differentiation

Related terminology

Not recommended: acute myeloid leukaemia, M0.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The blood and bone marrow are always involved.

Clinical features

Patients present with clinical features similar to those of other acute leukaemias, typically secondary to cytopenias {10516750; 17158236; 21397944; 22936064; 31000771}.

Epidemiology

AML with minimal differentiation accounts for < 5% of AML cases. Children and adults may be affected, and the median age in the latter is 50–60 years. There is a slight male predominance (M:F ratio: ~1.5:1) {10516750; 10721774; 17158236; 21397944; 22936064; 31000771}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Recent evidence points to rearrangements of BCL11B on chromosome 14q32 imparting a pathogenetic continuum across AML with minimal differentiation, T/myeloid mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, early T-precursor lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, and acute undifferentiated leukaemia {31817495; 33876209; 34103329}. Such rearrangements are present in about 30% of cases of AML with minimal differentiation. Resultant fusions include ZEB2::BCL11B, CCDC26::BCL11B, ARID1B::BCL11B, and CDK6::BCL11B {26186352; 33876209; 34103329}. Dysregulation of BCL11B is associated frequently with cooperative mutations in FLT3 (~85%, most as FLT3 internal tandem duplication [ITD]) and mutations involving epigenetic modulators, most frequently DNMT3A, TET2, and WT1 {33876209; 34778799}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The bone marrow is usually markedly hypercellular with sheets of poorly differentiated blasts. The blasts lack features of myeloid differentiation and are usually medium-sized with dispersed nuclear chromatin and round or slightly indented nuclei with one or two nucleoli. The cytoplasm lacks granules or Auer rods. Less frequently, the blasts are small to medium-sized, resembling lymphoblasts.

Immunophenotype

The blasts express at least two myeloid-associated markers, most often CD13, CD33, and CD117. Most cases also express CD34, CD38, CD123, and HLA-DR. CD7 and TdT are expressed in about 30% of cases {22936064}. Myeloperoxidase is negative. Markers of specific lineages, including granulocytic (e.g. CD15, CD65), monocytic (e.g. CD11c, CD14, CD36, and CD64), and B-cell (e.g. CD19), are often absent {22936064; 31000771}.

Cytochemistry

Blasts are negative (< 3%) for myeloperoxidase, Sudan Black B, and naphthol AS-D CAE.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Approximately 40% of cases have an abnormal karyotype, including trisomies (e.g. trisomy 13) and other structural abnormalities {11380465; 15527902; 22936064; 31000771}. Frequent mutations are detected in RUNX1 (~30%), ASXL1 (~30%), DNMT3A (~20%), IDH1/IDH2, FLT3 ITD (~20%), STAG2 (10%), and spliceosome genes (~30%, including SRSF2 ~20%) {12393381; 19773259; 22936064; 25022553; 27137476; 27285584; 29961066; 31000771}. The clinical relevance of detecting BCL11B rearrangement in cases of AML with minimal differentiation is not clear at this time {34865701}. It should be noted that some of the aforementioned mutations now qualify for myelodysplasia-related AML (see Acute myeloid leukaemia, myelodysplasia-related).

 

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood, lacking morphological and cytochemical evidence of myeloid differentiation; positive for at least two myeloid-associated immunophenotypic markers (e.g. CD13, CD33, CD117); not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for AML types with defined genetic alterations; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

There are limited data specifically on prognosis and prediction in patients classified as having AML with minimal differentiation using the current criteria.
