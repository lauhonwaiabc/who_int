2.2.3.2 Myelodysplastic neoplasm with low blasts and 5q deletion

Definition

Myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS) with low blasts and 5q deletion (MDS-5q) is a myeloid neoplasm with cytopenia and dysplasia, characterized by chromosome 5q deletion occurring in isolation or with one additional cytogenetic abnormality other than monosomy 7 or 7q deletion.

ICD-O coding

9986/3 Myelodysplastic neoplasm with low blasts and 5q deletion

ICD-11 coding

2A36 Myelodysplastic syndrome with isolated del(5q)

Related terminology

Acceptable: myelodysplastic syndrome with del(5q).

Not recommended: 5q− syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood

Clinical features

Most patients present with clinical symptoms related to anaemia, which is often macrocytic and transfusion dependent. Thrombocytosis (platelet count ≥ 450 × 109/L) is present in one third of cases. Thrombocytopenia is uncommon {28324772} and reflects advanced disease.

Epidemiology

MDS-5q accounts for about 2.5% of all MDS cases and has an incidence of about 0.1 cases per 100 000 person-years {21708407}. Patients have a median age of 67 years, and women are more frequently affected than men.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Cases with a history of benzene exposure {10706450} or previous radiotherapy or chemotherapy {1868912; 17574959} have been reported.

Pathogenesis

The presumed pathogenesis is a loss of genes in the commonly deleted region (CDR) of approximately 1.5 Mb at 5q32-q33.1, flanked by D5S413 and the gene GLRA1.

The erythroid phenotype involves haploinsufficiency of the gene RPS14, which encodes a structural protein of the small ribosome subunit, through MDM2-driven p53 pathway activation {19966810; 18202658; 19287375; 21068437; 26878232}. Erythroid lineage sensitivity to a decreased ribosome pool {18202658; 19966810; 32327500} results in translation selectivity at the expense of erythroid genes, including the gene encoding the master erythroid transcription factor GATA1 {29551269; 32327500}.

Innate immune signalling and NF-κB activation may involve the combined haploinsufficiency of miR-146a and TIFAB, resulting in increased expression of the toll-like receptor mediator TRAF6 and activation of TLR4 {19898489; 26458771}. The high production of calprotectin (the S100A8/S100A9 heterodimer) by innate immune cells participates in the erythroid defect {26878232}.

Additional loss of RIOK2 at 5q15 may contribute to anaemia through p53-dependent T-cell overproduction of IL-22 {33753942}.

Haploinsufficiency of miR-145 and miR-146a contributes to thrombocytosis and activation of innate immune signalling in the bone marrow {19898489; 28024152}.

Clonal dominance is driven by haploinsufficiency of CSNK1A1 (encoding casein kinase 1α) through the deregulation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway {25242043}. Haploinsufficiency of additional genes outside the CDR in the more centromeric portion of 5q31, such as APC (another WNT pathway regulator) and EGR1, or inactivation of CTNNA1 by deletion and hypermethylation of the remaining allele, may also play a role {17420284; 17159988}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The bone marrow is usually normocellular or hypercellular, and it frequently exhibits erythroid hypoplasia. Megakaryocytes are usually increased in number and are normal to decreased in size, with conspicuously non-lobated and hypolobated nuclei. In contrast, dysplasia in the erythroid and granulocytic lineages is less pronounced. Significant granulocytic dysplasia is uncommon. Blasts constitute < 5% of bone marrow cells.

Ring sideroblasts (or SF3B1 mutation) may be present and do not exclude the diagnosis of MDS-5q, provided the other criteria are fulfilled. Bone marrow fibrosis is typically absent.

Assessment of p53 expression pattern by immunohistochemistry may be helpful to identify high-risk disease in patients with MDS-5q (see below). Strong p53 expression in ≥ 1% of bone marrow cells has been shown to be significantly associated with a higher risk of transformation to acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) and a shorter overall survival {24682512}.

Cytology

See Histopathology, above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The defining cytogenetic abnormality involves an interstitial deletion of chromosome 5. The size of the deletion and the breakpoints vary, but bands q31-q33 are invariably deleted. Compared with other MDS types, MDS-5q cases more often contain only one driver event {32347921}. Patients with a single additional clonal chromosomal abnormality have similar features and outcomes to those with MDS-5q, provided the abnormality is not monosomy 7 or 7q deletion {31558664}.

MDS-5q with SF3B1 mutation (20% of the cases) is included in this category, because the SF3B1 mutation is probably a secondary event in this context {24835589}. A minority of patients with 5q deletion have concomitant mutations in JAK2p.V617F or MPL p.W515L, which do not appear to alter the disease phenotype or prognosis {20485371}; in some of these cases, the JAK2 mutation and 5q deletion have been found in different clones {19819015}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: anaemia, with or without other cytopenias and/or thrombocytosis; dysplasia involving megakaryocytes, with or without dysplasia involving other lineages; blasts constitute < 5% of cells in the bone marrow and < 2% in the peripheral blood; detection of 5q deletion, isolated or with one additional cytogenetic aberration other than monosomy 7 or 7q deletion; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for MDS with biallelic TP53 inactivation, MDS with increased blasts, or myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The immunomodulatory drug lenalidomide benefits patients with MDS-5q by targeting casein kinase 1A for ubiquitin-mediated degradation. Transfusion independence is achieved in two thirds of patients and is closely linked to suppression of the abnormal clone {21753188; 20825315}.

The defining cytogenetic criteria for MDS-5q are in the “good” prognosis cytogenetic subgroup in the Revised International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS-R), and most patients are stratified as low-risk or intermediate-1-risk {22740453}. Male sex, older age, transfusion need, low absolute neutrophil count, and low platelet count are associated with a worse prognosis {22289990; 25960152}.

TP53 mutation, detected at diagnosis in as many as 18% of patients, is associated with a decreased response to lenalidomide and an increased risk of transformation to AML {21519010; 27133825}. Mutations detected in patients who developed AML transformation have included TP53, RUNX1, and TET2 {27884971}. SF3B1 mutations may also predict worse outcomes in MDS-5q {32347921; 28642303}.
