2.2.5.2 Childhood myelodysplastic neoplasm with increased blasts

Definition

Childhood myelodysplastic neoplasm (cMDS) with increased blasts (cMDS-IB) is a myeloid neoplasm with cytopenia and dysplasia arising in children and adolescents (≤ 18 years of age), defined by increased blasts (≥ 5% and < 20% in bone marrow and/or ≥ 2% and < 20% in peripheral blood).

ICD-O coding

9983/3 Childhood myelodysplastic neoplasm with increased blasts

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peripheral blood and bone marrow, with occasional extramedullary sites

Clinical features

The clinical symptoms of myelodysplastic neoplasms (MDSs) in children are similar to those in adults, typically reflecting the underlying cytopenia(s), including anaemia, infections, and bleeding tendency.

Epidemiology

cMDS-IB represents 10–25% of cMDS cases {34621053; 21554264}, with an annual incidence of 1–2 cases per 1 million children {10520007}. It affects more boys than girls {21212791}.

Etiology

Although the etiology is mostly unknown, some cases have preceding severe aplastic anaemia, bone marrow failure syndrome, or germline mutations predisposing to myeloid neoplasia, or they arise after exposure to chemotherapy or radiation {29146900; 26702063; 27876779; 28708320; 29146883; 30047423; 34597127}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenetic and genetic landscape of cMDS-IB is similar to that of cMDS with low blasts (cMDS-LB), and they both differ from that of MDS arising in adults. Acquired cytogenetic abnormalities and RAS pathway mutations are more common in cMDS-IB than in cMDS-LB {34621053; 33038986}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The cytological, histological, and immunophenotypic findings are largely similar to those described for cMDS-LB, except with increased numbers of blasts. Unlike in adult MDS, there are no data to support making a distinction between cases with 5–9% bone marrow blasts and/or 2–4% peripheral blood blasts and those with 10–19% bone marrow blasts and/or 5–19% peripheral blood blasts {22734597; 21212791}.

cMDS-IBs arising after exposure to cytotoxic therapy for unrelated conditions are classified as myeloid neoplasms post cytotoxic therapy (see section Myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy).

Cytology

See Histopathology, above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Germline and somatic genetic abnormalities described for cMDS-LB can also be found in cMDS-IB. However, acquired cytogenetic abnormalities are more common in the latter, with an increased frequency of 7q deletion and structurally complex karyotypes {34621053; 33038986; 18302710; 12763938}. Of note, a constitutive trisomy 21 is an exclusion criterion (see section Myeloid proliferations associated with Down syndrome).

Similarly, the mutation frequency is increased in cMDS-IB, with an average rate of 5 per case and ≥ 1 somatic mutation in approximately 50–70% cases {29146900; 27876779}. In addition to the mutations described in cMDS-LB, RAS pathway mutations are common in cMDS-IB and often involve PTPN11 {29146900; 27876779}. As with cMDS-LB, the mutation burden of cMDS-IB is lower than and different from that identified in adult MDS {27876779; 29146900}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cytopenia involving one or more lineages; dysplasia involving one or more lineages; ≥ 5% and < 20% blasts in the bone marrow and/or ≥ 2% and < 20% blasts in peripheral blood; exclusion of Down syndrome, juvenile myelomonocytic leukaemia, and acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with defining genetic abnormalities.

Desirable: detection of a clonal cytogenetic and/or molecular abnormality.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Patients are typically treated with haematopoietic stem cell transplantation, with an overall cure rate of 60% {21554264; 21212791}. Prognosis is dictated in part by cytogenetics, with complex karyotype having the worst prognosis {34621053}. Unlike in adult MDS, there is no evidence that the presence of ≥ 10% bone marrow blasts or ≥ 5% peripheral blood blasts denotes a worse prognosis {22734597; 21212791}. Other prognostic and clinically relevant variables include underlying germline genetic predisposition and availability of well-matched donors for stem cell transplant therapy {21554264; 29438960}.
