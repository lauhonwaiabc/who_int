2.3.1.6 Acute myeloid leukaemia with DEK::NUP214 fusion

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with DEK::NUP214 fusion is characterized by fusion of the proto-oncogene DEK and the nucleoporin 214 gene (NUP214).

ICD-O coding

9865/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with DEK::NUP214 fusion

ICD-11 coding

2A60.0 & XH9Y46 Acute myeloid leukaemia with recurrent genetic abnormalities & Acute myeloid leukaemia with t(6;9)(p23;q34); DEK-NUP214

Related terminology

Acceptable: acute myeloid leukaemia with t(6;9)(p22.3;q34.1).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Blood and bone marrow

Clinical features

Most patients present with anaemia and thrombocytopenia {16628187; 33558656}. Leukocytosis is common at presentation, but some patients, particularly those with features of myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS), may present with pancytopenia {16628187; 33558656}.

Epidemiology

AML with DEK::NUP214 is rare. It accounts for 0.6–1.7% of AML cases in children {24441146; 24661089} and about 1% of AML cases in adults {16628187; 27276561}. The median age at diagnosis is 10–13 years in children {16628187; 24441146; 24661089} and 35–51 years in adults {15362364; 33558656; 31004014}. There is no clear sex predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The mechanisms for leukaemogenesis induced by DEK::NUP214 are not fully elucidated, but the fusion appears to disrupt the localization of proteins essential for nucleocytoplasmic transport {27114368; 32934780}, probably by inhibiting NF-κB–mediated transcription {27114368}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The bone marrow is typically hypercellular. Bone marrow smears show increased blasts with maturation or with myelomonocytic differentiation {1586743; 24441146; 33558656}. In some patients the morphological features are those of MDS with increased blasts {1586743; 33558656}. Multilineage dysplasia is common {16628187; 24441146; 28318095; 33558656}, including abnormal nuclear lobulation in erythroblasts and myeloid cells, ring sideroblasts, hypogranular myelopoiesis, and micromegakaryocytes {15362364; 33558656; 9124211}. Auer rods are infrequent, and basophils may be increased in a minority of cases {1586743; 24441146; 33558656}.

Immunophenotype

Leukaemic cells are usually positive for CD13, CD33, CD34, CD38, KIT (CD117), CD123, HLA-DR, and myeloperoxidase {15362364; 16628187; 28318095; 33558656}. CD7, CD9, CD15, CD64, and TdT are variably expressed, and CD34 may be negative {9124211; 15362364; 16628187; 21156248; 28318095; 33558656}.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

This AML type is defined by karyotype-based evidence of t(6;9)(p22.3;q34.1) or molecular confirmation of in-frame fusion of DEK on chromosome 6p22.3 and NUP214 (CAN) on 9q34.1. The oncogenic fusion of 5′ DEK and 3′ NUP214 occurs on the derivative chromosome 6 {1549122}. The t(6;9) translocation is a driver alteration and often the sole karyotype finding {16628187; 27276561}. Less commonly, some cases have additional karyotypic abnormalities and occasionally a complex karyotype {15362364; 24441146}. Detection of the DEK::NUP214 fusion may be achieved with FISH and/or molecular diagnostic techniques. Concurrent FLT3 internal tandem duplication (ITD) mutation occurs in 50–88% of patients, whereas FLT3 tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) mutations are generally absent {15362364; 16628187; 24661089}. Mutations involving RAS pathway genes are common {33558656}.

 

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a myeloid neoplasm with increased peripheral blood and/or bone marrow blasts (may be < 20%); presence of DEK::NUP214 fusion; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy.

Desirable: detection of t(6;9)(p22.3;q34.1).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

AML with DEK::NUP214 is associated with a poor prognosis and short overall survival {16628187; 24441146; 33558656}. In a comparison of patients with DEK::NUP214 who were initially diagnosed with AML versus those diagnosed with myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS), there was no difference in overall survival, and 61% of patients with MDS developed AML. In a recent study, patients who did not undergo allogeneic stem cell transplantation died within 45 months, although the median survival time of patients with AML was shorter than that of patients with MDS (13 vs 26 months) {33558656}. Based on the high frequency of progression to AML, similar prognosis, and patterns of co-mutations, the presence of DEK::NUP214 is regarded as an AML-defining alteration irrespective of the blast count at presentation {24441146; 33558656}.

FLT3 ITD is present in a majority of cases and does not impact overall survival, but it has been associated with faster relapse kinetics {25605311}. Whether FLT3 inhibition is effective in this type of AML remains to be seen. Allogeneic stem cell transplantation seems to improve survival {21869835; 31004014; 33558656}.
