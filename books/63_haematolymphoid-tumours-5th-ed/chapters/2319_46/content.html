2.3.1.9 Acute myeloid leukaemia with NUP98 rearrangement

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with NUP98 rearrangement is characterized by chromosomal translocations involving NUP98 on chromosome 11p15.4 and various partner genes.

ICD-O coding

9861/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with NUP98 rearrangement

ICD-11 coding

2A60.0 Acute myeloid leukaemia with recurrent genetic abnormalities

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peripheral blood and bone marrow, occasionally with extramedullary sites

Clinical features

Patients present with anaemia and thrombocytopenia and usually show leukocytosis. Those with NUP98::KDM5A tend to have lower white blood cell counts {31681706; 32381579}.

Epidemiology

NUP98 rearrangements have been reported in 2–4.8% of childhood AML cases, with an age range of 10–15 years {21813447; 23630019; 25145343; 27694926; 27890935; 31467532; 31612466; 32381579}. Patients with NUP98::KDM5A have a median age of 3.2 years {25145343; 32381579}. NUP98::NSD1 has a male predilection {25145343; 23630019}. In adults, 2.3% of patients with AML have NUP98 rearrangement {21813447}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

More than 40 fusion partners of NUP98 have been reported (see #31740Box 2.24) {32766874}. Those fusion partners are categorized into HOX and non-HOX genes, of which NSD1, a non-HOX partner, is the most common, especially in paediatric patients. The NUP98 gene encodes a component of the nuclear pore complex. NUP98 fusion partners are typically placed at the C-terminal portion of the fusion gene. Fusion partners are categorized according to the presence or absence of a homeodomain moiety. These fusions leverage NUP98-associated functions along with transcriptional and epigenetic regulatory activities of the partner genes. Altered expression of HOXA- and HOXB-family genes, MEIS1, and several other genes has been reported {32766874}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Morphological findings vary considerably, but most cases have a high blast count in the bone marrow. Leukaemic blasts may or may not show maturation, which when present tends to be myelomonocytic or monocytic {11737860; 11986249; 20935257; 21813447; 22929522; 22945772; 23630019; 32381579}. Dysplastic changes may be seen. In as many as 34% of patients aged < 3 years, leukaemic blasts have megakaryoblastic differentiation {31612466; 32381579} and are seen especially in association with NUP98::KDM5A {23531517}. Acute leukaemias with erythroid differentiation in children are commonly associated with NUP98 rearrangement {33284945}.

Immunophenotype

Leukaemic blasts usually express stem cell and myeloid markers, including CD34, KIT (CD117), CD13, CD33, HLA-DR, and myeloperoxidase {23800796}. Other markers that may be expressed include CD123, CD11b, CD11c, CD14, CD36, CD7, CD15, and CD64 {11737860; 11986249; 17287853; 19665070; 33284945}. Megakaryocytic (CD41, CD42b, CD61) and erythroid (CD71, E-cadherin) markers usually correlate with morphological features.

Cytochemistry

The blasts are often positive for myeloperoxidase.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Rearrangements involving NUP98 are often cryptic on conventional karyotyping, owing to the terminal location of NUP98 on chromosome 11p15.4 {31467532; 27694926; 21813447}. Most patients actually have a normal karyotype, although trisomy 8 and chromosome 13 abnormalities may be observed {32766874}. FISH using NUP98 break-apart probes, RT-PCR for NUP98::NSD1, and RNA sequencing form the major diagnostic modalities.

Evaluation for NUP98 rearrangement has been proposed in AML cases with normal karyotype and FLT3 internal tandem duplication (ITD) mutation and/or WT1 mutation {31467532; 32766874}. Most cases with NUP98::NSD1 harbour FLT3 ITD at frequencies ranging from 67% to 91% {27694926}, and WT1 mutation is reported in 33–55% of cases {32766874}. Other recurrent alterations concurrent with NUP98 rearrangement include mutations in NBPF14, BCR, ODF1, NRAS, KRAS, MYC, KIT, ASXL1, and CEBPA {27694926; 32766874}. Loss of RB1 (at 13q14) is particularly associated with NUP98::KDM5A {32766874}.

Paediatric patients with acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia should be evaluated for NUP98 rearrangement, particularly NUP98::KDM5A {23531517; 31612466}. A NUP98::RARG fusion associated with morphological findings resembling acute promyelocytic leukaemia has been reported {20935257}. Rare cases of NUP98::HOXA9 and NUP98::DDX10 in the context of blast phase and imatinib resistance in chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML) have also been reported {10848835; 15721630}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a myeloid neoplasm with increased peripheral blood and/or bone marrow blasts (may be < 20%); detection of NUP98 rearrangement and/or specific fusion products such as NUP98::NSD1.

Desirable: identification of the NUP98 fusion partner at diagnosis is desirable to enable PCR-based disease monitoring.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

AML with NUP98 rearrangement is associated with a poor prognosis {32381579}. Patients who also have FLT3 ITD have much worse outcomes than those who do not {27890935; 32766874}. Among paediatric patients with refractory AML, as many as 50% have NUP98 rearrangement. Allogeneic stem cell transplantation should be considered for patients who achieve remission {30760869}.
