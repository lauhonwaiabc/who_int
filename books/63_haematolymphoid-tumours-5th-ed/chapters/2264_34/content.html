2.2.6.4 Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm NOS (unclassifiable)

Definition

Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neo­plasm (MDS/MPN) NOS is a myeloid neoplasm with dysplastic and proliferative features that does not meet the criteria for other defined MDS/MPN entities.

ICD-O coding

9975/3 Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm, NOS (unclassifiable)

 

ICD-11 coding

2A4Z Myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative neoplasms, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm, unclassifiable.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved. The spleen, liver, and other extramedullary tissues may also be involved.

Clinical features

The clinical features of MDS/MPN-NOS are not specific, but the simultaneous existence of dysplasia and proliferation is mandatory.

Clinical signs can include myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN)-like symptoms, such as weight loss, night sweats, organomegaly (most often splenomegaly), and thromboembolic complications, and myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS)-like symptoms of ineffective haematopoiesis, including fatigue, dyspnoea, infections, and bleeding, sometimes with transfusion-dependent anaemia {10492067; 8947580; 15217205; 24627528; 31511613}.

Epidemiology

MDS/MPN-NOS is estimated to be a rare entity that represents a few minor subset of all MDS/MPN cases, but the incidence of this heterogeneous disease is difficult to assess. Given the rarity of MDS/MPN with neutrophilia and MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis, MDS/MPN-NOS may be the second most common diagnosis in the category of MDS/MPN. The median age at diagnosis is about 70 years, and the majority (> 60%) of patients are male {34869027}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The accumulation of nonspecific somatic genetic events in a haematopoietic stem or progenitor cell appears to drive disease emergence and progression, possibly after evolution of a clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Complete blood count shows hybrid features with significant and sustained cytosis and cytopenia(s) in all patients. Leukocytosis is present in most patients, and thrombocytosis in about 20% of them. Cytopenias mainly manifest as anaemia, followed by thrombocytopenia, and rarely neutropenia. Increased LDH (beyond the normal upper limit of each institution) is common, and significant bone marrow fibrosis is observed in about 30% of cases.

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

An abnormal karyotype is observed in about 50% of cases. There are no specific findings: common abnormalities include trisomy 8, monosomy 7 / 7q deletion, 20q deletion, and a monosomal or a complex karyotype. The appropriate categorization of cases associated with isolated isochromosome 17q is uncertain. Emerging evidence suggests that these patients have unique clinical, morphological, and genetic characteristics, including concomitant SRSF2 or SETBP1 mutations, and a poor outcome {22038701; 10460605; 26883102; 26859077; 34775472}.

Molecular analysis

The presence of one or more mutations in the appropriate clinicopathological context may help to confirm clonality. Relatively high frequencies of TET2, NRAS, RUNX1, CBL, SETBP1, and ASXL1 mutations have been reported {23628959; 24627528; 25212680}. Monoallelic or biallelic TP53 mutations occur in 10–15% of cases (biallelic mutation is an exclusion criterion; see below).

SF3B1 mutation should prompt careful exclusion of MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis (MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T), including rare instances of disease progression from myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS) with ring sideroblasts.

Several molecular signatures have been identified that further classify cases of MDS/MPN-NOS. In 60%, the molecular signature mimics the ones observed in other MDS/MPN, including chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia (CMML) (17%), MDS/MPN with neutrophilia (33%), and MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T (11%). Cases with such molecular subtypes of MDS/MPN-NOS show corresponding haematological parameters, with higher monocyte counts in CMML-like cases, higher white blood cell counts in MDS/MPN with neutrophilia cases, and a higher percentage of ring sideroblasts in MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T–like cases. In addition, rare cases with TP53 mutations are found, especially in patients with more anaemia and a higher bone marrow blast percentage {32573691}.

A small fraction of cases of MDS with del(5q) are associated with JAK2 p.V617F, proliferative features in the bone marrow, and higher median platelet counts {16617322}. It is unclear whether their clinical presentation and prognosis are any different from those of MDS with isolated del(5q) and wildtype JAK2 {20485371; 19819015}. Until more evidence is published, it is recommended that cases with combined del(5q) and JAK2 p.V617F mutation be classified as MDS with isolated del(5q) rather than MDS/MPN-NOS.

 

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #32938Box 2.22 .

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Previous clinical study cohorts, largely single institutional and retrospective, have demonstrated poor outcomes for affected patients, with median overall survival times ranging from 12 to 24 months. In one of these studies, patients with MDS/MPN-NOS had a median overall survival time of 21.8 months, and a leukaemia-free survival time of 18.9 months {24627528}. Prognosis is variable, with its uncertainty further compounded by inadequate representation of the subgroup in commonly used prognostic scoring systems, including the International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS) and Revised IPSS (IPSS-R) {24378705}.

As with other MDS/MPN overlap disorders, the treatment for patients with MDS/MPN-NOS is based on therapies used for MDS or MPN and is guided by symptoms and/or cytopenias {24378705}. Erythropoietin growth factors and hypomethylating agents {29465270} can alleviate cytopenias, whereas leukocytosis can be managed with cytoreductive therapies {24492324; 27664113; 31511613}.

Peripheral blood:

·   A combination of cytopenia(s) and proliferative feature(s)

Bone marrow cytology:

·   A combination of cell dysplasia and proliferative features {22195406; 18480833; 15217205}

Molecular analyses of blood or bone marrow:

·   A combination of mutations seen in proliferative and dysplastic myeloid malignancies

To be excluded:

·   Therapy-related myeloid neoplasms

·   Disease-defining gene fusions such as BCR::ABL1 or rearrangement of PDGFRA, PDGFRB, JAK2, or FGFR1

·   Biallelic TP53 mutations

·   Other MDS/MPN entities, including chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia, MDS/MPN with neutrophilia, and MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis

Specific situation:

·   Some MDS cases evolve to MDS/MPN-NOS with acquisition of proliferative features {19692701}. If the proliferative feature is persistent, these cases should be considered MDS/MPN-NOS, pending careful evaluation to ensure that proliferation is not reactive to growth factor administration, other previous or concurrent therapy, or a pathological situation (evaluation for the presence of a newly acquired mutation associated with proliferative features is encouraged).
