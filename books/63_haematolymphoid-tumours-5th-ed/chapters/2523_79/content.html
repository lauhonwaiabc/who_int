2.5.2.3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with KMT2A rearrangement

Definition

Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL) with KMT2A rearrangement is an acute leukaemia that fulfils the criteria for MPAL and in which the KMT2A locus on chromosome 11q23.3 is fused with any of a number of partner genes.

ICD-O coding

9807/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with KMT2A rearrangement

ICD-11 coding

2A61 & XH2S51 Acute leukaemias of ambiguous lineage & Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with t(v;11q23); MLL-rearranged

Related terminology

Not recommended: mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with MLL rearrangement.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved. Some patients present with myeloid sarcoma.

Clinical features

Patients present with clinical features similar to those of other acute leukaemias, typically secondary to cytopenias. This MPAL type has no distinctive clinical features.

Epidemiology

This MPAL type is rare. It is more common in children than in adults, and within the former age group it is relatively more common in infancy {10457405; 16437134; 21228332}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The cell of origin is probably a haematopoietic stem cell or progenitor cell with lineage plasticity {30209392}. KMT2Agene rearrangements result from deletions, duplications, inversions, and translocations at the 11q23 locus {10468849}. These rearrangements typically disrupt a 100 kb region in exons 5–11. The resulting derivative chromosome 11 harbours the KMT2A promoter and coding regions 5′ to the breakpoint {10468849}. The rearrangement results in a chimeric protein with a KMT2A-encoded N-terminus and a C-terminus encoded by the partner gene {10468849}. The most common 5′ KMT2A partners in MPAL are AFF1 (AF4) at 4q21, MLLT3 (AF9) at 9p22, and MLLT1 (ENL) at 19p13 {23888868; 30209392}. MPAL with KMT2A rearrangement has a low mutation burden and classically has few copy-number alterations {30209392}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Peripheral blood smears and bone marrow aspirate smears often show a dimorphic blast population, with some blasts resembling monoblasts/promonocytes and others resembling lymphoblasts. Some cases have only one dominant blast morphology, or undifferentiated blasts. Most cases present extensive blast infiltration, but in rare instances blasts may be < 20% of cells in the bone marrow and peripheral blood.

Immunophenotype

MPAL with KMT2A rearrangement usually has a B/myeloid immunophenotype {26276768}. Rare cases of acute undifferentiated leukaemia with KMT2A rearrangement, as well as MPAL with combined B/T lineage, have also been described {21228332; 22581002; 32372024}. Commonly expressed myeloid antigens include CD11b, CD13, CD15, CD25, CD65, KIT (CD117), myeloperoxidase, and lysozyme. Commonly expressed lymphoid antigens include CD2, CD7, CD19, CD22, cCD22, and cCD79a. CD10 is usually negative in the B-lymphoblastic component. CD56 may be expressed {19131545; 21228332}.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of KMT2A rearrangements may be accomplished using conventional karyotyping. However, some KMT2Arearrangements can be cytogenetically cryptic and require alternative techniques for detection, such as FISH or RNA sequencing. Complex rearrangements, particularly KMT2A::USP2, may be undetectable by FISH {30899083; 32196814; 32372024}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: increased blasts in bone marrow and/or blood with an immunophenotype that meets the diagnostic criteria for MPAL (may be < 20%); presence of a KMT2A rearrangement; no history of exposure to cytotoxic therapy.

Desirable: identification of the KMT2A fusion partner.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Outcome data on cases classified using contemporary MPAL criteria are limited. Anecdotal reports describe this MPAL type as a high-risk disease with poor survival even in younger patients {29720486; 32419244}.
