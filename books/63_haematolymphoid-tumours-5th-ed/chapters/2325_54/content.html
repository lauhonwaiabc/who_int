2.3.2.5 Acute basophilic leukaemia

Definition

Acute basophilic leukaemia is an acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with basophilic differentiation that lacks defining genetic abnormalities.

ICD-O coding

9870/3 Acute basophilic leukaemia

ICD-11 coding

2A60.37 Acute basophilic leukaemia

Related terminology

Not recommended: basophilic leukaemia.

Subtype(s)

A rare subtype occurs in infant boys, characterized by t(X;6)(p11;q23) generating a MYB::GATA1 fusion gene {21474671; 28418072}.

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood

Clinical features

Patients usually present with features of bone marrow failure. Cutaneous involvement, hepatosplenomegaly, lytic bone lesions, and symptoms related to hyperhistaminaemia may be present {28424883; 28090091}.

Epidemiology

This is a very rare type of AML and the epidemiology is unknown.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Recurrent translocation of t(X;6)(p11.2;q23) resulting in a MYB::GATA1 fusion gene has been reported in infant boys {21474671}. The fusion product promotes basophilic differentiation by inducing the expression of IL-33 and nerve growth factor (NGF), the ligands of IL1RL1 and NTRK1, respectively {28418072}. A case of acute basophilic leukaemia with t(16;21)(p11.2;q22) resulting in FUS::ERG fusion has also been reported {29225830}. Other genetic aberrations reported include U2AF1 mutation {30827760} and monosomy 7 {17213028}. Sporadic cases with TET2, TP53, and NPM1 mutations have also been reported, but no clear molecular pathway has been suggested {34132463}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Bone marrow biopsy shows hypercellularity with diffuse replacement by blasts. Blasts are medium sized to large and characterized by a high N:C ratio; an oval, round, or irregular nucleus with dispersed chromatin; and one to several prominent nucleoli. The cytoplasm contains variable numbers of coarse basophilic granules. Auer rods are absent. Immature basophils constitute 20–80% of cellular elements in the bone marrow {28424883; 28090091}. Immature basophils are characterized by irregular or segmented nuclei with relatively clumped chromatin and a varying number of coarse basophilic granules. Mature basophils are usually sparse {10037024; 28090091}. Electron microscopy shows electron-dense membrane-bound basophil granules; theta granules containing pale amorphous material are also seen {10037024; 28090091; 29225830}.

Immunophenotype

Flow cytometry usually detects a blast population and a population of cells showing basophilic maturation. The blasts are usually positive for CD11b, CD13, CD33, CD34, CD38, CD123, and CD203c. Blasts may also express CD9 and weak CD117 but are usually negative for HLA-DR. Strong expression of CD117 in abnormal mast cells distinguishes mast cell leukaemia from acute basophilic leukaemia {28090091; 29225830; 30827760; 34132463}.

Cytochemistry

Blasts and immature forms show characteristic metachromatic staining with toluidine blue. Blasts are negative (< 3%) for myeloperoxidase, Sudan Black B, naphthol AS-D CAE, and nonspecific esterase. A lack of CAE reactivity can distinguish basophilic blasts from mast cells {10037024; 28090091; 29225830}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes the blast phase of a myeloproliferative neoplasm (chronic myeloid leukaemia [CML]), other AML types with basophilia (e.g. AML with DEK::NUP214), AML with BCR::ABL1, and mast cell leukaemia). Concomitant eosinophilia may indicate the presence of a tyrosine kinase fusion gene such as FIP1L1::PDGFRA, or others (see above).

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetic and molecular characterization is needed to exclude potential differential diagnostic considerations and assess for molecular features such as MYB::GATA1.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts, with increased immature and mature basophils; blasts/basophils metachromatic on toluidine blue staining and negative for myeloperoxidase, Sudan Black B, and nonspecific esterase; blasts positive for at least two myeloid-associated markers (e.g. myeloperoxidase, CD13, CD33, CD117); not fulfilling criteria for AML types with defined genetic alterations; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy.

Desirable: blasts positive for CD9 and/or CD203c, and negative for HLA-DR.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

There are limited data on prognosis and prediction specifically. Reported cases have shown varying outcomes.
