2.2.6.3 Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis

Definition

Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm (MDS/MPN) with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis (MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T) is a myeloid neoplasm with myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative features, characterized by SF3B1 mutation, ring sideroblasts, and thrombocytosis.

ICD-O coding

9982/3 Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis

ICD-11 coding

2A43 Refractory anaemia with ring sideroblasts associated with marked thrombocytosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm (MDS/MPN) with ring sideroblasts and thrombocytosis.

Not recommended: refractory anaemia with ring sideroblasts associated with marked thrombocytosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peripheral blood and bone marrow are always involved. Splenomegaly has been reported in about 40% of patients {20334914}.

Clinical features

The clinical features overlap with those seen in patients with myelodysplastic neoplasms (MDSs) and myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs), particularly essential thrombocythaemia and other BCR::ABL1-negative MPN types. Complete blood count indexes are usually intermediate in severity between those seen in MDS and those seen in essential thrombocythaemia {22532522; 34448437}. Thrombosis risk is high, comparable to that in essential thrombocythaemia {27479179}.

Epidemiology

The median age at diagnosis is 68–75 years, and in most studies there is a slightly higher incidence in women than in men {34448437; 35105856; 23594705; 22532522; 25527566; 26874914}.

Etiology

Recent data suggest that the disease arises in the context of clonal haematopoiesis, through the sequential acquisition of somatic mutations in haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells {32573691}.

Pathogenesis

Ring sideroblasts are erythroid precursors with an abnormal accumulation of iron in mitochondria {26637696}. They are closely associated with mutations in SF3B1, which encodes a core component of the U2 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein (snRNP) spliceosome that is critical for RNA splicing {21909114; 21995386}. Mutant SF3B1 activity can result in the use of a cryptic 3′ splice site, generating splicing isoforms of the mitochondrial iron–sulfur cluster transporter gene ABCB7, several other mitochondrial metabolism genes, and iron homeostasis regulatory genes, including ERFE {23070040; 26565915; 31292266}. In contrast to MDS with SF3B1 mutation, MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T is characterized by concomitant activating mutations in genes regulating cell signalling pathways. Such mutations commonly include JAK2p.V617F (57%) and mutations in other genes, such as TET2 (20%), DNMT3A (15%), ASXL1 (15%), SETBP1 (10%), MPL (5%), and SH2B3 (5%) {26874914; 25527566; 35105856; 33428785}. A small subset (6.7–2.7%) of cases have mutations in spliceosome genes other than SF3B1, such as SRSF2, U2AF1, and ZRSR2 {25527566}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Peripheral blood

The peripheral blood typically shows normochromic macrocytic or normocytic anaemia. The red blood cells in the blood smear may show anisocytosis, often with a dimorphic pattern. Circulating blasts are absent or rare. Thrombocytosis with a platelet count of ≥ 450 × 109/L is one of the defining features. The platelets often display anisocytosis, ranging from tiny forms to atypical large, or giant, platelets. Irregularly shaped or agranular platelets may be seen. The white blood cell count and differential are usually normal, although mild leukocytosis may occur.

Bone marrow

The bone marrow is usually hypercellular and shows increased erythropoiesis with megaloblastic and/or other dysplastic features, including the presence of ≥ 15% ring sideroblasts on iron staining. Multilineage dysplasia may be identified in some cases {11940483; 18166783}. Megakaryocytes are increased and usually have morphological features similar to those seen in BCR::ABL1-negative MPN. Bone marrow fibrosis may be present in a subset of patients {11940483; 18166783}.

Immunophenotype

There are no specific immunophenotypic features associated with MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T. By flow cytometry, CD34-positive haematopoietic precursor cells usually have abnormalities consistent with a haematopoietic stem cell neoplasm, like in other MDS/MPN types.

Differential diagnosis

Non-clonal conditions associated with ring sideroblasts and reactive thrombocytosis should be excluded. MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T should be distinguished from myeloid neoplasms that may have ring sideroblasts and those that may have thrombocytosis. In particular, it is important to exclude MDS with low blasts and SF3B1 mutation, and MDS with low blasts and isolated 5q deletion. Similarly, it is important to exclude chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML) as well as other BCR::ABL1-negative MPN types. Rarely, acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with MECOM rearrangement and myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase fusions may mimic MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T at presentation and therefore should be carefully excluded. In addition, a history of prior exposure to cytotoxic therapy excludes a diagnosis of MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T.

For practical purposes, in the absence of a confirmed SF3B1 mutation (or, rarely, a confirmed equivalent other spliceosome gene mutation), the diagnosis of MDS/MPN with ring sideroblasts and thrombocytosis may be rendered and considered from a clinical management standpoint equivalent to MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T, provided there is the presence of ≥ 15% ring sideroblasts.

 

Cytology

See Histopathology, above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetic abnormalities are detected in 15% of patients {35105856}.

Molecular analysis

The diagnosis is based on the detection of SF3B1 mutation (and, in rare cases, mutations in other spliceosome genes). The most common SF3B1 mutations include p.K700E, p.H662Q, and p.K666R {35105856}. The mutant allele burden is comparable to that seen in other myeloid neoplasms with ring sideroblasts. Concurrent mutations include JAK2 p.V617F (in ~50% of cases), TET2, ASXL1, DNMT3A, SETBP1, SRSF2, U2AF1, ZRSR2, EZH2, IDH2, ETV6, RUNX1, CBL, CALR, and MPL mutations {32573691; 26874914; 25527566}.

Patients diagnosed with MDS with low blasts and SF3B1 mutation who acquire JAK2 p.V617F or equivalent mutations with resultant thrombocytosis may be regarded as having developed MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T through clonal evolution.

 

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #32937Box 2.21 .

Essential: anaemia associated with dysplastic erythropoiesis and ≥ 15% ring sideroblasts, with or without dysplasia in the megakaryocytic and granulocytic lineages; persistent thrombocytosis, with a platelet count of ≥ 450 × 109/L; SF3B1 mutations or, in the absence of these mutations, concurrent biologically similar mutations involving spliceosome factors and cell signalling; not meeting diagnostic criteria for MDSs, MPNs, chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia, AML with MECOM rearrangement, or myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia.

Desirable: concurrent JAK2 p.V617F or other myeloid neoplasm associated mutations.

 

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Among all MDS/MPN types, MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T has the most favourable prognosis, with a reported median overall survival of 76–128 months {35105856; 23594705; 22532522; 34448437}. Haemoglobin ≤ 10 g/dL and abnormal karyotype were found to correlate independently with overall survival in a recent study {35105856}. Among the mutations common in MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T, frameshift and nonsense ASXL1 mutations may be prognostic {26874914; 32573691; 35105856}. Quantification of ring sideroblasts {22538853} and molecular abnormalities has little to no prognostic value {31511613; 35105856}. A proposed prognostic model based on abnormal karyotype, ASXL1 and/or SETBP1 mutations, and haemoglobin < 10 g/dL stratified patients into three risk categories, with significantly different median overall survival rates {26874914; 32573691}.

 

Peripheral blood:

·   Anaemia (haemoglobin below the normal range)

·   Thrombocytosis (platelet count > 450 × 109/L)

·   No or very rare blast cells

Bone marrow cytology:

·   Dysplasia, especially dyserythropoiesis with ring sideroblasts {10492088; 6619310; 25899435; 16741247}

Molecular analyses of blood or bone marrow:

·   SF3B1 heterozygous mutation

·   Concurrent JAK2 p.V617F or, in the absence of JAK2 p.V617F, mutation in another myeloproliferative gene such as MPL or CALR {16926301; 16932338; 17244688; 19692701; 25957392; 22096241; 16670082; 16990759; 11940483; 17713548; 17194663; 16741247; 16871284}

If molecular analyses are unavailable:

 

Peripheral blood:

·   Sustained thrombocytosis for ≥ 3 months substitutes JAK2, MPL, or CALR mutation

 

Bone marrow cytology:

·   ≥ 15% ring sideroblasts substitutes SF3B1 mutation

To be excluded:

·   Therapy-related myeloid neoplasms

·   MDS with isolated del(5q)

·   Myeloid neoplasms with a double-hit TP53 alteration

·   Myeloid neoplasms with t(3;3)(q21.3;q26.2) or inv(3)(q21.3q26.2)

·   Disease-defining gene fusions such as BCR::ABL1

Specific situation:

·   Rare cases that initially present as a myeloid neoplasm with SF3B1 mutation and ring sideroblasts and later evolve to MDS/MPN with ring sideroblasts and thrombocytosis upon acquisition of a JAK2, MPL, or CALR mutation may be classified as MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis {19692701}
