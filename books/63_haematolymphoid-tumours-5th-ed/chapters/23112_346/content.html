2.3.1.12 Acute myeloid leukaemia with CEBPA mutation

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with CEBPA mutation is characterized by CEBPA mutation that should be biallelic (biCEBPA) or, if single, must be located in the bZIP region of the gene (smbZIP-CEBPA).

ICD-O coding

9878/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with CEBPA mutation

ICD-11 coding

2A60.0 & XH4M02 Acute myeloid leukaemia with recurrent genetic abnormalities & Acute myeloid leukaemia with biallelic mutation of CEBPA

Related terminology

Acceptable: acute myeloid leukaemia with biallelic mutation of CEBPA.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Blood and bone marrow

Clinical features

Presenting symptoms and signs are typically secondary to bone marrow failure and include fatigue, bruising or bleeding, and infection. Patients with biCEBPA and smbZIP-CEBPA are younger and have higher white blood cell counts than those with a single mutation in the N-terminal transactivation domain (TAD) {34320176}. Detection of biCEBPA should raise suspicion of a germline CEBPA variant and referral for genetic counselling (see section Myeloid neoplasms associated with germline predisposition).

Epidemiology

AML with CEBPA mutation constitutes approximately 5% of AML cases in children and 5–11% of AML cases in adults {33951732; 34320176; 34448807}.

Etiology

Among cases with biCEBPA, approximately 5–10% have a germline N-terminal mutation {21177436}. In the familial form, AML has very high penetrance and presents relatively early (median age: 24.5 years) {26162409}.

Pathogenesis

The gene CEBPA encodes for a bZIP transcription factor that is essential for myeloid lineage commitment and regulates myeloid differentiation and stem/progenitor cell function {28179278}. The bZIP region is required for dimerization and DNA binding of this protein {15122210; 15674366}. The protein can interact with CDK2 and CDK4. Mutations can be biallelic or monoallelic (see #30053Table 2.16).

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Smear preparations show increased blasts with or without maturation {20038735; 20439648; 21134981; 34320176}. Dysplasia is common, especially dysgranulopoiesis and dysmegakaryopoiesis {22442349}.

The leukaemic cells are often positive for CD7, CD13, CD15, CD33, CD34, and HLA-DR, but they rarely express CD56 or CD14 {15746035}.

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CEBPA mutation can be defined by various molecular techniques (including PCR with fragment analysis and next-generation sequencing) that appropriately interrogate the CEBPA gene for mutation location.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts with a myeloid immunophenotype in the bone marrow or blood; presence of biallelic mutations in CEBPA, or a single mutation located in the bZIP region; absence of criteria allowing for classification into other AMLs with defining genetic abnormalities; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognostic impact of different CEBPA mutations is shown in #30053Table 2.16 . biCEBPA was previously required for this AML subtype because of the association with a favourable prognosis {19171880; 20038735; 20439648}. A similarly favourable prognosis has recently been shown for smbZIP-CEBPA in cohorts of children and adults aged up to 70 years {33951732; 34320176; 34448807}, justifying the inclusion of this additional CEBPA mutation subset.
