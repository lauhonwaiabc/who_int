2.2.6.2 Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with neutrophilia

Definition

Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with neutrophilia (MDS/MPN-N), formerly known as atypical chronic myeloid leukaemia, is a myeloid neoplasm with myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative features characterized by sustained peripheral blood neutrophilia and neutrophilic left shift.

ICD-O coding

9876/3 Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with neutrophilia

ICD-11 coding

2A41 Atypical chronic myeloid leukaemia, BCR-ABL1-negative

Related terminology

Not recommended: atypical chronic myeloid leukaemia, BCR::ABL1-negative.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The peripheral blood and bone marrow are always involved; splenic and hepatic involvement is common.

Clinical features

Patients usually have symptoms related to bone marrow insufficiency, particularly anaemia and thrombocytopenia {17043019; 10847463}. Symptoms may also be related to hepatosplenomegaly {11387365; 24627528}. Approximately 40% of cases progress to acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) {24627528}.

Epidemiology

MDS/MPN-N is uncommon {24627528}. Diagnosis is usually in the seventh or eighth decade of life, although younger patients may be affected {17043019; 10847463; 11387365; 2070054; 24627528}. There is no sex predilection.

Etiology

Recent data suggest that the disease arises from clonal haematopoiesis through the sequential acquisition of somatic mutations in haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells {31366621; 33196013}.

Pathogenesis

ETNK1 and SETBP1 mutations have been identified as key pathogenic events {29875417; 33230096}. ETNK1 mutations result in relative depletion of phosphoethanolamine, a negative regulator of mitochondrial complex II, which causes increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and mutagenesis. SETBP1 mutations destroy a degron and stabilize the protein, which forms a multimeric complex (with HCF1/KMT2A/PHF8) to modulate gene transcription and histone modification. This results in the upregulation of several genes including MECOM. Clonal architecture reconstruction shows that ETNK1 mutations are invariably an early event, whereas SETBP1 mutation is a late event, with mutations involving the RAS pathway (e.g. KRAS, NRAS, CBL mutations) being intervening events {33196013}. Notably, CBL mutations are often associated with copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity through somatic uniparental disomy.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Peripheral blood

Peripheral blood smear evaluation is helpful for evaluating for neutrophilic dysplasia and left shift. Circulating immature myeloid cells (promyelocytes, myelocytes, and metamyelocytes) should, by definition, constitute ≥ 10% of the leukocytes. Although the absolute monocyte count may be elevated, monocytes should not exceed 10% of the leukocytes. If they do, the diagnosis of chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia should be strongly considered. In addition, there may be other stigmata of myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms, such as mild basophilia. Anaemia and thrombocytopenia are frequently present {7986717; 17043019; 10847463; 11387365; 2070054; 24627528}.

Bone marrow

The bone marrow is typically hypercellular for age, with an elevated myeloid-to-erythroid ratio (> 10:1). Dysgranulopoiesis is a characteristic feature and is detected in ≥ 10% of granulocytes {24627528}, manifesting as cytoplasmic hypogranulation and abnormalities in nuclear segmentation and chromatin condensation, which include pseudo–Pelger–Huët nuclei (hypolobation), hypersegmentation, abnormally dense clumping (hypercondensation), and nuclear projections. The number of megakaryocytes is normal or increased. Dysmegakaryopoiesis is frequent, seen as small megakaryocytes with hypolobated nuclei and micromegakaryocytes. Dyserythropoiesis may be present. Bone marrow reticulin fibrosis can be present but is often mild. Blasts must be < 20% of all cells, without large clusters or sheets {24627528; 7986717; 2757974; 3179227; 2098294; 17043019; 10847463}.

There are no specific immunophenotypic features associated with MDS/MPN-N. By flow cytometry, CD34-positive haematopoietic precursor cells usually have aberrancies consistent with a haematopoietic stem cell neoplasm, similar to other myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm types.

No specific abnormalities are noted. Neutrophils may show aberrant loss or decreased expression of myeloperoxidase. Nonspecific esterase on smears to evaluate monocytes can help exclude the diagnosis of chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia. Iron stain can highlight occasional ring sideroblasts. Neutrophilic alkaline phosphatase is variable and not considered helpful for diagnosis {11387365; 2070054}.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetics

Karyotypic abnormalities are detected in 30–40% of cases, with chromosomes 8 and 20 being most commonly involved {2070054; 10847463; 28314085; 29700173; 33914911}. Isolated isochromosome 17q has been reported in this disease subset {26859077; 26883102}. Chromosomal abnormalities are sometimes associated with disease progression {33196013}. The diagnosis of MDS/MPN-N requires exclusion of BCR::ABL1 fusion, which may require careful evaluation to exclude cryptic rearrangements and/or alternate BCR::ABL1 transcripts.

Molecular analysis

Point mutations occur in most cases, with a median of 2–4 per case {23222956; 23656643; 23604229; 23896413; 25343957; 26341525; 28314085; 29700173; 31366621; 32573691; 33196013; 33914911; 33931725}. The diagnosis is supported by the presence of mutations in SETBP1 and/or ETNK1 {23222956; 23896413; 25343957; 33196013}. Mutations associated with clonal haematopoiesis are common, usually involving ASXL1 (60–80%), TET2 (30–40%), and/or DNMT3A (< 10%). Other genes frequently mutated impact proliferative signalling pathways (e.g. CBL, JAK2, NF1, NRAS), splicing machinery (e.g. SRSF2, U2AF1, SF3B1, ZRSR2), transcription factors (CUX1, GATA2, RUNX1), enzymes (ETNK1), epigenetic regulators (SETBP1, EZH2), and chromosomal separation regulators (STAG2).

Differential diagnosis

The overall mutation profile of MDS/MPN-N is similar to that of chronic neutrophilic leukaemia, chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia, and MDS/MPN-NOS. CSF3R mutations are distinctly more common in chronic neutrophilic leukaemia than in MDS/MPN-N (> 60% vs < 20%), and SETBP1 mutations are most frequently identified in MDS/MPN-N (> 30%). Hence, morphological criteria are important for distinguishing these entities.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with MDS/MPN-N have a poor prognosis, with a median survival of 14–29 months {17043019; 10847463; 11387365; 24627528}. Age > 65 years, female sex, white blood cell count > 50 × 109/L, thrombocytopenia, and haemoglobin level < 10 g/dL have been reported to be adverse prognostic factors {17043019; 10847463}.

Patients who receive stem cell transplant therapy may have better outcomes {15516946}. A higher than average number of variants of known significance (> 4) or a three-gene signature have been reported to confer an inferior outcome {31366621; 33196013}, as have mutations of NRAS, GATA2, ASXL1, SETBP1, RUNX1, TP53, or ASXL1 {31366621; 23222956; 33931725}.

Essential:

·      Peripheral blood white blood cell count ≥ 13 × 109/L, with neutrophilia and ≥ 10% circulating immature myeloid cells (promyelocytes, myelocytes, and metamyelocytes), as well as neutrophilic dysplasia

·      Hypercellular bone marrow with granulocytic predominance and granulocytic dysplasia, with or without dysplasia in the megakaryocytic and erythroid lineages

·      < 20% blasts in blood and bone marrow

·      Not meeting diagnostic criteria for myeloproliferative neoplasms (specifically, exclusion of BCR::ABL1 fusion)a, myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase gene fusions, chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia, or myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis

Desirable:

·      Detection of SETBP1 and/or ETNK1 mutations

·      Absence of mutations in JAK2, CALR, MPL, and CSF3Rb

aThe diagnosis of MDS/MPN-N requires exclusion of BCR::ABL1 fusion, which requires careful evaluation to exclude cryptic rearrangements and/or alternate BCR::ABL1 transcripts by available methodologies (e.g. cytogenetics, in situ hybridization, or PCR-based assays). bMutations in these genes are uncommon in MDS/MPN-N and should prompt morphological review to exclude alternative diagnoses.
