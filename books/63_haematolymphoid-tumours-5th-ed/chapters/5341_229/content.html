5.3.4.1 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma is an aggressive mature T-cell lymphoma characterized by a proliferation of cytotoxic T cells within the spleen, liver, and bone marrow.

ICD-O coding

9716/3 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A90.8 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: erythrophagocytic Tγ lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma involves the liver, spleen, and bone marrow {12907441; 26872013}. Peripheral lymph nodes are uncommonly involved {26872013; 27755009}. A leukaemic phase is uncommon at initial diagnosis but can occur, particularly late in the clinical course {26708981}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with marked hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, and B symptoms (see #30909Table 5.14) {10865963; 12907441; 19237479; 26872013}. This neoplasm can (rarely) involve the skin, most often at the time of relapse {27562705}. Laboratory abnormalities are common, particularly cytopenias and elevated serum levels of B2M and LDH (see #30909Table 5.14) {12907441; 26872013}. Virtually all patients have advanced-stage disease, shown by pathological evaluation and/or radiological imaging studies {12907441; 26872013; 33160933}.

 

Epidemiology

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma is rare, representing 1.4–2% of peripheral T-cell lymphomas and far less than 1% of all non-Hodgkin lymphomas {18626005; 31709579}. Early studies showed that most patients are adolescents or young adults, with a median age in the fourth decade of life {29337025; 32756940}, but a recent study showed that 51% of patients were aged > 60 years {31709579}. The gamma-delta subtype shows a male predominance {29337025; 32756940}. By contrast, patients with the alpha-beta subtype are more often women and tend to be older {11224598}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

About 20% of hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma cases arise in the context of immunosuppression or autoimmune disease, most often in transplant recipients or patients with Crohn disease treated with azathioprine and infliximab {19562701; 29337025; 28038706}. Chronic antigenic stimulation and/or impaired immunosurveillance may therefore be involved in pathogenesis.

Isochromosome 7q is common and thought to be an early event in pathogenesis {7536454; 9264394}. Loss of 7p22.1-p14.1 and gain of 7q22.11-q31.1 are associated with overexpression of CHN2, ABCB1, RUNDC3B, and PPP1R9A {25057852}. Mutations involving genes in the JAK/STAT pathway and/or chromatin epigenetic modification also occur (see #30927Table 5.15) {25586472; 28122867}. Gene expression profiling has shown that the alpha-beta and gamma-delta subtypes have a similar profile, with overexpression of NK cell–associated molecules, FOS, VAV3, Syk, and S1PR5 {22510872}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

The spleen is markedly enlarged, typically > 1000 g or > 200 mm in diameter by radiological imaging {26872013}. The cut surface has a homogeneous appearance without nodules, reflecting diffuse red pulp involvement and atrophy of the white pulp. The liver is also diffusely enlarged.

Histopathology

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma involves cords and sinuses of the splenic red pulp {10865963; 12907441; 17509984}. The liver and bone marrow also show prominent sinusoidal involvement {11554170; 27755009}. The neoplastic cells in the bone marrow usually expand sinusoids {26872013; 27755009}. The bone marrow may also show mild to moderate haematopoietic cell dyspoiesis, but these features do not appear to have clinical importance {26997444}. Erythrophagocytosis can be present, and uncommonly haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis can develop {21683978}. The neoplastic cells are small to intermediate in size, with irregular nuclear contours, dense chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli, and pale cytoplasm {12907441; 17509984}. The lymphoma cells do not have cytoplasmic granules as shown by Giemsa {27755009}. Occasionally, the neoplastic cells can resemble blasts, or they may exhibit substantial atypia and pleomorphism {11554170; 12907441}. Blastoid or pleomorphic cytological features can be detected at time of initial presentation or at time of progression {11554170; 12907441}.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells are of cytotoxic T-cell lineage and have a distinctive immunophenotype {17509984; 29337025} (see #30928Table 5.16). They are usually positive for CD2 and CD3, they express cytotoxic markers such as TIA1, perforin, granzyme M, and granzyme B (in 30–40% of cases), and they are usually negative for CD4, CD5 (~15% dim positive), and CD8 (~20% positive). Approximately 75% of cases are positive for TCRγδ, about 20% are positive for TCRαβ, and 5% lack T-cell receptors (silent or null). TCRVδ expression seems to be restricted to the Vδ1 chain {11272897}. The NK cell–associated markers CD56 (~70%) and CD16 (~60%) are often positive. In situ hybridization analysis for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is negative.

Differential diagnosis

Other mature T-cell lymphoma subtypes may involve the liver and spleen, and an extensive panel of immunohistochemical markers should be used {32947231}.

 

Cytology

The diagnosis is most often established by bone marrow examination (described above) and there is a limited role for FNAB of the spleen or liver.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma carries clonal TR rearrangements. Isochromosome 7q and/or trisomy 8, present in as many as two thirds and half of all cases, respectively, can help in establishing the diagnosis {27755009; 32756940}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic pattern of extranodal disease with sinusoidal involvement of the liver, spleen, and bone marrow; small to intermediate-sized lymphoma cells without intracytoplasmic granules; demonstrated cytotoxic T-cell lineage.

Desirable: characteristic immunophenotype: CD4−, CD5−/+, CD8−/+, CD56+/−; isochromosome 7q; trisomy 8.

Staging

The Lugano system is used to stage lymphomas and has been adopted by the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients have a poor prognosis, with a median survival time of < 2 years {18626005; 33160933}. Patients with the TCRαβ subtype may have a poorer prognosis {11224598}. Chemotherapy using highly aggressive regimens and/or allogeneic stem cell transplantation may improve long-term survival {25234166}.
