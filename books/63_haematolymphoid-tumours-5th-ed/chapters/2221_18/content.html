2.2.2.1 Mastocytosis: Introduction

Mastocytosis comprises rare, heterogeneous diseases characterized by the accumulation of abnormal mast cells in various organs or tissues. It exhibits a complex pathology and diverse clinical behaviour. This disease category includes cutaneous mastocytosis, systemic mastocytosis with its various clinicopathological subtypes, and mast cell sarcoma (see #32609Box 2.15).

Mastocytosis develops via activating mutations of the gene encoding the KIT receptor, which is involved in the proliferation of mast cells, germ cells, melanocytes, and haematopoietic cells. A somatic point mutation in KIT at codon D816 is found in > 80% of patients with systemic mastocytosis. Rare activating KIT aberrations comprise mutations in the extracellular domain (e.g. deletion of codon 419 in exon 8 or p.A502_Y503dup in exon 9), transmembrane domain (e.g. KIT p.F522C), or juxtamembrane domain (e.g. KIT p.V560G), which are found in < 1% of advanced systemic mastocytosis cases but seem to be enriched in cases of indolent systemic mastocytosis. Most patients with KIT p.D816V and advanced systemic mastocytosis have somatic mutations in additional genes, most frequently TET2, SRSF2, ASXL1, RUNX1, and JAK2. In patients with these mutations, an associated haematological neoplasm, most commonly a myeloid neoplasm, may be detected.

Whereas patients with indolent systemic mastocytosis have a normal or near-normal life expectancy, patients with advanced mast cell neoplasms, including aggressive systemic mastocytosis and mast cell leukaemia, have a poor prognosis with a short survival time. Independent of the category of mastocytosis and the serum tryptase level, patients may have mediator-related symptoms and/or osteopathy. Depending on the presence of comorbidities, the symptomatology in such patients may be mild, severe, or even life-threatening. The most relevant comorbidities in such patients are IgE-dependent allergies, vitamin D deficiency, and psychiatric and psychological conditions. The diagnosis and management of mastocytosis is an emerging challenge in clinical practice and requires specialized knowledge, a multidisciplinary approach, and personalized procedures.
