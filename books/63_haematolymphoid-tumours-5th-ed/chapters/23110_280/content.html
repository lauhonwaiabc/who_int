2.3.1.10 Acute myeloid leukaemia with other defined genetic alterations

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with other defined genetic alterations includes emerging AML subtypes with distinct genetic features.

ICD-O coding

9861/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with other defined genetic alterations

9861/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with CBFA2T3::GLIS2 fusion

9861/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with KAT6A::CREBBP fusion

9861/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with FUS::ERG fusion

9861/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with MNX1::ETV6 fusion

9861/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with NPM1::MLF1 fusion

ICD-11 coding

2A60.0 Acute myeloid leukaemia with recurrent genetic abnormalities

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Acute myeloid leukaemia with CBFA2T3::GLIS2 fusion; acute myeloid leukaemia with KAT6A::CREBBP fusion; acute myeloid leukaemia with FUS::ERG fusion; acute myeloid leukaemia with MNX1::ETV6 fusion; acute myeloid leukaemia with NPM1::MLF1 fusion

Localization

Blood and bone marrow

Clinical features

There are no unique clinical features.

Epidemiology

See #35863Table 2.17 .

AML with CBFA2T3::GLIS2: This cryptic inversion/fusion is exclusively seen in children aged < 5 years, with the highest incidence during infancy, and is often associated with non–Down syndrome–related acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia (18–30% of newly diagnosed childhood AML cases) {23045605; 23153540; 28112737}.

AML with KAT6A::CREBBP: This rare entity can occur at any age. In children, the peak incidence is in the neonatal period {23974201}. In adults, the median age is 60 years, and it is more often seen in AML post cytotoxic therapy than in de novo AML {19194466; 18528428}.

AML with FUS::ERG: This entity is mainly found in young adults (median age: 30 years) {9242552; 30150206}.

AML with MNX1::ETV6: This entity constitutes < 1% of newly diagnosed childhood AML cases, but as many as one third of the cases in patients aged 0–2 years {16646086; 29569294}.

AML with NPM1::MLF1: This entity is rare. It occurs in both children and adults, with a mean age of 34.5 years and a male preponderance {20471513; 14506644}. A small subset of patients may have antecedent myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS) {14506644}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

CBFA2T3::GLIS2: Cells harbouring this fusion demonstrate enhanced self-renewal in vitro {23153540}. Mechanistically, gene expression of known targets of the hedgehog pathway, including BMPs and PTCH1, is upregulated by this fusion {23045605; 23153540}.

KAT6A::CREBBP: Gene expression profiling has shown striking similarities with AML with KMT2A rearrangements, with deregulation of the HOXA cluster member genes {19194466; 23974201}. It may also regulate microRNA that targets the proto-oncogene RET {23022987}. Recurrent co-mutations are identified in ASXL1, FLT3, TP53, RUNX1, and TET2 {33529373}.

FUS::ERG: The fusion acts in the context of a heptad of proteins (ERG, FLI1, G-ATA2, LYL1, LMO2, RUNX1, and TAL1) central to the proper expression of genes involved in maintaining a stem cell haematopoietic phenotype {26148230}. Moreover, the fusion protein co-occupies genomic regions bound by the nuclear receptor heterodimer RAR/RXR inhibiting target gene expression and interfering with haematopoietic differentiation {10523652; 26148230}. Co-mutations in genes such as RUNX1, DNMT3A, ASXL1, and BCOR can be seen {24374719; 29427188}.

MNX1::ETV6: Although the production of a chimeric MNX1::ETV6 protein as an oncogenic trigger remains unclear {26605042}, highly increased MNX1 expression in these patients suggests that ectopic expression of MNX1 may promote leukaemogenesis {30598492; 16646086; 19212340}.

NPM1::MLF1: This fusion may influence the organization of the chromatin-remodelling complexes SWI/SNF, nucleosome remodelling and deacetylase (NuRD) and polybromo-associated BAF (PBAF) in haematopoietic cells, resulting in differential gene expression {31675375}.

Other rare fusions

Reported RARG (12q13) fusions include CPSF6::RARG, t(12;12)(q13;q15) {29568099; 29487068}; NUP98::RARG, t(11;12)(p15;q13) {20935257}; PML::RARG, t(12;15)(q13;q22) {28555082}; and HNRNPC::RARG (cytogenetic analysis not available) {32354871}. These fusions are associated with acute promyelocytic leukaemia–like features and resistance to all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA)-based therapy.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

See #35863Table 2.17 .

CBFA2T3::GLIS2: The blasts often show the RAM immunophenotype – strong CD56 expression and lack of HLA-DR and CD38 {27133823; 30592296; 33811445}.

KAT6A::CREBBP: Patients often present with acute monoblastic/monocytic leukaemia (defined by differentiation). AML with KAT6A::CREBBP is frequently associated with erythrophagocytosis (70%), leukaemia cutis (58%), and disseminated intravascular coagulation (39%) {19194466; 23974201}. Blast cells show strong peroxidase and esterase activities and express CD4, CD14, CD13, CD33, CD56, and HLA-DR, whereas they lack CD34 and KIT (CD117) expression {19194466; 30759270}.

FUS::ERG: The blasts have no specific differentiation pattern or immunophenotype {9242552; 30150206}.

MNX1::ETV6: The blasts have no specific differentiation pattern but approximately 10% show megakaryoblastic differentiation {29569294}. Leukaemic cells may express T-cell markers in addition to myeloid markers.

NPM1::MLF1: The blasts have no specific differentiation pattern or immunophenotype, but multilineage dysplasia can be seen {14506644}.

Cytology

See #35863Table 2.17 .

Diagnostic molecular pathology

These subtypes are defined by their genetic abnormalities as detected by chromosome analysis for non-cryptic alterations and/or by proving fusion presence by FISH or another molecular method. The presence of additional mutations does not alter the subclassification or diagnosis.

CBFA2T3::GLIS2, inv(16)(p13.3q24): Concomitant cytogenetic abnormalities are uncommon, and cases usually have few somatic mutations {31294507; 23045605; 23153540; 28112737}.

KAT6A::CREBBP, t(8;16)(p11.2;p13.3): Concomitant cytogenetic abnormalities, even complex karyotypes, can be seen {30759270}.

FUS::ERG, t(16;21)(p11.2;q22): In about one third of cases, this translocation is part of a complex karyotype {9242552; 30150206}.

MNX1::ETV6, t(7;12)(q36;p13): This is a cryptic translocation and may also be misdiagnosed as del(12)(p13) and/or del(7q), therefore FISH analysis is needed for its identification. Most cases also have trisomy 19, suggesting a potential cooperative role in leukaemogenesis {29569294}.

NPM1::MLF1, t(3;5)(q25;q35): In 70% of cases, t(3;5) is the sole chromosomal abnormality. Trisomy 8 is the most common specific additional chromosomal abnormality (9.8%), and approximately 10% of patients have a complex karyotype {20471513}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts with myeloid immunophenotype in bone marrow and/or blood; detection of one or more of the cytogenetic or molecular aberrations listed in #35863Table 2.17 ; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for AML with defining genetic abnormalities, myelodysplasia-related AML, AML post cytotoxic therapy, or mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

CBFA2T3::GLIS2: This fusion is associated with an adverse outcome {27133823; 30592296}.

KAT6A::CREBBP: Similarly to in transient abnormal myelopoiesis in Down syndrome, spontaneous remission may occur in children diagnosed with KAT6A::CREBBP AML in the early neonatal period. Some cases with spontaneous remission subsequently recur {23974201}. In adults with KAT6A::CREBBP AML but no prior cytotoxic therapy or antecedent myeloid neoplasm, allogeneic stem cell transplantation can substantially improve outcome {33529373}.

FUS::ERG: The prognosis is dismal {30150206}, despite allogeneic stem cell transplantation {29959436}. The t(16;21)(p11;q22) (FUS::ERG) fusion should not be confused with the t(16;21)(q24;q22) (RUNX1::CBFA2T3 [RUNX1T3]) fusion, with which AML has a favourable outcome {30150206}.

MNX1::ETV6: Studies have demonstrated a high risk of relapse in patients with MNX1::ETV6 fusions, and variable survival {16646086; 26605042; 29569294}.

NPM1::MLF1: Patients may achieve remission but may relapse, with a median survival time of < 1 year; allogeneic stem cell transplantation may substantially improve outcome {14506644}.
