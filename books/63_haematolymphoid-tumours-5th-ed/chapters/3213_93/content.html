3.2.1.3 Langerhans cell sarcoma

Definition

Langerhans cell sarcoma (LCS) is an aggressive malignant neoplasm with a Langerhans cell immunophenotype and high-grade cytological features.

ICD-O coding

9756/3 Langerhans cell sarcoma

9756/3 Primary Langerhans cell sarcoma

9756/3 Secondary Langerhans cell sarcoma (following or associated with another haematological neoplasm)

ICD-11 coding

2B31.3 Langerhans cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: malignant histiocytosis of Langerhans phenotype.

Subtype(s)

Primary Langerhans cell sarcoma; secondary Langerhans cell sarcoma (following or associated with another haematological neoplasm)

Localization

Most cases manifest in extranodal sites, such as skin, lung, bone, and soft tissue {25805533; 31005630}. Lung, liver, spleen, lymph nodes, and bone are affected in multiorgan disease, with widespread disease occurring in 45% of cases {12121233; 25805533}. Isolated lymph node involvement is seen in 20% of cases [[Swerdlow SH, Campo E, Harris NL, et al., editors. WHO classification of tumours of haematopoietic and lymphoid tissues. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2017. (WHO classification of tumours series, 4th rev. ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int/556.]] {27980300}.

Clinical features

Clinical presentation varies according to the organ system involved.

Epidemiology

LCS has an incidence of 0.02 cases per 1 million population in the SEER database {27980300; 31005630}. It affects adults and has a slight male predilection {25805533; 31005630}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

LCS is a clonal disease, but investigations focusing on the separate pathogenesis of the primary and secondary subtypes are lacking. Mutations in MAPK pathway genes, including KRAS and (infrequently) BRAF p.V600E, have been described in a subset of cases {29194093; 33904632; 26468227}. Deletions in CDKN2A and TP53 have been observed, and PTEN is mutated in a few cases, in association with other MAPK pathway mutations {33904632; 29194093}. LCS as a secondary malignant histiocytosis has been associated with follicular lymphoma {23759932; 30322385} and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia {21666687; 29194093; 23923114}. There is often a shared clonality / molecular aberration with the original haematological neoplasm, suggesting transdifferentiation. Langerhans cell neoplasm arising in such a setting may show anaplastic progression from the low-grade form (i.e. Langerhans cell histiocytosis) to the high-grade form (i.e. secondary LCS), whereas there is no such well-documented progression in primary (de novo) LCS {19642834; 26966089}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not clinically relevant

Histopathology

LCS is a high-grade malignancy not readily diagnosable without panel immunophenotyping. The pleomorphic tumour cell nuclei are plump or elongated, with clumped chromatin. The nuclear grooves characteristic of Langerhans cell histiocytosis may not be present, and eosinophil content is variable. Mitotic activity is high, and atypical mitoses are often present. In lymph nodes, prominent sinusoidal infiltration may be seen. Birbeck granules can be demonstrated on ultrastructural examination {12121233}.

Immunohistochemistry

LCS expresses markers of Langerhans cell differentiation, i.e. CD1a, S100, and CD207 (langerin). The cells are also positive for vimentin, CD68, cyclin D1, and HLA-DR. Expression can be variable and focal: assessment of staining should be made on the high-grade cells and not the intermixed smaller spindle/dendritic cells.

Differential diagnosis

The histological features often overlap with those of other histiocytic malignancies, including the histiocytic, interdigitating, and indeterminate cell types. Some cases may have an atypical immunophenotype, with overlapping dendritic and macrophage phenotypes, defying strict classification as either LCS or histiocytic sarcoma {26966089}.

 

Cytology

Fine core needle biopsies may show highly atypical histiocytes with the Langerhans cell phenotype; however, it may be difficult to make a definitive diagnosis on this material.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A subset of cases have mutations in MAPK pathway genes, including KRAS and (infrequently) BRAF p.V600E {29194093; 33904632; 26468227}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pleomorphic histiocytes with high-grade cytology; increased mitoses; immunoreactivity for CD1a, S100, and CD207, any of which can be focal.

Desirable: clinical evidence of rapid tumour progression; molecular alteration, preferably in the MAPK pathway.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

LCS is an aggressive disease with an estimated 50% mortality rate and a median survival time of 19–27 months {12121233; 31005630; 25805533}; however, some cases may respond to targeted therapy or chemotherapy {27502715; 26468227}. Localized disease confers a survival advantage if complete surgical excision can be achieved {25805533; 33565241}.
