2.2.3.3 Myelodysplastic neoplasm with low blasts and SF3B1 mutation

Definition

Myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS) with low blasts and SF3B1 mutation (MDS-SF3B1) is a myeloid neoplasm with cytopenia and dysplasia characterized by SF3B1 mutation and often ring sideroblasts.

ICD-O coding

9982/3 Myelodysplastic neoplasm with low blasts and SF3B1 mutation

ICD-11 coding

2A4Y Other specified myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative neoplasms

Related terminology

Acceptable: MDS with low blasts and ring sideroblasts.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MDS-SF3B1 occurs in the bone marrow and peripheral blood. The liver and spleen may show evidence of iron overload.

Clinical features

Patients may present with macrocytic normochromic or normocytic normochromic anaemia. Initial symptoms are usually related to anaemia (57%) and less commonly to thrombocytopenia (13%) and neutropenia (8%). Symptoms later in the disease course might be related to iron overload in heavily transfused patients.

 

Epidemiology

MDS-SF3B1 accounts for 17% of all MDS cases {28324772; 21708407}, with an incidence of 0.84 cases per 100 000 person-years {21708407}. Patients have a median age of 70–75 years {28324772}. There is a slight male preponderance. Cases with low blasts and ≥ 15% ring sideroblasts without SF3B1 mutation account for 3–4% of all MDS cases {28324772}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Ring sideroblasts are erythroid precursors with an abnormal accumulation of iron within the mitochondria, including some iron deposited as mitochondrial ferritin {26637696}. The disease is associated with heterozygous mutations in SF3B1, which encodes a core component of the U2 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein (snRNP) spliceosome that is critical for RNA splicing {21909114; 21995386}. SF3B1 mutations occur in haematopoietic stem cells {24835589; 26643973; 27856460}. Cases of MDS with ring sideroblasts and wildtype SF3B1 usually have mutations in other genes that control RNA splicing; the optimal classification of such cases is unclear at this time.

Haematopoietic progenitors display poor erythroid colony formation in vitro, mitochondrial apoptosis, and deregulated expression of genes involved in mitochondrial biology and iron metabolisms {25955609; 26242354; 25574665; 31292266}. Mutant SF3B1 activity can result in the use of cryptic 3′ splice-site generating splicing isoforms of the mitochondrial iron–sulfur cluster transporter gene ABCB7, several other mitochondrion metabolism genes, and iron homeostasis regulatory genes including ERFE. These alterations contribute to the disease phenotype, including ineffective erythropoiesis with ring sideroblasts and systemic iron overload preceding transfusions {23070040; 26565915; 31292266}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The red blood cells in the peripheral blood smear may show a dimorphic pattern, with a major population of normochromic red blood cells and a minor population of hypochromic cells. Blasts in the peripheral blood are absent or very rare. Most cases do not show dysplastic features in granulocytes {32347921}.

The bone marrow is typically hypercellular and shows erythroid predominance. Erythroid precursors are dysplastic, often with left-shift maturation. Staining for iron typically shows increased numbers of ring sideroblasts, which are defined by having ≥ 5 iron granules encircling at least one third of the nucleus {18838480; 22421409; 32347921}. The granulocytic lineage may show dysplastic changes, but megakaryocytic dysplasia is uncommon. Blasts constitute < 5% of bone marrow cells.

Erythroid-lineage cells can be highlighted using immunostains such as CD71, and the increase in immature erythroid cells can be highlighted with E-cadherin and CD117 {32347921; 16498394; 33765355}. Bone marrow fibrosis is typically absent.

Differential diagnosis

Ring sideroblasts as well as SF3B1 mutation can be detected in other MDS types or other myeloid neoplasms, including acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) and MDS with increased blasts. Cases that fulfil the criteria for MDS with low blasts and 5q deletion should be classified as such, even if ring sideroblasts and/or SF3B1 mutation are identified {32347921}. In patients who present with thrombocytosis, detection of SF3B1 mutation concurrently with JAK2 p.V617F, CALR, or MPL mutations qualifies for a diagnosis of myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis {30618061}. Non-neoplastic causes of ring sideroblasts, including alcohol, toxins (e.g. lead and benzene), drugs (e.g. isoniazid), copper deficiency (which may be induced by zinc administration), and congenital sideroblastic anaemia must be excluded. Patients with congenital sideroblastic anaemia tend to present at a much younger age and with microcytic (rather than macrocytic) anaemia {22983749}.

Notably, the related term “MDS with low blasts and ring sideroblasts” should be only used when SF3B1 mutation analysis is not available and ring sideroblasts constitute ≥ 15% of erythroid precursors.

 

Cytology

See Histopathology, above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The identification of typically heterozygous SF3B1 mutations (~50% affecting codon 700, other hotspots at codons 666, 662, 622, and 625), usually at a high variant allele frequency (VAF; median: 35–43%), is required for diagnosis. Identification of ring sideroblasts constituting ≥ 15% of erythroid precursors can be used as a surrogate when molecular analysis is not possible. SF3B1 mutation is detected in 90% of MDS cases that have ≥ 5% of ring sideroblasts. The presence of a SF3B1 variant at a VAF of < 5% does not qualify for MDS-SF3B1. The presence of a mutation in a spliceosome component in a case with wildtype SF3B1 excludes MDS-SF3B1, although the term “MDS with low blasts and ring sideroblasts” (not “MDS-SF3B1”) may be used in such a context when a case has ring sideroblasts constituting ≥ 15% of the erythroid precursors. Additional studies are needed to determine the optimal classification of such cases.

Certain co-mutations with SF3B1 mutation have been suggested as adverse prognostic markers in MDS-SF3B1 patients. Initial data implicated TP53, RUNX1, EZH2, and FLT3 {32347921; 34036300}. More recent data suggest that the presence of co-mutations involving BCOR, BCORL1, NRAS, RUNX1, SRSF2, or STAG2 are associated with significantly different outcomes to mutation in SF3B1 alone [[Bernard E, Tuechler H, Greenberg PL, et al. Molecular International Prognostic Scoring System for myelodysplastic syndromes. NEJM Evid. 2022;1(7):EVIDoa2200008. Doi:10.1056/EVIDoa2200008.]].

Most cases of MDS-SF3B1 have a normal karyotype or abnormalities involving a single chromosome {33609081; 25957392; 35832562}. Cases with 5q deletion, monosomy 7 / 7q deletion, or complex karyotype are excluded {25957392}, although additional studies are needed to support this premise, particularly the impact of 5q deletion in rare cases of MDS with mutant SF3B1.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cytopenia involving one or more lineages, without thrombocytosis; erythroid lineage dysplasia; blasts constitute < 5% of cells in the bone marrow and < 2% of cells in the peripheral blood; detection of SF3B1 mutation (if SF3B1 mutation analysis is not available, demonstration of ring sideroblasts constituting ≥ 15% of the erythroid precursors); absence of 5q deletion, monosomy 7 / 7q deletion, or complex karyotype; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for AML, MDS with low blasts and 5q deletion, MDS with biallelic TP53 inactivation, MDS with increased blasts, or any type of myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

MDS-SF3B1 has the best outcome among all the MDS types {22921018; 25957392}. The presence of multilineage dysplasia has no significant prognostic impact in the presence of SF3B1 mutation {25957392; 32347921}. Most patients are stratified into lower-risk groups in the Revised International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS-R) {22740453}. Patients who depend on red blood cell transfusion have worse outcomes and a higher risk of AML transformation. Erythrocyte-stimulating agents and luspatercept are widely approved for the management of anaemia in these patients {28870615; 31914241}.

MDS with low blasts, ring sideroblasts, and wildtype SF3B1 has a less favourable overall survival and leukaemia-free survival {25957392}. The favourable outcome associated with SF3B1 mutation is lost as soon as an excess of blasts is observed {34036300}.

The spectrum of subclonal genetic alterations identified in MDS-SF3B1 and driving disease progression is limited. Additional mutations in epigenetic regulators, including DNMT3A, TET2, and ASXL1, do not affect the disease outcome. In contrast, mutations in TP53, RUNX1, and EZH2 {32347921; 34036300} are associated with a poorer outcome.
