2.1.1.1 Clonal haematopoiesis: Introduction

The clonal origin of cancer was first documented using the principle of random X-chromosome inactivation in females {14467629; 5833538}. Skewing from the expected balanced ratio of X-chromosome inactivation was documented in healthy ageing women without haematological abnormalities {8111064; 8704202}, leading to the identification of mutation-driven clonal haematopoiesis (CH) several years later {23001125}. Analysis of sequencing data from > 30 000 people identified CH as a common age-dependent occurrence in healthy people that was associated with an increased risk of haematological cancers, cardiovascular diseases, and all-cause mortality {25426837; 25426838; 25326804; 25732814}.

Because “clonal haematopoiesis” is a broad term that is applicable to numerous entities, several terms were introduced to describe clonality detected in people without a diagnosed haematological disorder {28592887} (see #32517Table 2.01). The term “age-related clonal haematopoiesis” has been used to refer to CH associated with ageing; however, it has no defined specific gene alteration or quantitative criteria {29141946}. The term “clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential” (CHIP) was coined to denote the presence of myeloid cancer–associated somatic mutations in otherwise healthy individuals without underlying myeloid neoplasia or unexplained blood count abnormalities {25931582}. A variant allele frequency (VAF) cut-off point of ≥ 2% has been proposed as a criterion for CHIP {25931582}, and this has been adopted to define CH in the present edition of the WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours. CHIP is regarded as a premalignant state, and risk factors for clonal expansion, and ultimately cancer progression, are beginning to be identified. Although screening haematologically normal individuals for CHIP outside the research setting is not routinely advocated at this stage, CHIP may be incidentally discovered in persons undergoing genetic sequencing for other conditions {29189820; 30216591; 31768066; 29678827}, including direct-to-consumer genetic tests {27365426}.

CH detected in individuals with unexplained cytopenia is designated as “clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance” (CCUS) and confers a significant risk of progression to haematological cancers. In this edition, CCUS includes any demonstrable molecular or cytogenetic clonal event detected in an individual with cytopenia lacking morphological evidence of myeloid neoplasia. It is anticipated that data will emerge in the coming years to help risk-stratify CCUS patients and identify optimal management approaches accordingly. In the absence of evidence of clonality, sustained unexplained cytopenia(s) should be referred to as “idiopathic cytopenia of unknown significance”.

The recently described CH-related disorder referred to as VEXAS syndrome (vacuoles, E1 enzyme, X-linked, autoinflammatory, somatic UBA1 mutations) {33108101; 37084382} is listed under the section on CH in this edition (see section Clonal haematopoiesis). As additional data continue to emerge on this disorder, it is likely that it might constitute a distinct disease entity/type in future editions of the WHO classification.

Scientific data on CH and CCUS are accumulating rapidly, and definitions may require updates as the field develops further. Nevertheless, the maiden inclusion of these premalignant states at the start of this volume reflects the importance of their recognition as precursors of diverse myeloid neoplasms and their links to an increasingly broad range of non-haematological diseases.

Condition

Description

Clonal haematopoiesis (CH)

Clonal outgrowth of haematopoietic cells from affected stem or progenitor cells, regardless of cause or disease state

Age-related clonal haematopoiesis (ARCH)

CH detectable in blood or bone marrow and associated with ageing without a diagnosed haematological disorder or cytopenia; does not refer to specific gene alterations or quantitative criteria reflecting clonal abundance

Clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP)

CH with somatic mutations of myeloid malignancy–associated genes detected in the blood or bone marrow present at a variant allele frequency of ≥ 2% in individuals without a diagnosed haematological disorder or unexplained cytopenia

Clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance (CCUS)

CH detected in the presence of one or more persistent cytopenias that are otherwise unexplained by haematological or non-haematological conditions and that do not meet diagnostic criteria for defined myeloid neoplasms

Idiopathic cytopenia of unknown significance (ICUS)

Sustained and unexplained cytopenia without detectable blood cancer–associated driver mutations
