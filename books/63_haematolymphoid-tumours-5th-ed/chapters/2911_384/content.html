2.9.1.1 Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms: Introduction

Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms (MDS/MPNs) is a category of myeloid neoplasms defined by overlapping pathological and molecular features of myelodysplastic neoplasms (MDSs) and myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs), often manifesting clinically with various combinations of cytopenias and cytoses. The definition of cytopenias is the same as that for MDS. This edition of the WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours includes major revisions to the diagnostic criteria for chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia (CMML) and terminology changes for other MDS/MPN types.

The most common MDS/MPN is CMML, which is characterized by sustained peripheral blood monocytosis and various combinations of somatic mutations involving epigenetic regulation, spliceosome, and signal transduction genes. The diagnostic criteria have been revised to include prerequisite and supporting criteria. Abnormal partitioning of peripheral blood monocyte subsets has been introduced as a new supporting criterion. Additional studies are needed to determine the optimal approach to classifying cases of unexplained clonal monocytosis that do not fit the new diagnostic criteria for CMML. Another change is the recognition of myelodysplastic CMML and myeloproliferative CMML as distinct disease subtypes, and blast-based subgroups have been reverted to two tiers: CMML-1 and CMML-2.

The term “MDS/MPN with neutrophilia” replaces the term “atypical chronic myeloid leukaemia, BCR::ABL1-negative”. This change underscores the MDS/MPN nature of the disease and avoids potential confusion with chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML). MDS/MPN with ring sideroblasts and thrombocytosis has been redefined according to the SF3B1 mutation and renamed “MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis”. MDS/MPN, unclassifiable, is now termed MDS/MPN-NOS; this is in line with an intentional effort to remove the paradoxical qualifier “unclassifiable” from the entire WHO classification.
