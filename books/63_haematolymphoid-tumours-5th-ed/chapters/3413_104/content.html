3.4.1.3 Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma

Definition

Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma (IDCS) is a malignant neoplasm composed of spindle to epithelioid cells showing morphological and immunophenotypic features similar to those of interdigitating dendritic cells.

ICD-O coding

9757/3 Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B31.4 Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: interdigitating dendritic cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Solitary lymph node involvement is most common, but a wide variety of extranodal sites, such as the skin, liver, gastrointestinal tract, lung, spleen, parotid gland, bone marrow, soft tissue, and pleura, can also be involved {23755890; 31741033}.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with an asymptomatic mass, although some may have systemic symptoms (fatigue, fever, and night sweats) or diverse clinical manifestations (such as hepatosplenomegaly or paraneoplastic pemphigus) {34491664}.

Epidemiology

IDCS is a rare neoplasm. It occurs predominantly in adults, with a median age of 58 years (range: 3 months to 88 years) and an M:F ratio of 1.65:1 {31741033}.

Etiology

The etiology remains unknown. Approximately 13% of reported IDCS cases have associated haematological malignancies, with chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma being the most common {21666687; 31741033}. In cases associated with t(14;18)(q32;q21)-positive follicular lymphoma, identical monoclonal IGHV gene rearrangements and IGH::BCL2 gene breakpoints are present in both neoplasms, suggesting transdifferentiation of neoplastic B cells into mature neoplastic interdigitating dendritic cells {18272816}.

Pathogenesis

Genomic alterations, including mutations and rearrangements in MAPK pathway genes (BRAF p.V600E, KRAS, NRAS, and MAP2K1 mutations, and CBL::USP2) and other mutations (TET2, FBXW7), have been reported {33904632; 25787243; 26047060}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are usually circumscribed and vaguely lobulated, with a fleshy cut surface.

Histopathology

The tumour consists of a proliferation of pleomorphic spindle to epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and indistinct cell borders, arranged in sheets, fascicles, or whorls, or displaying a storiform pattern. A paracortical distribution is often present in lymph nodes. The nuclei of tumour cells appear spindle-shaped to ovoid, with occasional multinucleated forms. They often show reniform indentation or grooves, with vesicular chromatin and distinct nucleoli {29660568}. The mitotic count is usually low (< 6 mitoses/2 mm2) but may be high in pleomorphic tumours. Small lymphocytes and plasma cells are interspersed or form small aggregates throughout the lesion.

Ultrastructurally, the neoplastic cells feature complex interdigitating processes and scattered lysozymes, but well-formed desmosomes or Birbeck granules are lacking {3338024; 11293908; 11914634}.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic cells consistently express S100 and usually do not express specific melanocytic markers. They are usually positive for fascin, CD68, lysozyme, CD4, CD45, p75-NGFR, and β-catenin {27299798}, but negative for Langerhans cell markers (CD1a and CD207 [langerin]), follicular dendritic cell markers (CD21, CD23, CD35), B or T cell–specific antigens, CD30, myeloperoxidase, cytokeratins, EBV, and KSHV/HHV8. The Ki-67 proliferation index ranges from 10% to 70% {23755890}.

Differential diagnosis

A diagnosis of IDCS can be challenging because of its rarity and its morphological similarity to various spindle cell tumours, in particular spindle cell melanoma, with which it shares overlapping morphological, immunophenotypic, and molecular features {27299798; 27680605}. The dendritic cell appearance (best appreciated with S100 immunostaining), coexistence of hyperplastic and dysplastic to overtly malignant interdigitating dendritic cells, expression of haematolymphoid markers (such as CD45, CD4, CD68), lack of melanocyte-specific antigens, and lack of ultraviolet (UV) radiation mutation signature (for non-cutaneous IDCS) may aid in confirming a diagnosis of IDCS {33904632}.

 

Cytology

Cytological preparations are variably cellular and contain dispersed single cells and/or loosely cohesive clusters of large polygonal cells with round to oval or irregular contoured nuclei. Interconnecting cytoplasmic processes are sometimes identified {15945093}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a proliferation of spindle to epithelioid cells with abundant cytoplasm and indistinct cell borders; vesicular nuclei with or without nuclear grooving; positivity for S100 (with dendritic cell processes highlighted) and one or more haematolymphoid markers (e.g. CD45, CD4, CD43); negative for Langerhans cell and follicular dendritic cell markers; lack of expression of melanocytic markers.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical course is generally aggressive, with a disease-specific mortality rate of 36%. Young age, intra-abdominal involvement, and disseminated disease are associated with a worse outcome {31741033; 23755890}.
