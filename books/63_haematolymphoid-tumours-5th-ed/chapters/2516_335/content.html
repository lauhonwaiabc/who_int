2.5.1.6 Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with FLT3 rearrangement

Definition

Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with FLT3 rearrangement are haematopoietic stem cell neoplasms associated with gene rearrangements forming a fusion gene involving the fms-related receptor tyrosine kinase 3 gene (FLT3), usually producing a myeloproliferative neoplasm and/or T-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma with associated eosinophilia, although the phenotypic presentation may be variable and diverse.

ICD-O coding

9968/3 Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with FLT3 rearrangement

ICD-11 coding

2A22 Other and unspecified myeloproliferative neoplasms

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The bone marrow and peripheral blood are commonly involved but may be spared in a subset of patients, at least at initial presentation. Extramedullary involvement is common {33990705; 32886902}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with peripheral blood abnormalities including leukocytosis, anaemia, and thrombocytopenia. Eosinophilia is common but not universal. A subset of patients present with relative and/or absolute monocytosis. Lymphadenopathy and splenomegaly may or may not be present. Lytic bone lesions may be seen on imaging studies {33990705}.

Epidemiology

The disease appears to be more common in men (M:F ratio: ~1.4:1). The median age at presentation is in the fifth decade of life (range: 2–80 years) {33990705}.

Etiology

The etiology remains unknown.

Pathogenesis

The formation of the FLT3 fusion gene leads to constitutive phosphorylation and growth factor / ligand–independent proliferation {17764812}. The most common partner gene is ETV6/12p131; however, additional partners have been reported, including BCR/22q11.2, ZMYM2/13q12, TRIP11/14q32, SPTBN1/2p16, GOLGB1/3q13, CCDC88C/14q32, ZBTB44/11q24, and MYO18A/17q12 {31511612; 30559310}. Rarely, in a subset of myeloid neoplasms, FLT3 fusion genes may be acquired during the course of the disease and occur in the setting of clonal evolution; although the therapeutic implications of the FLT3 fusion genes in this setting are important, cases with secondary acquisition of FLT3 fusion genes are excluded from this category {33990705}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Bone marrow involvement may manifest with features of myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS), myeloproliferative neoplasms (including cases with features of chronic eosinophilic leukaemia), myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms (including cases with chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia–like or juvenile myelomonocytic leukaemia–like features {17764812; 33792628}), or with increased blasts of myeloid, T-cell, or B-cell lineage in the range of acute leukaemia. Aberrant mast cells may be detected in the bone marrow in a subset of cases, rarely fulfilling diagnostic criteria for systemic mastocytosis {18663058}; these cases are best classified as myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with FLT3 fusion gene if the presence of the fusion gene is confirmed. Extramedullary involvement may manifest as T-lymphoblastic lymphoma or myeloid sarcoma, or (rarely) with mixed-phenotype features including B-cell, T-cell, or myeloid-lineage disease.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Chromosomal rearrangements involving 13q12 are typically identifiable on routine karyotyping studies; however, rare cryptic cases have been reported, particularly when the partner gene is ZMYM2 (which is also located at 13q12) {28751768; 32852395}. The presence of the FLT3 fusion gene should be confirmed using additional methods (including break-apart FISH probes for FLT3, RT-PCR, or RNA sequencing), because only a third of cases with 13q12 rearrangements involve FLT3 {33990705}. Additional chromosomal abnormalities, including complex karyotype, are common. Limited information is available on associated somatic mutations; however, mutations involving ASXL1, SETBP1, U2AF1, STAT5B, TP53, SRSF2, TET2, RUNX1, and PTPN11 have been reported in small case series. The prognostic significance of co-mutations remains unknown {33990705}. Cases with rearrangements of 13q12 not involving FLT3 are excluded from this diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a myeloid or lymphoid neoplasm, with or without associated eosinophilia, with chromosomal rearrangements leading to the formation of a FLT3 fusion gene.

Staging

Most patients are diagnosed in the chronic phase. Cases with ≥ 20% blasts at initial presentation or during the course of the disease are best classified as blast phase (either myeloid or lymphoid lineage) of myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with FLT3 fusion gene.

Prognosis and prediction

The disease is generally associated with an aggressive clinical course {32886902}. Haematological and cytogenetic responses have been achieved with small-molecule FLT3/multikinase inhibitors such as sorafenib, sunitinib, and midostaurin. Responses are often of short duration, and allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation for curative intent may therefore be considered {21705501; 24854988; 34533850}.
