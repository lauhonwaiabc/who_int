2.5.2.8 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, rare types

Definition

Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL), rare types, are acute leukaemias that express rare combinations of B-, T-, myeloid-, and megakaryocytic-lineage markers but do not meet criteria for MPAL with defined genetic alterations.

ICD-O coding

9805/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, B/T

9805/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, B/T/myeloid

9805/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, T/megakaryocytic

ICD-11 coding

2A61 Acute leukaemias of ambiguous lineage

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, B/T (MPAL-B/T); mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, B/T/myeloid (MPAL-B/T/M); mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, T/megakaryocytic (MPAL-T/Mk)

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved. Lymph node involvement is common in MPAL with T-lineage differentiation {30530780; 30117174; 34473233}, whereas liver and spleen involvement is common in MPAL-T/Mk {29935384; 30498070; 32865882}.

Clinical features

Patients present with clinical features similar to those of other acute leukaemias, typically secondary to cytopenias or extramedullary disease.

Epidemiology

MPAL-B/T and MPAL-B/T/M represent about 6% and about 3% of MPAL cases, respectively {21228332; 22581002; 29550836; 30209392; 30530780; 31661160}. MPAL-B/T and MPAL-B/T/M have similar clinical features, often occurring in children, adolescents, and young adults (median age: ~15 years), with an M:F ratio of about 1.5:1 {21228332; 22581002; 27208809; 30117174; 30209392; 30530780; 31661160}. MPAL-T/Mk is extremely rare; most reported cases are in infants, with a male predominance {29935384; 30498070; 32865882}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

MPAL-B/T and MPAL-B/T/M

Most patients have cytogenetic abnormalities {22581002; 30117174; 30530780}. Recurrent mutations have been identified in PHF6 (50%); JAK3 (40%); NOTCH1, RUNX1, ETV6, and IL7R (each ~25%); PTPN11 and TP53 (each ~15%); WT1 (10%); and DNMT3A (5%) {21228332; 27208809; 30117174; 30209392; 30530780; 34473233}. Gene fusions have also been reported, including SET::NUP214, DDX3X::MLLT10, CRBN::GABRB2, and SEMA4B::BCL11A {22581002; 30117174; 30209392; 30530780}. Overall, the genetic profiles of MPAL-B/T and MPAL-B/T/M share some features with those of MPAL-T/myeloid and early T-precursor lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, with common mutations in PHF6 and the RAS and JAK/STAT pathways {30209392; 30530780}.

MPAL-T/Mk

Most cases have cytogenetic abnormalities with a non-complex karyotype, including recurrent non-constitutional trisomy 21. CBFA2T3::GLIS2 and CBFA2T3::GLIS3 fusions have been reported {30498070; 32865882}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Blasts do not have distinguishing features in most cases. They can resemble lymphoblasts, myeloblasts, or megakaryoblasts, and they may consist of dimorphic populations {21228332}.

Blasts in most cases of MPAL-B/T express CD34 and/or KIT (CD117), along with one or more T-cell markers and one or more myeloid markers {22581002; 30117174}. CD79a and CD10 should not be considered by themselves evidence of B-lineage commitment.

Blasts in most cases of MPAL-T/Mk express CD34 and KIT (CD117), megakaryocytic markers (e.g. CD41, CD42, and/or CD61), cCD3 (often as the only T-lineage marker), myeloid markers (e.g. CD13 and CD33), and CD56. They usually lack myeloperoxidase, HLA-DR, CD1a, and TdT expression {29935384; 30498070; 32865882}.

To date, there have been no reports of B/erythroid, T/erythroid, or B/megakaryocytic MPAL.

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetic analysis and molecular assessment are needed to exclude defining genetic abnormalities such as KMT2Arearrangements and BCR::ABL1. Molecular evaluation may also be helpful to identify mutations (e.g. PHF6 and RUNX1) and fusion transcripts (e.g. CBFA2T3::GLIS2).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood expressing combinations of B-, T-, myeloid-, and megakaryocytic-lineage markers; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for MPAL with defined genetic alterations, B/myeloid MPAL, or T/myeloid MPAL; no history of exposure to cytotoxic therapy; no history of myeloid neoplasms.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

It is unclear whether clinical outcomes of these rare leukaemias differ from those of other MPAL types. The overall survival of patients with MPAL-T/Mk remains poor {22581002; 29935384; 30117174; 30209392}.
