3.4.1.2 Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour

Definition

Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour (IDCT) is a clonal proliferation of histiocytes expressing dendritic cell markers (CD1a, S100) and lacking CD207 (langerin).

ICD-O coding

9757/3 Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B31.6 & XH3ZM0 Indeterminate cell histiocytosis & Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: indeterminate cell histiocytosis; indeterminate dendritic cell histiocytosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Generalized skin eruption is the most common clinical presentation; solitary lesions often involve the arms or scalp. Extracutaneous involvement has been reported to occur in bone, spleen, lung, thyroid, parotid, and lymph node {3095403; 16115054; 17459045}.

Clinical features

IDCT is usually a skin-limited disorder manifesting with single, multifocal, or generalized papules and/or nodules. These lesions are usually soft, erythematous to brownish papules that may be ulcerated and tend to coalesce {16115054; 3401031; 26272726}. Infrequently, patients may present with single large nodules. Patients with extracutaneous IDCT present with symptoms related to the mass lesion.

Epidemiology

IDCT is rare, with about 100 cases reported in the literature {16115054}. The median age at diagnosis is 48 years; children are infrequently affected. The M:F ratio is 1.4:1 {2845833; 1856341; 8380541; 10102017}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. A small fraction of IDCT cases may represent immunophenotypically aberrant Langerhans cell histiocytosis lacking CD207 expression {16115054}. About 20% of cases are associated with haematological neoplasms such as non-Hodgkin lymphomas {21566494; 18813122} and chronic or acute myeloid neoplasms {28880462; 17459045; 28885734}. Cases associated with myeloid neoplasms represent cutaneous manifestations of the underlying (sometimes clonally related) malignancy {22895265; 21228369; 28885734}.

Pathogenesis

The cell of origin is considered to be the immature Langerhans cell or veiled cell, which expresses CD1a but not CD207 (langerin) and also lacks Birbeck granules ultrastructurally {18813122}.

Occasional cases have been reported to show mutations in KRAS (p.G12R) or KMT2A (MLL), and BRAF p.V600E has been reported in exceptional cases {21566494; 25787243; 28885734}. Cytogenetic anomalies, including der(1)t(1;1)(p13;q21), trisomy 21, and recurrent ETV3::NCOA2 gene fusion, have been reported {10102017; 18813122; 25228298; 26438513; 29070620}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cutaneous macules, papules, or large nodules are skin-coloured to somewhat darker.

Histopathology

In involved skin, the epidermis is usually spared {28880462}. The dermis and/or subcutis is infiltrated by sheets of monomorphic mononuclear cells resembling Langerhans cells, with kidney-shaped and deeply grooved nuclei and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Rarely, the cells can appear spindled {11737523}. These cells are positive for S100 and CD1a, and negative for CD207 (langerin); no Birbeck granules are recognizable ultrastructurally {18422973; 18813122}. There is often an inflammatory infiltrate at the periphery of the lesion. Eosinophils and lymphocytes may be prominent, as can reactive mononuclear or multinucleated macrophages with a CD68+, CD163+, S100+/−, and CD1a− immunophenotype {16115054}.

Extracutaneous involved sites show infiltration by similar lesional cells, which may exhibit varying degrees of nuclear atypia and mitotic activity.

Differential diagnosis

Langerhans cell histiocytosis expresses CD207 (langerin) and shows Birbeck granules ultrastructurally {19471157}. In rare cases of Langerhans cell histiocytosis with nodal involvement, CD207 (langerin) expression may be attenuated or completely absent. Despite CD207 negativity, such cases should still be diagnosed as Langerhans cell histiocytosis on the basis of the clinical, pathological, radiological, and molecular features.

Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma lacks CD1a expression (which is positive in IDCT).

Reticulohistiocytoma, characterized by histiocytes with epithelioid morphology and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, is negative for CD1a, rarely positive for S100, and positive for macrophage markers (CD68, CD163, factor XIIIa) {31955466}.

 

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analysis of BRAF may be helpful, to exclude other diagnoses.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tissue infiltration by cells morphologically reminiscent of Langerhans cells; lesional cells positive for S100 and CD1a, and negative for CD207 (langerin); absence of the typical picture of multisystem Langerhans cell histiocytosis (lytic bone lesion, pituitary dysfunction).

Desirable: Ki-67 assessment.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Upon diagnosis, it is important to exclude the involvement of other organs/tissues with whole-body imaging, complete blood counts, liver and kidney function tests, and neurological examination. In the presence of haematological dyscrasia and/or hepatomegaly or splenomegaly, a bone marrow biopsy is warranted, to exclude associated haematological neoplasms.

Most cases of cutaneous IDCT show complete remission after treatment, and some may even show spontaneous regression. As many as 25% of cases may show disease progression and/or malignant behaviour {28880462}; such cases often show atypical cytological features, a high Ki-67 proliferation index (> 20%) {28633156; 3886125; 10102017}, or an association with other haematological neoplasms {3886125; 18813122}. Outcome data on extracutaneous IDCTs are limited.

Targeted therapy for disseminated IDCT exhibiting BRAF p.V600E mutations has been reported to produce a good response {33324955}.
