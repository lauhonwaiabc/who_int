2.5.2.7 Acute leukaemia of ambiguous lineage with other defined genetic alterations

Definition

Acute leukaemia of ambiguous lineage (ALAL) with other defined genetic alterations includes acute leukaemias with mixed-lineage or ambiguous immunophenotypes harbouring newly recognized defined genetic alterations.

ICD-O coding

9805/3 Acute leukaemia of ambiguous lineage with other defined genetic alterations

9805/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with ZNF384 rearrangement

9805/3 Acute leukaemia of ambiguous lineage with BCL11B rearrangement

ICD-11 coding

2A61 Acute leukaemias of ambiguous lineage

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with ZNF384 rearrangement; acute leukaemia of ambiguous lineage with BCL11Brearrangement

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved.

Clinical features

Patients present with clinical features similar to those of other acute leukaemias.

Epidemiology

Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL) with ZNF384 rearrangement occurs in children and accounts for 20% of MPAL cases. ZNF384 rearrangement is detected in 48% of B/myeloid MPAL cases {30209392}.

ALAL with BCL11B rearrangement accounts for 10–15% of MPAL cases and as many as one third of T/myeloid MPAL cases {34103329}. Some cases have features of acute undifferentiated leukaemia.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

MPAL with ZNF384 rearrangement

Rearrangements of ZNF384 involve the entire ZNF384 coding region, loss of the C-termini of the partner genes, and translation of both the wildtype ZNF384 and the chimeric fusion protein. Partner genes include TCF3, EP300, TAF15, and CREBBP {30209392}. Rare indel mutations of the last ZNF384 exon have also been described {34529760}. At the transcriptome levels, ZNF384-rearranged MPAL shows a continuum with B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, suggesting a biological continuum. Chimeric ZNF384 oncoproteins exhibit perturbed chromatin binding and drive transcriptional dysregulation {30209392}.

ALAL with BCL11B rearrangement

Rearrangements of BCL11B (a critical transcription factor for T-cell fate) reposition ARID1B, MYC-BENC, or CDK6 regulatory sequences upstream or downstream of the BCL11B gene. This results in enhancer hijacking and aberrantly high expression of the BCL11B protein {33876209; 34103329}. Alternatively, BCL11B expression can be driven by amplification of a 2.5 kb region downstream of BCL11B (termed BCL11B enhancer tandem amplification [BETA]) {34103329}. JAK/STAT pathway activation is usually present. The most frequently co-mutated genes in BCL11B-rearranged ALAL include FLT3 internal tandem duplication (ITD) and tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) (90%), WT1 (30%), and RUNX1 (30%). Identical genetic profiles are also observed in a subset of early T-precursor lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma and, rarely, in cases designated as acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with minimal differentiation or without maturation, suggesting a distinct subset of acute leukaemia within a shared biological continuum {33876209; 34103329}. In contrast, NOTCH1-activating mutations and CDKN2A/B loss-of-heterozygosity aberrations are nearly absent, unlike in patients with T-lymphoblastic leukaemia {21878675}. ALAL with BCL11B rearrangement has no detectable TR gene rearrangements. Overexpression of BCL11B in haematopoietic stem/progenitor cells leads to a T-cell phenotype, which is facilitated by coexpression of FLT3 ITD, suggesting an origin from a haematopoietic stem/progenitor cell {34103329}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Blasts do not have distinguishing features and may be monotonous or dimorphic. Blasts can morphologically resemble lymphoblasts, myeloblasts, or megakaryoblasts.

Immunophenotype

Blasts in MPAL with ZNF384 rearrangement express haematopoietic stem cell antigens, including CD34, HLA-DR, and/or KIT (CD117), together with B-lineage markers (e.g. CD19, CD10, CD22, cCD79) and myeloid markers (e.g. CD13, CD33, and myeloperoxidase) {30209392; 34529760}.

Blasts in ALAL with BCL11B rearrangement express haematopoietic stem cell antigens as well as T lineage–associated (e.g. CD2, CD7) and myeloid markers. Some cases show no lineage differentiation.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Techniques for detecting ZNF384 and BCL11B rearrangement include RNA sequencing and FISH.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood expressing a mixed-lineage or ambiguous immunophenotype; detection of ZNF384 or BCL11B rearrangement.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Additional data are required.
