2.5.2.5 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, T/myeloid

Definition

T/myeloid mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL-T/M) is an acute leukaemia that expresses T-lineage and myeloid-lineage markers but does not meet criteria for mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL) with defined genetic alterations.

ICD-O coding

9809/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, T/myeloid

ICD-11 coding

2A61 & XH4YB5 Acute leukaemias of ambiguous lineage & Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, T/myeloid, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved. Lymph node involvement is common in MPAL with T-lineage differentiation {30530780; 30117174; 34473233}.

Clinical features

Patients present with clinical features similar to those of other acute leukaemias, typically secondary to cytopenias or extramedullary disease.

Epidemiology

MPAL-T/M is the second most common MPAL immunophenotype, representing about 20–30% of cases after excluding MPAL with BCL11B rearrangement {21228332; 22581002; 29550836}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Genetic aberrations have been identified in MPAL-T/M. PICALM::MLLT10 rearrangement, resulting in a nuclear-localized fusion protein, is reported in 10–15% of MPAL-T/M cases, occasionally with B-lineage differentiation {22581002; 30209392; 30530780}. Other fusion genes have also been reported, including ETV6, NUP214, and BCL11A {30209392; 30530780; 33049052}. Additional genetic aberrations include WT1 mutations, which often co-occur with ETV6 and RUNX1 mutations in paediatric cases. DNMT3A and IDH2 mutations are primarily described in adults {27208809; 29991687; 30209392; 30530780}. PHF6 is frequently mutated and is often associated with some degree of B-lineage differentiation {30530780; 30117174}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Blasts do not have distinguishing features in most cases. They can resemble lymphoblasts or myeloblasts, or they may consist of dimorphic populations {21228332}.

Immunophenotype

Blasts express both T- and myeloid-lineage markers. In addition to cCD3, CD7 is expressed in the majority of cases. CD2, CD4, CD5, and CD8 may also be present {33544265; 34019867}. sCD3 expression is uncommon and may occur in a separate population of T-lineage blasts {33544265}. Myeloid-lineage markers include myeloperoxidase and other myeloid lineage–associated antigens such as CD13, CD15, CD33, CD65, and KIT (CD117). Some cases meet the criteria for monocytic differentiation and express monocytic markers such as CD11c, CD14, CD36, and CD64 {21228332; 31033213; 33544265}. Bilineage immunophenotypes (different lineages in two separate populations) and/or biphenotypic immunophenotypes (two different lineage features within a single population) can be seen in MPAL, including MPAL-T/M.

A proportion of early T-precursor lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma cases may have immunophenotypic features that overlap with those of MPAL-T/M. In the initial definition of early T-precursor lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, a threshold of < 3% was adopted to define negative myeloperoxidase expression {19147408}. Various groups have proposed alternative flow cytometry thresholds for positive myeloperoxidase expression in MPAL-T/M, ranging from 10% to 20% blasts {23325578; 23432206; 33161592}. A threshold of > 10% for myeloperoxidase positivity seems to be supported by orthogonal data {23325578}, but no consensus has been established, and studies are needed to establish a threshold in the context of MPAL.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetic analysis and molecular assessment are needed to exclude defining genetic abnormalities such as KMT2Arearrangements and BCR::ABL1. Some recurrent translocations (e.g. PICALM::MLLT10 rearrangement) can be cryptic on conventional karyotyping {24441149}. Mutation profiling can help detect mutations that have been shown to be common in MPAL-T/M, including those in RUNX1, ETV6, WT1, PHF6, and FLT3.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood expressing T-lineage and myeloid-lineage antigens; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for MPAL with defined genetic alterations; no history of exposure to cytotoxic therapy.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

MPAL-T/M is generally regarded as a high-risk acute leukaemia and is treated with intensive chemotherapy, with or without haematopoietic stem cell transplantation. In children, measurable residual disease status by flow cytometry may be predictive of relapse and survival {29720486}. Elimination of measurable residual disease may occur later during chemotherapy than for other MPAL types {29720486; 32060402} and is more similar to what is observed in patients with T-lymphoblastic leukaemia {21719599}. Although molecular and genomic alterations are common (as described above) and may confer an unfavourable {30530780; 33049052} or favourable prognosis {31118806}, additional studies are needed to establish their prognostic significance.
