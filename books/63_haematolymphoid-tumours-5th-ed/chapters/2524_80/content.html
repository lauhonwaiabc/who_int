2.5.2.4 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, B/myeloid

Definition

B/myeloid mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL-B/M) is an acute leukaemia that expresses B-lineage and myeloid-lineage markers but does not meet criteria for mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL) with defined genetic alterations.

ICD-O coding

9808/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, B/myeloid

ICD-11 coding

2A61 & XH1928 Acute leukaemias of ambiguous lineage & Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia, B/myeloid, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved.

Clinical features

Patients present with clinical features similar to those of other acute leukaemias, typically secondary to cytopenias. This MPAL type has no distinctive clinical features.

Epidemiology

MPAL-B/M without defining genetic alterations is rare. Adults and children may be affected {21228332; 25389334}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Genomic data suggest that genetic alterations acquired in a haematopoietic stem cell/progenitor with multilineage potential drive the lineage aberrancy and phenotypic diversity in MPAL independently of somatic mutations {22344248; 29167569; 30209392}. Rearrangements of ZNF384 are common in childhood MPAL-B/M (reported in 48% of cases) and involve TCF3, EP300, and CREBBP {30209392}. However, ZNF384 rearrangements were not detected in a study of adult patients {29991687}. Mutations in genes encoding transcriptional regulators, particularly PAX5 and IKZF1, are present in the majority of cases. Other common mutations include NRAS and PTPN11, resulting in activation of the RAS signalling pathway {27208809; 30209392}. Mutations in epigenetic regulators are also common, and they include MLLT3, KDM6A, EP300, CREBBP, TET2, EZH2, and ASXL1 {22581002; 30209392}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Blasts do not have distinguishing features in most cases. They can resemble lymphoblasts, monoblasts, or myeloblasts, or they may consist of dimorphic populations {21228332}.

Immunophenotype

Blasts express both B-lineage antigens (e.g. CD19, CD20, CD22, cCD79a, PAX5) and myeloid lineage antigens (e.g. myeloperoxidase, CD13, CD14, CD15, CD64, CD65, KIT [CD117]), but otherwise they show significant immunophenotypic heterogeneity {21228332; 31033213}. An inverse correlation between myeloid and lymphoid antigens and cell size is usually seen. Bilineage and/or biphenotypic lineage expression patterns can be seen in MPAL, including MPAL-B/M.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetic analysis and molecular assessment are needed to exclude defining genetic abnormalities such as KMT2Arearrangements and BCR::ABL1. Most cases have clonal cytogenetic abnormalities, including complex karyotypes {21228332; 23888868}. The cytogenetic abnormalities that have been seen in > 1 case include trisomy 4, del(6q), 12p11.2, structural abnormalities of chromosome 7, and numerical abnormalities including near-tetraploidy {8709632; 16437134; 22581002}. Exclusion of KMT2A rearrangement and other defining genetic alterations in MPAL is required.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood expressing B-lineage and myeloid-lineage antigens; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for MPAL with defined genetic alterations; no history of exposure to cytotoxic therapy.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

MPAL-B/M is generally regarded as a high-risk acute leukaemia, with outcomes that are intermediate between those of acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) and B-lymphoblastic leukaemia despite intensive therapy {25858895}. Although there is no consensus treatment regimen for MPAL-B/M, retrospective studies have demonstrated a higher remission rate with acute lymphoblastic leukaemia–type regimens in comparison with AML-type regimens in both paediatric and adult patients {27088964; 29550836; 29720486; 33230570}. Early response to chemotherapy as assessed using flow cytometry measurable residual disease evaluation may be prognostic {29720486; 32060402}. The role of haematopoietic stem cell transplant treatment remains uncertain and may vary between paediatric and adult populations {28971902; 29720486; 27088964; 32060402}. The prognostic impact of cytogenetic and molecular alterations has not been established {32060402}.
