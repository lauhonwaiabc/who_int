2.3.2.4 Acute myeloid leukaemia with maturation

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with maturation is an AML with morphological features of granulocytic maturation that lacks defining genetic abnormalities.

ICD-O coding

9874/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with maturation

ICD-11 coding

2A60.32 Acute myeloid leukaemia with maturation

Related terminology

Not recommended: acute myeloid leukaemia, M2.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This leukaemia predominantly involves the peripheral blood and bone marrow.

Clinical features

Patients generally present with weakness, easy fatigability, and other symptoms related to complications of bone marrow failure, such as anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia. There may be leukocytosis with markedly increased blasts.

Epidemiology

There are limited data using the current definition criteria.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Bone marrow biopsy sections usually show hypercellularity with infiltration of a monotonous blast population. Blasts may have azurophilic granules and Auer rods. Maturing granulocytic-lineage cells, including promyelocytes, myelocytes, and mature neutrophils constitute ≥ 10% of bone marrow cells. Variable degrees of mainly neutrophilic dysplasia may be present. Monocytes should not be increased and comprise < 20% of bone marrow cells.

Immunophenotype

The blasts usually show intermediate to strong expression of myeloperoxidase and two or more myeloid-associated antigens, such as CD13, CD33, and CD117. Blasts are often positive for CD34 and HLA-DR. They usually express markers associated with granulocytic maturation, including CD11b, CD15, and CD65. The expression of monocytic markers (e.g. CD11c, CD14, CD36, and CD64) is rare. CD7 and CD56 are occasionally expressed. Expression of CD2, CD5, CD19, c/sCD22, and cCD79a is uncommon.

Cytochemistry

Blasts are positive for myeloperoxidase and/or Sudan Black B. They are negative or show limited positivity for naphthol AS-D CAE.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Approximately two thirds of cases have a normal karyotype. The rest have either single trisomies (e.g. trisomy 8, 11, 13, or 21) or rare structural abnormalities {15527902}. Frequently mutated genes include ASXL1 (~40%), RUNX1 (~30%), STAG2 (~30%), IDH2 (~25%), and DNMT3A (~20%); less commonly mutated genes include IDH1, TET2, NRAS, SRSF2, U2AF1, SF3B1, and ZRSR2. FLT3 internal tandem duplication (ITD) and tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) mutations are present in 5–10% and 3% of cases, respectively {27285584}. It should be noted that some of the aforementioned mutations now qualify for myelodysplasia-related AML (see section Acute myeloid leukaemia, myelodysplasia-related).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood, with cytochemical evidence of myeloid differentiation and morphological features of granulocytic maturation in ≥ 10% of bone marrow cells; positive for at least two myeloid-associated markers (e.g. myeloperoxidase, CD13, CD33, CD117); monocytic-lineage cells constitute < 20% of bone marrow cells; not fulfilling criteria for AML types with defined genetic alterations; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

There are limited data specifically on prognosis and prediction in patients classified as having AML with maturation using the current definition criteria.
