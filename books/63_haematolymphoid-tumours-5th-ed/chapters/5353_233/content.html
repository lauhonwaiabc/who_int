5.3.5.3 ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphoma

Definition

ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL) is a mature T-cell lymphoma with uniform strong expression of CD30, without ALK expression or ALK rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

9715/3 ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A90.B Anaplastic large cell lymphoma, ALK-negative

Related terminology

Acceptable: anaplastic large cell lymphoma, ALK-negative.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lymph nodes and extranodal sites are affected in a 1:1 ratio {18385450}, with single extranodal sites involved in about 50% of cases and multiple extranodal sites in 26% of cases {34327343; 33560375}. Sites include soft tissue, mediastinum, bone marrow, liver, spleen, gastrointestinal tract, and breast {20660290; 23045585; 18385450}.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with stage III or IV disease, associated with B symptoms {33560375}.

Epidemiology

ALK− ALCL represents 5.5–15% of mature T-cell neoplasms {33560375; 34327343}, with some geographical variation {33560375}. Most cases occur in adults (median age: 54 years, range: 18–89 years) {33560375; 11564061; 11090048}, with an M:F ratio of 1.6:1 {33560375}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The tumour cells are CD4+ lymphocytes with features of an innate cytotoxic immune cell {31610171}. Most cases show clonal rearrangement of TR genes. Activating mutations of JAK1 and STAT3 lead to constitutive activation of the JAK/STAT3 pathway {25873174}. Oncogenic fusions involving tyrosine kinase genes other than ALK also lead to STAT3 activation {25873174; 25349176}. Rearrangements of the DUSP22 locus at 6p25.3 occur in about 20–30% of cases {24894770}, and these cases appear distinct from other ALK− ALCL cases in that they lack STAT3 activation and result in a DNA hypomethylation state leading to enhanced immunogenicity through reduced expression of PDL1 and overexpression of CD58 and HLA class II {30093402}. MSC p.E116K mutations in cases with DUSP22 rearrangement reverse the inhibitory effect of musculin over MYC {31101622}. In about 5% of cases, rearrangement of TP63 occurs as an inversion of 3q28, involving the partner gene TBL1XR1 {24894770; 22855598}. Copy-number abnormalities, including loss of 17p13 (TP53) {24004669; 18275429; 15111330}, are seen in a subset (30%) of cases {30953147}. Gene expression profiling demonstrates that the molecular signature of ALK− ALCL shows some overlap with that of ALK+ ALCL {22740451; 16288225; 24632715; 23857971; 20159827} but is distinct from that of peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS {22740451; 24632715}. Transcription factors such as IRF4 and MYC are essential for the survival of ALCL {25359993}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Involved tissues show complete or partial effacement by diffuse and cohesive infiltrates of tumour cells, which are large and pleomorphic with lobated vesicular nuclei and prominent nucleoli, similar to those observed in ALK+ ALCL, including the presence of hallmark cells. Multinucleated cells, including wreath-like cells, may also be present {10194450; 9414185; 9250148; 7655022}. A starry-sky pattern is occasionally observed, and mitotic figures are easily identified. In cases with partial lymph node involvement, a sinusoidal or perifollicular pattern may be present {24128129}.

ALCLs with DUSP22 rearrangement are characterized by neoplastic cells with a doughnut-cell appearance {26379151} and a sheet-like growth pattern with reduced numbers of pleomorphic cells. A subset of cases with Hodgkin-like morphology may show aberrant expression of ERBB4 {26463425}, whereas more anaplastic cells are seen in cases with JAK2 rearrangement {34105517}.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic cells uniformly express CD30 with a membrane and Golgi pattern, although some cases treated with anti-CD30 brentuximab vedotin may lose CD30 expression {27531242}.

By definition, ALK is negative. CD2, CD3, CD5, and CD7 are expressed in < 50% of cases, whereas CD43, a marker not confined to T cells, may be the only positive marker. CD4 is less commonly expressed (70%), and CD8 expression is rare {32032618}. Fewer than 20% of cases express T-cell receptors, mainly TCRαβ {33686426}, and occasional cases lack immunophenotypic evidence of T-cell lineage (null cell). Clusterin and IRF4 (MUM1) are expressed in most cases {12428799; 15151211; 12429802}, whereas EMA is positive in < 50% of cases. One or several cytotoxic markers, including TIA1, granzyme B, or perforin, can be detected, except in cases with DUSP22 rearrangement {24894770}. LEF1 expression appears specific for DUSP22-rearranged cases {33165091}. Nuclear phosphorylated STAT3 is expressed in approximately 50% of cases {25873174}, a feature in common with other T-cell lymphomas that reflects cytokine activation {34503066}. Markers of immune evasion, PDL1, TGF-β, and IL-10, are also frequently expressed {28344319}. Notably, EBV is absent.

Differential diagnosis

ALK− ALCL needs to be distinguished from other malignancies expressing CD30, including classic Hodgkin lymphoma rich in Hodgkin cells, embryonal carcinoma, primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma {31567132}, and myeloid sarcoma {34572893}. ALK− ALCL should also be distinguished from primary cutaneous ALCL and breast implant–associated ALCL, which are localized forms of ALCL and clinically distinct. CD15 expression raises the possibility of classic Hodgkin lymphoma or peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS {14657710}. Regional lymph nodes adjacent to primary cutaneous ALCL, lymphomatoid papulosis {22367293}, mycosis fungoides / Sézary syndrome, and breast implant–associated ALCL {29194092} may be difficult to distinguish from systemic ALK− ALCL.

 

Cytology

Neoplastic cells are large, usually 20–30 µm in air-dried smears, with abundant bluish and vacuolated cytoplasm with Wright–Giemsa staining. Nuclei are usually central and commonly lobated, with thin nuclear membranes and conspicuous single or multiple nucleoli {28715445}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

TR gene rearrangement studies reveal a clonal T-cell population in about 80% of cases. Rearrangements of the DUSP22 locus (20–30% of cases) and TP63 (~5%) and JAK1 and STAT3 mutations (~30%) {33370049; 37127078} can be detected by appropriate molecular methods {30715169}.

 

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: complete or partial infiltration of lymph node or extranodal tissue by large pleomorphic cells with lobated nuclei and distinct nucleoli, including hallmark cells; uniform strong expression of CD30; absence of ALK protein expression or ALK rearrangement; negative for EBV.

Desirable: expression of T-cell markers and cytotoxic markers, albeit with frequent losses; clonal TR gene rearrangement.

Staging

The Cotswold modification {2809679} of the Ann Arbor staging system is the standard anatomical staging system used.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of systemic ALK− ALCL is worse than that of ALK+ ALCL, but better than that of peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS using conventional chemotherapy {18385450}, with a 5-year overall survival rate of approximately 50% {33560375}. Outcomes are better when chemotherapy is combined with brentuximab vedotin {25135998}. Initial reports suggested that DUSP22 rearrangement was associated with survival comparable to that of ALK+ ALCL {28522440}, but more recent studies have refuted a survival impact of DUSP22 rearrangement {30873584; 36453104}. Poor outcomes have been observed in patients with tumours bearing TP63 rearrangements {28522440; 24894770}, TP53 deletion/mutation {24004669; 33247178; 31078446}, or overexpression of IL-2Rα {34552066}.
