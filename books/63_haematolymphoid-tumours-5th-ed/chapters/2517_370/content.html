2.5.1.7 Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with ETV6::ABL1 fusion

Definition

Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with ETV6::ABL1 fusion is a haematopoietic stem cell neoplasm associated with fusion of the genes ETV6 and ABL1.

ICD-O coding

9968/3 Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with ETV6::ABL1 fusion

ICD-11 coding

2A22 Other and unspecified myeloproliferative neoplasms

Related terminology

Not recommended: BCR::ABL1-negative chronic myeloid leukaemia; chronic myeloid leukaemia with atypical ABL1 fusions.

 

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The peripheral blood and bone marrow are invariably involved. Extramedullary infiltration, including splenic involvement, is common.

Clinical features

Splenomegaly is common. Eosinophilia is nearly universal in cases manifesting as myeloid neoplasms and less common in cases presenting as lymphoblastic leukaemia {27229714}. Leukocytosis, anaemia, thrombocytosis or thrombocytopenia, and basophilia are common {32299902}.

Diagnosis is typically made in the chronic phase and should be distinguished from B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma with ETV6::ABL1 (see section B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma with BCR::ABL1-like features).

Epidemiology

The M:F ratio is 1.9:1. Patients presenting with the myeloid phenotype are predominantly male. The median age of presentation is in the fifth decade of life {27229714; 32279331; 32299902}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Like BCR::ABL1, ETV6::ABL1 due to t(9;12)(q34;p13) leads to activation of the non-receptor tyrosine kinase ABL1, with initiation of similar downstream pathways affecting cellular survival, growth rate, and independence, as well as transforming capacity {24053143}.

The fusion gene ETV6::ABL1 cannot be the result of a reciprocal, balanced translocation, because the orientation of the genes on their respective chromosomes prohibits the formation of a functional fusion gene. The successful creation of a functional fusion gene requires that the fused genes be located originally on either the short (p) or the long (q) arms of their respective chromosomes and on similarly oriented strands (+ vs −). Alternatively, the fused genes can be on opposite arms of their respective chromosomes and on oppositely oriented strands. ETV6 is located on chromosome 12p on the + strand, and ABL1 is located on chromosome 9q on the + strand. Therefore, formation of a functional fusion gene from a reciprocal translocation is biologically implausible.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with ETV6::ABL1 may show overlapping histopathological features with chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML), including hypercellular bone marrow with an increased myeloid-to-erythroid ratio and prominent eosinophilia with or without basophilia. Other cases may resemble various myeloid neoplasms, such as myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with neutrophilia, chronic eosinophilic leukaemia, or other myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms. Megakaryocytes may show abnormal morphology (hypolobation or hyperlobation) and abnormal clustering {9365838; 12505259; 19394693; 32299902}. The disease can present in a chronic phase or with blastic transformation (myeloid or lymphoid).

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Chromosome banding analysis as the sole technique is insufficient to reliably identify cases with ETV6::ABL1 fusion, because it results from cytogenetically cryptic inversions, insertions, or complex rearrangements involving chromosomal bands 12p13 and 9q34.

Identification of additional ABL1 signals by FISH in the absence of BCR::ABL1 should prompt investigation for other ABL1 fusions if clinical and histological findings are suggestive of this disease. FISH with a combination of ETV6 and ABL1 probes, RT-PCR, and RNA sequencing are more reliable for the identification of ETV6::ABL1 {27229714}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a haematopoietic (myeloid or lymphoid) neoplasm, usually in the chronic phase; presence of ETV6::ABL1.

Desirable: cytogenetic testing for t(9;12)(q34;p13) or complex aberrations involving other chromosomes.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm with ETV6::ABL1 fusion typically has a poor prognosis, with an aggressive natural course if left untreated {32279331}. Complete and durable remissions have been reported with ABL1 inhibitors (e.g. dasatinib, nilotinib, imatinib, bosutinib, and ponatinib) {32299902; 32279331}.
