2.3.2.3 Acute myeloid leukaemia without maturation

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) without maturation is an AML with myeloid differentiation and maturation arrest that lacks defining genetic abnormalities.

ICD-O coding

9873/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia without maturation

ICD-11 coding

2A60.31 Acute myeloid leukaemia without maturation

Related terminology

Not recommended: acute myeloid leukaemia, M1.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The blood and bone marrow are always involved.

Clinical features

Patients generally present with weakness, easy fatigability, and other symptoms related to complications of bone marrow failure, such as anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia. There may be leukocytosis with markedly increased blasts.

Epidemiology

There are limited data using the current definition criteria.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The bone marrow is usually markedly hypercellular, although exceptions may occur. The blasts are medium-sized to large, with high N:C ratios, pale grey-blue cytoplasm, round or slightly indented nuclei, and one to two distinct nucleoli. Some cases are characterized by blasts with azurophilic granules and/or Auer rods. In other cases, blasts resemble lymphoblasts and lack azurophilic granules. A defining feature of this AML type is the presence of granulocytic maturation, albeit such cells should by convention constitute < 10% of cellular elements in the bone marrow.

Immunophenotype

The blasts express cytoplasmic myeloperoxidase (≥ 3%) and two or more myeloid-associated antigens such as CD13, CD33, and CD117. Blasts are often positive for CD34 and HLA-DR. Markers of granulocytic maturation (e.g. CD15, CD65) and monocytic differentiation (e.g. CD11c, CD14, CD36, and CD64) are usually negative {10637492; 10910899; 12646939; 15875082; 18803279}. CD7 and/or CD56 expression is detected in as many as one third of cases. Expression of CD2, CD5, CD19, c/sCD22, and cCD79a is rare {10637492; 10910899; 12646939; 15875082; 18803279}. CD3 is typically negative.

Cytochemistry

Blasts are positive for myeloperoxidase and/or Sudan Black B (≥ 3%). They are usually negative for naphthol AS-D CAE.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Approximately two thirds of cases have a normal karyotype. The rest have either single trisomies (e.g. trisomy 8, 11, 13, or 21) or rare structural abnormalities {15527902}. Frequently mutated genes include DNMT3A (~30%), RUNX1 (~30%), ASXL1 (~25%), IDH2 (~25%), and IDH1 (~20%); less commonly mutated genes include TET2, STAG2, NRAS, SRSF2, U2AF1, SF3B1, and ZRSR2. FLT3 internal tandem duplication (ITD) and tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) mutations are present in 15% and 10% of cases, respectively {27285584; 29961066}. It should be noted that some of the aforementioned mutations now qualify for myelodysplasia-related AML (see section Acute myeloid leukaemia, myelodysplasia-related).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood, with cytochemical evidence of myeloid differentiation (≥ 3%) and limited (< 10%) morphological features of granulocytic maturation; positive for at least two myeloid-associated markers (e.g. CD13, CD33, CD117); not fulfilling criteria for AML types with defined genetic alterations; not fulfilling diagnostic criteria for myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

There are limited data specifically on prognosis and prediction in patients classified as having AML without maturation using the current definition criteria.
