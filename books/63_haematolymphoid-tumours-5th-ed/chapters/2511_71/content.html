2.5.1.1 Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase gene fusions: Introduction

This disease category includes myeloid and lymphoid neoplasms driven by rearrangements involving genes encoding specific tyrosine kinases leading to fusion products in which the kinase domain is constitutively activated, resulting in cell signalling dysregulation that promotes proliferation and survival. The use of the terms “rearrangement” and “fusion” is based on the principles defined in the introduction to this volume.

Newly recognized members of this category include myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with JAK2 rearrangement, myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with FLT3 rearrangement, and myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with ETV6::ABL1 fusion. A placeholder has been created for cases with other rare and as yet insufficiently defined alterations involving other tyrosine kinase gene fusions. The various currently defined entities may resemble myeloproliferative neoplasms, myelodysplastic neoplasms, systemic mastocytosis, or de novo or secondary acute leukaemias/lymphomas of mixed or ambiguous phenotype. Eosinophilia is often a presenting feature, but not invariably so. Specific genetic alterations have served as a basis for targeted treatment approaches that have dramatically altered clinical outcome in some groups of patients.

Treatment with imatinib has significantly improved the overall prognosis for PDGFRA- and PDGFRB-rearranged neoplasms. Patients with FGFR1, JAK2, and FLT3 rearrangements and ETV6::ABL1 fusion are more variably sensitive to currently available tyrosine kinase inhibitors and still generally experience a more aggressive disease course. Allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation is still considered essential for achieving long-term disease-free survival in most of these patients.
