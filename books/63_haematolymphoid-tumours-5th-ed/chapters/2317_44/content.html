2.3.1.7 Acute myeloid leukaemia with RBM15::MRTFA fusion

Definition

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with RBM15::MRTFA (MKL1) is a specific type of AML characterized by megakaryocytic differentiation.

ICD-O coding

9911/3 Acute myeloid leukaemia with RBM15::MKL1 fusion

ICD-11 coding

2A60.0 & XH16K4 Acute myeloid leukaemia with recurrent genetic abnormalities & Acute myeloid leukaemia (megakaryoblastic) with t(1;22)(p13;q13); RBM15-MKL1

Related terminology

Acceptable: acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia with RBM15::MRTFA.

Not recommended: acute myeloid leukaemia with RBM15::MKL1; acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia with RBM15::MKL1; acute myeloid leukaemia with OTT::MAL.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The peripheral blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and spleen are commonly involved. The disease can often arise in extramedullary sites as myeloid sarcoma.

Clinical features

Most patients present with marked hepatosplenomegaly, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and a slightly elevated white blood count, with or without peripheral blood blasts. Infants often present with extramedullary disease and fibrotic abdominal organs such as the liver; the latter may result in presentation with Budd–Chiari syndrome {32743872}.

Epidemiology

The disease is rare, accounting for < 1% of all AML cases and 10–12% of paediatric acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia cases {27114462}. It occurs more frequently in infants (first 6 months of life) and young children (aged ≤ 3 years) without trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), with a female predominance. A few adult cases have been reported {15849773; 32572949}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The RBM15::MRTFA fusion gene modulates HOX-induced differentiation and extracellular signalling pathways, which are associated with leukaemogenesis {11344311; 26186939}. However, in an RBM15::MRTFA knock-in mouse model, the fusion gene resulted in abnormal megakaryopoiesis but was not sufficient to induce leukaemia {19287095}, suggesting that additional mechanisms contribute to the development of AML. A case associated with a TERT promoter variant has been reported {32743872}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not applicable

Histopathology

Blasts have megakaryocytic differentiation and may or may not exceed 20% of bone marrow cellularity. Megakaryoblasts are usually medium-sized to large, with a round, irregular, or convoluted nucleus containing several nucleoli and fine reticular chromatin. The cytoplasm is often agranular and basophilic, and it may have blebs. Micromegakaryocytes are common, but dysplasia of granulocytic and erythroid lineages is usually absent. Megakaryoblasts may be mixed with more morphologically undifferentiated blasts with a high N:C ratio, resembling lymphoblasts.

The bone marrow may be normocellular or hypercellular, usually with reticulin and collagenous fibrosis. Fibrosis often results in limited bone marrow aspirates and could lead to a falsely low blast count; therefore, a bone marrow trephine biopsy is required for full evaluation.

Immunophenotype

Blasts and megakaryocytes express one or more platelet glycoproteins: CD41, CD61, and/or CD42b. Cytoplasmic CD41 or CD61 is more specific and sensitive than surface staining. CD36 is typically positive but not specific. Blasts can be positive for CD13 and CD33, whereas CD34, CD45, HLA-DR, myeloperoxidase, TdT, and lymphoid markers are negative.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cases are defined by karyotype-based evidence of t(1;22)(p13.3;q13.1) or molecular confirmation of in-frame fusion between RBM15 (OTT) at 1p13.3 and MRTFA (MKL1) at 22q13.1. The oncogenic fusion of RBM15 and MRTFA on the derivative chromosome 22 produces a transcript encoding all of the putative functional domains of both genes {11431691}. Most cases have t(1;22)(p13.3;q13.1) as the sole karyotype abnormality at diagnosis. Less frequently, and typically in older infants (aged > 6 months), a hyperdiploid karyotype with t(1;22) and +der(1)t(1;22) can be observed {1859887}. Paediatric non–Down syndrome acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia may also arise from chimeric oncogenes and cooperating mutations that are mutually exclusive with RBM15::MRTFA {27114462; 28112737}.

The high incidence of myelofibrosis may complicate cytogenetic diagnosis based on conventional karyotyping; therefore, other tests (e.g. FISH) to detect RBM15::MRTFA or its fusion product may be required for an accurate diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a myeloid neoplasm with increased peripheral blood and/or bone marrow blasts (may be < 20%); detection of RBM15::MRTFA fusion by FISH and/or RT-PCR or a similar molecular technique.

Desirable: detection of t(1;22)(p13.3;q13.1) by karyotype analysis; demonstration of megakaryocytic differentiation.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is not uniform. Two studies showed intermediate overall survival and poor event-free survival {25913479; 26215111}. However, a large international paediatric study showed a 4-year overall survival rate of 70%, a 4-year event-free survival rate of 62%, and a 4-year cumulative incidence of relapse of 19% {27114462}. Furthermore, a hyperdiploid karyotype was an independent good prognostic factor for event-free survival but not for overall survival.
