2.5.2.2 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with BCR::ABL1 fusion

Definition

Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL) with BCR::ABL1 fusion is a de novo acute leukaemia that fulfils the criteria for MPAL and harbours BCR::ABL1 at initial diagnosis, without evidence of chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML).

ICD-O coding

9806/3 Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with BCR::ABL1 fusion

ICD-11 coding

2A61 & XH97B7 Acute leukaemias of ambiguous lineage & Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with t(9;22)(q34;q11.2); BCR-ABL1

Related terminology

Acceptable: MPAL with t(9;22)(q34.1;q11.2).

Not recommended: Philadelphia chromosome–positive (Ph+) MPAL.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Bone marrow and peripheral blood are always involved.

Clinical features

The limited number of studies of MPAL with BCR::ABL1 show patients frequently presenting with leukocytosis and variable degrees of anaemia and thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly, and/or hepatomegaly {22015493; 24750307}.

Epidemiology

MPAL with BCR::ABL1 fusion accounts for < 0.5% of all cases of acute leukaemia and 15–20% of all cases of MPAL, making BCR::ABL1 fusion the most common cytogenetic abnormality in MPAL. Most patients are adults, and there is a male predominance {21228332; 22015493; 22581002; 25389334; 32419244}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The chimeric BCR::ABL1 fusion protein is a constitutively active tyrosine kinase. The reported frequencies of p210 and p190 BCR::ABL1 transcripts vary, with one study showing p190 as more common {22015493}, and another showing p210 predominance {24750307}. Additional cytogenetic abnormalities are observed in 13–30% of patients {22015493; 24750307}, compared with 50–60% of patients with acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with BCR::ABL1 {17369142; 22691121; 23521501; 33433047} and 70–80% of patients with blast-phase CML {27560111}. Cryptic deletions within IG and TR genes, frequently accompanied by losses of IKZF1 and/or CDKN2A/B, are detected in MPAL with BCR::ABL1 by array comparative genomic hybridization. Although not seen in the myeloid blast phase of CML, these deletions are recurrent in the lymphoid blast phase of CML and AML with BCR::ABL1 {20082691; 23521501}. Other mutations are rare, based on next-generation sequencing using various gene panels applied to very low numbers of cases {29492206; 29991687; 30530780}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

There are no unique morphological features that distinguish MPAL with BCR::ABL1 from other types of MPAL. The blasts in MPAL with BCR::ABL1 may resemble lymphoblasts or myeloblasts, and many cases show dimorphic blast populations.

Immunophenotype

The vast majority of cases have a B/myeloid immunophenotype, and rare cases have a T/myeloid, B/T, or B/T/myeloid immunophenotype {21228332; 22015493; 22581002; 25389334}.

Differential diagnosis

Given that B-lymphoblastic leukaemia with BCR::ABL1 frequently expresses myeloid-associated antigens and AML with BCR::ABL1 may aberrantly express lymphoid antigens, both B-lymphoblastic leukaemia and AML with BCR::ABL1 should be considered in the differential diagnosis. Distinguishing MPAL with BCR::ABL1 from the initial mixed-phenotype blast phase of CML is often challenging. Also excluded from this category are rare cases of MPAL post cytotoxic therapy and MPAL acquiring BCR::ABL1 secondarily, later in the disease course.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Karyotype analysis identifies the t(9;22)(q34;q11.2) translocation that defines this type of MPAL; the t(9;22)(q34;q11.2) may be the sole finding or occur with additional chromosomal abnormalities {21228332; 22015493}. Alternatively, molecular methods to detect the resulting BCR::ABL1 fusion, with primers/probes designed to identify all common breakpoint variants when applicable, include dual-colour dual-fusion FISH, RT-PCR, qPCR, and RNA or DNA sequencing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts in bone marrow and/or blood with an immunophenotype that meets the diagnostic criteria for MPAL; BCR::ABL1 and/or t(9;22)(q34;q11.2) detected at initial diagnosis; no prior or subsequent evidence of CML; no history of exposure to cytotoxic therapy.

Desirable: determination of the BCR::ABL1 transcript subtype and establishment of a quantitative baseline for monitoring treatment response.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Current treatment algorithms recommend acute lymphoblastic leukaemia–directed therapy with inclusion of a tyrosine kinase inhibitor {29720486}. Treatment with contemporary regimens inclusive of a tyrosine kinase inhibitor may abrogate the historically adverse prognosis and improve survival to a level at least equivalent to that of B-lymphoblastic leukaemia with BCR::ABL1 {21228332; 24750307; 32419244; 32419244}. Markers of response and measurable residual disease have not been validated as prognostic in MPAL with BCR::ABL1.
