2.3.2.10 Acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia

Definition

Acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia (AMKL) is characterized by ≥ 20% blasts with megakaryocytic differentiation.

ICD-O coding

9910/3 Acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia

ICD-11 coding

2A60.36 Acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This leukaemia predominantly involves the peripheral blood and bone marrow. Extramedullary disease may also occur.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation is similar to that of other types of acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), with symptoms related to peripheral cytopenias. Some patients may have thrombocytosis. Some AMKL cases are associated with mediastinal germ cell tumours in young men {24831771; 28578457; 32815783}.

Epidemiology

AMKL represents 3–5% of AML. The incidence is higher in paediatric patients (4–15% of childhood AML) and rare in adults (1–2% of AML) {26186939; 28867167; 31548219}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Patients with AMKL fall into three clinical groups: children with Down syndrome (see section Myeloid proliferations associated with Down syndrome), children without Down syndrome, and adults. The majority (> 75%) of cases arising in children without Down syndrome harbour chromosomal translocations that encode fusion oncogenes that drive leukaemogenesis {28867167}. Examples include CBFA2T3 (ETO)::GLIS2, RBM15::MRTFA (MKL1), NUP98::KDM5A, and KMT2A rearrangements. In cases with CBFA2T3::GLIS2, the fusion protein is thought to be sufficient for leukaemogenesis. Other fusion proteins are not sufficient by themselves and require additional mutations for complete leukaemogenesis (as examples: NUP98::KDM5A with mutated RB1, KMT2A rearrangements with RAS mutations, and HOX gene fusion events with mutations in MPL) {28112737; 28867167; 29227476}. Paediatric cases without fusion proteins often have similarities to Down syndrome cases with focal amplification of the Down syndrome critical region (DSCR) of chromosome 21, which includes several genes involved in megakaryopoiesis (RUNX1, ERG, and ETS2), along with mutations in GATA1 (50%) and cohesin/CTCF {28112737; 31548219}. Cases that arise in adults are less well defined, with mutations usually in TP53, RB1, cohesin complex genes, splicing factor genes, ASXL1, and DNMT3A {31548219}.

 

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

On bone marrow aspirate smears, blasts vary from small to large and from round to oval, with a slightly irregular or indented nucleus with fine chromatin, and one to three nucleoli. A few scattered binucleated (occasionally multinucleated) blasts may be present. The cytoplasm is usually moderately to deeply basophilic and agranular, and sometimes has cytoplasmic blebs. Rarely, blasts may show a few azurophilic granules and vacuoles. Some cases have megakaryocytic dysplasia and micromegakaryocytes, which should not be counted as blasts. Cases with small blasts and scant cytoplasm are often indistinguishable from those in acute undifferentiated leukaemia, AML with minimal differentiation, or acute lymphoblastic leukaemia. In some cases, megakaryoblasts appear somewhat cohesive and can mimic clumps of metastatic tumour cells {2411180; 6087961; 6890405; 7496359; 11001891; 12691142; 18275433}.

Bone marrow trephine biopsy usually shows sheets of undifferentiated blasts and sometimes a mixture of blasts, micromegakaryocytes, and dyspoietic megakaryocytes, typically with varying degrees of reticulin fibrosis {2411180; 6890405; 11001891}.

Immunophenotype

Blasts are characteristically positive for megakaryocytic markers by flow cytometry, including CD36, CD41 (glycoprotein IIb), CD42b (glycoprotein Ib), and CD61 (glycoprotein IIIa). Cytoplasmic expression of CD41 or CD61 is more specific than surface expression alone and avoids false positive staining resulting from platelet aggregates and adhesion to blasts (cytoplasmic positivity should be interpreted along with surface staining, to avoid interpretation errors). Among myeloid-associated antigens, CD33 is commonly expressed, but CD13 and KIT (CD117) expression is less frequent. Myeloperoxidase is consistently negative. CD34 is positive in approximately half and HLA-DR is positive in one third to half of cases. CD45 expression is usually weak to negative. Expression of granulocytic markers (e.g. CD15, CD65) or monocytic markers (e.g. CD11c, CD14, CD64) is rare. Expression of CD4, CD7, and CD56 is common, but expression of other lymphoid markers, including CD2, CD5, CD19, CD22, and cCD79a, is rare {10910899; 12691142; 18275433}.

A subset of cases expresses a characteristic immunophenotypic signature known as the RAM phenotype {27133823; 32865882}, with strong CD56 expression, variable or moderate CD34, variable KIT (CD117), and negative expression of CD7, CD11b, CD13, CD36, CD45, CD38, and HLA-DR. This immunophenotype is distinctly associated with the CBFA2T3::GLIS2 fusion {30592296}. Immunohistological staining with von Willebrand factor is positive in biopsy sections {30991862}.

Cytochemistry

Megakaryoblasts are negative for myeloperoxidase, Sudan Black B, and naphthol-AS-D-chloroacetate esterase. They may show diffuse positivity with PAS and focal positivity with nonspecific esterase {2411180; 6087961; 7496359; 11001891; 18275433}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnoses of AMKL includes entities/types of AML with defining genetic abnormalities, especially AML with RBM15::MRTFA fusion and AML with MECOM rearrangement, and other entities/types of AML defined by differentiation. A comprehensive immunophenotype including markers of megakaryocytic differentiation usually adequately distinguishes AMKL from other types of acute leukaemia. Cases in patients with a history of chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML) or other forms of myeloproliferative neoplasm that have transformed should be classified as myeloproliferative neoplasm in blast phase.

 

Cytology

See above.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Paediatric cases with chromosomal translocations that produce fusion proteins, including CBFA2T3::GLIS2, RBM15::MRTFA, NUP98::KDM5A, and KMT2A rearrangements, are identified by a combination of cytogenetics and mutation profiling. CBFA2T3::GLIS2 is a small cryptic translocation that can be missed by routine cytogenetics.

 

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ≥ 20% blasts with megakaryocytic differentiation in bone marrow and/or peripheral blood; blasts express at least one of the platelet glycoproteins: CD41, CD61, or CD42b; criteria for other defined AML types are not met; no history of myeloproliferative neoplasm.

Desirable: evaluation for possible Down syndrome.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of AMKL depends on the molecular drivers. AMKL representing myeloid leukaemia of Down syndrome has an excellent prognosis, with high rates of long-term remission and overall survival. The prognosis of AMKL arising in other settings is more heterogeneous. Cases with fusion oncogene expression in paediatric patients have a poor prognosis; specifically, cases with the RAM immunophenotype have a high induction failure rate and an extremely poor prognosis {27133823}. Adult AMKL also has an extremely poor prognosis {28112737; 31548219; 33257285}.
