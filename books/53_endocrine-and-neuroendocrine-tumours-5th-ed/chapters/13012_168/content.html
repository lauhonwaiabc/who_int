13.0.1.2 Non-syndromic familial follicular cell-derived thyroid tumours

Definition

Non-syndromic familial follicular cell–derived thyroid tumours are a heterogeneous group of hereditary thyroid cancers characterized by a predominance of follicular cell–derived or non-medullary thyroid carcinomas.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: non-syndromic inherited follicular epithelial-derived thyroid carcinoma; familial papillary thyroid carcinoma; follicular cell–derived thyroid carcinoma; non-syndromic familial non-medullary thyroid carcinoma (NSFNMTC).

Subtype(s)

NSFNMTC is genetically heterogeneous {33495912}.

Localization

NSFNMTCs can occur anywhere in the gland and are often multifocal and bilateral, usually occurring in combination with thyroiditis and benign tumours {33495912; 19081481; 32556933; 22136209; 9395820; 11114641; 11965446; 10030330; 10372725; 18948764; 19958934; 7632152; 9024215; 11701697; 20878367; 25637073; 26711636; 33488516}.

Clinical features

Patients with NSFNMTC usually present at a younger age (on average 5 years younger) than patients with sporadic thyroid cancer (~43 vs ~48 years) {17429563; 21190444; 22136209; 11114641; 16029126; 26284028}, although there is not always a significant difference between the two groups {15564841; 19081481; 20954815; 11038215; 11965446; 22131992}. In families with NSFNMTCs, the second generation exhibits an earlier age at diagnosis (clinical anticipation), more extrathyroidal invasion, a higher recurrence rate, and shorter recurrence-free survival than the first generation {22280228; 18832444}; the possibility of bias, however, due to more frequent evaluations in the family group than in sporadic cases, cannot be excluded {11038215}. No differences in the M:F ratio have been found between sporadic and familial follicular cell–derived thyroid cancers {32556933; 11038215}.

Epidemiology

The prevalence of NSFNMTC ranges between 4.5% and 10% of all thyroid tumours {21190444; 17429563; 19081481; 20954815; 22280228}, in some series reaching as high as 13.5%, with 85% of patients having 2 affected relatives and 15% having ≥ 3 affected relatives {32556933}.

Etiology

The genetic basis of NSFNMTC remains poorly understood. Most pedigrees are consistent with the inheritance of one autosomal dominant gene with reduced penetrance and variable expressivity, supporting a monogenic mode of inheritance (see #24323Table 13.09); a polygenic mode of inheritance involving CHEK2, EWSR1, and TIAM1 has also been recently proposed {33692755}. Somatic mutations of BRAF (p.V600E), NRAS, HRAS, KRAS, RET/PTC1, RET/PTC3, NTRK1, and TERT have been identified in NSFNMTCs, so there appear to be no differences in the number or type of somatic mutations between sporadic and hereditary cases of papillary thyroid cancer {21190444; 31501191; 33488516; 25381600; 32041497; 31703344; 18310288}.

Pathogenesis

Whole-genome sequencing of NSFNMTC has shown the central role of MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT signalling pathways in this type of thyroid cancer {31614935}, which would explain its similar morphological appearance to that of sporadic thyroid cancers. In addition, dysfunction of telomeres could in some cases be a mechanism predisposing individuals to familial papillary thyroid carcinoma {31928178; 21865371; 32492864}. The coexistence of NSFNMTC with multiple thyroid nodules, including the hyperplasia–neoplasia sequence, is consistent with a multistep model of tumour progression {11114641; 9024215}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, NSFNMTC may be indistinguishable from a multinodular goitre because of its frequent multicentricity, bilaterality, and/or association with benign nodules {33495912}.

Histopathology

NSFNMTC is mainly of the papillary type, including subcentimetre cases and the classic and follicular-type subtypes, but other subtypes of papillary carcinoma, oncocytic thyroid carcinoma, and other types of differentiated thyroid carcinoma have been reported as well (see #24323Table 13.09). No distinct morphological findings differentiate these carcinomas from their sporadic counterparts in most cases, but the multifocality and the combination with benign lesions (a background of hyperplastic nodules and/or follicular adenomas) and with Hashimoto thyroiditis {26711636} suggest a hereditary origin {21455198; 33495912; 29239041}. In cases with germline PLCB1 sequence variants there is a multinodular goitre of adolescent onset with an increased risk of progression to papillary thyroid carcinoma; this multinodular goitre is typically made up of sharply demarcated nodules lined by micropapillary projections (papilloid adenomata or so-called papillary hyperplasia) in a thyroid background of normal appearance {29897006; 32189189}. Overexpression of PLCB1, HABP2, EGFR, and NOP53, with lower immunostaining for CHK2, has been detected in thyroid tumour cells, associated with pathogenic germline variants of the genes PLCB1, HABP2, BROX, NOP53, and CHEK2, respectively {29897006; 26222560; 32385852; 31703244; 32041497}.

Cytology

No specific cytological features distinguish NSFNMTCs from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The identification of pathological germline mutations in susceptibility genes is confirmatory, but some of these mutations have yet to be confirmed.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the clinical diagnosis of NSFNMTC has been defined by the presence of papillary thyroid carcinoma in ≥ 2 first-degree relatives, in the absence of previous ionizing radiation exposure and cancer syndromes {11038215; 33025555; 33175120}, but this is controversial because of the high probability of including sporadic cases; other studies, however, indicate NSFNMTC when a follicular cell–derived thyroid carcinoma appears in ≥ 3 first-degree relatives {17429563; 23734600; 32556933; 27561845; 11038215}, because the possibility of it being sporadic is therefore < 5% {16613533; 27561845}. The essential and supportive clinical criteria that can be used to identify patients with NSFNMTC are presented in #24322Box 13.05 .

Desirable: the identification of a pathogenic germline (constitutional) mutation in susceptibility genes.

Staging

See specific tumour types.

Prognosis and prediction

Although some studies have found no differences in clinical behaviour between sporadic and NSFNMTC {15564841; 16613533; 20954815}, NSFNMTC is commonly associated with a higher rate of lymph node metastases {17429563; 32556933; 22136209; 18832444; 10030330; 25637073; 26711636}, extrathyroidal extension {22136209; 22131992; 25637073; 26711636}, and recurrence {22136209; 24132694} than sporadic cases, particularly in families with ≥ 3 affected members {11038215; 11114641; 18931957; 22131992; 16479341}. Despite the more aggressive presentation of NSFNMTC, appropriate treatment leads to an outcome similar to that observed for sporadic cases {33488516}. Screening of at-risk family members allows for early diagnosis of NSFNMTCs and less aggressive initial treatment {28657510}. A less invasive surgical treatment could be considered in familial papillary carcinoma < 10 mm {25894401}. Total thyroidectomy has been indicated, however, because of increased multifocality and the presence of benign nodules with an increased risk of recurrence and/or progression to carcinoma {32556933; 26284028; 11965446; 10547601}.
