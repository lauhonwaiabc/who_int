10.0.0.7 Erdheim-Chester disease

Definition

Erdheim–Chester disease (ECD) is a clonal histiocytosis characterized by infiltration of foamy histiocytes and occasional Touton giant cells, chronic inflammation, and variable fibrosis, driven by alteration of a MAPK pathway gene.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Erdheim–Chester disease

ICD-11 coding

2B31.Y & XH1VJ3 Other specified histiocytic or dendritic cell neoplasms & Erdheim–Chester disease

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ECD is a multisystem disease where any organ may be involved. Long bones are affected in > 90% of cases. The most commonly involved endocrine organs are the pituitary and adrenal glands, whereas thyroid involvement has been described only occasionally {15509691; 26565949}.

Clinical features

Although ECD may be asymptomatic, most patients have general, inflammatory, or site-related symptoms. Endocrine involvement is frequent, and careful clinical, biological, and radiological evaluation reveals anterior pituitary dysfunction in 40–91% of patients, with diabetes insipidus in 25–50%, testicular insufficiency in as many as 53%, and adrenal infiltration/insufficiency in 6–39% of patients with ECD {26565949; 32187362}. Hypothyroidism is found in 20–28% of patients {32187362; 33119105}. Among 64 patients with ECD, no patient was free of endocrine hormonal or structural involvement {26565949}.

Epidemiology

ECD is a rare but probably underdiagnosed disease. The median age at diagnosis is between 46 and 56 years, with an M:F ratio of 3:1 {32187362}. Cases in children aged < 15 years are very rare and overlap with systemic juvenile xanthogranuloma.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ECD is a clonal neoplastic disorder driven by alteration of a MAPK pathway gene and ERK activation in nearly all cases {26566875; 32187362}. Common alterations include BRAF p.V600E mutations (in as many as 60% of cases, depending on the study) {25124781; 22879539; 24894769; 25150293}, MAP2K1 mutations (in as many as 30%), KRAS or NRAS mutations (in as many as 20%) {25150293; 26566875; 31807955}, and other genetic alterations pertaining to the MAPK pathway, with single cases exhibiting ARAF mutations or BRAF, NTRK1, ALK, or ETV3::NCOA2 fusions {26566875; 31768065}. Single cases with CSF1R mutations or RET fusions have been reported {31768065}. PIK3CA mutations can occur as single events or in combination with other alterations, mostly with BRAF p.V600E mutations (~11% of cases) {25150293}. Activating mutations in CSF1R suggest that a subset of tumours may arise from extraembryonic macrophage progenitors {31768065}, and mutant alleles in CD34-positive haematopoietic stem/progenitor cells have suggested several potential precursor pathways {28566492}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tissue samples in ECD often appear firm and sclerotic.

Histopathology

Like in juvenile xanthogranuloma, the affected tissue is infiltrated by foamy to epithelioid histiocytes with small nuclei, Touton-type multinucleated giant cells, scant lymphocytes and plasma cells, and rare neutrophils and eosinophils, accompanied by variable fibrosis.

The neoplastic histiocytes are positive for CD68, CD163, CD14, and factor XIIIa, variably positive for S100 (20–30% of cases), but negative for CD1a and CD207. About half express BRAF p.V600E mutations {29096034}. Expression of phosphorylated ERK is frequent.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Testing for potentially mutated genes (BRAF, in combination with PIK3CA, MAP2K1, and KRAS/NRAS in selected cases) should be performed with sensitive assays, because of the frequently low fraction of neoplastic cells. If this is negative, testing for fusions involving NTRK1, ALK, or ETV3::NCOA2 may reveal rare variants of pathway activation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infiltration of foamy cells positive for histiocytic antigens and negative for Langerhans cell markers; appropriate clinical and/or radiological context.

Desirable: exclusion of other lines of differentiation (epithelial, melanocytic, lymphocytic, etc.); exclusion of inflammatory lesions; mutation analysis of the MAPK pathway.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis depends on the site and extent of involvement. CNS involvement and multisystem disease are poor prognostic factors {21239701}. Therapeutic options include surgery, cladribine, immunomodulators, and molecular targeted therapies including BRAF inhibitors and MEK inhibitors {30120160; 32187362}.
