5.1.1.5 Gastrinoma

Definition

Gastrinoma of the pancreas is a functioning well-differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET) composed of gastrin-producing cells (G cells), with uncontrolled gastrin secretion causing Zollinger–Ellison syndrome.

ICD-O coding

8153/3 Neuroendocrine tumour, gastrin-producing (gastrinoma)

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH0GY2 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas & Gastrinoma, malignant

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Although > 80% of gastrinomas are located within the gastrinoma triangle, in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1), 60–90% of the tumours are in the duodenum. These duodenal gastrinomas are usually very small and may not be visible on endoscopic ultrasound or preoperative imaging studies {31528093}. Therefore, a gastrinoma that presents as a tumour adjacent to the pancreas or with predominantly peripancreatic lymph node involvement requires careful exclusion of metastatic disease from occult duodenal gastrinoma {18520436}. Pancreatic gastrinomas have no preferential localization within the pancreas {17972563; 22261919; 24298844}.

Clinical features

The typical clinical features of Zollinger–Ellison syndrome are advanced peptic ulcer disease, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain {26784901}. Hypergastrinaemia may also lead to fundic enterochromaffin-like cell (ECL-cell) proliferations. MEN1-associated pancreatic gastrinomas may lead to type 2 ECL-cell fundic neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) {33190337} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Digestive system tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 1). https://publications.iarc.who.int/579.]].

Biochemical investigations

Symptoms are caused by elevated circulating gastrin levels (> 150 pg/L) and gastric acid hypersecretion (gastric pH ≤ 2). Serum chromogranin A levels are also increased in about 90% of pancreatic gastrinoma cases {22261919; 24298844}.

Imaging

The primary tumour site and its metastatic spread are determined using conventional imaging (CT, MRI, EUS) and functional imaging studies such as SSTR scintigraphy (111In-pentetreotide scan) and 68Ga-DOTATATE/DOTATOC PET-CT {7698538; 16625862; 22261919; 24298844}.

Epidemiology

Pancreatic gastrinomas account for 4–8% of all PanNETs {17006979; 24499825}, with an estimated annual incidence of 0.5–2 cases per 1 million people. Zollinger–Ellison syndrome is the second most common hormonal syndrome associated with functioning PanNETs {24121170; 24499825}. Patients with sporadic pancreatic gastrinomas, who usually are younger than those with non-functioning PanNETs, are often in their fifth or sixth decade of life, and the male and female distributions are equal {22261919; 22525418}. MEN1-associated pancreatic gastrinomas usually arise in patients who are about a decade younger {14747767; 15527675; 22261919} [[Schmitt AM, Blank A, Perren A. Gastrinoma. In: La Rosa S, Sessa F, editors. Pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms: practical approach to diagnosis, classification, and therapy. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2015. pp. 105–8.]].

Etiology

With the exception of MEN1, there are no known pancreatic gastrinoma-specific etiological factors [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Digestive system tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 1). https://publications.iarc.who.int/579.]].

Pathogenesis

The gastrin cell hyperplasia-to-neoplasia (gastrinoma) sequence has only been identified in MEN1-associated duodenal gastrinomas {15887103; 33771883}. The pathogenesis of pancreatic gastrinomas is largely unknown. They are often sporadic. Rare cases occur in the setting of familial predisposing conditions such as MEN1, neurofibromatosis type 1, neurofibromatosis type 2 {16042772; 24961548}, or tuberous sclerosis {8161496}.

Specific MEN1 mutations have been reported to lead to abnormal menin interaction with FOXN3 (CHES1). This may be associated with a higher risk of aggressive behaviour {25210877}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pancreatic gastrinomas are usually large (mean: 38 mm) but do not have distinctive macroscopic features.

Histopathology

Pancreatic gastrinomas are often indistinguishable from other PanNETs but they tend to display trabecular and/or glandular structures. The margin of the tumour can be expansive or focally infiltrative. The stroma is normally delicate but may be prominent in large tumours.

Grading

Most are G1 or G2 tumours.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells are positive for keratins (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, CK8/18, CK19), neuroendocrine differentiation markers (chromogranin A, synaptophysin, INSM1), pancreas lineage–related transcription factors (insulin gene enhancer protein ISL1 and PDX1) {19065104; 19240184; 21739268; 23503646}, and SSTR2A {11772967; 22251942}. Gastrin expression may be focal, but the proportion of gastrin-producing cells is irrelevant for the concurrent clinical syndrome. Additional hormones that may be expressed include insulin, glucagon, PP, and serotonin.

Cytology

The cytology is indistinguishable from that of other PanNETs.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a keratin-positive well-differentiated NEN (PanNET) and elevated fasting plasma gastrin levels (> 150 pg/L); Ki-67 immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: gastrin immunoreactivity in a PanNET; gastric acid hypersecretion; duodenal ulcer.

Staging

The pathological staging of pancreatic gastrinomas, like that of all PanNETs, is based on the eighth edition (2017) of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]] and the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) cancer staging manual [[Amin MB, Edge S, Greene F, et al., editors. AJCC cancer staging manual. 8th ed. New York (NY): Springer; 2017.]].

Prognosis and prediction

Tumour stage and grade are important prognostic factors. Approximately 60% of pancreatic gastrinomas metastasize to regional lymph nodes {3421754; 22864882}. Although regional lymph node metastases do not seem to influence the prognosis too much, survival does decrease once liver metastases occur {9683758; 17939142; 22864882; 28483790}. Recently, initial fasting serum gastrin levels (≥ 20 times the upper limit of normal), PanNET size (≥ 20 mm), and multiple concurrent NETs have been also reported to be associated with overall survival in MEN1 patients with gastrinomas {31401809}.

Pancreatic gastrinomas expressing SSTR2A, either by immunohistochemistry or by imaging procedures, usually qualify for treatment with somatostatin analogues {19704057; 25014687; 28253514; 28483790; 30566620} and SSTR-targeted radiotherapy {28428192}.
