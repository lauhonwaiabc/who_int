7.1.1.3 Ganglioneuroblastoma, nodular

Definition

Nodular ganglioneuroblastoma (GNBn; composite Schwannian stroma–rich/dominant and Schwannian stroma–poor) is composed of distinct histologies (clones): intermixed ganglioneuroblastoma (GNBi) or ganglioneuroma and neuroblastoma.

ICD-O coding

9490/3 Ganglioneuroblastoma, nodular

ICD-11 coding

2A00.21 & XH77W7 Mixed neuronal-glial tumours & Ganglioneuroblastoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Primary GNBn occurs in sites associated with neural crest migration, including the adrenal medulla {14601099; 10964346}.

Clinical features

Adrenal GNBn is detected as a suprarenal mass {22137163; 28465480}. Occasionally, metastatic disease leads to the diagnosis {14601099}.

Epidemiology

GNBn is uncommon, accounting for 8% of all peripheral neuroblastic tumours filed at the Children’s Oncology Group (COG) Neuroblastoma Pathology Reference Laboratory. It is rare in infancy, and the majority of cases are diagnosed in children aged > 18 months {22137163}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

GNBn has multiple components, i.e. GNBi or ganglioneuroma (biologically favourable with advanced tumour maturation) and neuroblastoma (either biologically favourable or unfavourable with less tumour maturation) in a single mass. These are considered to represent different clones rather than focal maturation or dedifferentiation {14601099; 10964346}.

Macroscopic appearance

The neuroblastoma component, often haemorrhagic and necrotic, is typically a discrete nodule in a background of GNBi/ganglioneuroma that is solid/firm and white/yellow. Multiple nodules may be present. Sampling of all nodules for histopathological and molecular evaluation is critical for case management. The proportion of neuroblastoma and GNBi/ganglioneuroma in an individual tumour varies case by case.

Histopathology

Diagnosis requires the identification of GNBi/ganglioneuroma and neuroblastoma in a single tumour. The neuroblastoma nodule is separated from the GNBi/ganglioneuroma by a fibrous capsule, a pushing border without a definite capsule, or an infiltrative margin. Rarely, a primary site biopsy shows GNBi/ganglioneuroma with neuroblastoma detected in a metastatic site. The neuroblastic differentiation, grade, and mitosis-karyorrhexis index of each neuroblastoma nodule is essential, with categorization as “favourable histology” or “unfavourable histology” according to the International Neuroblastoma Pathology Classification (INPC; see section Neuroblastoma).

Cytology

Cytopathological smears are similar to those of GNBi {31231909}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genomic/molecular investigation is focused on the neuroblastoma component (see section Neuroblastoma). MYCN amplification is rare (2%) {22137163}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: distinct histologies (clones) of GNBi/ganglioneuroma and neuroblastoma; assessment of the neuroblastoma component as per the INPC criteria.

Desirable: genomic/molecular testing of the neuroblastoma component.

Staging

The International Neuroblastoma Risk Group Staging System (INRGSS) is recommended for clinical staging based on the presence or absence of image-defined risk factors {19047290}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for patients with GNBn is determined by the clinical, histopathological, and molecular characteristics of the neuroblastoma component {14601099; 22137163; 28465480} (see section Neuroblastoma).
