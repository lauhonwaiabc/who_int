2.2.0.1 Pituitary blastoma

Definition

Pituitary blastoma is an embryonal sellar neoplasm composed of primitive blastemal cells, neuroendocrine cells, and Rathke pouch epithelium.

ICD-O coding

8273/3 Pituitary blastoma

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y & XH5QV8 Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures & Pituitary blastoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: pituitary embryoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pituitary blastoma originates within the sella and frequently extends into the suprasellar region and invades the cavernous sinus {21805093}.

Clinical features

Cushing disease is one of the most common presenting symptoms {21805093; 24839956}. Other hormones may also be overexpressed {32339726}. Ophthalmoplegia frequently occurs due to tumour growth into the optic chiasm {21805093}.

Epidemiology

Pituitary blastoma is extremely rare, with < 20 well-characterized cases. It usually occurs in children aged < 2 years (median age: 9 months), with a slight female predominance {21805093; 24839956}. Recent evidence suggests that pituitary blastoma may also occur in young adults {32339726; 34677675}. Patients may present with or subsequently develop other DICER1-related tumours {24839956}.

Etiology

Pituitary blastoma is linked to germline and somatic DICER1 variants {24839956}.

Pathogenesis

Pituitary blastoma appears to arise due to genetic alteration of DICER1, which encodes an enzyme involved in microRNA processing; mature microRNAs in turn regulate the translation of mRNAs. Virtually all tumours harbour at least one DICER1 alteration, typically a germline loss-of-function pathogenic variant coupled with a somatic RNase IIIb hotspot missense mutation {24823459; 24839956}. In a minority of cases, the second somatic alteration may be the loss of the wildtype allele {24839956}. Defective DICER1 leads to a reduced 5p:3p microRNA ratio with altered target RNAs and PRAME gene upregulation in the retinoic acid receptor signalling pathway, as well as activation of PI3K, WNT, and NOTCH pathways {33644822}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gross examination details are largely unknown. Focal cystic or haemorrhagic changes and partial necrosis can be seen {18551299}.

Histopathology

Pituitary blastomas are composed of three cellular components: (1) large adenohypophysial neuroendocrine cells arranged in lobules or diffuse sheets, (2) cuboidal or columnar primitive Rathke pouch epithelium with rosette or gland/follicle formation, and (3) small undifferentiated blastemal cells {18551299; 21805093; 24839956}. Ultrastructural features resemble fetal pituitary gland of 10–12 weeks but are distinguished by a marked complex cellular proliferation of mature TPIT-lineage corticotroph cells (some with Crooke hyaline change) and PIT1-lineage somatotrophs in the background of adenohypophysial cells with small secretory granules that simulate null cells, and other elements including undifferentiated Rathke pouch epithelial cells and folliculostellate cells {18551299; 21805093}.

Immunohistochemistry

Neuroendocrine cells variably express POMC derivates, including ACTH, β-endorphin, MSH, and (to a lesser extent) growth hormone {18551299}. Rare LH-β {18551299} and/or FSH-β {18551299; 24823459} staining has also been reported. Unlike neuroendocrine cells, blastemal and Rathke pouch cells rarely show pituitary transcription factor expression {21805093}. EMA and keratins are expressed in various components, with stronger staining in the Rathke pouch epithelium {18551299}. Galectin-3 variably stains cellular components {18551299}. Unlike postnatal normal pituitaries, these tumours often express biomarkers of fetal pituitary including PRAME and EZH2 {33644822}. Proliferative heterogeneity varies among cases and tumour components {18551299; 21805093}. An increased Ki-67 labelling index (as high as 60%) along with p53 expression is more frequent in the Rathke pouch epithelium {18551299; 21805093; 24839956}. Necrosis can occur {21805093}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of these tumours includes sellar teratoma and other germ cell tumours or pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs) with synchronous Rathke cleft cyst {28102527; 31802354}.

Cytology

The details of intraoperative touch and/or squash preparations of these tumours are unknown.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

DICER1 variants may be considered a diagnostic molecular marker of pituitary blastoma. Germline and tumour sequencing should be performed.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: Rathke pouch epithelial glands; primitive blastomatous cells; secretory and folliculostellate adenohypophysial cells; DICER1 alterations.

Desirable: diagnosis in children aged < 24 months (although possible in young adults); Cushing disease; personal or family history of DICER1 syndrome or related tumours.

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms. A CT-era approach based on tumour size and patterns of invasion was proposed in 1973 by Hardy [[Hardy J. Transsphenoidal surgery of hypersecreting pituitary tumours. In: Kohler PO, Ross GT, editors. Diagnosis and treatment of pituitary tumors. International Congress Series No. 303. Amsterdam (Netherlands): Exerpta Medica; 1973. pp. 179–98.]]. Subsequently, a standardized assessment of parasellar tumour invasiveness based on MRI was published by Knosp in 1993 {8232800}.

There are no pathology-standardized reporting templates yet endorsed by international pathology organizations. A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognostic factors are not yet fully understood; however, the extent of tumour excision is a significant factor in survival {33236116}. In the largest series of 17 patients, 1 out of 8 deaths were related to tumour progression {33236116}. Evidence suggests the most deaths are linked to early or late treatment-related complications {24839956; 33236116}.
