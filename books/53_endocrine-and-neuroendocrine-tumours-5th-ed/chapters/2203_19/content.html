2.2.0.3 Papillary craniopharyngioma

Definition

Papillary craniopharyngioma is a solid or partially cystic, non-keratinizing squamous epithelial tumour usually localized to the pituitary–hypothalamic axis and characterized by BRAF activating mutation.

ICD-O coding

9352/1 Papillary craniopharyngioma

ICD-11 coding

2F7A.Y & XH2BF0 Other specified neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of endocrine glands & Craniopharyngioma, papillary

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Papillary craniopharyngioma arises anywhere along the pituitary–hypothalamic axis, but there is a strong predilection for intrinsic localization within the infundibulum and tuber cinereum of the third ventricle floor. They can expand into the third ventricle cavity and can be located entirely within the ventricle above an intact ventricular floor. Intrasellar involvement is not common {26113067; 26636252; 28984520; 30481321; 31435663}.

Clinical features

Primary manifestations include headache (70%) and visual deficits (63%), the latter resulting from compression of the optic chiasm, which often improves or stabilizes postoperatively but rarely worsens after surgery. Nearly all patients have some evidence of hypopituitarism that is either partial (70%) or panhypopituitarism (30%) presenting as hypothyroidism (80%), hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (56%), hypocortisolaemia (50%), and growth hormone deficiency (20%). Hyperprolactinaemia is also seen, due to stalk effect (30%). Diabetes insipidus is a primary manifestation in 25% of patients and often develops anew (70%) after surgery. Hydrocephalus is common (30%). Preoperative hypothalamic disturbances (63%) include weight gain and psychiatric and cognitive disturbances {30187175; 30240857}, as well as alterations in body core temperature and sleep–wake cycle {27903128}.

Imaging

Papillary craniopharyngiomas are often solid, but some tumours are mixed solid/cystic or predominantly cystic. They are generally spherical (not lobulated or irregular), and calcification is infrequently seen {9010523; 25339645; 30187175; 30481321}. Postcontrast T1-weighted MRI shows homogeneous enhancement, with only a small fraction showing heterogeneous enhancement. The pituitary stalk is often visible and thickened, and the hypothalamus is often below the tumour {28984520; 31435663}. Cystic lesions often have a solid, cauliflower-like nodule. Cysts are hypointense on non-contrast T1-weighted images {9010523}.

Spread

Local recurrence occurs in about 25% of patients with involvement of the hypothalamus and other vital neural and vascular structures {26636252; 31699993}. Ectopic recurrence is a rare complication that occurs along the surgical track or at other sites in the CNS via cerebrospinal fluid spread in the subarachnoid space {25311042; 30877004}.

Epidemiology

Incidence

Craniopharyngiomas constitute 1.2–4.6% of all intracranial tumours, accounting for 0.5–2.5 new cases per 1 million population per year {9761047; 21336771; 22735773; 25375987}. Papillary craniopharyngiomas account for about 10% of all craniopharyngioma diagnoses and about 12–33% of those arising in adults {2352012; 10690718; 20871436; 26636252; 30187175; 32036264}.

Age and sex distribution

Papillary craniopharyngioma is principally a disease of adults (peak incidence: 30–59 years), with tumours arising in paediatric patients only rarely {7616262; 20871436; 22735773; 30069716}. There is no reported sex predilection {7616262; 22735773}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Craniopharyngiomas have been proposed to arise from cellular elements related to the Rathke pouch / craniopharyngeal duct, which is integral to pituitary development {31699993}. SOX2+ progenitors may underlie the formation of papillary craniopharyngioma {28506993} and Rathke cleft cyst {32453714}. Rathke cleft cysts with marked squamous metaplasia and craniopharyngiomas with ciliated squamous epithelium overlap histologically, and some authors propose a spectrum of progression from the Rathke cleft cyst, to columnar craniopharyngioma, to the classic papillary craniopharyngioma {19961318}. Almost all papillary craniopharyngiomas have BRAF p.V600E mutations {24413733; 24715106; 25442675; 26927026; 28500561; 28069929; 30074466} leading to the activation of the MAPK/ERK pathway. No other recurrent mutations have been reported. Only a relatively low number of non-synonymous somatic mutations are present in papillary craniopharyngioma, when compared with large cohorts of other tumour types {24413733}. Consistent with the distinct histology and driver mutations of papillary craniopharyngioma (BRAF mutations) and adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma (CTNNB1 mutations), the tumours also display distinct methylation and transcription profiles {26927026}. Papillary craniopharyngiomas show stable genomic copy-number profiles without recurrent chromosomal gains or losses {12531965; 28069929}.

Macroscopic appearance

Although papillary craniopharyngiomas tend to be predominantly solid or mixed solid/cystic, a small fraction can also be mostly cystic, in which case they generally have a cauliflower-like solid nodule. The cyst contents are typically described as viscous and yellow {7616262}. Calcifications are generally absent. The tumours are generally circumscribed and spherical and are not widely adherent to surrounding brain tissue. The surface can have a papillary pattern.

Histopathology

Papillary craniopharyngiomas have non-keratinizing mature squamous epithelium covering fibrovascular cores or a cyst wall. Stellate reticulum and flaky and wet keratin are absent {2352012; 7616262}. Calcifications are rare. Crowding is present in the basal cell layer, but pronounced palisading is absent. Epithelial whorls and collagenous whorls are present but are distinct from those of adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma. Mitoses are infrequent. The tumour–brain interface is well demarcated, and invasive protrusions are absent {2352012}. Tumour-infiltrating neutrophils are common; T cells and macrophages are also present throughout the fibrovascular cores and tumour epithelium. In as many as one third of cases, there are single or small groups of PAS-positive goblet cells within the squamous epithelium, and in a small number of cases there are regions of ciliated epithelium. These histological features overlap with those of Rathke cleft cyst with extensive squamous metaplasia.

Malignant progression of papillary craniopharyngioma has been reported {20872276; 31785438}, but none have been tested for BRAF p.V600E mutations.

Immunophenotype

p63 is expressed in all epithelial layers {14756444}. Both high-molecular-weight cytokeratins (34βE12 [K903], CK5/6) and low- to intermediate-molecular-weight cytokeratins (CK7, CK17, and CK19) are expressed {12296753}. CK7 expression is confined to the superficial epithelial layer {11459086}. Some studies indicate that craniopharyngiomas lack CK8 and CK20 expression except in rare cells, in contrast to Rathke cleft cyst; the value for distinguishing these two lesions by cytokeratin expression is unresolved {12296753; 17652797}. Primary cilia are present in the basally oriented tumour cells near the fibrovascular stroma {27562488}. PDL1 is expressed in multiple layers of tumour cells circumferentially surrounding the fibrovascular stroma {29509940}. SOX9 is variably expressed, and the expression of SOX2 requires further assessment {28506993; 29158570}.

Nearly all papillary craniopharyngiomas have BRAF p.V600E mutations {24413733}, which can be identified across the tumour epithelium using mutation-specific antibodies, thereby distinguishing papillary craniopharyngioma from adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma and Rathke cleft cyst {25412847; 25442675; 25820214}; the latter displays antibody cross-reactivity in motile cilia {25412847}. In papillary craniopharyngioma, β-catenin is confined to the cytoplasmic membrane {24413733}. In general practice, immunohistochemistry can replace genetic analysis as an aid to determining the subtype diagnosis of craniopharyngioma {32965631}.

Grading

Papillary craniopharyngioma is regarded as CNS WHO grade 1.

Proliferation index

Ki-67 is generally confined to the basal layers {26498373; 28506993}. The proliferation index varies widely between cases and provides no prognostic information {14531547; 15146007}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of papillary craniopharyngioma includes adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma, xanthogranuloma, Rathke cleft cyst, epidermoid and dermoid cysts, and pilocytic astrocytoma. When accompanied by extensive squamous metaplasia, Rathke cleft cyst can be difficult to discriminate from papillary craniopharyngioma, because the latter can also contain ciliated columnar cells and goblet cells. Importantly, Rathke cleft cyst lacks BRAF p.V600E mutations {25412847; 25442675}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of BRAF mutation confirms the diagnosis, whereas CTNNB1 mutation suggests adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma {30074466}. Although BRAF and CTNNB1 mutations are mutually exclusive, rare examples of the coexistence of these mutations have been reported in adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma {24715106}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-differentiated non-keratinizing squamous epithelial tumour; fibrovascular cores.

Desirable: demonstration of BRAF p.V600E mutations by immunohistochemistry and/or molecular testing; absence of nuclear β-catenin immunoreactivity; absence of CTNNB1 mutation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Papillary craniopharyngiomas are less adherent to hypothalamic structures than are adamantinomatous craniopharyngiomas, which could, in theory, favour better and safer gross total removal. In contrast, there appear to be no significant differences between papillary and adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma with respect to resectability, efficacy of radiation therapy, and overall survival {7616262}. Similarly, a systematic review and meta-analysis of adult patients with craniopharyngioma (34.3% of whom had papillary craniopharyngiomas) showed no statistically significant difference in rates of recurrence between the two types of tumours whether treated with gross total resection or with incomplete resection plus radiotherapy after surgery {29267973}. Indeed, the current standard-of-care treatments for both types of craniopharyngioma are similar, but the vast majority of papillary craniopharyngiomas harbour targetable BRAF p.V600E mutations, offering promising new perspectives for the treatment of this tumour {31369091}.
