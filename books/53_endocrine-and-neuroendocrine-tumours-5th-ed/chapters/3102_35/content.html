3.1.0.2 Other congenital thyroid abnormalities

Definition

Defects in thyroid organogenesis can lead to a variety of congenital thyroid abnormalities, including agenesis (athyreosis), hemiagenesis, ectopic thyroid, and hypoplasia, collectively termed thyroid dysgenesis (TD). Dyshormonogenetic goitre (DG) is due to inborn defects in any of the proteins indispensable for thyroid hormone synthesis.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

5A0Y Other specified disorders of the thyroid gland or thyroid hormones system

Related terminology

Acceptable: congenital hypothyroidism; athyreosis; agenesis; aplasia; thyroid ectopy; aberrant thyroid; thyroid hemiaplasia; thyroid dyshormonogenesis.

Subtype(s)

Agenesis (athyreosis); hypoplasia; hemiagenesis; ectopic thyroid

Localization

Ectopic thyroid tissue is found in the base of tongue (lingual thyroid), adjacent to the hyoid bone, in the midline infrahyoid, and (very rarely) in the lateral neck. Lingual thyroid is the most common site of ectopic thyroid tissue (90% of cases). Other ectopic sites include adjacent to the mandible, trachea, oesophagus or para-oesophageal region, and inferior to the aortic arch, as well as unexpected locations such as the heart, duodenum, and gallbladder {30969714; 32871503; 31283464; 30105609; 29238947; 28648504}. Exceptionally, islands of ectopic thyroid tissue (usually comprising few follicles) may be seen in the central lymph nodes (also known as thyroid inclusions) {3281503; 26459007; 15744160; 6940550}. Because of the thyroid gland’s close proximity to other branchial apparatus derivatives, adipose tissue, salivary gland remnants, or skeletal muscle may be identified within the thyroid gland parenchyma {31869075; 32119467; 185189}. DG affects both thyroid lobes {22989466; 12114713; 27867886}.

Clinical features

Most cases of ectopic thyroid are asymptomatic; however, cases associated with compressive symptoms (dysphagia, dyspnoea, airway obstruction) occur during puberty and pregnancy. In 75% of cases of lingual thyroid, it is the only source of thyroid hormone production. Thyroid malignancies of follicular cell derivation (papillary and follicular carcinomas) have been reported in ectopic locations {30969714; 32871503; 31283464; 30105609; 29238947; 28648504; 30364552}. DG is one of the commonest causes of hypothyroidism in children and adolescents, along with chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis and iodine deficiency {30086865; 33272083}.

Epidemiology

TD is responsible for > 80% of cases of congenital hypothyroidism; the overall incidence of congenital hypothyroidism is 1 in 3500 live births {30324792}. TD is common in people who are genetically female and shows a higher prevalence in White and Hispanic people {15466939; 33723755; 12021164; 30086865}. DG is the second most common cause of congenital hypothyroidism and afflicts 1 in 30 000–50 000 live births {25332965}.

Etiology

TD is predominantly sporadic, with about 2% of cases being familial. It has been shown that some cases of TD are due to the defective function of genes regulating various aspects of thyroid development, including NKX2-1 (encoding thyroid transcription factor 1 [TTF1]), FOXE1, PAX8, HHEX, and TSHR {15466939; 33723755; 12021164; 30086865}. DG can be caused by the following: defects in iodide uptake by the follicular cells due to mutations in the gene encoding the sodium/iodide symporter (SLC5A5 [NIS]); organification defects due to mutation in TPO, DUOX2, DUOXA2, and SLC26A4 (encoding pendrin); defects in thyroglobulin synthesis, storage, or release; and defective IYD1 (DEHAL1) causing failure of iodide recycling {30086865; 33272083; 33650047}.

Pathogenesis

Agenesis (athyreosis): Total absence of thyroid follicular tissue in normal or ectopic location, due to the failed formation of the median anlage or alterations causing defects in its growth and proliferation {12021164; 15466939; 33723755}.

Hypoplasia: Reduced thyroid gland volume (~5% of total cases of TD) {12021164; 15466939; 33723755}.

Hemiagenesis: Failed development of one of the lateral lobes of the thyroid gland {12021164; 15466939; 33723755}.

Ectopic thyroid: Descent of the entire thyroid primordium, or a portion of it, is halted, leading to thyroid development in an abnormal position (between the foramen caecum and mediastinum).

Macroscopic appearance

Lingual thyroid tissue usually appears as a circumscribed mass covered by mucosa. Carcinomas in this location will show the gross characteristics of their counterparts in the normal location {30364552; 32871503} [[Swaby R. Lingual thyroid: ectopic gland in the tongue base. Arch Dis Child. 2020;105:586. doi:10.1136/archdischild-2018-316508.]]. The thyroid gland in DG is asymmetrically enlarged and shows multiple nodules and degenerative changes such as cystic change, haemorrhage, and fibrosis {12114789; 12114713; 27867886}.

Histopathology

Ectopic thyroid tissue shows normal follicular architecture intermixed with surrounding structures (skeletal muscle, adipose) native to the location. Carcinomas arising in ectopic tissue maintain their histopathological features {30364552; 32871503} [[Swaby R. Lingual thyroid: ectopic gland in the tongue base. Arch Dis Child. 2020;105:586. doi:10.1136/archdischild-2018-316508.]].

Thyroid inclusions in the lymph nodes can be diagnostically challenging {3281503; 26459007; 15744160; 6940550}. Strict criteria must be applied when diagnosing benign thyroid follicular tissue in a lymph node: identified in lymph nodes medial to the jugular vein; identified within the capsule; and composed of normal-appearing thyroid without atypical nuclei, psammoma bodies, or papillary formation. Even though normal thyroid gland tissue may be present in lateral neck soft tissues, thyroid tissue in a lymph node lateral to the jugular vein, no matter the appearance, most likely represents metastatic papillary thyroid carcinoma {3281503; 26459007; 15744160; 22674918; 5796776}. Thyroid nodules in DG are hypercellular and can show papillary, trabecular/solid, microfollicular, and microcystic architecture. The colloid is usually pale-staining. The follicular cells can show cytoplasmic clearing. Random nuclear atypia is often observed in the form of irregularly shaped enlarged and hyperchromatic nuclei, most commonly identified between nodules. The extreme hypercellularity and the random nuclear atypia can lead to a misdiagnosis of carcinoma. Nevertheless, differentiated thyroid carcinoma (usually papillary carcinoma) can arise in the background of DG {7285382; 12114789; 12114713; 27867886}.

Cytology

FNAB (usually ultrasound-guided) can help identify normal thyroid tissue and diagnose malignancy {23397793; 17119431; 7486437; 8458284}. The aspirates from nodules in DG are usually hypercellular and show a microfollicular growth pattern with focal cytological atypia {16138369; 11883139}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing on specimens from ectopic locations can help differentiate between reactive and neoplastic thyroid conditions.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Ectopic thyroid

Essential: normal thyroid follicular architecture intermixed with surrounding structures native to the location; if lymph node, must be medial to jugular vein.

Dyshormonogenetic goitre

Essential: multinodular gland showing hypercellular nodules with a microfollicular growth pattern and random nuclear atypia.

Staging

Not applicable (carcinomas in ectopic sites are not staged)

Prognosis and prediction

Ectopic thyroid has no significant functional impact, unless it is the sole tissue responsible for hormone production. Carcinomas arising in ectopic thyroid tissue carry a prognosis similar to that of their counterparts arising in the normal location.
