13.0.0.13 Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia

Definition

Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia (GCHN) is an autosomal recessive syndrome usually attributable to pathogenic germline GCGR variants and characterized by widespread glucagon cell hyperplasia of the pancreas that progresses to multifocal neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) expressing glucagon almost exclusively.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

619290 Mahvash disease; MVAH

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas

Related terminology

Not recommended: glucagon cell adenomatosis; Mahvash disease.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GCHN arises throughout the pancreas.

Clinical features

Patients present with nonspecific symptoms, such as abdominal pain, altered bowel habits, fatigue, diabetes, and/or acute pancreatitis. Serum glucagon levels are very high, but this is not accompanied by the anticipated actions of this hormone. Because of the receptor’s deficient signalling, glucagonoma syndrome is not expected. However, 1 patient with wildtype GCGR presented with glucagonoma syndrome and necrolytic migratory erythema {21763589; 29702528; 27933176}. A patient with PTH-independent hypercalcaemia has been reported {30032256}. Abdominal imaging may reveal lesions of various sizes throughout the pancreas, which may also be SSTR-reactive on PET imaging {18957496; 25695890; 18437091}. The only reported paediatric patient showed elevated levels of serum amino acids (e.g. glutamine, alanine, and arginine) at 2 months of age. During the first few years of life, the patient had anorexia and repeated emesis {30294546}.

Epidemiology

GCHN is an extremely rare disease; 14 cases have been reported to date. Both sexes seem to be equally affected. The age ranges from 7 to 68 years {29702528}.

Etiology

More than 50% of the examined patients with GCHN showed germline sequence variants of GCGR, located on chromosome 17q25 {18957496; 25695890; 18437091; 25914784}. Examinations in 1 index patient and his parents indicate that GCHN with GCGR mutation follows an autosomal recessive inheritance {25695890}. The genetic disease mechanism in patients without GCGR mutation remains unclear {25695890; 23407487}. No alterations in MEN1 or VHL have been identified.

Pathogenesis

GCHN harbours either homozygous GCGR sequence variants or at least two compound heterozygous sequence variants leading to premature stop codons or deficient protein expression. Deficient glucagon signalling in the liver causes a disturbance in a presumed feedback mechanism between the liver and the glucagon cells, which is probably mediated by amino acids {30032256}. This leads to glucagon cell hyperplasia, elevated serum glucagon levels, and finally to neuroendocrine tumours (NETs).

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically, the pancreas can be normal-sized or enlarged. On the cut surface there are multiple white-yellow nodules ranging from a few millimetres to many centimetres in diameter {18437091; 18957496; 25695890}.

Histopathology

Throughout the entire pancreatic tissue there are normal-sized and hypertrophic islets randomly distributed between microtumours, and in some cases also single macrotumours. Large tumours may undergo cystic transformation and focal calcification. The tumour cells are monomorphic and exhibit round or oval nuclei. The mitotic activity is very low. Micrometastases have been reported in regional lymph nodes but are rare {18957496; 25695890; 30032256}.

Immunohistochemically, the microtumours and macrotumours are almost exclusively composed of glucagon-positive cells, but they may occasionally also express PP {30032256}. In the hypertrophic islets, glucagon-positive cells outnumber insulin cells and somatostatin cells. All endocrine cells express SSTR2A. Ki-67 labelling is very low (< 1%) {18437091; 18957496; 25695890}. GCHN with GCGR mutation seems to have a more advanced phenotype regarding the number of hypertrophic islets and microtumours and the occurrence of macrotumours. Lymph node metastases were found in 1 patient with wildtype GCGR {25695890}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Sequencing all exons of GCGR, including splice sites, is obligatory before GCGR mutation can be excluded. To date, sequence variants of GCGR have been reported in exons 2, 4, 8, 10, 11, and 12 {30294546}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: markedly elevated serum glucagon levels; hypertrophic islets merging with multiple pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PanNETs), expressing glucagon predominantly or exclusively.

Desirable: identification of pathogenic germline GCGR variants; exclusion of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 and other syndromes associated with pancreatic neuroendocrine cell hyperplasia and microtumours.

Staging

Staging follows the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems for PanNETs.

Prognosis and prediction

To date, all reported cases of GCHN have followed a benign clinical course except one. No recurrence of the disease has been noted in patients who underwent partial pancreatectomy {25695890}. However, lifelong follow-up of these patients is indicated, because the tumours can metastasize. One patient died of severe migratory necrotizing erythema and sepsis {21763589}.
