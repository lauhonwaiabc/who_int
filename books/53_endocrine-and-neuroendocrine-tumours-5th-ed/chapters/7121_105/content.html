7.1.2.1 Ganglioneuroma

Definition

Ganglioneuroma (Schwannian stroma–dominant) is the final stage of maturation of peripheral neuroblastic tumours of neural crest origin.

ICD-O coding

9490/0 Ganglioneuroma

9490/0 Maturing ganglioneuroma

9490/0 Mature ganglioneuroma

ICD-11 coding

2A00.21 & XH03L9 Mixed neuronal-glial tumours & Ganglioneuroma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Maturing ganglioneuroma; mature ganglioneuroma

Localization

Ganglioneuroma occurs in sites associated with the development of the sympathetic nervous system {11346873; 27465021; 29369484}.

Clinical features

Most ganglioneuromas are incidental discoveries during imaging for unrelated events {25501841} and are usually non-functioning {27465021; 18375900}.

Epidemiology

Ganglioneuroma is a rare tumour; its incidence is unknown. In paediatric series, older children and adolescents are affected {29369484; 18375900; 19530234}. Adrenal ganglioneuroma is a rare incidentaloma in adults {25501841; 28506406}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Maturation from neuroblastoma to ganglioneuroma is supported by cross-talk between neural crest–derived neuroblastic tumour cells and Schwannian stromal cells. The latter are recruited by biologically favourable neuroblasts that release factors promoting Schwann cell proliferation and development {10421272; 15743800}. In return, Schwann cells may secrete antiproliferation and differentiation factors that are critical for neuronal differentiation {9788610}.

Macroscopic appearance

Adrenal ganglioneuroma is a localized, firm, yellow mass without haemorrhagic or necrotic areas. Focal calcification may be present. It can encase large vessels and may compress but not invade other vital structures {28506406}.

Histopathology

Ganglioneuroma contains abundant Schwann cells in sheets and fascicles organized and bundled by perineurial cells. Variable numbers of mature and maturing ganglion cells that differ in size and shape and may be multinucleated are distributed individually or in small clusters in the maturing subtype of ganglioneuroma. In the mature subtype of ganglioneuroma, all ganglion cells are mature and covered with satellite cells. In both subtypes, naked neuritic processes (neuropil) are not detected around the ganglion cells because they are immediately incorporated into the cytoplasm of Schwann cells {10421272}.

Cytology

Smears show a variable number of ganglion cells admixed with considerable cellular fibrillary Schwannian stroma {31231909}. There is no neuroblastoma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a dominant Schwannian stromal component; maturing and/or mature ganglion cells.

Desirable: no neuroblast clusters in neuropil (naked neurites); no neuroblastoma nodules.

Staging

In the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group Staging System (INRGSS), ganglioneuromas are typically locoregional tumours of stage L1 or L2 {19047290; 19530234; 27465021; 29369484}.

Prognosis and prediction

The International Neuroblastoma Pathology Classification (INPC) classifies both ganglioneuroma subtypes into the favourable histology group {11745303}. The prognosis is excellent, even if excision is incomplete {25501841; 27465021; 29369484}. Rarely, biopsy may miss a coexisting neuroblastoma nodule whose biological characteristics may alter behaviour (see section Ganglioneuroblastoma, nodular), and therefore a disclaimer, such as “Favourable histology based on review of limited material”, should be added.
