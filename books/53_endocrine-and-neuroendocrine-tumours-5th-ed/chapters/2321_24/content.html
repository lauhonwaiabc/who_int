2.3.2.1 Gangliocytoma and mixed gangliocytoma-PitNET/adenoma

Definition

Gangliocytoma and mixed gangliocytoma–pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenoma is a tumour composed of mature ganglion cells resembling hypothalamic neurons, in some instances admixed with neoplastic pituitary neuroendocrine cells.

ICD-O coding

9492/0 Gangliocytoma

9492/0 Mixed gangliocytoma–PitNET/adenoma

9505/1 Ganglioglioma

ICD-11 coding

2A00.21 & XH6KA6 Mixed neuronal-glial tumours & Gangliocytoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: pituitary tumour/adenoma–neuronal choristoma; PitNET/adenoma with gangliocytic component.

Subtype(s)

Mixed gangliocytoma–PitNET/adenoma; ganglioglioma

Localization

These lesions usually arise within the sella turcica, although suprasellar and cavernous sinus extension is frequent, and bone invasion has been reported. One ectopic case involved the sphenoid and was associated with empty sella {15887863}.

Clinical features

Headache and visual impairment are common at onset. Acromegaly and hyperprolactinaemia are the most frequent hormonal dysfunctions, followed by Cushing disease {27521460; 30109498; 30379651; 32706866}. The duration of signs and symptoms ranges from weeks to several years. Clinically non-functioning examples are exceedingly rare {27521460}.

Imaging

Mixed and pure forms are heterogeneously enhancing solid or solid/cystic masses. CT may reveal calcification {30109498}.

Epidemiology

Pure and mixed gangliocytomas represent < 0.5% of sellar tumours {27521460; 30109498}, with about 15% being pure forms. The incidence of gangliocytomas associated with prolactinoma is probably underestimated, because they may respond to medical treatment and therefore are not operated. Literature reviews reported a wide range of age at presentation (average: 44 years); > 75% of patients are female {25259876; 27521460; 30109498}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Transdifferentiation of neoplastic endocrine cells into ganglion cells is the most current hypothesis, based on the expression of pituitary transcription factors in ganglion cells {16699777; 17875653; 26551086; 32706866}. Cells with intermediate features between neurons and adenohypophysial cells, as well as NeuN and neurofilament expression in neuroendocrine cells, corroborate this hypothesis {7855931}. A common pituitary stem/progenitor cell {16699777} and neoplastic transformation of adenohypophysial cells induced by hypothalamic hormones are also postulated {27521460}. Cases in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 may represent multiple primary tumours (i.e. collision tumours) {28079577}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pure and mixed forms are described as well-demarcated grey tumours with a soft or heterogeneous texture {28532920; 30109498}.

Histopathology

Gangliocytoma

These tumours are composed of large mature ganglion cells in abundant neuropil. The ganglion cells often show dysplastic features with binucleation and multinucleation {7584515}. They express synaptophysin, NeuN, NSE, NFPs, and MAP2 {31635149; 34238480}. They have been reported in patients with acromegaly, where they produce GHRH {6423659}, and in patients with Cushing disease, where they produce CRH {6333843} or vasopressin {30379651}. Thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) expression is reported {30379651}.

Mixed gangliocytoma–PitNET/adenoma

These tumours can be stratified on the basis of hormonal profile and immunoprofile. Dispersed single cells or clusters of ganglion cells are admixed with neoplastic adenohypophysial neuroendocrine cells. The proportion of ganglion cells and the fibrillary matrix vary among cases and within tumours. Intermediate features between epithelial and neuronal cells are documented {28079576}.

Immunohistochemically, ganglion cells express synaptophysin, NeuN, NSE, NFPs, and MAP2, as well as one or more pituitary hormones, GHRH, GnRH, CRH, and vasopressin; expression of the pituitary transcription factors PIT1, TPIT, and SF1 has also occasionally been described. VIP {2922994}, somatostatin, and glucagon {6333843} can be observed. Gangliocytomas secreting GHRH only {6423659; 15887863; 19789938}, ACTH, CRH {25183169}, and TSH/TRH {32706866} are exceedingly rare. The neuropil is positive for synaptophysin, neurofilaments, and MAP2. The ganglion cells are negative for GFAP and cytokeratins {28079576}.

About 50% of mixed forms contain a growth hormone–producing tumour, most commonly a sparsely granulated somatotroph tumour with its characteristic fibrous bodies. Lactotroph and corticotroph cells are less frequent. FSH-β, LH-β, and α-subunit are exceptional. A case with distinct corticotroph and lactotroph tumours has been reported in a patient with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 {28079577}. The pattern of cytokeratins reflects that of conventional adenohypophysial tumours. SSTR2 can be expressed in neuroendocrine cells. Its therapeutic significance is unclear; SSTR5 is reportedly absent {27521460; 32706866}. The Ki-67 labelling index is typically < 5%.

Ganglioglioma

Occasional tumours, defined as gangliogliomas, either alone or mixed with PitNET/adenoma, contain a glial component {27068013; 30045287; 31442637; 33340839}.

Cytology

A report of 2 mixed gangliocytoma–adenoma/PitNETs describes variable proportions of ganglion cells, neuroendocrine cells, and fibrillary background {21053582}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The molecular profile has not been investigated.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: intrasellar/suprasellar location; dispersed or clustered ganglion cells and neoplastic or hyperplasic neuroendocrine cells (in mixed gangliocytoma–PitNET/adenoma); dispersed or clustered ganglion cells without an epithelial component (in pure gangliocytoma).

Desirable: fibrillary matrix resembling neuropil; expression of one or more pituitary hormones and transcription factors in the neuroendocrine component; expression of epithelial and neuroendocrine markers in neuroendocrine cells; expression of pituitary transcription factors in ganglion cells (not a consistent finding); expression of hypothalamic releasing hormones in ganglion cells.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete surgical resection can be curative with postoperative biochemical normalization; recurrence is rare even after incomplete resection {25259876; 27535625}. Response to medical treatment depends on the subtype of the neuroendocrine component {27521460}. Resistance to medical therapy is reported {19789938; 27535625}. Brain metastasis from the neuroendocrine component of a mixed gangliocytoma with a lactotroph component has been documented {11081198}.
