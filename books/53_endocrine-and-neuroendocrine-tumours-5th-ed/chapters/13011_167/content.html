13.0.1.1 Syndromic familial follicular cell-derived thyroid tumours

Definition

Syndromic familial follicular cell–derived thyroid tumours are a heterogeneous group of familial follicular cell–derived carcinomas with a predominance of non-thyroid cancer.

Localization

Syndromic familial non-medullary thyroid carcinoma is often multifocal and usually affects both lobes at presentation {33495912}.

Clinical features

This is a heterogeneous group of familial cancer syndromes in which clinical manifestations of non-thyroid lesions predominate (see #24791Table 13.08).

Epidemiology

The prevalence of PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (PHTS) is about 1 case per 200 000 population {20301661}. The prevalence of FAP is 1 in 8 000–10 000 and it affects men and women equally {18194984}; however, thyroid cancer in this setting is much more frequent in women (M:F ratio: 1:61) {29785019}. The prevalence of DICER1 loss-of-function variants in population databases ranges from 1 in 2529 to 1 in 10 600 {28748527}. Carney complex is a rare disease with an unknown prevalence {26130139; 33444222} and a female predominance {19293268}. Werner syndrome is thought to be underdiagnosed {27859906}, and its prevalence is highly influenced by founder mutations and consanguinity {8722214}. The frequency of pathogenic WRN variants was 2.12% among 9019 individuals in a recent study {27859906}; in this group, the average age of patients with thyroid cancer was 39 years and the M:F ratio was 1:1–2.3 {10189141; 20301687}.

Etiology

PHTS is caused by germline mutations in PTEN {20301661}, in other genes (PIK3CA, AKT1, or SDH genes), or by germline KLLN methylation epimutation {21979946; 32568377}; another 3–6% of individuals with PHTS have germline heterozygous SEC23B pathogenic variants, which are particularly associated with thyroid cancer {26522472; 29792313}.

FAP is secondary to germline mutations in APC that result in a truncated or absent APC protein {12057910}.

Over 70% of individuals with DICER1 syndrome have a germline loss-of-function mutation with a second, tumour-specific missense mutation in the RNase IIIb domain; most mutations are inherited, but 10–20% appear to arise de novo {29343557; 25176334; 24909177}. Intronic mutations and large deletions have also been reported {29343557; 29698806; 27441995}.

Most cases of Carney complex are due to germline heterozygous inactivating mutations of PRKAR1A, encoding the type I regulatory subunit of PKA {19293268; 33444222}; PRKAR1A c.491-492del is associated with more frequent thyroid tumours {33444222}. Deleterious PDE11A variants have been described in patients with Carney complex associated with a PRKAR1A mutation {21047926}. Germinal mutation of PRKACB has also been described in rare cases of Carney complex without PRKAR1A mutation {24571725}. In other patients with clinical criteria of Carney complex without PRKAR1A mutation, another locus has been identified at 2p16 (CNC2) {12676898}.

Werner syndrome is caused by pathogenic variants in the WRN gene, which encodes a multifunctional nuclear protein with exonuclease and helicase activities {27667302; 27859906}.

Pathogenesis

PTEN regulates cell proliferation, cell migration, and apoptosis through inhibition of AKT via the PI3K/AKT pathway; inactivation of the second copy of the gene allows deregulation of the AKT pathway with upregulation of mTOR signalling {29792313; 21194675}. Germline PTEN frameshift mutations seem overrepresented in thyroid cancer {21956414}, but no correlation between specific mutations and thyroid pathological features has been evidenced {21190448}. Germline mutations in non–PTEN pathway genes also associated with PHTS are SDH genes (SDHB, SDHC, SDHD), KLLN (epimutation), SEC23B, USF3, TTN, PTK2, and RASAL1 {21177507; 21956414; 29792313; 24712574}. Some variants in SDH genes can act as modifiers of PTEN-associated thyroid cancer risk and tumour features {21979946; 29792313}. Somatic BRAF p.V600E mutations and a large deletion in MT-ND1 (encoded by mitochondrial DNA) were found in 1 papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) and 1 oncocytic cell carcinoma, respectively, in the thyroid of a patient with PHTS {21926107}.

APC acts as a classic tumour suppressor gene, and the tumour phenotype appears when the non-mutant allele is spontaneously lost or mutated by a somatic event (second hit) {10470088}. Most germline APC gene mutations in patients with thyroid cancer arise in exon 15 (codons 463–1387), the same genomic area associated with congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium {10634400}. Codon 1061 is a hotspot for thyroid cancer and hepatoblastoma {10634400}. Mutations predominate in the 5′ side of the APC gene, between codons 308 and 935 {16680592}. Interestingly, in patients with FAP, thyroid cancer can even occur in the absence of biallelic inactivation of APC {11232035}. The truncated or absent APC protein produces an accumulation of cytoplasmic β-catenin that is then translocated to the nucleus, where it leads to the constitutive expression of WNT target genes. For this reason, strong cytoplasmic and nuclear staining for β-catenin is the hallmark of cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma, which is the type of thyroid cancer affecting patients with FAP {11092973; 19095577; 29785019}. Thyroid cancer in FAP is often multifocal, with each tumour focus usually having a different somatic APC mutation (second hit) {11106555}. Somatic RET::PTC rearrangements {9916927; 11232035; 24702198} and somatic mutations in the genes AXIN1 {29785019}, KMT2C, or KMT2D {32024448} (second hit) have also been reported. A role for estrogen and/or progesterone as tumour growth promoter(s) has also been suggested {29785019}.

DICER1 is an RNase III endoribonuclease involved in the maturation of several classes of small non-coding RNAs, such as microRNAs {29343557; 25176334; 24909177}. In DICER1 syndrome, alteration of DICER1 protein leads to aberrant posttranscriptional regulation of multiple cancer gene networks {33444222; 19293268}.

Inactivating PRKAR1A mutations lead to dysregulation of the cAMP/PKA signalling pathway, resulting in the transcription of genes containing the cAMP response element (CRE) domain and tumour formation in Carney complex {33444222}.

WRN protein is thought to be involved in the optimization of various aspects of DNA metabolism, including DNA repair, recombination, and replication; telomere maintenance; and gene expression {27667302; 26984941}. All of the variants thus far identified in patients with clinically ascertained Werner syndrome result in a null biochemical phenotype that abolishes the catalytic activities of the WRN protein {25182132}, producing genetic instability, dysfunction, and senescence in many lineages and tissues {26993153}. In Japanese individuals, follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC) has been associated with the c.3139-1G>C mutation (exon 26 skip), whereas PTC was associated with the c.1105C>T p.R369* mutation, but the genotype–phenotype relationship seems different between Japanese and White patients {10189141}.

Macroscopic appearance

In patients with PHTS, FAP, Werner syndrome, or DICER1 syndrome, the thyroid gland is macroscopically multinodular with involvement of both lobes and isthmus {33495912}.

Histopathology

In PHTS, follicular cell–derived thyroid cancer usually appears in the context of multiple bilateral cellular adenomatous nodules and follicular adenomas that support an adenoma-to-carcinoma progression sequence {10594284; 26185318; 33088792; 21190448; 20962022; 20966648; 23158187; 22431287; 29239041}. Lipoadenomas and/or numerous cellular adenomatous nodules (microadenomas) are also characteristic {10594284; 21455198; 26185318; 21921783; 20962022}. Oncocytic or clear cells, as well as a hyalinizing trabecular adenoma-like pattern, are not uncommon {10594284; 33088792; 21190448; 24379037}. Small foci of adipose tissue scattered throughout the gland and thyroiditis are also typical {21194675; 26185318; 33088792; 21190448; 22431287}. Secondary (reactive) C-cell hyperplasia (but not medullary carcinoma) has been described {15067177}. Loss of immunohistochemical expression of the PTEN protein in all nodules or in a subset of thyroid nodules is both sensitive and specific for PHTS {21921783; 29239041}; although tumour cells are negative for PTEN, positivity in normal follicular cells and endothelial cells can be used as a positive control {26185318; 32632840; 26975628; 29239041}.

Thyroid cancer in FAP is typically cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma, which is histologically characterized by a predominance of the cribriform component with a variable combination of follicular, papillary, trabecular, and solid growth patterns, with morular (squamoid) areas {7698732; 10229505; 34019236}. Tumour cells are positive for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), weakly positive or negative for PAX8, and typically negative for thyroglobulin {34019236}, HBME1, calcitonin, and CK20 {29785019; 34019236}. This is the only thyroid tumour with both nuclear and cytoplasmic reactivity for β-catenin {29785019; 34019236}. Tumour cells are also immunoreactive for pancytokeratins (AE1/AE3), EMA, vimentin, ERα, ERβ, and PR, sometimes showing focal staining for AR and PTEN {23161723; 24702198; 26054797; 29785019; 18081818}. The morular structures show strong nuclear positivity for β-catenin, as well as for CD5 and CK5 {34019236}, and selective positivity for CDX2, CA19-9, and CD10 {29785019; 23161723; 31901919}. The differential diagnosis includes breast metastatic carcinoma, metastatic colon cancer, and the tall cell and columnar cell subtypes of PTC {29785019}.

Thyroid follicular nodular disease in patients with DICER1 syndrome is characterized by nodules of follicular cells with frequent papillary growth pattern (follicular adenoma with papillary architecture), intermediate nuclei {29538609}, and involutional changes {29474644}. Most thyroid cancers are well differentiated, mainly the follicular variant of PTC and the minimally invasive FTC {29538609}, but rare cases of the solid/trabecular subtype of PTC {29884466} and high-grade follicular-derived carcinoma (poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma of childhood) have also been reported {31937902}. The multinodularity, along with the description of some cases of PTC arising within one of these nodules {26555935}, suggests a stepwise progression to malignancy {33495912; 29538609}.

In Carney complex the thyroid is typically multinodular, displaying encapsulated and unencapsulated follicular proliferations, sometimes with oncocytic change, distributed throughout the gland {9215269; 20301463; 29666959}. This follicular adenomatosis {29635258} is analogous to the so-called microadenomas or adenomatous nodules described in PHTS. Papillary hyperfunctioning adenomas and oncocytic adenomas have also been reported {29635258; 19265501}. Both PTC and FTC have been described, generally in patients with a history of multiple adenomas {20301463; 9215269; 29635258; 9415461; 22297707}. Lymphocytic thyroiditis and Graves disease have also been reported {2586567}.

The most common thyroid cancer in Japanese patients with Werner syndrome is FTC, followed by PTC and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma {10189141}; the presentation of anaplastic thyroid carcinoma at a younger age fits with the premature ageing of patients with Werner syndrome {33495912}.

Marfan syndrome {22847364}, multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 {26732163}, and multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A {12111520; 17455883} can also present with follicular thyroid tumours. An unusual histological pattern of PTC exhibiting prominent long villous fronds has been reported in Marfan syndrome {22847364}. Perhaps papillary microcarcinomas in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A may not be related to the germline mutation of the RET gene but instead are the result of the careful study of the gland {33495912}. Some cases of familial paraganglioma phaeochromocytoma syndromes caused by SDH mutations have been associated with thyroid tumours {31687641}.

Cytology

The cytological characteristics of thyroid tumours within a familial syndrome are not different from those of sporadic tumours. If suspected, β-catenin immunostaining of the cytology specimens can help to identify both the familial and sporadic forms of cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma {20091902; 19534622}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Germline pathogenic PTEN mutations cause PHTS, but rarely, other genes can be involved: PIK3CA, AKT1, SDH genes, KLLN, and SEC23B {20301661}. In FAP patients with cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma, about 85% of germline APC mutations occur in exon 15, usually outside the mutation cluster region {10634400; 29785019}. When the suspicion of DICER1 syndrome exists, the pathogenic mutation must be confirmed. The identification of a germline pathogenic PRKAR1A mutation helps to confirm the Carney complex diagnosis, but such a mutation may be absent in 20–30% of patients {19293268; 20301463}; rarely, germline mutations in PRKACB or PDE11A have been detected in some patients with Carney complex {24571725; 21047926}. The definitive diagnosis of Werner syndrome can be achieved by molecular confirmation of biallelic loss-of-function mutations in WRN {20301687}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: familial thyroid carcinoma of follicular origin usually with multiple benign bilateral follicular cell nodules (follicular microadenomas / adenomatous nodules / adenomatosis) in a patient with a non-thyroid cancer syndrome; cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma with nuclear and cytoplasmic reactivity for β-catenin in a patient with FAP; loss of immunohistochemical expression of PTEN protein in more than one thyroid tumour fits with PHTS {21921783}.

Desirable: the identification of a pathogenic germline (constitutional) mutation in susceptibility genes.

Staging

See specific tumour types.

Prognosis and prediction

Pathologists play an important role in the recognition of these syndromes, facilitating cancer screening and genetic counselling {21455198; 20301661; 20966648; 26185318; 20484290; 29239041}.

Total thyroidectomy is usually indicated, because of the frequent multicentricity of these lesions and the risk of recurrence and/or progression to carcinoma {20301661; 10594284; 23158187}.
