12.0.0.1 Metastasis: Introduction

Endocrine glands are common targets of metastases from distant sites because their high vascularity promotes the homing of metastatic deposits. Interaction of the endocrine microenvironment recipient of the metastasis with the properties of specific tumour cell types is probably equally important for the successful implant and growth of these deposits {32761606}, as shown by the spread of clear cell renal cell carcinoma to the thyroid gland {32897542} or of lobular carcinoma of the breast to the pituitary gland or ovary {8250706}.

Metastases are frequently found in patients with a previous cancer diagnosis and disseminated disease (see #27120Table 12.01). Intensive follow-up and prolonged patient survival have increased their identification in cytology or surgical pathology material {31645015}. An incidentally identified thyroid gland or adrenal gland nodule can be the first manifestation of metastases from occult neoplasms, typically clear cell renal cell carcinoma for the thyroid gland {32897542} and lung carcinoma for the adrenal gland {26121453}. By decision, metastases to the endocrine pancreas are not included here, because any metastatic deposit in the pancreas generally involves both the exocrine and endocrine pancreas. Metastases to the parathyroid glands are rare and not covered in a specific section in this volume, but they have been reported and analysed in a comprehensive literature review {28875280}.

Although endocrine organ dysfunction is rare, diabetes insipidus and adrenal insufficiency due to metastases to the pituitary or adrenal gland, respectively, are common manifestations {31645015}. Although they are excluded from the definition of metastatic disease, systemic haematolymphoid neoplasms also affect endocrine glands, and the testis is a well-known pharmacological sanctuary site in patients treated for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.

Endocrine tumours are frequent recipients of tumour-to-tumour metastases (the uncommon phenomenon of metastatic dissemination of a primary donor tumour into a recipient tumour) reported in virtually every endocrine gland {19830028; 21951669; 26687112; 31109373; 32774962}.
