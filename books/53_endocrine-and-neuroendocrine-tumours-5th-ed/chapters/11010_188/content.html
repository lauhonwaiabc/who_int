11.0.1.0 Germ cell tumours: Introduction

In this edition of the WHO classification of endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours, the classification of germ cell tumours in endocrine organs and teratomas with endocrine differentiation has been updated. The modifications and changes involve various organs and tumour entities. Germ cell tumours in endocrine organs include teratomas with mature or immature tissues derived from all three germ cell layers (endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm). Teratomas in endocrine organs are rare and found in the thyroid gland, the adrenal glands, and the pituitary gland. Most thyroid gland teratomas occur in neonates and young children, often as congenital tumours {10699906; 15832097; 26611341}. Most adrenal gland teratomas are identified in adults {31949356}. Thyroid gland teratomas that are high-grade / highly malignant by histological criteria have been reclassified as thyroblastoma, supported by the recurrent finding of DICER1 mutations, and are no longer regarded as true teratomas {27784193; 31917706; 32507920}. Thyroblastoma is discussed separately in the chapter on thyroid tumours, in the sections on embryonal thyroid neoplasms. Teratomas with endocrine differentiation that arise in non-endocrine organs include struma ovarii (teratomas with > 50% thyroid tissue or with functional or malignant thyroid tissue of any amount), thyroid carcinoma arising in struma ovarii, and ovarian neuroendocrine tumours (teratomas with unequivocal evidence of a neoplastic neuroendocrine component). Although endocrine differentiation is most often identified in ovarian teratomas, it can also arise in testicular or extragonadal teratomas {28078619}.
