5.1.1.6 VIPoma

Definition

VIPoma is a vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP)-producing pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasm (PanNEN) that causes WDHA syndrome (watery diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, and achlorhydria; also known as Verner–Morrison syndrome).

ICD-O coding

8155/3 VIPoma

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH8LS0 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas & VIPoma, malignant

Related terminology

Acceptable: VIP-producing pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour.

Not recommended: diarrhoeagenic tumour; islet cell tumour with watery diarrhoea.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most pancreatic VIPomas are located in the tail, with 70% in the body or tail and 30% in the head {9854582; 10089056; 17510774; 31609932}.

Clinical features

About 75–90% of cases of WDHA syndrome originate from a VIP-secreting pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET). Approximately 10–25% of WDHA syndrome cases originate from extrapancreatic sources {10089056; 26742109} that are often linked to neurogenic (neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma, ganglioneuroma) and sympathetic paraganglial (phaeochromocytoma, composite phaeochromocytoma, paraganglioma) neoplasms of the retroperitoneum, adrenal medulla, and mediastinum {3035922; 10089056; 16027147; 24936559; 31501391}. VIP production from medullary thyroid carcinoma and respiratory tract / lung neoplasms can also occur, but this often does not lead to WDHA syndrome {29733; 166313; 9071985}.

The cardinal features of WDHA syndrome consist of profuse secretory-type watery diarrhoea (usually > 1 L of stool volume per day) that persists with fasting, weight loss, flushing, hypokalaemia, hypochlorhydria/achlorhydria, and metabolic acidosis due to bicarbonate and potassium loss via the stools together with hypovolaemia. Other (less common) manifestations are hypercalcaemia and glucose intolerance {3035922; 9854582; 10089056}.

Biochemical investigations

An excess of various hormones and peptides, including but not limited to neurotensin and secretin, can simulate WDHA syndrome {27501174; 25905306}. The diagnosis requires documentation of elevated plasma VIP levels (often > 60 pmol/L or > 190 pg/mL).

Imaging

Primary tumour localization and the presence of metastatic spread are determined using conventional imaging (CT, MRI, EUS) and functional imaging studies (111In-pentetreotide [Octreoscan] SPECT-CT, 68Ga-DOTATATE/DOTATOC PET-CT) {9854582; 20664472; 31268974}. A series identified a T2 hyperintensity and T1 hypointensity on MRI studies of pancreatic VIPomas {31268974}. Functional imaging studies (111In-pentetreotide and/or 68Ga-DOTATATE PET-CT) yielded positivity in about 80% of pancreatic VIPomas {31268974}, and scan positivity is further required for peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT).

Epidemiology

Pancreatic VIPomas account for 0.6–1.5% of all PanNENs {24121170; 24499825} and approximately 2–6% of functioning tumours {24499825; 26704781}. The incidence is 0.05–0.2 cases per 1 million person-years {26742109} and there is no sex predilection {31609932; 31268974}. NNeurogenic tumours such as neuroblastomas and ganglioneuroblastomas are more common in the paediatric population (mean age: 7.3 years) than are pancreatic VIPomas (mean age: 50.5 years) {28730220; 10089056}.

Etiology

There are no established etiological factors. About 3–10% of pancreatic VIPomas occur in the context of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 {10089056; 9854582; 33649917}.

Pathogenesis

Autonomous VIP secretion activates G protein–coupled receptors on the intestinal epithelial cells, which leads to watery diarrhoea and related findings {181379; 8103215}.

Somatic mutations in MEN1, VHL, PTEN, or SMAD4 (DPC4) mutations have been reported {10327057; 11523053}. Frequent aberrations of 11q, Xq, and the Y chromosome are detected {10595906}. Allelic losses of 3p and 6q in the tumours, found by loss-of-heterozygosity analysis, may be involved in tumour progression {11395364; 11523053}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pancreatic VIPomas are usually solitary and well-delineated tumours (mean size: 45–53 mm) {2839087; 10089056; 17510774; 31268974} and can show solid and cystic components {31268974}.

Histopathology

These tumours are often indistinguishable from other well-differentiated PanNETs, but they tend to show solid, trabecular, or tubuloacinar patterns {2839087}. Variable oncocytic or clear cell change can occur {2839087}. Vascular and lymphatic invasion are common {10089056}. Some functioning VIPomas can manifest as neuroendocrine microtumours (measuring < 5 mm) {31275551}.

Grading

They are graded similarly to other PanNETs, and most are G1 or G2 {24121170; 31268974}, but G3 tumours also occur {31268974}.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells express neuroendocrine differentiation markers (chromogranin A, synaptophysin, INSM1), keratins (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, CK8/18, CK19), and functional markers (SSTR2A). Some tumours display paranuclear fibrous bodies on low-molecular-weight keratin immunohistochemistry. VIP immunolabelling is found in 90% of cases, but the extent of labelling is variable given the rapid turnover of protein biosynthesis. Although PP coexpression is frequent {2839087}, synchronous calcitonin expression can also occur {22406608}. Insulin coexpression was also reported at time of metastatic spread {32168252}.

Cytology

The cytology is indistinguishable from that of other neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs).

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a keratin-positive PanNEN with elevated plasma VIP levels; Ki-67 immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: VIP immunoreactivity; confirmation of pancreatic origin on imaging studies.

Staging

The eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) / Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system is currently applied to these tumours, which relies on the tumour size, extent of tumour spread, and metastases [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]] [[Amin MB, Edge S, Greene F, et al., editors. AJCC cancer staging manual. 8th ed. New York (NY): Springer; 2017.]]. This approach largely corresponds to that of the European Neuroendocrine Tumor Society (ENETS) classification {22525418}.

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 50–80% of cases present with distant metastases at diagnosis, mostly in the liver {3035922; 9854582; 10089056}. Ovarian metastases are also reported {26657531}. Tumour stage and grade (based on the Ki-67 labelling index and mitotic count) are important prognostic factors. The management of VIP excess (using somatostatin analogues, PRRT, or cytoreductive therapies) is also a critical factor {31268974}. The 5-year survival rates are 60% and 94%, respectively, for patients with metastatic disease and those who underwent intended curative surgery {10089056}.
