9.0.6.3 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a malignant spindle cell tumour with limited Schwannian differentiation. Molecular hallmarks are the combined genetic inactivation of NF1, CDKN2A and/or CDKN2B, and SUZ12 or EED, as well as complex genomic rearrangements.

ICD-O coding

9540/3 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, NOS

9542/3 Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y & XH2XP8 Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures & Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant schwannoma; neurofibrosarcoma; neurogenic sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid MPNST

Localization

Rare MPNSTs have been reported in the pituitary {23830587; 31233145; 31910151}, thyroid {15299203; 32327463}, pancreas {15299203; 30720497; 30785006}, and adrenal glands {2894207; 15022069; 19561238; 24739852; 2635211; 27338175}, with tumours in the adrenal glands often arising in association with phaeochromocytoma or ganglioneuroma. A rare case of epithelioid MPNST has been reported in the thyroid {17972308}.

Clinical features

MPNSTs have a slight male sex predilection and typically present in patients aged 20–50 years, with most paediatric and young adult cases arising in patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1). Symptoms are nonspecific. Tumours are identified during imaging studies or as an enlarging mass; pain and anorexia may be reported. Organ-specific findings include dyspnoea (thyroid) or dyspepsia and other bowel/biliary disturbances (pancreas) {32327463; 30785006}.

Epidemiology

MPNST is extremely rare within endocrine organ sites.

Etiology

MPNST may be associated with NF1 or prior irradiation.

Pathogenesis

Conventional MPNSTs have complex karyotypes and frequent concurrent inactivating mutations involving three pathways: NF1, CDKN2A and/or CDKN2B, and PRC2 core components (EED or SUZ12) {25240281; 25119042; 25305755; 30722027}. About 80% of all high-grade MPNSTs exhibit complete loss of PRC2 activity through loss of the PRC2 core component (EED or SUZ12) and complete loss of H3 p.K27me3 expression {25240281; 26585554; 26645727}. In contrast to the conventional subtype, epithelioid MPNST is not NF1-associated, often arises from schwannoma rather than neurofibroma, and is associated with SMARCB1 inactivation {25240281; 25602794; 30864974}.

Macroscopic appearance

MPNSTs are usually large and have a tan-white, fleshy cut surface, often with areas of haemorrhage and necrosis. They are destructively infiltrative into the native organ, but may appear pseudoencapsulated.

Histopathology

MPNST is a destructively infiltrative malignancy composed of fascicles of spindle-shaped cells, a variably haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern, and alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas (marbled appearance). A peritheliomatous concentration of tumour cells may be seen. There are often areas of geographical necrosis and high mitotic activity. The cells have pale cytoplasm and hyperchromatic, wavy, buckled, and tapered nuclei. Occasional MPNSTs (especially radiation-induced ones) feature highly atypical nuclei, simulating an undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. Heterologous skeletal muscle (malignant triton tumour), bone, cartilage, or vascular differentiation is seen in about 15% of tumours {19561238}.

Epithelioid MPNST consists of lobules of large epithelioid, melanoma-like cells with vesicular nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.

Immunohistochemistry

MPNSTs may be positive for S100 (< 50% of tumours), SOX10 (< 70%), and GFAP (20–30%) {23929265}, although staining is patchy, focal, and weak; in fact, diffuse staining for S100 or SOX10 is not usually compatible with conventional MPNST. Complete loss of immunostaining for H3 p.K27me3 can be diagnostically useful, with high-grade sporadic and radiation-induced MPNST tumours showing the most frequent loss {26645727; 26585554; 28776579; 29482987}. Epithelioid MPNST is typically strongly and extensively positive for S100 and SOX10, is negative for more specific melanoma markers, retains H3 p.K27me3, and shows loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression {30864974}.

Cytology

Fine-needle aspirates of MPNST are similar in endocrine and soft tissue sites, but they are often not sufficiently specific to obviate the need for biopsy or resection confirmation.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In diagnostically challenging cases, DNA methylation profiling can identify a specific tumour signature of MPNST {33479225}. Other molecular data providing support for an MPNST diagnosis include mutations in NF1 and SUZ12 or EED.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: alternating light and dark zones with pleomorphic spindled cells showing tapered, buckled nuclei; epithelioid cytology and loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression (epithelioid MPNST).

Desirable: a haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern; variable S100 and/or SOX10 reactivity; loss of H3 p.K27me3 expression; association with a large nerve, NF1, pre-existing neurofibroma, and/or prior irradiation; association with pre-existing schwannoma and extensive expression of S100 and SOX10 (epithelioid MPNST).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The reported 5-year overall survival rate for NF1-associated MPNST (35%) is lower than that for sporadic tumours (65%); deep location, positive surgical margins, and high-grade histology were additionally associated with worse outcomes {27158754}. Radiation-associated MPNSTs have a reported 5-year overall survival rate of 23.5% {31078939}. Specific prognostic factors for endocrine organs have not been reported.
