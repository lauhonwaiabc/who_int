3.2.3.4 Cribriform morular thyroid carcinoma

Definition

Cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma (CMTC) is a malignant thyroid tumour with a particular growth pattern secondary to constitutive activation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway that can occur with familial adenomatous polyposis or sporadically.

ICD-O coding

8201/3 Cribriform morular thyroid carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D10.Y & XH1YZ3 Other specified malignant neoplasms of thyroid gland & Cribriform carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: cribriform-morular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma (CMTC) is usually multifocal and/or bilateral in familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), whereas sporadic cases appear as a solitary nodule {10229505; 20878367; 21455198; 29239041}.

Clinical features

CMTC almost exclusively occurs in young women (97%) with 89% occurring in individuals aged < 40 years (mean age: 28 years, range: 8–69 years) {29320754}. The M:F ratio is 1:31–61 {29320754; 29785019}. As many as 53% of CMTCs are associated with FAP {29320754}. Tumours may present as a painless mass, hoarseness, or dysphagia, or they may be incidentally discovered on ultrasound.

CMTCs are circumscribed and hypoechoic with a heterogeneous echotexture, and they lack a halo and calcifications by ultrasonography {22892017}.

Epidemiology

In a review of 129 CMTCs in the English literature, 63% of reported cases were from Asia, 26% were from America, and 11% were from Europe {29320754}. The frequency of thyroid carcinoma in FAP has increased with screening ultrasound and FNAB {29785019}. Ultrasonographic screening of 129 Japanese individuals with FAP identified CMTCs in 6.2% (8 CMTCs and 3 papillary thyroid carcinomas) {27623068}.

Etiology

In patients with FAP, CMTC is secondary to germline APC mutations. Somatic mutations resulting in permanent activation of the WNT/β-catenin signalling pathway are responsible for sporadic tumours {29785019}. Female sex is a risk factor {21878742; 29785019}.

Pathogenesis

Most germline APC mutations in FAP with thyroid tumours occur in exon 15 (codons 463–1387), whereas codon 1061 is a hotspot for hepatoblastoma and CMTC {10634400}. APC mutations lead to a truncated APC protein, which is unable to contribute to β-catenin proteasomal degradation. Stabilized β-catenin is translocated into the nucleus and binds TCF/LEF proteins, leading to the constitutive expression of WNT target genes involved in proliferation and loss of differentiation {29785019}. In FAP with multiple CMTCs, each tumour has a different somatic APC mutation (second hit) {11106555}. A second-hit somatic mutation in KMT2C and KMT2D was described in some familial CMTCs {32024448}.

Somatic APC mutation in exon 15 (codon 1309) has been detected in a sporadic CMTC {11293895}. Somatic mutations in functionally equivalent genes, such as CTNNB1 (exon 3) {12474227; 34019236} and AXIN1 (exons 1 and 7) {29785019} are reported in sporadic CMTCs. Somatic alterations involving RET (in the form of rearrangements) {9916927; 11232035}, PIK3CA {26054797}, and KRAS {24702198}, as well as TERT promoter mutations {27688081}, could be additional effectors of the WNT/β-catenin pathway in familial and sporadic CMTC {29785019}.

Macroscopic appearance

CMTCs in FAP are often multifocal (4–13 foci), bilateral, and small, whereas sporadic tumours are larger (mean: 30 mm) single nodules {34019236; 7698732; 10229505; 21455198; 29785019; 31469036}. CMTCs are well circumscribed or encapsulated, solid but may be cystic, and white-tan {29785019}.

Histopathology

CMTCs are circumscribed or encapsulated and may have a lobulated appearance due to fibrous septa {20878367; 29785019; 7698732}. Angioinvasion occurs in 30% of cases and capsular invasion in 40% {29785019}. CMTCs have an admixture of growth patterns not seen in other thyroid carcinomas {20878367; 7698732}. As originally described, CMTCs display “an intricate blending of cribriform, follicular, papillary, trabecular, and solid patterns of growth, with morular (squamoid) areas” {10229505}. The cribriform structures are composed of anastomosing bars and arches without intervening fibrous stroma that merge with tubular follicles that lack colloid {10229505; 29785019}. Papillae and pseudopapillae are lined by tall, cuboidal or pseudostratified cells, and trabecular areas show spindle cells reminiscent of hyalinizing trabecular tumour {10229505; 21455198; 29785019}. Squamoid morulae are whorls of spindle to ovoid cells that lack keratinization and show nuclear clearing due to biotin accumulation {10229505; 29239041; 29785019}. Psammoma bodies are rare {29320754; 29785019}. With these distinctive features, the tumour was initially named “cribriform-morular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma” {10229505}. Correlation between the morphological features of the tumour and recently understood molecular alterations brought about the name change to “cribriform-morular thyroid carcinoma” {20966648; 21455198; 29785019; 31217615; 34019236}.

Immunohistochemistry

Aberrant strong nuclear and cytoplasmic β-catenin staining is the hallmark of this thyroid tumour {21455198; 29239041; 29785019}. CMTC is positive for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) (clones SPT24 and 8G3G7/1), various keratins, EMA, ER, and PR; it is negative for CK20, calcitonin, and WT1 {18081818; 23161723; 24702198; 26054797; 29239041; 29785019}. Thyroglobulin expression is typically negative; however, focal nonspecific diffusion-type staining can occur due to entrapped non-tumorous thyroid follicles {34019236}. PAX8 immunohistochemistry using either the monoclonal BC12 or polyclonal antisera shows no or focal weak staining in these tumours {34019236}. Morulae are negative for keratin 34βE12, thyroglobulin, TTF1, PAX8, ER, PR, and E-cadherin, but stain for CDX2, CD10, CD5, and CK5 {18081818; 23161723; 24702198; 26054797; 29785019; 34019236}. The Ki-67 index is usually < 5%. High-grade CMTCs have increased mitotic activity, necrosis, and Ki-67 indexes of 15–60%; they may have neuroendocrine differentiation, and can occur in sporadic and familial settings {19095577; 23349472; 30176755; 30376202}. In a study of 33 CMTCs, high-grade features were present in 2 sporadic CMTCs and in none of the FAP-associated CMTCs {34019236}.

Cytology

Smears are hypercellular, with predominantly large complex tissue fragments that show a papillary arrangement of tall columnar cells, and a cribriform pattern due to slit-like spaces, with swirling of nuclei representing morulae, and in some cases hyaline strands. There are spindle cells and haemosiderin-laden macrophages and histiocytes, but no colloid in the background. Nuclei can show peculiar nuclear clearing with thick membranes, as well as grooves and pseudoinclusions. Nuclear β-catenin immunocytochemistry on cell blocks is helpful in the differential diagnosis with other thyroid gland tumours, including the aggressive tall cell papillary thyroid carcinoma {19534622; 20091902}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Evaluation for germline APC mutations is desirable in patients with CMTC. About 85% of germline APC mutations are in exon 15, usually outside the mutation cluster region {10634400; 29785019}. In familial and sporadic cases, constitutive WNT/β-catenin pathway activation is evidenced by strong nuclear β-catenin immunostaining – a hallmark of CMTC and an important diagnostic criterion. BRAF mutations are absent.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed or locally invasive TTF1-positive thyroid neoplasm; diffuse nuclear β-catenin expression; predominant complex architecture including cribriform and squamoid morulae, with no colloid formation.

Desirable:

Cribriform areas: positive for pancytokeratin, ER, and PR; negative (or focal weak) reactivity for PAX8; negative for thyroglobulin. Morulae: positive for CD10, CDX2, CD5, and CK5; negative for TTF1, PAX8, thyroglobulin, ER, and PR.

Staging

Staging is according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

CMTC is associated with lymph node metastases at presentation in 12% of cases and distant metastases in 3%, with an overall mortality rate of 2% {29320754}. High-grade CMTCs behave aggressively {23349472; 27688081; 30376202}.
