8.2.0.1 Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour

Definition

Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour (CoGNET), previously known as gangliocytic paraganglioma, is a triphasic tumour composed of neuroendocrine epithelial cells, Schwannian spindle cells, and ganglion cells.

ICD-O coding

8683/3 Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B80.01 & XH3JF3 Neuroendocrine neoplasm of duodenum & Composite paraganglioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: mixed ganglioneuroma–neuroendocrine neoplasm (MiGNEN); gangliocytic neuroendocrine tumour.

Not recommended: duodenal ganglioneuroma; duodenal paraganglioma; gangliocytic paraganglioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CoGNET almost exclusively arises in the second part of the duodenum (periampullary region) {31572665; 32668278}. Extremely rare cases have been reported in the jejunum, pylorus, oesophagus, pancreas, appendix, lung, and thymus {12653602; 17653595; 26447134; 28096810; 28764742; 32205710}.

Clinical features

Patients with CoGNET usually present with gastrointestinal bleeding due to mucosal ulceration or nonspecific epigastric pain {30565068}. Biliary and intestinal obstruction have been reported {19517054}. Most CoGNETs are clinically non-functioning, although high serum levels of somatostatin and PP have been reported {8633524; 24047035}.

Epidemiology

Most frequent in the sixth decade of life {30522176; 31572665}, CoGNETs occur across a broad age range (15–92 years) {30522176; 30565068}. A slight male predominance (M:F ratio: ~3:2) {31572665} was reported in some series but not in others {30565068}.

Etiology

Although a few cases were reported in the setting of neurofibromatosis type 1 {11876254; 2894342}, the majority of CoGNETs are sporadic, with no known risk factors or syndromic associations.

Pathogenesis

CoGNET is thought to derive from a defective sheet of intraembryonic endodermal progenitor cells, progenitor neuroectodermal cell rests, and smooth muscle {6139954; 2578747; 11819111; 2750701}. About 20% of CoGNETs harbour somatic gain-of-function mutations in EPAS1 (HIF2A) {27130043}.

Macroscopic appearance

CoGNETs are solitary, polypoid, pedunculated or sessile lesions {2877566}, 20–30 mm in median size (range: 7–190 mm) depending on site {30565068; 31572665}. In the pancreatic head, CoGNETs are larger (median size: 40 mm) {30565068}. Tumours are typically centred in the submucosa and are well demarcated but unencapsulated, with borders infiltrating the mucosa and/or muscularis propria in more than half of the cases {21599949}. The cut surface varies from tan-grey to yellow-white. The overlying mucosa is often ulcerated, even in the absence of mucosal invasion.

Histopathology

CoGNETs display three morphologically distinct cell components: (1) an epithelial cell component, arranged in nests and gland-like structures with granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, round to oval nuclei, speckled chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli; (2) a spindle cell Schwannian component, in slender fascicles with elongated plump nuclei and attenuated eosinophilic cytoplasm, wrapping around nests of epithelial cells; and (3) a ganglion cell component, scattered or in clusters, intermixed with the epithelial and spindle cell components. The proportions of the three components vary widely; ganglion cells may be difficult to identify. Mitoses are rare. Cytological atypia is minimal, and necrosis is absent. Any one or all three components may be present in metastases.

Immunohistochemistry

Almost all epithelial cells express cytokeratins {32668278}, chromogranin A, synaptophysin, NSE, and PR. PP and somatostatin may also be expressed. Spindle cells express SOX10, S100, and NSE. Ganglion cells express NSE and synaptophysin but are negative for SOX10 {32668278}. CoGNETs are usually negative for or show only very focal, weak expression of GATA3 and tyrosine hydroxylase {32668278}.

Differential diagnosis

Exclusion of gastrointestinal stromal tumour, leiomyoma, schwannoma, neuroendocrine tumour (NET), adenocarcinoma, ganglioneuroma, and paraganglioma is based on specific markers (KIT [c-KIT] and DOG1 for gastrointestinal stromal tumour, desmin for leiomyoma) and adequate sampling. Ganglioneuroma lacks the epithelial component of CoGNET, whereas conventional paraganglioma lacks the ganglion and Schwannian components and is usually cytokeratin-negative {27130043; 22931260; 30101131; 32668278}. CoGNET is often misdiagnosed as conventional well-differentiated NET (G1). Increasing evidence indicates that CoGNET is more closely related to NET than to conventional paraganglioma {32668278; 27130043}. Immunohistochemistry for PR and PP, although not specific, may be useful {25886293}.

Cytology

Aspirates are often hypercellular and predominantly composed of epithelial cells, with a minor component of spindle and ganglion cells {22102547; 33764657}. Rare cases with predominant spindle cells have been described {1510067}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: triphasic differentiation with variable neuroendocrine epithelial cells, spindle cells with Schwannian differentiation, and ganglion cells.

Desirable: location in the periampullary region of the duodenum.

Staging

Staging utilizes a NET-specific system for the digestive tract only [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Digestive system tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 1). https://publications.iarc.who.int/579.]]. The other (exceedingly rare) sites adopt the staging systems of non-neuroendocrine cancer.

Prognosis and prediction

Most CoGNETs are indolent and do not recur after complete removal. However, lymph node metastases were reported in 10–14.5% of cases and liver metastases in 1.8% of cases {26910321; 30101131; 30565068}. In a recent study, the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates for periampullary CoGNETs were 100%, 83.3%, and 55.6%, respectively {30565068}. No known histopathological factors are predictive of metastasis. Tumour size > 20 mm, young age, and submucosal invasion could be risk factors for lymph node metastasis {21599949; 26717062; 26910321; 27656281; 28096810; 28213067}. Pancreatic CoGNETs are thought to have a greater potential for malignant behaviour {28764742}. Because of the lack of validated predictive malignancy markers, all CoGNETs should be regarded as potentially malignant; long-term follow-up is recommended.
