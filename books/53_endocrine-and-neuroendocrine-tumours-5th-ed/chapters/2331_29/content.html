2.3.3.1 Meningiomas

Definition

Meningiomas are a family of neoplasms that are most likely derived from the meningothelial cells of the arachnoid (CNS WHO grades 1, 2, or 3).

ICD-O coding

9530/0 Meningioma, NOS

9531/0 Meningothelial meningioma

ICD-11 coding

2A01.0Z Meningiomas, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: tuberculum sellae meningioma; diaphragma sellae meningioma; suprasellar meningioma.

Subtype(s)

Meningothelial meningioma is the most common subtype; for other subtypes, see the Central nervous system tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Central nervous system tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2021. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 6). https://publications.iarc.who.int/601.]].

Localization

Most tumours in the sellar region are suprasellar; they can originate in the planum sphenoidale, tuberculum sellae, and diaphragma sellae {3982616}. Intrasellar meningiomas are derived from the inferior leaf of the diaphragma sellae {7643985}.

Other endocrine organs are uncommon sites of meningioma development, with single case reports of incidental meningioma in the adrenal gland {17886105; 30558028} or metastatic meningioma in the thyroid gland {11585373}.

Clinical features

Visual deficits and headache are the most common signs and symptoms at presentation {10864387; 16793435; 21470998; 25171391}. Endocrine abnormality is uncommon {7643985; 22644157; 25171391}. Cranial nerve palsies may be seen in cases with cavernous sinus invasion {16793435}. Sellar meningioma can become symptomatic during pregnancy {33200305}. Coexistence of meningioma and pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenoma has been reported {31476461}.

Imaging

On imaging, the tumours are typically well-circumscribed, homogeneously isodense or hyperdense masses, with diffuse enhancement on CT. The tumours are isointense to grey matter on T1-weighted MRI, but variable on T2-weighted images, with most being T2-hyperintense {9987763}. Contrast enhancement is strong and uniform, allowing most meningiomas to be distinguished from PitNETs/adenomas {1407446; 9987763}. Involvement of the dura mater can occur in about 30% of cases {23975080}. Invasion of the sellar floor is uncommon.

Epidemiology

Meningiomas account for about 5% of all sellar neoplasms {17287410; 18981830} and are the second most common suprasellar neoplasm in adults {7643985; 18981830}. The peak incidence is in the fifth or sixth decade of life, and there is a female predominance {10864387; 16793435; 22644157; 23975080; 25304271}.

Etiology

Ionizing radiation is an established risk factor for meningioma {23816298; 28339329}. However, after radiation to the skull, meningiomas typically arise at the convexity, not in the sellar region {28474103; 28775249}.

The expression of hormone receptors in meningioma {7861224; 8587697}, the higher incidence of meningioma in women than in men, and the increased risk after hormone treatment {23101448; 20821343} suggest an as yet mechanistically unproven role of hormones. Meningiomas developing after progestin therapy typically arise at the skull base and harbour PIK3CA mutations according to one study {29346520}. Neurofibromatosis type 2 is the most common among tumour syndromes associated with meningiomas.

Pathogenesis

Meningiomas are believed to originate from arachnoid cells. Whereas the molecular genetics of intracranial meningiomas is well studied, little is known about those arising in the sellar region.

Macroscopic appearance

Meningiomas are typically solid and firm lesions with a whorled or lobulated appearance; psammomatous subtypes may have a gritty texture. Invasion of the dura and cavernous sinus is common.

Histopathology

Meningiomas have several histopathological appearances, varying in grade and biological behaviour. The most common subtypes are meningothelial, fibrous (fibroblastic), and transitional (mixed). The exact characteristics of the subtypes are discussed in the Central nervous system tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Central nervous system tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2021. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 6). https://publications.iarc.who.int/601.]].

Grading

The WHO grading system for meningiomas designates the tumours as benign (grade 1), atypical (grade 2), or anaplastic (grade 3) according to specific histopathological criteria. Recognized histological subtypes with a greater likelihood of recurrence and/or aggressive behaviour include chordoid and clear cell meningiomas (grade 2). Mitotic activity and the Ki-67 proliferation index increase with tumour grade.

Atypical meningiomas are characterized by increased mitotic activity (≥ 2.5 mitoses/mm2 or ≥ 4 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.16 mm2, as originally described) {9414189}, brain invasion, or any three of the following features: increased cellularity, loss of pattern or sheet-like growth, small cells with a high N:C ratio, prominent nucleoli, and foci of spontaneous necrosis.

Anaplastic meningiomas can be histologically similar to but are biologically distinct from meningeal sarcomas. Anaplasia is diagnosed in the presence of morphology resembling carcinoma, high-grade sarcoma, or melanoma; increased mitotic activity (≥ 12.5 mitoses/mm2 or ≥ 20 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.16 mm2, as originally described) {9414189}; TERT promoter mutation; or homozygous deletion of CDKN2A and/or CDKN2B.

Immunohistochemistry

Meningiomas are typically immunoreactive for vimentin and EMA, but the latter can be weak, especially in the fibroblastic subtype. S100 reactivity is variable. PR is expressed in most benign meningiomas, whereas ER is less common. SSTR2A is strongly expressed in most meningiomas, including atypical and anaplastic tumours {26195322}. STAT6 (nuclear) and thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) are not expressed.

Ultrastructure

Features include complex interdigitating cellular processes, abundant intermediate filaments, and desmosomal junctions.

Differential diagnosis

The entities include TTF1-expressing tumours of the posterior lobe, schwannoma, and solitary fibrous tumour of the sella.

Cytology

Fibrous meningiomas can be difficult to smear; cellularity can be low on touch imprints. Compact fascicles of spindle cells, a whorling pattern, calcification, and nuclear pseudoinclusions are features of meningioma on squash preparation {26729974}. Distinction from pituicytoma and spindle cell oncocytoma is challenging.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Meningothelial meningiomas, the most common subtype in the sellar region, typically carry AKT1 p.E17K mutations (often combined with TRAF7 mutations) or, alternatively, isolated PIK3CA or SMO mutations {23348505; 26826201}. They rarely show any copy-number alterations {23348505}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: classic histopathological features matching at least one of the meningioma subtypes; suggestive histopathological features combined with biallelic inactivation of NF2 or other classic drivers of conventional meningioma (TRAF7, AKT1, KLF4, SMO, PIK3CA), clear cell meningioma (SMARCE1), or rhabdoid meningioma (BAP1); suggestive histopathological features combined with one of the defined DNA methylation classes of meningioma.

Desirable: dural or meningeal localization; EMA immunoreactivity; strong and diffuse SSTR2A immunoreactivity; classic copy-number alterations of NF2-mutant meningioma, such as monosomy 22/22q in lower-grade meningiomas, with additional losses of 1p, 6, 10q, 14q, and/or 18 in higher-grade meningiomas.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis depends on invasion of parasellar structures, tumour histopathology (see subtypes), and tumour grade. Outcomes are favourable in patients aged < 50 years and patients with clinical presentation for < 1 year {11838796}. Recurrence is associated with incomplete surgical resection {11838796; 16793435; 23975080}. Perioperative mortality rates are low {16793435}, but postoperative persistence of disease and hypopituitarism are not uncommon {22644157}.
