13.0.0.14 MAFA-related familial insulinomatosis

Definition

MAFA-related familial insulinomatosis is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by pathogenic MAFA sequence variants. It is characterized by multiple insulin-secreting pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PanNETs) that usually present as adult-onset hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

147630 Insulinomatosis and diabetes mellitus; INSDM

ICD-11 coding

2E92.9 Benign neoplasm of endocrine pancreas

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial insulinomatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The disorder is restricted to the pancreas, where MAFA plays a critical role as a transcription factor regulating pancreatic B-cell function and proliferation.

Clinical features

Reflecting the role of MAFA as both an oncogene and an islet B-cell transcription factor, presentation is with either hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia or diabetes, with the hypoglycaemic presentation being more common in female patients and the diabetic presentation being more common in male patients {29339498}. Although the two manifestations are generally mutually exclusive, 1 patient with diabetes went on to develop hyperinsulinism and multifocal islet B-cell pancreatic proliferations {29339498}. Onset is in adulthood, which distinguishes it from congenital hyperinsulinism. Clues to the diagnosis in patients presenting with diabetes include a normal body mass index, no other clinical features of insulin resistance, no history of diabetic ketoacidosis, and negative islet autoantibodies {29339498}. Imaging identifies multicentric PanNETs that can measure > 10 mm {29339498}.

Epidemiology

This disorder is too rare to determine the actual incidence and prevalence. Although the mutation is found equally in both sexes, hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia has been reported to be more frequent in women, whereas men in the same kindreds develop diabetes more commonly. The onset of symptoms is usually in the fourth decade of life and there is no history of congenital hyperinsulinism {29339498}.

Etiology

This disorder is attributed to a specific germline mutation, p.S64F, in the gene encoding the transcription factor MAFA. The presence of a MAFA mutation distinguishes this disorder from sporadic insulinomatosis {29339498}.

Pathogenesis

MAFA is a bZIP-containing transcription factor that regulates glucose-stimulated insulin secretion {17785922; 24726898}; it also has oncogenic transformation potential in vitro. A specific mutation, p.S64F, impairs phosphorylation in the MAFA transactivation domain, resulting in increased protein stability that is insensitive to glucose concentrations, thereby interrupting the regulatory action of MAFA. In addition, MAFA p.S64F exhibits enhanced transactivation compared with wildtype MAFA {29339498}.

Macroscopic appearance

The pancreas of patients with this disorder shows multifocal small tumours that vary in size and can reach > 10 mm.

Histopathology

The pancreas of patients with MAFA-related insulinomatosis contain innumerable multicentric islet B-cell proliferations, ranging from dysplastic B-cell cords to multifocal neuroendocrine trabecular microtumours (< 5 mm) and solitary well-differentiated tumours (> 5 mm) (Ki-67 index: < 2%). Although the islets contain insulin, glucagon, PP, and somatostatin-producing cells in normal distributions and numbers, the trabecular proliferations are exclusively positive for insulin, which helps distinguish the condition from multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 and from glucagon cell adenomatosis and hyperplasia {19011561; 29339498}.

Cytology

PanNETs may come to FNAB, and their cytopathological features are described in the WHO reporting system for pancreaticobiliary cytopathology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A missense MAFA c.191C>T p.S64F sequence variant has been shown to segregate with the manifestations of both hyperinsulinism and diabetes. To date, other MAFA mutations have not been reported.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a pathogenic sequence variant of MAFA, with evidence of multifocal pancreatic trabecular islet B-cell proliferations staining for insulin.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Medical therapy yields poor results with persistent hypoglycaemia {29339498}. Most patients have undergone partial pancreatectomy, but this has not led to cure, and several patients have undergone multiple surgical procedures. The multifocal nature of the islet B-cell proliferations suggests that total pancreatectomy would be required for cure {33434149}. Local or distant metastases have not been documented.
