2.3.2.2 Sellar neurocytoma

Definition

Sellar neurocytoma is a tumour composed of uniform round cells with neuronal differentiation and immunohistochemical resemblance to hypothalamic neurons.

ICD-O coding

9506/1 Sellar neurocytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F9A & XA8J35 Neoplasms of unknown behaviour of endocrine glands & Pituitary gland

Related terminology

Acceptable: neurocytoma of the sellar region; hypothalamic neurocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Sellar neurocytomas are located in the sellar or hypothalamic region. Ectopic neurocytomas with immunohistochemical resemblance to hypothalamic neurons have been reported {32043038}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with visual disturbances and hypothalamic–pituitary dysfunction {26137226; 27398267; 30379651; 30551183}. Like other lesions within the sellar region, they can interfere with stalk delivery of dopamine. The resulting prolactin excess contributes to galactorrhoea, menstrual irregularities, and infertility. However, unlike other sellar lesions, they can also present with neurological features including nausea, vomiting, and speech difficulties {30379651}. Neurocytomas can also be an important source of hormone excess. In particular, vasopressin overproduction contributes to water retention with significant hyponatraemia {30379651}. The resulting syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis (SIAD) can be enigmatic until histopathological examination confirms the diagnosis. Acromegaly has been reported in a single case {1509347}.

Imaging

Neurocytoma represents one of the differential diagnoses of large sellar or suprasellar masses. They are typically detected as partially calcified lesions on CT. On MRI they show mixed cystic changes with heterogeneous enhancement after gadolinium administration. The solid portion is frequently isointense to hypointense on T1-weighted images and predominantly hyperintense on T2-weighted images {27398267; 30775062}. Suprasellar extension and/or invasion of the parasellar sinuses have been reported {27398267; 30379651}.

Epidemiology

Sellar neurocytomas are rare tumours typically diagnosed in adults with a median age of approximately 45 years, although age at presentation varies widely {27398267; 30551183; 31528420}. According to the population-based data reported by the Central Brain Tumor Registry of the United States (CBTRUS), it is estimated that extraventricular neurocytomas in all localizations occur with an incidence rate of approximately 0.01 cases per 100 000 person-years {30859483}. A female predominance has been noted for sellar neurocytomas {27398267; 30379651; 30551183}.

Etiology

The etiology of sellar neurocytoma is unknown. No risk factors or inherited genetic susceptibility have been reported to date.

Pathogenesis

The cellular origin of neurocytoma of the sellar region is unknown. The fact that central and extraventricular neurocytomas form a distinct molecular group suggests that these tumours may arise from a specific precursor cell population {29978331}. However, it is not clear whether these findings can be extrapolated to neurocytomas of the sellar region. Evidence for coexpression of thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) and vasopressin in at least a proportion of tumours {30379651} may indicate that these neoplasms originate from neurons of the paraventricular or supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus {11161473; 11675118}.

Genetic profile

Genetic data specifically addressing sellar neurocytomas have not been reported. It is unclear whether these tumours can be regarded as part of the spectrum of other (extraventricular) neurocytomas, which have been reported to share a unique DNA methylation profile {29539639; 29978331}, or as a separate type of tumour.

Macroscopic appearance

Neurocytoma of the sellar region usually appears as a large, mostly round mass, often with focal calcifications.

Histopathology

Sellar neurocytomas are histologically characterized by uniform neoplastic cells with small round nuclei, often with finely speckled chromatin and clear cytoplasm embedded in a finely fibrillar neuropil matrix {11688459; 26137226; 27398267; 30379651}. Tumour cells can be arranged in sheets, cell clusters with neuropil islands, or neurocytic rosettes {22122710; 27398267; 30379651}. A subset of tumours contain ganglion cells. Microcalcifications may be present. Microvascular proliferation and necrosis are not commonly observed in sellar neurocytomas. Mitotic activity is typically very low. Electron microscopy confirms neuronal differentiation {23250388; 32138406}.

Grading

The majority of sellar neurocytomas present histologically as well-differentiated low-grade tumours and correspond to CNS WHO grade 2. However, a subset of tumours may show increased mitotic and proliferative activity, which has been reported to be associated with a more aggressive clinical behaviour.

Immunohistochemistry

Sellar neurocytomas are characterized by diffuse cytoplasmic positivity for synaptophysin present in the tumour cells and neuropil islands {27398267; 30379651; 30551183}. NeuN, chromogranin A, and neurofilament labelling confirms neuronal differentiation but might be very focal {27398267; 30379651}. GFAP staining is typically absent in the tumour cells {27398267; 30775062}. Cytokeratin, pituitary transcription factors, and hormone stains are negative {26137226; 27398267; 30379651}. Expression of TTF1 and vasopressin has been reported in a subset of cases {30379651; 32138406}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is usually low (1–3%), but it can be elevated in a subset of cases.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis usually includes pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenoma, sellar paraganglioma, gangliocytoma, neuroblastoma, and pituicytoma. The diffuse cytoplasmic immunoreactivity for synaptophysin and neurofilament, as well as nuclear NeuN and TTF1, and vasopressin in a subset of cases, in the absence of reactivity for cytokeratins, pituitary transcription factors, and hormones, usually allows the recognition of a sellar neurocytoma. Glial tumours can typically be distinguished by their GFAP immunoreactivity and/or the presence of characteristic molecular markers.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genome-wide DNA methylation profiling has provided evidence for a specific epigenetic signature of both central and extraventricular neurocytoma {29978331; 29539639}. However, it is not clear whether these findings can be extrapolated to neurocytomas of the sellar region.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sellar or parasellar neoplasms composed of monomorphic round cells showing neuronal differentiation.

Desirable: focal NeuN expression; neurofilament expression; TTF1 expression; vasopressin expression.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical course of sellar neurocytomas is usually favourable, with the extent of resection and the absence of atypical morphological features being the most important prognostic factors {22054208; 27398267; 30379651; 30551183}. However, growth of the tumour and dissemination is observed in a subset of cases, and long-term follow-up is recommended {23250388; 27398267}. SSTR ligand therapy has been suggested as a potential therapeutic approach for residual tumours {30722009}.
