10.0.0.8 IgG4-related disease

Definition

IgG4-related disease is a chronic fibroinflammatory condition characterized by a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate enriched in IgG4-positive plasma cells, often with storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis, and elevated serum IgG4.

ICD-O coding

9760/1 IgG4-related disease

ICD-11 coding

4A43.0 IgG4-related disease

Related terminology

Not recommended: IgG4-related autoimmune disease; IgG4-related systemic disease; IgG4-related sclerosing disease.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

IgG4-related disease can affect a single organ or multiple sites. Among endocrine organs, the pancreas is most commonly involved {25988916; 22198501}. The thyroid was involved in 5.6% of patients with IgG4-related disease in one series {25988916}, and the pituitary was involved in 8% of cases in another study {22198501}. Rare cases of adrenal involvement by IgG4-related disease have been reported {29766416; 23842043}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation varies with the site of involvement. In the thyroid, IgG4-related disease can present with hypothyroidism, or painless thyroid enlargement in IgG4-related Riedel-like thyroiditis {30697253; 30889549}. IgG4-related disease involving the pituitary may be associated with clinical symptoms of hypophysitis, such as nausea, headache, sexual dysfunction, fatigue, weight loss, polydipsia, generalized weakness, or blurred vision {21593109; 33837927}.

Imaging

Radiographic features of IgG4-related disease in the pancreas include a focal mass-like lesion mimicking pancreatic carcinoma and diffuse or segmental pancreatic enlargement {23789654}. IgG4-related thyroiditis may show hypoechoic lesions by ultrasound. IgG4-related hypophysitis may cause pituitary gland and stalk mass-like thickening {32126548}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of IgG4-related disease is 0.28–1.08 cases per 100 000 person-years, and the prevalence was 62 per million in a Japanese cohort {22899936}. There is a male predominance (M:F ratio: 1:0.8), and the average age of onset is 59 years {22899936}. The disease can occur at any age, including childhood {27012661}. The incidence of IgG4- related Riedel-like thyroiditis is 1.06 cases per 100 000 person-years, whereas the reported prevalence of IgG4-related Hashimoto thyroiditis ranges from 5% to 34% {30889549}. IgG4-related hypophysitis constituted 30% of hypophysitis cases and 4% of hypopituitarism cases in one study {24165017}.

Etiology

The etiology of IgG4-related disease is not well understood. Environmental exposures, immune dysfunction, infections, and genetic predispositions linked to a variety of genes have all been proposed to contribute to the development of IgG4-related disease {33490938}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenetic mechanisms leading to IgG4-related disease are unclear. TGF-β and IL-10 are elevated and the TGF-β pathway is activated, stimulating fibrogenesis {33490938}. Whether the increased number of IgG4-producing plasma cells is part of the pathogenesis or a downstream effect is unknown.

Macroscopic appearance

There are no macroscopic findings specific for IgG4-related disease, because the features can be highly variable in different organs.

Histopathology

The characteristic histological features of IgG4-related disease in various organs are quite similar to those of IgG4-related pancreatitis (type 1 autoimmune pancreatitis), which is the prototype of endocrine IgG4-related disease. A dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate is associated with fibrosis, which often shows a storiform pattern. Obliterative phlebitis is seen in a subset of cases and is considered almost pathognomonic of IgG4-related disease in some organs. Eosinophils are often present. A prominent neutrophilic infiltrate, tissue necrosis, and epithelioid granulomas are generally not seen {21107087}. Depending on the specimen type and particular endocrine organ, different densities of IgG4-positive plasma cells define IgG4-related disease {22555173; 23992023; 23695895; 25898816; 26669779; 30889549}. See #33046Table 10.01 for specific thresholds of IgG4-positive plasma cells per mm2 for the diagnosis of IgG4-related disease in various endocrine organs {22596100}. IgG4-related thyroid disease exhibits features of Riedel-like thyroiditis or the fibrotic subtype of Hashimoto thyroiditis; for other types of thyroid disease that do not fulfil all the main criteria for IgG4-related disease, terms such as “thyroid disease with an elevation of IgG4” have been suggested, particularly in cases without histological confirmation {30889549}. IgG4-related hypophysitis shows lymphoplasmacytic infiltration of the pituitary gland and IgG4 serum levels exceeding 140 mg/dL {32126548}. When the pituitary gland is involved by IgG4-related disease, fibrosis and phlebitis are rarely seen. IgG4-related pancreatitis has been studied more extensively than IgG4-related disease affecting other endocrine organs {22596100}. Depending on the specimen cellularity, a ratio of IgG4-positive to IgG-positive plasma cells of > 40% is a general cut-off point for possible involvement by IgG4-related disease. Of note, IgG4-related disease cannot be diagnosed solely on the basis of IgG4-positive plasma cell count or the IgG4+:IgG+ plasma cell ratio.

Differential diagnosis

Many conditions can be associated with increased numbers of IgG4-positive plasma cells that are not considered IgG4-related disease, including inflammatory diseases, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-associated inflammatory diseases, infections, low-grade B-cell lymphoproliferative disorders, carcinomas with prominent intratumoural or peritumoural IgG4-positive plasma cells, among others.

Cytology

There are no specific features of IgG4-related disease in cytology specimens. Aspirates show low cellularity; an inflammatory background of lymphocytes, plasma cells, and eosinophils; and spindle cells {27666423}. Cases diagnosed on cytology have relied on the integration of clinical, radiographic, and serological parameters {27666423; 21194763}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic histological features (lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate; storiform fibrosis; obliterative fibrosis) and plasma cell density depending on specimen type and individual organ in the appropriate clinical and radiographic settings; an IgG4+:IgG+ plasma cell ratio of > 40%.

Desirable: elevated serum IgG4 (> 140 mg/dL); evidence of other organ involvement; response to glucocorticoid therapy.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

IgG4-related disease is a chronic condition with a relapsing and remitting course. Glucocorticoids and B-cell depletion treatments generally effect a good response {25809420}.
