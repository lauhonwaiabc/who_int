5.1.1.3 Glucagonoma

Definition

Glucagonoma is a well-differentiated functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET) composed of cells producing glucagon and preproglucagon-derived peptide, with uncontrolled glucagon secretion, causing glucagonoma syndrome.

ICD-O coding

8152/3 Neuroendocrine tumour, glucagon-producing (glucagonoma)

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH87C1 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas & Glucagonoma, malignant

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Glucagonoma predominantly involves the pancreatic tail {29435000}.

Clinical features

Glucagonomas are characterized by glucagonoma syndrome, which includes a characteristic necrolytic migratory erythema (occurring in approximately 80% of patients); painful glossitis, cheilitis, and angular stomatitis (in ~41% of patients); onychodystrophy (in female patients); significant weight loss (in ~60% of patients); deep vein thrombosis; depression; new-onset or worsening diabetes mellitus (in ~70% of patients); normochromic normocytic anaemia (in ~50% of patients); hypoaminoacidaemia; and low zinc levels. In rare cases, glucagonomas are associated with dilated cardiomyopathy. The average time from symptom onset to final diagnosis is 31.4 months {29435000}.

Almost invariably, the necrolytic migratory erythema and cardiomyopathy resolve after successful removal of the tumour. Patients who cannot undergo curative resection can be treated with surgical and non-surgical debulking therapies, somatostatin analogues, everolimus, peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT), or cytotoxic chemotherapy like for other PanNETs.

A second (rare) syndrome associated with hyperglucagonaemia, but not the clinical glucagonoma syndrome, is the autosomal recessive inherited glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia syndrome (also known as Mahvash disease), in which numerous glucagon-producing PanNETs and microtumours are present, but the clinical syndrome is not present because of germline mutations in GCGR {29702528} (see section Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia).

Biochemical investigations

The diagnosis of glucagonoma syndrome is based on the combination of elevated plasma glucagon levels and symptoms previously described. Mildly elevated glucagon levels may be associated with several other diseases like cirrhosis, chronic renal failure, sepsis, acute or chronic pancreatitis, chronic hepatic failure, Cushing syndrome, acute trauma, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, stress, burns, portocaval shunting, and familial hyperglucagonaemia. However, a fasting plasma glucagon level > 500 pg/mL (reference range: 70–160 pg/mL) is diagnostic for glucagonoma {6268399}.

Imaging

Glucagonomas and their metastases highly express SSTR subtypes (97%) and therefore can be identified using SSTR PET with 68Ga-DOTA–labelled somatostatin analogues (DOTATATE, DOTANOC, and DOTATOC). Other imaging modalities include CT, MRI, and EUS {29432346}.

Epidemiology

Glucagonoma is rare, accounting for 1–2% of all PanNETs, with an estimated annual incidence of about 1 case per 20 million people. It is the fourth most common functioning PanNET after insulinoma, gastrinoma, and VIPoma {17896148; 22261919}. The average age of patients at diagnosis is 52.5 years, and there is a slight female predilection {9880781}.

Etiology

There are not known etiological factors for sporadic glucagonomas. Approximately 10% of glucagonomas are associated with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) (see section Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1) {9880781; 15201270; 25553769; 29435000; 31555796}.

Autosomal recessive inherited glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia syndrome (also known as Mahvash disease) is covered separately (see section Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia).

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Glucagonomas are solitary, well-demarcated, large tumours, ranging from 30 to 70 mm in size {8606627; 29435000}. Rarely, they are cystic {10457311; 20922407}.

Histopathology

Glucagonomas are composed of well-differentiated cells, which form densely packed trabecular structures in a scant fibrous stroma. The cytological appearance of tumour cells overlaps that of cells of other PanNETs. Mitotic figures are rare, as is necrosis.

Grading

Most glucagonomas are G1 or G2, but some may progress to G3.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells are positive for keratin, general neuroendocrine markers, glucagon, and SSTR2A {8606627}. Some cells may also be positive for PP.

Differential diagnosis

It is worth noting that PanNETs composed of glucagon-immunoreactive cells but lacking symptoms of glucagonoma syndrome are defined as non-functioning glucagon-producing PanNETs or A-cell PanNETs. They are frequently small and indolent tumours (or microtumours) associated with an excellent prognosis, whereas glucagonomas are larger and more frequently metastatic neoplasms.

MEN1-related glucagon-secreting PanNETs are distinguished from sporadic manifestations by the presence of the islet dysplasia–neoplasia progression sequence as well as the presence of a plurihormonal immunohistochemical profile in tumours (see section Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1).

Patients with Mahvash disease develop a multifocal glucagon cell–lineage hyperplasia–dysplasia–neoplasia sequence, characterized by multifocal glucagon-expressing neuroendocrine microtumours and PanNETs that do not cause glucagonoma syndrome. The latter should not be mistaken for a pancreatic glucagonoma (see section Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia).

Cytology

The cytology is the same as that of other PanNETs.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Rarely, glucagonoma occurs in MEN1 {22261919; 9880781}. FOXA2, which plays a crucial role in islet development, is reported to be deregulated in MEN1-associated glucagonomas {28188614}. In addition, biallelic inactivation of DAXX was reported in a sporadic glucagonoma {29688432}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a keratin-positive PanNEN (PanNET) with elevated plasma glucagon levels causing glucagonoma syndrome; Ki-67 immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: glucagon immunoreactivity.

Staging

The staging of glucagonomas is the same as for other PanNETs.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis mainly depends on stage {17896148; 25602797}. Metastatic disease at initial diagnosis is common in patients with glucagonomas, most commonly affecting the liver, followed by the lymph nodes, mesentery/peritoneum, bone, lung, and spleen {29435000}. The Ki-67 proliferation index was not found to have a prognostic impact in one study {17873310}. The overall 5-year survival rate has been estimated as close to 70%, and the mean survival time is > 6 years {8606627; 21470282; 29912000; 30631852}, although in a retrospective study, the average survival time was 32.1 months {29435000}. Death is usually related to tumour growth rather than tumour-related functional complications {8606627; 17873310}.
