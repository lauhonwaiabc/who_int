10.0.0.3 Langerhans cell histiocytosis

Definition

Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) is a clonal neoplasm of myeloid dendritic cells that have a Langerhans cell phenotype (CD1a and CD207 expression) and lack high-grade cytological features.

ICD-O coding

9751/1 Langerhans cell histiocytosis, NOS

9751/3 Langerhans cell histiocytosis, disseminated

ICD-11 coding

2B31.2 Langerhans cell histiocytosis

Related terminology

Not recommended: eosinophilic granuloma; histiocytosis X (obsolete).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LCH can be localized to a single site (single-system LCH [SS-LCH]), occur in multiple sites within a single system (usually bone), or affect multiple systems (multisystem LCH [MS-LCH]) {12376981; 11496348}. The most commonly affected endocrine organs are the pituitary and thyroid {31269469}, usually as a single affected site {8657721; 11235902}. Involvement of the parathyroid and adrenal glands is rare.

Clinical features

Involvement of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis often leads to diabetes insipidus and growth hormone deficiency {27273725; 31269469}, but there can also be a deficiency of gonadotropins (LH, FSH), TSH, and ACTH, as well as hyperprolactinaemia {31269469}. Thyroid LCH typically presents as a diffuse goitre or a single nodule {18439957; 20848238}.

Endocrine system involvement in LCH is evaluated using hormone assays and imaging {29198444}. Increased uptake by FDG PET can be used for staging in hypothalamopituitary lesions {17682085}. Neck sonography may reveal isolated or multiple hypoechoic and heterogeneous nodules in the thyroid {24696742}, which is a nonspecific finding.

Epidemiology

LCH is a rare disease, with an annual incidence of 5–9 cases per 1 million children and 1–2 cases per 1 million adults {32106306}. There is a slight male predilection {27273725}. Endocrine manifestations are reported in 15–35% and are more prevalent in childhood-onset LCH than in adult-onset LCH {27273725; 31269469}. Cases limited to the thyroid are extremely rare, and most cases with thyroid involvement occur in patients with multifocal disease {22019948; 22198822}.

Etiology

The presence of frequent BRAF and MAP2K1 mutations in LCH suggests it is a neoplastic process of myeloid origin {20519626; 24982505; 25202140; 24894775}. A subset of cases show antigen receptor gene rearrangements {20551822}. Cases of synchronous LCH and acute leukaemia of ambiguous or myeloid lineage suggest a possible clonal relationship {24186134}. Nevertheless, the disorder shares features with other IL-17A–related inflammatory diseases {24894775}. An IL-1 activation loop has been proposed, based on elevated levels of Merkel cell polyomavirus DNA sequences, high levels of the tyrosine phosphatase SHP-1 in lesional tissues, and increased IL-17A in peripheral blood and lesional tissues, particularly in multiorgan disease {24321520; 25889448}.

Pathogenesis

About 50% of cases of LCH have BRAF p.V600E mutations {24625419}. Mutations in MAP2K1 are identified in one third to half of the BRAF-wildtype cases {24982505; 25202140}. Mutations in other genes in the MAPK pathway (ARAF and MAP3K1) or other signalling pathways (PIK3CA) have also been described {25858893; 24652991; 25899310; 26414464}.

Macroscopic appearance

None

Histopathology

LCH is characterized by histiocytoid cells with grooved, convoluted, indented, or lobed nuclei {26414464; 8657721} and moderately abundant, lightly eosinophilic cytoplasm. The background often includes eosinophils with a variable admixture of neutrophils and lymphocytes.

In the thyroid, the LCH cells show interstitial infiltration and destruction of thyroid follicles. A background of chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis is common. Sometimes, thyroid LCH can spread to cervical lymph nodes {25349760}.

The ultrastructural hallmark is the cytoplasmic Birbeck granules, which have a tennis-racket shape (200–400 nm long and 33 nm wide) and a zipper-like appearance.

LCH cells are positive for CD1a, S100, and CD207 (langerin) {26414464}. VE1 immunostaining (anti–BRAF p.V600E) is positive in half of the cases and correlates with BRAF mutations by sequencing or PCR {24625419}.

Cytology

Cytological preparations show cellular smears with large mononuclear or multinucleated histiocytoid cells with irregular nuclear contours, nuclear grooves, and foamy granular cytoplasm {26414464; 33493367; 18502328}. The inflammatory background contains eosinophils, occasional Charcot–Leyden crystals, and scattered lymphocytes {33493367; 23860349; 25596951; 28407623}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not applicable

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clusters/sheets of histiocytoid cells with grooved to contorted nuclei positive for immunohistochemical markers of Langerhans cells (CD1a+, CD207+) in the correct histological/anatomical context.

Desirable: mutation analysis in the MAPK pathway, with sensitive testing for low-frequency allele detection.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

In reports before targeted therapy was available, the clinical outcome depended largely on the extent of the disease (SS-LCH vs MS-LCH), with ≥ 99% survival for SS-LCH with unifocal disease. MS-LCH with risk-organ involvement has a variable course, with only ~60% of patients achieving no evidence of disease after 1 year on standard therapy. In MS-LCH without high-risk organ involvement, there is still about a 37% relapse/reactivation rate after 1 year of standard therapy with vinblastine and prednisone {11343051; 12376981; 23589673; 11496348; 30157397}. There are promising clinical responses in patients with relapsed and refractory LCH treated with BRAF or MEK inhibitors {33177341}.
