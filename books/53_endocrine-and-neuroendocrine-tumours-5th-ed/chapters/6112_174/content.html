6.1.1.2 Adrenal cysts

Definition

Adrenal cysts are benign, circumscribed, fluid-containing, tumour-like lesions.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LC8Y Other specified structural developmental anomalies of the adrenal glands

Related terminology

Not recommended: cystic adrenal tumour.

Subtype(s)

Endothelial (vascular) cyst; epithelial (mesothelial) cyst; parasitic cyst; pseudocyst

Localization

They occur in adrenal glands and may show retroperitoneal extension.

Clinical features

Adrenal cysts occur mainly in the third to sixth decade of life {15378490; 25447606}. They are often detected as incidentalomas but may also present with mass-related nonspecific symptoms (e.g. abdominal pain, vena cava compression). Ultrasonography, CT, and MRI are useful in recognizing cystic lesions but are often inadequate for their subtyping {15378490; 2504065; 29194699}. Large cysts and pseudocysts may simulate malignancy {33295135; 30041023} [[Duan K, Giordano TJ, Mete O. Adrenal cortical proliferations. In: Mete O, Asa SL, editors. Endocrine pathology. Cambridge (UK): Cambridge University Press; 2016. pp. 602–27.]].

Epidemiology

Adrenal cysts represent approximately 4% of adrenal masses {30041023}, with a prevalence rate of 0.06–0.18% in autopsy series {15378490; 15536959; 25447606}. Pseudocysts (39–78%) are the most common primary adrenal cysts followed by endothelial, epithelial, and parasitic cysts {18204230; 5901251; 33295135; 9585385; 18972224; 15378490}.

Etiology

Depending on the subtype, cysts can be developmental, infectious, post-haemorrhagic, or traumatic.

Pathogenesis

Endothelial cysts are postulated to result from dilated and thrombosed vessels, whereas pseudocysts are thought to be post-haemorrhagic or traumatic [[Duan K, Giordano TJ, Mete O. Adrenal cortical proliferations. In: Mete O, Asa SL, editors. Endocrine pathology. Cambridge (UK): Cambridge University Press; 2016. pp. 602–27.]] or may be related to vascular cysts with repeated haemorrhage. Epithelial cysts are related to mesothelial rests. Echinococcus infestations cause parasitic cysts {18204230; 33643845; 32417730}.

Macroscopic appearance

Cysts are often unilocular, and they have a thin fibrous wall with clear or haemorrhagic/fibrinous content {15378490; 18972224}.

Histopathology

Cysts have a thin wall. Endothelial cysts more commonly arise from the lymphatic endothelium and may show papillary protrusions into the lumen. Epithelial cysts are lined by mesothelial cells {30041023}. Immunohistochemistry for epithelial and endothelial markers helps in the distinction between endothelial and epithelial cysts {18446449}. Parasitic (hydatid) cysts are uniloculated or multiloculated, with a fibrous wall, calcifications, and clear fluid content {27672642; 32417730; 33643845}. Pseudocysts are often demarcated by dense fibrous tissue, lack a cellular lining, and contain fibrinous/haemorrhagic material with cholesterol clefts and haemosiderin-laden macrophages {30041023}. The differential diagnosis includes cystic degeneration in adrenocortical neoplasms, cystic phaeochromocytoma, vascular tumours or malformations, cystic renal cell carcinoma, and ectopic thyroid {23618356; 18972224; 33295135; 15378490; 23510370; 30041023}.

Cytology

Pseudocysts on FNAB can yield nonspecific proteinaceous material, whereas vascular tumours generally yield blood and are usually sampled in the workup of adrenal masses {6587703; 15376200}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Pseudocyst

Essential: no cyst lining or parasitic infestation; haemorrhagic/fibrinous cyst content.

Epithelial (mesothelial) cyst

Essential: a cyst lined by epithelial cells; often positive for mesothelial markers.

Endothelial (vascular) cyst

Essential: a cyst lined by endothelium; positive for at least one endothelial marker (D2-40, ERG, CD34, CD31).

Parasitic cyst

Essential: a cyst related to a parasitic (Echinococcus) infestation.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Compression of surrounding organs, rupture, and haemorrhage are potential complications.
