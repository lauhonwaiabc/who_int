13.0.0.12 DICER1 syndrome

Definition

DICER1 syndrome is an autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndrome caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in DICER1. It is characterized by an increased incidence of benign and malignant lesions involving multiple organ systems, including pleuropulmonary blastoma (PPB), cystic nephroma, embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma of the uterine cervix, and endocrine-related lesions such as multiple benign and malignant thyroid neoplasms, ovarian Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour (SLCT), and pituitary blastoma.

Subtype(s)

GLOW phenotype (global developmental delay, lung cysts, overgrowth, and Wilms tumour), associated with mosaic DICER1 RNase IIIb hotspot mutations

Localization

Associated tumours arise in almost all major organ systems, including the thyroid, lungs and pleura, kidneys, urinary bladder, brain, liver, genitourinary tract (predominantly the ovaries, uterine corpus, and cervix in female patients, and the testes and paratestes in male patients), eye, and pituitary and pineal glands {31342592; 34599283}. Individuals with clinically overt DICER1 syndrome typically manifest one or two sites of disease in childhood or early adulthood; those with predisposing mosaic RNase IIIb hotspot mutations often develop numerous sites of disease, generally at an earlier age than those with germline variants {26925222}. Bilateral disease in paired organs is not unusual {32333409}.

Clinical features

DICER1 syndrome features a number of characteristic tumours and dysplastic conditions {31342592; 34599283}. #24200Table 13.07 summarizes the associated conditions, their age ranges, and their specificities for the syndrome; the syndrome phenotype is still emerging and may not be limited to the listed conditions. The most notable endocrine manifestations are multinodular goitre, differentiated thyroid carcinoma (DTC), and (more rarely) poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma {31937902}, as well as ovarian sex cord stromal tumours, particularly SLCT. DICER1-related gynandroblastoma, juvenile granulosa cell tumours, and ovarian and testicular Sertoli cell tumours have also been reported {29660837; 32910017; 33782093}. These gonadal tumours may produce symptoms relating to abnormal hormone secretion. Pituitary blastoma is associated with Cushing disease in infants {33236116}. Non-neoplastic syndrome manifestations include macrocephaly {27441995} and ocular {30339877}, structural renal {30178239}, and dental abnormalities {31313479}. A subset of patients with mosaic RNase IIIb {24676357} and germline RNase IIIa {33208384} hotspot mutations also exhibit global developmental delay and/or overgrowth (GLOW phenotype [global developmental delay, lung cysts, overgrowth, and Wilms tumour]).

Epidemiology

DICER1 syndrome is rare and exhibits incomplete penetrance that is dependent on both age and sex. In a recent study using the genome-first approach, it was determined that putative loss-of-function DICER1 sequence variants are present in about 1 in 4000 individuals {33630087}, which is more prevalent than the previous estimate of about 1 in 10 000 individuals {30672147}. In this study, the penetrance of malignant tumours in DICER1 heterozygotes was 16% by the age of 50 years {33630087}. The most penetrant manifestation is multinodular goitre, occurring in about 75% of heterozygous women and 10–20% of heterozygous men by the age of 40 years {28323992}. The risk of thyroid carcinoma is estimated to be increased 9-fold to 16-fold {33630087; 28323992}. Macrocephaly is documented in about 42% of patients with DICER1 syndrome {27441995}, and lung cysts (presumptively considered PPB type I/Ir) have been observed in about 45% of systematically screened at-risk patients {26925222}. The penetrance of other tumours is lower. DICER1 syndrome disproportionately affects the female sex, a bias accounted for by the increased prevalence of thyroid disease and the presence of gynaecological manifestations in female patients {31342592}.

Etiology

DICER1 syndrome is caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic sequence variants in DICER1, located on chromosome 14q32 {25176334}. Therapeutic or diagnostic radiation exposure in patients with DICER1 syndrome may increase the risk of thyroid malignancy {24617712}.

Pathogenesis

DICER1 functions canonically as an RNase III endoribonuclease central to microRNA biogenesis. DICER1 cleaves 20- to 22-nucleotide-long microRNAs from each strand of a hairpin-shaped precursor; the RNase IIIb domain cleaves the 5p microRNA from the 5′ arm, and the RNase IIIa domain cleaves the 3p microRNA from the 3′ arm. Mature microRNAs function to posttranscriptionally silence expression of target genes {25176334}. DICER1 syndrome tumours follow a two-hit model of pathogenesis whereby germline variants, which typically inactivate one allele, are coupled in tumours by highly characteristic second somatic hotspot missense mutations, which affect one of five metal-ion binding or adjacent residues in the RNase IIIb domain {25176334}. RNase IIIb–mutated DICER1 exhibits impaired processing of 5p microRNAs, resulting in an altered 5p:3p microRNA ratio {23132766; 24909177; 33644822}, which, together with concomitant dysregulation of gene expression, is hypothesized to underlie tumorigenesis.

Macroscopic appearance

Other than showing a tendency towards multifocality, syndromic lesions are grossly indistinguishable from sporadic tumours.

Histopathology

DICER1-related endocrine manifestations

Pituitary blastoma is a characteristic DICER1-related neoplasm consisting of primitive blastemal cells, neuroendocrine cells, and Rathke pouch epithelium {21805093}.

Most patients with DICER1-related thyroid disease feature thyroid follicular nodular disease that consists of multiple benign follicular nodules with variable architecture and subtle atypia, or follicular adenomas including those with intrafollicular centripetal papillary growth {29474644; 29538609; 31937902; 33495912}. The diagnosis of malignancy is restricted to tumours with invasive growth and is enriched in low-risk follicular-patterned DTCs {29474644; 31701808; 32712880; 33495912}. Poorly differentiated thyroid carcinomas arising in childhood or adolescence are less common but also strongly related to DICER1 mutations {31937902}. In the appropriate morphological and clinical (e.g. absence of thyroid hormone administration or hyperthyroidism) context, the identification of variable involutional changes in the non-nodular thyroid parenchyma should raise suspicion of DICER1-related pathogenesis {29474644; 33495912}. Thyroblastoma, a rare DICER1 gene–related thyroid manifestation, shows a primitive microfollicular structure surrounded by primitive spindle cells with or without heterologous elements {32507920; 31917706}.

Ovarian sex cord stromal tumours are almost always SLCT, but occasionally they are juvenile granulosa cell tumours or tumours of mixed forms (gynandroblastoma). DICER1-related SLCT are always moderately/poorly differentiated, and the presence of retiform and heterologous elements is highly predictive of DICER1 mutation {28654427; 27664536; 30986800}.

Other manifestations

PPB type I cysts are lined by epithelium, beneath which lie primitive mesenchymal tumour cells. The solid component of PPB type II/III is characterized by a high-grade sarcoma featuring at least two of the following: blastema, embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, spindle cell sarcoma, and/or cartilage. Cystic nephroma is composed of variably sized cysts with a myxoid mesenchymal stroma, mimicking PPB type I. Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma of the cervix or bladder shows primitive cells under an intact epithelium, likewise resembling PPB type I. Nasal chondromesenchymal hamartoma is composed of cysts lined by respiratory epithelium, immature cartilage, and mesenchyme.

Cytology

Because of the risk of thyroid neoplasia, thyroid nodules often require further investigation by FNAB {28323992; 25900731}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Germline DICER1 alterations are most often loss-of-function (frameshift, nonsense, or splice-site) mutations and are distributed across the entire coding region of the gene. A small minority are deletions of the entire DICER1 locus, in-frame and out-of-frame intragenic deletions involving one or more exons, or (rarely) somatic mosaicism of loss-of-function variants or RNase IIIb hotspot mutations {31342592}. In contrast to the wide distribution of germline variants, the second somatic mutations in DICER1-related lesions occur predominantly at hotspots within the RNase IIIb domain. Therefore, identification of an RNase IIIb hotspot mutation in a tumour should lead to broader testing of DICER1 in the germline, because many panels designed to identify hotspot alterations may not identify the pathogenic germline variants. In a small percentage of tumours, the second mutation may be loss of the wildtype allele {31342592; 32124011}.

In DTC, somatic DICER1 alterations appear to be mutually exclusive with the most common somatic driver alterations, including BRAF p.V600E, RAS mutations, and RET rearrangements {29474644; 32228164; 29538609; 31937902}. Therefore, a useful clue to a DICER1 etiology is the occurrence of a dark-matter thyroid malignancy (i.e. a thyroid tumour with no other known driver) occurring in a younger patient, particularly if the malignancy is poorly differentiated or a macrofollicular pattern of follicular thyroid carcinoma {31701808; 32712880; 32228164; 31937902}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of a germline DICER1 pathogenic variant.

Desirable: a personal or family history of pathologically confirmed syndrome-related lesions, particularly those with a high specificity for DICER1 syndrome, as detailed in #24200Table 13.07 .

Staging

Staging of individual tumours is based on the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Presently, there are no data to suggest that the presence of a germline DICER1 pathogenic variant influences the prognosis of syndrome-related tumours as compared with their sporadic counterparts. In general, outcome is favourable after diagnosis of multinodular goitre, DTC, ovarian sex cord stromal tumour, cystic nephroma, embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma of the uterine cervix, nasal chondromesenchymal hamartoma, and ciliary body medulloepithelioma. The prognosis is worse for poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma {31937902}, and survival rates may be as low as 50% for PPB types II and III; pituitary blastoma; and anaplastic renal, peritoneal, and cerebral sarcomas {17895746; 25209242; 33236116; 32291395}.
