6.1.2.2 Adenomatoid tumour

Definition

Adenomatoid tumour is a benign tumour of mesothelial differentiation arising in the adrenal gland.

ICD-O coding

9054/0 Adenomatoid tumour, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F10 & XH6BY3 Benign neoplasm of mesothelial tissue & Adenomatoid tumour, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The left and right adrenal glands are equally affected.

Clinical features

Most cases are diagnosed incidentally during imaging or surgical procedures, or found at autopsy. Some cases are diagnosed during hypertension workup {18251587; 15692945; 14506647}, but this association needs further investigation.

Epidemiology

Adenomatoid tumour is a rare entity, with about 50 cases published to date. The age at diagnosis is 22–65 years (median: 40 years). There is a marked male predominance (> 90% of cases are in men) {19851133; 32769378}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Adenomatoid tumours have not yet been evaluated in the adrenal gland, but in the genital tract they harbour somatic missense mutations in TRAF7 {29148537} and do not show alterations in BAP1, NF2, or CDKN2A {29148537; 20518890}. Despite well-documented mesothelial differentiation, tissue origin is unknown given the absence of adrenal gland mesothelial lining. Proposed theories include an origin from mesothelial inclusions or from pluripotent mesenchyme associated with the Müllerian tract {12826889}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is usually well circumscribed, grey-tan, and solid, but in almost half of the cases a cystic component is reported; this is usually in the form of multiple small cystic spaces, giving a spongy appearance, but rarely, macroscopic cystic tumours are reported {19851133; 8933514}. The tumours measure 5–180 mm (median: 38 mm) in greatest dimension. Haemorrhage is also reported in some cases {18251587}.

Histopathology

The tumours usually show variably sized interanastomosing tubules, channels, and cystic spaces lined by flat to cuboidal cells with scant epithelioid cytoplasm or cytoplasmic vacuoles, often giving a signet ring–like appearance {12826889; 15692945; 8933514}. Some tumours harbour papillary formations {16498291; 11184184; 26243749; 8933514}. The tumours often reveal infiltrative margins. No atypia, mitosis, or necrosis is found. The stroma is fibrous and often harbours lymphoid aggregates. Other adrenal lesions are occasionally reported in conjunction with adenomatoid tumours, such as nodular cortical hyperplasia {14506647; 28382483}, myelolipoma {28382483; 18251587}, ganglioneuroma {28382483}, vascular cyst {15692945}, and cortical adenoma {25861051}.

The tumour stains for cytokeratins and mesothelial cell markers such as calretinin, D2-40, and WT1 {12826889; 15692945; 28382483}. Adenomatoid tumours should have intact BAP1 expression {27813512; 30640754}.

The main differential diagnoses include lymphangioma, angiosarcoma, mesothelioma, and metastatic adenocarcinoma, which should be excluded by immunohistochemistry {32769378}. Adenomatoid tumours show retained BAP1 expression {27813512; 30640754} and frequent L1CAM expression {29148537; 33667423; 33847416}. They harbour somatic missense mutations in TRAF7 {29148537}, and they do not show alterations in BAP1, NF2, or CDKN2A {29148537; 20518890}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a proliferation composed of tubular and cystic spaces lined by flattened or cuboidal cells in a fibrous stroma often harbouring lymphoid aggregates; expression of mesothelial markers.

Desirable: expression of BAP1 in selected cases.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours follow a benign clinical course after complete excision.
