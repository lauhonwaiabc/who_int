3.2.2.3 Thyroid tumours of uncertain malignant potential

Definition

Thyroid tumours of uncertain malignant potential (UMP tumours) are well-differentiated thyroid tumours with follicular architecture that are encapsulated or unencapsulated but well circumscribed, in which invasion remains questionable after thorough sampling and exhaustive examination.

ICD-O coding

8335/1 Follicular tumour of uncertain malignant potential

8348/1 Well-differentiated tumour of uncertain malignant potential

ICD-11 coding

2F9A & XA8RK3 Neoplasms of unknown behaviour of endocrine glands & Thyroid gland

Related terminology

Not recommended: atypical adenoma.

Subtype(s)

Follicular tumour of uncertain malignant potential (FT-UMP); well-differentiated tumour of uncertain malignant potential (WDT-UMP)

Localization

UMP tumours occur in the thyroid gland, although they may arise at any site where thyroid parenchyma may be present {27256097; 32189188}.

Clinical features

Like patients with follicular adenoma or non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features, patients with an UMP tumour usually present with single, oval, solid, slightly hypoechoic or isoechoic nodules with a hypoechoic halo by ultrasound that are typically cold by scintigraphy {27256097}.

Epidemiology

The prevalence is 0.5–3% of all thyroidectomies in institutions where UMP terminology is used in routine diagnostic practice {19543912; 27256097}. The mean age at diagnosis is 50–55 years, but the age range is wide {19543912; 27256097}. Female patients predominate (M:F ratio: 1:2–3) {19543912; 27256097}. The proportions of cases diagnosed as FT-UMP and WDT-UMP are similar {19543912; 31077238}.

Etiology

None

Pathogenesis

RAS-like molecular alterations promote the development of UMP tumours {25417114}. EIF1AX and TSHR mutations, as well as PAX8::PPARG rearrangement, have been reported in a minority of cases {19543912; 31077238; 33394038}.

Macroscopic appearance

The size is variable, with a mean of 20–30 mm and a few cases reaching ≥ 50 mm {19543912; 27256097; 31077238; 32282345}. FT-UMPs tend to be larger than WDT-UMPs {29692346; 31077238}.

Histopathology

General features are the same as those of other encapsulated well-differentiated follicular-patterned neoplasms such as follicular adenoma, follicular carcinoma, or encapsulated follicular variant papillary carcinoma. When the nodule is large its interface with the surrounding tissues should be examined particularly carefully. There are two histological subtypes of thyroid UMP tumours: FT-UMP, in which tumour cells have regular, round nuclei that lack the nuclear features of papillary thyroid carcinoma (nuclear score: 0–1 {27078145}); and WDT-UMP, in which tumour cell nuclei have papillary thyroid carcinoma–type nuclear alterations (nuclear score: 2–3 {27078145}). For the latter, if invasion has been thoroughly excluded, the term “non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features” should now be used (see section Non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features, and #25016Table 3.02). Some tumours are oncocytic {19543912; 31077238; 32282345}, others may display unusual features such as clear cells {30370813} or a glomeruloid architecture {33833889}. If there is high mitotic activity or tumour necrosis, the tumour should be diagnosed as high-grade encapsulated follicular cell–derived carcinoma with questionable vascular invasion. The term “UMP” should not be used for cases in which the question of invasion remains unresolved because of incomplete resection of a thyroid tumour or inadequate sampling. Pseudoinvasive artefacts such as those induced by FNA or surgical manipulation must be distinguished from true invasion {16879388}.

The tumour capsule is usually well defined and thicker in FT-UMP, and it tends to be thinner and sometimes incomplete or absent in WDT-UMP {19543912}. Invasion is questionable when there are broad protrusions of neoplastic cells deep into the capsule casting doubt as to the completeness of its penetration, such as tumour mushrooms abutting the outer surface of the capsule. In some well-circumscribed nodules, particularly in those where the capsule is thin or incomplete, irregularities of the contour of the nodule with outpouching of tumour cells may be inconsistently regarded as a sign of invasion.

The possibility of vascular invasion is more worrisome than that of capsular invasion, because of the higher associated risk of tumour dissemination. Cases with doubtful (or certain) capsular invasion upon close scrutiny may represent doubtful (or certain) vascular invasion. Intertwining of vascular spaces and tumour cells raises concern for early vascular invasion. Tumour cell nests may undermine vascular endothelium in the tumour capsule, and the variable protrusion of the nests within vessels can be variably interpreted as evidence for vascular invasion. Vascular invasion is questionable in the case of intraluminal tumour cell nests with a regular contour that have neither endothelial lining nor definite evidence of associated thrombus. In well-circumscribed nodules (particularly in those with an incomplete or thin fibrous capsule), it can be impossible to define whether intravascular tumour nests belong to the inside of the tumour and are therefore not to be regarded as evidence of vascular invasion, or whether the tumour nests are within the tumour capsule and are therefore diagnostic of vascular invasion.

Cytology

FT-UMP has the same cytological features as follicular adenoma or carcinoma, whereas WDT-UMP exhibits enlarged, clear nuclei and few nuclear grooves, but no pseudoinclusions. Most cases belong to the follicular neoplasm and atypia of undetermined significance categories (IV and III of the Bethesda system) {19543912; 22484994; 27256097}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HRAS, KRAS, or NRAS mutations are identified in as many as 30–40% of cases, NRAS p.Q61R being the most common. If BRAF p.V600E mutation is present, the diagnosis of NIFTP is excluded. If TP53 or TERT promoter mutations are detected, the tumour should be meticulously examined to rule out carcinoma {29692346; 31077238; 31561592; 32282345; 33394038}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: questionable invasion; the feature of concern (i.e. invasion of vessels and/or of the tumour capsule) must be specified; no high-grade morphology.

Desirable: molecular investigation for HRAS, KRAS, or NRAS mutations; BRAF p.V600E, TP53, or TERT promoter mutations in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most series have not reported nodal or distant metastases, tumour recurrence, or tumour-related deaths {20463572; 21455197; 21070478; 27256097; 31077238}. The risk of recurrence or metastases is extremely low for encapsulated follicular-patterned tumours where invasion of the tumour capsule is the only concern {1455315; 11169933; 16900519; 18571588; 20463572; 21455197; 21908930; 25721865}. In retrospective studies of cases that metastasized after an initial benign diagnosis, some have been reclassified as UMP tumours. In one series of 2978 cases originally diagnosed as benign thyroid nodule/tumour, 5 were found to later develop distant metastases: among these, 2 would qualify as FT-UMP (1 with questionable vascular invasion and 1 with questionable invasion of the thyroid parenchyma) {21908930}. In another series of 10 992 consecutive patients with differentiated thyroid cancer, slide review in 26 of the 39 cases with distant metastases after an initial benign diagnosis reclassified 10 cases as FT-UMP and 5 cases as WDT-UMP {32189188}.
