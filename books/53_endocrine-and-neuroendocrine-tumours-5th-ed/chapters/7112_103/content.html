7.1.1.2 Ganglioneuroblastoma, intermixed

Definition

Intermixed ganglioneuroblastoma (GNBi) is a form of peripheral neuroblastic tumour transitional between neuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma, composed of Schwannian stroma containing scattered well-defined foci of neuroblastic cells at various stages of maturation.

ICD-O coding

9490/3 Ganglioneuroblastoma, intermixed

ICD-11 coding

2A00.21 & XH0RB7 Mixed neuronal-glial tumours & Ganglioneuroblastoma, intermixed

Related terminology

Acceptable: Schwannian stroma–rich intermixed tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GNBi occurs most frequently in the abdominal cavity / retroperitoneum (usually adrenal), but also in the mediastinum, cervical region, and pelvis {28465480}.

Clinical features

GNBi often presents as an asymptomatic mass {27465021; 29369484; 19530234}, generally lacking the elevated urine catecholamines seen in neuroblastoma. Imaging shows an encapsulated, well-circumscribed mass. MRI diffusion-weighting characteristics may help distinguish ganglioneuroma/GNBi from neuroblastoma {20462716; 27340927}. Tumours are less likely to be MIBG-avid than neuroblastomas {27465021; 29369484}.

Epidemiology

Ganglioneuroma and GNBi together account for approximately 25% of peripheral neuroblastic tumours {27465021; 29369484; 19530234}. Compared with neuroblastoma, GNBi tumours occur in older children (median age: 4–7 years) and patients are less likely to have elevated urine catecholamines {27465021; 29369484}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Peripheral neuroblastic tumours originate from neural crest cells that give rise to adrenal medulla and sympathetic ganglia.

Histologically and genetically low-risk neuroblastomas (see section Neuroblastoma) may spontaneously mature into GNBi {10421272; 8618605}. MYCN oncogene amplification is rare (< 1%) {19530234; 22744966}. Segmental chromosomal aberrations are infrequent, but neuroblastic cells may harbour numerical chromosomal aberrations. DNA content is frequently diploid {19530234} because of an abundance of stromal cells.

Macroscopic appearance

GNBi is a solid, well-defined mass displaying a homogeneous pale-grey cut surface.

Histopathology

Well-circumscribed lacunar areas of neuroblastic cells embedded in neuropil are randomly distributed in ganglioneuromatous tissue composed of Schwannian cells and maturing/mature ganglionic cells. The neuroblastic cells comprise various stages of differentiation. No pushing borders are encountered between areas of neuroblastic cells and the ganglioneuromatous component {10421272}.

Cytology

Smears and touch imprints show a heterogeneous population of larger ganglionic cells with large round to mildly irregular nuclei; prominent single nucleoli; considerable coarsely granular, dark blue (Giemsa), usually eccentric cytoplasm; a variable number of smaller neuroblasts with the same features as those seen in neuroblastoma; and a variable amount of Schwannian stroma {31231909}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a Schwannian stroma–prominent tumour; well-demarcated microscopic foci of neuropil and mainly differentiating neuroblastic cells; a ganglioneuromatous component constituting > 50% of the tumour volume.

Desirable: immunohistochemistry for synaptophysin and S100 aids in distinguishing neuropil and Schwann cells.

Staging

All neuroblastic tumours are staged according to the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group Staging System (INRGSS) {19047290}.

Prognosis and prediction

According to the INRGSS, GNBi is usually a locoregional tumour, classified as low risk with extremely rare metastatic spread {27465021; 29369484; 20462716}. GNBi is usually chemotherapy-insensitive {27465021}.
