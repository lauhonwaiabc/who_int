3.3.1.3 Intrathyroidal thymic carcinoma

Definition

Intrathyroidal thymic carcinoma (ITC) is a malignant epithelial tumour with thymic differentiation occurring within the thyroid gland.

ICD-O coding

8589/3 Intrathyroidal thymic carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C27.0 Carcinoma of thymus

Related terminology

Acceptable: thymic carcinoma.

Not recommended: CD5-positive thyroid carcinoma; lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma of the thyroid; intrathyroidal epithelial thymoma; primary thyroid thymoma; thyroid carcinoma showing thymus-like differentiation; intrathyroidal carcinoma showing thymus-like elements.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ITC occurs within the thyroid (mainly in the lower pole of both thyroid lobes) or attached to the thyroid gland {16861971; 17210519; 21597887; 23176314; 26954328; 27643719} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Thoracic tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2021. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 5). https://publications.iarc.who.int/595.]].

Clinical features

Presentation is typically with a firm, painless neck mass in euthyroid patients, sometimes accompanied by hoarseness and/or dysphagia {26954328; 27110248; 29134304; 30416983; 31284751; 32860379}. The tumour appears as a cold nodule on thyroid scan, and ultrasound, CT, PET-CT, and MRI findings are nonspecific {26954328; 27643719; 30416983}. At presentation, lymph node metastases occur in as many as 50% of cases {16861971; 17210519; 21597887; 32594366}. Fever of unknown origin has been attributed to ITC in 1 case {30099866}, but neither myasthenia gravis nor polymyositis has been reported.

Epidemiology

ITC is a rare neoplasm, accounting for up to 0.15% of all malignant thyroid tumours {17085008; 27643719; 29134304}. Most published cases have occurred in Asian people {17085008; 17210519; 21597887; 22619154; 23176314; 23543082; 25648096; 27643719; 29134304; 30416983; 32594366; 32860379}. This tumour typically occurs in middle-aged patients (median age: 52 years, range: 25–79 years), with a slight female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:1.2).

Etiology

The higher prevalence of ITC in Asian countries suggests that genetic, ethnic, and/or environmental factors may contribute to tumorigenesis {26893738}.

Pathogenesis

ITC is thought to arise in or adjacent to the thyroid from intrathyroidal or perithyroidal thymic tissue, vestiges of the thymopharyngeal duct, or branchial pouch remnants (including solid cell nests) {2050369; 16861971; 23078135}. Gains on chromosome arm 1q, as well as losses on 6p, 6q, and 16q, are frequent chromosomal imbalances {21735166} and are similar to those found in thymomas and thymic carcinomas, thus indicating analogous tumorigenesis. Although KIT (also called c-KIT and CD117) is immunohistochemically expressed in all ITCs, no activating mutations of KIT have been detected {24934485; 32594366}. Somatic EGFR mutations in exon 20 (p.T790M) {29115061} and exon 21 (p.R841K) {32594366} have been found in isolated cases {24934485; 32594366}. TERT promoter mutation has been identified in some ITCs {32594366}. BRAF p.V600E {32594366}, KRAS {24934485}, and PDGFRA {24934485} mutations have not been detected. There is no association with EBV {32594366}.

Macroscopic appearance

ITC is a hard, pink-white, solid, lobulated tumour, usually lacking calcifications or cysts on its cut surfaces.

Histopathology

ITC displays histopathological features similar to those of thymic squamous cell carcinoma {2050369; 3984364; 16861971; 17210519}. There are expansile irregular lobules and cords of epithelial tumour cells, accompanied by sclerohyaline to desmoplastic stroma that is variably infiltrated by lymphocytes and plasma cells. The tumour cells are squamoid or syncytial-appearing, with occasional single-cell keratinization. They are oval or spindled with large oval nucleus, pale to vesicular chromatin, and distinct nucleoli. Nuclear atypia is usually mild to moderate, and mitotic figures are rare (< 3 mitoses/2 mm2) {3984364}. Some cases have obvious keratinization; others have basaloid features or prominent lymphoplasmacytic infiltration similar to that seen in nasopharyngeal non-keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma (basaloid squamous cell carcinoma or lymphoepithelial carcinoma, respectively) {2050369}. In some cases there are rosette-like arrangements and expression of neuroendocrine markers in addition to CD5 {3984364; 19067852; 31318494} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Thoracic tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2021. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 5). https://publications.iarc.who.int/595.]]. Focal glandular structures can be seen exceptionally {16389991; 19067852}.

Immunophenotype

Tumour cells are immunoreactive for pancytokeratin, CK19, p63, and p40. Like mediastinal thymic carcinomas, they express CD5 and KIT (also called c-KIT and CD117). They also commonly express BCL2, calretinin, CEA, and p53 {3056830; 9543665; 9796728; 23176314}. Scattered cells positive for chromogranin A and/or synaptophysin can be detected in some cases {23176314; 23920320}. ITCs are negative for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), thyroglobulin, calcitonin {21597887; 23176314; 26893738}, and monoclonal PAX8 {30210064}. The Ki-67 index usually varies from 2% to 40% {23176314; 23543082; 24934485}.

Differential diagnosis

Because of its better prognosis, it is important to distinguish ITC from primary or metastatic squamous cell carcinoma and from anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (including the squamous cell carcinoma pattern). Squamous cell carcinoma usually displays more keratinization and invasiveness and is negative for CD5 {16861971; 32252028}. Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma is also negative for CD5 and typically has more pleomorphism, atypical mitoses, and necrosis {17393982}. ITC contains a small number of S100A9-expressing cells in a scattered pattern, whereas both squamous cell carcinoma and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma have diffuse and laminated positivity for S100A9 {3056830; 17393982}. Cytology

Cytological features consistent with ITC are 3D tissue fragments without a papillary or follicular pattern, basaloid structures, loosely arranged clusters of round or spindly tumour cells, hyperchromatic to vesicular chromatin, distinct nucleoli, lymphocytic background, few keratinized cells, and occasional mitotic figures {22619154; 31318494}. Because the cytomorphological features overlap with those of other tumours, a diagnosis not more specific than that of carcinoma is usually made {8955605; 27643719; 31318494}. Immunohistochemical positivity for CD5 and KIT (CD117) in paraffin-embedded cell-block material can aid in a more specific diagnosis {8955605; 27643719; 31318494}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive squamoid or syncytial epithelial lobules delimited by lymphoplasmacytic-rich fibrous septa; mild to moderate nuclear atypia and generally low mitotic activity; immunohistochemical demonstration of thymic origin.

Staging

Currently, no staging system is available for ITC.

Prognosis and prediction

ITC has a more favourable clinical outcome than eutopic thymic carcinoma and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma {2050369; 21597887; 29134304; 32594366; 32860379; 32963401}. The 5- and 10-year survival rates are 90% and 82%, respectively {17210519}. Only occasional fatalities have been reported {3260644; 26893738; 30416983}. Unfavourable pathological findings are venous invasion, laryngeal nerve invasion {32860379; 17210519}, a high proliferation index {32860379}, and extrathyroidal extension including tracheal invasion {9543665; 16389991; 17085008; 17210519; 23543082; 27110248; 30416983}. Lymph node metastases are found at initial surgery in 40–50% of cases {17210519; 23543082}. PDL1 expression has been detected in ITCs and may predict response to anti-PD1 treatment {32594366; 31475102}. The roles of COX-2 and EGFR, and of TERT promoter mutations, remain to be determined {24934485; 32594366}.

Surgery is the mainstay of treatment {17210519; 19239992}. Postoperative radiotherapy may improve locoregional control of the tumour and may even improve survival {17095008; 27626313}.
