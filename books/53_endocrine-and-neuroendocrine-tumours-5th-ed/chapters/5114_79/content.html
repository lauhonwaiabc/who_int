5.1.1.4 Somatostatinoma

Definition

Somatostatinoma is a functioning, well-differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET) associated with clinical manifestations of inappropriate somatostatin secretion (somatostatinoma syndrome).

ICD-O coding

8156/3 Neuroendocrine tumour, somatostatin-producing (somatostatinoma)

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH9Z82 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas & Somatostatinoma, malignant

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Somatostatinomas can arise throughout the pancreas, but approximately two thirds involve the head of the gland {66472; 1969977; 7733095; 10374671; 17645474; 24413789}.

Clinical features

The clinical findings indicative of excess somatostatin secretion are not as dramatic or distinctive as the features of other functioning PanNETs {66472; 1969977; 7733095; 10374671; 17645474; 24413789; 26505735}. Tumours that demonstrate D-cell differentiation based on immunohistochemical labelling with somatostatin but lack symptoms of somatostatinoma syndrome, such as those observed within the ampulla and duodenum, should be designated as somatostatin-producing well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) and are not considered somatostatinomas.

The classic triad of somatostatinoma syndrome comprises diabetes mellitus / glucose intolerance, cholelithiasis, and diarrhoea/steatorrhoea as a result of hormonal suppression of insulin, exocrine pancreatic function, and CCK due to the elevated plasma somatostatin levels. Many patients with somatostatinomas also have gastric hypochlorhydria due to decreased gastric secretion. However, this classic triad is rare. These tumours usually progress asymptomatically and present with obstructive symptoms secondary to regional lymph node or hepatic metastases.

Biochemical investigations

Somatostatinoma syndrome can be confirmed by a fasting plasma somatostatin level > 25 pmol/L (> 60 pg/mL) {1969977}.

Imaging

The initial diagnosis of a PanNET is usually based on imaging studies such as CT, MRI, and EUS. Adjunct tests include SSTR scintigraphy (111In-pentetreotide [Octreoscan]) and functional PET: 68Ga-DOTATOC, 68Ga-DOTATATE, FDOPA, and 11C-5-HTP PET {9691916; 20652423; 31555796}.

Epidemiology

Somatostatinomas are rare pancreatic neoplasms, with an incidence of 1 case per 40 million person-years, and they account for < 1% of functioning PanNETs {17645474}. The mean patient age at diagnosis is 55 years (range: 30–74 years), and patients are more commonly female {10374671; 2820062}.

Etiology

The etiology and pathogenesis of pancreatic somatostatinoma are unknown. Ampullary and duodenal somatostatinomas are often associated with a history of neurofibromatosis type 1, but pancreatic somatostatinomas are rarely found in neurofibromatosis {7745981}. Patients with other predisposing genetic syndromes for PanNETs, such as multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 and von Hippel–Lindau syndrome, infrequently develop somatostatinomas {7496080; 10374671; 11040195; 15657529}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Pancreatic somatostatinomas are grossly indistinguishable from other PanNETs. These neoplasms are solitary, well circumscribed, and large (average diameter: 50–60 mm) {10374671}. On cross-section, pancreatic somatostatinomas are unencapsulated and composed of yellow to tan parenchyma. They are usually multinodular and demonstrate invasive growth into adjacent structures, such as the duodenum and spleen. Because ampullary and duodenal somatostatinomas are considerably more common than pancreatic somatostatinomas, careful inspection of the central location of the tumour and correlation with the microscopic findings are critical.

Histopathology

Most somatostatinomas have the typical appearance of other PanNETs. The neoplastic cells are cuboidal to round, with eosinophilic cytoplasm and uniform nuclei. They are often characterized by a trabecular, solid, and/or acinar architecture. PanNETs with a striking nested pattern, resembling paragangliomas, often immunolabel for somatostatin {32668277}. Vascular invasion and perineural invasion are frequent, but extensive necrosis is typically absent. A few pancreatic somatostatinomas exhibit histological features similar to those of ampullary/duodenal somatostatinomas, with a tubular and glandular architectural pattern and intraglandular psammomatous calcifications.

Grading

A systematic analysis of mitotic count and Ki-67 proliferation index has not been documented for pancreatic somatostatinomas, but most cases are intermediate-grade (G2) {26505735}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour shows diffuse positivity for keratins, INSM1, synaptophysin, and somatostatin and is less consistently positive or negative for chromogranin A. Scattered positivity for PP, calcitonin, gastrin, ACTH, glucagon, and insulin can also occur. Tumours that demonstrate D-cell differentiation based on immunohistochemical labelling with somatostatin but lack symptoms of somatostatinoma syndrome are designated as somatostatin-producing well-differentiated NETs rather than somatostatinomas.

Cytology

The cytology is similar to that of other PanNETs {21938182}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The molecular pathology of sporadic pancreatic somatostatinomas is not well studied. There are no known genetic differences from non-functioning PanNETs.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a keratin-positive PanNEN (PanNET) with clinical and laboratory diagnosis confirming somatostatin excess (fasting plasma somatostatin level > 18.33 pmol/L or > 30 pg/mL); Ki-67 immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: immunoreactivity for somatostatin.

Staging

The staging of pancreatic somatostatinomas, like that of all pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (PanNENs), follows the eighth editions (2017) of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]] and the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) cancer staging manual [[Amin MB, Edge S, Greene F, et al., editors. AJCC cancer staging manual. 8th ed. New York (NY): Springer; 2017.]] {28752469}, which rely on macroscopic assessment of site, tumour size, and metastases. This approach largely corresponds to that of the European Neuroendocrine Tumor Society (ENETS) classification {22525418}.

Prognosis and prediction

There are insufficient survival data for patients with pancreatic somatostatinomas. However, the 5-year overall survival rate ranges from 60–100% in patients with localized disease to 15–60% in patients with distant metastases. Large tumour size (> 30 mm) and lymph node involvement are poor prognostic markers {10374671}.
