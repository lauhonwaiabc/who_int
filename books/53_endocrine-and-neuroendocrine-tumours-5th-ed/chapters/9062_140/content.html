9.0.6.2 Neurofibroma

Definition

Neurofibroma is a benign peripheral nerve sheath tumour consisting of mature neoplastic Schwann cells intermixed with non-neoplastic cell types.

ICD-O coding

9540/0 Neurofibroma, NOS

9540/0 Ancient neurofibroma

9540/0 Cellular neurofibroma

9540/1 Atypical neurofibroma

9540/0 Plexiform neurofibroma

ICD-11 coding

2F37.Y & XH87J5 Other specified benign neoplasm of endocrine glands & Neurofibroma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Ancient neurofibroma; cellular neurofibroma; atypical neurofibroma; plexiform neurofibroma (see the Soft tissue and bone tumours or Central nervous system tumours volumes of this series)

Localization

Neurofibromas arising within endocrine organs are extremely rare, with only a few reported cases in the thyroid gland, adrenal gland, and pancreas {12067331; 22440272; 23744851; 24594377; 25657564}.

Clinical features

Sporadic and neurofibromatosis type 1–associated neurofibromas of endocrine organs typically mimic more common primary tumours in those locations {12067331; 22440272; 23744851; 24594377; 25657564}.

Epidemiology

Epidemiological studies of neurofibromas in endocrine organs do not exist, because of their rarity, although these tumours have been reported in both paediatric and adult patients.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Only the subpopulation of Schwann cells is considered neoplastic within neurofibromas {11438454}, and biallelic genetic inactivation of the tumour suppressor gene NF1 in this Schwann cell population is generally the only recurrent somatic event detectable {25293717; 28068329}. Therefore, complete loss of function of the NF1 gene product, neurofibromin (NF1), is considered a prerequisite for tumour development. Neurofibromin (NF1) is a negative regulator of RAS oncogenes {28666118}.

Macroscopic appearance

Neurofibromas are usually unencapsulated nodular or polypoid lesions. Cut surfaces are most often uniformly tan or grey, glistening, mucoid, semitranslucent, and firm.

Histopathology

Neurofibromas are unencapsulated tumours characterized by bland spindle cells with thin, wavy nuclei, representing the neoplastic Schwann cell, immersed in a variably loose myxoid stroma. The tumour cells are typically smaller than those of schwannoma. Stromal collagen is characteristic, often likened in classic pathology descriptions to shredded carrots. A variety of other cell components are also identifiable in neurofibroma, including perineurial and perineurial-like cells, fibroblasts, and mast cells. Nerve fibres are variably identifiable in intraneural subtypes, which are characterized by the expansion of single or multiple nerve fascicles; plexiform neurofibroma is associated with neurofibromatosis type 1.

Immunohistochemistry

Individual nerve fascicles are recognized by a peripheral EMA-positive perineurial layer. The neoplastic Schwann cells are positive for S100, SOX10, and collagen IV, albeit in smaller proportions than in schwannomas. Non-neoplastic cellular components include a limited number of EMA-positive and GLUT1-positive perineurial cells, as well as CD34-positive fibroblastic cells, this last component often forming a lattice-like network that is not seen in schwannomas.

Cytology

Intraoperative smears and FNA specimens of neurofibroma are often paucicellular, owing to the increased collagen matrix. Nevertheless, the presence of a mucin-rich background and small spindled cells with thin, wavy nuclei can provide diagnostic clues.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analyses do not have an established role in the diagnosis of neurofibroma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an infiltrative, low-cellularity spindle cell neoplasm associated with a variably myxoid to collagenous stroma.

Desirable: S100 positivity in the Schwann cell population, with CD34 highlighting a lattice-like stromal component.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Neurofibromas are benign, although plexiform neurofibromas in patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 are at increased risk of transformation to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour.
