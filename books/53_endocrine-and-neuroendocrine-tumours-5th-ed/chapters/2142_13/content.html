2.1.4.2 Plurihormonal PitNET/adenoma

Definition

Plurihormonal pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenomas are PitNETs composed of a single population of tumour cells expressing adenohypophysial hormones and/or transcription factors belonging to more than one cell lineage.

ICD-O coding

8272/3 Plurihormonal PitNET/adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures

Related terminology

Acceptable: unusual plurihormonal tumour/adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pituitary gland, with tumour invasion into the parasellar structures in some cases

Clinical features

Plurihormonal PitNETs/adenomas may cause clinical symptoms due to the hypersecretion of any or all hormones expressed in the tumour cells, or they may be clinically non-functioning.

Epidemiology

There are limited data on the epidemiology of plurihormonal PitNETs, because studies in which tumour classification has been based on the complementary use of immunohistochemistry for pituitary hormones and transcription factors are still rare. In a recent study including 1055 PitNETs, only 5 tumours (0.5%) expressed unusual combinations of PIT1, SF1, and various hormones related to the respective cell lineages {29434339}. In a case report, a double adenoma composed of a corticotroph component and a plurihormonal component expressing PIT1, SF1, growth hormone, TSH-β, and FSH-β was described {30610567}.

Etiology

The etiology of plurihormonal PitNETs/adenomas is unknown.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of plurihormonal PitNETs/adenomas is unknown. An origin from stem cells or progenitor cells with the capacity to differentiate towards multiple cell lineages has been hypothesized {29434339}. Gene expression profiles that overlap multiple pituitary cell lineages (SF1/PIT1 and SF1/TPIT) have been recently revealed using whole-genome mRNA transcriptomics analysis {31883967}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of plurihormonal PitNETs/adenomas does not differ from that of other types of tumours.

Histopathology

The microscopic appearance of plurihormonal PitNET/adenoma is similar to that of its individual cell lineages when found in pure form.

Immunohistochemistry

According to the authors’ experience and sparse published literature {30610567}, plurihormonal PitNETs/adenomas usually coexpress the transcription factors PIT1 and SF1, along with associated hormones from these two lineages that are present within the tumour. Recently, a plurihormonal tumour expressing all the three transcription factors has been reported in a patient with acromegaly {33852418}.

Cytology

The cytology of these tumours is similar to that of other pituitary tumours.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no molecular tests required to diagnose these tumours. Germline screening for inherited tumours should be considered on the basis of clinicopathological findings.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: immunohistochemical expression of adenohypophysial hormones and transcription factors belonging to at least two different pituitary cell lineages.

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms. A CT-era approach based on tumour size and patterns of invasion was proposed in 1973 by Hardy [[Hardy J. Transsphenoidal surgery of hypersecreting pituitary tumours. In: Kohler PO, Ross GT, editors. Diagnosis and treatment of pituitary tumors. International Congress Series No. 303. Amsterdam (Netherlands): Exerpta Medica; 1973. pp. 179–98.]]. Subsequently, a standardized assessment of parasellar tumour extension based on MRI was published by Knosp in 1993 {8232800}.

There are no pathology-standardized reporting templates yet endorsed by international pathology organizations. A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}.

Prognosis and prediction

There are no data providing prognostic criteria in plurihormonal PitNETs/adenomas. We anticipate that general prognostic markers combining cell proliferation and tumour invasion {23400299} of a PitNET/adenoma may also be applied to this group.

Expression of SSTRs may suggest responsiveness to somatostatin analogues; however, there are no studies describing the effects of somatostatin analogues in patients with plurihormonal tumours.
