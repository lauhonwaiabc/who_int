3.6.1.1 Thyroblastoma

Definition

Thyroblastoma is an embryonal high-grade thyroid neoplasm composed of primitive thyroid-like follicular cells surrounded by a primitive small-cell component and mesenchymal stroma with variable differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8970/3 Thyroblastoma

ICD-11 coding

2D10.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of thyroid gland

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant thyroid teratoma; immature teratoma; carcinosarcoma; teratocarcinosarcoma; malignant teratoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

One or both thyroid lobes

Clinical features

Thyroblastoma presents as a rapidly growing mid-cervical thyroid mass diffusely infiltrating one or both thyroid lobes and extending into perithyroid tissue {32507920}. There is a striking female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:3) and a median age of 43 years (range: 17–65 years) {32507920}.

Epidemiology

Thyroblastoma is a rare tumour that is likely underrecognized, with 8 recently reported and molecularly verified cases; most of these were initially diagnosed as malignant teratoma {27784193; 30083234; 31917706; 32507920}. Most thyroblastoma cases would previously have been classified as malignant (grade 3) teratoma of the thyroid gland {10699906; 15832097; 23702575}, and some as carcinosarcoma {30083234}. No epidemiological data are available, because of the lack of standard terminology in earlier series.

Etiology

Etiological factors other than DICER1 mutations are not known. Pre-existent nodular goitre was present in 1 case {32507920}. Older age at presentation and a lack of personal or family history argue for a sporadic neoplasm unrelated to the hereditary DICER1 syndrome.

Pathogenesis

Thyroblastomas are probably due to somatic mutations in DICER1, which encodes an enzyme involved in microRNA processing. DICER1 mutations have been detected in all tested thyroblastoma cases {27784193; 32507920; 31917706}.

Macroscopic appearance

On cut surface, a soft, friable, grey-whitish to pink tan-yellow mass infiltrating the entire lobe or both lobes and extending to margins and soft tissues, measuring 17–100 mm, is noted.

Histopathology

Histologically, thyroblastoma is an embryonal neoplasm that mostly resembles thyroid parenchyma within the first trimester of prenatal development. Thyroblastoma comprises three cellular components: (1) a component of embryonal-type thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1)+ / PAX8+ / focal thyroglobulin+ primitive-appearing thyroid follicles and fetal-type glands, (2) primitive small round to oval cells arranged into irregularly communicating solid aggregates and sheets with foci of necrosis and brisk mitotic activity that may form focal periglandular cuffs, and (3) a primitive spindle cell stroma of variable cellularity arranged into fascicles, usually positive for SMA and (focally) for desmin and myogenin. Foci of cartilage are seen in half of all cases, but well-differentiated adult-type organoid structures, pilosebaceous elements, and other skin adnexa as seen in genuine teratomas are absent. However, mature skin, skeletal muscle, and smooth muscle tissue have been identified focally in 1 case. 

Immunohistochemistry

The primitive small cells and the stroma show frequent rhabdomyoblastic differentiation, with desmin and myogenin immunoreactivity, but they are negative for TTF1, PAX8, and thyroglobulin. The primitive small-cell component expresses SALL4 but not OCT3/4 or PLAP. SMARCA4 expression is retained in the different tumour components {30083234; 31917706; 32507920; 32897630}.

Differential diagnosis

The entities include thyroid teratoma (benign or immature), carcinosarcoma, teratocarcinosarcoma, poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma, and metastatic sarcoma or poorly differentiated carcinoma.

Cytology

Smears show marked hypercellularity, epithelial proliferations, absence of colloid, and predominance of immature spindle cells in the small number of cases reported, and represent a challenge to FNAB diagnosis {32448730}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Pathogenic somatic DICER1 variants are present in all tested cases and help to distinguish them from teratomas {27784193; 32507920; 31917706}. Thyroblastoma lacks isochromosome 12p, a characteristic of genuine germ cell neoplasms {31120625}.

Tumours with an immature phenotype are a feature of DICER1 syndrome (due to germline DICER1 pathogenic variants), and the syndrome is also associated with frequent thyroid abnormalities: usually multinodular hyperplasia, but sometimes well-differentiated carcinoma (both papillary and follicular) {33630087}. Because it is possible that some thyroblastoma cases may be hereditary, germline DICER1 testing should be considered {32507920}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primitive thyroid epithelium; small-cell blastemal component; mesenchymal stroma with or without rhabdomyoblastic differentiation; absence of conventional thyroid carcinoma and conventional germ cell components; expression of specific germ cell markers.

Desirable: thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1)/PAX8/thyroglobulin expression within primitive follicles; minute foci of cartilage.

Staging

No staging system is currently available.

Prognosis and prediction

Thyroblastoma pursues a highly aggressive course, with > 50% of patients dying at a median follow-up time of 11.5 months.
