3.1.0.1 Thyroglossal duct cyst

Definition

A thyroglossal duct cyst (TGDC) is the persistence and cystic dilatation of the thyroglossal duct in the anterior neck, commonly midline.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DA05.Y Other specified cysts of oral or facial-neck region

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority (> 95%) of TGDCs occur in the midline neck, at or just below the level of the hyoid bone (75% are infrahyoid), with 2–4% just at the tongue base {22192899; 23458940; 27161104}. Rarely, TGDC may be seen within the thyroid gland (pyramidal lobe, isthmus) {9731820}. The lateral (non-midline) neck cases are usually displaced by recurrent infections or prior surgery {27161104}.

Clinical features

Patients generally present with a mobile, midline, infrahyoid, painless neck mass that moves upward with swallowing {19248939; 21237423; 22338239; 27161104}. An associated skin fistula (~10% of patients) is twice as common in paediatric patients as it is in adult patients {19248939; 22338239; 27161104}. A malignancy may be identified in about 3% {1745980; 9121176; 11510730; 23318645; 27161104}.

Imaging

By imaging, a midline round or elongated cystic lesion, sometimes with irregular walls, septa, and solid components, may expand into or destroy the hyoid bone {22338239; 23689821; 25234770; 30717638; 30725193}. The amount of thyroid follicular tissue is usually insufficient to be detected on radioisotope studies. Nodular soft tissue excrescences may suggest carcinoma.

Epidemiology

About 2.23 individuals per 100 000 general population have TGDCs, representing 60–70% of patients with congenital neck cysts {27161104}. Furthermore, in adult patients undergoing neck ultrasound, 0.9% were noted to have a TGDC {30467213}, and 7% of adult laryngectomy specimens demonstrate thyroglossal duct remnants {850454}. There is a wide age range (0.8–87.0 years), with a median age of 32 years {27161104; 23676810}, but with bimodal peaks in the first and fifth decades of life {21237423; 23458940; 27161104}. There is an equal sex distribution overall, although there is a slight male prevalence in the paediatric population and a slight female prevalence in the adult population {21237423; 23676810; 24332664; 25577275; 27161104}. In patients with carcinoma there is a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:2.3), with a mean age at presentation of 40 years {11510730; 21453651; 23318645; 24457400; 27704385; 28322121}, although children can be affected {12764621; 27704385; 28293858}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The thyroglossal duct is an embryonic developmental structure whose remnant remains anywhere along the thyroid anlage migration pathway from the tongue foramen caecum to a final pretracheal position. Normal involution between 7 and 10 weeks of gestation may anomalously persist, with dilatation resulting in a TGDC {7169333; 17720342; 23689821; 25577275; 27161104}.

Macroscopic appearance

A smooth-walled cystic structure includes clear, mucinous fluid, which can become purulent when secondarily infected. The size ranges from 2 to 85 mm (median: 25 mm); cysts are smaller in children than in adults {27161104; 18314017}.

Histopathology

There is an epithelial-lined cyst adjacent to the hyoid bone, containing respiratory or squamous epithelium alone, or a mixture of both (50% of cases show both), with squamous epithelium alone being more common in inflamed cases {27161104}. Some cases lack an epithelial lining. Cysts rupture frequently (65%), inciting fibrosis, with histiocytes, cholesterol clefts, and foreign body giant cell reaction. Thyroid follicular epithelium, commonly localized in the cyst wall as several millimetres of tissue, is identified in about 70% {27161104; 11404612}. Mucoserous salivary gland tissue (15%) and cartilage (< 1%) may also be identified.

Malignant neoplasms are identified in about 3% of TGDCs {27704385; 27161104; 29938764; 28293858; 25577275; 1745980}, with papillary thyroid carcinoma accounting for > 95% of malignancies {1745980; 11510730; 19565286; 21453651; 23318645; 25577275; 27161104; 27704385; 28293858; 28322121} and with other carcinoma types rarely detected {9413065; 21512669}. Soft tissue involvement is common (60%). Some patients may show concurrent malignancy in thyroidectomies performed as part of management {19565286; 21453651; 24457400; 25577275; 27161104; 27704385; 30091370}. C cell–derived tumours are not identified, because of an origin from the ultimobranchial apparatus, which is not seen in lingual-based foramen caecum lesions {27161104; 28536894}.

Differential diagnosis

The entities include epidermal inclusion cyst, dermoid cyst, branchial cleft cyst, bronchogenic cyst, cervical thymic cyst, and metastatic papillary thyroid carcinoma, which can usually be separated on the basis of the anatomical site of involvement, imaging findings, and pertinent histological features.

Cytology

Smears from TGDCs are often nondiagnostic, with a low cellularity and more inflammatory cells than epithelial cells {12049106; 19248939; 22277268; 25577275; 27161104}. The epithelial cells are usually squamous or respiratory, seldom containing thyroid follicular cells. Smears obtained from firm, fixed lesions may show nuclear features of papillary thyroid carcinoma when malignancy is concurrently present {25577275; 27161104; 27704385}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: perihyoidal location.

Desirable: a midline lesion; a respiratory- or squamous-lined cyst with associated thyroid follicular epithelium.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence is higher in patients with infected cysts and in cases not managed by the Sistrunk procedure, with perihyoidal thyroglossal duct remnants resulting in persistent clinical disease {30194760; 30388395; 23331819; 17165669}. Infection, rupture, haemorrhage, and fistula formation are recognized complications {19248939; 22249624; 23676810; 24332664; 29521676; 30388395}. When carcinoma is identified, the outcome is excellent, although staging is not presently required for extrathyroidal sites {19565286; 27161104; 32042395}.
