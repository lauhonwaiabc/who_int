9.0.5.1 Rhabdomyosarcomas

Definition

Rhabdomyosarcomas are a group of malignant mesenchymal tumours with skeletal muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8900/3 Rhabdomyosarcoma, NOS

8910/3 Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma

8920/3 Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

8901/3 Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma

8912/3 Spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y & XH0GA1 Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures & Rhabdomyosarcoma, NOS

2B55.Z & XH83G1 Rhabdomyosarcoma, unspecified primary site & Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, NOS

2B55.Z & XH7099 Rhabdomyosarcoma, unspecified primary site & Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

2B55.Z & XH7NM2 Rhabdomyosarcoma, unspecified primary site & Spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: myosarcoma; malignant rhabdomyoma.

Subtype(s)

Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS); alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS); pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma; spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma (SCSRMS)

Localization

Rhabdomyosarcoma has been reported arising as a primary tumour in the pituitary {16239205; 18037040; 26462864; 27422470}, adrenal {21412240; 23480948; 24348835} and thyroid glands {6870584; 15656963; 22642938; 23625578; 27595524}, and (rarely) in the pancreas {15332041; 19231522; 26092712}.

Clinical features

Rhabdomyosarcoma arising at endocrine sites is reported in both adult and paediatric populations. Patients with tumours may present with palpable masses (e.g. anterior neck / thyroid) or mass effect symptoms (e.g. visual disorders or headache from pituitary primary tumours, hypertension from adrenal tumours, or epigastric pain from pancreatic tumours) {15332041; 23480948; 32785833}. Rhabdomyosarcomas at endocrine sites are infrequently associated with prior irradiation {16239205}.

Epidemiology

Rhabdomyosarcoma is extremely rare within endocrine organ sites. In several reports, rhabdomyosarcoma coexisted with another endocrine tumour (e.g. pituitary neuroendocrine tumour [PitNET]) {22343339; 26462864; 27422470}. More commonly, however, rhabdomyosarcoma may present synchronously or metachronously with other endocrine tumours in some cancer predisposition syndromes. For instance, Li–Fraumeni syndrome (TP53 mutations) is associated with rhabdomyosarcoma and adrenal cortical carcinoma, and DICER1 syndrome is associated with rhabdomyosarcoma of the uterine cervix in addition to thyroid nodules or pituitary blastoma {29395683; 32539319; 32682898}.

Etiology

ERMS is associated with radiation and several tumour predisposition syndromes, including Li–Fraumeni syndrome (associated with an inactivating mutation of TP53), Costello syndrome (also called faciocutaneoskeletal syndrome; HRAS mutation), neurofibromatosis type 1 (inactivating mutation of one allele of NF1), and Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome (mutation or deletion of the 11p15.5 chromosomal region) {20186103; 27345568; 27617148; 31697451; 33372952}. Such associations are not reported for other rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes.

Pathogenesis

ARMS harbours a PAX3/7::FOXO1 fusion in most tumours {8098985; 8187070; 8275086}. PAX3 and PAX7 are transcription factors with essential roles in myogenesis {26424495}, with fusion proteins activating many downstream target genes, including MET, ALK, MYCN, and MYOD1, among others {8643596; 15688035; 20663909; 28446439; 30617281}. Less common fusions include PAX3 with FOXO4, NCOA1, and FGFR1 {12183429; 19953635; 21666686; 24436047}. SCSRMS separates into three genetic groups: NCOA2::VGLL2 rearrangement; MYOD1 mutation; and no identifiable genetic alteration {23463663; 30181563}. Skull/mandibular intraosseous SCSRMS shows EWSR1::FUS-TFCP2 {33382123}. As many as 50% of ERMS cases have RAS pathway mutations {30617281}.

Macroscopic appearance

Rhabdomyosarcoma is soft, fleshy, and grey, with variable amounts of fibrous tissue and occasional necrotic areas and haemorrhage. Deep-seated tumours commonly infiltrate surrounding tissues.

Histopathology

ERMS consists of primitive round to spindle cells, with scant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic nuclei. There may be scattered rhabdomyoblasts. Botryoid ERMS typically shows linear aggregates of tumour cells close to the mucosa (cambium layer), with a cellularity gradient {666648; 9724344; 17443282; 18985676; 26646016}.

ARMS typically shows fibrovascular septa separating cellular nests of small monomorphic round cells with scant cytoplasm, which tend to coalesce in the centre and are dyscohesive at the periphery. The solid pattern lacks fibrovascular septa and forms sheets of tumour cells {16006807; 24113309; 25186315; 28521080; 28875443}.

SCSRMS consists of a fasciculated proliferation of spindle cells with elongated nuclei and pale, indistinct cytoplasm, with interspersed spindled or polygonal rhabdomyoblasts with abundant, brightly eosinophilic cytoplasm {31950473}.

Immunohistochemistry

The skeletal muscle–specific proteins myogenin (MYF4) and MYOD1 are variably expressed both within and between tumours, the proportion of positive cells reflecting the degree of differentiation {16861966}. Occasional expression of keratin, neuroendocrine markers, S100, and ALK may also be observed {2455782; 16006807; 18973919; 23307059; 28875443; 33382123}, informing a panel approach to avoid misdiagnosis.

Differential diagnosis

The major differential diagnoses depend on the pattern of tumour, and metastatic tumours must be excluded {18606509}. Fetal rhabdomyoma, dedifferentiated liposarcoma, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation (formerly malignant triton tumour), Ewing sarcoma, haematolymphoid tumours, melanoma, carcinoma, and neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) must be actively excluded.

Cytology

Smears are highly cellular, with primitive uniform round, spindled, and stellate cells with scattered rhabdomyoblasts, sometimes in a tigroid background {16680771; 17636492; 24599626}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PAX3::FOXO1, PAX7::FOXO1, or NCOA2::VGLL2 rearrangement or MYOD1 mutation can aid diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for all rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes, a malignant skeletal muscle tumour showing:

ERMS: primitive round to spindle cell morphology ARMS: monomorphic, primitive-appearing round cells, usually dyscohesively arranged in a nested to solid pattern SCSRMS: spindled morphology with/without sclerotic collagenous matrix

Desirable (in selected cases): rhabdomyoblasts; myogenin/MYOD1 immunohistochemistry; detection of supporting genetic alterations.

Staging

Rhabdomyosarcoma may be staged according to the site of origin, using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) system for soft tissue sarcomas, with the exception of head and neck tumours, which are excluded. In paediatric rhabdomyosarcoma, modified TNM staging (the Intergroup Rhabdomyosarcoma Study Group [IRSG] grouping system) is used. This system relies on the site of origin, with risk stratification based on favourable site (e.g. bile ducts, orbit, head and neck, or genitourinary tract [excluding bladder, prostate, or parameningeal]) or unfavourable (other) site. The IRSG grouping system is used for surgicopathological evaluation, including assessment of margins and residual disease {22378628}.

Prognosis and prediction

Risk stratification predictive of outcome has been addressed with surgicopathological staging and fusion-based classification (IRSG grouping system) {24326270; 30373318; 30617281}. Overall, rhabdomyosarcomas are considered systemic diseases, with specific sites showing a relatively poor prognosis (5-year survival rate: 40–45%) {16025051; 22777866; 25186315; 28875443}. Age and tumour stage (most adult patients have stage IV disease at presentation) are the most important risk factors, with children and female patients having a better outcome than adults or male patients {23424037; 23743294; 31456361}. Patients with fusion-positive ARMS have a worse outcome than those with fusion-negative ARMS and ERMS, with a worse prognosis for PAX3::FOXO1 versus PAX7::FOXO1 fusions {20351326; 22447499; 22454413; 23526739; 24326270; 28035744; 30373318; 30617281; 31456361}. SCSRMS with MYOD1 mutations has a poor prognosis, whereas infants with VGLL2::NCOA2-positive SCSRMS have an excellent outcome {26501226; 30181563}.
