2.1.5.1 Multiple synchronous PitNETs/adenomas of distinct lineages

Definition

Multiple synchronous pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenomas of distinct lineages are multiple well-differentiated PitNETs consisting of at least two different cell lineages (PIT1, SF1, and TPIT), or a null cell tumour combined with any lineage-specific PitNET.

ICD-O coding

Code as PitNET/adenoma types

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures

Related terminology

Acceptable: multiple PitNETs of different cell lineages.

Not recommended: double PitNET/adenoma; triple PitNET/adenoma; plurihormonal PitNET/adenoma; mixed somatotroph and lactotroph PitNET/adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours arise within the sella but may show variable extension into parasellar structures {15212641; 16645024; 27209585; 30215160}.

Clinical features

Although biochemically non-functioning tumours can occur, one or more components of a multiple PitNET of distinct cell lineages can be hormonally functional {11383480; 27209585; 30215160; 31505284}. Acromegaly (elevated growth hormone / IGF1 levels), Cushing disease (elevated ACTH and cortisol levels), hyperthyroidism (elevated thyroid hormone with inappropriate TSH secretion), and galactorrhoea-amenorrhoea (elevated prolactin levels) have been reported in patients with multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages {1988594; 11081204; 15212641; 19955848; 27209585; 30215160; 30610567; 33538680}. Non-functioning tumours can manifest with visual field defects or headache {27209585}. Multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineage are often macrotumours with a mean tumour size of 22.3 mm (range: 9–39 mm), according to a well-characterized clinical series {30215160}.

Imaging

Contiguous PitNETs of distinct cell lineages have been reported to occur more frequently than non-contiguous tumours {27209585; 30215160}. Contiguous multiple PitNETs are often indistinguishable from solitary PitNETs on MRI studies even when examined by expert radiologists {27209585}. Occasional multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages may be distinguished on preoperative imaging studies or during pituitary surgery if they are clearly separate tumours {15212641; 27209585}.

In patients with Cushing disease due to a small microtumour, an incidental second PitNET may be a decoy lesion that can be mistaken for the source of ACTH and resected, resulting in failure of cure {27519220}.

Epidemiology

Synchronous multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages were reported to account for 1.2% of surgically resected PitNETs in a large series that was assessed using transcription factors in addition to hormone immunohistochemistry {30215160}. Non-contiguous double PitNETs were reported to occur in 0.7% of 600 PitNETs surgically resected in a series, wherein half of these were of distinct cell lineages {15212641}. In other series, the frequency of double PitNETs of different cell lineages ranged from 0.4% to 1.7% {1988594; 11059654; 11081204; 19955848; 33538680}, and autopsy studies have suggested even higher frequencies {1988594; 16645024}. Triple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages are rarer {30215160}. In one series, a female predominance was reported {27209585}; however, there was no significant sex difference in a larger series with a mean age of 55.2 years (range: 36–73 years) at the time of diagnosis {30215160}.

Etiology

The etiology is largely unknown, with the exception of reported links to germline susceptibility in some patients with double PitNETs (see Pathogenesis) {11081204; 15212641; 18300794; 30215160}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of synchronous multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages is largely unknown. Although one may suspect the possibility of an underlying genetic susceptibility, only a few cases showed a link to familial isolated pituitary adenoma (FIPA) {11081204; 15212641} or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 {11059654; 11081204; 18300794; 30215160}. The incidence of multiple PitNETs was more frequent in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 than in those without it {18300794}. Specific genomic studies on sporadic cases of multiple synchronous PitNETs of distinct cell lineages are lacking.

Macroscopic appearance

Multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages have been described as white to yellow soft tumours {15212641}.

Histopathology

Multiple PitNETs of at least two distinct cell lineages combine a PIT1, SF1, TPIT, or null cell tumour with at least one different PitNET type. Common combinations occur between TPIT- and PIT1-lineage tumours, or between SF1-lineage tumours and either TPIT- or PIT1-lineage tumours {11383480; 27209585; 30215160; 32370421}. Immature PIT1-lineage PitNETs and unusual plurihormonal PitNETs {30215160; 30610567} can also be a component of multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages. Accurate subtyping of each tumour component is required in the diagnostic workup of these tumours. Specific diagnostic criteria applied to individual PitNETs are identical to those applied to components of multiple PitNETs; however, the diagnosis of multiple PitNETs requires routine application of pituitary transcription factors {16299401; 30215160; 32813227; 33538680; 34017065}, because hormone immunohistochemistry alone may result in misclassification or mischaracterization {32813227; 33852418}. These tumours can show variable Ki-67 labelling indexes, ranging from 1% to 5% {30215160}.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no molecular tests required to diagnose multiple PitNETs of distinct cell lineages. Germline screening for genetic susceptibility should be considered on the basis of clinicopathological findings, such as age < 30 years, family history, and other syndrome-associated findings.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a PitNET with components of at least two different cell lineages (PIT1, SF1, and TPIT) or a single-lineage tumour combined with a null cell tumour.

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms. A CT-era approach based on tumour size and patterns of invasion was proposed in 1973 by Hardy [[Hardy J. Transsphenoidal surgery of hypersecreting pituitary tumours. In: Kohler PO, Ross GT, editors. Diagnosis and treatment of pituitary tumors. International Congress Series No. 303. Amsterdam (Netherlands): Exerpta Medica; 1973. pp. 179–98.]]. Subsequently, a standardized assessment of parasellar tumour extension based on MRI was published by Knosp in 1993 {8232800}.

There are no pathology-standardized reporting templates yet endorsed by international pathology organizations. A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}.

Prognosis and prediction

Accurate histological subtyping for each component of multiple PitNETs is of clinical significance {30215160}. High-risk (aggressive) histological subtypes of PitNETs and the status of invasive growth were found to predict disease progression in patients with synchronous multifocal PitNETs of different cell lineages {30215160}.
