12.0.1.1 Metastasis to the pituitary gland

Definition

Metastases to the pituitary gland are tumours involving the anterior and/or posterior pituitary secondarily by spread from primary sites in other organs.

ICD-O coding

Code according to histology of metastasis

ICD-11 coding

2E0Y & XA8J35 Malignant neoplasm metastasis in other specified sites & Pituitary gland

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The posterior pituitary is more frequently involved than the anterior gland or the pituitary capsule {24445565; 26186025}.

Clinical features

Pituitary metastases usually occur in patients with generalized metastatic disease {29974330; 32189207}. Although the pituitary can be the only site involved, pituitary metastases are rarely the first manifestation {26186025; 29974330}.

Headache, visual field defects, cranial nerve palsy, and ophthalmoplegia are presenting signs and symptoms {29974330}. Hypopituitarism and diabetes insipidus are common {29974330; 32189207}. Apoplexy is rare {29974330}. Rapid growth of a sellar tumour with infiltration of adjacent structures should raise the suspicion of metastasis. Metastases can be asymptomatic and discovered when imaging is performed for other neurological deficits {32189207}.

Epidemiology

Metastases represent about 0.6–1% of pituitary tumours {17287410; 26186025} and 0.14–28.1% of brain metastases at autopsy {24445565}. The incidence is increasing because of patients’ longer survival and more sensitive neuroimaging {26186025}. Peak incidence is in the sixth or seventh decade of life; there is no sex predilection {26186025; 30139817}. Metastases to pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs)/adenomas have been reported {28383413}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Tumours can spread to the pituitary via direct blood supply to the posterior lobe, involve the infundibulum via the hypothalamic–hypophysial portal system, or disseminate from the suprasellar cistern {22480977; 25667485; 31645015; 32189207}.

Macroscopic appearance

Metastases to the pituitary are intrasellar/suprasellar lesions, often with a dumbbell shape due to compression of the diaphragma sellae. Erosion of the sellar floor is common, and oedema of the optic apparatus can occur {26186025}.

Histopathology

Any tumour can metastasize to the pituitary, with breast and lung the most common primary sites {24445565; 31645015}. Histological features are similar to those of the tumour of origin and may appear as a discrete mass, small aggregates, or single cells. Distinguishing metastatic low-grade neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) from a PitNET/adenoma is challenging. An absence of pituitary transcription factors favours metastasis.

Cytology

Squash and imprint preparations show atypical cells, at times admixed with normal adenohypophysial cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing may have a role in identifying the primary site of origin of the tumour or in guiding therapy, depending on the tumour type.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: deposits of non-hypophysial malignant cells in the anterior and/or posterior pituitary.

Desirable: an absence of pituitary hormones and pituitary transcription factors in tumour cells (in selected cases).

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for the primary tumour metastatic to the pituitary gland

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is poor {26186025}. Outcomes are improving with earlier diagnoses and better management of primary and systemic tumours {25667485; 32189207}. Prognosis and treatment response depend on the primary tumour and extent of disease. A minority of patients die from progression of the pituitary lesion {26186025}.
