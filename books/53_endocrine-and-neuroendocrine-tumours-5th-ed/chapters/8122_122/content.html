8.1.2.2 Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

Definition

Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC) is a poorly differentiated neuroendocrine neoplasm (NEN) composed of non-small cells.

Subtype(s)

Localization

LCNEC can arise in any epithelium-containing organ (see Table #25597). In absolute numbers, the main site of LCNEC is the lung, where it represents 15% of all NENs {26039012}, although in other sites it may represent a higher relative proportion of NENs {18360283; 22534245; 31042566; 31882335}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation of LCNEC overlaps with that of any non-neuroendocrine cancer occurring at various sites and is dominated by mass compressive symptoms, lumen occlusion, ulceration, and bleeding. The disease often presents at an advanced stage, with early metastatic dissemination. Circulating neuroendocrine markers (e.g. chromogranin A, NSE) do not directly correlate with tumour grade {26370353}, and their levels may be lower than in neuroendocrine tumour (NET) {26482044}. 68Ga-DOTATOC/DOTANOC PET-CT and FDOPA PET-CT are not typically positive in LCNEC, which instead shows high FDG avidity {34292419; 19910422}.

Epidemiology

LCNEC is rare, accounting for about 2% of all primary malignancies outside the lung {23806528}. In extragenital organs, LCNEC preferentially affects male patients, except in the biliary tree, where the M:F ratio is 1:2.1 {32739935}. The mean age at diagnosis is in the sixth to seventh decade of life, but it varies with site: LCNEC of the uterine cervix affects premenopausal women in their fourth or fifth decade of life {22534245; 32408525}.

Etiology

Although etiological factors have not been defined, specific causative agents have been identified in selected locations (see Table #25597).

Pathogenesis

Although many LCNECs share genomic alterations with small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (SCNEC), LCNECs also share the genetic landscape of non-neuroendocrine carcinomas {33433884}. Specific data on lung LCNECs demonstrate distinct pathogenetic pathways, including mutations of TP53 and RB1 (78% and 38%, respectively) {26960398}. Three alternative or only partially overlapping molecular profiles have been demonstrated: (1) LCNEC with pulmonary SCNEC-like features, characterized by TP53 and RB1 mutations and, to a lesser extent, MYC amplifications and PTEN mutations; (2) LCNEC with non-small cell lung carcinoma–like features, which lacks TP53 and RB1 alterations and is characterized by STK11, KRAS, KEAP1, PIK3CA, and NOTCH-family gene alterations; and (3) LCNEC with NET (carcinoid)-like features, with a low mutation burden and MEN1 alterations {26960398; 29535388; 29066508}. Some cases classified as LCNEC in the lung or thymus would be regarded as well-differentiated G3 NET in the gastroenteropancreatic system. In analogy to the lung, TP53 mutations represent the most common molecular alteration in LCNEC of extrapulmonary sites, with the exception of LCNEC of the cervix, the pathogenesis of which is HPV-related {32544169}. LCNEC of colorectal and sinonasal sites lacks frequent RB1 mutations, which are found at nearly all other sites {33433884}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of LCNEC does not differ from that of non-neuroendocrine cancer at various sites (see Table #25598). LCNECs are often large and infiltrative lesions, with areas of necrosis and haemorrhage.

Histopathology

LCNEC shows organoid (solid nests, trabeculae, rosettes, and glandular structures) or diffuse growth. Necrosis is frequent, either extensive (geographical-chart necrosis) or focal. The cells are large, with irregular nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and distinct cell borders (see #25148Table 8.02) {1709558; 25065926}. Merkel cell carcinoma may show a spectrum of cytological features from small cells to intermediate and large cells. In the small and intermediate cell subtypes, a fine granular salt-and-pepper chromatin pattern is observed, nuclear moulding is uncommon, and nucleoli are small but distinct {11486166}. A high mitotic count is present, > 10 mitoses/2 mm2 by definition in the lung and > 20 mitoses/2 mm2 in digestive organs, and the Ki-67 (MIB1) proliferation index is usually > 55% {9706973; 1709558; 18360283; 25065926}. In a significant subset of cases, LCNEC associates with a clonally related non-neuroendocrine carcinoma (see section Mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasms) {27169712}.

Immunohistochemistry

Expression of general neuroendocrine markers (synaptophysin, chromogranin A, INSM1) is mandatory to confirm the diagnosis. Synaptophysin is considered the most sensitive marker and often is diffusely expressed in the neoplastic cells, whereas chromogranin A may be focal {29520563}. INSM1 has been reported to have a high sensitivity and specificity in NEC {31857137}. Low-molecular-weight cytokeratins are always expressed {29520563}. Site-specific transcription factors are not useful in identifying the site of origin of a metastatic LCNEC, with thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) being expressed in as many as 50% of cases, independently of the primary location; Merkel cell carcinoma displays a characteristic immunoprofile (see Table #25598) {29520563; 31857137; 30067951; 30349028; 31233624}. SSTR2 may be expressed in a subset of LCNECs, usually with a weaker intensity than in NETs {29520563; 31857137}. Abnormal expression (hyperexpression or complete absence) of p53 and, to a lesser extent, a lack of RB1 are frequently observed {22251937; 27259015; 28059096}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is generally elevated. In the digestive system the Ki-67 proliferation index is > 20% by definition and averages 50–80%, which is generally lower than in SCNEC {33809007}. In a series of 77 lung LCNECs, a wide range of Ki-67 proliferation indexes (1–64%) was observed {33025627}, generally lower than in SCNEC {33809007}. The Ki-67 index may be focally or diffusely reduced after treatment {33177340}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes SCNEC, high-grade (proliferating) NET (G3 NET in the digestive system), and poorly differentiated non-neuroendocrine carcinomas with neuroendocrine-like morphology or with classic morphology and (focal) immunohistochemical expression of neuroendocrine markers. A diagnostic algorithm, including morphological and immunohistochemical analysis, is helpful in discriminating among the various entities. The diagnosis of LCNEC should rely on morphology, proliferation index, and the expression of general neuroendocrine markers {29520563; 30485478}. Merkel cell carcinoma may be distinguished on the basis of CK20 expression and its peculiar immunoprofile (see section Merkel cell carcinoma) {30067951; 30349028; 31233624}.

Cytology

In smears, LCNEC exhibits intermediate to large cells containing variably prominent nucleoli, frequently in a necrotic background; single-cell component and crush artefact with nuclear streaming are frequent features {11135471; 11747218; 16138368; 18433011; 23166111; 29271588}. The application of pertinent immunostains, most feasible on cell blocks, is mandatory for a definitive diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: poorly differentiated neuroendocrine morphology; large cell (non-small cell) cytology; a high proliferation rate; immunohistochemical evidence of neuroendocrine differentiation (e.g. chromogranin A, synaptophysin, and/or INSM1).

Desirable: a high Ki-67 proliferation index (often > 55%); negative immunohistochemistry for SSTR2 and SSTR5.

Staging

Staging is the same as for non-neuroendocrine carcinoma of each primary site.

Prognosis and prediction

LCNEC has an ominous prognosis, with shorter survival times and higher recurrence rates than stage-adjusted non-neuroendocrine carcinoma at each site {16314638; 17075564; 17643676; 21867986; 25664707; 28459956}. Compared with SCNEC, LCNEC presents at a lower stage and is more likely to be surgically treated and to have a better outcome (e.g. colonic LCNEC has a 5-year survival rate of ~20%, vs ~10% for SCNEC) {23352435; 25664707}. In a non-surgical metastatic setting, median survival times are similar to those for SCNEC (~10 months) for chemotherapy-treated pulmonary and extrapulmonary LCNECs {22967994; 26898325; 29895024; 30482824; 31413741; 31601526}. Adjuvant or neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens are largely based on those employed in both SCNEC and LCNEC of the lung {26898325; 30482824}. Although controlled clinical studies are lacking, isolated cases of LCNEC responsive to PD1/PDL1 checkpoint inhibitors have been reported {28923100; 30642453; 32499913}. PDL1 is expressed in a fraction of tumour cells in 10–20% of LCNECs at various sites {28625622; 29037958; 29572007; 30885341; 32684990} and in the inflammatory cells in the majority of cases {32684990}. Systematic studies on the predictive value of PDL1 expression in LCNEC are lacking.

A spectrum of cytological features from small to intermediate and large cells has been described; fine granular salt-and-pepper chromatin pattern; nuclear moulding is uncommon but may be observed; nucleoli are inconspicuous; rosette-like structures may be seen; mitotic figures and apoptotic bodies are numerous
