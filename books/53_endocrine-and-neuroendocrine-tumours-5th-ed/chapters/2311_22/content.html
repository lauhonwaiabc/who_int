2.3.1.1 Pituicytomas

Definition

Pituicytomas are a distinct group of related low-grade neoplasms composed of pituicytes of the posterior pituitary and infundibulum.

Related terminology

Pituicytoma

Not recommended: pilocytic astrocytoma of the posterior pituitary; posterior pituitary astrocytoma; infundibuloma.

Granular cell tumour of the sellar region / granular cell pituicytoma

Acceptable: pituicytoma with granular cell features.

Not recommended: Abrikossoff tumour; choristoma; granular cell myoblastoma; granular cell neuroma; granular cell schwannoma.

Spindle cell oncocytoma / oncocytic pituicytoma

Acceptable: pituicytoma with oncocytic features.

Not recommended: spindle cell oncocytoma of the anterior pituitary.

Ependymal pituicytoma

Acceptable: pituicytoma with ependymal features.

Not recommended: pituitary ependymoma; sellar ependymoma.

Subtype(s)

Granular cell tumour of the sellar region / granular cell pituicytoma; spindle cell oncocytoma / oncocytic pituicytoma; ependymal pituicytoma

Localization

These tumours arise along the length of the posterior pituitary, forming suprasellar or sellar/suprasellar masses {33610869}. They may extend into the cavernous sinus and invade the sellar floor {29500747; 33610869} and have been reported to erode the clinoid process {13392185}.

Clinical features

Tumour presentations are clinically indistinguishable from non-functioning pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs)/adenomas and include headaches, visual field defects, and hypopituitarism {29102750; 29500747}. Diabetes insipidus is uncommon {21960498}. The tumours have been reported in association with synchronous functioning corticotroph and somatotroph tumours {26553184; 32577204; 32613546}; a small number have presented with hypercortisolism or acromegaly without demonstration of a synchronous PitNET/adenoma {32613546}. Massive intraoperative bleeding or spontaneous haemorrhage can occur, possibly owing to their hypervascularity {23701182}.

Neuroimaging

No specific features have been identified to distinguish these lesions from other clinically non-functioning pituitary tumours. They are isointense on T1-weighted MRI and show either homogeneous or heterogeneous enhancement. T2-weighted MRI reveals various intensities {21960498; 29102750; 29500747}.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare; there are no epidemiological data available currently. A recent meta-analysis of literature published in English or Spanish identified about 270 examples {30864049}. The ependymal type is the rarest, with only 14 cases reported {18992914; 21590491; 21951670; 24242699; 25893822; 29102750; 29188210}.

The majority of these tumours occur in adults in their fifth or sixth decade of life {30864049; 29102750}, with a higher mean age (61.6 years) in patients with oncocytic tumours {12170092}. Sex distribution varies in the literature; a slight male predominance is reported for pituicytomas and ependymal types {21960498; 29102750; 30030746}, whereas a slight female predominance has been reported for oncocytic tumours {21960498} and granular cell tumours {21960498; 30017767}.

Etiology

The etiology of these neoplasms is unknown. No germline susceptibility has been identified.

Pathogenesis

Ubiquitous nuclear thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) expression indicates a common derivation of pituicytoma, granular cell tumour, and spindle cell oncocytoma from the pituitary infundibulum / forebrain ganglionic eminence (ventral neuroectoderm) rather than from the endocrine cells of the anterior pituitary or from folliculostellate cells {19525896; 21590491; 24242699; 25893822}. Similarities between these tumours and subtypes of normal light, dark, granular, oncocytic, and ependymal pituicytes are in keeping with an origin from the posterior pituitary {7188885; 1547358; 19525896; 23887161}.

Genetic profile

Molecular genetic studies have been restricted to the most common tumours of this group, i.e. pituicytomas, granular cell tumours, and spindle cell oncocytomas. Methylation-based classification studies show close clustering, suggesting that these tumours may be a single spectrum of tumour types with a shared histogenesis, yet with distinctive morphologies {29539639; 34661724}. The IDH1 p.R132H mutation and KIAA1549::BRAF oncogene fusion are absent {23887161}. A limited number of case series identified various somatic alterations, with some evidence supporting the MAPK pathway activation in pituicytomas and spindle cell oncocytomas {27175596; 31046843}. Whole-exome sequencing identified several mutations, including in HRAS, SND1, and FAT1, in 4 spindle cell oncocytomas from 3 patients {27175596}. The HRAS-mutant tumour also had a MEN1 frameshift mutation {27175596}. Similarly, constitutive MAPK activation was found in 10 of 11 pituicytomas and included HRAS somatic mutations as well as pathogenic BRAF p.V600E, NF1, and TSC1 sequence variants {31046843; 33212494}. A spindle cell oncocytoma with BRAF p.V600E mutations has been shown to respond to targeted inhibition of the MAPK/ERK signalling pathway {31262927}. The whole microRNA signature of spindle cell oncocytomas shows global downregulation of microRNA expression, with a differential profile between primary and recurrent tumours versus the normal pituitary gland {30945144}. A recent study showed chromosome 1 alterations in spindle cell oncocytomas, including 1p deletion (in 4 of 5 cases) and 1p gain (in 1 of 5 cases) {33212494}. A more detailed analysis of a large number of tumours (47 cases) detected two epigenomic DNA methylation subgroups with only subtle overall methylation differences. The first subgroup, composed mainly of granular cell tumours and some spindle cell oncocytomas, lacked identifiable genetic alterations. Pituicytomas and a subset of spindle cell oncocytomas formed the largest cluster, with a high number of pathogenic mutations in genes of the MAPK/PI3K pathways (FGFR1, HRAS, BRAF, NF1, CBL, MAP2K2, PTEN); rare tumours had accompanying TERT promoter mutation. Pituicytomas and a subset of spindle cell oncocytomas were further segregated by the presence of copy-number alterations into a favourable group (no copy-number changes) and a less favourable group (copy-number imbalances present) that had a significantly reduced progression-free survival {34661724}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours cannot be distinguished on the basis of gross features. Texture reportedly ranges from similar to normal brain, to firm and vascular, with a grey to tan colour {18992914; 25777996}. They can occasionally be associated with haemorrhage {20495848; 30864049}.

Histopathology

Pituicytoma

Pituicytomas are composed of elongate bipolar spindle cells often arranged in solid sheets and short fascicles that can have a storiform pattern {10716149; 23701182; 23887161; 28766057}. Tumour cells tend to show distinct cell borders. These tumours lack eosinophilic granular bodies, Rosenthal fibres, and hyalinized blood vessels {23701182}. Inflammatory infiltrates can sometimes be present {19525896; 23701182}. Some pituicytomas may display regions with ependymal and oncocytic changes {24242699; 25893822}, again raising the possibility of morphological continuity among pituicyte-related tumours of the posterior lobe {23887161; 24242699; 29500747}. Pituicytomas typically show low mitotic activity, but rare examples with atypical features characterized by increased cellularity, pleomorphism, and mitotic activity have been reported {27744503; 31046843}. Their ultrastructural characteristics resemble light and dark pituicytes (two of the five ultrastructural classes of non-tumorous pituicytes) {7188885} that are enriched in intermediate filaments {23887161}.

Granular cell tumour of the sellar region / granular cell pituicytoma

Granular cell tumours consist of densely packed polygonal cells with granular eosinophilic cytoplasm due to lysosomal accumulation. The architecture is typically nodular; sheets and/or spindled/fascicular patterns can also be seen. PAS staining of cytoplasmic granules is resistant to diastase digestion {23701182}. The tumour cell nuclei are small, with inconspicuous nucleoli and evenly distributed chromatin. Perivascular lymphocytic aggregates are common. Mitotic activity is usually inconspicuous. Ultrastructural analysis highlights the abundant cytoplasmic lysosomal population of the tumour cells that confers the granular aspect of the cytoplasm seen with light microscopy. Neurosecretory granules are absent.

Spindle cell oncocytoma / oncocytic pituicytoma

Spindle cell oncocytomas are typically composed of interlacing fascicles and poorly defined lobules of spindle to epithelioid cells with eosinophilic, variably granular cytoplasm. Oncocytic change can be focal to widespread. Whorls, myxoid change, clear cells, osteoclast-like giant cells, and follicle-like structures can also be features of this tumour {21590491; 24721021; 25893822}. Mild to moderate nuclear atypia and (less commonly) marked pleomorphism can be observed. Focal infiltrates of mature lymphocytes are common. Hallmark ultrastructural features include an increased number of (often abnormal) mitochondria, intermediate filaments, and cell–cell junctions including well-formed desmosomes and intermediate-type junctions {12170092; 27031178}. Follicular structures {19642510} and intracytoplasmic lumina with microvillous projections have been reported {21590491; 25777996}.

Ependymal pituicytoma

Ependymal pituicytomas (previously known as sellar ependymomas) are described as tumours resembling either pituicytomas or spindle cell oncocytomas composed of spindled to plump epithelioid and oncocytic cells arranged in sheets and small fascicles {18992914; 21590491; 21951670; 24242699; 25893822}. Remarkably, the tumours show variable ependymal histological features, including perivascular pseudorosettes and true rosettes. Mitoses are rare and no necrosis is evident. Rosenthal fibres and eosinophilic granular bodies have not been reported in these tumours. Despite the presence of ependymal features, these tumours appear not to be biologically related to ependymal tumours arising in the supratentorium, posterior fossa, and spinal cord {32502305}. Ultrastructural analyses reveal tumour cells with ependymal-like features, including intracellular and intercellular microlumina containing microvilli and occasional cilia and desmosomal junctions, in addition to moderate numbers of intermediate filaments, rare microtubules, occasional lysosomes, and mitochondria {17044567; 18992914; 21590491; 25893822}. However, all these features have also been described in non-tumorous pituicytes with ependymal features {1547358; 7188885}.

Immunohistochemistry

These tumours invariably express TTF1 {19525896; 23887161; 21590491; 24242699; 25893822} and are negative for cytokeratins, pituitary hormones and transcription factors, chromogranin A, synaptophysin, and neurofilaments, as well as CD34 and markers of skeletal and smooth muscle differentiation {23701182; 23887161}. They show reactivity for S100 and vimentin; they may express GFAP, EMA, CD56, annexin, galectin-3, CD68, BCL2, CD44, nestin, and αB-crystallin {22585606; 23701182; 23887161; 28807344; 28668473; 30276594; 30485213; 17044567; 18992914; 21590491; 24242699; 29188210}. Granular cell tumours are also positive for CD68, α1-antitrypsin, α1-antichymotrypsin, and cathepsin B, whereas spindle cell oncocytomas are strongly immunoreactive for antimitochondrial antigen {21833579; 24823438}. Studies documented phosphorylated ERK, AKT, and S6 expression {22585606; 27175596}. SSTRs and DRD2s have been found {30607746; 31568904}.

Although these tumours typically show low a Ki-67 labelling index (often < 3%) {27744503; 31046843}, rare examples with an elevated Ki-67 labelling index (> 5%) have been reported {27744503; 31046843; 30485213; 33610869}.

Differential diagnosis

Pituicytomas may be distinguished from normal posterior pituitary by the identification of Herring bodies and the presence of axons, the latter identified by the expression of NFP. Unlike schwannomas, reticulin histochemistry shows no pericellular staining {23701182}.

Cytology

In cytological preparations, pituicytomas can display fibrillary to fine and wispy cytoplasm with occasional spindled cell morphology, sharing glial and meningioma-like features {31883319}. Granular cell tumour cytological preparations are characterized by a uniform appearance of the polygonal cells, with round to ovoid nuclei and abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm dispersed in a granular background {18064694}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No specific molecular test results are used in the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a spindle cell or epithelioid sellar neoplasm with diffuse S100 and nuclear TTF1 positivity and lacking reactivity for adenohypophysial neuroendocrine markers, transcription factors, and hormones.

Desirable: GFAP immunoexpression; for the granular cell subtype: PAS positivity (diastase resistance) and CD68 or α1-antitrypsin positivity; for the oncocytic subtype: antimitochondrial antibody positivity; for the ependymal subtype: EMA positivity with a dot-like, cellular apical, and/or luminal pattern.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Pituicytoma, granular cell tumour, and spindle cell oncocytoma are usually indolent, slow-growing tumours, curable by gross total surgical excision. However, there seems to be a higher frequency of recurrence in spindle cell oncocytomas, and most likely secondary to low rates of tumour gross total resection due to their rich vascularity and extensive adhesion to adjacent structures {15644783; 20495848; 23701182; 28668473; 30485213; 31496509; 33610869}. As noted above, the presence of copy-number imbalances in pituicytomas and spindle cell tumours has been significantly associated with reduced progression-free survival {34661724}. The prognosis of the rare sellar ependymoma is unknown; only 1 case with established follow-up has been described, with a lack of tumour progression and residual tumour control after radiation therapy within a 10-year follow-up after initial surgery {29188210}. Malignant transformation and distant metastases have not been reported in any of these tumours.
