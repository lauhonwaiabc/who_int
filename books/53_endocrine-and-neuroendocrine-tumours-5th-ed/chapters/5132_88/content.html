5.1.3.2 Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma-neuroendocrine neoplasms

Definition

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine neoplasms are pancreatic mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasm (MiNEN) subtypes composed of morphologically and immunohistochemically recognizable ductal adenocarcinoma associated with a neuroendocrine component, represented by poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) or, very rarely, by well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumour (NET). Both components constitute ≥ 30% of the tumour volume, and they are presumed to be clonally related.

ICD-O coding

8154/3 Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine carcinoma

8244/3 Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine tumour

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH6H10 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas & Mixed adenoneuroendocrine carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: mixed adenoneuroendocrine carcinoma (MANEC); mixed ductal–neuroendocrine carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NEC; mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NET (very rare)

Localization

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine neoplasms are most frequently located in the pancreatic head.

Clinical features

The symptoms are usually nonspecific and related to tumour growth and/or metastatic dissemination. One patient with mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NET presenting with Zollinger–Ellison syndrome has been reported {10628803}.

Epidemiology

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs account for about 0.5–2% of all ductal adenocarcinomas {10839610; 12647216}. About 18% of pancreatic NECs (PanNECs) have components of adenocarcinoma {24503751}. There is no sex predilection. The average patient age at presentation is 68 years (range: 21–84 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

No association with genetic syndromes has been documented. No definitive molecular pathogenetic data are yet available. Data from gastrointestinal MiNENs suggest a monoclonal origin of the two components, but this has not been well tested in pancreatic mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs {28456055}. Considering the molecular information of conventional ductal adenocarcinomas (KRAS, CDKN2A, TP53, and SMAD4 somatic mutations) and of NECs (TP53 and RB1 somatic mutations), mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs are expected to show the same molecular profile.

Macroscopic appearance

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NEC and mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NET are solid tumours measuring 20–100 mm in diameter.

Histopathology

In some mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs, the two morphologically recognizable components are intermingled and characterized by neoplastic ductal and neuroendocrine cells forming glandular, cribriform, solid, and/or trabecular structures. In other cases, the two components are distinct and consist of a ductal adenocarcinoma usually combined with a NEC, either small cell or large cell subtype. In very rare cases, the neuroendocrine component is well-differentiated NET {33309225}.

Immunohistochemistry

The ductal adenocarcinoma component is positive for CEA, EMA (MUC1), and/or MUC2, whereas the neuroendocrine component is positive for synaptophysin, chromogranin A, and INSM1.

Differential diagnosis

The most important differential diagnoses include ductal adenocarcinoma with entrapped islets, which can be intimately attached to well-differentiated neoplastic glands, and pancreatic NETs (PanNETs) with entrapped ductules {32538468}. Another consideration in the case of mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NET is the coincidental occurrence of a clonally distinct PanNET within the same pancreas as a ductal adenocarcinoma (collision tumour), which must be excluded before establishing the diagnosis of mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NET. The neuroendocrine component of mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NEC shows a high Ki-67 index (> 20%); the NET component of mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NET has a Ki-67 index < 20%.

Cytology

Cytology is not clinically relevant, because the diagnosis is made on resected specimen.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mixed tumour composed of two morphologically recognizable components; ≥ 30% of the neoplasm composed of each type; neuroendocrine marker expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Staging

Pancreatic MiNENs are staged using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for pancreatic carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs usually metastasize to lymph nodes and liver. Resectability is the most important prognostic factor. Patients rarely survive > 3 years. The 2-year and 5-year survival rates are 25% and 0%, respectively. However, the few prognostic data available seem to suggest that mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs have a slightly longer median survival time than pure NECs (20 months vs 12 months) {24503751}. Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NETs seem to show a better survival than mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs {33309225}.
