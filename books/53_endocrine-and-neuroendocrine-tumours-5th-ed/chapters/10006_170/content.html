10.0.0.6 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS

Definition

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS (DLBCL-NOS) is a neoplastic proliferation of medium-sized to large B cells with a diffuse growth pattern, not meeting the diagnostic criteria for specific large B-cell lymphoma neoplasms.

ICD-O coding

9680/3 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2A81.Z Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Centroblastic subtype; immunoblastic subtype; anaplastic subtype; germinal-centre B-cell–like subtype; activated B-cell–like subtype; diffuse large B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL6 rearrangements

Localization

In the endocrine organs, primary DLBCL-NOS can occur in the thyroid, adrenal, and pituitary, but it has not been described in the parathyroid.

Clinical features

Patients with thyroid DLBCL-NOS usually present with a mass in the thyroid, with or without cervical lymphadenopathy {22367111; 21371004; 15845932; 10800981; 10931213}. Rapid enlargement of the mass, hoarseness, dyspnoea, and increased serum LDH are common {22367111; 21371004; 15845932; 10800981; 10931213; 17071486}. Most patients (84–100%) have stage I/II disease {32982962; 23647064}.

Primary adrenal DLBCL-NOS is defined as lymphoma involving one or both adrenals, lacking a history of lymphoma, and with or without involvement of lymph nodes or other organs but always with the adrenal lesions being unequivocally dominant {23771429}. About 70% of cases are bilateral {28685093}. Patients usually present with abdominal pain and constitutional symptoms, although some patients are asymptomatic. Clinical and biochemical features of adrenal insufficiency are common {33071959; 32664092; 29344029; 23915571; 31192909}.

Patients with primary pituitary lymphoma usually present with a mass lesion manifesting as cranial nerve palsy, headache, and visual field defects, as well as impaired hormonal status such as anterior pituitary failure, panhypopituitarism, diabetes insipidus, and hyperprolactinaemia {33471665; 27581598; 11081150}.

Epidemiology

Primary lymphomas of the thyroid are uncommon, accounting for 1–5% of all thyroid malignancies and approximately 1–2% of extranodal lymphomas {10375088; 8838117; 11788631}. DLBCL-NOS accounts for > 65% of cases {19958938}. Thyroid DLBCLs are about 2.5 times as prevalent in women as in men, with a median age of 65 years {26118762; 22367111; 21371004; 11788631; 23647064; 32982962}.

Primary lymphomas of the adrenal gland are very rare, accounting for < 1% of all lymphomas, and with < 300 cases reported in the literature {23915571; 31192909}. DLBCL-NOS accounts for > 85% of cases {33071959}. The mean age is 70 years, and the M:F ratio is 7:1 {28685093}.

Primary lymphomas of the pituitary are even rarer, accounting for 0–0.2% of all pituitary lesions according to retrospective large surgical or radiological series {10207686; 17287410; 20874772; 21470998}. DLBCL-NOS accounts for 55% of all cases {33471665; 27581598}. In a literature review published in 2020, 23 of the 44 reported cases represented DLBCL {33471665}, with a median age of 65 years and with no sex predilection.

Etiology

Thyroid lymphomas are strongly associated with chronic lymphocytic or Hashimoto thyroiditis {10800981; 8838117; 8649497; 3141260; 17071486}. The odds of developing thyroid lymphoma are significantly increased for patients with chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis {3838363; 3936828; 21371004}. Sequence similarity is reported in the clonal populations in Hashimoto thyroiditis and the ensuing thyroid lymphoma {18006670}. Thyroid lymphomas often exhibit common homologous germline IGHV genes used by antithyroid antibodies, further implicating derivation from Hashimoto thyroiditis {16980947; 19128828}.

Pathogenesis

The genomic profile of DLBCL-NOS has been characterized, with different genetic subtypes being recognized {33642095; 29713087; 29641966; 32187361}. However, specific information on primary DLBCLs of endocrine organs is limited or lacking. In primary thyroid DLBCLs, MYD88 mutation has not been detected, whereas BCL2 or MYC translocation is found in a small proportion of cases {25423572}. In primary adrenal DLBCL-NOS, MYD88 p.L265P mutation and CD79B mutation are found in 24% and 48% of cases respectively, with the frequency being broadly comparable to that in various other extranodal large B-cell lymphomas.

Macroscopic appearance

The lymphoma forms a poorly defined, solid, fleshy tumour mass, which may exhibit necrotic or haemorrhagic foci {10800981}.

Histopathology

DLBCL-NOS shows replacement of the normal parenchyma by a diffuse, dense infiltrate of large lymphoid cells with round or irregularly folded nuclei, distinct nucleoli, and amphophilic cytoplasm. Mitotic figures are readily identified.

In the thyroid, the lymphoma cells can infiltrate into the thyroid follicular epithelium and follicular lumina. In a proportion of cases, a component of extranodal marginal zone lymphoma or follicular lymphoma is present, suggesting transformation from these underlying indolent lymphomas {22367111; 21371004; 10800981; 10931213; 25423572}.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic cells express pan–B cell markers, such as CD20, PAX5, and CD79a. They show variable expression of CD10, BCL6, IRF4 (MUM1), BCL2, and MYC. Subclassification into germinal-centre B-cell (GCB) and non-GCB subtypes can be achieved by immunohistochemical algorithms {24398326; 21135273}. For primary thyroid DLBCL-NOS, some studies report a predominance of the GCB subtype {21371004; 17429099; 25423572}, whereas others report a predominance of the non-GCB subtype {32982962}. In primary adrenal DLBCL-NOS, the non-GCB subtype markedly predominates {33071959; 31192909}.

Cytology

The aspirates show non-cohesive large lymphoid cells with variable degrees of nuclear pleomorphism. The diagnostic sensitivity of FNA is increased when supplemented with cell-block production and immunohistochemistry {30566902; 32609434; 32988789}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Usually not required for diagnosis

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a destructive infiltrate of large lymphoid cells; mature B-cell phenotype; exclusion of other specific entities of large B-cell lymphoma.

Desirable: immunophenotype or gene expression profiling to determine GCB or activated B cell–like / non-GCB subtype; reporting of isolated MYC or dual MYC and BCL6 rearrangements; genetic testing, if relevant for clinical decision-making.

Staging

Staging is by the Lugano classification, a modification of the Ann Arbor staging system {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with localized thyroid DLBCL have a favourable prognosis, with a median overall survival time of 9.3 years and a 5-year disease-specific survival rate of 75% {11788631; 19958938}. Poor prognostic factors include high stage, poor performance status, bulky tumour, advanced age, non-GCB subtype, MYC/BCL2 dual expression, and a lack of administration of rituximab {10800981; 21338283; 32982962; 23647064}.

For primary adrenal DLBCL, the 2-year overall survival rate is reported to be 68.3–72.5% {22889180; 33071959}. Treatment with chemotherapy and early-stage disease are associated with longer survival.

Information on the outcome of patients with primary pituitary DLBCL is limited. Among 17 patients, 10 were alive with no or stable disease at 2–52 months, 5 died of disease at 1–24 months, and 2 died of unrelated causes {33471665}.
