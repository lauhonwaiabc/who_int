3.2.2.1 Non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features

Definition

Non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP) is a non-invasive, encapsulated/well-demarcated follicular cell–derived tumour with a follicular growth pattern and nuclei resembling those of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), with an extremely low malignant potential.

ICD-O coding

8349/1 Non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features

8349/1 Subcentimetre non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features

8349/1 Oncocytic non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features

ICD-11 coding

2F9A & XA8RK3 Neoplasms of unknown behaviour of endocrine glands & Thyroid gland

Related terminology

Not recommended: non-invasive follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

Subcentimetre NIFTP (< 10 mm); oncocytic NIFTP (≥ 75% oncocytic cells)

Localization

Thyroid lobe or isthmus

Clinical features

Patients usually present with an asymptomatic nodule detected during routine physical examination or by imaging. By ultrasonography, NIFTP typically appears as a solid, mildly hypoechoic or isoechoic mass, round to ovoid in shape, with smooth borders and lacking calcifications {29317047}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of NIFTP varies between different regions of the world. Tumours currently classified as NIFTP were formerly classified as non-invasive encapsulated follicular variant of PTC (FVPTC). Among retrospectively reviewed PTCs, the prevalence of cases reclassified as NIFTP or previously labelled as non-invasive encapsulated FVPTC is much lower in Asia (0.5–5%) than in countries with populations largely of European descent (15–20%), especially North America {20066572; 27102347; 28293988; 29476382}. There are also significant variations between different institutions within the same geographical location {33484411}. Most of these discrepancies are related to different thresholds used to diagnose the nuclear features of papillary carcinoma and NIFTP {20066572; 27732333; 29476382; 33484411}. There is a female preponderance (M:F ratio: ~1:5) {27078145; 27660403; 33484411}. NIFTP can occur at any age, including in childhood {31483741; 33484411}. In most series, the mean and median age at diagnosis fall within the fifth decade of life {27078145; 27660403; 32057425; 33484411}.

Etiology

There are no known etiological factors in NIFTP.

Pathogenesis

RAS-like molecular alterations promote the development of NIFTP {25417114}. RAS mutations are the main drivers of NIFTP, occurring in 52% of cases, with most located in NRAS {27078145; 28192645; 30052723; 32124418}. Rearrangements of PPARG and THADA, as well as EIF1AX mutations, have also been reported but at a lower frequency (6–17%) {27078145; 28192645; 30052723; 32124418}. BRAF p.V600E is now considered an exclusion criterion for NIFTP {31452453}; however, BRAF mutations other than p.V600E, such as p.K601E, have been detected in 4% of these tumours {27078145; 30052723; 32124418}. In rare instances, RAS mutations have been found to coexist with TP53 and PTEN mutations {30052723}.

Macroscopic appearance

NIFTP is a well-delineated or encapsulated solid nodule that usually looks white/tan to brown on cross-section {28280647}. When present, the capsule is usually thin. If necrosis and haemorrhage are found, they are almost always due to a previous FNA procedure. There is a wide variation in the reported size, with tumours ranging from < 10 mm to 100 mm, with an average of approximately 30 mm {29052599; 33484411}. Because most NIFTP studies have excluded subcentimetre tumours, most publications do not reflect the accurate average dimension of this tumour type. In the rare series that include subcentimetre NIFTP, the median size is 17 mm {33484411}.

Histopathology

In order to qualify as NIFTP, a tumour must be totally encapsulated or completely demarcated from the surrounding non-neoplastic parenchyma. Because NIFTP is by definition non-invasive, the entire periphery of the lesion should be submitted for microscopic examination to rule out infiltration, capsular invasion, and vascular invasion. A third requisite is the presence of nuclear atypia. The nuclear atypia is usually not very pronounced and often multifocal, and therefore difficult to assess. A scoring system composed of a set of three nuclear features can assist in that endeavour. These categories are size and shape (nuclear enlargement/elongation/overlapping/crowding), nuclear membrane irregularities (including grooves and pseudoinclusions), and chromatin characteristics (clearing) {27078145; 29052599}. For each category of nuclear features, a tumour is assigned a score of 0 or 1, such that a neoplasm’s total score can range from 0 to 3 {27078145; 29052599; 29508145}. A score of 2–3 is necessary for the diagnosis of NIFTP {27078145}. Although the presence of diffuse well-developed PTC nuclear features does not preclude a diagnosis of NIFTP, these lesions need to be entirely examined histologically. This is essential because diffuse overt nuclear PTC features increase the probability of the lesion being classic PTC with a predominantly follicular pattern {29239036; 29052599}. The presence of a predominantly follicular growth pattern is the fourth mandatory element. The follicles can vary in size (microfollicular, normofollicular, and macrofollicular) and colloid content. A minor solid growth pattern (< 30% of the tumour) is allowed. Although abortive (short, rudimentary) papillary formations or hyperplastic Sanderson polster foci can be seen {27078145; 29052599}, true papillae (with a fibrovascular core and lined by cells with score 2–3 nuclei) should be very rare or absent altogether (< 1% of the lesion). There is a debate regarding the proportion of true papillae allowed. Because three studies have reported a small rate of metastasis in cases with < 1% papillae {28281551; 29343212; 28828746}, several authors have proposed that a complete absence of true papillae is a requirement for the diagnosis of NIFTP {29902314}. Subsequent series assessing the proportion of papillae in 235 encapsulated tumours with PTC nuclei found no metastasis in all non-invasive tumours with < 10% papillae, including 120 tumours fulfilling the original NIFTP criteria (< 1% true papillae) {31452453}. Because the vast majority of studies using < 1% papillae as a cut-off point did not report adverse oncological events {27078145; 27102347; 27660403; 28136139; 29204912; 30689169; 31452453; 30004059}, the original threshold permitting < 1% true papillae remains valid {31452453}. In addition to a papillary architecture, any of the following findings are exclusion criteria for NIFTP: psammoma bodies, a mitotic count of ≥ 3 mitoses/2 mm2, tumour necrosis, and cytological and architectural features characteristic of other PTC subtypes {27078145; 29052599}. For example, the presence of even a small number of tall or columnar cells is sufficient to exclude a lesion from the NIFTP category {29052599}. The presence of a BRAF p.V600E mutation (which is usually associated with other exclusion criteria such as papillae, psammoma bodies, or tall cells when complete histological examination of the entire tumour is performed) is now also considered an exclusion criterion for NIFTP {29052599; 29902314; 31452453}. NIFTP can have cells with oncocytic cytoplasm (oncocytic NIFTP) {30689169} and measure < 10 mm (subcentimetre NIFTP) {27102347; 29204912; 31963890}. Although NIFTP can be very small, the diagnosis is almost unattainable in tumours measuring < 2 mm and difficult in those < 5 mm if a capsule is not present.

Differential diagnosis

NIFTP is differentiated from invasive FVPTC by its lack of invasion, from classic follicular predominant PTC by its very scant papillary component (< 1% papillae), and from other PTCs by the absence of the cytoarchitectural features of these subtypes (e.g. tall cells). Those rare non-invasive follicular-patterned tumours with PTC nuclei that are excluded from NIFTP because of a mitotic count of ≥ 3 mitoses/2 mm2 are best reported as mitotically active encapsulated PTC with a predominant follicular growth pattern, and if the mitotic count is ≥ 5 mitoses/2 mm2 or if there is tumour necrosis they should be reported as non-invasive encapsulated FVPTC with high-grade features {34449926}. Follicular adenomas are separated from NIFTP by the lack of PTC nuclear alterations (PTC nuclear score: 0–1).

Cytology

Smears are composed predominantly of microfollicles consisting of crowded cells with enlarged, pale, irregular nuclei with grooves {27717284; 30339322}. Nuclear pseudoinclusions are rarely observed, and papillae and psammoma bodies are absent {27717284; 30339322}. Histologically proven NIFTP cases are diagnosed predominantly as atypia of undetermined significance (30%), followed by suspicious for malignancy (24%) and follicular neoplasm (21%). A malignant diagnosis, usually PTC, is rendered in a minority (8%) of cases {30426887}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRAF p.V600E mutation detected by immunostaining or genotyping excludes the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

Encapsulation or clear demarcation Follicular growth pattern with all of the following: < 1% true papillae, no psammoma bodies, and < 30% solid/trabecular/insular growth pattern Nuclear features of papillary carcinoma (nuclear score of 2–3) No vascular or capsular invasion No tumour necrosis Low mitotic count (< 3 mitoses/2 mm2) Lack of cytoarchitectural features of papillary carcinoma subtypes other than the follicular subtype (tall cell features, solid, etc.)

Desirable: immunohistochemistry or molecular testing for BRAF and NRAS mutation (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging is not performed for NIFTP {32920035}.

Prognosis and prediction

NIFTPs, including the oncocytic and subcentimetre forms, are extremely indolent neoplasms provided they are completely resected. A compilation of studies including > 1500 NIFTP cases reveals a rate of adverse oncological events < 1% and no reported mortality {28904306; 29204912; 30689169; 30913533}. Inadequate resection can lead to tumour recurrence {23025507}. Lobectomy without radioactive iodine therapy is the treatment of choice.
