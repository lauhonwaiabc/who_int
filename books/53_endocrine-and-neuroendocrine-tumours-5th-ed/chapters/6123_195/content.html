6.1.2.3 Adrenal melanoma

Definition

Adrenal melanoma is a malignant melanocytic neoplasm arising within the adrenal gland.

ICD-O coding

8720/3 Adrenal melanoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2D11.Y & XH4846 Other specified malignant neoplasms of adrenal gland & Melanoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: melanoma of the adrenal gland.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is unilateral involvement of the adrenal gland. Bilateral adrenal gland involvement strongly favours metastatic disease originating from an extra-adrenal primary melanoma {6479972}.

Clinical features

Primary adrenal melanoma usually occurs as a unilateral suprarenal mass. Patients may present with constitutional symptoms, flank pain, or symptoms related to mass effect, or cases may be detected incidentally on radiological imaging performed for other reasons {21722390; 29390427; 12746568; 3798606}. Most cases show no hormonal abnormalities {29390427; 31290484; 28536111}, but rare patients have elevated serum and urine catecholamines {27376430; 16508056; 15170557}. Tumour thrombus and extension into the inferior vena cava has also been described {23176924}. Adrenal melanomas are usually avid on FDG PET-CT {16026441; 27775937; 19943154; 22248874}.

Epidemiology

Primary melanoma involving the adrenal gland is extremely rare (< 30 reported cases) and accounts for < 0.1% of all adrenal malignancies {21722390; 29390427; 12746568; 27376430; 15170557; 23176924; 32399011; 16509796; 16771734; 10660767; 2194410; 859207; 20999933; 14367536}. The median age at diagnosis is 50 years (range: 24–78 years), and there is no racial or sex predisposition {21722390}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Melanocytes are not a normal cell population of the adrenal gland. However, adrenal medullary chromaffin cells and melanocytes share a common embryological origin through neuroectodermal progenitor cells of the neural crest {2039141; 8129941}. Therefore, it is hypothesized that ectopic melanocytes can exist within the adrenal gland and can undergo metaplastic and neoplastic transformation {22248874}. Nevertheless, it has been questioned whether the condition actually exists. The difficulty arises because an unrecognized primary cutaneous melanoma can occasionally undergo complete regression and disappear without a trace, whereas an adrenal metastasis that has arisen from it may continue to grow and eventually be detected as an apparently isolated focus of melanoma. Molecular analysis of carefully selected cases may address this hypothesis {32581366}.

Isolated cases with documented mutation testing showed a wildtype BRAF genotype {29390427; 27376430; 32399011}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are often large (60–170 mm), with attenuation of the surrounding adrenal gland. The cut surface often shows areas of dark-brown discolouration, haemorrhage, and cystic necrosis {21722390; 29390427; 23784764}. The tumour may involve adjacent structures, including large vessels {23176924}.

Histopathology

Adrenal melanoma is typically formed by sheets of pleomorphic epithelioid and fusiform cells and may contain multinucleated tumour cells {29390427}. The nuclei have irregular nuclear membranes, vesicular chromatin, intranuclear inclusions, and prominent nucleoli. Immunohistochemical stains for S100, SOX10, melan-A, and HMB45 are often positive, and the tumour is consistently negative for cytokeratins, synaptophysin, and chromogranin {21722390}. The presence of melanosomes can be detected on ultrastructural examination {10660767}.

Cytology

Cytological preparations of aspirate samples from melanoma (including of metastatic origin) typically demonstrate loosely cohesive large pleomorphic epithelioid to plasmacytoid cells with irregular nuclear membranes and prominent nucleoli. Pigment, if present, can also be detected within tumour cells or histocytes {21979216}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of malignant and melanocytic differentiation; identification of adjacent adrenal tissue; absence of evidence for previous or synchronous melanoma outside of the adrenal gland.

Staging

There are no Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) staging criteria for primary adrenal melanoma.

Prognosis and prediction

There is limited information from case reports about the prognosis of primary adrenal melanoma, but the prognosis is generally poor in the absence of intervention {2194410}. Patients may progress rapidly to disseminated peritoneal disease, and distant metastasis to lymph nodes, lung, liver, spleen, brain, and bone has been described {29390427; 15170557}.
