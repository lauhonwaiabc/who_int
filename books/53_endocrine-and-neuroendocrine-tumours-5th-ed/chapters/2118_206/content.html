2.1.1.8 Mixed somatotroph-lactotroph PitNET/adenoma

Definition

Mixed somatotroph–lactotroph pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenoma is a well-differentiated PitNET composed of PIT1-lineage adenohypophysial cells with distinct populations of somatotroph and lactotroph cells.

ICD-O coding

8280/3 Mixed somatotroph–lactotroph PitNET/adenoma

8280/3 Densely granulated somatotroph and sparsely granulated lactotroph tumour

8280/3 Sparsely granulated somatotroph and sparsely granulated lactotroph tumour

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures

Related terminology

Acceptable: mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumour.

Not recommended: growth hormone and prolactin-secreting PitNET/adenoma; somatotroph–mammotroph PitNET/adenoma.

Subtype(s)

Densely granulated somatotroph and sparsely granulated lactotroph tumour; sparsely granulated somatotroph and sparsely granulated lactotroph tumour

Localization

Most tumours are intrasellar with suprasellar and/or parasellar extension {11549628; 31368083}. These can arise in ovarian teratomas {32632841}.

Clinical features

Most somatotroph–lactotroph tumours are clinically functioning tumours that cause gigantism and/or acromegaly and clinical evidence of hyperprolactinaemia, including hypogonadism, amenorrhoea, or galactorrhoea due to the cosecretion of growth hormone (GH) and prolactin {11549628; 24925503; 27245663}. Signs and symptoms of mass effect may also be present in patients with large tumours.

Imaging

At diagnosis, these tumours are mostly macrotumours with suprasellar extension {11549628; 27519216; 31368083}. Cavernous sinus invasion has been reported in as many as 38.5% of tumours {31368083}.

Epidemiology

Mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours are rare, corresponding to 1–4% of all pituitary tumours and about 6–14% of tumours presenting with acromegaly/gigantism {11549628; 24925503; 29434339; 31368083; 33690870}.

There is no reported sex predilection, and the mean age (fifth to sixth decade of life) does not differ from that of pure somatotroph tumours {11549628; 24925503; 29434339; 31368083}, but there are insufficient detailed data in the literature separating mixed tumours from the more common pure somatotroph tumours and mammosomatotroph tumours.

Tumours in the setting of hereditary syndromes or in sporadic, non-syndromic acrogigantism (familial isolated pituitary adenomas [FIPAs], X-linked acrogigantism [X-LAG]) arise in younger patients, mostly those of prepubertal age {25905378; 27245663; 27657986}. In X-LAG–associated tumours, there is a predominance of female patients {27245663}.

Etiology

The etiology of mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours is unknown in most cases. A small subset arise in the setting of hereditary tumour syndromes, including FIPA, X-LAG, Carney complex, and McCune–Albright syndrome {27245663; 32604740; 32741481; 33543431}.

Pathogenesis

There are no specific molecular or genetic data discriminating the mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours from the larger population of somatotroph and mammosomatotroph tumours. Mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours are associated with germline or somatic mosaic duplication of GPR101 causing X-LAG and with AIP mutation {27245663; 32741481}.

Macroscopic appearance

Mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours are soft and white to grey or red and indistinguishable from other mature PIT1-lineage tumours.

Histopathology

Mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours are composed of two distinct cell populations, somatotroph cells and lactotroph cells. All combinations of sparsely and/or densely granulated somatotroph and lactotroph cells have been described {11549628; 29434339; 31368083; 33690870}. Most typically, tumours are composed of large, eosinophilic, densely granulated somatotrophs admixed with chromophobic, sparsely granulated lactotrophs; a sparsely granulated somatotroph cell component is less frequently described {11549628; 31368083; 33690870}.

Immunohistochemistry

Both tumour cellular components express PIT1. Somatotroph cells are immunoreactive for GH, and variably immunoreactive for α-subunit depending on the granularity of the cells. Lactotroph cells express prolactin and ER. A low-molecular-weight cytokeratin immunostain reveals perinuclear expression in densely granulated somatotrophs; alternatively, paranuclear fibrous bodies are present in tumours with sparsely granulated somatotrophs.

Differential diagnosis

The distinction of mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours from mammosomatotroph tumours may be difficult without ultrastructural analysis. Distinct cell populations of somatotroph and lactotroph cells are seen in mixed tumours, whereas mammosomatotroph tumours are composed of a monomorphic population of cells with large pleomorphic secretory granules and granular extrusions (misplaced exocytosis) {773951; 6402839}. Another tool for the distinction of these two entities is the analysis of the pattern of ER immunoexpression that correlates with lactotroph cells and is clearly absent in somatotroph cells of mixed tumours {7515390; 8530610; 9361268}.

Cytology

The cytology of mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours is indistinguishable from that of PitNETs/adenomas with large cells; tumours with sparsely granulated somatotrophs may be recognized by the presence of fibrous bodies.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no molecular tests required to diagnose mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours. Genetic screening for germline and/or sporadic mutations has been recommended in young patients (aged ≤ 30 years) with acromegaly/gigantism {21753072; 23321498; 25905184; 27245663; 31996917}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a PitNET composed of two PIT1-immunopositive cell populations separately expressing GH and prolactin.

Desirable: ER immunoreactivity (for better definition of the lactotrophs); absence of the α-subunit of glycoprotein hormones in the lactotroph component; low-molecular-weight cytokeratin (for better definition of the somatotrophs).

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms. A CT-era approach based on tumour size and patterns of invasion was proposed in 1973 by Hardy [[Hardy J. Transsphenoidal surgery of hypersecreting pituitary tumours. In: Kohler PO, Ross GT, editors. Diagnosis and treatment of pituitary tumors. International Congress Series No. 303. Amsterdam (Netherlands): Exerpta Medica; 1973. pp. 179–98.]]. Subsequently, a standardized assessment of parasellar tumour extension based on MRI was published by Knosp in 1993 {8232800}.

There are no pathology-standardized reporting templates yet endorsed by international pathology organizations. A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}.

Prognosis and prediction

Although data on this separate entity from the overall population of GH-producing tumours are very sparse, two studies have shown that patients with mixed somatotroph–lactotroph tumours have lower disease remission rates than patients with tumours in the other tumour categories including somatotroph (densely and sparsely granulated) and mammosomatotroph tumours {11549628; 31368083}.
