2.1.6.1 Metastatic PitNET

Definition

Metastatic pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET) is a tumour of adenohypophysial neuroendocrine cells that manifests with metastatic disease to lymph nodes and/or distant sites, or that has discontinuous spread through the CNS.

ICD-O coding

8272/3 PitNET, malignant / pituitary carcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y & XA8J35 & XH7KL6 Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures & Pituitary gland & Pituitary carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: pituitary carcinoma.

Not recommended: neuroendocrine carcinoma; pituitary neuroendocrine carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

All types and subtypes of PitNET can be metastatic.

Localization

The primary site is usually in the sella turcica but may occasionally be extrasellar, in ectopic locations such as the sphenoidal sinus. Metastatic foci occur in lymph nodes (usually cervical), liver, and bone, and the tumour may spread along the craniospinal axis as the result of dissemination in the subarachnoid space {9024719}.

Clinical features

The initial clinical presentation is usually that of a PitNET. Most common is Cushing disease {1327728; 7802565; 12114677; 19356251; 28938458; 29404894; 30706322} and the enhanced tumour growth associated with Nelson syndrome due to a lack of feedback inhibition {9024719; 23134557}; other functioning tumours produce hyperprolactinaemia {1479402; 8319373; 12114677; 16645009; 17912159; 30706322}, acromegaly {1395070}, or hyperthyroidism due to TSH excess {16645009; 8432799}. Hormonally inactive tumours include gonadotroph, null cell, silent corticotroph, silent somatotroph, and poorly differentiated tumours of PIT1 lineage {3589946; 3817801; 8559312; 9024719; 19811617; 29299820; 30706322}. The latency period between the diagnosis of an intrasellar PitNET and the development of metastasis varies widely.

Biochemical investigations

The biochemical features vary with the hormonal activity of the tumour; hypopituitarism may result from tissue destruction.

Neuroimaging

PET imaging using the 68Ga-DOTATATE tracer can identify the extent of disease and SSTR status {22222543}. The latter can inform on the potential utility of peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) {30706322}.

Epidemiology

Metastatic spread of a PitNET is rare. Most published information is as single case reports; the largest series includes 40 patients {29330228}. Metastatic PitNETs accounted for 0.12% of the pituitary tumours in the German Pituitary Tumor Registry {17287410} and represented 0.4% and 0.56% of PitNETs in published series from two referral centres of pituitary pathology {30706322; 32306401}. Although most patients have been adults, a paediatric case has been reported {19073854}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

It is unclear whether metastatic potential is due to genetic or non-genetic (e.g. epigenetic, tissue microenvironment) alterations {11838811}. Mutations identified in PitNETs are not implicated in progression to metastatic disease. Mutations in HRAS have been identified in metastatic deposits of 3 pituitary carcinomas, but not in their corresponding primary lesions {8157709} or in other carcinomas examined {8157709; 8288721}. In 1 case, loss of function of RB1 was implicated in the progression of an ACTH-producing pituitary carcinoma with distant metastases {9585068}. Distinct clonal compositions of a primary and metastatic ACTH-producing pituitary carcinoma {11678840} suggest the emergence of novel clones. Immunoreactivity for p53 has been reported in pituitary carcinomas {9024719; 8692397} and mutation of TP53 has been documented in several tumours {7889618; 18026859; 18771563; 31222332; 33106857}. Mutations of ATRX and PTEN have been described in metastatic tumours {33106857; 30460199}.

Metastatic PitNETs have been reported in patients with a germline tumour predisposition to neoplasia, including SDHB mutation {28284009} and Lynch syndrome {28938458}.

Molecular profiling has shown evidence of multiple alterations in genes implicated in angiogenesis, invasion, and metastasis {27893664}.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross appearance of these lesions is similar to that of non-metastasizing PitNETs.

Histopathology

Metastasizing PitNETs are histopathologically similar to their non-metastasizing counterparts. Some have been described as showing hypercellularity, nuclear pleomorphism, mitoses, necrosis, haemorrhage, and invasion, but these are also seen in non-metastatic invasive PitNETs {30058871}.

Immunohistochemistry

Pituitary immunohistochemical stains and/or electron microscopy are required for the classification of PitNETs, including within metastatic deposits. The Ki-67 labelling index may be high {8747957; 29330228; 30706322}, but this also is not specifically associated with metastatic potential. Metastatic tumours have been reported to exhibit a high microvascular density {11469692}, high p53 labelling {8692397}, loss of p27 immunoreactivity {9250163; 12050228}, increased TOP2A {12429800}, and overexpression of ERBB2 {12114662}; however, none of these reliably predict metastatic behaviour.

Cytology

Cytological features are similar to those of non-metastasizing PitNETs.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

At this time, there are no molecular tests required to diagnose metastatic pituitary tumours. Germline screening for inherited somatotroph tumours should be considered on the basis of clinicopathological findings.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diagnostic features of a PitNET with documented metastasis or discontinuous CNS spread.

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms. A CT-era approach based on tumour size and patterns of invasion was proposed in 1973 by Hardy [[Hardy J. Transsphenoidal surgery of hypersecreting pituitary tumours. In: Kohler PO, Ross GT, editors. Diagnosis and treatment of pituitary tumors. International Congress Series No. 303. Amsterdam (Netherlands): Exerpta Medica; 1973. pp. 179–98.]]. Subsequently, a standardized assessment of parasellar tumour extension based on MRI was published by Knosp in 1993 {8232800}.

There are no pathology-standardized reporting templates yet endorsed by international pathology organizations. A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}.

Prognosis and prediction

The number and variability of published cases is insufficient to draw accurate conclusions concerning appropriate therapy and prognosis. Aggressive surgical resection of metastases has shown survival benefit, whereas radiation therapy has only been palliative {9024719}.

Temozolomide has shown some efficacy {17044568; 19654234; 27106209; 29330228; 31349217}; functioning tumours and those with low expression of MGMT display possibly better responses {29330228}. Bevacizumab has also been reported to have efficacy {30447452}. The concurrent administration of capecitabine and temozolomide (CAPTEM) has also been used, and subsequent immunotherapy with ipilimumab and nivolumab resulted in a significant reduction of hepatic and intracranial disease burden in a tumour with a somatic mutation burden consistent with alkylator-induced hypermutation that was not seen in the treatment-naïve tumour {30085142}. In another study, 2 of 4 corticotroph pituitary carcinomas responded to pembrolizumab after prior exposure to alkylating agents {33427689}.
