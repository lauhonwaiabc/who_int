3.3.1.1B Thymoma

Definition

Intrathyroidal thymoma is an organotypic thymic epithelial tumour occurring within or attached to the thyroid gland.

Localization

Intrathyroidal thymoma occurs within or attached to the thyroid gland, more commonly in the lower pole of the left lobe (anterolateral neck, deep to the sternocleidomastoid muscle) {2050369; 6687653; 9755676; 23406665; 25648096}.

Clinical features

This tumour usually presents as an asymptomatic euthyroid mass that often has been present for years {2050369; 12462094; 12729482; 26826412}; however, some cases may be associated with pain {26826412}, compressive symptoms (dysphagia and/or dyspnoea) {29626459; 15880711}, and tracheal deviation {26622659}. Intrathyroidal thymomas are hypofunctioning on radionuclide scan {1609105}. Myasthenia gravis and other paraneoplastic disorders have not been reported {26622659; 26826412}.

Epidemiology

Intrathyroidal thymomas are very rare. Most occur in the fifth to sixth decade of life (mean: 53 years, range: 28–81 years), with a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:3) {12729482; 15880711; 16430482; 23406665; 26622659; 26826412; 29626459}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These tumours are thought to arise from thymic or branchial pouch remnants displaced during embryological development {2050369; 26826412}. There is no association with EBV {16430482}.

Macroscopic appearance

They are encapsulated and/or well circumscribed, with a nodular, yellow-tan cut surface interspersed with fibrous septa {26826412; 26622659; 15880711; 12729482}. Tumours vary in size from 10 to 80 mm in diameter {2050369; 3677008; 26826412; 20551539; 14621201; 12462094; 10607013}.

Histopathology

Intrathyroidal thymoma displays similar histopathological appearances/subtypes to those seen in mediastinal thymoma {2050369; 6687653; 29626459}. The prototypical morphology is that of an encapsulated or well-delimited tumour forming jigsaw puzzle–like lobules separated by sclerotic septa. There are polygonal to spindled, occasionally squamoid, epithelial cells interspersed with variable numbers of small lymphocytes {6687653}. Some cases can show entrapment of thyroid follicles within the tumour {26826412}, dystrophic calcifications {15880711}, and/or residual ectopic thymus tissue at the periphery {2050369}. Intrathyroidal thymomas are usually non-invasive, but invasion through the tumour capsule or into the surrounding fatty tissue has also been reported {23406665; 25648096}.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic epithelial cells are diffusely positive for pancytokeratin and CK5/6 {15880711; 26826412}, and negative for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) {16430482} and monoclonal PAX8 {26826412; 30210064}. The lymphocytic component consists of immature T cells characteristically positive for TdT {15880711; 20551539; 23406665; 26622659; 26826412}.

Differential diagnosis

Intrathyroidal thymoma should be distinguished from ectopic thymic tissue {26826412}, mediastinal thymoma with cervical extension {2050369}, branchioma (ectopic hamartomatous thymoma), anaplastic carcinoma {12729482}, medullary carcinoma {16430482}, intrathyroidal thymic carcinoma {26826412}, and the spindle cell subtype of papillary thyroid carcinoma. Branchioma is not a true thymoma; it selectively occurs in the sternoclavicular region, lacks a lobulated pattern, and is characterized by a fascicular or lattice-like pattern with bland-looking spindle cells interspersed with fat. This lesion often includes squamoid islands, syringoma-like tubules, simple glands, and squamous cysts {2050369; 32026292}.

Cytology

FNA smears show variable proportions of lymphocytic and epithelial cells {15880711}, which might lead to misdiagnosis as Hashimoto thyroiditis {26826412; 10607013; 1927177}, lymphoproliferative disorders {1927177; 9755676; 12462094; 12729482; 26622659; 29626459}, or carcinoma {23406665} (mainly papillary thyroid carcinoma {20551539; 23406665}). If the lymphocytic component is prevalent, flow cytometry immunophenotyping may help in diagnosis {12462094}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: jigsaw puzzle–like tumour lobules separated by sclerotic septa; a variable mixture of bland-looking epithelial cells and TdT-positive lymphocytes.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The vast majority of patients do not show recurrence or metastasis after surgical excision. Two patients with lymph node metastases {29626459; 16430482} and another with lung metastases {23406665} had tumours with extensive infiltration of the thyroid gland, venous invasion, and/or infiltration of the adjacent connective tissue.
