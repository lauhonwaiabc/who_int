2.1.1.6A Immature PIT1-lineage PitNET/adenoma

Definition

Immature PIT1-lineage pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenoma is an immature PitNET composed of PIT1-lineage adenohypophysial cells with cytological atypia and limited differentiation towards thyrotroph, somatotroph, and/or lactotroph cells.

ICD-O coding

8272/3 Immature PIT1-lineage PitNET/adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures

Related terminology

Acceptable: immature PIT1-lineage tumour, immature PIT1-positive tumour.

Not recommended: silent subtype 3 PitNET/adenoma; poorly differentiated PIT1-lineage PitNET/adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These intrasellar tumours often invade into surrounding anatomical structures.

Clinical features

The majority of immature PIT1-lineage tumours are clinically non-functioning, thus explaining the prior taxonomy of “silent subtype 3”. However, as many as 30% show evidence of hormone excess, including hyperthyroidism, acromegaly, galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, and/or hyperprolactinaemia {19170710; 26743473; 34067494}. Symptoms of mass effect, such as headaches, visual deficits, cranial nerve palsies, hypopituitarism, and apoplexy, are encountered in roughly two thirds of patients {19170710; 26743473}.

Neuroimaging

They are nearly always contrast-enhancing macrotumours with extensive invasion into adjacent structures. Inferolateral extension through the floor of the sella and into the sphenoidal sinus, clivus, and nasopharynx, as well as into cavernous sinuses, predominates over suprasellar growth in about 17% of cases, but combined inferolateral and suprasellar growth is most common {19170710; 26743473}.

Epidemiology

Immature PIT1-lineage tumours are rare, accounting for roughly 1–3% of all PitNETs {19170710; 26743473}. In 58 patients from two large series, the M:F ratio was close to 1:1, and the average age at presentation was about 40 years {19170710; 26743473}.

Etiology

In most cases, the etiology is not known, although rare examples have been reported in the setting of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 {19170710}.

Pathogenesis

Histopathological series suggest that these tumours arise from an early PIT1-lineage progenitor and partially overlap with thyrotroph tumours. In fact, a pangenomic study found that the PIT1-lineage plurihormonal tumour transcriptome most closely clusters with the thyrotroph tumours; however, the study in question did not distinguish mature from immature forms {31883967}.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross appearance is similar to that of other large, invasive macrotumours.

Histopathology

Immature PIT1-lineage tumours are often diffuse to nodular proliferations of polygonal to spindle-shaped chromophobic cells with marked nuclear atypia, intranuclear cytoplasmic pseudoinclusions, and macronucleoli. Like in thyrotroph tumours, stromal fibrosis may be seen, possibly related to local production of FGF {12114648}. Ultrastructurally, they partially overlap with thyrotroph cells, showing a monomorphic population of large polar cells with abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum, aggregates of smooth endoplasmic reticulum, multipolar Golgi complexes, plexiform cellular interdigitations, and sparse 100–200 nm secretory granules mostly concentrated at the cell periphery and cytoplasmic processes; a unique feature is the presence of electron-dense nuclear inclusions, known as spheridia [[Asa SL, Perry A. Tumors of the pituitary gland. Rockville (MD): American Registry of Pathology; 2020. (AFIP atlas of tumor and non-tumor pathology, series 5; fascicle 1).]] {26743473}.

Immunohistochemistry

They are diffusely PIT1-immunoreactive, with variable but usually limited expression of TSH, growth hormone, and/or prolactin. Although 70–80% are plurihormonal with at least two hormones represented, rare examples express only one, or even none, of the PIT1-family hormones; in such cases, the diffuse PIT1 staining, classic cytological features, and/or ultrastructural features (see below) allow diagnostic confirmation. Most tumours also express α-subunit, ER, and GATA3, along with variable cytokeratin staining, with both perinuclear and paranuclear fibrous body patterns encountered.

Differential diagnosis

Immature PIT1-lineage tumours overlap with their mature counterparts, as well as with thyrotroph tumours and acidophil stem cell tumours. The mature PIT1-lineage tumours have much more cytological uniformity, with abundant acidophilic cytoplasm and extensive hormone immunoreactivities, and they generally lack nuclear atypia, macronucleoli, and nuclear pseudoinclusions {31112312}. Thyrotroph tumours similarly lack the cytological features of immature PIT1-lineage tumours and should be monohormonal (TSH only). Acidophil stem cell tumours are oncocytic, vacuolated, and typically prolactin hormone–predominant.

Cytology

Cytological studies have not been published.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no molecular tests required to diagnose these tumours. Germline screening for inherited tumours should be considered on the basis of clinicopathological findings.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffuse PIT1 positivity, usually with patchy expression of one or more PIT1-lineage hormones (TSH, growth hormone, and/or prolactin); histology showing nuclear atypia, macronucleoli, and nuclear pseudoinclusions.

Desirable: a large invasive tumour on neuroimaging; nuclear spheridia on ultrastructural examination; variable GATA3 and ER immunoreactivity.

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms. A CT-era approach based on tumour size and patterns of invasion was proposed in 1973 by Hardy [[Hardy J. Transsphenoidal surgery of hypersecreting pituitary tumours. In: Kohler PO, Ross GT, editors. Diagnosis and treatment of pituitary tumors. International Congress Series No. 303. Amsterdam (Netherlands): Exerpta Medica; 1973. pp. 179–98.]]. Subsequently, a standardized assessment of parasellar tumour invasiveness based on MRI was published by Knosp in 1993 {8232800}.

There are no pathology-standardized reporting templates yet endorsed by international pathology organizations. A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}.

Prognosis and prediction

This usually invasive PitNET/adenoma subtype is known for its more aggressive biology, lower rates of resectability, and a proclivity for tumour recurrence; nevertheless, some data suggest that it is sensitive to radiation therapy {3372677; 16316952; 19170710; 26743473}. Although proliferation indexes are often higher in these tumours, they are not known to be prognostic {26330191; 29434339; 30897548}. Overall, surgery has been the main therapeutic approach, and in one series, the mean time to a second operation was only 16.6 months {19170710}. Another study found that nearly 80% of immature PIT1-lineage tumours are immunonegative for MGMT, suggesting that temozolomide may be an effective therapeutic strategy; however, immunohistochemistry for this marker is generally challenging, and a proof of principle for temozolomide therapy is currently lacking.
