10.0.0.5 Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma

Definition

Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma (FDCS) is a malignant neoplasm showing morphological and phenotypic characteristics of follicular dendritic cells (FDCs), which are stromal-derived cells normally found in the germinal centres.

ICD-O coding

9758/3 Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B31.5 Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: follicular dendritic cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

FDCS can occur in nodal sites or diverse extranodal sites. Cases occurring in endocrine organs include 7 cases reported in the thyroid and 1 case reported in the adrenal, with no cases reported so far in the parathyroid or pituitary {10208672; 19448067; 24663046; 32589853; 32895103; 32991350}.

Clinical features

Patients with thyroid FDCS present with a slow-growing, painless thyroid mass, sometimes accompanied by cervical lymph node metastasis {10208672; 19448067; 24663046; 32589853}. The patient with adrenal FDCS presented with paraneoplastic pemphigus {32895103}.

Epidemiology

FDCS is extremely rare in endocrine organs {27318412; 23755890}. Patients with thyroid FDCS are adults with a median age of 48 years, and there is a marked female predominance {10208672; 19448067; 24663046; 32589853; 32991350}. The patient with adrenal FDCS was a boy aged 16 years {32895103}.

Etiology

The etiology is generally unknown. FDCS shows no association with EBV.

Pathogenesis

Limited cytogenetic studies show a complex karyotype {23422480}. Next-generation sequencing has revealed recurrent alterations in NF-κB pathway genes, CDKN2A, and TP53 {26564005; 33904632}. The frequent occurrence of NF-κB pathway gene mutations and the lack of MAPK pathway gene mutations contrast with the genomic profile of true histiocytic/dendritic cell neoplasms (including histiocytic sarcoma, interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma, and Langerhans cell sarcoma) {33904632}. Genomic analysis of a thyroid FDCS identified CLTCL1, ATM, FLT1 (VEGFR1), and TP53 mutations, as well as HDGFRP3::SHC4 fusion {28975018}.

Macroscopic appearance

FDCS has a fleshy appearance, with no distinctive features.

Histopathology

FDCS is composed of syncytial-appearing spindled to oval cells exhibiting a storiform, fascicular, whorled, or vague nodular pattern. The neoplastic cells have oval or elongated vesicular nuclei with a delicate nuclear membrane and distinct nucleoli. Nuclear pseudoinclusions and multinucleated tumour cells are common. The tumour is variably infiltrated by small lymphocytes. In some reported thyroid FDCS cases, the background thyroid shows features of chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic cells are positive for one or more of the FDC markers CD21, CD23, CD35, CXCL13, and podoplanin, and new FDC markers such as FDCSP and SRGN {17951199; 18792075; 25310210; 28145886}. Expression of clusterin, desmoplakin, vimentin, fascin, EGFR, and HLA-DR is usually observed, whereas cytokeratin is usually negative {12121233; 18792075; 24369219; 25310210; 28695297}. The admixed small lymphocytes include B cells and T cells.

Cytology

FDCS is often difficult to diagnose on cytological materials alone. Spindled and epithelioid cells forming a fascicular, syncytial, and/or whorled pattern are often observed. The tumour cells have moderate amounts of cytoplasm, round to spindled nuclei, vesicular chromatin, and prominent nucleoli {33493367}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not required

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour with a storiform, fascicular, whorled, or nodular growth pattern; spindled or oval tumour cells with indistinct cell borders, vesicular nuclei, and distinct nucleoli; intermingled small lymphocytes; positivity for at least two FDC markers, such as CD21, CD23, CD35, and CXCL13; negative staining for lymphocyte-specific markers and generally negative staining for S100.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

FDCS is a low- to intermediate-grade malignancy. A pooled analysis of the literature shows local recurrence and distant metastasis rates of 28% and 27%, respectively {23755890}. Among the reported examples of thyroid FDCS, the patients were disease-free 6–36 months after diagnosis.
