12.0.1.4 Metastasis to the adrenal gland

Definition

Metastases to the adrenal gland are tumours that involve the adrenal gland secondarily by haematogenous spread from primary sites in other organs.

ICD-O coding

Code according to histology of metastasis

ICD-11 coding

2E07 Malignant neoplasm metastasis in adrenal gland

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adrenal cortex and/or medulla

Clinical features

Adrenal metastases are usually asymptomatic, but they may be associated with abdominal pain. Bilateral involvement is an important clue that a tumour is a metastasis. Adrenal insufficiency is unusual and requires bilateral involvement with destruction of > 90% of the cortex {2293863; 3581024; 30178435}.

Epidemiology

Metastases are the second most common adrenal tumour after adrenal cortical adenomas. The adrenal glands are the fifth most common site for metastatic involvement, after the lymph nodes, lung, liver, and bone {16518827}.

Etiology

None

Pathogenesis

The rich sinusoidal blood supply of the adrenal glands predisposes them as a site of haematogenous spread.

Macroscopic appearance

Metastatic tumours usually lack the typical yellow to orange colour of primary adrenocortical tumours, with the macroscopic appearance dependent on the primary tumour. Haemorrhage and cystic change are common.

Histopathology

Melanoma, as well as lung, breast, kidney, and gastrointestinal tract carcinomas, are among the most common primary tumours to spread to the adrenal, but virtually any tumour may do so {16518827}. Definitive pathological diagnosis of metastasis is usually straightforward by morphology with or without immunohistochemistry when the primary site is known.

Differential diagnosis

Metastatic hepatocellular carcinoma, clear cell renal cell carcinoma, and melanoma may be particularly difficult to distinguish from primary adrenal cortical carcinomas because of morphological and immunohistochemical overlap, and a panel of immunohistochemical markers is usually necessary for the differential diagnosis. Nuclear expression of SF1 in tumour cells favours a primary adrenal cortical neoplasm.

Cytology

Although rarely used for primary lesions, FNA may provide definitive diagnostic confirmation when used for metastases to the adrenal gland {30880721}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing may have a role in identifying the primary site of origin or in guiding therapy, depending on the tumour type.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: deposits of non-adrenal malignant cells in the adrenal gland; appropriate pathological and/or clinical steps should be undertaken to exclude a primary adrenal tumour.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for the primary tumour metastatic to the adrenal

Prognosis and prediction

Metastasectomy may be beneficial in carefully selected patients {22205385; 27664424; 30270570; 32419066}. The prognosis of tumours that have metastasized to the adrenal gland is dependent on the underlying biology and extent of the primary tumour, but it is generally poor. In one study, the 2-year survival rate of patients with adrenal metastasis was 7% {26121453}, which is considerably worse than the survival rate of patients with primary adrenal cortical carcinoma {33058949}.
