8.2.0.2 Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumour (previously paraganglioma)

Definition

Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumour (NET), previously called paraganglioma, is a neuroendocrine neoplasm (NEN) arising from specialized neural crest cells in the cauda equina / filum terminale region.

ICD-O coding

8693/3 Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumour

ICD-11 coding

XH1X68 Paraganglioma, benign

XH0EW6 Paraganglioma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: paraganglioma of the cauda equina; cauda equina paraganglioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cauda equina / filum terminale region (intradural)

Clinical features

Cauda equina NETs exhibit no clinical features that allow their distinction from other intradural lumbosacral tumours (see #27992Table 8.03). The most common presenting symptoms include a history of low back pain and sciatica. Less common manifestations are numbness, paraparesis, and incontinence {15796363}. Fully developed cauda equina syndrome is uncommon. Signs of increased intracranial pressure and papilloedema are unusual at presentation {15269482; 15796363; 16283440; 22271522; 23918468}. Cerebrospinal fluid protein is usually markedly increased {2875784; 6831356}.

Imaging

MRI appearances of cauda equina NETs are indistinguishable from those of schwannoma or (myxopapillary) ependymoma {22271522; 25013343}. Tumours are sharply circumscribed and occasionally partly cystic, and they can be hypointense, isointense, or hyperintense on T1- and T2-weighted images; contrast enhancement may be present or absent. A low-signal-intensity rim (cap sign) on T2-weighted images can be caused by subcapsular haemosiderin {8456679; 15803375; 25720695}. The hypervascular architecture can give a salt-and-pepper appearance on T2-weighted images. Serpentine flow voids along the tumour surface or spinal cord margin may also be seen on MRI {20637630; 31379984}.

Epidemiology

A meta-analysis of 297 cases {31247160} identified a peak in incidence between the ages of 40 and 49 years, with a mean age at presentation of 47.0 years (range: 9–77 years). The M:F ratio was 1.5:1.

Etiology

These are sporadic NETs. The etiology is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Cauda equina NETs are histogenetically and molecularly distinct from paragangliomas and phaeochromocytomas outside the CNS. They overexpress the transcription factor HOXB13 {33768604}, which is developmentally expressed in the caudal extent of the spinal cord and in the urogenital sinus. This coincides with dynamic changes associated with the formation of the secondary neural tube {8756292; 12679105}, providing circumstantial evidence of a cell of origin. To date, these tumours have not been seen in patients with familial paraganglioma phaeochromocytoma syndrome due to mutations in SDH genes {32741965}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are oval to sausage-shaped, delicately encapsulated, soft, red-brown masses that bleed freely and may cause superficial siderosis of the CNS {9609190; 23287334; 32152998; 32959357}. In five series of 59 spinal tumours, sizes ranged from 10 to 112 mm in greatest dimension {22770767; 25013343; 25720695}. Capsular calcification and cystic components may be found. Occasional tumours penetrate the dura to invade bone. A macroscopically identifiable attachment may be found, either to the filum terminale or (less often) to a caudal nerve root {2875784}.

Histopathology

These well-differentiated NETs are composed of neuroendocrine cells arranged in nests or lobules surrounded by an inconspicuous single layer of sustentacular cells. The nests are surrounded by a delicate capillary network and a delicate supporting reticulin fibre network that may undergo sclerosis. The uniform round or polygonal neuroendocrine cells possess central round to oval nuclei with finely stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. Degenerative nuclear pleomorphism (endocrine anaplasia) is generally mild. Cytoplasm is usually eosinophilic and finely granular. In some instances, it is amphophilic or clear. Sustentacular cells are thin and spindle-shaped. Encompassing the lobules, their long processes are often so attenuated as to be undetectable by routine light microscopy and are visible only on immunostaining for S100. Approximately 25% of cauda equina NETs contain mature ganglion cells and/or Schwann cell components (gangliocytic or ganglioneuromatous tumours). Ependymoma-like perivascular formations are common. Some tumours show angiomatous, adenomatous, and pseudorosette patterns {2875784}. Tumours with spindled cytology {9279569}, melanin-containing cells {2422107; 9279569}, and oncocytic change {12053045} have been described. Foci of haemorrhagic necrosis may occur, and scattered mitotic figures can be seen. Neither these features nor nuclear pleomorphism are of prognostic significance {2875784}.

The neuroendocrine cells are immunoreactive for chromogranin A and synaptophysin {24476517; 2684087; 2875784} and show variable S100 immunoreactivity. Immunoreactivity for cytokeratins (especially CAM5.2, AE1/AE3, and MNF116) has been described in a majority of these lesions {7782837; 1280616; 10199484; 23912549; 24476517; 31442521; 32892244}. Furthermore, gangliocytic tumours containing a variable mixture of epithelioid neuroendocrine cells, Schwann-like cells, and scattered ganglion-like cells can show cytokeratin positivity in the epithelioid cells {15892009; 20515359}. Expression of serotonin (5-HT) and of various neuropeptides (somatostatin, gastrin, calcitonin, PP, PYY, leu-enkephalin, and met-enkephalin) has been demonstrated {2875784; 9279569}.

Sustentacular cells show inconsistent S100 reactivity {23599157; 29239044} and occasionally express GFAP {15921911}; the latter may also highlight a thin rim of peripheral non-neoplastic tissue. They often express the neural crest transcription factor SOX10 {18636017}. The value of proliferation markers has not been established.

Although previously considered to be paragangliomas, unlike paragangliomas of the autonomic nervous system, these tumours do not express the zinc finger transcription factor GATA3 {23599157; 36543154}. They express HOXB13 (a transcription factor that is developmentally expressed in progenitor cells of the caudal spinal cord {33768604; 36543154}), a feature they share with myxopapillary ependymoma {33768604}. A minority express SATB2 {36543154}.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

DNA methylation and chromosomal copy-number profiles of cauda equina NETs are distinct from paragangliomas {32892244; 32926213}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-demarcated tumour with nested architecture; synaptophysin and chromogranin immunoreactivity; cauda equina / filum terminale location; methylation profile (in selected cases).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The vast majority of cauda equina NETs are slow-growing and curable by total excision; only 4% recur after gross total removal {7782837}. Cerebrospinal fluid seeding has occasionally been documented {7782837; 8738400; 16283104; 28128698}.
