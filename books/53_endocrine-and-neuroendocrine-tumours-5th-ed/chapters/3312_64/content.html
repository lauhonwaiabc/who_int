3.3.1.2 Spindle epithelial tumour with thymus-like elements

Definition

Spindle epithelial tumour with thymus-like elements (SETTLE) is a malignant intrathyroidal tumour arising from intrathyroidal thymic tissue, also occurring in the mediastinum, characterized by a lobulated architecture and biphasic cellular composition, with spindle epithelial cells that merge into glandular structures.

ICD-O coding

8588/3 Spindle epithelial tumour with thymus-like elements

ICD-11 coding

2D10.Y & XH6ZG8 Other specified malignant neoplasms of thyroid gland & Spindle epithelial tumour with thymus-like element

Related terminology

Acceptable: spindle epithelial tumour with thymus-like differentiation.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intrathyroidal, arising from intrathyroidal thymic tissue

Clinical features

The usual presentation is a painless thyroid mass of variable duration. Less common presentations include a rapidly enlarging neck mass, local tenderness, and diffuse thyroid enlargement. The tumour appears cold on thyroid scan, and it displays heterogeneous solid and cystic density on CT. Approximately 10% of patients have cervical lymph node metastasis at presentation {24677409; 26289127; 31209931}.

Epidemiology

SETTLE is very rare. It predominantly affects children and young adults, with a median age of 19 years (range: 2–70 years) and a male predilection (M:F ratio: 1.5:1) {24677409; 26289127; 29854712}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Single cases have been reported to show mutation of KRAS, NRAS, SMYD1 (KMT3D), or KMT2C {12612889; 29736783}.

Macroscopic appearance

SETTLE is grossly encapsulated, partially circumscribed, or infiltrative, with an average size of 42 mm (range: 11–120 mm) {26289127}. The cut surface is firm, predominantly solid, greyish-white to tan, and vaguely lobulated {2050369; 19417583}.

Histopathology

SETTLE is a highly cellular tumour, vaguely lobulated by thick or thin fibrous septa {2050369; 19417583}. Although most cases are biphasic, occasional cases (also referred to as the monophasic pattern) consist almost exclusively of spindled cells or glandular structures {9726052; 16199899}. Compact admixtures of reticulated, microcystic, and/or hyalinized long fascicles of spindle cells merge imperceptibly with areas showing an epithelial appearance, with tubulopapillary, glomeruloid, cystic, and glandular structures. Cleft-like spaces containing grey-blue mucin are common. The spindle cells have scant cytoplasm and elongated nuclei with fine chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli; ultrastructurally the spindle cells have an epithelial nature (tonofilaments, desmosomes, and basal lamina) {12211053; 2050369}. Mitotic figures are rare, although prominent mitotic activity and focal necrosis may be found in isolated cases {10366154}. The epithelial component is composed of cuboidal to columnar cells sometimes containing mucin or cilia. Their nuclei are similar to those of spindled cells except for their oval or round shape. Infrequently, there are foci of abrupt squamous differentiation {2050369}. Few lymphocytes and rare calcifications may be seen {33794064; 30706893}. Vascular invasion may be detected {29736783}.

Immunohistochemistry

Both spindled and glandular cells express pancytokeratins, with a higher reactivity for high-molecular-weight (versus low-molecular-weight) types, including CK7 {19417583; 30998418}. Myoepithelial markers (SMA and MSA) may be found in the spindle cell component {9160319; 12612889; 30998418}. Vimentin and EMA are also expressed {27011438; 23635450; 29736783}, as are p40/p63 {31209931; 29854712; 29736783}, CD99, BCL2, KIT (CD117), SMARCB1 (INI1), and TLE1 {19417583; 31209931; 31530165}, as well as (focally) D2-40 and MOC31 {29736783}. No reactivity was found for CK20, thyroglobulin, calcitonin, CEA, thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), CD5, TdT, S100, desmin, CD34, calretinin, or WT1 {29736783; 29193099; 19417583; 30998418}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is low (3–5%) {31183079; 31209931}, although it has been reported to be as high as 25% in metastatic tumour tissue {29402817}.

Differential diagnosis

SETTLE can be distinguished from synovial sarcoma by the lobulated growth pattern, lower overall nuclear grade, glomeruloid glandular structures, absence of intraglandular necrotic debris, stromal hyalinization, paucity of mast cells, and diffuse expression of high-molecular-weight cytokeratin and p63/p40, as well as the absence of SS18::SSX1, SS18::SSX2, or SS18::SSX4 gene fusion {19417583} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Thoracic tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2021. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 5). https://publications.iarc.who.int/595.]]. SETTLE should also be distinguished from sarcomatoid anaplastic thyroid carcinoma, intrathyroidal thymoma, and spindle cell medullary thyroid carcinoma. Anaplastic carcinoma does not usually present in young people and is an infiltrative high-grade pleomorphic tumour with necrosis and high mitotic activity. Thymoma has more jigsaw puzzle–like lobulation and is accompanied by a variable number of immature TdT-positive lymphocytes. In medullary carcinoma, the stroma may show amyloid and the cell nuclei have coarser chromatin; calcitonin and CEA immunostains are also positive.

Cytology

Aspirates are highly or moderately cellular, including cohesive and single dissociated spindle cells with bland oval nuclei. Occasional groups of epithelioid and columnar cells can be seen. Mitotic figures are rare or absent. Extracellular metachromatic material should not be misinterpreted as amyloid and therefore lead to a false diagnosis of medullary carcinoma {30998418; 17230570; 22136375; 29193099; 33794064}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

SETTLE lacks SS18::SSX1, SS18::SSX2, or SS18::SSX4 gene fusion. KRAS {12612889}, NRAS, KMT2D, or KMT2C {29736783} somatic mutations have been reported in single cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a biphasic tumour with fascicles of spindled cells merging into complex glandular elements; a reticulated or sieve-like pattern in some areas; diffuse immunoreactivity for keratins (especially high-molecular-weight keratins).

Desirable: no SS18::SSX1, SS18::SSX2, or SS18::SSX4 gene fusion (in selected cases).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

SETTLE is a slow-growing tumour, with an overall survival rate of 86% (median follow-up: 6.3 years) {24677409}. The metastatic rate is 26%, but this increases to 40% when follow-up exceeds 5 years {24677409; 11048811}. The time from diagnosis of the primary tumour to metastasis is highly variable (2–25 years), with a median of 10 years {26289127}. The presence of cervical lymph node metastasis is associated with a high risk of subsequent development of distant metastasis {24677409}. The tumour can metastasize to lymph node, bone, and kidney, but the most common site of metastasis is lung {26289127}. Metastatic disease can still be compatible with long survival after treatment {11048811}. In a review of 42 cases, 5 of the 37 patients with follow-up information available died of delayed distant metastasis or treatment complications {26289127}.
