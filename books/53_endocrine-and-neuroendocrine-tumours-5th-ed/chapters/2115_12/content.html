2.1.1.5 Mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage PitNET/adenoma

Definition

Mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET)/adenoma is a well-differentiated PitNET composed of PIT1-lineage adenohypophysial cells with acidophilic cytoplasm, extensive immunoreactivity for growth hormone (GH), and variable immunoreactivity for TSH, prolactin, and α-subunit.

ICD-O coding

8272/3 Mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage PitNET/adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures

Related terminology

Acceptable: mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage adenoma; mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage neuroendocrine tumour; mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage tumour; mature plurihormonal PIT1-positive tumour.

Not recommended: plurihormonal PitNET/adenoma of the acidophil cell line.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumours are located in the sellar region and may show variable extension into the adjacent structures {31368083}.

Clinical features

Patients usually exhibit clinical features of acromegaly/gigantism with hyperthyroidism and/or hyperprolactinaemia, separately or in combination. Secondary mass effects can compromise normal pituitary function and/or vision.

Epidemiology

Mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage PitNETs/adenomas are rare. Their exact incidence is yet to be clarified. Limited data suggest that such mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage tumours occur in older individuals than their immature counterparts {26743473; 31112312}.

Etiology

In most cases, the etiology is not known. However, rare examples have been reported in syndromic patients with multiple pituitary tumours {26869991}.

Pathogenesis

It remains unclear how multipotent plurihormonal pituitary cells become neoplastic and whether such transitions are distinct from those in their monohormonal counterparts.

Macroscopic appearance

No distinctive macroscopic features have been reported for this tumour type.

Histopathology

The tumour shows a solid or sinusoidal growth pattern and is composed predominantly of acidophilic cells with interspersed chromophobes. The histological features resemble those of mammosomatotroph tumours.

Immunohistochemistry

There is diffuse nuclear immunoreactivity for PIT1 {33852418} and usually extensive cytoplasmic positivity for GH, with abundant prolactin, TSH, and α-subunit, albeit in varying proportions among cases. They are also positive for ER and GATA3 {30390035}.

Differential diagnosis

Mammosomatotroph tumours have a similar histological appearance; however, they produce GH and prolactin but not TSH {34067494}.

Immature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage tumours should be distinguished from these tumours because of their potentially more aggressive behaviour. Immature PIT1-lineage tumours are composed of epithelioid or spindle-shaped chromophobic cells with large, often pleomorphic nuclei that have prominent nucleoli and intranuclear pseudoinclusions, and they usually show only patchy expression of GH, prolactin, and TSH {26743473; 31112312; 31766255}.

Cytology

The cytology of these tumours is similar to that of other PitNETs in general.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no molecular tests required to diagnose these tumours. Germline screening for inherited tumours should be considered on the basis of clinicopathological findings.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a PitNET with PIT1 immunoreactivity in all or most of the tumour cells; extensive expression of GH, with variable α-subunit, TSH, and prolactin positivity.

Desirable: GATA3 and ER expression.

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms. A CT-era approach based on tumour size and patterns of invasion was proposed in 1973 by Hardy [[Hardy J. Transsphenoidal surgery of hypersecreting pituitary tumours. In: Kohler PO, Ross GT, editors. Diagnosis and treatment of pituitary tumors. International Congress Series No. 303. Amsterdam (Netherlands): Exerpta Medica; 1973. pp. 179–98.]]. Subsequently, a standardized assessment of parasellar tumour extension based on MRI was published by Knosp in 1993 {8232800}.

There are no pathology-standardized reporting templates yet endorsed by international pathology organizations. A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}.

Prognosis and prediction

Compared with immature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage tumours, the mature plurihormonal tumours have been reported to exhibit less aggressive behaviour {26743473; 3372677}.

There are limited data on SSTR expression in mature plurihormonal PIT1-lineage tumours. However, it is reasonable to assume that immunohistochemical analysis of SSTRs may be of predictive value for the effectiveness of somatostatin analogues {24182563}.
