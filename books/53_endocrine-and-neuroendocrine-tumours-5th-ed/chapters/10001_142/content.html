10.0.0.1 Haematolymphoid tumours: Introduction

This chapter begins with a disease that mimics haematolymphoid neoplasms, IgG4-related disease, then describes lymphomas and histiocytic proliferations that occasionally involve endocrine organs, and concludes with follicular dendritic cell sarcoma, a mesenchymal dendritic cell neoplasm.

IgG4-related disease is new to this edition of the WHO classification of endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. IgG4-related disease is a chronic fibroinflammatory condition characterized by a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate enriched in IgG4-positive plasma cells, often with storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis, and elevated serum IgG4. The pancreas is the most commonly involved endocrine organ. In the thyroid gland, IgG4-related disease can present as Riedel-like thyroiditis or the fibrotic subtype of Hashimoto thyroiditis. IgG4-related disease also affects the pituitary.

Primary lymphomas of the thyroid account for 1–5% of all thyroid malignancies {10375088; 11788631}. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma accounts for > 65% of all thyroid lymphomas {26118762; 22367111; 32982962}. Approximately 1–5% of extranodal marginal zone lymphomas of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue, the second most frequent type of primary thyroid lymphoma, are located in the thyroid {10561186; 10800981; 33829216}. Primary lymphoma of the adrenal gland is rare; > 85% of cases are diffuse large B-cell lymphomas {31192909; 33071959; 28685093}. Primary pituitary lymphomas account for up to 0.2% of pituitary lesions; 55% of cases are diffuse large B-cell lymphomas {20874772; 21470998; 33471665}.

Histiocytic proliferations affecting endocrine organs include Langerhans cell histiocytosis, Rosai–Dorfman disease, and Erdheim–Chester disease. In endocrine organs, Langerhans cell histiocytosis most often affects the pituitary and thyroid {31269469}. Fewer than 10 cases of thyroid Rosai–Dorfman disease have been described, and only rare cases affect the posterior pituitary or adrenal glands {22438629; 31053123; 33744421; 24758864}. Erdheim–Chester disease is rare, but endocrine involvement is frequent in this disorder {26565949}. Mutations (particularly BRAF p.V600E and MAP2K1 mutations) leading to the activation of the RAS/RAF/MAPK pathway are a common feature of Langerhans cell histiocytosis {24625419; 24982505; 25202140} and Erdheim–Chester disease {26566875; 31807955; 32187362}. Recent evidence of similar gain-of-function mutations raises the possibility of a clonal origin in some cases of Rosai–Dorfman disease {29720485; 28664935}. Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma is rare in endocrine organs, with cases showing recurrent alterations in NF-κB pathway genes and an absence of MAPK pathway abnormalities {26564005; 33904632}.
