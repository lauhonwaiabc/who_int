3.2.2.10 Sclerosing mucoepidermoid carcinoma with eosinophilia

Definition

Sclerosing mucoepidermoid carcinoma with eosinophilia (SMECE) is a thyroid carcinoma composed of epidermoid and mucous cells in a background of marked stromal sclerosis with infiltration of eosinophils and lymphocytes.

ICD-O coding

8430/3 Sclerosing mucoepidermoid carcinoma with eosinophilia

ICD-11 coding

2D10.Y & XH1J36 Other specified malignant neoplasms of thyroid gland & Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Unilateral thyroid lobe involvement is most common, although diffuse, bilateral involvement may occasionally be seen {27910944}.

Clinical features

SMECE typically presents as a slow-growing, unilateral painless mass in euthyroid or hypothyroid patients, the latter presentation being more common. SMECE shows a female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:13). The average age at presentation is 55 years (range: 26–89 years) {25754017; 31399413; 33891143}.

Ultrasound imaging shows a smooth, isoechoic mass with possible coarse calcifications. The tumours are hypofunctioning (cold) on radioiodine scan {15011206; 31399413; 32562215}.

Epidemiology

SMECE is a rare tumour, with < 100 cases reported to date {31399413; 31882020; 32562215; 32671907; 33080749; 33484239; 33891143}. Women are almost exclusively affected (> 90% of cases) {15001991; 25754017; 27910944; 31399413}. SMECE is nearly universally associated with chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis {31882020; 32562215}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

SMECE is likely to be of ultimobranchial body / solid cell nest origin {15001991; 27910944; 30677251}. Genomic studies of SMECE have thus far been largely uninformative, generally showing a notable absence of alterations characteristic of mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MAML2 rearrangements) or BRAF mutations {27910944; 30677251; 33891143}. Isolated cases of SMECE have been reported to harbour several different alterations of thus far undetermined significance, including MET hyperploidy in the absence of amplification; point mutations in APC, NTRK3, and NF1; and NTRK1 overexpression without an associated translocation {27910944; 30677251; 33484239}. Very recently, BRAF p.V600E has been reported in tumours believed to represent combined SMECE with papillary or anaplastic carcinoma {31882020; 33080749}.

Macroscopic appearance

The average tumour size is 43 mm in largest dimension, with reported sizes ranging from 5 to 130 mm {27910944; 32562215; 8982585}. Cut surfaces are firm, solid, and white-yellow, and they may have cystic change {25754017; 27910944}. Gross extrathyroidal extension is noted in 25% of cases {32562215}.

Histopathology

SMECE shows distinctive histology characterized by an admixture of squamoid/epidermoid cells and mucocytes set in a densely sclerotic stroma with lymphocytes, eosinophils, and plasma cells. The tumour cells form cords, strands, and nests with areas of cellular dyscohesion imparting a pseudoangiomatous pattern. Foci of clear cell change (glycogenation), mucus extravasation, and/or keratinization may be identified {15018117}. Lymphocytes often predominate within the stromal inflammatory component, and although eosinophils are always present, their quantity can range from limited to abundant. The tumour cells show mild to moderate nuclear atypia with small, distinct nucleoli. Nuclear irregularities may be seen, but well-developed nuclear features of papillary carcinoma are not present. Mitotic rate is variable, ranging from 0 to 6 mitoses/5 mm2 (equating to 20 HPF of 0.25 mm2). The background thyroid parenchyma invariably demonstrates chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis, often the fibrosing pattern of Hashimoto thyroiditis, and in areas, the associated squamous metaplasia may merge imperceptibly with foci of tumour {2035738; 27910944; 25754017}. A separate synchronous or metachronous focus of papillary carcinoma is identified in 20% of cases {27910944; 30677251; 32562215}. Rarely, SMECE has been reported to arise as a component of papillary or anaplastic thyroid carcinoma {31399413; 33080749}.

Immunohistochemistry

By histochemistry, the mucus cells and foci of extracellular mucin are positive for mucicarmine and PAS. Immunohistochemistry shows consistent reactivity for p63 and cytokeratins in the squamoid tumour cells {15001991; 31399413}. Differential staining between the luminal cell compartment (highlighted by CK7) and basal cell compartment (reactive for CK5/6) may be seen {33080749; 33891143}. Focal and weak expression of thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) is present in approximately 50% of cases {15018117; 27910944; 31882020; 32562215}. Thyroglobulin and PAX8 are typically negative, whereas calcitonin, chromogranin, and S100 are consistently negative {25754017; 31399413; 32562215}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes conventional mucoepidermoid carcinoma, metastatic squamous cell carcinoma, anaplastic thyroid carcinoma, diffuse sclerosing papillary thyroid carcinoma, and intrathyroidal thymic carcinoma {25754017; 29676367; 30677251}. Particularly within a lymph node metastasis, Hodgkin lymphoma enters the differential diagnosis {10705405; 15018117; 25754017}, as does IgG4-related disease.

Cytology

Cytological features are frequently nonspecific. Fine-needle aspirates are usually highly cellular, and the tumour cells have variable amounts of cytoplasm with ovoid nuclei, small nucleoli, and rare grooves or pseudoinclusions. Squamoid cells, keratin pearls, and rare intracytoplasmic vacuoles have been reported. The background commonly shows lymphocytes; eosinophils and mucinous or keratinaceous material may be present. Fine-needle aspirates are most commonly diagnosed as atypia of undetermined significance, with few cases reported as positive for malignancy but without a specific diagnosis of SMECE rendered {8982585; 29415898; 29479842; 30677251; 32671907; 33484239}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primary thyroid carcinoma showing epidermoid/squamoid and mucous cells in a background of stromal sclerosis with a rich inflammatory infiltrate containing eosinophils.

Desirable: a background of chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis; thyroglobulin and PAX8 are typically negative.

Staging

No staging is available.

Prognosis and prediction

As many as 40% of patients have regional lymph node metastases {31882020; 32562215}. In some series, upon clinical follow-up, two thirds of patients were reported to be alive without disease {31399413; 31882020; 33080749; 33891143}. Occasional instances (< 15% of cases) of distant metastases to lung, bone, liver, mediastinum, or kidney have been documented {27910944; 31882020}. Long-term follow-up is needed because recurrences and/or metastases may appear as long as 20 years after the original surgery. Reliable prognostic or predictive indicators have not been developed.
