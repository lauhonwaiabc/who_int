6.1.2.1 Adrenal sex cord stromal tumour

Definition

Adrenal sex cord stromal tumour is a tumour with sex cord stromal differentiation arising in the adrenal cortex.

ICD-O coding

8590/1 Sex cord stromal tumour

8650/1 Leydig cell tumour

8620/3 Granulosa cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F9A & XH0667 Neoplasms of unknown behaviour of endocrine glands & Sex cord-stromal tumour, unclassified

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Leydig cell tumour; granulosa cell tumour

Localization

These tumours arise in the adrenal cortex.

Clinical features

Patients with adrenal granulosa cell tumour present with irregular uterine bleeding and/or an abdominal mass {4687888; 19423096}. In contrast, patients with adrenal Leydig cell tumour typically present with virilization and have elevated serum testosterone and DHEAS, with pituitary suppression of LH.

Epidemiology

Few cases have been reported {4687888; 19423096; 26126046; 6270941; 3000178; 3022613}; all occurred in postmenopausal women (median age: 60 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The histogenesis is uncertain {4687888; 19423096; 26126046; 6270941; 3000178; 3022613}. An origin from ectopic gonadal stroma is suggested by the close embryological origin of gonads and adrenals {9100075; 10385563; 19266086} and from reports of non-neoplastic Leydig cells in the adrenal cortex and within adrenal ganglioneuromas {4347248; 7190543; 6685441; 13749451}. Alternatively, they may arise from pluripotential mesenchymal cells exposed to high levels of gonadotropins or adrenocorticotropin {7190543}. Primitive spindle cells with Reinke-like crystals have been described in the adrenal cortex, supporting this hypothesis {4347248}. So-called ovarian thecal metaplasia in the adrenal supports this mechanism {3653882; 5110570; 851092; 21858518}.

Macroscopic appearance

These solitary unilateral solid tumours are well circumscribed, often encapsulated, and only occasionally cystic. Tumour sizes range from 19 to 90 mm. The cut surface is yellow, white, or brown {6685441}.

Histopathology

Like their ovarian adult granulosa cell counterparts, granulosa cell tumours display a microfollicular architecture with Call–Exner bodies and with macrofollicular, solid, and sarcomatoid patterns {29132723; 4687888; 19423096; 26126046}. Tumour cells are round to oval with scant cytoplasm and pale nuclei. Nuclear grooves are variably seen.

Leydig cell tumour is composed of closely packed cell nests with a prominent intervening capillary network. Trabeculae or cords interrupted by oedema cause small cysts or pseudoglands. Tumour cells have abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and round to oval nuclei with small to moderate-sized nucleoli. Brown cytoplasmic lipochrome pigment is common. Reinke crystals are necessary for classification as Leydig cell tumour {6270941; 3000178; 3022613}.

Sex cord tumours express SF1, α-inhibin, calretinin, and melan-A, but not adrenocortical-specific steroidogenic enzymes (CYP11B1, CYP11B2) {23240671; 19033865}.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

For granulosa cell tumour: a neoplasm composed of microfollicular architecture, large neoplastic cells, Call–Exner bodies, and round nuclei with irregular nuclear membranes and grooves Leydig cell tumour: a neoplasm composed of bland steroid-producing cells in closely packed nests, showing cytoplasmic Reinke crystals

Desirable: positivity for inhibin, calretinin, SF1, and melan-A (MART1), and negativity for EMA and other keratins, in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Surgical resection results in hormone normalization, with a good prognosis for the limited cases reported {26126046}.
