11.0.1.4 Teratomas with endocrine differentiation

Definition

Endocrine differentiation is frequently seen in teratomas that arise outside endocrine organs and ranges from benign endocrine tissue to malignant endocrine tumours.

ICD-O coding

9090/0 Struma ovarii, NOS

9090/3 Thyroid carcinoma arising in struma ovarii

9091/1 Ovarian neuroendocrine tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F76 & XH22M4 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of female genital organs & Struma ovarii, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: proliferative struma ovarii; ovarian carcinoid.

Not recommended: malignant struma ovarii; strumal carcinoid.

Subtype(s)

Struma ovarii; thyroid carcinoma arising in struma ovarii; ovarian neuroendocrine tumour

Localization

Endocrine differentiation is most commonly seen in ovarian teratomas but can also (rarely) arise in testicular or extragonadal teratomas {28078619}. The most common manifestations include struma ovarii, a mature ovarian teratoma in which thyroid tissue is the predominant or sole component, and ovarian teratomas that either have components of well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumour (NET) or consist solely of well-differentiated NET. Struma ovarii can give rise to thyroid carcinomas.

In the retrorectal space (presacral region), embryonic hindgut remnants may give rise to retrorectal cystic hamartoma (tailgut cyst), which may demonstrate neuroendocrine neoplasms {30176741; 29966957; 27917670}.

Clinical features

Endocrine differentiation is usually an incidental finding in teratomas that otherwise present as a mass or with local mass effect. Among patients with struma ovarii, as many as one third have some degrees of ascites, and 5% show clinical evidence of hyperthyroidism {5448747; 8253550}. As many as one third of patients with ovarian NET showing enterochromaffin-like cell (ECL-cell) differentiation can present with carcinoid syndrome, and rare patients with ovarian L-cell NET have chronic constipation due to PYY secretion {1157010; 25473750; 26630222}. Although adrenal or pituitary tissue is quite rare, occasional functioning pituitary tumours arising in teratomas can manifest as hormone hypersecretion syndromes {16990705; 10643021; 16137552; 22105889; 32632841}.

Epidemiology

At least focal thyroid tissue can be seen in 15–20% of ovarian teratomas, but other endocrine tissues are only rarely seen {5909538}. Struma ovarii accounts for 3% of ovarian teratomas, with thyroid carcinomas arising in just 3% of these cases {19696610}. Ovarian NETs account for < 1% of ovarian teratomas {5909538}. The peak incidence of struma ovarii and associated thyroid carcinomas is in the fifth decade of life {16990705; 17365830}. Most patients with ovarian NET are postmenopausal {6715397; 834404}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Ovarian teratomas are thought to derive from benign germ cells that develop into various mature tissues, including those of endocrine origin {15761467}. It is not clear what drives the dominant thyroid differentiation in struma ovarii. Thyroid carcinomas arising in struma ovarii frequently demonstrate additional somatic mutations that are identical to those in their counterparts in the thyroid gland, including BRAF, KRAS, and NRAS mutations and CCDC6::RET, NCOA4::RET, and PAX8::PPARG rearrangements {8058267; 17721188; 26362194; 31538107; 33763376; 32743766}. Ovarian NETs are thought to arise from neuroendocrine cells within the constituent epithelia of mature teratoma {6715397; 1157010}.

Macroscopic appearance

Struma ovarii is usually < 100 mm in size and has a red-tan, beefy cut surface with variable nodularity and cystic change that resembles normal or hyperplastic thyroid tissue {8037292}. Thyroid-type carcinomas arising in struma ovarii may form more discrete masses {19696610}. Ovarian NETs are generally small and form a homogeneous yellow to tan solid nodule {11342766}.

Histopathology

For classification as struma ovarii, teratomas should be composed of > 50% thyroid tissue, although tumours with functional or malignant thyroid tissue fall into this category regardless of proportion {16990705}. The thyroid tissue generally consists of mature, colloid-filled follicles of various sizes. Cystic degeneration, a solid or microfollicular architecture, and oncocytic or clear cell features can confound recognition {8037292}. The designation “proliferative struma ovarii” can be applied if the tumour demonstrates densely packed small follicles or pseudopapillary architecture but no specific features of malignancy {8253550}. Struma ovarii expresses thyroglobulin, thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), and PAX8 {17285841; 16512597}.

Diagnosis of thyroid carcinoma arising in struma ovarii should be based on criteria for specific tumour types in the thyroid gland. The majority of these malignancies are classic papillary carcinoma, followed by the follicular subtype of papillary carcinoma, and follicular carcinoma {19696610; 19620939; 18753973; 18058267; 17721188; 22288964; 18317221; 8253550; 33836675; 33763376; 26374222}; only rare cases of poorly differentiated or anaplastic carcinomas have been reported {26185700; 26630220; 25272300}. A subset of cases can show minimal cytological atypia but still behave in a clinically malignant fashion {19696610; 19620939; 22288964}.

Ovarian NETs can be subdivided into insular, trabecular, or mucinous types depending on architectural pattern {1157010; 6715397; 7104979; 11342766}. All of these histological patterns demonstrate uniform cuboidal to columnar cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm and centrally located nuclei with speckled chromatin and occasional cytoplasmic granules. The term “strumal carcinoid” has traditionally been used when other patterns are intimately mixed with thyroid follicles {7427909}. Ovarian NETs demonstrate variable expression of chromogranin, synaptophysin, and CDX2, and most commonly are CK7-positive and CK20-negative {19047909; 28735441}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant (aspiration of ovarian lesions is not performed)

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not applicable

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

For struma ovarii: teratoma with thyroid tissue predominating For thyroid carcinoma arising in struma ovarii: meets diagnostic criteria for specific tumour type in the thyroid gland or demonstrates clinically malignant behaviour For ovarian NET: teratoma with unequivocal evidence of a neoplastic neuroendocrine component

Staging

Struma ovarii is not staged. Malignancies arising in teratoma are staged according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) / Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of struma ovarii is generally excellent {8253550; 26374222}. The risk of recurrent disease for patients with thyroid carcinoma arising in struma ovarii is as high as 35% at 10 years, but these patients still have a 10-year survival rate of 90% {33836675; 25375817; 19696610}. Risk factors for disease recurrence and progression are not well established, although carcinomas confined to the ovary at presentation have better outcomes {33836675}. Ovarian NETs also have an excellent prognosis when confined to the ovary, with a 5-year survival rate of 95%, but can follow an aggressive course in advanced disease, with a 5-year survival rate of 53% {31959493}.
