3.2.2.12 Secretory carcinoma

Definition

Secretory carcinoma (SC) is a salivary gland–type carcinoma often composed of microcystic, tubular, and solid structures with abundant eosinophilic homogeneous or bubbly secretions. It is usually characterized by specific rearrangement of the ETV6 gene.

ICD-O coding

8502/3 Secretory carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D10.Y & XH44J4 Other specified malignant neoplasms of thyroid gland & Secretory carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: mammary analogue secretory carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SC can occur in either lobe or in the isthmus.

Clinical features

SC usually presents as a uninodular mass. Compression or invasion of adjacent structures and nodal disease are more common than in salivary SC {30564997; 32562215}.

Epidemiology

Average age at presentation is slightly older than at salivary gland sites (61.5 years, range: 36–74 years). There appears to be a female predilection {30564997; 32562215}.

Etiology

The etiology is unclear. Occasional co-occurrence with papillary thyroid carcinoma, including rare cases with shared translocations, is noted {27282352}, implying a possible follicular origin.

Pathogenesis

SCs are characterized by ETV6 translocations, with ETV6::NTRK3 exclusively seen in the thyroid to date {34655408}.

Macroscopic appearance

The mean size of reported thyroid SCs is 44 mm (range: 24–76 mm). They show firm, white-tan, often infiltrative cut surfaces {30564997; 32562215}.

Histopathology

Thyroid SCs are morphologically similar to SCs at other sites and are composed of cells with eosinophilic, vacuolated cytoplasm, arranged in papillary, tubular, microcystic, and solid patterns. SC characteristically shows eosinophilic, bubbly luminal secretions. Rare cases show high-grade transformation {28556362; 30564997; 34655408}.

Immunohistochemistry

SCs characteristically stain for S100, mammaglobin, GATA3, and (often) GCDFP-15. Thyroglobulin and thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) are typically negative, with weak, focal PAX8 staining {27400918; 32562215}. PAS and mucicarmine usually highlight the secretions.

Differential diagnosis

Architecture, nuclear features, and eosinophilic cytoplasm may raise consideration for oncocytic carcinoma, medullary carcinoma, and papillary thyroid carcinoma. However, the characteristic cytoplasm, secretions, and immunophenotype are highly unlikely to be seen outside of SC. Secondary involvement by a non-thyroid SC should be excluded clinicoradiologically.

Cytology

Aspirates show cohesive vaguely histiocytoid cells with eosinophilic vacuolar cytoplasm arranged in sheets or arborizing 3D structures. Nuclei have moderate size variation and may show clearing, prominent nucleoli, and membrane irregularities such as grooves or rare pseudoinclusions {27633961}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

ETV6 translocations confirm the diagnosis when present along with the typical morphology and immunophenotype.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: eosinophilic vacuolar cells; bubbly secretions; microcystic, tubular, solid, and papillary architecture; S100/SOX10 (not well studied in thyroid SC) and mammaglobin expression; absent to weak thyroid differentiation marker expression.

Desirable: ETV6 translocation.

Staging

Staging is according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Thyroid SCs appear more aggressive than their salivary counterparts. More than half of patients experience either locoregional recurrence or distant metastases. Positive margins and extrathyroidal extension may be associated with recurrence {30564997; 32562215}.
