5.1.1.2 Insulinoma

Definition

Insulinoma is a functioning well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumour (NET) composed of insulin-producing cells, causing a hypoglycaemic syndrome due to uncontrolled insulin secretion.

ICD-O coding

8151/3 Neuroendocrine tumour, insulin-producing (insulinoma)

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH3UK0 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas & Insulinoma, malignant

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Insulinomas are evenly distributed in the pancreas {24921202}. Extremely rare extrapancreatic insulinomas have been described in the lung, duodenum, ileum, jejunum, hilum of the spleen, and gastric antrum {4296076; 5314568; 6372987; 24006218; 33787590}.

Clinical features

Insulinomas are more frequently diagnosed in adults and the peak of incidence is in the sixth decade of life, although individuals of any age can be affected {24921202}. Women are slightly more affected than men. About 10% of insulinomas develop metastases, which occur more frequently in men with tumours larger than 20 mm {1677058; 18156937}. Multiple insulinomas are seen in about 10% of patients, most often in the setting of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 or, more rarely, MAFA-related familial insulinomatosis {19011561; 29339498}.

Hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia is the clinical hallmark, characterized by autonomic and neuroglycopenic symptoms. Adrenergic symptoms include palpitations and tremor, while cholinergic symptoms include sweating, hunger, and/or paraesthesia. The neuroglycopenic symptoms include severe weakness and a variety of psychiatric and neurological manifestations. Other common signs and symptoms include confusion, agitation, blurred vision, seizures, transient loss of consciousness, and hypoglycaemic coma {19088155}. The diagnosis is based on the presence of hypoglycaemic symptoms and low plasma glucose levels, which resolve after glucose administration (the Whipple triad).

Biochemical investigations

The best diagnostic test is prolonged fasting (48–72 h) with measurement of blood glucose, serum insulin, C-peptide, and proinsulin {17312378}. Biologically aggressive and metastatic insulinomas can change hormone production and, consequently, clinical syndrome during the course of the disease {25984844; 26672633}.

Imaging

Insulinomas are detected by CT, MRI, EUS, 68Ga-DOTATOC/DOTATATE PET, and radiolabelled GLP analogue scintigraphy {15763703; 19060454; 19077417; 31951592}. 68Ga-DOTATOC/DOTATATE PET may show variable uptake and has a sensitivity of 25% in detecting insulinomas {33297381}.

Epidemiology

Insulinoma is the most common functioning pancreatic NET (PanNET), accounting for 42% of functioning PanNETs and 4–20% of resected PanNETs {17896148; 24499825}. The incidence in a well-defined population (Olmsted County, south-eastern Minnesota, USA) was estimated to be 0.4 cases per 100 000 person-years {1677058}, whereas in less well defined populations the incidence was found to range from 0.07 to 0.12 cases per 100 000 person-years {18508996}.

Etiology

There are no known etiological factors for sporadic solitary insulinomas. Pathogenic germline MEN1 variants can cause insulinomas. There may be an association with diabetes {25291526}. Rare cases of insulinoma have also been observed in patients with tuberous sclerosis {33632913} and neurofibromatosis type 1 {16861979}. Familial insulinomatosis has recently been attributed to pathogenic MAFA variants {29339498}.

Pathogenesis

Recent molecular findings suggest that sporadic small and indolent insulinomas show a different molecular profile than aggressive insulinomas and other sporadic PanNETs {32103422}. Indeed, indolent insulinomas show a lower tumour mutation burden than non-functioning PanNETs (NF-PanNETs). In addition, the genes that are frequently altered in NF-PanNETs (e.g. MEN1, DAXX, ATRX, CDKN2A, PTEN, and TSC2) are rarely mutated in sporadic indolent insulinomas {31462556; 32103422}. Conversely, YY1 mutation (p.T372R mutation), which is extremely rare in other PanNETs, is found in about 30% of cases and is considered a specific driver mutation, associated with older age and lower serum glucose level {24326773; 25763608; 31462556}. Although YY1 mutation was not found to be associated with YY1 protein loss, strong YY1 immunoreactivity is thought to correlate with a better prognosis {24326773; 33705973}. Accordingly with the gene mutation profile, insulinomas cluster separately from other PanNET types at the RNA level {26446169}. Alternative-lengthening-of-telomeres phenotype, ATRX/DAXX loss, and CDKN2A loss have been observed in some insulinomas that are associated with metastatic disease and ARX expression, suggesting that cellular differentiation and tumorigenic mechanisms in aggressive insulinomas are more closely related to NF-PanNETs than to small indolent insulinomas {32103422; 32590616}.

Macroscopic appearance

Insulinomas showing indolent behaviour are small (10–20 mm in diameter), solitary, and well-demarcated PanNETs, generally not associated with metastatic dissemination. Sometimes they can be haemorrhagic and (very rarely) cystic {33122530}. Biologically aggressive insulinomas are typically larger than 30 mm and have a higher risk of metastatic dissemination {32103422}.

Histopathology

Insulinomas are composed of well-differentiated neuroendocrine cells forming trabecular, solid, or (more rarely) tubuloacinar structures, sometimes entrapping non-neoplastic ductules. In some cases, prominent hyalinized sclerosis can be observed. Stromal amyloid deposits determined by the accumulation of islet amyloid polypeptide (IAPP; also called amylin) can be found in about 5% of cases and represents a quite specific morphological feature. However, amyloid deposition has also been described in extremely rare IAPP-secreting PanNETs not showing insulin expression {32399832}.

Grading

Most insulinoma are G1, with a Ki-67 labelling index < 3%. Metastatic and aggressive insulinomas are more frequently G2. Very rare G3 cases (Ki-67 index > 20%) have been described {25866230}.

Immunohistochemistry

Like in other PanNETs, tumour cells are positive for general neuroendocrine markers including synaptophysin, chromogranin A, and INSM1. They are also immunoreactive for insulin, proinsulin, and IAPP; IAPP is also positive in extracellular amyloid deposits. Insulin expression may vary in intensity among tumour cells and in some cases shows an apical cytoplasmic distribution, whereas proinsulin shows a perinuclear pattern reflecting Golgi localization. In patients with hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia and multiple PanNETs, immunohistochemistry for insulin is required in order to distinguish insulinoma from other PanNETs, because the largest tumour is not always the one that is responsible for the clinical syndrome {16699310}. About half of insulinomas contain scattered cells positive for glucagon, PP, somatostatin, and/or other hormones. Small indolent insulinomas are strongly positive for PDX1 (an islet B-cell marker) and negative for ARX (an A-cell marker), whereas aggressive insulinomas are ARX-positive and show variable PDX1 expression {32103422}.

Cytology

The cytological features are identical to those of other PanNETs.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinical findings of hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia associated with a keratin-positive PanNEN (PanNET); Ki-67 immunohistochemistry.

Staging

The staging of insulinomas is the same as for other PanNETs.

Prognosis and prediction

Stage is an important prognostic factor, whereas grade seems to have less prognostic impact in insulinomas, because it does not predict the prognosis between G1 and G2 cases {25866230}. Insulinomas < 20 mm are associated with a 10-year survival rate close to 100%, whereas insulinomas > 20 mm and with ALT and ATRX/DAXX loss have an increased risk of metastasis and are associated with a 10-year survival rate of about 30% {24148618; 25866230; 28199314; 29486199; 30315258}. Recently, ARX protein expression was found to be associated with metastatic dissemination and worse survival, suggesting a distinct tumorigenic mechanism in aggressive insulinomas, with similarities to NF-PanNETs, compared with indolent ARX-negative / PDX1-positive insulinomas {32103422}.
