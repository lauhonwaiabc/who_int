9.0.3.2A PEComa

Definition

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa) is a mesenchymal neoplasm composed of perivascular epithelioid cells (PECs) – distinctive epithelioid cells that are often closely associated with blood vessel walls and express both melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

ICD-O coding

8714/3 Perivascular epithelioid tumour, malignant

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH4CC6 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Perivascular epithelioid tumour, benign

Related terminology

Acceptable: angiomyolipoma; epithelioid angiomyolipoma; lymphangioleiomyomatosis.

Not recommended: clear cell myomelanocytic tumour.

Subtype(s)

Perivascular epithelioid tumour, benign; perivascular epithelioid tumour, malignant; angiomyolipoma; angiomyolipoma, epithelioid

Localization

PEComas show a wide anatomical distribution but are extremely rare in the thyroid, pituitary, pancreas, and adrenal glands {11533079; 15842631; 16327428; 18277881; 20975340; 22154607; 24061520; 27189222; 30001237; 31921981}.

Clinical features

Clinical presentation is site-dependent; many PEComas of endocrine organs are incidentally detected radiologically, but some present as a mass or with endocrine dysfunction, depending on the site of involvement {25127092; 32201083}.

Epidemiology

PEComas are rare and show a female predilection (M:F ratio: ~0.2:1), with a wide age range and a peak in young to middle-aged adults (mean age: 45 years) {10976698; 16327428; 18223480; 24061520}.

Etiology

Most PEComas are sporadic; a small subset are associated with tuberous sclerosis {11406657; 16327428; 18223480}.

Pathogenesis

Loss of heterozygosity involving the TSC2 locus has been found {18085521}. Deletion of 16p, the location of the TSC2 gene, indicates the oncogenetic relationship of PEComas and angiomyolipomas as TSC2-linked neoplasms. TP53 mutations have been identified in 63% of TSC2-mutated PEComas {25651471}. A small subset of PEComas harbour TFE3 gene fusions, correlating with strong nuclear immunoreactivity for TFE3 {20871214}. The most common fusion partner of TFE3 is SFPQ (PSF) {19606011; 25651471; 26274027}. DVL2::TFE3 and NONO::TFE3 gene fusions have also been identified in PEComas arising in soft tissues {26975036}. TFE3-rearranged PEComa lacks TSC2 mutations / loss of heterozygosity and thus appears to be pathogenetically distinct {22456611; 25651471}. The molecular alterations found in PEComas are associated with an aberrant increase in mTOR pathway signalling {32320927}.

Macroscopic appearance

PEComas are grossly well circumscribed, with a firm and fibrous or fleshy cut surface. Tumours show a wide size range (mean: 50–80 mm) {16327428; 24061520; 30001237}.

Histopathology

PEComas typically show a nested architecture and are composed of epithelioid cells with abundant granular eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm and round nuclei with small nucleoli. The nests or trabeculae are typically surrounded by thin-walled capillary vessels. A small subset of tumours have predominantly spindle cell morphology {16327428; 24061520}. PEComa usually shows a distinctive perivascular pattern of growth, with tumour cells radially arranged around vessels, replacing the vessel wall and approaching the endothelium. Epithelioid angiomyolipoma is synonymous with PEComa {18852677; 20410812}. Typical PEComa may contain occasional multinucleated cells and show limited pleomorphism (symplastic change), but mitotic figures are usually scarce or absent.

Within the adrenal gland, angiomyolipoma is the most common subtype of PEComa and is composed of variable amounts of mature fat, bland spindle cells with granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, and branching thin- and thick-walled vessels.

Malignant PEComas are characterized by a variable combination of mitotic activity, necrosis, and pleomorphism {11406657; 16327428; 24061520; 30001237}.

Immunohistochemistry

PEComas characteristically express both melanocytic markers (e.g. HMB45, the most sensitive immunomarker; melan-A; and MITF) and muscle markers (e.g. SMA, desmin, and caldesmon {11800647; 18223480; 19604538; 24061520; 30001237}. Expression of melanocytic markers is more common in epithelioid cells than spindled tumour cells. Desmin and h-caldesmon are often less extensively positive than SMA {18223480}. Some tumours lack expression of muscle markers {19604538}. Nuclear expression of S100 is generally not observed in PEComas. Approximately 15% of cases show strong nuclear staining for TFE3 {16327428; 20871214; 24061520; 26975036}. PEComas with TFE3 rearrangement tend to occur in younger patients, have a prominent alveolar growth pattern and epithelioid morphology, and often lack expression of smooth muscle markers {22456611; 25651471; 26274027; 26975036}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

For cases with strong TFE3 protein expression, identification of TFE3 gene rearrangement or a corresponding fusion gene can help confirm the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid and/or spindle cells with granular eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm; nested, trabecular, or sheet-like architecture with frequent perivascular orientation; variable coexpression of melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

Desirable: TFE3 expression if smooth muscle markers are negative.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Typically, malignant tumours are large; show marked nuclear atypia and pleomorphism, conspicuous mitoses, necrosis, and infiltrative margins; and tend to pursue an aggressive clinical course {16327428; 30001237}. Tumour size > 50 mm has been significantly associated with recurrence {22619565}. The most common metastatic sites are the liver, lymph nodes, lungs, and bone {16327428; 24061520; 30001237}. Patients with unresectable/advanced malignant PEComas may respond to mTOR inhibitors {32320927}.
