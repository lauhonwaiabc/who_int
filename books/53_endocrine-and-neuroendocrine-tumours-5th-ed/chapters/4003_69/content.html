4.0.0.3 Parathyroid lipoadenoma

Definition

Parathyroid lipoadenoma is a benign parathyroid neoplasm characterized by an unequivocal increase in parenchymal parathyroid tissue associated with abundant adipose tissue.

ICD-O coding

8324/0 Parathyroid lipoadenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F37.Y & XH8P28 Other specified benign neoplasm of endocrine glands & Lipoadenoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: parathyroid hamartoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Parathyroid lipoadenomas can arise in normally positioned glands, predominantly in left-sided locations {26585863}. Ectopic sites have also been reported, most commonly the mediastinum {7258157; 7364585; 26585863}. Subsets of patients develop multiglandular disease with two or more enlarged glands exhibiting abundant fat, termed lipohyperplasia {6648802; 18813123; 32193823}.

Clinical features

Most parathyroid lipoadenomas are functioning, and patients usually exhibit mild biochemical evidence of hyperparathyroidism with classic symptomatology {26585863; 32193823; 31773224}.

Epidemiology

Fewer than 100 cases have been reported in the literature {32193823}. A slight female predominance and a mean age of 56 years are noted {26585863}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. There is no known hereditary predisposition. An association with hypertension has been suggested {32193823}.

Pathogenesis

The underlying molecular events are unknown.

Macroscopic appearance

The reported size and glandular weights vary, and parathyroid lipoadenomas tend to be larger than parathyroid adenomas {26585863}, with mean weights > 1 g reported in some case series {18813123}. Tumours are often encapsulated and display a yellow cut surface.

Histopathology

The diagnosis should be restricted to adenomas with a significant increase in both adipose and parenchymal tissue {31773224}. Parathyroids that are enlarged solely because of an accumulation of adipose tissue but without an increase in parenchymal content should not be considered lipoadenoma {31773224}. Similarly, the presence of some adipose tissue in an otherwise typical parathyroid adenoma should not warrant the diagnosis {32193823}. A useful general rule is that > 50% of the volume of the gland should be adipose tissue to warrant the diagnosis {32193823}. The stromal component may include brown fat and myxoid or fibrous differentiation {26585863; 32193823}. A rim of compressed non-tumorous parathyroid tissue is usually present.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a definite increase in both parenchymal and adipose tissue in a parathyroid tumour; > 50% of the glandular volume is adipose tissue.

Desirable: a significant drop in intraoperative PTH or evidence of postoperative biochemical cure, or additional parathyroid gland(s) without a pathological diagnosis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lipoadenomas are benign. A series from Sweden suggested that patients with hyperparathyroidism treated by removal of lipoadenoma may have a lower initial biochemical cure rate and higher recurrence rate than those treated by removal of usual adenoma; however, this observation requires further validation {32193823}.
