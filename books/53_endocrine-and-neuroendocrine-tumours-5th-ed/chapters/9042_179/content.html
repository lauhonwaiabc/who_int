9.0.4.2 Leiomyosarcoma

Definition

Leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is a sarcoma composed of cells showing smooth muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8890/3 Leiomyosarcoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2D12.Y & XH7ED4 Other specified malignant neoplasms of other endocrine glands or related structures & Leiomyosarcoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common endocrine organ site for LMS is the adrenal gland {32871746}, followed by the thyroid gland {9028371}.

Clinical features

Adrenal LMSs most often present with abdominal or flank pain/discomfort, and less often with weight loss, anorexia, and an enlarging abdominal mass {32871746}. Symptom duration varies from sudden onset to days to several months and years {32871746}. Adrenal LMSs are non-functioning and are not associated with signs/symptoms of hormonal secretion. However, elevated levels of serum NSE may occur (normalizing after excision) {18250540}, and elevated serum CA125 {17910774} may rarely occur. On imaging, adrenal LMSs are heterogeneous-appearing; they may be well-defined, lobulated, or with irregular borders; and they may have necrosis {32871746}. Some tumours show infiltrative growth (including into the inferior vena cava through tumour thrombus), infiltration of adjacent organs (e.g. kidney, liver, pancreas), or lymph node or visceral metastases (liver, lung, bone) {32871746}. Tumours are unilateral, with no side predilection {32871746}.

Thyroid LMS typically presents as a painless, rapidly growing neck mass {9028371; 27313727}. Additional symptoms may include hoarseness, dysphagia, dyspnoea, weight loss, and arm pain {27313727}. The majority of patients with LMS are euthyroid with no prior history of radiation exposure {27313727}. Tumours are typically solitary and limited to a single lobe {9028371; 27313727}, although large tumours may involve both lobes and the isthmus {27313727}. Thyroid nuclear scans may identify a cold nodule or an enlarged gland with areas of increased or decreased uptake of radioactive iodine {27313727}. Ultrasound may reveal a poorly defined or well-defined hypoechoic mass, a solid or partially cystic nodule, or a calcified nodule {27313727}. CT may show a low-density mass with dense calcification and necrosis, a well-demarcated mass, a soft tissue mass with calcification, or (in select cases) direct tumorous invasion of the adjacent structures {27313727}. MRI may reveal a mass of intermediate signal on T2-weighted images and an isointense mass on T1-weighted images with a fair gadolinium enhancement {11274837}.

Epidemiology

Primary LMSs of endocrine organs, including the adrenal and thyroid, are rare. Adrenal LMS may occur over a wide age range, from the second to the ninth decade of life (median: 60 years) {32871746}. There is no sex preference {32871746}.

Thyroid LMS is rare: about 0.014% of all LMSs develop in the thyroid gland {27313727}. Primary thyroid gland LMS may occur over a wide age range, from the fourth to the tenth decade of life (median: 65 years). There is a slight female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:1.3) {27313727}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Smooth muscle cell tumours of the adrenal gland are thought to derive from the adrenal vein or its branches, considering its smooth muscle differentiation. However, it is not clear if all tumours originate from the adrenal vein or from other, smaller, intra-adrenal vasculature {9808515; 32871746}. Thyroid gland LMSs are thought to arise from perithyroidal vascular smooth muscle cells {9028371}.

A subset of patients with adrenal LMS have AIDS {7503667; 9278611; 15523410}, which in turn may be associated with EBV {7503667}. Thyroid LMS may occur in children in the setting of congenital immunodeficiency {10199478}.

Macroscopic appearance

Adrenal LMS appears as a lobulated/multinodular, greyish to white-tan, well-defined, roundish tumour that may have areas of cystic degeneration, haemorrhage, myxoid-appearing change and/or necrosis. These tumours may be encapsulated or unencapsulated and extra-adrenal invasion may be identified, including into the inferior vena cava or tumour thrombus in the adrenal vein {32871746}. Tumour size ranges from 8 to 270 mm (median: 90 mm).

Thyroid LMS is circumscribed to widely infiltrative, and on cut section it is white or tan-grey with a whorled appearance and may have areas of haemorrhage, cystic degeneration, and/or necrosis. The tumour size ranges from 11 to 160 mm (mean diameter: 66 mm) {9028371; 27313727}.

Histopathology

Regardless of site, the histology of LMS shows spindle cells arranged in compact interlacing fascicles. Tumour cells include oval to elongated (cigar-shaped) and frequently blunt-ended hyperchromatic nuclei with a perinuclear vacuole and eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm. The cellularity, nuclear pleomorphism, mitotic activity, and tumour necrosis vary, but typically these tumours are hypercellular with moderate to marked nuclear pleomorphism, increased mitotic activity (with or without atypical forms), and tumour necrosis {9028371}. Tumour giant cells may be present. Entrapped adrenal or thyroid parenchyma may be seen.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic cells show immunoreactivity for SMA, MSA, desmin, and caldesmon {9028371; 27080750; 29549682; 31244910; 32871746} but are negative for cytokeratins, S100, SOX10, KIT (CD117), CD34, STAT6, myogenin, and myoglobin. In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) may be positive {10199478}.

In adrenal tumours, the tumour cells are negative for markers associated with adrenal cortical neoplasms (e.g. SF1, synaptophysin, inhibin, and melan-A) and for phaeochromocytoma (e.g. tyrosine hydroxylase, dopamine hydroxylase {33405069}, synaptophysin, chromogranin, INSM1, and sustentacular S100/SOX10). In thyroid tumours, the tumour cells are negative for cytokeratins, thyroglobulin, thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), PAX8, calcitonin, CEA, and neuroendocrine markers (synaptophysin, chromogranin, and INSM1). Proliferation rates by Ki-67 staining vary but typically are increased and may be as high as 60% {31244910}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis for adrenal LMS includes leiomyoma, peripheral nerve sheath tumours, solitary fibrous tumour, and sarcomas of the retroperitoneum secondarily invading the adrenal gland.

The differential diagnosis for thyroid LMS primarily includes anaplastic thyroid carcinoma; secondary invasion of the thyroid from a neoplasm in the neck; and metastasis to the thyroid gland from LMS originating in other organs including the uterus {17988195; 32665867; 27777040; 16445675; 12423830; 8575279; 27015437}, vagina {32996738}, lung {21784409}, soft tissues of the lower extremity {20419977; 9684512}, and the pulmonary artery {15148841}.

Cytology

FNA preoperative diagnosis of thyroid LMS may be useful in demonstrating malignant spindle-shaped cells with nuclear pleomorphism and increased mitotic activity {20419977; 2292224; 23335096}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sarcoma arranged in fascicles composed of spindled cells with blunt-ended hyperchromatic nuclei, nuclear pleomorphism, and increased mitoses.

Desirable: tumour necrosis; atypical mitoses; immunoreactivity for SMA, desmin, and/or caldesmon; presence of adrenal or thyroid parenchyma.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM eighth edition

Prognosis and prediction

Surgery is the treatment of choice. For adrenal LMS, the 1-year survival rate is reported to be 60%, and the 2-year survival rate is 15.3% {32871746}. For thyroid LMS the prognosis is poor, with an estimated 1-year survival rate of 5–10% {18274920}.
