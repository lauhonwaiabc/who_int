8.1.2.3 Merkel cell carcinoma

Definition

Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) is a high-grade primary cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC).

ICD-O coding

8247/3 Merkel cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C34 & XH81N8 Cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma & Merkel cell carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: primary neuroendocrine carcinoma of skin.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MCC most commonly arises on sun-exposed sites. A review of 14 414 patients with MCC demonstrated that 43% of cases developed on the head or neck {27198511}.

Clinical features

MCC presents as a rapidly growing, often violaceous papule or nodule. The broad clinical differential diagnosis includes basal cell and squamous cell carcinomas, lymphoma, atypical fibroxanthoma, and melanoma. The majority (65%) of patients present with disease localized to the skin, 26% with regional lymph node metastases, and 8% with distant metastases {27198511}.

Epidemiology

MCC most commonly affects older White, non-Hispanic males. A population-based survey of 11 028 cases from 21 countries showed the highest rates in Australia, New Zealand, and the USA {29533867}. MCC incidence per 1 million person-years has increased significantly in the USA from 1.5 cases in 1986, to 4.4 cases in 2001, to 7.9 cases in 2011 {15611998; 26215243; 29102486}.

Etiology

Risk factors for MCC development include male sex, old age, chronic sun exposure, and immunosuppression. Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) infection and ultraviolet (UV) radiation–induced mutagenesis reflect two distinct pathways for MCC development. Asymptomatic MCPyV infection occurs early in life, with seropositivity rates of 60–70% {28893943}.

Pathogenesis

Clonally integrated MCPyV expresses the viral oncoproteins large T-antigen (LT) and small T-antigen {25681708; 28802497; 29072302; 30417062} and is responsible for 60–80% of cases {18202256; 27815175}. Truncated LT binds and inactivates RB1 protein, allowing cell-cycle progression. Virus-negative MCC possesses a significantly higher genomic instability and somatic mutation burden than MCPyV-positive MCC – predominantly UV radiation signature mutations affecting TP53, RB1, and NOTCH family members {26215243; 26238782; 26655088; 26627015; 31399473}. MCPyV-positive MCCs predominate in areas with high ambient UV radiation, whereas MCPyV-negative MCCs predominate in low-exposure areas {30417062}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Scanning magnification shows a densely cellular dermal malignancy with frequent extension into the subcutis, infiltrative or pushing borders, and variably dense lymphohistiocytic inflammatory infiltrate. Intraepidermal MCC is rare {27198511; 28802497}. Tumour cells with scant cytoplasm, round to oval nuclei, and finely granular chromatin are arranged as sheets, cords, and trabeculae. Mitotic figures and apoptotic bodies are common. Tumour cells are usually intermediate in size, but large cell and small cell morphologies may occur {28802497; 29556962}. MCC may show divergent differentiation, most commonly glandular {8751563; 23530585; 25699980} or squamous {9810922; 19609205; 19702685; 21453956; 23664542; 23530585; 26541273}, and less commonly sarcomatoid or neuroblastic {28802497}. Distinct foci of invasive squamous cell carcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma in situ are found in as many as 15% of cases, and there may be a relationship between them {30417062; 34593967; 34480892}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical studies are essential for confirming the diagnosis of MCC. The most specific marker is CK20, which highlights most MCCs (~88%) {9042291; 10027519; 11175640; 11127923; 16625069; 30067951; 31201352} with characteristic perinuclear dots. MCCs show neuroendocrine differentiation, including synaptophysin (92%) and chromogranin A (84%). Neurofilament is expressed in ~80% of MCCs. Immunohistochemical detection of MCPyV LT (CM2B4) is sensitive (88%) and specific (94%) {27815175}. A subset of MCPyV-negative MCCs demonstrate divergent immunophenotypic findings (thyroid transcription factor 1 [TTF1] positivity, or negativity for neurofilament and/or CK20) {30067951; 31201352}. CK20-negative MCCs may be diagnosed by neurofilament positivity and TTF1 negativity; MCPyV staining has limited sensitivity for CK20-negative tumours {25394777}. A meta-analysis identified frequent PAX5 (~90%), TdT (~65%), and KIT (CD117) (63%) in MCC {28693804}. SATB2 is also expressed in MCCs {30349028; 31233624}. Expression of B-cell markers is associated with MCPyV {32134459}. These are all important pitfalls, because detection of these biomarkers and MCPyV can assist the distinction of MCCs from other NECs {30349028}.

Cytology

Smears are highly cellular and have a predominantly dispersed pattern, with scattered irregular tissue fragments consisting of crowded cells that have tightly packed, moulded nuclei with minimal cytoplasm, although in some cases delicate cytoplasm can be seen. Single dispersed tumour cells have high N:C ratios, scant pale blue (Giemsa) cytoplasm, variable nuclear size and shape, finely stippled chromatin, and indistinct nucleoli. Stripped nuclei are plentiful, and apoptotic debris is often prominent, but necrosis is not usually seen {24678011}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular studies are not typically used in the diagnosis of MCC, although viral gene expression and UV radiation signature mutation detection have enabled distinction from histological mimickers {30986802; 32445471}. Immunohistochemical detection of MCPyV LT (CM2B4) is sensitive (88%) and specific (94%) {27815175}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a rapidly growing skin lesion; a highly cellular dermal malignancy composed of cells with round to oval nuclei and finely granular chromatin; immunopositive for cytokeratin (e.g. CK20) with dot-like perinuclear staining and for markers of neuroendocrine differentiation.

Desirable: expression of B-cell markers and/or MCPyV may be helpful in the distinction from other NECs.

Staging

The eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system forms the basis for MCC staging {27198511}.

Prognosis and prediction

Five-year overall survival rates are about 51% for those with localized disease, 35% for patients with regional lymph node metastases, and 14% when distant metastases are present. In node-negative patients, T stage robustly predicts 5-year overall survival: 55.8% for pT1 disease, 41.1% for pT2/pT3, and 31.8% for pT4 {27198511}, although recent data suggest that survival rates are substantially higher {32103415}. Cases with clinically evident lymph node metastases have a worse prognosis than cases with clinically occult lymph node involvement {27198511}, and the pattern and extent of sentinel lymph node involvement are additional prognostic factors {27166398; 31094923}. Patients with clinically evident regional lymph node disease without a known primary MCC are distinguished from patients with a known primary MCC (stage IIIA vs stage IIIB) and have a better prognosis {22030017; 23182060; 23835211}.

Additional prognostic features of the primary tumour include tumour thickness, pattern of growth, lymphovascular invasion, and tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, all of which are independently associated with patient survival {18798233; 21422430; 22467679; 27166398}. The pattern of sentinel lymph node involvement and the extent of disease burden in the sentinel lymph node are additional prognostic factors in MCC {27166398; 31094923}.

MCPyV-positive MCCs may have better survival rates than MCPyV-negative MCCs {19535775; 21642382; 22261808; 22795182; 27815175; 28763479}, although this is controversial {21422430; 22882157} and MCPyV status does not predict survival independently of stage {27815175}. Expression of p63 correlates with shorter survival times in MCC {17599745; 21765392; 22795182; 24746200}, but not independently of stage {24225752}. Ki-67 labelling index ≥ 55% {32696301} and TERT methylation {33909215} were associated with a bad prognosis.

Integrity of the host immune system is both a risk factor and a prognostic factor in MCC {10609948; 11853800; 18280333; 22120659}. Immunocompromised patients have worse survival rates than immunocompetent patients {23190897}. Increased expression of immune-related genes (e.g. CD8A and granzyme genes) and higher levels of tumour-infiltrative CD8+ T cells independently correlate with longer survival {21422430; 22467679; 27166398}. Furthermore, in MCPyV-positive MCCs, decreased expression of immune markers including granzyme and IDO1 correlated with shorter survival, whereas in MCPyV-negative MCCs, expression of several genes, including UBE2C, correlated with shorter survival {33547200}.
