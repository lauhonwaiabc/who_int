10.0.0.4 Rosai-Dorfman disease

Definition

Rosai–Dorfman disease (RDD) is a histiocytosis characterized by an accumulation of large S100-positive histiocytes commonly exhibiting emperipolesis.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Rosai–Dorfman disease

ICD-11 coding

EK92 Histiocytoses of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Acceptable: Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease.

Not recommended: sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RDD typically involves cervical lymph nodes. In as many as 43% of cases, RDD develops in extranodal locations, most frequently the head and neck, skin and soft tissues, and CNS {29720485; 26966089}. The thyroid is rarely affected, with < 10 cases published to date {10710276; 17542678; 12699598; 15738872; 22438629; 21630484; 31053123}. Isolated cases of RDD have been described in the posterior pituitary {7576285; 10341867; 20405324} and adrenal glands {24758864}.

Clinical features

RDD of the thyroid is usually accompanied by involvement of cervical lymph nodes and other extranodal sites {12699598; 22438629; 10710276; 21630484; 3512416; 15738872; 17542678}. Patients usually present with a cold thyroid nodule, which is typically tender on palpation. Systemic symptoms and abnormal laboratory findings (i.e. anaemia, leukocytosis, and hypergammaglobulinaemia) are common. RDD affecting the posterior pituitary presents as a suprasellar lesion and diabetes insipidus.

Epidemiology

In contrast to classic nodal RDD, which typically affects young men, most cases affecting endocrine organs arise in women between the fourth and sixth decades of life {10710276; 17542678; 15738872; 12699598; 22438629; 31053123; 10341867; 20405324}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. RDD may occur in a thyroid involved by autoimmune thyroid disease, for which a possible link has been postulated {12699598}. A proportion of cases of RDD show an increased number of IgG4-positive plasma cells, but it is doubtful whether there is a relationship with IgG4-related disease {24215263; 23955459}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is incompletely understood. Approximately 50% of cases carry gain-of-function mutations in MAPK/ERK pathway genes, including KRAS, NRAS, MAP2K1, ARAF, CSF1R, and (rarely) BRAF p.V600E, suggesting that at least a proportion of cases of RDD represent a neoplastic disorder {26566875; 26922062; 28597077; 28664935; 29236635; 32601132}.

Macroscopic appearance

RDD is usually well defined and tan-grey, with a soft or gritty texture.

Histopathology

The affected tissue shows infiltration by loose aggregates of large histiocytes with round vesicular nuclei, distinct nucleoli, and voluminous pale-staining cytoplasm, which typically exhibit emperipolesis of lymphocytes, neutrophils, and plasma cells. There are usually many admixed plasma cells. The histiocytes are immunoreactive for S100, CD68, fascin, and OCT2 {33177341} and variably immunoreactive for CD163 and CD14, but negative for CD1a and CD207. The background thyroid gland parenchyma often shows chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis. The differential diagnosis includes Erdheim–Chester disease, juvenile xanthogranuloma, and ALK-positive histiocytosis, all of which may express S100 and show focal emperipolesis {26966089; 33311000; 30573850}. The clinical, laboratory, and radiological context, as well as histopathological, immunohistochemical, and molecular features, will aid in their distinction.

Cytology

FNAC shows large histiocytes with emperipolesis in a background of mature lymphocytes, plasma cells, and neutrophils. Immunoreactivity for S100 is helpful for confirming the diagnosis {22438629; 21630484}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not applicable

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: accumulation of large histiocytes with round vesicular nuclei, distinct nucleoli, and abundant pale cytoplasm; emperipolesis; expression of S100.

Desirable: in diagnostically challenging cases, expression of OCT2 and cyclin D1 in the absence of CD1a, CD207 (langerin), and ALK.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Individuals with thyroid RDD have remained well after treatment. Thyroid involvement is not known to change the generally good prognosis of RDD {12699598}.
