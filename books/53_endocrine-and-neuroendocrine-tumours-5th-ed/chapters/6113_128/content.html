6.1.1.3 Adrenal myelolipoma

Definition

Adrenal myelolipoma is a benign tumour made of mature adipose tissue and haematopoietic cells.

ICD-O coding

8870/0 Adrenal myelolipoma

ICD-11 coding

2F37.Y & XH17C5 Other specified benign neoplasm of endocrine glands & Myelolipoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most adrenal myelolipomas occur in one gland, and bilaterality accounts for about 5% of cases {32275787}. Rare examples occur in adrenal rests {29164520}.

Clinical features

Myelolipoma often manifests with asymptomatic incidental adrenal lesions that may be detected as a result of nonspecific abdominal discomfort or pain {19481963}. Large myelolipomas, especially giant myelolipomas (> 100 mm), can cause compression, rupture, or haemorrhage {25878407; 33514622}. They are hormonally inactive unless combined with a functioning adrenocortical tumour or occurring in the setting of congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) {29164520; 28143811; 33471666}. In CAH, myelolipomas are more frequently bilateral {33471666}. Most are hypoechoic on ultrasound and are well-demarcated heterogeneous lesions on CT {33532014; 29194699}.

Epidemiology

Adrenal myelolipomas account for 3.3–6.5% of all primary adrenal tumours, with no sex bias and an average age of 50–60 years {29164520}. In patients with CAH, myelolipomas account for 25% of CAH-related adrenal tumours {33471666}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Several hypotheses have been suggested, including (1) interactions between two stem cell progenitors of adipocytes and bone marrow cells; (2) inflammation- or stress-induced metaplastic changes of blood capillary reticuloendothelial cells; (3) chromosomal translocation t(3;21)(q25;p11) or non-random X-chromosome inactivation {11996801; 16819325}; and (4) hormonal factors, mostly linked to ACTH overexpression or an androgen-mediated stimulus, as well as overexpression of MC2R and AR {29164520; 28143811; 33622747; 27307426; 24884994}.

Macroscopic appearance

Adrenal myelolipoma is a well-demarcated yellow or red nodule with a mean size of 100 mm (range: 5–430 mm) {29164520} and a median size of 23 mm {32275787}. The weight of giant myelolipomas can exceed 11 kg {12746567}.

Histopathology

Myelolipoma is composed of mature adipocytes and all three haematopoietic cell lineages. Calcifications and osseous metaplasia can occur. Myelolipoma can develop in combination with adrenocortical tumours, ganglioneuroma, hibernoma, bilateral macronodular adrenocortical disease, and CAH-related compact cell adrenocortical hyperplasia {29164520; 28143811; 28382483}. Myelolipomas with extensive haemorrhage can be mistaken for an adrenal haematoma or malignancy. The differential diagnosis includes adrenal lipoma, angiomyolipoma, and liposarcoma {29164520; 28143811}.

Cytology

Smears show scattered haematopoietic cells admixed with mature fat cells in a lipid-rich background, to suggest myelolipoma {18855887; 10572274; 32988789; 15376200}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed adrenal tumour composed of mature adipocytes and haematopoietic cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

No malignant progression has been reported. Acute haemorrhage and tumour rupture can (rarely) occur in large myelolipomas, usually those > 80 mm. Therefore, surgery can be considered for large and/or symptomatic tumours, with complete recovery expected.
