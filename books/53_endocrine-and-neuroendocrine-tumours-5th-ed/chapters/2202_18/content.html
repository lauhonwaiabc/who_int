2.2.0.2 Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma

Definition

Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma is a mixed solid and cystic squamous epithelial tumour with stellate reticulum and wet keratin (ghost cells), usually localized to the hypothalamic–pituitary axis and characterized by activating CTNNB1 mutations.

ICD-O coding

9351/1 Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma

ICD-11 coding

2F7A.Y & XH15X9 Other specified neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of endocrine glands & Craniopharyngioma, adamantinomatous

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma arises anywhere along the hypophysial canal, but most occur in the sellar and infundibulotuberal region {31699993}. The majority (~95%) have a suprasellar component (purely suprasellar, 20–41%; both suprasellar and intrasellar, 53–75%), whereas purely intrasellar craniopharyngiomas are less common (~5%) {15807869}. Occasionally, a tumour extends into the anterior (9%), middle (8%), or posterior (12%) fossa. Very rare examples present in the cerebellopontine angle and other ectopic sites {27908738}.

Clinical features

Craniopharyngioma rarely presents incidentally (< 2%) {31440945}. The diagnosis is often made years after the initial manifestation of nonspecific symptoms related to increased intracranial pressure, such as headache {15240606; 26392473}. Primary manifestations include visual impairment (62–84%) {28660394} and endocrine deficits (52–87%) affecting growth hormone (75%), LH/FSH (40%), TSH (25%), ACTH (25%), and vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone; 17–27% have central diabetes insipidus at diagnosis) {15807869; 31699993}. Reduced growth rates may be seen in children as young as 12 months, before a diagnosis of craniopharyngioma is made {15240606}. Weight gain, predictive of hypothalamic obesity, tends to occur as a later manifestation, shortly before and during the first year after diagnosis {11528558}. Almost half of patients develop hypothalamic syndrome from disease- or treatment-related hypothalamic involvement/damage {11528558; 15240606; 21490122; 23633208; 30925462} associated with morbid obesity, cognitive impairment, personality changes, and psychiatric symptoms {26369768; 30240857}.

Imaging

On MRI and CT, adamantinomatous craniopharyngiomas are intrasellar and parasellar tumours with solid and cystic components {15565547}. Imaging features follow a 90% rule: about 90% are predominantly cystic, about 90% have prominent calcifications, and about 90% take up contrast media in the cyst walls {3740186; 31699993}. Local invasion of hypothalamic, visual tract, and vascular structures, including encasement of the internal carotid arteries, is common (~25%) {28984520; 31699993}. Subarachnoid dissemination or implantation along the spinal cord, the surgical track, or the path of inserted catheters is rare {19859655; 21462099; 27278607; 30877004}.

Epidemiology

Craniopharyngioma constitutes 1.2–4.6% of all intracranial tumours, accounting for 0.5–2.5 new cases per 1 million person-years {9761047; 21336771; 22735773; 25375987}. Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma is the most common non-neuroepithelial intracerebral neoplasm in children (5–11% of intracranial tumours in this age group) {25375987; 16044344}. It accounts for nearly all craniopharyngioma diagnoses in children and about 80% of craniopharyngioma diagnoses in adults {20871436; 22735773; 26636252}.

There is a bimodal age distribution {21336771; 22735773}, with incidence peaks in children (5–15 years) and adults (45–60 years). Rare neonatal/fetal cases occur {11155065; 23329768; 27669698}. There is no sex predilection {9761047; 22735773; 25375987}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Craniopharyngiomas are proposed to arise from cellular elements related to the Rathke pouch/hypophysial duct, which is integral to pituitary development {31699993}. Expression of oncogenic β-catenin in early embryonic precursors and in SOX2+ stem cell populations of the pituitary drive formation of tumours resembling adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma {21636786; 24094324}. SOX2+ progenitors may also underlie formation of papillary craniopharyngioma {28506993} and Rathke cleft cyst {32453714}. Similar stem cell populations for adamantinomatous and papillary craniopharyngiomas {22031517} may explain shared patterns of cytokeratin expression {11459086; 11770017; 12296753; 17652797}; scattered cells expressing pituitary hormones {8098858}, chromogranin A {7773950}, and hCG {7903692}; and the occasional presence of mixed transitional tumour and cyst phenotypes {16465771; 19427020; 23344977; 29520296; 30010935}. In adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma, SOX2+ stem cells may contribute to the formation of epithelial whorls with nuclear localized β-catenin. The whorls are senescent and function as signalling centres secreting numerous factors including sonic hedgehog, FGF, TGF-β, BMPs, and proinflammatory mediators {17221204; 22349813; 27562488; 29180744; 29509940; 29541918; 32453714}. The whorls are analogous to the enamel knot that controls tooth morphogenesis {29541918; 32453714} and implicate paracrine signalling in tumour formation and signal transduction via primary cilia {27562488}. Histological and molecular parallels with odontogenic tumours suggest similar cells of origin and similar mechanisms of pathogenesis {6196702; 9067743} and explain the occasional presence of teeth in adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma {24571758}.

Macroscopic appearance

Adamantinomatous craniopharyngiomas are lobulated solid masses with one or several cysts filled with a dark green-brown liquid resembling machine oil.

Histopathology

The well-differentiated tumour epithelium forms cords, lobules, ribbons, nodular whorls, and irregular trabeculae. Peripheral crowding and palisading are prominent. Degenerative features such as fibrosis, calcification, and nodules/whorls of anucleate remnants of ghost-like squamous cells (wet keratin) are common. Loose microcystic areas of stellate reticulum often intermingle between the wet keratin and more densely arranged areas of tumour epithelium. Cysts are often lined by an attenuated, flattened epithelium. Finger-like tumour protrusions extend into surrounding gliotic brain tissue with numerous Rosenthal fibres. A secondary degenerative feature is a xanthogranulomatous reaction to ruptured cyst material, which is characterized by cholesterol clefts, haemosiderin deposits, xanthoma cells, multinucleated giant cells, and lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates. This extensive reaction can constitute a significant, and sometimes near-total, component of the surgically excised material, necessitating a careful search for residual identifiable epithelium and wet keratin. Some xanthogranulomas of the sellar region, however, are associated with a ruptured/haemorrhagic Rathke cleft cyst. Adamantinomatous craniopharyngiomas show a striking resemblance to odontogenic tumours including calcifying odontogenic cyst (50%), ameloblastoma (25%), and mixed calcifying odontogenic cyst and ameloblastoma (15%), and more rarely resemble a few other subtypes {9067743}. Malignant progression is exceedingly rare, generally developing after multiple recurrences and radiation therapy, often decades after first diagnosis {17592268; 24978859; 28290422; 31785438}, and resembling squamous cell carcinoma and ameloblastic or odontogenic ghost cell carcinoma {17592268; 31661068}. Primary suprasellar malignant tumour with odontogenic features may represent a morphological pattern of transformed adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma {17592268; 19625894}.

Immunophenotype

Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma have activating mutations in CTNNB1, which encodes β-catenin. The mutation is clonal and present across the tumour epithelium {24413733; 28069929; 32125720}; however, nuclear accumulation of β-catenin is spatially restricted and found in only a small percentage of cells, most pronounced within small epithelial whorls {15891929; 17221204; 29509940; 32125720}. Nuclear β-catenin is observed even when CTNNB1 mutations are not detected {28069929}. In general practice, immunohistochemistry can replace genetic analysis as an aid in determining the subtype diagnosis of craniopharyngioma {32965631}.

Proliferation index

Ki-67 is generally confined to palisading regions and is absent in epithelial whorls. The Ki-67 proliferation index varies widely between cases and provides no prognostic information {11118571; 14531547; 15146007; 26498373}.

Grading

Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma is regarded as CNS WHO grade 1.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes papillary craniopharyngioma, xanthogranuloma, Rathke cleft cyst, epidermoid and dermoid cysts, and pilocytic astrocytoma. The distinction from papillary craniopharyngioma can be made using immunohistochemistry; papillary craniopharyngiomas are positive for BRAF p.V600E using the VE1 antibody, whereas adamantinomatous craniopharyngiomas have β-catenin nuclear staining in the nodular whorls {32965631}. Xanthogranuloma of the sellar region is most often a reactive lesion resulting from the leakage, rupture, or haemorrhage of a Rathke cleft cyst, based on strips of columnar or ciliated columnar epithelium in the resected material {10208277; 23322180; 28120170; 28236350}, although occasionally it can also be secondary to the leakage of wet keratin from adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma. Epidermoid cysts are distinguished by the presence of a uniloculated cavity lined by keratinizing squamous epithelium and filled with flaky, dry keratin. Dermoid cysts additionally have adnexal structures. Rathke cleft cysts have a cyst wall lined by simple columnar or cuboidal epithelium, which often is ciliated, with mucinous goblet cells. Myxoid/mucoid cyst contents are common. Rosenthal fibres and dense piloid gliosis adjacent to adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma can mimic pilocytic astrocytoma, but the dense cellularity and biphasic nature characteristic of pilocytic astrocytoma are absent.

Cytology

Although not a specific finding, cyst fluid may contain cholesterol clefts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Adamantinomatous craniopharyngiomas are characterized by activating mutations in exon 3 of CTNNB1, the gene that encodes the WNT signalling pathway regulator β-catenin {12466115; 15221941; 15891929; 24413733; 26927026; 28500561; 28069929; 30074466; 32125720}, which lead to the overexpression of β-catenin targets such as AXIN2 and LEF1 {18540944}. Identification of CTNNB1 mutations can be variable, ranging from about 60–75% to nearly 100% in some series, depending on the sensitivity of sequencing methods and analytical approaches. CTNNB1 mutations are clonal driver events {24413733; 28414891; 32125720}, but nuclear localization of β-catenin is observed in only a fraction of tumour cells, mostly in epithelial whorls. Additional recurrent mutations have not been reported. Germline mutations in APC have rarely been identified, but only in combination with CTNNB1 mutations {32170310}. Consistent with the distinct histology and driver mutations of adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma and papillary craniopharyngioma, the tumours also display distinct methylation and transcription profiles {26927026}. Although CTNNB1 and BRAF mutations are mutually exclusive, coexistence of the mutations has been (rarely) reported {24715106}. Recurrent focal deletions of Xp28 have been described in a subset of samples from male patients, and other recurrent gains have also been described {28069929}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-differentiated squamous epithelial tumour; stellate reticulum and/or wet keratin foci.

Desirable: nuclear immunoreactivity for β-catenin; mutation in CTNNB1; absence of BRAF p.V600E mutations.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Overall mortality in craniopharyngioma is reported to be three to five times as high as that observed in the general population {15670196}. Overall survival (OS) rates reported in paediatric patients range from 83% to 96% at 5 years {11528558} and from 65% to 100% at 10 years {15077699; 20204458}; on average, the OS rate at 20 years is 62%. In mixed paediatric and adult patient cohorts, OS rates are 54–96% at 5 years {15670196; 15807869}, 40–93% at 10 years {15670196; 15807869; 16216361}, and 66–85% at 20 years {15670196; 16216361}. Increased late mortality without tumour progression results from metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, cerebrovascular and cardiovascular disease, severe infection, and adrenal insufficiency {20204458; 25375987}. A novel MRI-based grading system of presurgical hypothalamic involvement and surgical hypothalamic lesions shows that posterior hypothalamic involvement/lesions have a major negative impact on hypothalamic function and quality of life {25838139; 30925462; 31219033}.

The optimal treatment for craniopharyngiomas still remains controversial. The debate centres on the need for radical resection for surgical cure, versus limited resection plus adjuvant radiotherapy. Both therapies yield similar rates of disease control and OS {23910225}. Therefore, conservative surgical resection is of particular importance in cases with hypothalamic involvement {25838139; 26523617}. In contrast, gross total resection is possible when tumours are small and separate from the hypothalamus and optic pathway. Recent advances in the treatment of craniopharyngioma have focused on minimizing treatment-related toxicity. These advances include endoscopic surgery and precision radiotherapy including stereotactic radiotherapy and proton therapy {30363774}. Improved resection, decreased recurrence, and lower complication rates with the application of endoscopic endonasal approaches to paediatric craniopharyngioma are reported in the literature {31896083}.

Malignant transformation of craniopharyngioma is rare {17592268; 24978859; 28290422; 31785438}. Tumours with CTNNB1 T41 mutations or focal deletions of Xp28 may have a worse outcome {28069929}. In 1 patient, partial and durable disease control was achieved by MEK inhibition {33073916}, consistent with preclinical studies {29541918}. Distant intraventricular metastasis is extremely rare in childhood craniopharyngioma. A case of an isolated posterior ventricular recurrence of an adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma has been reported {27310220}.
