9.0.1.2 Lipoma

Definition

Lipoma is a benign neoplasm of mature adipocytes.

ICD-O coding

8850/0 Lipoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E80.0Z Lipoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lipomas may arise in the adrenal gland and pancreas {24678351; 11533079; 28143811; 31531331; 22229078; 2753287}. Pancreatic lipomas most commonly arise in the head, followed by the body and tail {26682506; 31531331}. Lipomas have not been described in the thyroid. The term “thyroid lipoadenoma” (thyrolipoma; adenolipoma) indicates a subtype of follicular adenoma containing a variable amount of mature adipose tissue (see section Follicular thyroid adenoma). Rare cases of diffuse fat infiltration throughout the thyroid parenchyma, i.e. thyrolipomatosis, have been also reported {19917474; 28588784}. For parathyroid lipoadenomas, see section Parathyroid lipoadenoma .

Clinical features

Most lipomas of the pancreas and adrenal gland are usually asymptomatic and incidentally discovered; a small subset of patients may report abdominal pain {11533079; 26682506}. Rare cases of pancreatic lipoma result in pancreatitis. CT shows a well-circumscribed, lobulated, purely fat-density mass.

Epidemiology

Adrenal lipomas are rare, accounting for < 1% of primary adrenal neoplasms {11533079}. The proportion of pancreatic lipomas is even rarer (~0.01%) {26682506}. Both sexes are affected equally, and these tumours typically present in the sixth and seventh decades of life.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most lipomas harbour translocations involving 12q13-q15, resulting in the fusion of HMGA2 with a variety of gene partners, driving the pathogenesis of these tumours {9530339}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gross examination reveals a circumscribed mass with a homogeneous yellow cut surface. Most adrenal lipomas measure 50–100 mm but may reach sizes as large as 200 mm, whereas the average size of pancreatic lipomas is approximately 20 mm {28143811; 26682506}.

Histopathology

Histological review shows a sheet-like proliferation of mature adipocytes without atypical hyperchromatic stromal cells. Fat necrosis and dystrophic calcification may be present.

Cytology

Aspiration specimens show adipocytes containing a single large lipid droplet with a compressed rim of cytoplasm and a flattened, inconspicuous nucleus.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FISH studies for MDM2 amplification may be helpful in excluding well-differentiated liposarcoma {17895748; 18500263; 26146760}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed yellow mass; a uniform proliferation of mature adipose tissue; no atypical hyperchromatic stromal cells.

Desirable: an absence of MDM2 amplification by FISH (in selected cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lipomas are benign. Surgical excision is generally reserved for symptomatic lesions.
