6.1.1.1 Adrenal rests

Definition

Adrenal rests represent isolated benign adrenocortical tissue outside orthotopic adrenal glands and may be associated with adrenomedullary (chromaffin) cells.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LC8Y & XH9RV1 Other specified structural developmental anomalies of the adrenal glands & Adrenal rest tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: adrenal ectopia; adrenal heterotopia; adrenal choristoma; adrenal remnants.

Not recommended: adrenal rest tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adrenal rests occur in anatomical locations that are aligned with their embryogenesis, and in unusual locations (choristomas) that cannot be explained by embryogenesis (see #24325Box 5.01).

Clinical features

Adrenal rests are generally incidental findings in organs resected for other diseases {34095993}. In the testis, they can be identified within specific screening for congenital adrenal hyperplasia {30882882; 29850650}, other endocrinological disorders {27901643}, or subfertility due to impaired rete testis blood flow and seminiferous tubule function {31745525; 33320831}.

Rests can become hyperplastic in the presence of Nelson syndrome {33091942; 7124773; 7072659}.

Tumours arising in adrenal rests may be endocrinologically functional {33434178}. Rarely, a mass as seen in adrenocortical rest tumours may be the presenting symptom. Imaging characteristics are dependent on adrenal rest location and size. Adrenal choristomas in the pituitary are seen in association with corticotroph pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs) {33586921; 23872913}. In the testis, key ultrasonographic features include bilateral lesions, well-circumscribed nodules, and a homogeneous hypoechoic texture {31694673; 26356704}. Precise radiological criteria for ovarian and other organ adrenal rests have not been developed {33433255}. Functional imaging studies (11C-metomidate PET-CT) may help localize adrenal rest tumours {33245336}.

Epidemiology

The exact prevalence of adrenal rests is not known, being reported in as many as 20% of autopsies {29873281; 30418186}. In congenital adrenal hyperplasia, testicular adrenal rests occur in 14–86% of cases depending on patient age, disease severity, and detection modalities {30882882; 22157069}.

Etiology

Most adrenal rests are explained with misplaced developmental tissues; however, the etiology is unknown in unusual locations.

Pathogenesis

Developmental defects {18279781; 26038200} in the delamination of the adrenogonadal primordia or failure in the encapsulation of SF1-expressing precursor cells of the fetal adrenal zone may be the source of embryogenesis-related adrenal rests. Differentiation of uncommitted mesenchymal stem cells in usual anatomical locations has also been suggested {23872913; 8605573}. A prolonged ACTH stimulation of pluripotent cells has also been suggested in testes {30882882; 25485724}. An association of adrenal rests with Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome remains to be clarified {17162526; 22309953; 433592}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most adrenal rests are not grossly evident, measuring only a few millimetres {28459169; 28930625; 26557532}. Adrenocortical tumours can arise in adrenal rests {30269470; 28258518; 32635927; 10792789; 18564144; 9234389; 23603421; 33434178; 21374072; 24750189}.

Histopathology

Adrenal rests appear as aggregates of large polygonal cells with eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm, a uniform round nucleus, and finely dispersed chromatin, resembling orthotopic adrenocortical cells {30882882; 29873281; 29850650; 34095993}. Adrenomedullary cells are rarely present. Cortical cells may be intermingled with other parenchymal cells or located in the fibroadipose tissue surrounding the organ. Like orthotopic cortical cells, SF1 and melan-A are positive in adrenal rests {18564144; 10792789; 34095993; 23872913}. Adrenal rest tumours may simulate steroid cell tumours, Leydig cell tumours, or renal cell carcinoma in some anatomical locations {30882882; 30418186; 29873281; 29850650; 25609776}. Adrenocortical-specific steroidogenic enzymes (CYP11B1, CYP11B2) may be helpful in the distinction of adrenal rest–related tumours {23603421; 25609776}.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clusters or nodules composed of large polygonal adrenal cortical cells with no sign of malignancy; may have associated adrenomedullary (chromaffin) cells; immunohistochemical confirmation of adrenocortical cell differentiation.

Desirable: SF1 positivity; adrenocortical steroidogenic enzyme positivity in the distinction of selected adrenal rest–related tumours.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most adrenal rests have no clinical significance; however, patients with congenital adrenal hyperplasia with poor hormonal control may present with large testicular adrenal rest–related tumours that can cause infertility {33524003}.
