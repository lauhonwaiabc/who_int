6.2.4 Weaver syndrome (EZH2)

Definition

Weaver syndrome is an epigenetic disorder caused by pathogenic variants in EZH2. In addition to key clinical features resulting from neurodevelopmental abnormalities, there is susceptibility to neuroblastoma and haematolymphoid malignancies.

MIM numbering

277590 Weaver syndrome

ICD-11 coding

LD2C Overgrowth syndromes

Related terminology

Acceptable: EZH2-related overgrowth.

Subtype(s)

Clinical subtypes of Weaver syndrome have not been specified, though EZH2-related overgrowth may be used as a category for patients who test positive for a pathogenic EZH2 variant but do not have the facial dysmorphisms or intellectual disability. A reciprocal undergrowth phenotype associated with gain-of-function protein variants in EZH2 is a distinct clinical and molecular entity with a distinct epigenetic signature, and should not be miscategorized as Weaver syndrome {32243864}.

Localization

Congenital anomalies of the brain including polymicrogyria have been described {26694085}. MRI may also be normal, frequently with macrocephaly as the only visible abnormality. Radiographic imaging of hands in childhood often reveals disharmonic bone growth with advanced bone age. Congenital heart disease is seen, albeit rarely {11241499;24214728}.

Clinical features

Developmental delays are notable in childhood, and intellectual disability is common among older children and adults. Tall stature in childhood normally is present and may be 4, 5, or more standard deviations above the mean {24214728}. Tall stature may persist into adulthood. Childhood tumour risk is largely attributable to neuroblastoma {19011474;23865096}, though haematological malignancies, such as acute lymphoblastic leukaemia {20101679}, acute myeloid leukaemia {26762561}, and non-Hodgkin lymphoma {24214728}, have been reported {19011474;23865096}. Other tumours (endodermal sinus tumour, sacrococcygeal teratoma) have been reported in cases diagnosed clinically {10507738;11146472}.

Epidemiology

Weaver syndrome is rare enough that the population prevalence is difficult to estimate. From a genetic epidemiology perspective, the pathogenic variants are typically de novo dominant, though sibling recurrence has been reported in families, typically from mildly affected or apparently unaffected parents. Sporadic inheritance (i.e. singleton cases without prior family history or affected siblings) is the inheritance pattern most commonly observed in practice, though a 1% sibling recurrence risk is typically quoted on the basis of the risk of gonadal mosaicism in one of the parents. Affected probands have a 50% risk of having affected offspring, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance of the mutant allele. Partially affected parents with mosaicism for a pathogenic / likely pathogenic EZH2 variant are thought to be at a risk of as much 50% of passing the variant to future offspring (more refined risk estimates are typically not possible).

Etiology

Weaver syndrome is caused by pathogenic variants in EZH2 at chromosome band 7q36.1 {22177091} (see #37459Table 7.07). These pathogenic variants impair the histone methyltransferase activity of PRC2 {26694085}, of which EZH2 forms part of the core.

Pathogenesis

When present at conception, pathogenic variants associated with partial loss of function in the EZH2 epigenetic writer cause Weaver syndrome. When present as a postzygotic de novo mutation in a sufficient proportion of somatic cells, these pathogenic variants cause a spectrum of clinical features, but may also be asymptomatic if present among sufficiently few tissue cells. Reduced function of the EZH2 component of the PRC2 impairs deposition of methyl tags on H3 p.Lys27me1 (H3K27me1) and H3 p.Lys27me3 (H3K27me3), which in turn affects the placement of methyl marks on nuclear DNA, leading to a characteristic epigenomewide profile or epiprofile {32243864}. This epiprofile is in turn thought to be associated with complex effects on the expression patterns of multiple genes that require the H3 p.Lys27me3 mark for appropriate suppression.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid tumours related to Weaver syndrome have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

See the descriptions of the individual tumour histology profiles. Theoretically, reduced immunostaining of H3 p.Lys27me3 would be considered supportive evidence of impaired PRC2 complex activity within an individual tumour.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In the absence of confirmatory DNA testing, the diagnosis of Weaver syndrome is often made on the basis of clinical expertise including generalized overgrowth and the facial gestalt. Peer-reviewed consensus diagnostic criteria have not yet been published, though the presence of a pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant in EZH2 would be considered diagnostic of EZH2-related overgrowth and/or Weaver syndrome, unless the patient’s clinical phenotype showed no significant matching to the clinical features described above (such as was the case in the patient with growth failure and an EZH2 protein variant with gain-of-function properties {32243864}).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant in EZH2, associated with tall stature or characteristic facial dysmorphism.

Desirable: disharmonic bone age in childhood or adolescence; in the presence of a variant of unknown significance in EZH2, confirmation that the variant is de novo, with exclusion of likely pathogenic and/or pathogenic variant genes known to cause syndromes with clinical overlap, such as NSD1, EED, and SUZ12.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

The presence or absence of pre-existing Weaver syndrome has not yet been incorporated into staging of patient-derived tumour samples.

Prognosis and prediction

Weaver syndrome is compatible with a normal or near-normal lifespan in the context of appropriate medical care. Mild to severe intellectual disability is present in as many as 83% of affected individuals.
