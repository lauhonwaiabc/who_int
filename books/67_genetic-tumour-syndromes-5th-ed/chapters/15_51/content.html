1.5 WNT/TGF-β signalling pathway

The WNT/TGF-β pathway is an essential regulator of differentiation in many cell types. It is often inactivated by somatic mutation in many sporadic tumours, but there are also many tumour predisposition syndromes which are caused by germline (constitutional) deactivation of the pathway. The key molecular players in the pathway seem to give rise to the most syndromes and others are being identified.

Many WNT/TGF-β germline genetic variants seem to result in gastrointestinal syndromes. Mutation of APC is well known as the cause of familial adenomatous polyposis and the allelic condition gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach (GAPPS). To that can be added germline pathogenic variants of AXIN2, which also result in colonic polyposis. RNF43 is a ring finger protein involved in the WNT pathway, variants of which give rise to a serrated polyposis of the colon. Cadherins are membrane proteins that are important at the start of the pathway, and mutation in CDH1 (encoding E-cadherin) is a cause of both diffuse gastric cancer and lobular breast cancer. CTNNA1 probably results in its effects through E-cadherin, owing to changes in dimerization, though that is still being researched. Increased GREM1 expression due to increased copy number has recently been found to be responsible for hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome, which disrupts the BMP pathway, reducing TGF-β pathway inhibition {25419707;29804199}. Within the nucleus, MEN1 is a nuclear scaffold protein that is active in regulating the WNT/TGF-β pathway, variants of which give rise to neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) as part of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1.
