Definition

Hyperparathyroidism–jaw tumour (HPT-JT) syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by pathogenic germline sequence variants or deletions in CDC73 (previously known as HRPT2). It is characterized predominantly by parathyroid adenoma and carcinoma, with a lower incidence of fibro-osseous jaw lesions.

MIM numbering

145001 Hyperparathyroidism–jaw tumour syndrome; HPT-JT

ICD-11 coding

5A51.0 Primary hyperparathyroidism

Related terminology

Acceptable: CDC73-related disorder.

Not recommended: familial cystic parathyroid adenomatosis; hyperparathyroidism 2 with jaw tumours.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In most kindreds, parathyroid tumours are the only feature {19529956;18436011;29324469}. Jaw tumours are relatively uncommon, being found in as few as 2.5% to 20% of known carriers of CDC73 pathogenic variants {25444225;19529956;18436011;23029104;18755853;29324469;33505612;32590342}. Clinically significant renal and uterine tumours occur at increased incidence but are rare {25444225;30452964;15606373;26450137;32590342}.

Clinical features

Primary hyperparathyroidism is the usual presentation of HPT-JT syndrome. Contrasting with other forms of hereditary hyperparathyroidism where multigland involvement is very common, a single adenoma or carcinoma is found in 60–80% of patients at presentation {32468209;31929790}. Metachronous tumours occur during long-term follow-up (decades) in the majority of patients in many series {18436011;2123361;25444225;19529956}. In addition to family history, clinical clues to the diagnosis of HPT-JT syndrome include young age of onset (particularly if < 40 years), very large tumours, cystic change, markedly elevated serum PTH levels, and severe hypercalcaemia {18755853;19529956;23293331;29324469;28674121}. Parathyroid carcinoma is extremely rare outside the setting of germline or somatic pathogenic CDC73 variants, whereas the lifetime risk of parathyroid carcinoma in HPT-JT syndrome may be as high as 15% {12434154;25444225;19529956;18436011;23293331;29324469}. Therefore, the possibility of HPT-JT syndrome should be considered in all patients with parathyroid carcinoma even in the absence of a family history {16931959;24402736;29324469}. In fact, as many as 30% of patients with apparently sporadic parathyroid carcinomas have pathogenic germline sequence variants or deletions in CDC73 indicating previously undiagnosed HPT-JT syndrome {15531515;14585940;29324469}. The jaw tumours arise more often in the mandible than the maxilla and about 15% are multiple {3856818;11294964;24678936;33505612}. They are commonly incidental findings on imaging, where they present as well-demarcated, usually radiolucent, and unilocular lesions {24678936;16458039}. They may present as an enlarging mass that interrupts dentition. Although clinically benign, they show a variable rate of growth and are usually slowly progressive. The incidence of pathogenic germline CDC73 variants in patients with clinically sporadic ossifying fibromas has not been extensively studied. It is probably very low given that even simply somatic CDC73 variants are only found in as many as 5% of patients {27658992}. Older reports suggested that a significant proportion of patients with HPT-JT syndrome develop renal pathology, predominantly simple cysts, but also adenomas, Wilms tumours, and mixed epithelial and stromal tumours {23293331;25444225;10770180;30452964}. However, simple cysts are not uncommon in the general population, and in practice clinically significant renal pathology is very rare, being absent from many large series {19529956;18436011;23029104;24716902;21652691;18755853;29324469}. Similarly, historically a significant proportion of female patients have been reported to have some form of uterine pathology {25444225;30452964}. These are usually lesions which are also very common in the general community – particularly benign leiomyomas, but also endometrial hyperplasias, polyps, adenomyosis, or adenofibromas, which may represent incidental findings in patients under surveillance {18755853;25444225;30452964}. However, a small number of uterine adenosarcomas (otherwise a rare pathology) have been reported {25444225}, and adenosarcomas also arise in mouse models of CDC73 mutation, suggesting a causal relationship and supporting an increased risk {28288139}.

Epidemiology

HPT-JT syndrome is a rare cause of hyperparathyroidism, and it has been estimated that only 0.19% of unselected parathyroid tumours are associated with pathogenic CDC73 variants {29324469}. In contrast to sporadic hyperparathyroidism, there is an equal sex distribution for both the parathyroid and jaw tumours {29324469;33505612}. The median age at presentation with either hyperparathyroidism or jaw tumours is the third to fourth decades of life (reported range 7–66 years for the parathyroid tumours, and 10–66 years for the jaw tumours) {33505612;23293331;25444225;21652691;29324469}. The estimated penetrance of primary hyperparathyroidism in non-index carriers increases from 8% at 25 years, to 53% at 50 years, and 75% at 70 years, while the penetrance of any manifestation is 11% at 25 years, 65% at 50 years, and 83% at 70 years {29040582}.

Etiology

HPT-JT syndrome is caused by heterozygous pathogenic germline variants or deletions in the CDC73 gene, which maps to 1q25-q31 and encodes the 531 amino acid protein parafibromin {7717405;24402736;12434154;16931959}.

Pathogenesis

CDC73 is ubiquitously expressed in most tissues and functions as a bona fide tumour suppressor gene. A somatic second hit is usually, but not always, able to be demonstrated in neoplasms arising in the setting of HPT-JT syndrome {12434154;24402736;16989776}. Parafibromin is a critical component of the polymerase-associated factor 1 complex, which binds to RNA polymerase II and controls transcription {11294964;25388829;29142233}. Parafibromin also represses expression of the proto-oncogenes CCND1 (encoding cyclin D1) and MYC {19906718;18987311}.

Macroscopic appearance

Compared with sporadic parathyroid tumours, parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumours are usually larger. In one study the median weight was 1.84 g (range 0.231–14 g) {29324469}. Like sporadic parathyroid carcinomas, carcinomas arising in the setting of HPT-JT syndrome may appear to be stuck down or adherent to other structures {29324469}.

Histopathology

Parathyroid tumours associated with biallelic CDC73 inactivation (with or without a germline variant) are termed “parafibromin deficient” {29324469}. There are morphological clues to a parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumour, including a sheet-like (rather than acinar) growth pattern, eosinophilic cytoplasm, perinuclear cytoplasmic clearing, nuclear enlargement occasionally with multinucleation, and a distinctive arborizing vasculature often with staghorn vessels {32468209;29324469}. Cystic change was originally described as a common feature {12434154}, but may be found in less than half of the parathyroid tumours {18436011;29324469}. Loss of immunohistochemical expression of parafibromin is usually seen in parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumours {24402736;29324469;16931959;19017757;30923346}. It is emphasized that for a tumour to be considered parafibromin deficient, there should be completely absent nuclear staining in all neoplastic cells, in the presence of preserved nuclear expression in non-neoplastic cells, which act as an internal positive control {16931959;29324469;30923346}. Interpreted in this way, loss of nuclear expression of parafibromin is highly specific but not completely sensitive for biallelic CDC73 inactivation. The significance of absent nucleolar expression of parafibromin with preserved expression elsewhere in the nucleus is uncertain, but some authors have suggested this pattern is also associated with CDC73 dysfunction {30903444;21221636;29626276;29324469;30923346}. Because some CDC73 variants may be associated with preserved parafibromin expression, positive staining for parafibromin in parathyroid neoplasms does not exclude the diagnosis of HPT-JT syndrome {19017757;16931959;21240254;25444225}. In most cases aberrant diffuse strong expression of PGP9.5 may serve as an adjunct to increase sensitivity, but on its own PGP9.5 overexpression lacks specificity for biallelic CDC73 inactivation {19017757;24402736;24081804}. Although biallelic somatic-only pathogenic variants do occur, as many as two thirds of all parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumours appear to be associated with pathogenic germline variants {29324469}. Therefore the identification of a parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumour is considered an indication for genetic testing at any age and irrespective of family history {29324469}.

The diagnosis of parathyroid carcinoma in HPT-JT syndrome is based on the standard criteria of unequivocal local invasion, vascular space invasion, and/or metastasis {32687943}. Given the known high risk of malignancy, it has been argued that a lower threshold for invasive growth should be applied in parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumours {24402736;29324469;24081804}. Although terminology is not uniform, the jaw tumours are usually classified as ossifying fibromas {24678936;16458039;33505612}. They are composed of a relatively avascular cellular fibroblast-rich stroma sometimes with a prominent storiform pattern admixed with bony trabeculae and/or cementum-like spherules. The histological appearance is quite different from that of osteitis fibrosa cystica (so-called brown tumours) associated with sporadic primary hyperparathyroidism, with which they may be confused clinically {24678936;16458039}. In contrast to the parathyroid tumours, parafibromin immunohistochemistry does not appear to be effective in identifying ossifying fibromas related to HPT-JT syndrome {24402736}. Being rare, the renal and uterine pathologies are less well described {23293331;25444225;10770180;19529956;18436011;23029104;24716902;21652691;18755853}. Interestingly, a kindred with HPT-JT syndrome with three family members with mixed epithelial and stromal tumour of the kidney was recently reported {30452964} and, together with uterine adenosarcoma {25444225;15606373}, these relatively rare histology profiles may be a clue to the diagnosis.

Cytology

As the parathyroid tumours are often very large, they occasionally undergo FNAB if mistaken clinically for thyroid lesions. In this situation, immunohistochemistry for PTH may be helpful to confirm parathyroid origin if a cell block preparation is available {35642308}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no true hotspots in CDC73, although variants are more common in some regions {20052758;23489357;23293331;29324469}. The majority of coding alterations are frameshift, nonsense, and splice-site variants {11294964;24340015;25959515;23293331}. As many as 35% of germline variants in CDC73 are large-scale deletions and may not be detected unless specifically sought with multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification or other dedicated studies {23293331;29324469}. Unidentified deletions, or other variants in the promoter, untranslated regions, or introns may account for the small proportion of otherwise typical HPT-JT cases in which no variants are found {20052758;20026646}. Recently it has been suggested that high-impact pathogenic variants in CDC73 (defined as those that cause conformational disruption or loss of expression and always impact the C-terminal domain of parafibromin) are associated with a significantly higher risk of parathyroid carcinoma and jaw tumours, despite a similar risk of hyperparathyroidism to low-impact variants {32590342}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of a germline pathogenic variant in CDC73; OR the presence of multiple histologically confirmed parafibromin-deficient neoplasms in an individual or kindred.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Lifelong surveillance is indicated for all patients with HPT-JT syndrome. This is primarily because of the risk of parathyroid adenoma and carcinoma {29324469} but also because of the risk of jaw lesions and much lower risk of gynaecological or renal pathology. The jaw lesions are benign but may be locally infiltrative and recur {3856818;11294964}. The optimal surgical approach to patients with hyperparathyroidism related to HPT-JT syndrome is debated and it is unclear whether to offer subtotal parathyroidectomy {25444225;18436011} or selective parathyroidectomy when there is clear localization to one gland {19529956;31929790}. The majority of non-infiltrative parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumours/adenomas are cured by surgery. Parafibromin-deficient parathyroid carcinomas may be at increased risk of recurrence and metastasis compared with carcinomas not related to CDC73 variants {16931959;24402736;24081804;29324469}. While it is a very rare event, it is worth noting that there are isolated case reports of unequivocally histologically benign parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumours which have metastasized {18436011;21899683;24402736;24081804;29324469}. However, the incidence of metastasis by an otherwise benign parafibromin-deficient parathyroid tumour is very low – estimated to be significantly less than 4% {29324469} – and far exceeded by the risk of a new primary tumour in a different gland during long-term follow-up {19529956;25444225;29324469;18436011;19169472}.
