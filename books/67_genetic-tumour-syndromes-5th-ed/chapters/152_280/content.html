1.5.2 Gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of stomach (APC promoter)

Definition

Gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach (GAPPS) is an autosomal dominant gastric cancer predisposition syndrome characterized by an increased risk of gastric (but not colorectal) adenocarcinoma, together with proximal polyposis of the stomach, which is caused by variants affecting promoter 1B of APC. As such, GAPPS is related to familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), but it has a unique phenotype.

Localization

The body and fundus of the stomach are involved by mucosal carpeting by polyposis, with sparing of the antrum and relative sparing of the lesser curve. There is no involvement of the duodenum, small intestine, or large bowel.

Clinical features

The age of onset among individuals with GAPPS is highly variable and is probably influenced by multiple factors, including genetics, environment, and lifestyle habits. The youngest patients diagnosed with GAPPS were 8 and 10 years old {21813476;34581903}. Patients can present with nonspecific gastrointestinal symptoms such as abdominal pain, dyspepsia, and melaena. Gastric polyposis may be asymptomatic, and such asymptomatic patients may be detected by screening endoscopy because they belong to a known GAPPS family. Patients with gastric adenocarcinoma may present with symptoms and signs of gastric malignancy {29968043;31409086;34554346}. Endoscopy reveals a characteristic mucosal carpeting by large numbers of polyps (polyposis), with the polyps generally < 10 mm in diameter {21813476}.

Epidemiology

This is an autosomal dominant syndrome with incomplete penetrance. Families have been identified in Australia, North America, Europe, and Japan {21813476;22027476;27087319;27343414;29141268;29112017;31409086}.

Etiology

GAPPS is caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants affecting the YY1 binding site of APC promoter 1B. Such variants also occur very rarely in families with FAP {27087319}. Helicobacter pylori is usually absent in patients with GAPPS {33975682}.

Pathogenesis

The single-base variants that cause GAPPS (NM_001127511.3:c.-191T>C, NM_001127511.3:c.-192A>G, and NM_001127511.3:c.-195A>C) are all positioned within the YY1 binding motif of APC promoter 1B, reducing the transcriptional levels of APC RNA expression {27087319}.

Macroscopic appearance

There are typically numerous polyps carpeting the mucosa of the gastric fundus and body. An arbitrary number of > 100 polyps has been proposed as a diagnostic criterion, although some family members may have fewer polyps {21813476;22027476;31409086}. Polyps are predominantly < 10 mm {29112017;21813476} and are sessile with a smooth surface. In some cases, larger, dominant polyps as large as 40 mm may be present {29112017;27343414}, or there may be a malignant tumour {29968043;31409086;34554346}.

Histopathology

A range of microscopic features has been described in the literature, including fundic gland polyps (FGPs), fundic gland–like polyps, hyperproliferative aberrant pits, hyperplastic polyps, gastric-type adenomas, and adenocarcinomas (tubular/intestinal and mixed with a poorly cohesive component). Some lesions show a mixture of the above features. The larger, dominant polyps tend to show foci of dysplasia or are predominantly adenomas {27343414;29112017}.

FGPs: These are described in all published reports, encompassing typical FGPs as well as dysplastic FGPs and fundic gland–like polyps {29112017;21813476;22027476}, which have atypical features, such as the absence of typical cystically dilated glands lined by a mixture of mucinous and oxyntic cells and an expanded or inverted foveolar compartment {29112017}.

Hyperproliferative aberrant pits: These polypoid lesions typically show disorganized proliferation of specialized/oxyntic glands high up in the mucosa, involving the attenuated foveolar region around the gastric pits, with proliferative activity highlighted by Ki-67 immunoreactivity. Hyperproliferative aberrant pits appear to be the most frequent early pathological findings {29112017}, and there is a morphological continuum of these lesions progressing to dysplasia/adenomas {29112017}.

Hyperplastic polyps: Occasional hyperplastic polyps have been described {21813476}. There may also be elongation, irregularity, dilatation, and branching of the foveolar compartment, imparting an appearance of inverted foveolar hyperplasia {29112017}.

Preinvasive neoplastic lesions: Some of the FGPs are inherently neoplastic, showing focal dysplasia. There may also be discrete adenomas, which are often multifocal {29112017;21813476;27343414;29141268}. The dysplastic epithelium shows eosinophilic cytoplasm with mostly basally located, enlarged oval to round nuclei; conspicuous to prominent nucleoli; and an apical mucin cap. These are similar to the changes described in gastric foveolar dysplasia/adenomas. Both low-grade and high-grade dysplastic changes are reported. High-grade dysplasia is characterized by advanced architectural complexity and crowding coupled with high-grade nuclear features comprising loss of polarity, round to oval vesicular nuclei, and prominent nucleoli as focal change in a background of low-grade dysplasia.

Adenocarcinomas: All adenocarcinomas described to date have been of tubular (per the WHO classification) / intestinal (per the Laurén classification) or mixed type.

Immunohistochemistry

Dysplastic lesions in the GAPPS setting are of a gastric foveolar phenotype (with immunoreactivity for MUC5AC) {29112017;10980114;24548295;24525509}. The immunophenotype of adenocarcinoma is described in two reports. One describes expression of MUC5AC, CK7, CDX2 (very focal, strong), and CD10 (moderate) in the absence of CK20, MUC2, and MUC6 {29112017}. The other report (of the analysis of different markers) describes increased expression of nuclear β-catenin, Ki-67, and p53 {27406052}.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis is fundic gland polyposis in the setting of FAP (classic and attenuated forms). The unique sparing of the antrum in patients with GAPPS, and the absence of colonic polyposis, is an important clinical feature that distinguishes GAPPS from (attenuated) FAP {35092716}. Prolonged use of proton-pump inhibitors may be the cause of FGPs/polyposis without dysplasia and should be considered in the differential diagnosis {27634363}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A second APC hit occurs in most FGPs in GAPPS by loss of the wildtype APC allele or frameshift variants in the APC gene, but this appears to be a late event, occurring in only a subset of the polyp’s epithelial cells.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

The clinical criteria for genetic testing are presented in #38027Box 2.05. See also #39034Table 2.23.

Staging

Adenocarcinomas developed in the setting of GAPPS should be staged using the latest editions of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification and the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) cancer staging manual.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis is often poor in patients with gastric adenocarcinoma {29968043;31409086;34554346}.

Essential criteria:

1. Phenotypic features:
 

Proximal (body and fundus) gastric polyposis with antral sparing; no evidence of colorectal or duodenal polyposisa

> 100 polyps carpeting the proximal stomach in the index patient or > 30 polyps in someone who has a first-degree relative with GAPPS

Predominantly fundic gland polyps and/or fundic gland–like polyps

2. Proband or family member with either dysplastic fundic gland polyps or gastric adenocarcinoma

3. Mutation in the chr5:112043220_112043224 region of promoter 1B of APCb,c

Desirable criteria (families in whom genetic testing could be considered):

1. Family history (autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance)

2. Spectrum of other histological lesions: hyperproliferative aberrant pits, hyperplastic polyps, gastric-type adenomas
