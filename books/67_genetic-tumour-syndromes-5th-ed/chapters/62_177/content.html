6.2 Chromatin remodelling pathway

Chromatin structure needs to be regulated to allow proteins involved in transcription access to condensed genomic DNA. This involves a number of processes that modify histones and chromatin remodelling complexes that alter nucleosomes. The SWI/SNF complex is altered in many cancers, and its constituents, encoded by SMARC family genes, are responsible for three of the four syndromes included in this family of disorders: rhabdoid tumour predisposition syndrome, schwannomatosis, and clear cell meningioma predisposition syndrome.

The final syndrome under this heading is Weaver syndrome, resulting from variants in EZH2, a key component of PRC2, thus impairing histone methyltransferase activity and making affected children susceptible to a range of tumours, particularly neuroblastoma and haematolymphoid neoplasms.
