2.2.2 Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma syndrome (FH)

Definition

Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma (HLRCC) syndrome is an autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndrome associated with germline FH pathogenic variants. It is characterized by cutaneous and uterine leiomyomas deficient in fumarate hydratase (FH), FH-deficient renal cell carcinoma (RCC), and (occasionally) phaeochromocytoma and/or paraganglioma (PPGL).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Common: skin, uterus, kidney

Rare: adrenal gland, paraganglia

Very rare: testes, ovary

Clinical features

Cutaneous leiomyomas, which may be sensitive to cold or light touch, are found in at least 75% of individuals with HLRCC syndrome, with a mean age of onset of 30 years, and an increase in size and number with age {20618355}. As many as 80% of women with HLRCC syndrome develop symptomatic uterine leiomyomas, with many undergoing surgery at a young age (< 35 years) {12772087;20618355;25985877;28300276;26574848}. About 15% of patients with HLRCC syndrome develop renal carcinomas with a median age of onset of 44 years {20301430;25012257;12772087}. Phaeochromocytoma and paraganglioma are recognized associations but are relatively uncommon {35993574}.

Epidemiology

HLRCC syndrome is rare, with no data on the exact prevalence. The estimated population incidence of pathogenic germline FH variants is 0.0308–0.0390% {32612247}. Tumours associated with germline FH variants occur at a younger age than their sporadic counterparts. The leiomyomas are more penetrant and on average arise 10–15 years before the renal carcinomas {12772087}. The majority of patients with even a single FH-deficient RCC at any age have identifiable germline variants {26900816;31524643}, as do close to 90% of patients with multiple cutaneous leiomyomas {15724016}. In contrast, it is thought that only 2.7–13.9% of patients with a single FH-deficient uterine leiomyoma carry pathogenic germline variants {32612247}. Although the great majority of patients undergoing surgery for FH-deficient uterine leiomyomas at a younger age have syndromic disease {25985877}, the majority of patients aged > 50 years who present with a single FH-deficient uterine leiomyoma do not {26574848;32612247}. Approximately 1% of patients with PPGL have germline FH variants {29239044;28300276;35993574}. There have been isolated case reports of patients with testicular and ovarian Leydig cell tumours {16757530;31792767;31271241}.

Etiology

HLRCC syndrome is caused by germline pathogenic variants in the FH gene (chromosome 1q43) {11283798;21398687;23211287}. More than 100 different pathogenic FH variants and large-scale deletions have been reported {15937070;21398687;33063682}. It has been suggested that FH deletions, or pathogenic variants that disrupt protein folding, may be more frequently associated with RCC and leiomyoma, whereas missense variants predominantly cause PPGL {35993574}.

Pathogenesis

Biallelic inactivation of FH, the gene encoding a Krebs cycle enzyme that converts fumarate to L-maleate, occurs in the syndromic setting owing to germline variants with the addition of a somatic second hit. The subsequent accumulation of fumarate and other oncometabolites leads to neoplasia through epigenetic modification and suppression of DNA repair {32494005;21398687;27580029}.

Macroscopic appearance

The renal carcinomas and uterine leiomyomas associated with HLRCC syndrome grossly resemble their sporadic counterparts. The cutaneous leiomyomas are more common on the trunk, and they may be single, clustered, or in a linear, segmental, or dermatome-like distribution {15724016}.

Histopathology

The renal carcinomas associated with HLRCC syndrome are now classified as a distinct entity termed FH-deficient RCC {23211287;26574848}. Most FH-deficient uterine leiomyomas show distinctive morphological features at least focally, including symplastic-type nuclear atypia, multinucleation, prominent eosinophilic nucleoli, a staghorn vasculature, stromal oedema, and occasionally eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions {27454940;26574848;24309325;23211287;26457356}. However, some FH-deficient uterine leiomyomas and the majority of FH-deficient cutaneous leiomyomas cannot be distinguished morphologically from usual leiomyomas {26574848;28288038;26945337}. FH-deficient PPGL {32686200;29079178;35993574} and Leydig cell tumours {31792767;16757530} resemble their sporadic counterparts.

Immunohistochemistry

Negative immunohistochemical staining for FH in the presence of a positive control (i.e. retained expression) in non-neoplastic cells is highly specific for all FH-deficient neoplasms, but is imperfectly sensitive. In contrast, aberrant positive staining for 2-succinocysteine (2SC) is highly sensitive for all FH-deficient neoplasms. It is less specific for renal carcinoma {24441663;26900816;31524643;27635946;29410489;28664937} but appears to maintain specificity for leiomyoma and PPGL {35993574}. Because the morphological clues may be subtle or absent, a low threshold for immunohistochemistry screening for FH and/or 2SC is helpful in any difficult-to-classify renal carcinoma {28165631;28898443;27635946;26900816;31524643} and in a solitary cutaneous leiomyoma {28288038;26945337}. Genetic testing is recommended in any patient with multiple cutaneous leiomyomas regardless of the immunohistochemical pattern. In view of the relative frequency of uterine leiomyoma resection specimens, and the much lower incidence of germline pathogenic variants in older patients with a single FH-deficient uterine leiomyoma, it is recommended that morphology and clinical features are used to triage immunohistochemistry for FH and 2SC in uterine leiomyomas {33099845;26574848;26457356;30741757}.

Cytology

In cytological specimens, the presence of prominent inclusion-like nucleoli, sometimes with a perinucleolar halo, represents a clue to the associated renal carcinoma {30339328}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Although FH and 2SC immunohistochemistry identify FH-deficient neoplasms, they do not replace genetic testing. Biallelic FH inactivation can occur as a purely somatic phenomenon (rarely in renal carcinoma and PPGL, but commonly in uterine leiomyoma, particularly at an older age). Immunohistochemistry may also be useful to assess the pathogenicity of genetic variants of uncertain significance {35993574}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of a germline pathogenic variant in FH; or the presence of multiple histologically confirmed FH-deficient leiomyomas or FH-deficient RCCs; or the presence of two or more of the following: FH-deficient leiomyoma, FH-deficient RCC, a first-degree relative with FH-deficient neoplasms {20618355}.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

FH-deficient RCCs arising in HLRCC syndrome are aggressive and active surveillance is not recommended {25018647;17895761;12772087;26900816}. Although the presence of symplastic cytological atypia and multifocality may lead to misclassification of the cutaneous and uterine smooth muscle tumours as leiomyosarcoma, no clinical evidence exists that such leiomyomas behave in a biologically malignant manner {26574848;27454940;16477632;28300276}.
