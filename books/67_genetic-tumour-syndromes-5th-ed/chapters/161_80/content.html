1.6.1 Naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (Gorlin syndrome) (PTCH1, SUFU, GPR161)

Definition

Naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (NBCCS) is an autosomal dominant disease associated with developmental disorders and predisposition to benign and malignant tumours, including cutaneous basal cell carcinomas, odontogenic keratocysts, palmar/plantar dyskeratotic pits, intratarsal keratinous eyelid cysts, intracranial calcifications, macrocephaly, and medulloblastomas (desmoplastic/nodular subtypes). NBCCS is caused by germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) of genes encoding members of the hedgehog signalling pathway, including PTCH1, SUFU, and exceptionally GPR161.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Sites of disease include the following:

  • skin – basal cell carcinomas (BCCs), sebaceous cysts / facial milia, palmoplantar pits, epidermal inclusion cysts, multiple naevi {35333541};
  • jaw – odontogenic keratocysts (OKCs), cleft palate/lip {26297395};
  • bone – congenital anomalies (bifid or splayed ribs, frontal bossing, hypertelorism, scoliosis, flame-shaped lucencies in hands/feet, digit syndactyly, long bone cysts, mandibular prognathism);
  • CNS – macrocephaly, medulloblastoma, meningioma, craniopharyngioma, and calcification of the falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli, and/or sella turcica {12879481}.

Other lesions are intratarsal eyelid cysts, fibromas (ovarian and cardiac {35735206}), and mesenteric cystic lymphangioma {17221269}.

Clinical features

The most frequent manifestations of NBCCS are multiple BCCs (sun-exposed and non-exposed skin), as well as jaw OKCs, found together in > 90% of affected individuals by the age of 40 years {8326488}. OKCs most commonly occur in the posterior mandibular ramus, and may be multifocal when syndrome associated {1065842;21159911}. The overall prevalence of OKCs in NBCCS is 66–86% {8326488;8042673;9096761;22246785}, but OKCs have an age-dependent onset and by the age of 40 the prevalence is reported to be > 90% {8326488}. Cysts commonly arise relatively early with a mean patient age of 15.5 years {8042673} but they may also occur much later in life {8326488}. Other frequent manifestations include calcification of the falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli, and/or sella turcica; palmar and plantar pits; and bifid or fused ribs {9096761;8042673}. These findings are considered major diagnostic criteria (#37877Box 2.09). Minor criteria include medulloblastoma {12879481}, ovarian fibroma, macrocephaly, congenital facial abnormalities (e.g. cleft lip or palate, frontal bossing, and hypertelorism), skeletal abnormalities (e.g. digit syndactyly), and radiological bone abnormalities (e.g. bridging of the sella turcica, flame cysts in the digits, long bone cysts) {12879481;9096761}. The clinical features manifest at different ages: macrocephaly and rib anomalies can be detected at birth; medulloblastoma typically develops within the first 3 years of life; OKCs usually present after 8 years of age; and BCCs are usually found after 10 years of age {25131638}. Radiation treatment of medulloblastoma may induce multiple BCCs as well as various other tumour types (e.g. meningioma) within the radiation field {6837723;9010431;11878577}.

NBCCS-associated medulloblastoma usually develops in children < 5 years of age, with the majority identified in patients < 2 years {12879481}, and nearly always showing desmoplastic/nodular histopathology {12879481;19276247;7856756;25403219}. As such, it is proposed that desmoplastic/nodular medulloblastoma in young children should serve as a major criterion for the diagnosis of NBCCS {12879481;19276247}. About 1–2% of patients with NBCCS with germline PTCH1 pathogenic sequence variants develop medulloblastoma, compared with about 20% in patients with germline SUFU pathogenic sequence variants {25403219}.

Epidemiology

Disease prevalence varies by reporting country: United Kingdom 1:30 827 {20082463}; Australia 1:164 000 {8042673}; Japan 1:235 800 {25131638}; and Italy 1:256 000 {30129523}. Of 131 children with medulloblastomas, 6% had GPVs in SUFU in one study {22508808}.

Etiology

NBCCS results from inactivating GPVs in the human homologue of the Drosophila segment polarity patched gene (PTCH1) located at chromosome 9q22 {8681379;8658145}, SUFU at 10q24 {25403219}, and exceptionally GPR161 at 1q24.2 {31609649}. Numerous different GPVs in PTCH1 associated with NBCCS have been reported {25131638;16419085}. The detection rate of specific pathogenic variants has increased significantly in recent years, owing to the development of improved methods {25131638}. The variants are distributed over the entire PTCH1 coding region, with no mutation hotspots {8981943}. Missense variants cluster in a highly conserved region (the sterol-sensing domain), and particularly in transmembrane domain 4. The rate of new PTCH1 GPVs has not been precisely determined, but it is estimated that a high proportion (14–81%) of cases are the result of de novo alterations {8326488;3547011;8042673;9415689}. GPVs in SUFU are only detected in approximately 5–6% of tested probands and are mostly frameshift or nonsense variants {25403219}, with most cases inherited from an unaffected parent, with the remaining cases showing a de novo variant {25403219}. GPVs in GPR161 have been detected in SHH subgroup medulloblastomas, suggesting possible NBCCS association {31609649}.

Pathogenesis

PTCH1 encodes a protein with 12 transmembrane domains, expressed on many progenitor cell types, that functions as a receptor for members of the secreted hedgehog protein family of signalling molecules (SHH, IHH, and DHH) and acts through downstream factors to repress transcription of genes encoding members of pathways active in poorly differentiated cells, such as TGF-β and WNT {8906794;8906787}. PTCH1 controls another transmembrane protein, SMO {9278137;8906787}. In the absence of a ligand, PTCH1 inhibits the activity of SMO {9278137;8906787}. Hedgehog signalling takes place in the primary cilium {25172843}. Binding of hedgehog proteins to PTCH1 can relieve its inhibition of SMO, allowing its translocation to the tip of the primary cilium, which results in the activation and translocation of GLI transcription factors into the cell nucleus and transcription of a set of specific target genes controlling the survival, differentiation, and proliferation of progenitor cells. In vertebrates, this pathway is critically involved in the development of various tissues and organ systems, such as limbs, gonads, bone, and the CNS {9883719;9529611}. SUFU is located downstream in the hedgehog pathway. SUFU has been found to directly interact with GLI proteins and is a negative regulator of hedgehog signalling {10564661}. The PTCH and SUFU genes represent classic tumour suppressor genes; the second allele of the mutant gene is lost in most NBCCS-related tumours. The inactivation of the PTCH or SUFU genes leads to pathological activation of the SHH signalling pathway.

Macroscopic appearance

Syndromic tumours appear grossly similar to their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Practically all medulloblastomas associated with NBCCS have the morphological features of nodular tumours, including desmoplastic/nodular medulloblastoma and medulloblastoma with extensive nodularity {7856756;12879481;19276247;22508808;25403219}. BCCs do not show any unique histological features, with all subtypes represented, including pigmented subtypes. Infundibulocystic differentiation in BCCs is frequent in NBCCS {33063358}. NBCCS-associated OKCs are typically multifocal and develop in younger patients than in cases not associated with the syndrome {1065842;21159911;2442330}. The thin epithelium typically lacks rete ridges and shows a surface with corrugated parakeratin, while the basal cells are palisaded and have hyperchromatic nuclei. Basal budding and microcysts in the wall are more likely in syndromic cysts {2441019}.

Cytology

The cytology of NBCCS tumours does not differ from that of their sporadic counterparts.

Evaluation of the presence of medulloblastoma cells in cerebrospinal fluid is required for staging.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A comprehensive variant analysis of the PTCH1 and SUFU genes by DNA sequencing and a search for deletions/duplications by the appropriate methods (e.g. multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification, targeted array-based analysis) identifies most NBCCS-related GPVs {20301330}. To detect more-complex alterations, additional analysis at the transcript level may be necessary in some cases. Despite improved detection rates {25131638}, in 15–27% of index cases, neither PTCH1 nor SUFU pathogenic variants can be identified {28596197}. Because GPVs are frequent in children < 5 years of age with extensive nodular or desmoplastic/nodular medulloblastomas, human genetic counselling should be offered to the families and early pathogenic variant analysis of PTCH1 and SUFU performed. The detection of a constitutional condition is important for appropriate treatment planning to avoid radiation therapy {19276247;22508808;25403219;29186568;29753700}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #37877Box 2.09 and #39036Table 2.34.

Staging

Malignancies are staged in accordance with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

The life expectancy of NBCCS patients at 73.4 years is only slightly reduced from unaffected populations when compared with other genetic tumour syndromes; however, there is considerable impact on quality of life {22362873}. Surveillance guidelines are well established {28620006;20301330;33860896}. For SUFU GPV carriers, these include a recommendation for repeated brain MRI screening for medulloblastoma development within the first 5 years of life {28620006}.

The prognosis for NBCCS-associated medulloblastomas seems to be better than that of sporadic cases, and it has been suggested that therapy protocols be adjusted in patients aged < 5 years with NBCCS, to prevent radiation-induced secondary tumours {12879481;11878577}. Still, medulloblastoma patients with GPVs in SUFU have a worse prognosis {29186568}, although no difference in survival has been found between patients with medulloblastoma carrying a PTCH1 versus a SUFU GPV in other studies {29753700}. The cumulative BCC burden (> 100 BCCs in about a third of children) requires careful treatment optimization of methods and timing of tumour removal to achieve a cosmetic result and the best quality-of-life outcomes {34816134;34375441}. Statistical modelling suggests earlier syndrome diagnosis if certain cardinal features (i.e. OKCs, BCCs, palmoplantar pits) are identified and result in genetic testing {34611197}.
