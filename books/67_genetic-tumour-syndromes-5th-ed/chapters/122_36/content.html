Definition

McCune–Albright syndrome (MAS) is a tumour disorder caused by somatic mosaic activating GNAS mutations and characterized by wide clinical variability and a broad spectrum of endocrine and non-endocrine manifestations, including the classic triad of fibrous dysplasia (FD), precocious puberty, and skin hyperpigmentation.

MIM numbering

174800 McCune–Albright syndrome; MAS

ICD-11 coding

FB80.0 Fibrous dysplasia of bone

Related terminology

Not recommended: Albright syndrome; fibrous dysplasia (polyostotic and monostotic); Mazabraud syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Disease localization is probably determined by the developmental timing of the mutation, with earlier mutations resulting in more-widespread disease {3720010}. MAS may involve any portion of the skin and/or skeleton. Skin lesions have irregular borders and are typically located along the midline of the body but may extend to involve the limbs. Endocrine manifestations involve the gonads, thyroid, pituitary, and adrenals. Involvement in any of these areas may occur in isolation or in combination with other disease features. Several non-endocrine neoplasms and/or abnormalities may occur in the upper gastrointestinal tract and pancreas {28188442;34143711}.

Clinical features

The clinical features of MAS are variable, depending on the distribution of variant-bearing cells and the importance of adenylyl cyclase and its second messenger cAMP in affected tissues. However, the classic clinical triad of FD, precocious puberty, and skin hyperpigmentation with irregular borders (also known as café-au-lait macules, but this terminology is not recommended) is characteristic of the syndrome {31196103}. In general, most manifestations start during infancy and childhood {17501668;22640971}. FD can be monostotic or polyostotic, and it presents with fractures, deformities, and pain {25719192}. Craniofacial FD may uncommonly lead to neurological complications and functional deficits such as vision and hearing loss {17143235;29192304;29924878}. Hyperfunctioning endocrinopathies include precocious puberty (more common in female patients), primary hyperthyroidism, growth hormone excess, hyperprolactinaemia, and hypercortisolism {22640971}. Renal phosphate wasting due to excess FGF23 production by FD cells may result in rickets/osteomalacia and bone pain {12952917}. Gastrointestinal and pancreatic involvement may manifest with pancreatic intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms {30124968}, gastric polyps or heterotopia {28188442}, and hepatobiliary lesions {24170100}. The association of FD with intramuscular myxomas has previously been termed Mazabraud syndrome {30653046}; however, myxomas are more accurately denoted as an additional feature of MAS.

Imaging

Plain radiograph reveals an expansile, lytic lesion (geographical) with a characteristic ground-glass appearance, as a result of defective mineralization, associated with cortical thinning. The lesion(s) can be completely radiolucent, sclerotic, or mixed radiolucent and sclerotic. These often display well-circumscribed margins, with or without a sclerotic border. The larger lesions can be associated with a shell of variable thickness, small perforations, and/or endosteal scalloping {15149980}. There can be associated deformities in the extremities (shepherd’s crook deformity / coxa vara of the proximal femur) and/or in the skull and craniofacial bones.

Epidemiology

MAS is rare, and the prevalence has been estimated to be 1 case per 100 000 to 1 million people. All racial and ethnic groups are affected equally. There is probably no sex predilection {25719192}.

Etiology

MAS/FD results from postzygotic somatic activating (gain-of-function) mutations in Gsα, one of several transcripts encoded by GNAS at 20q13.32 {1944469}.

Pathogenesis

MAS develops from postzygotic gain-of-function mutations in the GNAS gene, which encodes the α subunit of the Gs signalling protein {1944469}. GNAS is a complex locus with an imprinted expression pattern. Somatic GNAS mutations in MAS/FD occur at one of two residues: NM_000516.7:c.601C>T p.(Arg201Cys), accounting for > 95% of the mutations {15126527}; and NM_000516.7:c.680A>G p.(Gln227Arg), accounting for < 5% {17493233}.

The major product of GNAS is Gsα, which is generated by the most downstream promoter (exon 1). Gsα is the ubiquitously expressed heterotrimeric G protein alpha subunit, which couples seven transmembrane receptors to the cAMP-generating enzyme adenylyl cyclase and is required for the intracellular cAMP response to hormones and other extracellular signals. The GNAS variants at NM_000516.7:c.601C>T p.(Arg201Cys) and NM_000516.7:c.680A>G p.(Gln227Arg) disrupt Gsα’s intrinsic GTPase activity, causing constitutive activation and abnormal cAMP signalling {2549426}.

There are no known genotype–phenotype correlations {33512531}. Clinical presentation and disease severity are probably determined by the degree of mosaicism and the location and extent of affected tissues.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid tumours related to MAS have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

FD lesions are composed of proliferating fibroblast-like bone marrow stromal cells, which replace normal bone and marrow. Osteoid trabeculae are present throughout the lesions and are discontinuous and non-functional in their orientation {10365102}. Trabeculae are frequently composed of poorly mineralized woven bone {10365102}.

Follicular cysts lined by granulosa cells are frequently identified in girls and women with ovarian involvement {16789624;171361}. In the testes, large foci of Leydig cell hyperplasia that cannot be definitively distinguished from Leydig cell tumours are commonly found; however, malignancy is very uncommon {22745241}.

MAS-related pituitary disease is heterogeneous and manifests with a hyperplasia-to-neoplasia progression sequence and multicentric microtumours {22564667} of PIT1 lineage, particularly somatotrophs and mammosomatotrophs {26177314;22564667;26874696;6426154;24517150}. Thyroid findings commonly include follicular adenomas with papillary growth {9226216}, and rare cases of thyroid carcinoma have been reported {12970318}. Adrenal disease is characterized by unilateral or bilateral nodular adrenocortical disease and neoplasms with areas of atrophic cortex in the setting of adrenal Cushing syndrome {21836496;33534120;35288842}.

The pancreatic lesions that can be found in patients with MAS are typically represented by intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms, which can show both low-grade and high-grade dysplasia. Usually, these lesions show an intestinal phenotype {28188442}. Intestinal intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms arising in the context of MAS can also progress to colloid carcinoma {24898823;31852070}. Gastric heterotopia and several types of polyps may occur in the upper gastrointestinal tract {28188442}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology should include documentation of a somatic activating mutation in GNAS.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: any two or more of the three clinical features listed in #38982Box 2.02; OR monostotic FD with a demonstration of GNAS mutation in the patient.

Desirable: demonstration of GNAS mutation in addition to at least two of the three clinical criteria listed in #38982Box 2.02.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Individuals with MAS have a variable prognosis depending on disease location and severity. Endocrinopathies persist throughout childhood and adulthood, with the exception of FGF23-mediated hypophosphataemia, which may worsen during periods of rapid linear growth and ameliorate in adulthood {23532406}. Also, neonatal hypercortisolism often undergoes spontaneous remission among patients who survive the acute phase {31037426}.

Endocrinopathies can generally be managed through multimodal therapies {31196103;25719192}, and optimizing endocrinopathies may improve skeletal outcomes in patients with FD {15005844;29669167;29924878}. Given the heterogeneous nature of MAS-related pituitary disease, total hypophysectomy is required for surgical cure {11081212;22564667;28007843}. GNAS mutations in MAS are weakly oncogenic, and affected tissues probably carry a small increased risk of malignant transformation. FD rarely undergoes sarcomatous transformation {8111708;13775207}; the risk may be increased by radiotherapy and uncontrolled growth hormone excess {2754454}. Women with MAS are at increased risk of breast cancer {28856726}.
