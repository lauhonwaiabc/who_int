1.9 Transcription factors and regulators

Transcription factors are sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins that bind regulatory elements and control the rate of transcription from DNA to mRNA. Mutation of a number of these involved in regulation of cell growth, differentiation, and death leads to genetic tumour syndromes.

Hyperparathyroidism–jaw tumour syndrome is caused by heterozygous pathogenic germline variants or deletions in CDC73, the product of which, parafibromin, is ubiquitously expressed in most tissues and functions as a bona fide tumour suppressor gene. Parafibromin is a critical component of the polymerase-associated factor 1 complex that binds to RNA polymerase II and controls transcription {11294964;25388829;29142233}. It also represses expression of the proto-oncogenes CCND1 (encoding cyclin D1) and MYC {19906718;18987311}, and is regarded as a part of the p53 pathway, though of lesser importance in most cell types.
