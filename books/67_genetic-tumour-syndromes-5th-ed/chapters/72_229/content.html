7.2 RNA splicing

The conversion of RNA transcripts into mRNA requires the splicing of exons together by removing non-coding intron regions of the RNA. Splicing occurs in the nucleus during or soon after transcription.

Only one tumour predisposition syndrome is included in this family, Goldenhar syndrome. This has recently been reported to be due to loss-of-function variants in SF3B2, a component of the U2 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein complex and required for RNA splicing {34344887}.
