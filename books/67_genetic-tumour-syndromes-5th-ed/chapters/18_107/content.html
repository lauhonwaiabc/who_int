1.8 mTOR/PI3K pathway

The mTOR/PI3K pathway is an essential regulator of growth activation and survival via many growth factors and hormones. mTOR is a component of two molecular complexes, mTORC1 and mTORC2, which have complex interactions with other pathways, but are responsible for mediating growth and survival signals from PI3K in growing cells {27051587}. mTOR inhibitors are well established and already used in treatment {36612012}.

Tuberous sclerosis is caused by pathogenic sequence variants in TSC1 and TSC2 {28222202;28968464}, resulting in loss of normal inhibitory control of mTOR pathway activation and thus excessive activation of the pathway, increasing proliferation and cell growth {26893383;2889832}. Tumour (renal angiomyolipomas, subependymal giant cell astrocytoma, and lymphangioleiomyomatosis) progression can be controlled by treatment with mTOR inhibitors {23158522;25456370;27351628;27409709}.

PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (Cowden syndrome) carries a high risk of breast, thyroid, and other cancers caused by germline variant of PTEN, a key molecule mediating cell survival as part of the PI3K pathway {36830628}.

Activated PI3Kδ syndrome is caused by variants that constitutively activate the p110δ isoform of PI3Kδ, resulting in immune dysregulation and a high risk of the development of acute myeloid leukaemia {33617893;36841351}. Treatment with PI3K inhibitors is feasible and under investigation {36399712}.
