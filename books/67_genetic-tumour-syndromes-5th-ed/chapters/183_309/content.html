1.8.3 Activated PI3K-delta syndrome (PIK3CD)

Definition

Activated PI3Kδ syndrome (APDS) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by gain-of-function variants in PIK3CD (which encodes the PI3Kδ catalytic subunit; subtype APDS1) or PIK3R1 (which encodes the PI3K regulatory subunit; subtype APDS2). It is characterized by bacterial, viral, and fungal infections, autoimmunity, enteropathy, and benign and malignant lymphoproliferative disorders (LPDs).

Subtype(s)

Localization

LPDs in APDS present predominantly in lymph nodes and at extranodal sites, mainly in the digestive tract and as hepatosplenomegaly {31111319;27555459}.

Clinical features

APDS is clinically heterogeneous and characterized by early-onset recurrent otitis media, sinusitis, and bronchopulmonary infections (median 1.7 years at first presentation, range 0.4–2 years), followed later in life by enteropathy, autoimmune and inflammatory disease, and reactive and neoplastic LPD (ranging from median 3.0 to 18.0 years, range 1.0–24.2 years overall) {31111319}. Autoimmune and inflammatory diseases include rheumatological, gastrointestinal, and endocrine disorders and, most frequently, haematological disorders such as autoimmune haematolytic anaemia and immune cytopenic purpura {31111319;27555459;27221134}. Cancer risk is largely restricted to lymphoma (12.8%). The spectrum of lymphoproliferations also includes reactive LPDs {28395106;30082493}. Clinically, LPDs including lymphomas present as lymphadenopathy (58.4%), splenomegaly (43.7%), and hepatomegaly (4.2%), with lymphadenopathy being more frequently observed in APDS2 and hepatomegaly more frequently in APDS1 {31111319;27555459}.

Epidemiology

APDS is a rare disorder with currently only just over 240 patients reported in the literature {31111319}. APDS is classified according to the International Union of Immunological Societies (IUIS) in the “predominantly antibody deficiency” group with a common variable immunodeficiency phenotype {35748970;36198931}. Specific data on worldwide incidences are largely lacking {27221134;33401995}.

Etiology

APDS is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by gain-of-function variants in PIK3CD, which encodes the PI3Kδ catalytic p110d subunit (APDS1), or PIK3R1, which encodes the p85a regulatory subunit (APDS2) {25133428;25488983;24165795}. Expression of the p110d subunit is restricted to leukocytes and p85a is the regulatory subunit expressed in lymphocytes. Gain-of-function variants lead to hyperactivated PI3Kδ signalling in B and T cells, resulting in a heterogeneous immunological phenotype with both B-cell and T-cell abnormalities {27616589;28167755}.

Pathogenesis

B-cell lymphopenia and differentiation abnormalities are most frequent (74.8%) with low numbers of class-switched memory B cells, high IgM levels, and low IgG and IgA levels. The hyperIgM phenotype is reported more frequently in APDS2 than in APDS1 {31111319}. T-cell dysregulation is most prominent as CD4+ T-cell deficiency (64.8%), especially impacting the naïve CD4+ T-cell compartment with inverted CD4:CD8 ratios {25488983;29800648;30738173}. The resulting immune deficiency/dysregulation predisposes patients to bacterial, viral, and fungal infection complications. Increased incidence and persistence of viral infections, especially EBV, contribute to predisposition to the development of B-cell LPDs, including lymphomas {25488983;31111319}. Defective T-cell–mediated immune surveillance also contributes to the overall lymphoma risk.

Macroscopic appearance

Firmer, fleshy, or necrotic areas of specimens should be sampled to assess for lymphoma.

Histopathology

LPDs cover the complete spectrum of reactive and neoplastic LPDs as described in immune deficiency/dysregulation settings, including inborn errors of immunity (IEI) {28395106;30082493}. The LPDs include hyperplasia, EBV-associated mucocutaneous ulcers, polymorphic B-cell LPDs, and lymphomas. In patients with APDS, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is reported most frequently, followed by classic Hodgkin lymphoma and marginal zone lymphoma {27221134;27555459;31942637}. EBV association is reported, but not systematically evaluated. Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of a heterozygous pathogenic variant in PIK3CD or PIK3R1 by sequencing can confirm the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: at least one of the following: increased susceptibility to infection, autoimmune manifestations, granulomatous disease, unexplained polyclonal lymphoproliferation and/or lymphoma; AND at least one of the following: marked decrease of IgG and IgA with or without low IgM levels (measured at least twice; < 2 standard deviations of the normal levels for their age), poor antibody response to vaccines (and/or absent isohaemagglutinins), absence of protective levels despite vaccination where defined low switched memory B cells (< 70% of age-related normal value); AND exclusion of secondary causes of hypogammaglobulinaemia; AND demonstration of a pathogenic gain-of-function heterozygous variant in PIK3CD or PIK3R1.

Staging

Lymphoma staging is based on the diagnosis-appropriate staging system, such as the Lugano classification (modified Ann Arbor staging system) or the Revised International Paediatric Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma Staging System {25113753;25940716}.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis is largely determined by infectious complications, requiring early start of antibiotic prophylaxis and immunoglobulin replacement therapy. Immune suppression may be required for autoimmunity and inflammation {31111319}. Targeted treatment directed to the PI3K/mTOR pathway (rapamycin) is emerging, and allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation may be considered to cure both the lymphoma and underlying IEI {33275750;29599784;34033842}. Although chemotherapy with or without rituximab in lymphomas in the IEI setting in general often achieves remission, morbidity and mortality due to infectious complications are high {31580160}.
