1.4 PKA signalling pathway

The cAMP/PKA pathway is involved in the function of many cells. Abnormal activation of PI3K/AKT may be activated indirectly via a number of tyrosine kinase receptors, or by activating mutations of the p110α catalytic or p85 regulatory subunits of PI3K {30669581}. Activation of AKT leads to inhibition of apoptosis in growing cells, and is key to their survival, via PTEN. In sporadic cancers, such activation is common {19738044}.

Two syndromes are included in this family. The first is Carney complex, caused by mutation of a key component of the cAMP signalling pathway, PRKAR1A, or more rarely PDE8B or PDE11A. The related PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum syndrome is caused by postzygotic pathogenic variants in PIK3CA.
