1.8.2 PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (PTEN)

Definition

PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (PHTS), is a multiple hamartoma syndrome with a high risk of breast, thyroid, and other cancers. The subset of patients that carry germline PTEN mutations have PHTS. Thus, PHTS is a molecular diagnosis that encompasses a broad group of clinical diagnoses that also have a germline PTEN mutation.

MIM numbering

158350 Cowden syndrome; CWS1

ICD-11 coding

LD2D.Y Other specified phakomatoses or hamartoneoplastic syndromes; Cowden syndrome

Related terminology

Acceptable: Cowden syndrome; multiple hamartoma syndrome.

Subtype(s)

Bannayan–Riley–Ruvalcaba syndrome (BRRS)

Localization

PHTS involves organs derived from any of the three germ layers.

Clinical features

Cowden syndrome is characterized by mucocutaneous lesions (multiple facial trichilemmomas, acral keratoses, papillomatous papules, and mucosal lesions are considered pathognomonic). Mucosal papillomas present as small smooth whitish papules that often coalesce, resulting in the characteristic cobblestone appearance on the lips, gingiva, and buccal or tongue mucosa. The presence of a fissured tongue is considered a characteristic feature of Cowden syndrome {26975628;27889943;24560406}. Cowden syndrome is also characterized by an increased cancer risk, benign hamartomatous overgrowth of tissues (including hamartomatous/juvenile gastrointestinal polyposis), and macrocephaly {24136893;28476234}. Diffuse oesophageal glycogenic acanthosis in combination with colonic polyposis may be diagnostic of Cowden syndrome {21437855;23238744}. Cancer risk is comparatively broad, including risk of breast, thyroid, endometrial, renal cell, and colon cancers, as well as melanoma {20301661;28476234}.

PHTS also includes BRRS, which coexists with germline PTEN mutation, and 20% of Proteus syndrome cases {12938083}. However, about 80% of patients with classic Cowden syndrome have PTEN germline variants, and so these terms are often used interchangeably {19668082} even though ideally they should not be.

Adult-onset dysplastic cerebellar gangliocytoma (Lhermitte–Duclos disease), even in the absence of other features or family history, is highly predictive of a germline PTEN pathogenic variant {14566704}, and dysplastic cerebellar gangliocytoma is now considered pathognomonic of PHTS.

Epidemiology

The prevalence of Cowden syndrome has been estimated at about 1 case per 200 000–250 000 individuals in a European population. This may be an underestimate given the difficulty in diagnosing this syndrome {10234502}. Female patients with Cowden syndrome have a cumulative lifetime breast cancer risk (to the age of 70 years) as high as 85% and a relative risk of 25 {22252256;20565722}. Multifocal and bilateral breast cancer has been reported in 34% of patients with Cowden syndrome with breast cancer {20565722;28476234}. Male patients with Cowden syndrome are also at increased risk of breast cancer, although the exact magnitude of risk is unclear {11238682;28476234}. Benign breast lesions can be found in the majority of female patients with Cowden syndrome {9445133}.

Etiology

PHTS is an autosomal dominant disorder with age-related penetrance and variable expression. The germline PTEN pathogenic sequence variant (10q23.31) is found in as many as 85% of Cowden syndrome cases, as well as in subsets of other PHTS disorders (65% in BRRS, 20% in Proteus syndrome, and 50% in Proteus-like syndrome) {19668082;18794875;9140396;8673088}. Individualized risk calculation may aid in the assessment of a patient’s risk of carrying a germline PTEN variant on the basis of clinical features {21194675}.

Germline SDH variants have been found in about 5% of patients with PTEN variant–negative Cowden syndrome / Cowden syndrome–like phenotypes, and they are associated with increased frequencies of breast, thyroid, and renal cancers beyond those conferred by germline PTEN variant {18678321}. Other genes implicated in a minority of Cowden syndrome / BRRS cases that do not have germline PTEN variant include PIK3CA, AKT1 {23246288}, WWP1 {32459922}, TTN, and others {29263846;29684080}.

Pathogenesis

PTEN, which is a virtually ubiquitously expressed tumour suppressor, is a dual-specificity lipid and protein phosphatase that regulates cell proliferation, cell migration, and apoptosis through inhibition of AKT via the PI3K/AKT pathway {23613428}. Inactivation of the second copy of the gene allows deregulation of the AKT pathway.

Macroscopic appearance

Gastrointestinal polyps typically measure 3–10 mm with features similar to those of their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Oral papillomas in PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome are histologically indistinguishable from non–syndrome-associated papilloma. They may clinically appear similar to irritation fibroma. They are composed of fibrous connective tissue that may contain some dilated blood vessels and inflammatory infiltrate, lined by hyperplastic non-keratinizing squamous epithelium. The skin lesions and other tumours associated with Cowden syndrome lose PTEN expression, which is detected by immunohistochemistry {30730313}.

Clinically, trichilemmomas are often indistinguishable from other verrucous skin lesions and therefore histopathological confirmation is necessary. Trichilemmomas are typically associated with pre-existing hair follicles and characterized by thickening of the follicular infundibulum, with superficial hypergranulosis and hyperkeratosis, and focal trichilemmal differentiation. Trichilemmomas can be difficult to distinguish from basal cell carcinoma with trichilemmal differentiation, and the surface of a trichilemmoma may be indistinguishable from verruca vulgaris.

Gastrointestinal polyps in Cowden syndrome represent a mixture of histology profiles {20600018}. Hyperplastic polyps, hamartomatous/juvenile polyps, adenomas, and ganglioneuromas are the most frequent types of polyps in the colon {26721304}. Hyperplastic polyps are not part of the diagnostic criteria for PHTS. Colonic intramucosal lipomas have also been associated with Cowden syndrome {29192650}.

Polyps in the duodenum are mainly hamartomas and some ganglioneuromas and adenomas {26721304}. Most patients with Cowden syndrome have gastric polyps {21437855;23238744}, which are usually numerous, measure 1–20 mm, and are mostly diagnosed as hyperplastic or hamartomatous polyps. Dysplasia has not been reported in gastric Cowden syndrome polyps.

Benign breast pathologies occur in about 75% of female patients with Cowden syndrome and include ductal hyperplasia, intraductal papillomatosis, adenosis, lobular atrophy, fibroadenomas, and fibrocystic change. Many patients show features suggestive of a breast hamartoma. Malignant breast lesions are mostly invasive carcinoma of no special type, often associated with ductal carcinoma in situ {9445133}. A recent report suggests that apocrine differentiation is common in breast cancers from patients with germline PTEN variants, and expression profiling puts these cancers in the molecular apocrine category {20712882}.

In addition to the numerous adenomatous nodules and fibroadenomas that are often seen in thyroids from patients with Cowden syndrome {21190448;21921783}, approximately 25% have follicular thyroid carcinomas that may present in childhood {21956414;32228164;33495912}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PHTS patients all have germline pathogenic variants in PTEN.

About 80% of Cowden syndrome cases are caused by germline PTEN pathogenic variants.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

The International Cowden Consortium (ICC) operational diagnostic criteria are listed in #37428Box 2.12. A more quantitative set of criteria is nomogram-based {21194675} [[Cleveland Clinic Adult Clinical Scoring for PTEN Testing Internet. Available from: https://www.lerner.ccf.org/genomic-medicine/ccscore/documents/adult_criteria.html. Bookref]]. See also #37387Table 2.42.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with PHTS are at increased risk of several types of cancer, and interval screening is recommended (see #56868Table 2.43) {22252256;23613428;23335809;21956414;24778394;35487791;32533092;25132236}.
