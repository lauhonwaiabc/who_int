4.2 Homologous recombination

Homologous recombination repair is a type of genetic recombination in which genetic information is exchanged between two similar or identical molecules of DNA. It has the ability to repair double-strand breaks and is used during meiosis to produce new combinations of DNA sequence information in gametes.

The process involves enzymatic resection in which the 5′ ends of the double-strand breaks are removed, and overhanging 3′ ends of the damaged DNA then invade a similar or identical DNA molecule that is not broken. After strand invasion, repair may follow either of two main pathways: the double strand–break repair pathway or the synthesis-dependent strand-annealing pathway. Homologous recombination repair tends to result in non-crossover products, in effect restoring the correct sequence of the damaged DNA molecule as it existed before the double-strand break.

BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndromes may result from germline (constitutional) pathogenic sequence variants in either BRCA1 or BRCA2. Variants in other components of the homologous recombination repair pathway give rise to the PALB2-related cancer predisposition syndrome, the RAD51-related cancer predisposition syndrome, or Fanconi anaemia.
