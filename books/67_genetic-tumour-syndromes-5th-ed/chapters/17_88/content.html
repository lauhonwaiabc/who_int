1.7 NF-κB signalling pathway

The NF-κB complex comprises p50 and p65 (RELA). When inactive, cytosolic NF-κB is bound by its inhibitor, IκBα. Several membrane receptors lead to activation of IKK, which phosphorylates IκBα resulting in its ubiquitination, dissociation of IκBα from NF-κB, and proteasomal degradation. Activated NF-κB is then translocated into the nucleus and binds DNA response elements, recruiting coactivators and leading transcription of target genes. The pathway is important in a variety of cell types, including haematolymphoid cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells.

CYLD is a tumour suppressor gene regulating NF-κB activation and suppressing the NF-κB pathway, germline mutation of which leads to Brooke–Spiegler syndrome {34744449}.
