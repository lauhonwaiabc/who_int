7.1.2 Microprocessor-related tumour predisposition syndromes (DROSHA, DGCR8)

Definition

Microprocessor-related tumour predisposition syndromes are autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndromes of uncertain penetrance caused by heterozygous germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in either of the two genes that together encode the microprocessor (DROSHA and DGCR8). Variants in DROSHA are associated with the occurrence of pineoblastoma and probably Wilms tumour, whereas a single variant in DGCR8 leads to thyroid neoplasms and schwannomas.

Localization

Currently, established sites of lesions for those with likely deleterious variants are limited to the kidney (Wilms tumour) and pineal gland (pineoblastoma) for DROSHA {25190313;39992227}, but as noted below may expand with additional reports. Sites for DGCR8 are currently limited to the thyroid (thyroid nodular hyperplasia and neoplasia) and the peripheral nervous system (schwannomas) {31805011;34821987}, but as for DROSHA reports are very limited.

Clinical features

Reports of germline pathogenic variants in DROSHA are rare, but a recent report documents heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in DROSHA occurring in 9 children from 8 families affected by either pineoblastoma (n = 8) or Wilms tumour (n = 1). A somatic second hit in DROSHA was detected in all eight tumours analysed {39992227}. In addition, a Wilms tumour has been reported in a person with a likely deleterious DROSHA variant {25190313}. For DGCR8, there are two separate reports of the same germline pathogenic variant NM_022720.7:c.1552G>A p.(Glu518Lys) ins DGCR8 resulting in multiple schwannomatosis and thyroid hyperplasia and/or neoplasia {31805011;34821987}. Papillary thyroid carcinoma has been reported in one person {34821987}. A choroid plexus papilloma and ovarian desmoids were noted in one person each with a germline pathogenic variant in DGCR8, but it is not known whether these are chance associations or not {31805011}.

Epidemiology

For both DGCR8 and DROSHA, there have been no formal epidemiological studies, mainly because germline pathogenic variants appear to be so infrequently observed in clinical studies. The number of observed loss-of-function (LOF) variants over the expected number (O:E ratio) for a gene its size in the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD) is 0.31 (90% CI: 0.25–0.39), supporting modest intolerance of LOF variants in DROSHA (DROSHA). In UK Biobank, there were 49 variants in 97 individuals, and in the Geisinger DiscovEHR database 24 variants in 44 individuals were found, giving the prevalence of pathogenic LOF DROSHA variants to be 1:4843 (95% CI: 1:3970–1:5970) and 1:3875 (95% CI: 1:2886–1:5201), respectively {39992227}. The DGCR8 pathogenic LOF O:E ratio is 0.17 (90% CI: 0.11–0.27). A recent unpublished study estimates the frequency of DGCR8 predicted pathogenic variants in two cohorts from DiscovEHR and UK Biobank to be 1:6000 and 1:12 500 individuals for putative LOF variants and 1:12 500 and 1:10 000 with missense predicted pathogenic variants, respectively.

Etiology

The microprocessor-related tumour predisposition syndromes are caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in either DROSHA at chromosome 5p13.3 or its partner, DGCR8, located at chromosome 22q11.21. DROSHA was previously known as RNASEN and DGCR8 has been referred to as pasha (partner of DROSHA). In the case of DROSHA, eight different germline pathogenic variants were found, all of which were predicted to truncate or completely abolish the protein {39992227}. A germline pathogenic variant NM_022720.7:c.1552G>A p.(Glu518Lys) in DGCR8 in the double-stranded RNA-binding domain of DGCR8 was responsible for familial euthyroid multinodular goitre, papillary thyroid carcinoma, schwannomas, and a choroid plexus papilloma.

Pathogenesis

Both DROSHA and DGCR8 are key nuclear components of the microRNA processing pathway. They form a heterotrimeric complex (two molecules of DGCR8 combined with one of DROSHA). The microprocessor cleaves primary microRNAs to generate pre-microRNAs. Like DICER1, DROSHA contains an RNase IIIb domain responsible for cleaving near the 5′ end of the transcript and an RNase IIIa domain responsible for cleaving near the 3′ end. DGCR8 binds DROSHA to form the microprocessor and aids in primary microRNA processing by stabilizing the complex. It does not itself take part in the cutting of the primary microRNA. DROSHA-associated tumours appear to contain nonsense and frameshift pathogenic variants {39992227}. In contrast, DGCR8-associated tumours thus far show a very strong association with a single missense variant, c.1552G>A (p.Glu518Lys), which is always partnered with loss of heterozygosity of the wildtype allele independently of the tissue type {31805011;34821987}. Somatically, the p.Glu518Lys variant accompanied by loss of the other copy of chromosome 22q has been frequently reported in Wilms tumours and in a handful of thyroid cancers {28825729;25670082;24909261;31805011;34171097}. Somatic losses of DGCR8 or DROSHA have also been reported in pineoblastomas {31820118;32124011;31802236;31768671}. Patients with 22q11 microdeletion lack one copy of DGCR8. There are limited data as to any increased cancer risk in patients with the common 22q11 deletion syndrome that often includes loss of both the DGCR8 and LZTR1 genes {33879870;28940864}. Three reports of pineoblastomas in 22q11del patients have been noted. In one of them, homozygous loss of DGCR8 was molecularly confirmed {28396774;31802236;30217803}.

Macroscopic appearance

Syndromic lesions are grossly indistinguishable from sporadic tumours.

Histopathology

The lesions occurring in those with DROSHA or DGCR8 germline variants do not appear to differ histopathologically from their microprocessor-unrelated counterparts.

Cytology

Owing to the risk of thyroid neoplasia, thyroid nodules often undergo FNAB {28323992}. Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

For DROSHA, four pineoblastomas were studied for their methylation patterns and all were consistent with pineoblastoma-microRNA 1, which is associated with the inactivation of microRNA processing {39992227}. For DGCR8, only the p.Glu518Lys variant has been found to be pathogenic and therefore identifying this variant in thyroid tumours or schwannoma should lead to increased suspicion of a possible germline origin.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of germline pathogenic variants in either DROSHA or DGCR8.

Desirable: personal or family history of pathologically confirmed syndrome-related lesions, particularly those with high specificity for the microprocessor syndrome.

Staging

Staging of individual tumours is based on the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

There are currently no data on outcome, but to the authors’ knowledge, no person has died of a DGCR8-related tumour. There are no data regarding DROSHA-related outcomes yet, but the microRNA-altered subtypes of pineoblastoma (pineoblastoma-microRNA 1 and pineoblastoma-microRNA 2) are associated with significantly better prognosis compared with subtypes pineoblastoma-RB1 and pineoblastoma-MYC/FOXR2.
