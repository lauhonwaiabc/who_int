1.4.2 PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum syndromes (PIK3CA)

Definition

PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum (PROS) syndrome, caused by postzygotic activating pathogenic variants in PIK3CA, can be separated into distinct clinical syndromes based on the tissues and/or organs involved; CLOVES syndrome (congenital lipomatous overgrowth, vascular malformations, epidermal naevi, and spinal/skeletal anomalies/scoliosis) and MCAP syndrome (megalencephaly–capillary malformation) are entities distinct from other PROS disorders.

Subtype(s)

CLOVES (congenital lipomatous overgrowth, vascular malformations, epidermal naevi, and spinal/skeletal anomalies/scoliosis) syndrome; MCAP (megalencephaly–capillary malformation) syndrome

Localization

As defined by the name, CLOVES syndrome can affect a range of tissues and locations, including the trunk, epidermis (epidermal naevi), skeleton, spine, and lymphatic, capillary, and venous systems. Organs such as the kidneys can also be involved.

Clinical features

Before identification of the causative PIK3CA mutation, PROS was previously separated into distinct clinical syndromes based on the tissues and/or organs involved; CLOVES syndrome is an entity distinct from other PROS disorders such as MCAP syndrome {23946963;35526022}.

The clinical lesions in CLOVES syndrome include fatty masses / masses localized to the trunk; overgrowth of, or deformities in, the arms, hands, legs, or feet; curvature of the spine (scoliosis or other spinal problems); epidermal naevi; anomalies of the blood vessels; and other skeletal anomalies. The physical signs and symptoms allow diagnosis at birth, which can be confirmed with molecular testing to identify a postzygotic activating mutation in PIK3CA {23946963;35526022}. Imaging studies, including MRI and radiography of the torso, pelvis, spine, and limbs, can facilitate disease classification. Ultrasound may be used to examine vascular anomalies and the kidneys. Prenatal diagnosis with imaging tools is feasible.

Tumours have been reported in individuals with CLOVES syndrome, including Wilms tumour and nephroblastomatosis. Benign tumours, such as cavernous haemangiomas, have also been reported in the CNS {23946963}.

Epidemiology

PROS is a rare syndrome, and few data are available.

Etiology

It is generally believed that CLOVES syndrome is caused by the acquisition of an activating PIK3CA mutation {35526022}. There is no evidence that the condition is inherited, and the allele frequency of PIK3CA mutations in this condition suggests an early postzygotic event resulting in variable levels of mosaicism.

Pathogenesis

Activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR growth signalling pathway leads to overgrowth of many tissues, and an apparent predisposition to tumour formation {34238334}. Genetically distinct cell lineages occur in a mosaic pattern based on the developmental stage at which they occur {34238334}.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid tumours related to PROS syndrome have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

The histological features of the tumours found recapitulate their sporadic counterparts. The International Society for the Study of Vascular Anomalies (ISSVA) classification also lists other vascular anomalies associated with PIK3CA variants: CLAPO syndrome (capillary malformation of the lower lip,  lymphatic malformation of the face and neck, asymmetry, and partial/generalized overgrowth); common (cystic) lymphatic malformation; common vascular malformation; and fibroadipose vascular anomaly {26055853}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The presence of postzygotic activating sequence variants in PIK3CA is necessary for the diagnosis of CLOVES syndrome {23946963}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic physical signs and symptoms usually at birth; molecular testing to identify a postzygotic activating sequence variants in PIK3CA.

Desirable: MRI and radiography of the torso, pelvis, spine, and limbs for disease classification; ultrasound for the detection of vascular anomalies and kidneys; prenatal diagnosis with imaging tools.

Staging

Staging is as for sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

At present, data on the outcome of patients with CLOVES syndrome are still being collected. Expert management involving paediatric and plastic surgeons and neurosurgeons along with specialist haematology, neurology, radiology, and nephrology teams is likely to improve outcomes. Treatment with the PIK3CA inhibitor alpelisib is indicated and has been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) {23946963;36914722}. The use of mTOR inhibition is under investigation {36651691}. Tumour screening, particularly for Wilms tumour, has been suggested but remains controversial as the lifetime risk appears to be < 5% {23946963}.
