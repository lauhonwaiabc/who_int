5.1.2 POT1- and shelterin-related tumour predisposition syndrome (POT1, ACD, TERF2IP, TERT promoter)

Definition

POT1 is a tumour predisposition gene associated with the regulation of telomere end protection and telomere length. POT1 is part of a protein complex called shelterin, which includes proteins encoded by other genes, such as ACD and TERF2IP. Shelterin plays a role in the regulation of TERT, and, collectively, inherited alterations in POT1, ACD, TERF2IP, and the promoter of TERT have all been implicated in early-onset/high-penetrance melanoma.

Subtype(s)

Localization

There are insufficient data at this time to determine if melanomas that arise in the context of a germline alteration of one of the above-mentioned shelterin genes or in association with a TERT promoter variant present differently from population-ascertained melanomas. Of note, at this time there is no evidence of an association of variants in these genes with melanoma subtypes such as acral or mucosal or with melanocytic tumours that develop at other sites {33119245;26433962;25505254;24686849;24686846}.

Clinical features

POT1 tumour predisposition syndrome is defined depending on the tumour spectrum present in the kindred and assuming POT1 is the shelterin gene altered. There are no specific clinical features that define melanomas arising in the context of a POT1, shelterin, or TERT promoter variant. Evidence from several studies do, however, suggest an earlier age of presentation in POT1 variant carriers {24686846;24686849}. Further, several patients with the TERT promoter variant {26433962;23348503} have presented with advanced disease in their twenties suggesting an aggressive clinical course.

Pathogenic POT1 variants have also been associated with other cancer types, including haematological malignancies, cardiac angiosarcoma, and brain tumours {33119245;28853721;34442055}.

Epidemiology

There does not appear to be any specific population that has been found to carry POT1 variants. Given the role that ultraviolet (UV) light plays in melanoma development, well-studied European-descent populations from Australia, the United Kingdom, Europe, and the USA have been the source of most of the germline variants defined as pathogenic. A founder allele of POT1 known as NM_015450.3:c.233T>C p.(IIe78Thr) has been observed in several individuals who developed melanoma and other cancers and was found to be of Jewish origin by genotyping of linked SNPs {30586141}. The sequencing of a melanoma from a p.(IIe78Thr) variant carrier revealed a genome replete with C>T UV-associated somatic mutations, suggesting a role for UV in melanoma development in carriers. Sequenced naevi from this same patient carried established melanoma driver events, such as hotspot mutations in NRAS, BRAF, and KIT {30586141}.

Etiology

At present the cause of melanoma formation in patients with pathogenic germline variants in shelterin genes and the TERT promoter are unknown although it seems likely that UV light, which contributes to other melanoma predisposition syndromes, plays a role. One theory is that germline variants, which can lead to elongated telomeres in variant carriers {26433962;23348503}, may interfere with the normal programme of melanocyte senescence. Similarly, disruption of shelterin might lead to an aberrant DNA damage response at telomere ends.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of melanoma in these conditions is currently unknown. As above, it is presumed that perturbation of either senescence or DNA damage programmes contributes to tumorigenesis, and that there is an intimate interplay with UV exposure.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid tumours have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Limited information is available, but it has been suggested that melanomas that develop in POT1 variant carriers have an increased likelihood of having a spitzoid morphology {32283231}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology includes demonstration of a germline pathogenic variant in POT1, ACD, TERF2IP, or the TERT promoter.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pathogenic germline variant in POT1, ACD, TERF2IP, or the TERT promoter.

Desirable: personal and/or family history of melanoma with accompanying somatic alterations (e.g. BRAF variant NM_004333.6:c.1799T>A p.(Val600Glu) [V600E]), providing assistance with tumour subtyping.

Prognosis and prediction

Participation in a programme providing skin cancer screening and education about skin self-examination has been associated with thinner (earlier stage) melanomas in members of melanoma-prone families {33811164} and would seem a sensible approach to clinical management of melanoma risk. Guidelines for screening for other malignancies have not yet been formulated.
