1.9.2 MAFA-related familial insulinomatosis (MAFA)

Definition

MAFA-related familial insulinomatosis is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by germline pathogenic sequence variants in MAFA. It is characterized by multiple insulin-secreting pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PanNETs) that typically present as adult-onset hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia.

MIM numbering

147630 Insulinomatosis and diabetes mellitus; INSDM

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial insulinomatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The disorder is restricted to the pancreas where MAFA plays a critical role as a transcription factor regulating pancreatic B-cell function and proliferation.

Clinical features

Reflecting the role of MAFA as both an oncogene and an islet B-cell transcription factor, patients may present with either hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia or diabetes mellitus, with the hypoglycaemic presentation being more common in female patients and the diabetic presentation in male patients {29339498}. While the two manifestations are generally mutually exclusive, one patient with diabetes went on to develop hyperinsulinism and multifocal islet B-cell pancreatic proliferations {29339498}. Onset is in adulthood, which distinguishes it from congenital hyperinsulinism. Clues to the diagnosis in patients presenting with diabetes include a normal body mass index, no other clinical features of insulin resistance, no history of diabetic ketoacidosis, and negative islet autoantibodies {29339498}. Imaging identifies multicentric PanNETs that can measure > 10 mm {29339498;35406570}.

Epidemiology

This disorder is too rare to determine the actual incidence and prevalence. While the variant has no sex predilection, hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia has been reported to be more frequent in female patients, whereas male patients in the same kindreds develop diabetes more commonly. The onset of symptoms is usually in the fourth decade of life and there is no history to suggest congenital hyperinsulinism {29339498}.

Etiology

This disorder has been attributed to a specific germline variant, NM_201589.4:c.191C>T p.(Ser64Phe), in the MAFA gene, encoding the transcription factor MAFA. A recent report has identified another heterozygous missense variant, NM_201589.4:c.170C>G p.(Thr57Arg), in the same transactivation domain of MAFA, which can cause the same clinical manifestations {35406570}. The presence of a MAFA variant distinguishes this from sporadic insulinomatosis {29339498}.

Pathogenesis

MAFA is a basic leucine zipper–containing transcription factor that regulates glucose-stimulated insulin secretion {17785922;24726898;33514698}; it also has oncogenic transformation potential in vitro. A specific variant, NM_201589.4:c.191C>T p.(Ser64Phe), impairs phosphorylation in the MAFA transactivation domain, resulting in increased protein stability that is insensitive to glucose concentrations, thereby interrupting the regulatory action of MAFA. In addition, this variant exhibits enhanced transactivation compared with wildtype MAFA {29339498}.

Macroscopic appearance

The pancreas of patients with this disorder has multifocal small tumours that vary in size and can reach > 10 mm.

Histopathology

The pancreas of patients with MAFA-related insulinomatosis contains innumerable multicentric islet B-cell proliferations ranging from dysplastic B-cell cords to multifocal neuroendocrine trabecular microtumours (< 5 mm) and solitary well-differentiated tumours (> 5 mm) (Ki-67 < 2%). While the islets contain cells that produce insulin, glucagon, PP, and somatostatin in normal distribution and numbers, the trabecular proliferations are exclusively positive for insulin, which helps distinguish the condition from multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 and glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia {19011561;29339498}. MAFA protein is expressed in both the neoplastic and normal B-cell nuclei.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The missense MAFA sequence variants NM_201589.4:c.191C>T p.(Ser64Phe) and NM_201589.4:c.170C>G p.(Thr57Arg) have been shown to segregate with the manifestations of both hyperinsulinism and diabetes mellitus.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: evidence of multifocal pancreatic trabecular islet B-cell proliferations staining for insulin; germline pathogenic sequence variant of the MAFA gene.

Prognosis and prediction

Medical therapy yields poor results with persistent hypoglycaemia {29339498}. Most patients have undergone partial pancreatectomy but this has not led to durable cure and several patients have undergone multiple surgical procedures. The multifocal nature of the islet B-cell proliferations suggests that total pancreatectomy would be required for cure {33434149}. Local or distant metastases have not been documented.
