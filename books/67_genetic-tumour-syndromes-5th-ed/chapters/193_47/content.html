Definition

Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome (BHDS) is a rare autosomal dominant genodermatosis associated with pathogenic germline variants in the FLCN gene, characterized by benign skin lesions, basal lung cysts with spontaneous pneumothorax, and an increased risk of kidney tumours.

Localization

BHDS is primarily a genodermatosis that also affects the lungs and kidneys. Usually, there are multiple fibrofolliculomas/trichodiscomas and acrochordons. The most affected areas are the scalp, face, neck, and trunk {35907578;29157599;33664427;26334087}. Both kidneys can be affected by tumours, with early onset, frequent bilaterality, and multiplicity {12459621}.

Clinical features

The triad of skin papules, spontaneous pneumothorax, and renal tumours in a young adult > 20 years of age should always raise the clinical suspicion of BHDS, which occurs in both males and females. The most common pattern of presentation is multiple small, millimetre-sized lesions and waxy, white, dome-shaped papules on the face. These may also be present on the ears, neck, and trunk. The number of lesions varies, from a few localized ones to several hundred.

Sometimes the first manifestation is spontaneous pneumothorax, especially in young adults, caused by lung cysts, which can be an isolated clinical finding {36028846;33987191;32469710;31177286}. Bilateral multiple cysts in patients with BHDS are round or oval, with thin, visible, and uniform walls. The lung basilar and mediastinal regions are the most frequent sites of the cysts, which differ from the typical apical location of cysts in smoking-related or sporadic emphysema {34000505;23223565;23715758;19782489}.

BHDS should also be suspected in patients < 50 years of age who present with bilateral/multifocal kidney tumours or if the tumours are of oncocytoma / hybrid oncocytoma-chromophobe type {30586397}. Alternatively, the clinical presentation is a solitary nodule and a different histological type, other than oncocytic tumours {25119168}. The presence of several nodules with oncocytic histology raises suspicion of BHDS.

Other manifestations are unusual, and the relationship with other tumours remains unclear. There are reports of an association with colonic polyps/adenocarcinoma, but they are still controversial {34000505;31857718}. Other reported tumours seen in some patients include parotid gland tumours (oncocytomas), lipomas, thyroid nodules, and adrenal tumours {35946080}. Rhabdomyomas of the head and neck, mostly of adult type, have been described in BHDS {29744825}.

Epidemiology

The actual prevalence of BHDS in the general population is unknown. Most studies are based on clinical skin manifestations or cases of spontaneous pneumothorax in young adults.

A genetics-first approach has described likely pathogenic FLCN variants in patients without complete clinical manifestations, raising the possibility of higher prevalence in the general population than in clinical cohorts {35639097}.

Etiology

The etiology of BHDS is a germline FLCN pathogenic sequence variant.

Pathogenesis

A germline variant of FLCN, which encodes the tumour suppressor folliculin, underlies BHDS.

FLCN is a 14-exon gene located at 17p11.2. Many different genetic alterations – such as insertions, deletions, SNVs, and splice-site alterations leading to missense, frameshift, or nonsense alterations – are described in databases as the cause of FLCN protein function disruption. Affected family members may inherit a duplication or deletion of cytosine in a C8 tract in exon 11. C-deletion mutation results in fewer renal tumours than in those with the C-insertion mutation.

Since isolated cases continue to be reported, several novel variants have been described.

Multiple distinct variants have been identified. Although folliculin is thought to be a tumour suppressor, its exact function is unknown.

FLCN has been linked to the mTOR pathway, activated by AMPK, which interacts with folliculin, FNIP1, and FNIP2. This complex can inhibit mTORC1 and PGC1α.

Uniparental disomy (also known as copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity) is a common finding in BHDS {26776076}. Interestingly, bilateral multifocal renal oncocytomas in patients without BHDS have been found to have disruptive mitochondrial DNA mutations, whereas renal tumours from patients with BHDS do not {26428318;33664427}.

Macroscopic appearance

There are no specific gross findings of the skin lesions. Lung cysts are round or oval, with thin walls, with no other distinctive aspects. Kidney tumours show similar gross morphology to sporadic ones. Multiple mahogany brown to yellowish tumours, ranging from 4 to 110 mm, corresponding histologically to oncocytic lesions, known as oncocytosis, can be found in BHDS.

Histopathology

The most frequent lesions are fibrofolliculomas, which are hamartomas of the hair follicle, characterized by a dilated central follicle containing keratinous material, without atypia and surrounded by moderately well-circumscribed connective tissue. The infundibular follicular epithelium extends beyond the fibrous tissue forming epithelial strands or cords. The primary differential diagnosis is trichofolliculoma, but the key difference is that there are no radiating cords containing sebocytes emanating from the main infundibular wall. Moreover, fibrofolliculoma does not display secondary infundibula from which vellus hair emanates. Some authors regard trichofolliculomas as fibrofolliculomas in the late stage {28370423}. However, they are not associated with FLCN sequence variants.

The second most common skin lesions are trichodiscomas, which are histologically very similar to fibrofolliculomas. They are horizontally oriented, with less epithelial proliferation, and the cystic changes are less prominent. The connective stroma is present in both lesions. Clinically, trichodiscomas are usually peripherally located on the face in contrast to trichofolliculomas, which tend to be more central {26734858}.

There are no specific histopathological findings of the lung cysts in BHDS. Pneumocytes line the inner wall of the cyst, and neither active stromal cell proliferation nor inflammatory cell infiltration are present. Pneumocytes lining the lung cysts are positive for CK7 and pulmonary surfactant {19788617}.

For the kidney tumours, there are no differences in histopathological findings when compared with their sporadic counterparts. Oncocytomas are the most frequent, and oncocytosis may be present in the adjacent parenchyma. Hybrid oncocytic tumours consisting of tumours with eosinophilic cytoplasm showing overlapping features between chromophobe carcinoma and oncocytoma can be more commonly found in patients with BHDS, usually multifocal and bilateral. All tumours are positive for PAX8. Oncocytomas/oncocytosis are diffusely positive for KIT (CD117). In hybrid tumours, the positivity of KIT is patchy (in oncocytic cells), with positivity of CK7 and CD10 in clear cells. The proliferation index measured by Ki-67 is < 5%.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of a germline FLCN pathogenic / likely pathogenic {25741868} sequence variant is necessary for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: germline pathogenic variant in the FLCN gene.

Desirable: one or more of the following: skin papules on the face and neck consistent with trichofolliculomas/trichodiscomas and acrochordons; lung cysts in the basal position with/without spontaneous pneumothorax in a young adult; oncocytosis or multiple tumours with oncocytic histology, or hybrid renal tumours in patients aged < 50 years.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for patients with BHDS depends on the associated lesions, such as pneumothorax and pulmonary cysts. The characteristic renal cancers are now considered to be of low malignant potential because metastatic spread is very rare {26428318}. In general, the favourable oncological outcome of tumours that occur in the context of BHDS and their propensity to develop multifocal and bilateral disease supports the management of these cases in a conservative way, i.e. periodic imaging to detect and/or monitor the evolution of renal neoplasms. If surgical treatment is needed, nephron-sparing surgery is recommended whenever possible {15821464}.
