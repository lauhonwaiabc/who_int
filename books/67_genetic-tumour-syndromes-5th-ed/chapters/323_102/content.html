3.2.3 CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome (CDKN2A)

Definition

CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in the CDKN2A tumour suppressor gene encoding the p16 (p16INK4a) and p14ARF cell-cycle regulators. The syndrome is associated with an increased risk of multiple neoplasms, including cutaneous melanoma, astrocytomas, nerve sheath tumours, pancreatic cancer, and squamous cell carcinoma of the oropharynx.

Related terminology

Acceptable (depending on the tumour spectrum present in the kindred): familial dysplastic naevi/melanoma with germline CDKN2A alteration; familial atypical mole–malignant melanoma syndrome with germline CDKN2A alteration; susceptibility to cutaneous malignant melanoma type 2; melanoma and neural system tumour syndrome; melanoma-astrocytoma syndrome; melanoma-pancreatic cancer syndrome.

Not recommended (due to lack of specificity for the underlying causative gene): familial dysplastic naevi/melanoma; familial atypical mole–malignant melanoma syndrome; hereditary melanoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The dysplastic naevi and melanomas arising in the setting of CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome are usually cutaneous and not mucosal, acral, or uveal. The astrocytomas are usually located in the cerebral hemispheres or cerebellum. Both paraspinal and superficial/subcutaneous locations have been reported for the nerve sheath tumours.

Clinical features

CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome is characterized by multiple cutaneous dysplastic naevi and an increased risk of melanoma, astrocytomas, nerve sheath tumours, pancreatic cancer, and squamous cell carcinoma of the oropharynx {8414022;8635060;9259954;12352668;15606533;18981015;22083977;23613284;24884915;26876133;28699883}. CDKN2A-variant carriers usually have relatively early onset of melanoma (~10 years earlier than in the general population), multiple primaries, and a variable association with clinically atypical naevi {16905682}. Why some kindreds develop exclusively dysplastic naevi and melanoma, while others also develop astrocytomas, pancreatic cancer, and other neoplasms, is currently unknown. The association of germline CDKN2A variants with an increased risk of pancreatic cancer is reported as particularly strong in families living in North America and mainland Europe, and weaker in the United Kingdom and Australia {16905682}. More recently, smoking-related cancers have also been shown to be more common in families with CDKN2A founder variants in the Netherlands {18981015} and in Sweden {24935963}.

Epidemiology

CDKN2A is the best-established high-penetrance gene associated with familial melanoma, and pathogenic germline variants in CDKN2A are identified in 30–40% of families with ≥ 3 melanoma cases {16905682}. The variant penetrance of germline variants in CDKN2A has been estimated in both the familial context {12072543} and in population-based sampling {16234564}. The lifetime risk of melanoma in CDKN2A-variant carriers is approximately 0.14 (95% CI: 0.08–0.22) by age 50 years, 0.24 (95% CI: 0.15–0.34) by age 70 years, and 0.28 (95% CI: 0.18–0.40) by age 80 years using population-based estimates. While the exact incidence is unknown, this syndrome is rare and probably accounts for < 1% of the total melanoma, astrocytoma, and pancreatic cancer incidence worldwide.

Etiology

CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome is caused by disruption of the CDKN2A tumour suppressor gene, which encodes the p16 and p14ARF cell-cycle regulators {7987388;9622062}. Hair colour, number of naevi, and germline MC1R variants are known to be associated with melanoma risk in CDKN2A-variant carriers {20876876}. Interactions between melanoma susceptibility genes including CDKN2A and environmental factors (e.g. ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure and potentially also tobacco smoke) have been reported to influence tumour penetrance. There are no known unique somatic mutation signatures associated with melanomas arising in the setting of germline CDKN2A pathogenic variants.

Pathogenesis

The product of CDKN2A, p16, binds to and inhibits CDK4 and CDK6 to maintain cells in the resting G1 phase of the cell cycle. The structurally unrelated p14ARF is produced from an alternative reading frame and acts by antagonizing the p53 regulatory protein MDM2. The neoplasms that arise in the setting of CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome are thought to occur owing to abnormal proliferation of melanocytes, astrocytes, and other cells after somatic inactivation of the remaining CDKN2A allele, typically via loss of heterozygosity {26876133;28699883}.

One study of a syndromic pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma revealed CDKN2A homozygous deletion and a NM_004333.6:c.1799T>A p.(Val600Glu) sequence variant; a separate diffuse astrocytoma in this patient was IDH and H3 wildtype with CDKN2A homozygous deletion and PTPN11, NF1, and ATRX sequence variants {28699883}. The molecular pathogenesis of nerve sheath tumours arising in the setting of melanoma-astrocytoma syndrome has not been investigated to date, but CDKN2A deletion is not characteristic of either sporadic schwannomas or typical neurofibromas, suggesting a pathogenesis distinct from their sporadic counterparts. Nevertheless, it is seen in atypical neurofibroma / atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential and malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour {21987445;30722027}.

Macroscopic appearance

There are no known macroscopic differences between germline CDKN2A-associated neoplasms and their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

In a study of familial melanomas from families with and without CDKN2A germline variants, the tumours in variant carriers tended to have histological features of superficial spreading melanoma, including higher levels of pigmentation and increased pagetoid scatter. The presence of spindle cell morphology in the vertical growth phase was a strong negative predictor of CDKN2A-carrier status; of the 15 cases with this phenotype, none harboured a pathogenic CDKN2A variant {25592620}. The astrocytomas arising in the setting of CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome include both pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma and diffuse astrocytic glioma, ranging from low-grade (diffuse astrocytoma) to high-grade (glioblastoma) {28699883}. No histopathological features distinguishing these syndrome-associated astrocytomas from their sporadic counterparts have been identified to date. The nerve sheath tumours have been reported to histologically resemble either schwannoma or neurofibroma {9259954;23613284;26876133;28699883}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Sequencing analysis of the CDKN2A gene in a germline DNA sample assessing pathogenic germline variants or deletions is required for diagnosis. Genomic analysis of syndrome-associated melanomas, astrocytomas, and other tumours demonstrates somatic inactivation of the remaining CDKN2A allele via loss of heterozygosity or an acquired second variant or deletion {28699883}.

Notably, there are no clinical phenotypic differences between families with CDKN2A germline variants and those with CDK4 germline variants, with similar early onset of disease, increased occurrence of clinically atypical naevi, and development of multiple primary melanomas {23384855}. As such, the CDK4 gene should be examined when a melanoma-prone family tests negative for CDKN2A mutation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: germline pathogenic variant in the CDKN2A gene (usually heterozygous).

Desirable: personal and/or family history of dysplastic naevi and characteristic neoplasms; demonstrable somatic inactivation of the remaining wildtype CDKN2A allele in a resected tumour, with accompanying somatic alterations (e.g. BRAF variant NM_004333.6:c.1799T>A p.(Val600Glu) [V600E]) providing assistance with tumour subtyping.

Staging

Melanomas, pancreatic cancers, and squamous cell carcinomas of the oropharynx arising in the setting of a CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome are staged as their sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

Participation in a programme providing skin cancer screening and education about skin self-exams has been associated with thinner (earlier stage) melanomas in members of melanoma-prone families {33811164}. However, no differences in clinical outcomes have been reported to date for malignancies arising in the setting of CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome compared with their sporadic counterparts.
