Definition

Ataxia–telangiectasia (AT) is a rare autosomal recessive disease caused by pathogenic sequence variants in the ataxia–telangiectasia gene (ATM), leading to disease affecting multiple systems, which may manifest in infancy or early childhood. Characteristic disease manifestations include cerebellar degeneration with progressive ataxia, oculocutaneous telangiectasia, immunodeficiency, susceptibility to bronchopulmonary disease, and lymphoid tumours.

Related terminology

Acceptable: Cerebello-oculocutaneous telangiectasia; immunodeficiency with ataxia–telangiectasia; Louis–Bar syndrome; Boder–Sedgwick syndrome.

Subtype(s)

Not applicable

Localization

Leukaemias affect bone marrow and peripheral blood. Occasionally, a mediastinal mass is observed. Lymphomas manifest with persistent lymphadenopathy. Extranodal lymphomatous involvement may also be observed. In patients with T-cell prolymphocytic leukaemia (T-PLL), peripheral blood lymphocytosis and splenomegaly are observed.

Clinical features

AT is a pleiotropic disorder with multiple manifestations, including immunodeficiency, neurological deficits, and increased propensity for development of cancer {18813293}. As many as 10% of all AT homozygotes develop a malignancy, with a 70- and 250-fold excess for leukaemias and lymphomas {3459930}. The M:F ratio for lymphoid malignancies is approximately 1.4–2:1. The predisposition to leukaemia and lymphoma is observed from childhood with a high propensity for development of acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, which generally shows a high frequency of unfavourable prognostic features, including older age at presentation (median: 9 years), higher white blood cell count, male sex, and mediastinal mass.

Patients with AT exhibit a marked predilection for T-PLL, which occurs in young adults, in contrast to the T-PLL in patients without AT, which occurs at a median age of diagnosis of 69 years {8555463}. The majority of the malignancies seen in younger patients with AT are of T-cell lineage and a minor proportion are B-cell lineage {8555463}. The B-cell lymphomas are aggressive in nature and include diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and Burkitt lymphoma {9044508}. Infrequent cases of Hodgkin lymphoma are reported {8555463}.

Non-haematopoietic malignancies including epithelial tumours constitute 13–22% of all malignancies and can be seen in adolescents with a median age of 17 years {2848624;8555463}. Myeloid malignancies are virtually absent {8555463}. Neurological deficits include cerebellar degeneration with progressive ataxia presenting in infancy and oculocutaneous telangiectasia. The neurological symptoms include abnormal eye movements and dysarthria. Patients also exhibit growth retardation, hypogonadism, and elevated levels of AFP {8555463}. Most patients develop sinopulmonary infections.

Heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in ATM have been associated with young age at breast cancer onset {9463314;10677309;15928302;11830610;16958054;19781682;21787400;26534844;27595995}. Heterozygous carriers of ATM variants do not show the severe toxic effects of radiation therapy that can occur in patients with AT {11710885}, but there is an elevated risk of contralateral breast cancer in heterozygous carriers of deleterious ATM variants treated with radiotherapy {20305132;28627265}.

Epidemiology

AT is rare and is estimated to occur at a birth frequency of 1 in 40 to 100 000 live births {137828;2338342;3788973}. In some populations, the disease is reported at even lower frequencies (1 in 300 000) {8968760}. There is no sex predilection.

Etiology

AT is caused by germline pathogenic variants in the ATM gene at 11q22.3 {7792600}. Nonsense, frameshift, missense, and insertion/deletion variants have been described. Compound heterozygous mutation has also been observed {10817650}. The classic AT phenotype results from biallelic loss-of-function variants leading to loss of expression of, or defective, ATM protein {9887333;10817650}.

Pathogenesis

The protein encoded by the ATM gene is a tumour suppressor. ATM protein is a high-molecular-weight (350 kDa) serine/threonine kinase member of the PI3K-related protein kinase (PIKK) family and is involved in the response to double-strand DNA breaks {7792600}. ATM phosphorylates critical protein substrates including p53 and BRCA1 {9733514;9733515} and plays a crucial functional role in controlling genome stability, cell cycle–checkpoint signalling, and apoptosis. Mutation of ATM resulting in its impaired function leads to defective DNA repair; chromosomal instability {1196376}; sensitivity to ionizing radiation and agents that cause DNA double-strand breaks; and deregulation of multiple pathways and biological processes, such as G1 to S phase cell-cycle progression, cell growth, and apoptosis, resulting in an increased propensity for development of cancer.

Macroscopic appearance

Not applicable

Histopathology

The lymphoid malignancies that occur most frequently in AT include acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, T-PLL, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, and less frequently Burkitt and Hodgkin lymphoma.

The immunophenotype of the lymphoid malignancies is identical to that observed in corresponding malignancies in patients without AT.

ATM-associated breast tumours belong mostly to the luminal B subtype, harbour loss of heterozygosity of 11q, and display tetraploidy, but do not have large-scale state transition genomic alterations like those reported in BRCA1- and BRCA2-mutated breast cancers {29665859}.

Cytology

The cytology mimics that observed in the corresponding lymphoid malignancies occurring in patients without AT.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetics: Leukaemias observed in AT may demonstrate chromosomal rearrangements including inv(7)(p13q35), t(7;7)(p13;q35), t(7;14)(p13;q11), t(7;14)(q35;q11), t(14;14)(q11;q32), inv(14)(q11q32), and t(X;14)(q28;q11) {8555463}.

Molecular pathology: Clonal rearrangements of the TR genes are detected by molecular methods (PCR, next-generation sequencing) in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, T-PLL, and other T-cell neoplasias. Clonal immunoglobulin gene rearrangements are observed in B-cell neoplasms.

Differential diagnosis: Chronic ataxic syndromes {3228996} and disorders associated with deficiencies in DNA repair pathways, such as Cockayne syndrome, xeroderma pigmentosum, and Nijmegen breakage syndrome, should be considered.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of homozygous or compound heterozygous variants of the ATM gene; diagnosis of lymphoid malignancies employs the criteria required for establishment of the diagnosis of those entities in patients without AT.

Desirable: high AFP levels are not diagnostic but are observed in > 95% of patients.

Prognosis and prediction

AT has a poor prognosis. Many patients with AT succumb to progressive pulmonary disease or to cancer, with median age at death of 25 years {16790721}.
