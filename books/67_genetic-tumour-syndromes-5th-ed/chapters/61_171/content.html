6.1 Histone and DNA methylation

Methylation adds methyl groups to DNA and also to amino acids of histone proteins, an integral part of nucleosomes, which the DNA double helix wraps around to form chromosomes. Methylation can either increase or decrease transcription of genes, depending on the site affected and the degree of methylation.

The integral nature of the genes involved in this process is reflected by the single syndrome within this family, enchondromatosis, caused by postzygotic variants in IDH1 or IDH2, encoding metabolic enzymes involved in the tricarboxylic acid cycle, leading to epigenetic changes including altered histone methylation and DNA hypermethylation {22057234}.
