1.1.6 Encephalocraniocutaneous lipomatosis (FGFR1)

Definition

Encephalocraniocutaneous lipomatosis (ECCL) is a sporadic neurocutaneous syndrome characterized by congenital anomalies involving the skin, eyes, and CNS.

MIM numbering

613001 Encephalocraniocutaneous lipomatosis; ECCL

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: Fishman syndrome; Haberland syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ECCL is characterized by congenital unilateral anomalies affecting the skin, eyes, CNS, and rarely bones and heart. Bilateral involvement of the skin and eyes is observed in 40% of patients {19574261;23974968;21626669}.

Clinical features

Congenital skin anomalies: Naevus psiloliparus (NP; 80% of cases) is characteristic of ECCL. It is a well-demarcated, yellowish raised plaque on the scalp, with no or only rare hair follicles {9693178}. Frontotemporal or zygomatic subcutaneous lipomas, non-scarring alopecia, focal skin hypoplasia or aplasia of the scalp, periorbital skin tags, and pigmentary abnormalities along the lines of Blaschko are also reported.

Eyes: Unilateral or bilateral choristomas are found in 80% of cases.

CNS: Intracranial and intraspinal lipomas are found in 61% of cases. Less-frequent abnormalities include arachnoid cysts, enlarged ventricles, congenital abnormalities of the meninges, and leptomeningeal angiomatosis, resulting in highly asymmetrical changes. Mild to severe intellectual disabilities are observed in two thirds of patients, and seizures in 50%. Low-grade gliomas, mostly pilocytic astrocytomas (PAs), have been also reported {20881536;30143858;26942290;23974968;30891959}. In a recent series of five brain tumours in ECCL including three PAs, two tumours originally reported as dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumour and glioblastoma were reclassified by further molecular analysis and methylation studies as PA and pilomyxoid anaplastic astrocytoma, respectively {30143858}.

Skeletal abnormalities: Bone cysts and jaw tumours (odontomas, osteomas, and ossifying fibromas) are found.

Oculoectodermal syndrome is probably part of the ECCL spectrum. In the oculoectodermal syndrome phenotype, lipomas are extremely rare and brain tumours are not reported {17963257}.

Epidemiology

ECCL is rare, with < 90 cases clinically and/or molecularly documented {30143858;26942290;23974968}.

Etiology

ECCL shows the postzygotic somatic activating variants NM_023110.2:c.1638C>A p.(Asn546Lys) and NM_023110.2:c.1966A>G p.(Lys656Glu) in the FGFR1 gene at chromosome 8p11.23 {26942290;30143858;31173478}. Postzygotic KRAS variants have been reported in a subset of patients with the ECCL phenotype {26970110;30891959}.

Pathogenesis

FGFR1 is involved in epithelial–mesenchymal transition during embryogenesis and plays an important role in adipocytic differentiation. Thus, FGFR1 activating variants may be responsible for the adipocytic lesions identified in ECCL. In addition, NM_023110.2:c.1638C>A p.(Asn546Lys) and NM_023110.2:c.1966A>G p.(Lys656Glu) variants in FGFR1 are common in sporadic PA, which has a significantly higher frequency in patients with ECCL carrying these variants, but not in those with KRAS variants. Methylation profiling has demonstrated clustering with midline PA in a subset of brain tumours arising in ECCL {30143858}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features (above) and the related sections in the fifth edition of the WHO Classification of Tumours volumes according to the tumour type.

Histopathology

NP is characterized by abundant adipose tissue extending within the dermis. Mature hair follicles and sebaceous glands are absent or rare, with preserved arrector pili muscles parallel to the epidermis. Hair bulbs in arrested anagen may be seen {9693178}.

There are no specific histological features in PA arising in ECCL. PAs are composed of piloid and oligodendrocyte-like cells showing round to elongated nuclei and scattered multinucleated cells (pennies-on-a-plate pattern). Hyperchromatic and pleomorphic cells may be seen, and mitoses are rare {10068313}. A variable amount of myxoid background is present, with microcystic change and interspersed Rosenthal fibres and eosinophilic granular bodies. The vascular pattern is prominent with glomerular capillaries frequently associated with cysts. Calcifications, hyalinized vessels, and haemorrhages may be seen {25792358}. Cellular components are arranged in different patterns (biphasic, compact piloid, oligodendrocyte-like) according to the combination of the cellular components and stroma. Infarct-like necrosis may be seen. Palisading necrosis is absent.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FGFR1 or KRAS pathogenic variants are postzygotic, mosaic mutations, and thus affected tissues should be used for molecular genetic tests (either multigene DNA panels or exome and genome sequencing).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the clinical diagnosis is based on the involvement of at least three systems, with major criteria in at least two; OR one major criterion if there is biopsy-proven NP; OR a possible NP with at least one other minor skin criterion; see criteria in #38933Table 2.08.

Desirable: demonstration of a mosaic activating pathogenic variant in FGFR1 or KRAS in DNA derived from affected tissue.

 

See #39008Table 2.09.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical behaviour of glial tumours is similar to that of those occurring in non-syndromic patients and is influenced by tumour site and resectability {20881536;30143858;26942290;23974968;30891959}.
