4.8.1 Mosaic variegated aneuploidy (BUB1B, CEP57, TRIP13, BUB1, BUB3)

Definition

Mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA) is a group of autosomal recessive or possibly dominant tumour syndromes caused by pathogenic sequence variants affecting BUB1B (MVA1), CEP57 (MVA2), TRIP13 (MVA3), and provisionally BUB1 and BUB3. MVA is characterized by disruption of proper mitotic chromosome segregation leading to single and multiple whole-chromosome gains and losses (most frequently trisomy and monosomy) that vary between somatic cells and can cause phenotypes such as growth retardation and variable cancer risk.

Clinical features

Clinical features of MVA differ between the three recognized autosomal recessive subtypes of MVA1, MVA2, and MVA3.

Patients with MVA1 generally display severe prenatal and postnatal growth retardation with microcephaly, though at least 1 case of MVA1 has been reported without microcephaly {16059936}. Variable developmental delay, dysmorphic craniofacial features, eye abnormalities, seizures, and other anomalies also occur {15475955;3169747}. A high proportion of reported cases develop at least one early-onset cancer, most commonly rhabdomyosarcoma {9916837}, Wilms tumour {10429359}, and acute lymphoblastic leukaemia {11932988}.

Few cases of MVA2 have been described in the literature. Clinical features most often include marked prenatal growth retardation, distinctive facial features, short stature, and congenital heart defects {34500087}; the phenotype is generally less severe than that of MVA1. Other variable features reported in individuals with MVA2 include hypothyroidism, clinodactyly, rhizomelic shortening of the upper limbs, and additional phenotypes {21552266;24259107}. Importantly, unlike the other MVA subtypes, elevated cancer susceptibility has not been reported.

MVA3 has only 6 reported cases {28553959} and shares many clinical features with the other MVA subtypes, including growth retardation and short stature. Individuals with MVA3 have complete penetrance of cancer susceptibility, with all reported cases developing early-onset Wilms tumour.

The limited patients reported with heterozygous BUB1 or BUB3 alterations had variable presence of mosaic variegated aneuploidies, dysmorphic features, and early-onset colorectal cancer {23747338;29448935}. Additional evidence is needed to prove that variants in BUB1 or BUB3 are associated with cancer predisposition.

Epidemiology

To date, < 100 MVA cases have been described. In one study, BUB1 or BUB3 alterations were found in 2.9% (6 of 208) of patients with familial or early-onset (≤ 40 years) colorectal cancer {23747338}.

Etiology

In the majority of cases, individuals diagnosed with MVA have inherited germline (constitutional) variants in BUB1B, CEP57, or TRIP13 from both parents. These variants are exceedingly rare in the adult population. Individuals with biallelic BUB1B variants (MVA1) have a high risk of cancer, such as rhabdomyosarcoma and Wilms tumour {15475955}. Biallelic TRIP13 variant carriers (MVA3) have a predisposition to Wilms tumour, in some cases without causing other obvious clinical MVA phenotypes {28553959}. Cells from patients with MVA have inaccurate chromosome segregation, resulting in aneuploidy, which predisposes to cancer.

Pathogenesis

Currently, there are at least three types of MVA caused by pathogenic variants in genes required for proper chromosome segregation during mitosis. Pathogenesis of the mitotic disjunction instability has been resolved for roughly half of the diagnosed MVA cases, with biallelic germline variants found in genes that encode regulators of chromosome segregation (BUB1B, CEP57, TRIP13) {15475955;16411201;21552266;35434947;34500087;30010053;24259107;28553959}. The variants cause full or partial loss of function. The majority of resolved cases (~55%) have pathogenic variants in BUB1B. Note that cancer has not been observed in patients with CEP57 variants.

MVA1 is caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) pathogenic variants in BUB1B {15475955}. Monoallelic pathogenic BUB1B variants can result in premature chromatid separation, which is an autosomal dominant trait without phenotypic consequences {16411201;9677059}.

MVA2 is caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in CEP57. A recurrent NM_014679:c.915_925dup p.(Leu309ProfsTer9) variant has been reported in multiple unrelated individuals with MVA2 {21552266;24259107}.

MVA3 is caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in TRIP13. A recurrent NM_004237.4:c.1060C>T p.(Arg354Ter) variant has been reported in multiple unrelated individuals with MVA3 {28553959}.

Of note, recently, biallelic variants in CENATAC (CCDC84) were identified in two siblings with germline mosaic aneuploidy, but without known tumour development. CENATAC is a novel component of the minor (U12-dependent) spliceosome {34009673}.

One study has proposed an autosomal dominant form of MVA due to haploinsufficiency of BUB1 or heterozygous missense variants in BUB3 to be associated with early-onset colorectal cancer predisposition; the evidence is very limited and one of the reported BUB1 alteration carriers has a known pathogenic MLH1 variant {23747338}. BUB1 acts as a scaffold for the assembly of the mitotic checkpoint complex at the kinetochore, which includes BUB3 {11402067}. Additional population screening and a systematic review show a paucity of segregation data. This, combined with the presence of disruptive variants in these genes in normal controls, indicates that the evidence for an association of BUB1 and BUB3 alterations with colorectal cancer risk has yet to be established {27713038;29448935}. Of note, biallelic germline BUB1 pathogenic variants, resulting in variable degrees of reduced total protein level and kinase activity, were recently reported in two individuals with microcephaly, intellectual disability, and variable other features with or without aneuploidies by karyotype; the patients are approximately 3 and 16 years old without tumour development, requiring continued assessment for cancer risk {35044816}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Karyotype analysis from an individual with MVA will demonstrate extensive mosaicism, with aneuploidies in 25–50% of cells and the specific aneuploidies differing from cell to cell. The detection of biallelic (compound heterozygous or homozygous) pathogenic variants in BUB1B, CEP57, or TRIP13 (or provisionally CENATAC) by gene sequencing and deletion/duplication analysis can confirm the diagnosis. The paucity of established cancer-predisposing BUB1 and BUB3 variants does not support using diagnostic germline testing of these genes in familial colorectal cancer {29448935}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria for MVA have not been established. Biallelic germline pathogenic variants in BUB1B (MVA1), CEP57 (MVA2), or TRIP13 (MVA3) confirms the molecular diagnosis.

Staging

Staging is as for sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

The penetrance for MVA is presumed to be high but has not yet been specifically determined and may vary by gene alteration. For example, TRIP13 biallelic variants appear to confer a high risk of Wilms tumour but have a more-variable presentation for MVA {28553959}. There is substantial clinical variability among individuals with MVA, and the prognosis depends on the severity of the presentation and whether the individual develops a malignancy. Wilms tumour surveillance with renal ultrasonography for children < 5 years of age should be considered.
