3.2.4 CDK4-related melanoma predisposition syndrome (CDK4)

Definition

CDK4-related melanoma predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in the CDK4 gene. The syndrome is associated with an increased risk of multiple primary melanomas, and also non-cutaneous malignancies.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CDK4 mutation–associated melanomas are more likely than CDKN2A-related melanomas to occur on the limbs {23384855;31326397}.

Clinical features

CDK4-related melanoma predisposition syndrome is characterized by cutaneous melanomas and a higher number of atypical naevi, generally with an earlier onset of the disease compared with the general population, as reported in a study based on a White population {23384855}.

Additionally, CDK4 pathogenic variant carriers may be at higher risk of developing non-cutaneous malignancies. Associated tumours include breast, pancreatic, ovarian, cervical, and stomach cancers {32249949}, with an onset similar to that of sporadic cases {34442055}. Cutaneous carcinomas, lung cancer, prostate cancer, lymphomas {30190834}, and salivary gland cancer {36631500} have also been reported.

Epidemiology

CDK4 germline variants are very rare, with < 20 families reported in the literature {29124743;31326397}. The prevalence of germline CDK4 pathogenic variants in melanoma-prone families is < 1% {24686849;33322357}.

One study demonstrated a greater onset of melanoma in the fourth decade of life (with a range of 18–86 years) {32249949} and a similar median age of first diagnosis among male and female patients harbouring the CDK4 variants, with an estimated lifetime penetrance of 74% {23384855}.

Etiology

Similarly to CDKN2A pathogenic variants, CDK4 pathogenic variants predispose to early-onset multiple primary melanomas and increased numbers of atypical naevi {32249949}.

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation probably contributes to melanoma tumorigenesis in patients with germline CDK4 pathogenic variants, given the higher incidence of tumours occurring at non-truncal sites {32283231;31326397}.

Pathogenesis

CDK4 is an oncogene located at chromosome 12q14.1. It encodes a serine/threonine kinase and, when mutated, inhibits binding of p16, leading to phosphorylation of RB1 and cell-cycle progression. The result of such monophosphorylation is the release of the transcription factor E2F, which triggers the transcription of the cyclin E gene, CCNE1, and its binding to CDK2. The new cyclin E / CDK2 complex additionally hyperphosphorylates the RB1 protein at other serine and threonine phosphorylation sites and facilitates the progression of the cell cycle {9315635;9003781}.

Most sequence variants in CDK4 have been reported at codon 24 of exon 2, NM_000075.4:c.71G>A p.(Arg24His). This substitution results in a defective p16 binding domain, leading to progression of the cell cycle {23384855}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic features of CDK4-related melanoma predisposition syndrome tumours are similar to those of their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

In situ and invasive melanoma cases from CDK4-variant carriers have been reported to be histologically similar to tumours from non-carriers {32283231}.

The most common histological tumour type is low–cumulative sun damage melanoma (superficial spreading melanoma) {23384855}. A longitudinal study demonstrated that a precursor naevus, either intradermal or dysplastic naevus, is often present {12115352}. Melanocytes, sometimes heavily pigmented, are arranged as single cells and nests with upward scatter of single cells, and associated with epidermal hyperplasia {32249949}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Sequencing analysis of the CDK4 gene in a germline DNA sample assessing pathogenic germline variants is required for diagnosis.

Notably, there are no clinical phenotypic differences between families with CDKN2A germline variants and those with CDK4 germline variants, with similar early onset of disease, increased occurrence of clinically atypical naevi, and development of multiple primary melanomas {23384855}. As such, the CDK4 gene should be examined when a melanoma-prone family tests negative for CDKN2A mutation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: germline pathogenic variant in the CDK4 gene.

Desirable: personal and/or family history of multiple melanoma, atypical/dysplastic naevi and characteristic neoplasms.

Staging

Melanomas and other malignancies arising in the setting of a CDK4-related melanoma predisposition syndrome are staged as their sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

As for patients with CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome, participation in a programme providing skin cancer screening and education about skin self-exams has been associated with thinner (earlier stage) melanomas in members of melanoma-prone families {33811164}.
