4.7.4 DDX41-related haematological tumour predisposition syndrome (DDX41)

Definition

DDX41-related haematological tumour predisposition syndrome is a cancer predisposition disorder following an autosomal dominant trait and caused by pathogenic germline variants in the DDX41 gene, characterized by an increased risk of haematolymphoid neoplasms.

MIM numbering

616871 Myeloproliferative/lymphoproliferative neoplasms, familial (multiple types), susceptibility to; MPLPF

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Not recommended: familial myeloproliferative/lymphoproliferative neoplasms.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Neoplasms associated with germline pathogenic variants in the DDX41 gene usually involve the bone marrow and, less commonly, lymph nodes.

Clinical features

Germline DDX41 pathogenic variants are usually heterozygous and are passed on from parent to child in an autosomal dominant manner. Penetrance is age and sex dependent. The lifelong risk of developing myeloid neoplasia from pathogenic (P) or likely pathogenic (LP) variants is approximately 50% starting at 40 years of age {36322930}. Male carriers have nearly double the risk of developing myeloid neoplasia compared with female carriers {35671390;34644397;31484648;36322930}. In addition to increased risk of early presentation clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance {33626862;35671390}, carriers may develop acute myeloid leukaemia, myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS), myeloproliferative neoplasm, and/or myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm {35671390;36322930}. Myeloid malignancies often show acquisition of a DDX41 variant, most commonly NM_016222.4:c.1574G>A p.(Arg525His), on the non–germline mutated allele {36322930;36455200}. Some patients may develop B-cell lymphoma/leukaemia or plasma cell myeloma, albeit these associations seem uncommon {26712909;33585199;35671390}.

Epidemiology

Germline DDX41 pathogenic variants are the most common cause yet known for genetic predisposition to myeloid neoplasms, identified in 1.5–3.8% of such patients {25920683;31484648;30963592;36322930}.

Certain pathogenic / likely pathogenic germline variants of DDX41 are more common in particular populations, such as NM_016222.4:c.1496dup p.(Ala500fs) in Japanese/Korean individuals and NM_016222.4:c.3G>A p.(M1?) and NM_016222.4:c.415_418dup p.(Asp140delinsGly*) in those of Northern European descent {36322930;36455200}. Because of the high frequency of P/LP germline variants in populations, they are seen in general population databases such as the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD) and UK Biobank.

Etiology

The DDX41 gene is located at chromosome 5q35.3 and encodes an ATP-dependent RNA helicase involved in various aspects of RNA metabolism, including pre-mRNA splicing, RNA processing, ribosome biogenesis, and small nucleolar RNA processing {25920683;27174803;34473945}. DDX41 is expressed in haematopoietic cells. It is required for haematopoietic stem cell differentiation in mice, where its knockdown impairs myeloid cell differentiation {35303436}. In another study, knockdown of DDX41 increased proliferative capacity and sensitivity of CD34+ haematopoietic stem cells to growth factors and caused defective differentiation {25920683}. In addition to their impact on RNA metabolism, P/LP DDX41 variants appear to impair immunity through their impact on the stimulator of interferon genes (STING) pathway {25704810;21892174}. Another proposed pathogenic mechanism entails increased levels of double-strand breaks in human haematopoietic stem cells resulting in upregulation of inflammatory responses {34916496;33651979}.

Pathogenesis

Although the precise mechanism by which P/LP germline DDX41 variants drive haematopoietic malignancies is incompletely understood, it is likely that the protein’s roles interacting with double-stranded DNA and RNA:DNA hybrids as a member of the DEAD-box RNA helicase family contribute to the pathogenesis of neoplasms {36455200}. DDX41 is found in both the cytoplasm and the nucleus, where it has a variety of functions, including as a splicing regulator, in ribosomal RNA and small nucleolar RNA processing, and within the innate immunity pathway. DDX41 is a pattern recognition receptor that is stimulated by DNA-type pathogen-associated molecular patterns, specifically bacterial cyclic dinucleotides, viral double-stranded DNA, and RNA:DNA hybrids, to drive the type I interferon signalling cascade, acting as a stimulator of interferon genes (STING1, also known as TMEM173, MITA, MPYS, or ERIS), TBK1, IRF3, and NF-κB.

Not relevant

Histopathology

Haematolymphoid neoplasms arising in individuals carrying pathogenic DDX41 germline variants have no known distinctive morphological features except increased likelihood of bone marrow hypocellularity and erythroid dysplasia {33626862;30963592}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

P/LP germline DDX41 variants are increasingly detected in index patients by somatic mutation profiling using next-generation sequencing during workup for myeloid neoplasia. Confirmation requires germline DNA evaluation. Importantly, germline deletion variants are observed, which may not be detected by panel-based molecular profiling tests. Therefore, germline genetic testing for pathogenic variants of DDX41 should be performed when warranted by the personal/family history and molecular profiling.

P/LP germline DDX41 variants are typically heterozygous and include frameshift, nonsense, or splice-site variants {36322930;26712909;25920683}. The frequency of germline variants differs across various populations {33626862;36322930;35671390;32307695}. Somatic mutations are usually missense trans (heterozygous) variants and tend to affect the helicase 2 ATP binding domain {25920683;27174803}. The most common somatic mutation is p.Arg525His {36322930}. Germline and somatic truncating mutations are mutually exclusive.

Common somatic variants in myeloid neoplasms arising in DDX41 pathogenic variant carriers affect ASXL1 and TP53, followed by TET2, EZH2, RUNX1, SRSF2, and DNMT3A {25920683;30963592;31484648;35671390;36322930}.

First-degree relatives of DDX41 P/LP variant carriers should be counselled for genetic testing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: detection of a P/LP germline DDX41 variant.

Desirable: detection of P/LP germline DDX41 variant at a variant allele frequency consistent with germline status from DNA derived from cultured skin fibroblasts; or via segregation in other family members.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognostic impact of germline P/LP DDX41 variants is unclear, as associated outcomes have differed in various studies {25920683;33626862;31484648;35671390;34671111;35443031;36322930}. Truncating germline DDX41 variants in patients who develop MDS are associated with faster transformation to acute myeloid leukaemia compared with patients with non-truncating germline variants, but no difference in overall survival is observed between these groups {36322930}. However, as a group, patients with MDS associated with germline DDX41 P/LP variants tend to have better response to hypomethylating agents and better outcomes compared with those with wildtype DDX41, independent of risk stratification by the revised international prognostic scoring system {22740453} or TP53 mutation status {36322930}.
