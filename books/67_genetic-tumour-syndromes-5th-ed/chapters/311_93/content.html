Definition

Li–Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by pathogenic variants of the TP53 tumour suppressor gene at chromosome 17p13.1 and characterized by multiple primary neoplasms in children and adults, with a predominance of soft tissue sarcomas, osteosarcoma, breast cancer, brain tumours, and adrenocortical carcinoma.

MIM numbering

151623 Li–Fraumeni syndrome; LFS

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Not recommended: p53-associated Li–Fraumeni–like syndrome; sarcoma family syndrome of Li and Fraumeni.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Malignant tumours can occur in all organs of the body. Breast, soft tissue, bone, brain, and adrenal cortex are most commonly involved {29076966;1978757;7887414;9242456;11219776;21837677;34780712}.

Clinical features

LFS is characterized by early onset (often in children and young adults) and a high lifetime risk of a broad spectrum of multiple primary cancers (see #37310Table 4.01). In addition to the core tumours listed above, a broad range of other tumours has been reported in families with LFS, including but not limited to leukaemia, lymphoma, and melanoma, as well as cancers of the gastrointestinal tract (gastric and colorectal carcinomas), lung, female reproductive organs (ovarian and endometrial carcinomas), urogenital tract (carcinomas of the kidney and the prostate), and eyes {19204208;24706533;26101242;14583457;28509937;32179180;35875466}. Hepatoblastoma, neuroblastoma, germ cell tumour, hepatocellular carcinoma, and thyroid and other cancers have also been reported.

Traditionally, Chompret criteria have been used to identify individuals with an indication for genetic counselling and TP53 germline testing. With the increasing use of next-generation sequencing, it has become evident that the clinical spectrum is broader and many TP53 pathogenic / likely pathogenic variant carriers do not meet traditional testing criteria. Newer indications include osteosarcoma {32191290;33163847}, hypodiploid acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, and sonic hedgehog medulloblastoma {32899294}.

The various phases of life are associated with different tumours: childhood phase with adrenocortical carcinoma, choroid plexus carcinoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and medulloblastoma; childhood to young adulthood transition phase with osteosarcoma, leukaemia, and various glioma subtypes; early adulthood phase with breast, gastrointestinal, and lung cancers and different sarcomas {21837677}; and late adulthood phase with pancreatic, prostate, and lung cancer {29076966}. There is a wide range of age of onset for soft tissue sarcomas {21837677;26014290}.

Epidemiology

TP53 germline (constitutional) variants have been estimated to occur at a rate of about 1 in 500 to 1 in 5000 births, and account for as many as 17% of all familial cancer cases {19204208;27328919;28861920;30352134}. In a recent meta-analysis, TP53 is mutated in the germline of 1.4% of studied childhood and adolescent patients diagnosed with cancer {35980168}. There is a variant-dependent gradient of phenotype severity, with the most functionally severe variants associated with early tumour onset {29529297;32966936;34780712;35974385;30997946}. Individuals with LFS have a nearly 24-fold higher incidence of any cancer than the general population, with the highest comparative incidence from childhood to 30 years of age. The overall cancer incidence remains 10.3-fold higher than that of the general population after age 50 years, primarily driven by secondary or subsequent tumours during this time period {34780712}. Tumour patterns are generally stable regardless of geographical or population demographics, with the only notable exceptions being an excess of gastric cancers in South-East Asia {25318593}, soft tissue sarcomas in the Western Pacific {30997946}, and a low-penetrance variant NM_000546.6:c.1000G>C p.(Gly334Arg) or other substitution at that position in patients of Ashkenazi Jewish descent {32675277}. The NM_000546.6:c.1010G>A p.(Arg337His) founder variant is detected in up to 0.3% of the population of south-eastern Brazil and is associated with adrenocortical carcinoma, early onset of invasive breast carcinoma, and other cancer types {16494995;18248785;23733769;30974190}.

Etiology

LFS is an autosomal dominant disorder mainly caused by pathogenic variants of the TP53 tumour suppressor gene at chromosome 17p13.1 (point mutations, rearrangements, or indels) and characterized by a high, lifelong risk of a broad spectrum of often multiple cancers {26014290;1565144}.

The spectrum encompasses (1) phenotypic LFS, (2) LFS, (3) attenuated LFS, and (4) incidental LFS (#37310Table 4.01) {34709361}. Within the spectrum, LFS, attenuated LFS, and incidental LFS are also called heritable TP53-related cancer syndromes {32457520}.

Pathogenesis

The p53 protein is a multifunctional transcription factor involved in a wide range of biological processes {32404993;28886379}. Its best-characterized functions are in the control of cell-cycle progression, DNA integrity, evolution of genomic alterations in the context of tumour progression {35978189}, and the survival of cells exposed to DNA-damaging agents. Evidence indicates that p53 also regulates other important processes, such as cell oxidative metabolism, the cellular response to nutrient deprivation, fertility, ferroptosis, and stem cell maintenance. The extent and consequences of the biological response elicited by p53 vary according to stress, the specific mutation conferred {28948977}, and cell type {28886379}. The functions of p53 rely mainly on its transcriptional activity, but it can also act via interactions with various proteins {21941372;30525089}.

In LFS-associated tumours, TP53 is usually inactivated by a second hit involving the wildtype allele. Mutant p53 isoforms differ from each other in the extent to which they have lost suppressor function and in their capacity to inhibit wildtype p53 in a dominant negative manner {12826609;17311302}. In addition, some p53 mutants seem to exert oncogenic activity, but the molecular basis of this gain-of-function phenotype is still unclear {24651012;30224644}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of tumours in LFS is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Breast cancer is by far the most frequent malignancy associated with LFS, accounting for approximately 30% of cases in the TP53 Database, which has been recently moved from the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) to the US National Cancer Institute (NCI) [[The TP53 Database Internet. Bethesda (Maryland): National Cancer Institute (NCI) of the National Institutes of Health; 2019. Version R20, July 2019. Available from: https://tp53.isb-cgc.org. Bookref]] {27328919;35352025}. There is limited evidence on the histopathological and molecular types of LFS-associated breast cancer but most invasive carcinomas seem to be of no special type (NST) and the ERBB2-amplified molecular subtype (both classic and variant) predominates {21761402;20805372}. Malignant phyllodes tumours of the breast may also be associated with LFS {11498785}. The most frequent types of soft tissue sarcomas have been found to be leiomyosarcomas, rhabdomyosarcomas (mostly embryonal but often with anaplastic features) {24382691;32658383}, liposarcomas, and undifferentiated sarcomas that can be challenging to classify. Osteosarcoma is the most frequent bone tumour. The most frequent brain tumours are diffuse astrocytic gliomas (IDH-mutant and IDH-wildtype forms), choroid plexus carcinomas, and medulloblastomas (sonic hedgehog–activated and TP53-mutant type) {22265402}. Adrenocortical carcinomas account for 10% of LFS-associated cancers {25584008;26014290}. Low-hypodiploid acute lymphoblastic leukaemia with typical histopathological findings also commonly has TP53 germline alterations {23334668}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Candidates for TP53 germline testing are identified on the basis of the 2015 revised Chompret criteria {26014290} and the recommendations of the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) (#37309Box 4.01) {30333936}. In the context of a clinical suspicion, genetic testing for TP53 germline variants by next-generation sequencing is necessary {28509937}. TP53 should be considered in addition to BRCA1, BRCA2, and other genes for germline panel testing of female patients with breast cancer before the age of 45 {30086788;29753700;32191290}. The ClinGen TP53 Variant Curation Expert Panel recommends the use of the TP53-specific American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) / Association for Molecular Pathology (AMP) guidelines as the standard strategy for TP53 germline variant classification {33300245}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pathogenic or likely pathogenic germline variant in TP53 and/or appropriate family history.

Staging

Staging is as for sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

The specific cancer risk varies by age, being < 20% at ≤ 15 years, > 25% between 16 and 45 years, and > 50% at > 45 years, with differences between male and female patients {16912210}. Subsequent cancers tend to occur with shorter intervals after the first cancer {28859040;26029016;33971750;31719099}. For surveillance of germline pathogenic / likely pathogenic variant carriers, a combination of physical exams, blood tests, and imaging (including whole body MRI) is recommended {27501770;28772291;28572266;32814850}. LFS seems to confer an elevated risk of radiation-induced secondary malignancies and possibly increased sensitivity to low-dose radiation exposure from diagnostic tests {23355100} depending on many factors, including the type of mutation, the field of radiation, the radiation dose, and the concomitant use of other agents {30072235}. Secondary malignancies may be also related to chemotherapy {30072235}.

Classic Li–Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) criteria {3409256; 29039119}

A proband with:

·        A sarcoma diagnosed before the age of 45 years AND

·        A first-degree relative with any cancer before the age of 45 years AND

·        A first- or second-degree relative with any cancer before the age of 45 years or a sarcoma

Revised Chompret criteria {11332399; 18511570; 19652052; 34709361}

A proband with:

1.

A tumour belonging to the LFS tumour spectrum (soft tissue sarcoma, osteosarcoma, premenopausal breast cancer, brain tumour, adrenocortical carcinoma, leukaemia, or bronchoalveolar lung cancer) before the age of 46 years AND at least one first- or second-degree relative with an LFS tumour (except breast cancer if the proband has breast cancer) before the age of 56 years or with multiple tumours

OR

2.

Multiple tumours (except multiple breast tumours), two of which belong to the LFS tumour spectrum and the first of which occurred before the age of 46 years

OR

3.

Adrenocortical carcinoma or choroid plexus tumour, or anaplastic rhabdomyosarcoma of embryonal subtype, or osteosarcoma, or childhood hypodiploid acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, or sonic hedgehog medulloblastoma irrespective of family history

OR

4.

Breast cancer before the age of 31 years

Diagnosis

Essential diagnostic criteria

Phenotypic LFS

No P/LP germline (or mosaic) TP53 variant AND no other genetic explanation AND meeting classic LFS criteria or at least one of the first two Chompret criteria

LFS

P/LP germline (or mosaic) TP53 variant AND meeting LFS testing criteria AND/OR any cancer before age 18 years

LFS carrier

P/LP germline TP53 variant AND no (history of) cancer AND LFS in family

Attenuated LFS

P/LP germline (or mosaic) TP53 variant AND (history of) cancer but not meeting LFS testing criteria AND no cancer before age 18 years

Attenuated LFS carrier

P/LP germline TP53 variant AND no (history of) cancer AND attenuated LFS in family

Incidental LFS

P/LP germline (or mosaic) TP53 variant AND no (history of) cancer AND no LFS or attenuated LFS in family
