7.1.1 DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome (DICER1)

Definition

DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndrome caused by heterozygous germline (constitutional) pathogenic sequence variants in DICER1, which encodes a microRNA processing enzyme. It is characterized by increased incidence of benign and malignant neoplasms involving multiple organ systems, including pleuropulmonary blastoma, thyroid cancers, paediatric cystic nephroma, embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma of the uterine cervix, ovarian Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour, pineoblastoma, embryonal tumour with multilayered rosettes, pituitary blastoma, and primary intracranial sarcoma.

Localization

Tumours are typically organ-based and can arise at many sites including the brain, eye, nasal passages, thyroid, lung, liver, gastrointestinal tract, peritoneum, and genitourinary tract (predominantly ovary, fallopian tube, and uterine cervix {31342592;34599283;35778792}, with single reports of involvement of the testes and paratestes in male patients {32222066;33782093}). Loss-of-function (LOF) germline variants usually lead to involvement of one or two characteristic sites (see #37509Table 8.01) from childhood through to early adulthood. LOF mosaic variants tend to result in single tumours, whereas a germline or mosaic missense variant located in the RNase IIIb (and rarely the RNase IIIa) domain of DICER1 often leads to multiple early-onset tumours {26925222}. The location and numbers of tumours probably depends on the developmental stage and cell type in which the somatic RNase IIIb so-called second hit occurs {34544839}. Bilateral disease in paired organs can occur {31893257;32333409}.

Clinical features

Germline pathogenic DICER1 variants are associated with increased risk for close to 30 distinct tumours and hyperplastic conditions (see #37509Table 8.01 and summarized in de Kock et al. and González et al. {31342592;34599283}). Multiple primary tumours are not uncommon. Tumours are most commonly organ-based and may arise during development and present in early childhood. Tumours of endocrine organs present across a wider age range. While pleuropulmonary blastoma (PPB) is the hallmark lesion of DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome, manifestations that affect the endocrine system are probably the most frequently observed; these include thyroid nodules, differentiated thyroid carcinoma, and possibly poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma and thyroblastoma (these latter tumours have been reported to occur in the setting of biallelic somatic [i.e. tumour-restricted] DICER1 pathogenic variants {31937902;36069850}). Cystic tumours affecting the lung (PPB type I) and kidney (paediatric cystic nephroma) can progress to PPB type II/III and anaplastic sarcoma, respectively {24481001;27036314}, which require intensive chemotherapy and have poor prognoses. Cysts pathologically similar to PPB type I have also been reported in the liver {31067372;34907324}. Ovarian Sertoli–Leydig cell tumours (SLCTs) commonly present with symptoms of androgen production {30715996}. Ovarian tumours with the morphology of gynandroblastoma and juvenile granulosa cell tumour, and testicular Sertoli cell tumour have also been reported {29660837;32910017;33782093}. Pituitary blastoma is associated with Cushing disease in infants {33236116;21805093;24839956} but may also occur in young adults {34677675}. Other than pituitary blastomas, CNS manifestations include primary intracranial sarcoma, DICER1-mutant, pineoblastoma, embryonal tumour with multilayered rosettes, and ciliary body medulloepithelioma. Cerebral metastases from PPB are probably the most frequent CNS manifestation {30953130}. Female patients are overrepresented in DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome pedigrees because of the preponderance of thyroid and gynaecological manifestations, for the latter most notably ovarian SLCT and uterine cervical embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS). Non-neoplastic manifestations may include macrocephaly {27441995} and subtle changes affecting the eye {30339877}, kidney {30178239}, and teeth {31313479}, although function of these organs is nearly always normal.

While the more common germline LOF variants are not associated with developmental problems, global developmental delay and/or somatic overgrowth has been associated with some mosaic RNase IIIb hotspot variants {24676357}, and a similar phenotype has been reported in two individuals with germline {33208384} or likely germline {34331184} RNase IIIa hotspot variants. Notably, the single RNase IIIa variant reported functionally behaves as an RNase IIIb variant {31342592;31417090}. The GLOW phenotype (global developmental delay, lung cysts, overgrowth, and Wilms tumour) associated with mosaic DICER1 RNase IIIb hotspot sequence variants is insufficiently distinct from other manifestations of mosaicism for RNase IIIb variants to warrant use of this term.

Epidemiology

Putative LOF DICER1 sequence variants are present in 1 in 3700 to 1 in 4600 individuals {33630087}. About 84% of germline pathogenic variants are inherited from a parent {26925222}. Penetrance is difficult to determine as small benign lesions may not present clinically and rare tumours are not catalogued nationally/internationally. Benign tumours (some of which are hamartomas) appear more commonly than malignant tumours. Most tumours present by 20 years of age although tumours in the thyroid and female genital tract also occur in older patients. In an unbiased sequencing study, the penetrance of malignant tumours in DICER1 heterozygotes was 16% by age 50 years {33630087}. Benign thyroid neoplasms occur in three quarters of female carriers and a fifth of male carriers by age 40 years {28323992}. The risk of thyroid carcinoma is estimated to be increased at least 10-fold {33630087;28323992}. Lung cysts (presumptively considered PPB type Ir) are detectable by CT in 25% of patients with germline pathogenic variants {26925222}. Penetrance for the other clinically evident manifestations appears to be much lower.

Etiology

DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome is caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in DICER1, located at chromosome 14q32 {19556464;25176334}.

Pathogenesis

DICER1 is an enzyme that has both cytoplasmic and nuclear functions. It is responsible for cleaving the hairpin-structured precursor microRNA to 5′ and 3′ single-stranded mature microRNAs. Either a 5′ or a 3′ mature microRNA is loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex and subsequently binds mRNAs in a sequence-specific manner to negatively affect gene expression. DICER1-related tumours typically possess one germline LOF variant coupled with a missense variant in trans, the latter affecting one of five metal ion-binding residues within the RNase IIIb domain. These RNase IIIb missense mutants fail to cleave the mature microRNA (the 5′ microRNA) from the 5′ end of the hairpin pre-microRNA and are rapidly degraded {23132766;24909177}. The lack of 5′ microRNAs leads to overexpression of their target mRNAs, which in the proper cellular context (i.e. embryonic growth, endocrine stimulation) can lead to hyperplasia/neoplasia. The lack of 5′ microRNAs also results in an altered 5′ to 3′ microRNA ratio {23132766;24909177;33644822}. Studies using DNA methylation profiling have revealed a common DNA methylation signature in DICER1-associated high-grade malignancies {29881993;33846547;35779311}.

Macroscopic appearance

Neoplasms can be cystic, cystic and solid, or purely solid with similar appearances to sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Key histopathological features of the major DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome tumours are described in #37510Table 8.02. The syndrome has such rich and diverse pathological manifestations that it is challenging to summarize them in a few sentences; however, tumours associated with DICER1 pathogenic variants have highly characteristic features, which when recognized by the pathologist can help guide the need for genetic counselling and testing.

The common histological patterns shared among multiple DICER1 tumours include the following.

  • Morphology that resembles an embryonal stage of development with blastemal components (poorly differentiated primitive cells), seen in PPB, anaplastic sarcoma of the kidney (ASK), ERMS, pituitary blastoma, thyroblastoma, pineoblastoma, embryonal tumour with multilayered rosettes, ciliary body medulloepithelioma, and Wilms tumour. Like other embryonal tumours of childhood, these tumours resemble the organ at an early stage of development, being composed of primitive cells that are multipotent and rapidly proliferating.
  • Multiple histological patterns suggesting the tumours arise from pluripotent cells. These include components of blastema, rhabdomyosarcomatous and fibrosarcomatous elements, and cartilage in PPB, uterine cervical ERMS, primary intracranial sarcoma, and ASK; and Sertoli tubules, Leydig cells, and heterologous mucinous epithelium or rhabdomyosarcoma as well as areas resembling juvenile granulosa cell tumour (which may result in a diagnosis of gynandroblastoma) in SLCT; sometimes neuroectodermal elements are present {35245609;31296931}.
  • Cystic and/or cystic and solid components seen in PPB, cystic nephroma / ASK, thyroid nodules, nasal chondromesenchymal hamartoma, SLCT, ERMS, and others, perhaps reflecting an epithelial to mesenchymal signalling relationship that is not fully understood {25118636;25500911;25978641}.
  • Biological and pathological progression from a hyperplastic, or precancerous, stage to aggressive sarcoma best described in PPB, cystic nephroma / ASK, and thyroid carcinoma {9210721;24481001}. In PPB, ASK, and occasionally SLCT, tumour progression is characterized by overgrowth of more primitive elements of the tumour, often with anaplasia. Anaplasia correlates with variants and/or loss in TP53 {33637876}.

    • The histopathological similarities between lesions in different organs with different names have resulted in a proposal to recognize organ-based sarcomas with similar morphological features as a single entity (DICER1-associated sarcoma) and to harmonize the nomenclature for lesions that fall into this DICER1 tumour group {31537896;33825038;34642426;35779311;32572152}.

    Cytology

    Owing to the risk of thyroid neoplasia, thyroid nodules often undergo FNAB {28323992;25900731}.

    Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

    Diagnostic molecular pathology

    Most germline pathogenic variants in individuals with DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome are nonsense or frameshift variants. These germline pathogenic variants are broadly distributed across the gene and frequently not identified by panels designed to identify the somatic mutations, which cluster around the RNase IIIb domain. Pathogenic missense variants and larger deletions are seen in a small percentage {31342592;29315962;30597651}. Identification of a typical RNase IIIb sequence variant is pathognomonic of this group of tumours with rare exceptions such as pineoblastoma {31342592;32124011}.

    Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

    Essential: demonstration of a germline DICER1 pathogenic variant.

    Desirable: personal or family history of pathologically confirmed syndrome-related lesions, particularly those with high specificity for DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome.

    Staging

    Staging of individual tumours varies by age and site. Gynaecological neoplasms can be staged using Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) systems. Paediatric renal tumours use the Children’s Oncology Group (COG) staging system. Rhabdomyosarcomas use modified TNM staging and grouping. PPBs are classified by type rather than stage with evaluation for the presence of metastatic disease in types II and III.

    Prognosis and prediction

    Outcome is generally favourable after a diagnosis of benign thyroid nodules, differentiated thyroid carcinoma, SLCT, cystic nephroma, uterine cervical ERMS, nasal chondromesenchymal hamartoma, and ciliary body medulloepithelioma. The prognosis is worse for poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma than for differentiated thyroid carcinoma {31937902}. PPB types II and III, pituitary blastoma, ASK, peritoneal sarcomas, and primary intracranial sarcomas can have survival rates as low as 50% {17895746;25209242;33236116;34674226}.

    Other sites

    In subepithelial locations such as the uterine cervix, primitive rhabdomyoblastic cells form a subepithelial layer, i.e. cambium layer, and grossly the tumour typically has a multinodular, botryoid (grape-like) appearance. Larger, more invasive tumours are composed of hypocellular and hypercellular areas containing spindle, stellate, and ovoid-shaped rhabdomyoblasts in a pale-staining oedematous to myxoid matrix. Variable degrees of visible skeletal muscle differentiation may be present with cytoplasmic cross striations. Nodules of cartilage may also be present.

    Cells within cellular areas are desmin+ with variable numbers of cells showing nuclear staining for myogenin and myoD1
