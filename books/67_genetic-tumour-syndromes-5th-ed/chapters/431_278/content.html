4.3.1 MUTYH-associated polyposis (MUTYH)

Definition

MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP) is a germline (constitutional) DNA repair disorder of base excision repair, caused by autosomal recessively inherited pathogenic variants affecting MUTYH, characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps and an increased risk of colorectal cancer, together with other extracolonic neoplasms.

MIM numbering

608456 MUTYH-associated polyposis; FAP2

ICD-11 coding

2B90.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of colon

Related terminology

Not recommended: familial adenomatous polyposis 2.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adenomas and adenocarcinomas occur in the colorectum and duodenum {16042573;17161978;19414147}. Sebaceous neoplasms occur in the skin, and MAP is one of the causes of Muir–Torre syndrome (see section Muir–Torre syndrome).

Clinical features

Patients with MAP develop multiple adenomatous polyps of the large bowel in adulthood; the number of adenomas is typically 10–100, but rare individuals without polyps have been reported {16557584}. MAP accounts for 15–40% of cases in which a patient has 10–99 adenomas, and for about 15% of cases in which > 100 adenomas are present. About two thirds of patients with MAP develop colorectal cancer, and MAP accounts for 0.3–0.8% of all colorectal cancer cases {15931596;21846783}. The number of adenomas does not correlate with the risk of developing adenocarcinoma, which may indicate that pathways other than conventional adenoma–carcinoma progression may be involved. This may also suggest that adenomas do not necessarily grow or progress more rapidly to cancer in the setting of MAP {21846783}. Duodenal polyposis is observed in about 20% of cases, with a concomitant increased risk of duodenal adenocarcinoma {16557584;33130102}. Sebaceous skin tumours have also been described, indicating that MAP is a cause of Muir–Torre syndrome. The risk of malignancies of the ovary and bladder is also increased and other tumour predispositions are being investigated {33419231;33343895;23035301;32821650;35422474}. There is an increased risk of developing colorectal cancer in heterozygous carriers of monoallelic MUTYH variants {20663686;31094179;32362383;33059073;35422474}.

Epidemiology

The prevalence of MAP is approximately 1 in 2000 {27799157}. Several population-specific founder variants in NM_001048174.2 MUTYH have been identified: p.Tyr179Cys and p.Gly396Asp in northern Europeans, p.Glu480del in southern Europeans, p.Tyr104* in Pakistanis, and p.Glu480* in Indians.

Etiology

MAP is caused by germline biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) inactivating variants in MUTYH {17161978}.

Pathogenesis

Biallelic variants in the MUTYH gene inactivate the MUTYH protein, a DNA glycosylase enzyme that excises adenines misincorporated opposite 8-oxo-7,8-dihydro-2′-deoxyguanosine (8-oxoguanine), which is a stable product of oxidative DNA damage; 8-oxoguanine mispairs with adenine instead of cytosine {19414147;28087410;32307808;34142156}. Inactivation of both alleles of MUTYH inactivates this repair mechanism, leading to an increased mutation rate with predominantly G>T transversions. MUTYH functionally cooperates with OGG1, which eliminates 8-oxo-7,8-dihydro-2′-deoxyguanosine derived from excessive reactive oxygen species production {32821650}. MUTYH deficiency appears to have different impacts on various tissues according to the degree of oxidative stress {31505270}. Adenomas arise as a result of the acquisition of somatic APC mutations {12606733;23599153}. Somatic hotspot mutations in KRAS (NM_004985.5:c.34G>T p.(Gly12Cys)) and PIK3CA (NM_006218.4:c.1636C>A p.(GlnQ546Lys)) are associated with colorectal cancers in biallelic MUTYH-variant carriers {35668106}.

Macroscopic appearance

The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in MAP are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in MAP are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in MAP have characteristic G>T somatic mutation signatures (primarily SBS36, and also the oxidative DNA damage mutation signature SBS18 may be valuable) that indicate inactivation of MUTYH function {32025018;32025007;35668106}. As a result they may be more likely to have some otherwise uncommon somatic KRAS variants, such as NM_004985.5:c.34G>T p.(Gly12Cys); these rare pathogenic KRAS variants should therefore arouse suspicion of MAP. MAP may also be a cause of Lynch-like syndrome: the association with a family history, the relatively young onset of colorectal cancers (which may have acquired somatic mutations in mismatch repair genes causing microsatellite instability and mismatch repair protein immunohistochemical abnormalities), and the similarity of the tumours’ histopathological features to those of Lynch syndrome–associated cancers explain the molecular and pathological phenotypic overlap between MAP and Lynch syndrome {26309352;24518836;20663686;35668106}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of biallelic germline pathogenic variants in MUTYH.

Desirable: presence of colonic polyps or other associated neoplasms; identification of the MUTYH-associated tumour mutation signatures (SBS18 and SBS36).
