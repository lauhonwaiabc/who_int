1.2 G protein–coupled receptor pathway

G protein–coupled receptors (GPCRs) are the largest protein family encoded by the human genome. Located on cell membranes, they transduce ligands including hormones, lipids, neurotransmitters, and chemokines, converting extracellular signals into key physiological effects {36813353}. While many have been implicated in sporadic cancers, several have also been implicated in genetic tumour syndromes {27899192}.

GPCRs have seven transmembrane domains and are divided into four groups – A (rhodopsin), B (secretin and adhesion), C (glutamate), and F (frizzled) – based on their amino acid sequences. Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia is an example of a syndrome caused by mutation of a class B1 secretin-type receptor with large extracellular domains that binds to glucagon and glucagon-like peptides.

The GPCR signalling pathway includes a number of small membrane-located proteins, including those encoded by GNAS and GNAQ. GNAS encodes Gsα, germline mutation of which gives rise to McCune–Albright syndrome {1944469}. GNAQ encodes Gqα, germline mutation of which gives rise to Sturge–Weber syndrome. GNAQ variants may affect the G protein–coupled receptor endothelin, which has important roles in vasculogenesis {23656586;32532044}. GTP-binding proteins are considered emerging therapeutic targets {34230073;33414387}.
