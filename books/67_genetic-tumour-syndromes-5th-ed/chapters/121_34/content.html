1.2.1 Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia (GCGR)

Definition

Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia (GCHN) is an autosomal recessive syndrome usually attributable to germline GCGR pathogenic sequence variants and characterized by widespread glucagon cell hyperplasia of the pancreas which progresses to multifocal neuroendocrine neoplasms expressing almost exclusively glucagon.

MIM numbering

619290 Mahvash disease; MVAH

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas

Related terminology

Not recommended: glucagon cell adenomatosis; Mahvash disease.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GCHN arises throughout the pancreas.

Clinical features

Patients present with nonspecific symptoms such as abdominal pain, altered bowel habits, fatigue, diabetes, and/or acute pancreatitis. Despite very high serum glucagon levels, the anticipated actions of this hormone are not present. Owing to the receptor’s deficient signalling, glucagonoma syndrome is not expected. However, one patient with wildtype GCGR presented with glucagonoma syndrome including necrolytic migratory erythema {21763589;29702528;27933176}. A patient with PTH-independent hypercalcaemia has been reported {30032256}. Abdominal imaging may reveal lesions of varying sizes throughout the pancreas which may also be SSTR reactive on PET imaging {18957496;25695890;18437091}. The only reported paediatric patient showed elevated levels of serum amino acids, such as glutamine, alanine, and arginine, at 2 months of age. During the first years of life, the patient experienced anorexia and recurrent emesis {30294546}.

Epidemiology

GCHN is an extremely rare disease; 14 cases have been reported to date. There appears to be no sex predilection. Patient age ranges from 7 to 68 years {29702528}.

Etiology

More than 50% of the examined patients with GCHN showed germline sequence variants of the GCGR gene, located at chromosome 17q25.3 {18957496;25695890;18437091;25914784}. The GCGR point substitutions NM_000160.5:c.187G>A p.(Asp63Asn), NM_000160.5:c.674G>A p.(Arg225His), and NM_000160.5:c.1102G>A p.(Val368Met), which are associated with impaired receptor function, have been detected in GCHN {34801547}. In addition, frameshift variants in GCGR have also been described {25695890}. Examinations in 1 index patient and his parents indicate that GCHN with mutation of GCGR follows an autosomal recessive inheritance {25695890}. The genetic disease mechanism in patients without GCGR variants remains unclear {25695890;23407487}. No alterations in the MEN1 or VHL genes have been identified.

Pathogenesis

GCHN harbours either homozygous GCGR sequence variants or at least two compound heterozygous sequence variants leading to premature stop codons or probably deficient protein expression. Deficient glucagon signalling in the liver causes a disturbance in a presumed feedback mechanism between the liver and the glucagon cells, which is probably mediated by amino acids {30032256}. This leads to glucagon cell hyperplasia, elevated serum glucagon levels, and finally neuroendocrine tumours (NETs).

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically the pancreas can be a normal size or enlarged. On the cut surface there are multiple white-yellow nodules ranging from a few millimetres up to many centimetres in diameter {18437091;18957496;25695890}.

Histopathology

Throughout the entire pancreatic tissue, there are normal-sized and hypertrophic islets, randomly distributed between microtumours, and in some cases also single macrotumours. Large tumours may undergo cystic transformation and focal calcification. The tumour cells are monomorphic and exhibit round oval nuclei. The mitotic activity is very low. Micrometastases have been reported in regional lymph nodes but are rare {18957496;25695890;30032256}.

Immunohistochemically, the microtumours and the macrotumours are almost exclusively composed of glucagon-positive cells but may occasionally also express PP {30032256}. In the hypertrophic islets, glucagon-positive cells outnumber insulin and also somatostatin cells. All endocrine cells express SSTR2A. Ki-67 labelling is very low (< 1%) {18437091;18957496;25695890}. GCHN with GCGR mutation seems to have a more advanced phenotype regarding the number of hypertrophic islets and microtumours, and the occurrence of macrotumours. Lymph node metastases were found in 1 patient with wildtype GCGR {25695890}.

Cytology

Cytologically, the pancreatic NETs (PanNETs) are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Sequencing all exons of GCGR including splice sites is obligatory before GCGR mutation can be excluded. To date, sequence variants of GCGR have been reported in exons 2, 4, 8, 10, 11, and 12 {34801547}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: markedly elevated serum glucagon levels; hypertrophic islets merging with multiple PanNETs, expressing glucagon predominantly or exclusively.

Desirable: identification of germline pathogenic variants in GCGR; exclusion of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 and other syndromes associated with pancreatic neuroendocrine cell hyperplasia and microtumours.

Staging

Stage as per the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems for PanNETs.

Prognosis and prediction

To date, all reported cases of GCHN have followed a benign clinical course except one. No recurrence of the disease symptoms has been noted in patients who have undergone partial pancreatectomy {25695890}. However, lifelong follow-up of these patients is indicated, as the tumours can metastasize. One patient died of severe migratory necrotizing erythema and sepsis {21763589}.
