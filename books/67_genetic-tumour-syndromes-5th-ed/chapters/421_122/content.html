4.2.1 BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndromes (BRCA1, BRCA2)

Definition

BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndromes are inherited autosomal dominant disorders of germline mutation (pathogenic variants) of BRCA1 or BRCA2, in which there is an increased risk of breast cancer (especially before the age of 50 years), tubo-ovarian carcinoma, prostatic carcinoma, pancreatic carcinoma, and other cancer types.

MIM numbering

612555 Familial breast-ovarian cancer 2; BROVCA2 (BRCA2)

ICD-11 coding

2C65 Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome.

Subtype(s)

BRCA1-related hereditary cancer syndrome; BRCA2-related hereditary cancer syndrome

Localization

BRCA1- and BRCA2-related hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndromes are associated with carcinomas of the male and female breast, ovary / fallopian tube, peritoneum, and prostate, as well as biliary tract cancer and oesophageal, gastric, and pancreatic cancers {35420638}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are related to site-specific disease.

Epidemiology

In an unselected predominantly European-origin US population, germline pathogenic variants were identified in about 1 in 200 individuals {30646163}. Germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 carry a high lifetime risk of breast and tubo-ovarian cancer {17416853;23628597}. There is variation in germline pathogenic variant type and frequency by geographical region and race/ethnicity {29446198} with different founder germline pathogenic variants present in different population groups {28918466}, suggesting that population-specific mutation screening might be an efficient strategy for genetic testing in low-income regions {29446198}. Breast and tubo-ovarian cancer risk varies by type and location of BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants {25849179}. From a large prospective cohort study, the point estimate for the cumulative breast cancer risk to age 80 years was 72% for carriers of BRCA1 pathogenic variants and 69% for carriers of BRCA2 pathogenic variants. The corresponding tubo-ovarian cancer risks were 44% (BRCA1) and 17% (BRCA2) {28632866}. Between 10% and 15% of ovarian cancer is associated with inherited BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants {21324516} and 8–12% of unselected triple-negative breast cancers are associated with BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants {30099541;28724667}.

Male breast cancer risks to age 80 years are increased for carriers of germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 (~0.5%) and BRCA2 (~4%) {35077220}. BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants also increase prostate cancer risk (~25% risk to age 80 years) but the risk for BRCA1 germline pathogenic variant carriers may be minimally raised or not increased {30900310;35077220}. The risks for pancreas cancer are approximately 3% for both BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variant carriers {35077220}, but earlier studies have suggested higher risks for BRCA2 germline pathogenic variant carriers {29922827;12237281;28767289}.

Outside of the traditionally associated cancers, germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 have additionally been associated with biliary tract cancer and gastric and colorectal cancer for BRCA1 (all odds ratio: < 3.5), and gastric cancer for BRCA2 (odds ratio: 3.7) {35077220}. Other studies have found slightly different results, but the consistent pattern is that gastric cancer is a BRCA1- or BRCA2-associated tumour {35077220;35420638}. A very large recent study from Japan suggests the associated cancers may be broader than in European countries {35420638} (see #37382Table 5.09).

Etiology

BRCA1 and BRCA2 encode proteins that are key regulators of homologous recombination repair (HRR) of DNA double-strand breaks and prevent cell-cycle arrest, apoptosis, and/or genomic instability {29414795}.

BRCA1 is located at 17q21.31 and encodes a nuclear protein of 1863 amino acids (see #37383Table 5.10). A RING finger domain at the N-terminus mediates interactions with other proteins. BRCA1 is often mutated in the RING domain, between exons 11 and 13, and in the BRCA1 C-terminal domain {22737296}. BRCA2 maps to chromosome 13q13.1 and encodes a protein of 3418 amino acids. The BRCA1 and BRCA2 proteins share no structural homology with any other protein {21336636}.

The BRCA proteins are engaged in the highly conserved HRR pathway to accurately repair DNA breaks using the sister chromatid during S phase as a template, which is essential for cell survival {15546503;8589730}. If not repaired, these breaks promote genomic instability and can lead to the development of cancer.

Pathogenesis

During replication, double-strand breaks at replication forks lead to loss of segments of the genome with rearrangements. BRCA1 and BRCA2 proteins play a critical role in maintaining genomic stability by promoting error-free DNA repair {16998501}. BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants predispose individuals to a two-hit biallelic inactivation, involving the inherited pathogenic variant of one BRCA allele and then the somatic inactivation of the second wildtype allele, together with homologous recombination {31090900}.

Patients with BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants or somatic inactivation (of one or usually both alleles) can benefit from targeted treatments, such as the poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors (PARPis). PARPis interfere with the single strand–break repair enzyme PARP, which results in accumulation of unrepaired double-strand breaks that destabilize the genome and lead to cell death through synthetic lethality {29660759}. For breast cancer, patients with locally advanced or metastatic ERBB2 (HER2)-negative BRCA germline-mutated breast cancer, PARPis offer an improvement in progression-free survival, and probably improve overall survival {33886122}. In tubo-ovarian cancer, BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation predicts the sensitivity to platinum-based chemotherapy and PARPis, leading to an increased overall survival {31362850}. Nevertheless, reversion mutations in BRCA1 and/or BRCA2 that lead to PARPi resistance occur not uncommonly {33091561}.

BRCA-like syndromes

An additional subset of cancers exhibit so-called BRCAness, harbouring repair defects stemming from mutations in non-BRCA HRR genes. Thus, BRCAness may lie in aberration of BARD1, PALB2, RAD51C, and RAD51D, which are all genes involved in HRR (see section PALB2-related cancer predisposition syndrome and section RAD51-related cancer predisposition syndrome). Both BRCA1- and BRCA2-mutant cancers and cancers with a BRCAness phenotype are variably sensitive to PARPis. Studies of new combination strategies using small molecule agents that can induce BRCAness are being explored in order to expand the use of PARPis to a larger group of patients who could be repeatedly screened for DNA repair gene alterations {35681619;34712892}.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid tumours have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Histopathology is as per individual cancer types.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant inherited disorder with germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 or BRCA2.

Genetic testing of BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants using peripheral blood or other normal cells is generally based on family history and clinicopathological characteristics. Criteria for genetic testing, risk assessment, counselling, and management have been proposed by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) {33406487} and the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) [[Familial breast cancer: classification, care and managing breast cancer and related risks in people with a family history of breast cancer. (2019). National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE). Bookref]]. In contrast, the American College of Breast Surgeons has suggested that all women with breast cancer be offered BRCA1 and BRCA2 genetic testing {31342359}. Most practitioners now consider that all women with tubo-ovarian carcinoma should be offered germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 testing {27209246}. Moreover, with genetic testing being widely implemented, several studies have shown that as many as half of all BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants carriers have no relevant family history {26439132;29248130;32245629;32053139}. This argues for more-widespread BRCA1 and BRCA2 genetic testing. In particular, individuals with somatic BRCA1 and BRCA2 pathogenic variants in an appropriate tumour type should be offered germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 testing {29248130}.

BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant interpretation should be evaluated synthetically with the use of information from population- and disease-related databases, patient medical histories, and literature, according to the guidelines from the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) (updated in 2015) {25741868}. Only variants considered pathogenic or likely pathogenic have clinical significance. ClinVar has currently registered > 3500 variants of BRCA1 and > 4200 variants of BRCA2 as pathogenic / likely pathogenic germline variants. The most frequent variants encountered are SNVs and indels, which produce premature stop codons, reduce expression via nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, and occasionally produce a truncated protein {32862296}. The SNVs and indels of BRCA1 and BRCA2 account for 10–50% of the germline variants encountered in families with hereditary breast and ovarian cancer {21637503;16199546;17561994}. In addition, the frequency of BRCA1 and BRCA2 large genomic rearrangements in families with hereditary breast and ovarian cancer varies from 0% to 23% among different populations {21637503}. The Locus Reference Genomic (LRG) system should be included in the scope of BRCA1 and BRCA2 genetic testing in the clinic setting.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 pathogenic variants.

Staging

Staging of BRCA1- and BRCA2-related hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome cancers is similar to that of non-BRCA cancers.

Prognosis and prediction

Studies on prognosis for cancers arising in BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variant carriers compared with cancers arising in non-genetic forms can be confounded by age and biomarker status. With these limitations, male BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variant carriers do not seem to have a prognostic impact in pooled pancancer analyses {33054725}, although for many organs information is derived from low numbers of male patients and there are not enough data available. BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants lead to a greater sensitivity for platinum chemotherapy and PARP inhibition, and this could be one reason why women with fallopian tube cancer and BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants have better prognoses in the short term {35444210;35710751}. Compared with non–BRCA related breast cancer, women under 40 years of age with BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants experience a better outcome, while older women are more likely to have worse outcomes {27749552;35444210}. Men with prostate cancer and BRCA2 pathogenic variants have a worse prognosis than those with non-hereditary forms of prostate cancer {25101567;32875559}. The prognosis for other tumours, such as melanoma and oesophageal and cervical cancer, arising on a background of BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants, compared with the non-hereditary neoplasms, is unknown.
