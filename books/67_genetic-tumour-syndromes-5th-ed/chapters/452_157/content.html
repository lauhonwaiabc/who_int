4.5.2 CHEK2-related hereditary (breast) cancer predisposition syndrome (CHEK2)

Definition

CHEK2-related hereditary (breast) cancer predisposition syndrome is a hereditary cancer susceptibility syndrome resulting from germline pathogenic sequence variants in the CHEK2 gene. While an association with breast cancer susceptibility is established, affected individuals may also have increased susceptibility to a wide range of other cancers, including gastric, colon, thyroid, kidney, pancreas, and prostate cancers, and sarcoma and haematopoietic malignancies, but definitive risks have been harder to establish.

Localization

In addition to breast cancer, heterozygosity for germline CHEK2 pathogenic variants (predominantly the well-studied NM_007194.4:c.1100del variant) has been associated with an increased risk of gastric, colon, thyroid, kidney, and prostate cancers {26884562}, as well as sarcoma {26884562}, pancreatic cancer {34503238;26629066}, and haematopoietic malignancies {26629066;35674998;34622392}. The NM_007194.4:c.470T>C p.(Ile157Thr) variant of CHEK2 has been linked to a greater frequency of benign and low-grade malignant ovarian tumours {16828850}. However, current data indicate that CHEK2 pathogenic variants do not predispose to high-grade fallopian tube–ovarian epithelial cancer {26720728;31050813;15385111;27595995}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are related to site-specific disease.

Epidemiology

Breast cancer resulting from an underlying germline CHEK2 pathogenic variant accounts for 1–3% of all cases in female patients {28418444;29522266} and 4–9% in male patients {28008555;11967536;15122511}. The NM_007194.4:c.1100del variant is the most prevalent, with an overall odds ratio of 2.89 that stratifies to 2.88 for female breast cancer and 3.13–8.46 for male breast cancer {29909568;34461861}. The frequency of NM_007194.4:c.1100del variant of CHEK2 varies among regional breast cancer populations, from 0% (in Malaysia) to 2.7% (in the Russian Federation). In population-based studies of asymptomatic carriers, the frequency ranges from 0% (in Spain) to 0.7% (in Sweden) {28553140}.

The hazard ratio for cancers in other organs ranges between 1.60 and 14.8 and can vary depending on study ascertainment and variants studied {26884562;26629066;35674998;34622392}.

Etiology

CHEK2 is located at chromosome 22q12.1. CHEK2 (NM_007294.4) pathogenic variants produce malfunctioning proteins, resulting in disruption of DNA repair and consequent accumulation of genetic events that contribute to carcinogenesis. No external factors are known to influence carcinogenesis in patients with CHEK2 germline pathogenic variants.

Pathogenesis

CHEK2 was first identified in 1998 and encodes a key protein kinase in cell-cycle regulation that acts as a tumour suppressor with a key role in the ATM/CHK2/p53 DNA damage response pathway {9836640}. An early case series suggested that CHEK2 may predispose to Li–Fraumeni syndrome {10617473}, but subsequent studies have strongly refuted this {12442270;18178638}. Germline pathogenic variants in CHEK2 have an established role in breast cancer predisposition {33471991;33471974} and are generally considered to confer a moderate lifetime risk of breast cancer, with lifetime risk modified by other factors, such as family history and polygenic risk {27711073}. The association of pathogenic variants in CHEK2 with susceptibility to other cancers has been harder to establish.

CHEK2 is a tumour suppressor gene that encodes the serine/threonine cell cycle–checkpoint kinase CHK2 {11967536}. The CHK2 protein is activated by double-strand DNA breaks and in turn activates downstream repair proteins including BRCA1 and p53, playing an important role in DNA damage response by regulating cell-cycle checkpoints and triggering DNA repair by homologous recombination {19473886;10673500}. The most common genetic alteration is the NM_007194.4:c.1100del loss-of-function pathogenic variant that yields an unstable CHK2 protein incapable of kinase activity {27716369;16998506}. For female NM_007194.4:c.1100del CHEK2 variant carriers, there is currently no evidence that risk varies by PR or ERBB2 (HER2) status after adjustment for ER status, nor for variation in relative risk by grade or morphology {17132695}.

Array comparative genomic hybridization of NM_007194.4:c.1100del cases showed recurrent losses at 1p31.3-p13.3, 8p21.2-p21.1, 8p23.2-p23.1, and 17p13.1-p12, as well as gains of 12q13.11-q13.13, 16p13.3, and 19p13.3 {21542898}. In gene expression analysis, 26 CHEK2 c.1100delC (NM_007194.4:c.1100del ) tumours clustered with hormone receptor–positive breast cancers as luminal A (n = 8) or luminal B (n = 18) intrinsic subtype breast cancers {21614566}. Another study pointed to CLCA1 on 1p22.3 and CALCOCO1, MUCL1 (SBEM), and LRP1 on 12q13.3 as candidates for CHEK2 c.1100delC (NM_007194.4:c.1100del)–associated tumour-progression drivers {21542898}.

Most of the data on CHEK2 relate to the NM_007194.4:c.1100del variant, which is found frequently in northern European populations {11967536;27269948}. The risk of breast cancer is probably higher in homozygous NM_007194.4:c.1100del carriers than in heterozygous carriers {23652375;22058428}. There is evidence that other truncation variants and missense variants have a similar risk to the 1100delC variant, while most missense variants will have a lower risk estimate of about 1.75 {33471991;35585550}. CHEK2 missense variants such as NM_007194.4:c.470T>C p.(Ile157Thr) confer a lower risk of female breast cancer than the NM_007194.4:c.1100del  variant {26014596;15239132;35585550}. The p.(Ile157Thr) variant corrupts proper dimerization of the CHK2 protein, resulting in a dysfunctional protein that affects interactions at the interface of forkhead-associated and kinase domains {19782031}. The p.(Ile157Thr) variant was found to be associated with a luminal A phenotype and lower E-cadherin (CDH1) expression {15803365;27716369}, and CHEK2 variants appear to be associated with a luminal B phenotype {15803365}. More than 200 pathogenic or likely pathogenic CHEK2 germline variants have been reported (according to the ClinVar database as of November 2018), of which the NM_007194.4:c.1100del  variant and c.470T>C p.(Ile157Thr) missense variant appear to be most prevalent.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CHEK2-related hereditary breast cancer predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant inherited disorder with a germline pathogenic variant in CHEK2. Demonstration of this is necessary for diagnosis of the syndrome.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: germline pathogenic variant in CHEK2.

 

See #39051Table 5.20.

Staging

CHEK2-related breast and other cancer staging is similar to that of non-CHEK2 cancers.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with NM_007194.4:c.1100del-associated breast cancer experience an as yet unexplained decreased survival {24918820;15466005;27716369}. Furthermore, no significant difference in response to adjuvant chemotherapy has been reported in these patients {24918820}. CHEK2 p.(Ile157Thr) carriers have survival comparable to that of non-carriers {27716369;24557336}.

For other cancers, there are no meaningful data with regard to prognosis.
