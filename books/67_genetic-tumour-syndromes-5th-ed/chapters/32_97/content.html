3.2 RB1 pathway

In addition to RB1 itself, the RB1 pathway includes a number of cyclin-dependent kinases, and the E2F family of transcription factors, which together regulate the cell cycle and apoptosis. Mutation of several of the genes involved, as well as RB1 itself, causes tumour predisposition syndromes.

Retinoblastoma syndrome is caused by a germline RB1 pathogenic variant (first hit) followed by a somatic alteration (second hit), and originates from developing retinal cells, probably cone progenitors {25252974;30213853}.

Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 4 results from germline loss-of-function pathogenic variants in the CDKN1B gene, which encodes the CDK inhibitor p27 (p27KIP1) {17030811;23652671}. It is an autosomal dominant disorder with incomplete penetrance in some families, which explains why patients may display either clearly familial or apparently sporadic presentation. Nuclear p27 binds to and inhibits the cyclin E / CDK2 and cyclin A / CDK2 complexes that regulate the progression from the G1 to the S phase of the cell cycle. The main targets of CDK2 are members of the retinoblastoma family of proteins. When these proteins are phosphorylated by CDK2, they release members of the E2F family, which in turn activate the transcription of genes responsible for G1 to S progression {23652671}.

CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome is caused by disruption of the CDKN2A tumour suppressor gene, which encodes the p16 (p16INK4a) and p14ARF cell-cycle regulators {7987388;9622062}. The product of CDKN2A, p16, binds and inhibits CDK4 and CDK6 to maintain cells in the resting G1 phase of the cell cycle. The structurally unrelated p14ARF protein is produced from an alternative reading frame and acts by antagonizing the p53 regulatory protein MDM2. The neoplasms that arise in the setting of CDKN2A-related tumour predisposition syndrome are thought to occur owing to abnormal proliferation of melanocytes, astrocytes, and other cells after somatic inactivation of the remaining CDKN2A allele, typically via loss of heterozygosity {26876133;28699883}.

CDK4-related melanoma predisposition syndrome, caused by pathogenic variants in CDK4, predisposes to early-onset multiple primary melanomas and increased numbers of atypical naevi {32249949}. It encodes a serine/threonine kinase and, when mutated, inhibits binding of p16, leading to phosphorylation of RB1 and cell-cycle progression. The result of such monophosphorylation is the release of an E2F transcription factor, which triggers the transcription of the cyclin E gene, CCNE1, and its binding to CDK2. The new cyclin E / CDK2 complex additionally hyperphosphorylates the RB1 protein at other serine and threonine phosphorylation sites and facilitates the progression of the cell cycle {9315635;9003781}.
