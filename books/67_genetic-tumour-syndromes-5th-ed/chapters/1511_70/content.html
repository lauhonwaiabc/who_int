1.5.11 Hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome (CDH1, CTNNA1)

Definition

Hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome (HGBCS), previously known as hereditary diffuse gastric cancer, is an autosomal dominant cancer predisposition syndrome with inactivating germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in CDH1 or CTNNA1 in families or individuals characterized by diffuse gastric cancer and/or lobular breast cancer, occasionally with cleft lip/palate.

MIM numbering

137215 Diffuse gastric and lobular breast cancer syndrome; DGLBC

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary diffuse gastric cancer syndrome; hereditary lobular breast cancer; diffuse gastric and lobular breast cancer syndrome; diffuse gastric cancer syndrome / lobular breast cancer syndrome; CDH1-related tumour susceptibility syndrome; cadherin-related tumour susceptibility syndrome.

Subtype(s)

CDH1-related hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome; CTNNA1-related hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome

Localization

All topographical regions of the gastric mucosa can be affected, as well as breast lobules from carriers of pathogenic / likely pathogenic (P/LP) germline variants {11419427;11443625;15138207;15141383;18391748;20719348;29454568;32051609;32758476;34643667;35307354}.

Clinical features

Age of cancer onset is extremely variable (14–85 years) even within families, although cancers before the age of 20 years are rare {26182300;32758476}.

The cumulative incidence of gastric cancer was recently estimated to be 42% for men and 33% for women. The cumulative incidence of female breast cancer was estimated at 55% at 80 years, in a CDH1 P/LP variant carrier cohort not ascertained by HGBCS clinical criteria {31246251}. The clinical picture of CTNNA1 HGBCS differs mainly regarding the uncertain lobular breast cancer (LBC) risk {34425242} and the rarity of typical precursor lesions {29330337;26182300;23208944;30515673}.

Epidemiology

HGBCS is estimated to cause < 1% of all gastric cancer cases worldwide, and a lower fraction of breast cancers. HGBCS incidence is variable between countries and ethnicities {29589180;23264079;26049741}. In HGBCS family cohorts clinically defined using the 2010 {20591882}, 2015 {25979631}, or 2020 criteria {32758476}, CDH1 P/LP variants occur in 10–40% of cases, while CTNNA1 P/LP variants occur in < 2% {26182300;26025002;34425242}.

Etiology

HGBCS is predominantly caused by germline CDH1 P/LP variants, located at chromosome 16q22.1 {26182300;9537325;36436516}, with occasional P/LP variants in the CTNNA1 gene located at chromosome 5q31.2. No significant mutation hotspots or evidence for genotype–phenotype correlations has been observed in CDH1. Most are nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site single nucleotide variants, but large copy-number variants (mainly deletions) have also been identified {26182300;23812922;24037103;19168852;32758476;25638682}. CDH1 variants disturb homophilic cell-to-cell adhesion, cell polarity, epithelial tissue architecture, cell signalling, and the transduction of mechanical force {23127608;25925582;23547085}. Other recurrently affected gene candidates in HGBCS have not yet been identified, although there may be enrichment of PALB2 P/LP variants {28875981;29706558;34871783}.

Pathogenesis

Initiation of cancer in HGBCS families requires complete loss of E-cadherin function, accomplished by somatic inactivation of the second CDH1 allele through either epigenetic or genetic mechanisms {10973239;19223545;19269290;23341533}. In model systems, loss of functional E-cadherin has been shown to lead to misorientation of the mitotic spindle, resulting in epithelial cells being displaced into the lamina propria {11818059;19553471;19432899}. Initially, intramucosal signet-ring cell carcinomas (SRCCs) are indolent or even transient; however, mutation of TP53 and other oncogenic drivers, such as ARID1A and RHOA, can lead to the development of a more-aggressive phenotype {27573781}. In diffuse gastric cancer (DGC) from CTNNA1 germline P/LP variant carriers, RHOA, ARID1A, and PIK3CA somatic variants have also been identified {23208944}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most advanced DGCs in HGBCS are indistinguishable from sporadic DGC, presenting as linitis plastica. Prophylactic gastrectomy specimens from asymptomatic CDH1 and CTNNA1 P/LP variant carriers generally appear normal to the naked eye and palpation, and slicing shows normal mucosal thickness {15138207;11419427}.

LBC in CDH1 P/LP variant carriers often presents as poorly defined masses and is indistinguishable from sporadic LBC. Risk-reducing mastectomy specimens appear macroscopically normal {32758476}.

Histopathology

Gastric cancer

Early-stage DGC in CDH1 and CTNNA1 P/LP variant carriers is characterized by small foci (< 0.1 to 10 mm) of SRCC in the superficial gastric mucosa (pT1a), without nodal metastases; less commonly, larger foci of intramucosal carcinoma can also involve the deep mucosa {15138207;15141383;18391748}. Two intraepithelial lesions – carcinoma in situ and pagetoid spread of signet-ring cells – may also be found in the gastric mucosa of CDH1 P/LP variant carriers {32758476}. Single or multiple SRCC foci are detected in > 95% of prophylactic gastrectomies {30014492}. Intestinal metaplasia and Helicobacter pylori infection are rare. A proforma is available for guiding and reporting pathology findings from hereditary DGC gastrectomy specimens {32758476}.

Advanced DGCs in HGBCS usually show features of poorly cohesive gastric cancer, non-signet-ring–cell type. In most cases, there is at least a small component of typical SRCCs. The tumour bulk is heterogeneous with atypical cells, dispersed or in nests, cords, (micro) glands, and (small) mucinous lakes.

Intramucosal carcinomas (pT1a) frequently show an indolent phenotype, characterized by signet-ring cells without immunoexpression of Ki-67 or p53, while advanced carcinomas (pT > 1) usually display a more-aggressive appearance with pleomorphic cells expressing these and other proteins associated with tumorigenesis {27573781;17363565;29307626}.

Breast cancer

Risk-reducing mastectomies may show small foci of invasive LBC and widespread lobular in situ neoplasia, comprising both atypical lobular hyperplasia and lobular carcinoma in situ. LBC in CDH1 P/LP variant carriers is indistinguishable from sporadic LBC and characterized by diffuse infiltration of small round cells with monomorphic nuclei that invade individually or as single files and cords.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates are not taken from gastric lesions. LBC aspirates contain small monomorphic cells with mild atypia, often with intracytoplasmic mucin vacuoles. The cells are present singly, as loose clusters, or forming cords.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Although most CDH1-related cancers show absent or reduced staining for E-cadherin {15141383;17363565;18825658;19269290;27573781}, E-cadherin immunohistochemistry in DGCs is not recommended as a prescreening test for HGBCS (alterations of E-cadherin expression can also be seen in sporadic cancers).

In HGBCS, detection of loss-of-function CDH1 or CTNNA1 sequence variants is necessary for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

The detection of germline pathogenic variants in CDH1 or CTNNA1 is required. The criteria recommended for testing for germline pathogenic variants in CDH1 and CTNNA1 are given in #37421Box 2.08.

A summary of the main features of HGBCS is given in #37420Table 2.31.

Staging

In most gastrectomy specimens from asymptomatic CDH1 or CTNNA1 P/LP variant carriers, only intramucosal cancers are diagnosed and staged as pT1aN0. Most index cases are diagnosed at an advanced pTNM stage and are staged according to the ninth edition (2025) of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {9355980} and the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) cancer staging manual [[Amin MB, Edge SB, Greene FL, et al., editors. AJCC cancer staging manual. 8th ed. New York (NY): Springer; 2017. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

Advanced DGC in HGBCS carries a poor prognosis {26072394}. Intramucosal carcinomas can remain indolent for a long time or disappear, carrying a very low risk of dissemination {19432899}. Presymptomatic genetic screening of at-risk family members, as well as surveillance and/or lifesaving cancer risk–reducing gastrectomy, must be considered for carriers of CDH1 or CTNNA1 P/LP variants {32758476}.

Female carriers have a 40% lifetime risk of developing invasive LBC {26182300}. In the setting of HGBCS, DGC is the main cause of mortality in both sexes.
