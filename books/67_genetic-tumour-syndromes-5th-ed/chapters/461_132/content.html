4.6.1 Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis (POLD1, POLE)

Definition

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis (PPAP) is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants affecting the proofreading (exonuclease) domains of the DNA polymerase encoding genes POLD1 and POLE. The syndrome is characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps with increased risk of colorectal cancer and endometrial cancer, although other cancers may occur.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is a high risk of adenomatous polyposis and cancer affecting the colorectum, and endometrial cancer affecting the uterus. In addition, there is a low to moderate increased risk of cancer affecting other sites including stomach, duodenum, pancreas, breast, ovary, and brain {26822575}.

Clinical features

PPAP accounts for approximately 10% of all cases of familial adenomatous polyposis {29120461}. Adenomatous polyps of the large bowel occur in adulthood, generally by the age of 50 years. The risk of developing colorectal cancer by the age of 70 years is between 1 in 3 and 2 in 3 {29120461}. The most common tumour types in PPAP are colorectal, followed by endometrial in POLD1 heterozygotes and duodenal in POLE heterozygotes, as POLD1-variant cases are at a significantly higher risk of endometrial cancer than POLE heterozygotes {33948826;25529843}. The majority of endometrial cancers occur in patients > 50 years of age. Some individuals with a POLE pathogenic variant, but none with a POLD1 pathogenic variant, develop ovarian cancer, whereas some with POLE pathogenic variants but very few with a POLD1 pathogenic variant develop hypermutated medulloblastoma brain tumours, sometimes at a young age {31624068;33948826}. Multiple primary tumours may also be a feature of PPAP, more so in female patients. The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in PPAP are similar to sporadic tumours macroscopically and microscopically, but they have a characteristic ultramutant somatic mutation rate. This form of polyposis is thought to be rarer than MUTYH-associated polyposis, although the exact prevalence is unknown. Less than 15% of PPAP-associated tumours acquire somatic mismatch repair gene variants resulting in microsatellite instability (MSI) and immunohistochemical mismatch repair protein abnormalities suggestive of Lynch syndrome, and thus PPAP is one rare cause of so-called Lynch-like syndrome {26648449;25370038}. Ultramutant cancers associated with PPAP, being rich in neoantigens, including those with MSI, appear to be good targets for PD1/PDL1 immune checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy {28404093;27310809}.

The cumulative incidence of colorectal cancer in POLE and POLD1 variant heterozygotes with PPAP is estimated at approximately 90% and 50%, respectively {33948826}. Most PPAP cases have an attenuated colorectal polyposis (10–100 polyps) that seems to be more severe in carriers of germline POLE proofreading domain variants than in those with POLD1 proofreading domain variants (10–50 compared with < 20 colonic adenomatous polyps per patient, respectively) {26822575}.

Epidemiology

Germline POLE and POLD1 variants are present in 0.5–2% of patients in intestinal polyposis and colorectal cancer cohorts enriched for familial disease {26822575}.

Etiology

Germline variants affecting the proofreading exonuclease domains of POLD1 and POLE (encoding the replicative DNA polymerases POLD1 and POLE) that inactivate their proofreading function allow very high frequency misincorporation of bases during DNA replication, contributing to tumour formation.

A database of variants in POLD1, POLE, MSH2, MLH1, MSH6, PMS2, MUTYH, and MSH3, called the International Society for Gastrointestinal Hereditary Tumours (InSiGHT) Database, is available online [[International Society for Gastrointestinal Hereditary Tumours Database (Internet). Leiden (Netherlands): Leiden University Medical Center; 2018. Available from: https://www.insight-database.org/. Bookref]]. Confirmed germline POLD1 and POLE proofreading domain variants are also curated in the Leiden Open Variant Database (LOVD).

Pathogenesis

PPAP is caused by dominantly inherited variants in the proofreading (exonuclease) domains of the DNA polymerase encoding genes POLD1 and POLE, which code for the DNA replicases POLD1 and POLE, which act in the delta and epsilon DNA replication complexes to replicate DNA on the lagging and leading strands, respectively {23263490;26822575;23447401}. This produces a high-penetrance predisposition to development of colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas with a variable tendency to form multiple and large neoplasms.

These proofreading variants cause a defect in the correction of mispaired bases inserted during DNA replication, leading to an ultramutant phenotype with exceedingly numerous single nucleotide substitutions, without the other normal polymerase allele itself needing to undergo somatic mutation {23447401}, although loss of the wildtype allele has been reported {23263490} and may be favoured even if non-essential. PPAP is therefore an exception to the Knudson multiple-hit hypothesis; it is unique among the germline DNA repair disorders in that the mechanism does not require inactivation of both alleles. A notable feature is that tumours show characteristic mutation signatures (four Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer [COSMIC] #10 signatures – SBS10a, SBS10b, SBS10c, and SBS10d), with overrepresentation of C>A transversions and C>T transitions, sometimes together with a tendency to MSI (mutation signature SBS14 is associated with concurrent defective POLE and MSI, and SBS20 is associated with concurrent defective POLD1 and MSI), such that cancer cases may be mistaken for Lynch syndrome, but these are better regarded as Lynch-like syndrome. In < 15% patients with POLE proofreading variants there is an MSI-like increase in small indels in colorectal cancers. In addition to this, POLE proofreading variants may generate somatically acquired biallelic variants in one of the mismatch repair genes that result in MSI colorectal cancers regarded as Lynch-like syndrome.

Germline variants should be distinguished from the somatic POLE proofreading domain variants that occur in several tumour types, most commonly those of the endometrium (7–12%) and colorectum (1–2%), and occasionally in tumours of the breast, stomach, pancreas, and brain, where they define a distinct, ultramutated tumour subgroup. Interestingly, somatic POLD1 proofreading domain variants appear to be very uncommon {26822575}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

The histopathological appearances are closely similar to those of the sporadic counterparts for colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas. The predominant colorectal polyp type is adenomatous, although serrated polyps are observed, and these lesions are also closely similar in macroscopic appearance to their sporadic counterparts. Whereas in the uterus most cancers are endometrioid endometrial adenocarcinomas, often high-grade with prominent lymphocytic infiltration, there are some uterine carcinosarcomas.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Pathogenic and likely pathogenic PPAP variants in POLE and POLD1 map to the exonuclease domain of POLE (amino acids 268–471) or the equivalent region of POLD1 and result in a protein that retains polymerase and regulatory functions, while inactivating the proofreading function {33948826;26822575}. Although mutation sites within the exonuclease domain tend to differ between PPAP and sporadic POL mutation cases {26822575}, such variants that inactivate the proofreading exonuclease domains of either POLE or POLD1 enzymes generate predominantly base substitution variants within the affected tumour cells, producing unique mutation signatures with a very marked increase in C>A transversions in the context TCT and a marked increase in C>T transitions in the context TCG. Tumour mutation signatures SBS10a and SBS10b are associated with proofreading-defective POLE, whereas SBS10c and SBS10d are associated with proofreading-defective POLD1 (mutation signature SBS14 is associated with concurrent proofreading-defective POLE and MSI, and SBS20 is associated with concurrent proofreading-defective POLD1 and MSI) {32025007;32025018;35197584}.

This results in a strong bias for particular amino acid changes, with an overrepresentation of serine to tyrosine or leucine, and arginine to isoleucine or glutamine substitutions, and a substantial increase in glutamic acid to stop-codon variants. These manifest as a distinctive pattern of missense and truncation variants in oncoproteins and tumour suppressors, including PIK3CA (NM_006218.4:c.263G>A p.(Arg88Gln)), PTEN (NM_000314.8:c.389G>A p.(Arg130Gln)), APC (NM_000038.6:c.3340C>T p(Arg1114*) and NM_000038.6:c.4012C>T p.(Gln1338*)), MSH6 (NM_000179.3:c.2836G>T p.(Glu946*)) and MSH6 (NM_000179.3:c.3964G>T p.(Glu1322*)), FBXW7 (NM_001349798.2:c.1105G>T p.(Glu369*)), and p53 (NM_000546.6:c.637C>T p.(Arg213*)) {26822575}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of a germline heterozygous missense variant affecting the proofreading exonuclease domain–encoding region of POLE or POLD1 is required to distinguish PPAP from Lynch syndrome, familial adenomatous polyposis, and MUTYH-associated polyposis.

Desirable: phenotype consistent with colonic polyposis or other neoplasms.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Endometrial cancers with somatic POLE proofreading domain variants have an excellent prognosis, which may relate to ultramutation causing an abundance of antigenic neoepitopes that may stimulate a strong anti-tumour immune response {33948826}.
