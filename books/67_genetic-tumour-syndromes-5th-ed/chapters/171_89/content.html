Definition

Brooke–Spiegler syndrome (BSS) is an inherited autosomal dominant disease characterized by multiple cutaneous spiradenomas, cylindromas, spiradenocylindromas, and trichoepitheliomas (cribriform trichoblastomas) with rare salivary gland involvement.

ICD-11 coding

2F22 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages

Related terminology

Acceptable: CYLD cutaneous syndrome; familial cylindromatosis; multiple familial trichoepithelioma.

Not recommended: turban tumours; skin–salivary gland tumour diathesis.

Subtype(s)

Multiple familial trichoepithelioma

Localization

Spiradenoma, cylindroma, and spiradenocylindroma mainly involve the skin of the head and neck. Trichoepitheliomas predominantly affect the nasolabial folds, but can affect the entire face. A phenotypic subtype of BSS is multiple familial trichoepitheliomas (MFT), characterized by the development of trichoepitheliomas without other tumours {19730223;23249834}. Salivary gland tumours mostly occur in the parotid gland {19455704}. The occurrence of mammary cylindroma is extremely rare {15252315}.

Clinical features

Spiradenomas, cylindromas, and spiradenocylindromas occur as multiple (dozens to hundreds), often coalescing, variably sized (mostly 5–30 mm), skin-coloured, reddish, or blue nodules. Trichoepitheliomas are bilateral, small (2–10 mm), often confluent, skin-coloured papules. Cutaneous tumours demonstrate growth throughout life, and their number increases with age. Unilateral presentations of tumours suggest genetic mosaicism. Rapid growth, ulceration, and bleeding, especially in older patients, usually indicate malignant transformation, especially for cylindroma, spiradenoma, and spiradenocylindroma. Salivary gland neoplasms (1–2% of cases) present as a freely movable mass usually measuring 5–50 mm in diameter, typically benign membranous basal cell adenoma. Multifocal unilateral involvement of the parotid gland as well as bilateral metachronous and synchronous tumours can be seen. Pulmonary metastasis from the skin is a rare feature {19194280;19730223;23249834;29569226;31085270}.

Epidemiology

BSS and MFT are rare, but the exact incidence is not known. About 1–2% of patients with BSS develop salivary gland tumours, whereas patients with MFT appear to have skin lesions only. Cutaneous tumours usually present from puberty, whereas salivary gland involvement is usually seen after the age of 40 years {23249834}.

Etiology

Germline CYLD heterozygous pathogenic sequence variants are recognized to cause BSS {10835629;31093340;32298062}.

Pathogenesis

Most patients (80–85% with the classic BSS phenotype and 40–50% with the MFT phenotype) harbour germline pathogenic sequence variants in the CYLD gene, a tumour suppressor gene, located at 16q12.1 {10835629;26329847;16922728}. More than 100 private CYLD sequence variants have been reported {34744449}. Most variants are frameshift or nonsense sequence variants, and the remainder are missense and splice-site variants. Other rare mechanisms include large deletions in CYLD and sequence variants in intronic sequences leading to intronic exonization {19668078;25347032}. Cutaneous tumours show frequent loss of heterozygosity or a second inactivating variant in CYLD on the remaining normal allele {26861065}. Whole-genome sequencing of BSS skin tumours reveals recurrent variants in DNMT3A and BCOR in addition to CYLD in as many as a third of neoplasms {31624251}.

Macroscopic appearance

Benign cutaneous tumours are usually circumscribed, unencapsulated, ovoid to round nodules with a smooth surface. In larger lesions (especially spiradenomas), the cut surface may show foci of haemorrhage and cystic change. Malignant tumours are poorly circumscribed and have a non-homogeneous cut surface {22978388;25141971}.

Histopathology

Spiradenoma, cylindroma, and spiradenocylindroma are related tumours, usually well demarcated, multinodular or uninodular, with regular or somewhat pushing borders {21598248}. Spiradenoma is composed of small basaloid cells arranged in a trabecular, reticular, or solid fashion intermixed with paler cells and lymphocytes, an integral component of the tumour. A few tubular structures are often present. Droplets of PAS-positive hyaline basal membrane material can be seen. Cylindroma displays a jigsaw puzzle (mosaic-like) arrangement of islands of basaloid cells surrounded by variably thickened eosinophilic basement membrane material. The peripheral cells often show palisading and are usually darker compared with centrally located cells. Intratumoural lymphocytes are absent or are very few. Spiradenocylindroma combines areas of cylindroma and spiradenoma that are either closely intermingled or sharply segregated.

Malignant neoplasms evolving from cutaneous tumours include low-grade and high-grade lesions with a basaloid morphology resembling salivary gland basal cell adenocarcinomas. Less common are adenocarcinoma NOS and sarcomatoid (metaplastic) carcinoma {19633533;22588548}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology involves identification of biallelic pathogenic variants in CYLD. ALPK1 hotspot mutations are a feature of sporadic spiradenoma and malignant tumours evolving thereof and are not seen in BSS skin tumours {31101826}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: proband with multiple skin tumours (multiple inherited and histologically confirmed cutaneous cylindromas, spiradenomas, spiradenocylindromas, and/or trichoepitheliomas).

Desirable: identification of a germline heterozygous pathogenic variant in CYLD in a patient with multiple BSS skin tumours; cases with a cluster of a few lesions without a family history may be mosaic and not associated with a germline CYLD variant and genetic testing of skin tumours may be necessary; BSS should also be suspected with a salivary gland multicentric basal cell adenoma, membranous type, in a patient with single cylindroma, trichoepithelioma, or spiradenoma.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Offspring of an individual with BSS have a 50% chance of inheriting the variant {32298062}. No genotype–phenotype correlations with respect to the severity of the disease, the possibility of malignant transformation, and development of extracutaneous lesions have been found {15854031}.

Spiradenoma, cylindroma, and spiradenocylindroma are benign but progressively enlarge. Malignant tumours developing from these neoplasms may show local aggressive growth or distant metastases. Because of their rarity and microscopic heterogeneity, the exact prognosis is unknown.
