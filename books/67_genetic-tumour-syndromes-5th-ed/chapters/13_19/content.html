1.3 RAS/MAPK pathway

The RAS/MAPK mediated growth pathway is a complex signalling system with crosstalk to many others which control its effects, particularly the PI3K pathway. A number of germline (constitutional) disorders, commonly known as RASopathies, are defined by activation of different components of the RAS/MAPK signalling pathway {35103797}. Overlapping features may be present, depending upon the gene and cell type affected. RASopathies are clinical syndromes with overlapping phenotypic features caused by germline pathogenic variants associated with the RAS/MAPK pathway. They result in multiple pathogenetic mechanisms, and some of these underlie the tumours encountered, as well as other effects. Because RASopathies affect the RAS/MAPK pathway, some are now treatable using targeted therapeutic molecules.

The RAS/MAPK pathway family of disorders includes neurofibromatosis and schwannomatosis (caused by pathogenic variants in NF1 and NF2, respectively), as well as Costello, Noonan, and Schimmelpenning–Feuerstein–Mims syndromes.
