4.8 Chromosomal non-disjunction (aneuploidy)

The failure of chromosomes or sister chromatids to separate properly during cell division, known as non-disjunction, results in cells with abnormal numbers of chromosomes (aneuploidy). The best known of these is Down syndrome, trisomy 21, which is associated with myeloid neoplasia. In addition, incorrect segregation of X and Y chromosomes leads to Klinefelter and Turner syndromes with a predisposition to gonadal tumours.

Mosaic variegated aneuploidy, of which there are at least three subtypes, is caused by germline pathogenic variants in genes required for proper chromosome segregation during mitosis. The genes involved encode regulators of chromosome segregation and lead to complex patterns of aneuploidy.
