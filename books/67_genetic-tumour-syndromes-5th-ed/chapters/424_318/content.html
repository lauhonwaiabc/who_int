4.2.4 RAD51-related cancer predisposition syndromes (RAD51C, RAD51D)

Definition

RAD51-related cancer predisposition syndromes are inherited autosomal dominant disorders with germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants (GPVs) in RAD51 paralogs (RAD51B, RAD51C, RAD51D). Affected individuals have an increased risk of high-grade serous tubo-ovarian carcinoma and breast carcinoma.

MIM numbering

613399 Breast-ovarian cancer, familial, susceptibility to, 3; BROVCA3 (RAD51C)

614291 Breast-ovarian cancer, familial, susceptibility to, 4; BRVOCA4 (RAD51D)

ICD-11 coding

2C65 Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome – RAD51 paralogs associated.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RAD51-related cancers primarily affect the ovaries and breast. Other sites at risk possibly include pancreas and prostate. The risks for cancers at other sites are not known.

Clinical features

The clinical features are related to site-specific disease, primarily tubo-ovarian and breast tumours.

Epidemiology

The prevalence of RAD51B, RAD51C, and RAD51D constitutional (germline) pathogenic variants is unknown. In the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD v4.1.0, dated 28 August 2025), predicted loss-of-function variants are reported in RAD51B, RAD51C, and RAD51D in 644, 525, and 581 (0.079%; 0.065%; 0.072%), respectively, of 807 162 samples from sequenced individuals. In general, tubo-ovarian carcinoma (TOC) risk for RAD51C and RAD51D pathogenic variant carriers appears to peak in the early postmenopausal years. However, owing to preventive surgery, which is often recommended for women over the age of 50 years, the mean age of diagnosis among carriers is not accurate. Breast cancer is observed in carriers from many, but not all, families, and the triple-negative subtype has been identified as the most prevalent one. In one study, a SNP in RAD51B was associated with male breast cancer (rs1314913: P = 3.02 × 10−13, odds ratio: 1.57, 95% CI: 1.39–1.77) {23001122;27149063}. The prevalence of TOC cases arising in those with RAD51C or RAD51D GPVs may vary in different populations {32442341}. The estimated cumulative risks of developing TOC to age 80 years were 11% for RAD51C and 13% for RAD51D pathogenic variant carriers, whereas the estimated cumulative risks of developing breast cancer to 80 years were 21% for RAD51C and 20% for RAD51D pathogenic variant carriers {32107557}. The breast and ovarian cancer risks associated with RAD51B GPVs are not established but may be increased {34635660}. For all three paralogs, there may be increased risk of other cancers (especially pancreas and prostate cancers), but further data are required to confirm this.

Etiology

RAD51 paralog–associated cancer predisposition syndromes are due to germline alterations in the RAD51 paralogs. To date, GPVs in the three paralogs (RAD51C, RAD51D, RAD51B) have been linked to cancer predisposition {20400964;21822267;26261251;34923718;34635660}. They follow an autosomal dominant model of inheritance.

Germline loss-of-function variants in RAD51B, RAD51C, and RAD51D can be located along the gene. Missense variants in the Walker domains may be pathogenic {20400964;16236763;28646019;28829762;34923718}.

Pathogenesis

RAD51 paralogs form a multiprotein complex composed of RAD51C, RAD51D, RAD51B, XRCC2, and XRCC3. The RAD51 complex is essential in DNA repair mediated by homologous recombination to resolve double-strand breaks in the DNA and collapsed replication fork resolution during DNA synthesis {32301983}. If not repaired, double-strand breaks promote genomic instability and can lead to the development of cancer.

RAD51C is located at 17q22 and encodes a nuclear protein of 376 amino acids, which interacts directly with XRCC3 to form the CX3 subcomplex and with RAD51B, RAD51D, and XRCC2 to form the BCDX2 subcomplex. The two subcomplexes play sequential roles in homologous recombination–mediated repair {23149936}. RAD51D is located at 17q12 and encodes a nuclear protein of 328 amino acids. RAD51B is located at 14q24.1 and encodes a protein of 350 amino acids. Two specific domains named Walker A and Walker B are present in the three paralogs and have been shown to be essential in the functioning of the gene.

The main phenotypic consequence of GPVs in RAD51C and RAD51D is an increased risk of developing high-grade serous TOC {20400964;21822267}. An increased risk of developing breast cancer (mainly the triple-negative subtype) has also been demonstrated {32107557;33471991}. Single studies of prostate and pancreas cancer identifying carriers of RAD51C and RAD51D pathogenic variants have been described {27433846;29961768}. SNVs in RAD51B have been described to be associated with an increased risk of breast cancer, including male breast cancer {23001122;27149063}. Loss-of-function germline variants in RAD51B have been described in breast and ovarian cancer cases {34635660}. RAD51C has been proposed as a Fanconi anaemia gene (FANCO), as biallelic missense variants have been associated with a Fanconi anaemia–like phenotype {20400963}.

Hereditary forms of TOC are most often caused by germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 or BRCA2, two key members of the homologous recombination repair pathway (see section BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndromes). However, not all families with high-grade serous TOC have BRCA1 or BRCA2 GPVs identified. In these situations, GPVs affecting other members of the homologous recombination repair pathway, including RAD51C and RAD51D (see also section PALB2-related cancer predisposition syndrome), can result in a clinical picture that is very similar to that caused by GPVs in BRCA1 or BRCA2. Patients with biallelic pathogenic variants affecting RAD51C or RAD51D (whether germline or somatic in provenance) may benefit from treatment with the poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors {33941784;36176748;28646019;23512992;25292178}, which interfere with the single strand–break repair enzyme PARP, resulting in accumulation of unrepaired double-strand breaks that destabilize the genome and lead to cell death through synthetic lethality {29660759}. Resistance mechanisms include secondary variants in the RAD51C or RAD51D paralogs {32563252}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearances of high-grade serous ovarian cancer and breast cancers are indistinguishable from those of their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

The characteristic pathological features that accompany homologous recombination repair–associated triple-negative breast cancer and high-grade TOCs are present in both hereditary and non-hereditary forms of these tumours and are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Cytology

The cytology of high-grade serous ovarian cancer and breast cancers is indistinguishable from that of their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic testing of RAD51 paralog GPVs using peripheral blood or other normal cells is generally based on family history and clinicopathological characteristics. Germline testing for RAD51C or RAD51D variants in familial TOC falls within the ClinGen clinical validity framework {30504931}. Genetic testing is recommended when the criteria for BRCA1 and BRCA2 genetic testing proposed by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) {33406487} and the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) [[Familial breast cancer: classification, care and managing breast cancer and related risks in people with a family history of breast cancer. (2019). National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE). Bookref]] are met and no alterations in these genes are identified. Tumours with a deficiency in homologous recombination can be identified by their molecular signature of so-called BRCAness. There are several diagnostic and molecular methods to measure BRCAness.

RAD51C, RAD51D, and RAD51B variant interpretation should be evaluated synthetically with the use of information from population- and disease-related databases, patient medical histories, and literature, according to the guidelines from the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) (updated in 2015) {25741868}. Only variants considered pathogenic or likely pathogenic have clinical significance. Specific guidelines for variant curation are under elaboration by ClinGen variant curation expert panels, such as the Hereditary Breast, Ovarian and Pancreatic Cancer Variant Classification Expert Panel (https://clinicalgenome.org/site/assets/files/5986/clingen_hbop_coi.pdf).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of a germline RAD51C, RAD51D, or RAD51B pathogenic variant.

Desirable: family history of an associated tumour, usually tubo-ovarian or breast cancer.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Other than case reports, there are no reports of differences in outcome after a cancer diagnosis based on RAD51C or RAD51D status.
