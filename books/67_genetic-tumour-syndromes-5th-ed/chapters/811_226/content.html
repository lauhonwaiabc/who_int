8.1.1 BAP1-related tumour predisposition syndrome (BAP1)

Definition

BAP1-related tumour predisposition syndrome (BAP1-TPDS) is a hereditary cancer syndrome caused by autosomal dominant inheritance of heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in the BAP1 tumour suppressor gene.

MIM numbering

614327 Tumour predisposition syndrome 1; TPDS1

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: BAP1 loss familial cancer syndrome; BAP1 hereditary cancer syndrome.

Not recommended: cutaneous/ocular melanoma, atypical melanocytic proliferations, and other internal neoplasms (COMMON) syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Multiple organs are involved, including the eyes {21051595;21941004;32273508}, pleura, peritoneum {21874000}, skin {21874003;25225168}, kidney {23684012}, liver {24243779}, and meninges {21941004;26409435;25225168;28170043;30517737}.

Clinical features

Affected individuals can have multiple types of primary cancers {30517737}. In people with BAP1-TPDS, the median age of tumour onset is younger than in the general population {25380601;26719535;30517737;29981911;32509949}. A common manifestation of BAP1-TPDS is multiple BAP1-inactivated melanocytic naevi/tumours. Beginning in the second decade of life, affected patients frequently develop multiple inconspicuous, skin-coloured to reddish-brown, 4–12 mm, dome-shaped, well-circumscribed papules, predominantly on the sun-exposed skin. Some of these BAP1-inactivated melanocytic naevi/tumours display small brown areas at their periphery, likely because they evolve from conventional naevi. The number of lesions varies, typically ranging from a few to > 50 {21874003}. They occur in three quarters of germline carriers {28793149;31206729} yet are not specific for the syndrome {28560743}.

Individuals with germline BAP1 pathogenic variants have an increased risk of developing uveal melanoma, peritoneal and pleural mesothelioma, cutaneous melanoma, and clear cell renal cell carcinomas {30517737}. Mesotheliomas have substantially different clinical features when arising in BAP1-TPDS: they arise 20 years earlier, lack male predominance, and are frequently peritoneal rather than pleural {25380601;26719535;30376426}. In addition to these main tumour types, patients may develop basal cell carcinoma {25080371}, meningioma {21941004}, cholangiocarcinoma {24185509}, and hepatocellular carcinoma {31862487}, and probably other cancer types, including leptomeningeal melanoma {25900292}, lung adenocarcinoma {21941004}, thyroid cancer {26774355}, paraganglioma {22889334}, bladder cancer {32218990}, and ovarian sex cord stromal tumour {35348477}.

Epidemiology

Over 180 families have been described with 140 unique pathogenic germline BAP1 variants, 104 of which are null variants and 9 are missense variants in the UCH domain {30517737}. Carrier frequency of germline pathogenic variants is about 1 in 50 000 in people without cancer, and about 1 in 1900 among patients with cancer, suggesting that the syndrome prevalence is underestimated {29761599}. The most prevalent founder variant in BAP1 is NM_004656.4:c.1717del p.(Leu573TrpfsTer3) {30517737}. Lifetime risk of developing cancer is reported as high as 80–100%, with many carriers developing multiple cancers; most families have at least two types of tumours in first- or second-degree relatives {26096145;30517737}. Null variants predispose to earlier tumour formation than missense variants {30517737}.

While BAP1 is the most frequently mutated gene in sporadic mesothelioma {26928227} and in metastatic uveal melanoma {21051595;21941004;32273508}, < 1–4% of these BAP1-mutant tumours arise in germline carriers {21874000;25468148;28482042;31058963}. This contrasts with meningioma, in which half of BAP1-mutant meningiomas in a small series arose in germline carriers {28170043;28482042}. BAP1-mutant meningiomas are < 1% of all meningiomas.

Etiology

BAP1-TPDS is caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in the tumour suppressor gene BAP1, located at chromosome 3p21.1 (see #38714Table 9.01). The variants are most commonly deleterious nonsense, frameshift, or splice-site variants that result in markedly reduced or totally absent protein expression (null variants), or deleterious missense variants that interfere with the ubiquitin hydrolase function of BAP1 {23277170}. Interestingly, BAP1 germline variants have also recently been reported in a neurodevelopmental disorder (Kury–Isidor syndrome, MIM number: 619762) {35051358}.

Pathogenesis

The BAP1 gene at chromosome 3p21.1 comprises 17 exons encoding a protein with ubiquitin carboxyl hydrolase activity, first identified as interacting with BRCA1 {9528852}, a nuclear deubiquitinating enzyme {19117993;21642991}. BAP1 interactors include ASXL1 and ASXL2, FOXK1 and FOXK2, HCFC1, and YY1, consistent with roles in the DNA damage response, transcriptional regulation, cell-cycle regulation, metabolism, inflammatory responses, and lineage differentiation {28482042;28713672;30517737;31555735}. Nuclear localization and deubiquitination activity are both necessary for BAP1-mediated tumour suppression {18757409}. Inactivation of BAP1 in tumours may occur due to loss-of-function variants altering the BAP1 nucleotide sequence, often combined with a chromosomal deletion involving the wildtype BAP1 locus.

Macroscopic appearance

Similar to sporadic tumours

Histopathology

In most settings (skin, eye, thorax), the histological features of the tumours occurring in the setting of BAP1-TPDS are the same as those of tumours occurring sporadically.

Cytology

Similar to sporadic tumours

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Loss of BAP1 immunoreactivity in tumour nuclei readily identifies deficient tumours. Concordance between immunohistochemistry and genotyping is high but incomplete {21642991;24633195;25658628;27015033}. The vast majority of reported BAP1 pathogenic germline variants are < 51 bp in length, as opposed to large deletions or rearrangements {29165669}. Germline BAP1 null variants (nonsense, indels, frameshift, and splice site) are generally classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic. However, most missense variants are currently classified as variants of uncertain significance {29165669;25741868}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of a germline pathogenic variant in BAP1.

Staging

Staging is as for sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

Penetrance is incomplete, but the precise risk of an individual patient with BAP1-TPDS developing one or more of the associated tumours has not yet been determined. Additional environmental factors may increase the risk; for example, ultraviolet (UV) radiation and asbestos exposure may increase the risk of melanoma and mesothelioma, respectively. Prognosis depends on the tumour type and the stage of the malignancies. BAP1-inactivated melanocytic tumours of the skin have a good prognosis and rarely metastasize {23277170}.

Some studies suggest that individuals with BAP1-TPDS who develop mesothelioma have a longer survival than those with sporadic mesothelioma (a better 5-year survival: 47% vs 6.7%) {25380601;26719535;31323388}. However, this finding may be due to an epiphenomenon. Indeed, BAP1-associated mesotheliomas are more often epithelioid, and, as observed in their sporadic counterpart, they have a better prognosis than sarcomatoid mesotheliomas {27223342}. Uveal melanomas in BAP1 germline pathogenic variant carriers may be more aggressive than sporadic cases {25974357;22545102}. BAP1-mutant meningiomas display aggressive behaviour with frequent recurrences {28170043;28482042}; however, it is unclear if meningiomas arising in BAP1-TPDS have a different prognosis compared with sporadic BAP1-mutant meningioma.

Cancer screening criteria for uveal melanoma, cutaneous melanoma, mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, and renal cell carcinoma have been proposed for carriers of BAP1 germline pathogenic variants. To identify tumours at early stages, screening includes eye and skin examinations and ultrasound/MRI {26096145;29413689;30517737;31382694}. BAP1-inactivated melanocytic tumours often precede the onset of other tumours, and their recognition may facilitate early syndrome detection.
