1.5.3 AXIN2-related polyposis (AXIN2)

Definition

AXIN2-related polyposis is an autosomal dominant syndrome due to germline (constitutional) loss-of-function variants in AXIN2 and is characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps, increased risk of colorectal cancer, and variable extracolonic features of oligodontia and ectodermal dysplasia.

MIM numbering

608615 AXIN2-associated polyposis (oligodontia-colorectal cancer syndrome); ODCRCS

ICD-11 coding

2E92.40 Polyposis syndrome

Related terminology

Acceptable: oligodontia-colorectal cancer syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

AXIN2-related polyposis primarily affects the colorectum.

If present, oligodontia affects the teeth (tooth agenesis with reduced numbers of teeth) and ectodermal dysplasia affects the hair, nails, skin and its appendages such as sweat glands, and salivary glands.

Clinical features

Not all families with adenomatous polyposis (either classic or attenuated phenotype) have germline pathogenic APC variants identified. Other germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) can affect an alternative WNT pathway component, AXIN2, and this has been associated with colorectal polyposis and an increased risk of colorectal cancer. However, only a relatively small number of such affected families have been described. AXIN2 variant carriers have a mean age at diagnosis of colorectal cancer (CRC) / polyposis of 52.5 ± 9.2 years. Colorectal polyps are typically pancolonic, ranging in number from 5 to > 100 (median 12.5), comprising predominantly adenomatous polyps, but some hyperplastic or serrated polyps are also seen {34817745;30671715}. De novo loss-of-function AXIN2 GPVs have been reported although in cases without a reported colonic phenotype.

Oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia may be observed in carriers from some but not all families, and some only have oligodontia. Sparse hair and eyebrows have been included as part of the ectodermal dysplasia phenotype {15042511;21416598;26025668;23838596;34817745}. Penetrance appears to be incomplete. There may be increased risk of other cancers (e.g. hepatocellular carcinoma), but further data are required to confirm this {28759178}.

Epidemiology

Only a relatively small number of such affected families have been described, as yet.

Etiology

Loss-of-function GPVs in AXIN2 cause AXIN2-related polyposis with or without associated oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia {34817745}.

The catalogue of reported AXIN2 variants can be found in the Leiden Open Variation Database [[Leiden University Medical Center. LOVD v.3.0 - Leiden Open Variation Database (Internet). Leiden (Kingdom of the Netherlands): Leiden University Medical Center; 2021. The AXIN2 gene homepage; updated 2025 Jun 16. Available from: https://databases.lovd.nl/shared/genes/AXIN2. Bookref]].

Pathogenesis

The role of the WNT pathway is well established in the pathogenesis of colorectal neoplasia. AXIN2 is a scaffold protein that forms part of the so-called destruction complex together with the APC protein, which targets β-catenin (the signalling intermediate of the WNT pathway) for degradation: AXIN2 supports the GSK3β-dependent phosphorylation of β-catenin, which marks the protein for ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation {25236910;22745173}. AXIN2 is induced by WNT signals, and as such acts as a negative regulator of WNT signalling {11809808}. Inherited AXIN2 pathogenic variants have been described in families with adenomatous polyposis and ectodermal dysplasia and/or oligodontia, but they have also been described in individuals with adenomatous polyposis and no ectodermal dysplasia/oligodontia {15042511;21416598;23838596}. Some CRCs show evidence of a second or somatic hit to AXIN2 via loss of heterozygosity {34817745}. The paucity of published AXIN2-related polyposis families limits genotype–phenotype correlations (colorectal polyposis, colorectal cancer, and oligodontia / ectodermal dysplasia) {30671715}. Published AXIN2 variants indicate a potential mutation hotspot centred on exon 8.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearances of adenomas and colorectal cancers are closely similar to those of their sporadic counterparts {34817745}.

Histopathology

The colorectal neoplasms in AXIN2-related polyposis show a similar morphology to sporadic colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas {23838596}. Colorectal hyperplastic/serrated lesions may also be seen in this syndrome.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Adenomatous polyposis is most frequently caused by GPVs in either APC or (biallelic variants in) MUTYH. After exclusion of these, families with adenomatous polyposis (5 to > 100 polyps), some with CRCs, especially if there is evidence of oligodontia or ectodermal dysplasia, should be investigated for germline AXIN2 GPVs.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: familial adenomatous polyposis (5 to > 100 polyps) with increased risk of CRC, with or without oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia; exclusion of GPVs in APC and MUTYH; identification of AXIN2 GPVs.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Given the small number of published cases, there are currently insufficient data regarding the prognosis and prediction of tumours arising in the setting of AXIN2-related polyposis.
