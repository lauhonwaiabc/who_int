1.1.1 Hereditary papillary renal carcinoma (MET)

Definition

Hereditary papillary renal carcinoma (HPRC) involves a genetic predisposition to the development of bilateral and multifocal papillary renal cell adenomas and carcinomas. It is transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait with incomplete penetrance, and the syndrome is associated with activating germline variants of MET (7q31.2).

Localization

The tumours occur in the renal cortex and are bilateral and multifocal.

Clinical features

Radiological examination shows multiple and bilateral renal cell tumours, characterized by well-encapsulated masses with homogeneous low signal intensity on T2-weighted images, and homogeneous low-level enhancement after intravenous administration of contrast material. There is usually a family history of renal cancer {7853572;8308957;16434318;15371818;26559379}. No other types of carcinoma or non-cancerous health problems are known to be related to HPRC.

Epidemiology

HPRC is rare with an estimated incidence of 1 in 500 000 individuals {29680948}. It affects patients between the fourth and sixth decades of life. The number of affected individuals and families with HPRC is unknown.

Etiology

HPRC is caused by germline activating substitution variants in the MET proto-oncogene at 7q31.2. Partial duplication of chromosome 7q21-q35 and trisomy of chromosome 7 have been found in hereditary and sporadic papillary renal cell carcinoma {8536226}. SNVs located in the kinase domain of chromosome 7q31-q34 lead to constitutive activation of the c-Met protein. Reported variants and their clinical significance are shown in #37020Table 2.01 {9140397;33527590}. The transmission pattern is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with incomplete penetrance. It is an extremely rare disorder, affecting approximately 1 in 500 000 individuals {27075350}.

Pathogenesis

The MET gene encodes the c-Met oncoprotein, a member of the receptor tyrosine kinase family. The alpha and beta subunits form the receptor that binds to HGF, its only known ligand. This induces dimerization and activation of the receptor, which affects cellular survival, embryogenesis, and cellular migration and invasion {28603720}.

Macroscopic appearance

HPRC manifests as bilateral and multiple solid renal tumours, ranging from nodules of a few millimetres to larger masses; the neoplasms may be encapsulated or unencapsulated {24025519;33664427}.

Histopathology

Multifocal, macroscopic, and microscopic papillary adenomas and carcinomas (formerly known as type 1 papillary carcinomas) are present. Papillary renal cell carcinomas of this type have papillae covered by a single layer of cuboidal cells with mostly scant basophilic cytoplasm. Psammoma bodies may be present and macrophages are typically abundant. In addition to papillary renal cell carcinomas, tubulopapillary adenomas (≤ 15 mm) are also seen {24025519;33664427}. Some papillary carcinomas in HPRC have also been reported with so-called biphasic alveolosquamoid morphology, showing smaller cells with scant cytoplasm arranged in alveolar-like structures, with a second population of larger eosinophilic cells, frequently exhibiting emperipolesis {32770124}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPRC is caused by germline missense MET variants. Trisomy of chromosome 7 may be seen.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: family history of papillary renal cell carcinoma with multiple affected family members, or when an individual has bilateral, multifocal papillary renal cell carcinoma; germline activating variant of MET.

 

See #39025Table 2.02.

Staging

Staging follows the ninth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {9355980}.

Prognosis and prediction

The risk of low-grade papillary renal cell carcinoma in families with HPRC is extremely high, with some estimates showing the lifetime risk as nearly 100%. Active surveillance and treatment strategies are available {29680948;30017400;36602871}.
