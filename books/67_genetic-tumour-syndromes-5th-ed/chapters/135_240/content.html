Definition

Schimmelpenning–Feuerstein–Mims (SFM) syndrome is an epidermal naevus syndrome caused by somatic postzygotic mosaic mutation of HRAS, NRAS, or KRAS, characterized by linear naevus sebaceus and extracutaneous abnormalities, mainly neurological, ocular, and skeletal.

MIM numbering

163200 Schimmelpenning–Feuerstein–Mims syndrome; SFM

ICD-11 coding

LD2D.Y Other specified phakomatoses or hamartoneoplastic syndromes

Related terminology

Acceptable: Schimmelpenning syndrome; Feuerstein–Mims syndrome; linear naevus sebaceus syndrome; naevus sebaceus syndrome; Jadassohn–Schimmelpenning–Feuerstein–Mims syndrome.

Not recommended: epidermal naevus syndrome; organoid naevus phakomatosis; organoid naevus syndrome; Jadassohn naevus phakomatosis; Jadassohn sebaceous naevus syndrome; Solomon syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Skin is the main site affected, particularly the craniofacial area. SFM syndrome may also affect other organs, especially the CNS, eyes, and bones.

Clinical features

Although the triad of naevus sebaceus, seizures, and developmental disabilities was initially applied for the recognition of SFM syndrome, its use has been abandoned because the condition extends well beyond. The main feature is the linear naevus sebaceus, appearing as smooth and yellowish or verrucous and brownish plaques along the Blaschko lines and affecting mainly the craniofacial area {23597534}. Although neurological abnormalities are the most common extracutaneous anomalies, with seizures, developmental disabilities, enlargement of one lateral ventricle, and hemimegalencephaly being the major alterations {9746301}, they are not detectable in all patients. Kyphoscoliosis, hemihypertrophy of the limbs, skull asymmetry, and craniofacial exostosis are the main skeletal alterations {9746301;6504316;34650868;23597534}. Ophthalmological abnormalities are observed in 59% of patients and include colobomas and choristomas {3580338;9746301}. Cardiovascular impairments, mainly aortic coarctation and septal defect, are occasionally observed {3580338;34109654}. Genitourinary alterations (horseshoe kidney, arterial duct persistence, duplication of the excretory system) may be present {31402313}. Lymphoid abnormalities are also reported {34435383;29381910;34109654}. The main oral findings are papillomatous mucosal lesions, which may be contiguous with cutaneous naevus sebaceus {31402313}. Some patients also develop central giant cell lesion of the jaws, ameloblastoma, adenomatoid odontogenic tumour, odontoma, and fibrous dysplasia. Multiple adenomatoid odontogenic tumours have been observed {31402313;32770291}. Malignant neoplasms are rare {34109654}.

Epidemiology

The estimated number of affected individuals is currently unknown {29861184}.

Etiology

SFM syndrome is a mosaic RASopathy. An activating HRAS postzygotic somatic mutation, NM_005343.4:c.37G>C p.(Gly13Arg), is the main genetic alteration {22683711}, detected in > 85% of cases {34109654}. Other sequence variants, in HRAS (NM_005343.4:c.35G>A p.(Gly13Asp), NM_005343.4:c.38G>T p.(Gly13Val), and NM_005343.4:c.35G>A p.(Gln61Arg) {23096709;34109654}), KRAS (NM_033360.2:c.34G>T p.(Gly12Cys), NM_004985.5:c.35G>A p.(Gly12Asp), NM_033360.3:c.35G>T p.(Gly12Val), and NM_004985.5:c.436G>A p.(Ala146Thr) {22683711;26498739;30891959;34208656;34254724;26521233}), and NRAS (NM_002524.5:c.35G>A p.(Gln12Asp) {25914220}) have also been reported.

Pathogenesis

Autosomal lethal postzygotic sequence variants affect HRAS, KRAS, and/or NRAS and survive through mosaicism {3033033;10398256;22683711}. The RAS/RAF/MAPK and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathways are constitutively activated in the mutant cells {22683711;23096709;34109654} and have a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of naevus sebaceus, secondary cutaneous neoplasms, and multiorgan involvement {22683711;25914220}. Therefore, the earlier the mutations occur in embryonic development, the greater the proportion of mutant multipotent progenitor cells and, consequently, the extent of phenotypic abnormalities {3033033;22683711;25219651;31402313;34254724}. Recently, monism was included as one of the pathological processes underlying the phenotypic pleiotropism observed in the syndrome {34109654} owing to the coexistence of RAS mutations in both naevus sebaceus and some extracutaneous manifestations {34109654;34208656}. However, the exact mechanism of the distribution of a mutant cell clone during embryogenesis remains to be further elucidated.

Macroscopic appearance

Naevus sebaceus usually presents as a verrucous (cobblestone-like) plaque with a greasy appearance and yellowish colour, devoid of hair.

Histopathology

Naevus sebaceus becomes clinically prominent at puberty and attains the characteristic histopathological features where the epidermis shows hyperkeratosis and acanthosis in a verrucous/papillary pattern {19751879}. Hyperplasia of large sebaceous glands is the hallmark of naevus sebaceus. These are located high within the dermis and can communicate directly with the epidermal surface. No mature hair follicles are associated with the abnormal sebaceous glands; primitive hair follicles can be observed usually at the lesion periphery. Heterotopic apocrine glands may be present. Secondary cutaneous tumours have been reported to develop later in life, mostly benign (trichoblastoma, syringocystadenoma papilliferum) and rarely malignant (basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, sebaceous carcinoma) {21995782;24341474}.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Screening for RAS mutations in naevus sebaceus, secondary cutaneous tumours, and, whenever possible, tissue samples from extracutaneous manifestations is important for diagnosis. As SFM syndrome is a mosaic RASopathy, mutations are not detected in whole blood and unaffected tissues.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: congenital linear skin naevus sebaceus (often on scalp, face); RAS (HRAS, KRAS, NRAS) mutations; at least one extracutaneous abnormality.

Desirable: various abnormalities ipsilateral to naevus sebaceus, including CNS abnormalities (developmental disability, seizures, hemimegalencephaly), ocular abnormalities (coloboma, choristoma), skeletal defects (short stature, incomplete formation of bony structures, hypophosphataemic rickets), and vascular anomalies (aortic coarctation/aneurysm, carotid and/or renal artery stenosis); rare oral anomalies (ameloblastoma, odontoma, adenomatoid odontogenic tumour, as well as giant cell granuloma of the jawbones); elevated plasma levels of FGF.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Since SFM syndrome is a multisystem condition, the prognosis depends on clinical manifestations and their severity. Therefore, management is individualized and symptom based, aiming to investigate obvious or suspected abnormalities. It usually encompasses an interdisciplinary approach, involving specialties, such as dermatology, neurology, ophthalmology, orthopaedy, cardiology, oral and plastic surgery, and others. Imaging is crucial for assessing CNS abnormalities {6504316;19751880;29453776}. Skin biopsy is needed for establishing a diagnosis of naevus sebaceus and for any suspected neoplastic change that might occur in this lesion. Laboratory studies (serum/urine calcium and phosphate, liver and renal function tests) and cardiac screening should be performed as necessary. Rare cases of fatal outcomes have been reported, mainly due to significant neurological damage {20949522;4440656}. KRAS and HRAS sequence variants could be associated with the excessive production of FGF23 {16222671}, whose action results in phosphate and calcium wasting and leads to the development of vitamin D–resistant rickets. Patients with SFM syndrome–associated hypophosphataemia could benefit from treatment with neutralizing antibodies that target FGF23 {31485552;33204932}.
