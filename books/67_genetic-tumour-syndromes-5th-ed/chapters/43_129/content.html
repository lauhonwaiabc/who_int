4.3 Base excision repair

Base excision repair repairs small base lesions within DNA throughout the cell cycle. It removes damaged bases that could otherwise produce variants by mispairing or causing breaks in DNA during replication. Base excision repair is initiated by DNA glycosylases, which recognize and remove specific damaged or inappropriate bases, forming apurinic/apyrimidinic sites. These are then cleaved by an apurinic/apyrimidinic endonuclease. The single-strand break produced can then be repaired by either short-patch base excision repair (where a single nucleotide is replaced) or long-patch base excision repair (where 2–10 new nucleotides are synthesized).

MUTYH-associated polyposis is caused by germline biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) inactivating variants in MUTYH {17161978}, which encodes a DNA glycosylase enzyme that excises adenines misincorporated opposite 8-oxo-7,8-dihydro-2′-deoxyguanosine (8-oxoguanine), which is a stable product of oxidative DNA damage from excessive reactive oxygen species production {19414147;28087410;32307808;34142156}. MUTYH deficiency appears to have different impacts on various tissues according to the degree of oxidative stress {31505270}, and colorectal adenomas arise as a result of the acquisition of somatic APC mutations {12606733;23599153}. Somatic hotspot mutations in KRAS (NM_004985.5:c.34G>T p.(Gly12Cys)) and PIK3CA (NM_006218.4:c.1636C>A p.(Gln546Lys)) are associated with colorectal cancers in biallelic MUTYH-variant carriers {35668106}.

NTHL1-related tumour syndrome {25938944;32239880} is a germline DNA repair disorder of base excision repair, caused by recessively inherited (homozygous or compound heterozygous) inactivating variants in NTHL1. The NTHL1 glycosylase enzyme repairs a variety of DNA lesions, including 5-hydroxycytosine and 5-hydroxyuracil, two products of cytosine oxidation that are mutagenic because they mispair with adenine, resulting in C>T transitions {29105096}. The increased mutation rate caused by NTHL1 deficiency contributes to tumour development.

MBD4-associated neoplasia syndrome, also known as MBD4-related tumour predisposition syndrome, is a recently recognized autosomal recessive tumour predisposition syndrome, caused by biallelic loss-of-function germline variants in MBD4, which encodes the MBD4 glycosylase. MBD4 is responsible for base excision repair of deaminated 5-methylcytosine residues which give rise to G:T mismatches at CpG dinucleotides in DNA {30049810;31322271}. MBD4 binds preferentially to 5-methylcytosine CpG:TpG mismatches, which are the primary product of deamination at methyl-CpG sites. Complete loss of functional MBD4 protein (resulting from biallelic inactivation of MBD4) causes accumulation of C>T transitions at CpG dinucleotides (mCpG>TpG) {35381620;35460607}.
