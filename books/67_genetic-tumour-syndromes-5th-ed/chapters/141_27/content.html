1.4.1 Carney complex (PRKAR1A, PDE8B, PDE11A)

Definition

Carney complex (CNC) is an autosomal dominant syndrome characterized by myxomas, endocrinopathy, and pigmented skin lesions; the main CNS manifestation is malignant melanotic nerve sheath tumour. In > 70% of patients a heterozygous inactivating germline pathogenic variant (GPV) is detected in the PRKAR1A gene coding for the type 1α regulatory (R1α) subunit of PKA.

MIM numbering

160980 Carney complex, type 1; CNC1

ICD-11 coding

5A70.Y Carney complex

Related terminology

Acceptable: LAMB syndrome (lentigines, atrial myxoma, mucocutaneous myoma, blue naevus syndrome); NAME syndrome (naevi, atrial myxoma, skin myxoma, ephelides syndrome); Carney syndrome; myxoma syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CNC affects multiple organ systems (including the skin, heart, gonads, and endocrine system) with multiple, usually benign, neoplasms {4010501}. Overactivity of endocrine organs, especially Cushing syndrome, may lead to other widespread systemic changes {11549623}.

Clinical features

Individuals with CNC most commonly present with pale brown to black lentigines that increase in number and intensity around puberty. These lesions commonly manifest at the vermillion border of the lips, conjunctivae, and inner and outer canthi. The vaginal and penile epithelia may also be involved. Development of unusual tumours is also a feature of the syndrome, such as myxomas, large cell calcifying Sertoli cell tumours, thyroid tumours (follicular adenomas or carcinomas), psammomatous melanotic schwannomas, blue naevi, pigmented epithelioid melanocytomas, ductal adenomas and myxoid fibroadenomas of the breast, pituitary adenomas, and/or osteochondromyxomas. Endocrinopathy may result from the pituitary adenomas or primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease, which may result in Cushing syndrome.

Cardiac myxomas are the second most common presenting feature, present in more than half of cases. The occurrence of cardiac myxoma is somewhat equally spread across the age spectrum, whereas other manifestations, such as primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease, tend to appear in the second to third decades of life and are more common among women. Large cell calcifying Sertoli cell tumours are seen in approximately a third of boys presenting in the first decade of life and will be seen almost universally in men. Thyroid nodules are a very common finding, seen in as many as three quarters of cases.

Epidemiology

CNC is a relatively rare syndrome, with < 1000 affected individuals having been identified. They have been described in various ethnicities around the globe, without a particular ethnicity predilection.

Etiology

Approximately two thirds of those diagnosed with CNC have an affected parent, with the remaining third having what appears to be a de novo GPV {19577711;24170103}.

Pathogenesis

Inactivation of PRKAR1A is observed in most CNC-related tumours {34359735;34532875;34780072}. It leads to increased activity of the catalytic subunits of PKA (encoded by PRKACA and PRKACB), the key components of the cAMP signalling pathway, which regulates the proliferation of tissues susceptible to developing CNC-associated tumours {12424709}. Activation of PRKACA due to PRKACA fusions or microinsertions has been shown to have a similar effect in fibrolamellar carcinoma of the liver and cardiac myxoma {29222914;28369983}, and suggests that aberrations involving components of the cAMP signalling pathway other than PRKAR1A may be responsible in PRKAR1A-wildtype CNC-associated tumours. PRKAR1A inactivation is usually superimposed on other background genetic abnormalities specific for the individual tumours {34359735}.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid tumours related to CNC have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

See the dedicated chapters in other volumes of the fifth edition of the WHO Classification of Tumours.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic linkage analysis has implicated two independent loci in CNC: 17q23-24 and 2p16. Inactivating PRKAR1A GPVs located at 17q24.2, coding for PRKAR1A, have been described in two thirds of CNC cases {15371594}. The gene at locus 2p16 remains unknown; however, the presence of an activated oncogene at this locus is considered to be responsible for tumorigenesis in at least a subset of patients {12676898;19577711}. Additional candidate genes that are the subjects of active study include PRKACB, PDE11A, and PDE8B.

More than 60% of cases of CNC are caused by an exonic GPV in PRKAR1A that can be detected by routine DNA sequencing. Approximately another 10–20% are detected by targeted deletion/duplication analysis {24170103}. The penetrance of a GPV in PRKAR1A is nearly 100% by age 50 years.

A diagnosis of CNC may be suspected by demonstrating loss of immunohistochemical expression of PRKAR1A in tumour cells, which should then be followed up with germline testing for an underlying variant in PRKAR1A {24618615}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Establishing a diagnosis of CNC requires two major criteria or a combination of one major criterion and one supplemental criterion (see #37511Box 2.04).

Although not formally in the diagnostic criteria, some conditions have been seen in association with CNC and therefore may raise suspicions for its diagnosis.

See also #39012Table 2.20.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Because cardiac myxomas are a serious cause of morbidity and mortality in this patient population, those diagnosed with CNC (or first-degree relatives at risk of having CNC) should receiving annual echocardiographic surveillance to evaluate for cardiac myxomas. In addition, annual testicular examinations and thyroid examinations should be considered after puberty in this population. Urinary free cortisol and serum IGF1 levels can also be a consideration. The finding of any cardiac myxoma should lead to evaluation for CNC, a diagnosis that necessitates follow-up for the patient and their relatives {24618615;22219403}.
