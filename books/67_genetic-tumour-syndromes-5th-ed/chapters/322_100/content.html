3.2.2 Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 4 (CDKN1B)

Definition

Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 4 (MEN4) is an autosomal dominant tumour syndrome caused by inactivating variants in the CDKN1B gene.

MIM numbering

610755 Multiple endocrine neoplasia, type IV; MEN4

600778 Cyclin-dependant kinase inhibitor 1B; CDKN1B

ICD-11 coding

2F7A.0 Multiple polyglandular tumours

Related terminology

Not recommended: Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1–like syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Patients with pathogenic germline inactivating variants of CDKN1B develop a clinical phenotype that mimics multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1); they have tumours that arise in the pituitary, parathyroid, and pancreas. Parathyroid disease is the most prevalent, occurring in about 75% of patients. Pituitary tumours are identified in about one third of patients. Other neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) have also been reported, including those arising in the lung, thymus, stomach, and small bowel {35323929}.

Clinical features

The diagnosis should be considered in a patient with primary hyperparathyroidism, diagnosed by an increase in serum levels of calcium and of intact PTH, associated with NET(s) of the pituitary and/or gastroenteropancreatic system, and a negative MEN1 genetic test using a technique that is able to detect point mutations, small and large deletions, and mosaicism. Gastrointestinal NETs are reported but seem to be less prevalent in MEN4 than in MEN1 {28824003;30990521;35323929}. Non-NETs and autoimmune diseases have been reported in a few patients with MEN4, but it is unclear if they are also caused by CDKN1B loss of function (see #39039Table 4.04).

Epidemiology

With < 50 published cases worldwide, MEN4 is a very rare disease. It has been identified mainly in patients who have presented as having MEN1 but are negative for variants in the MEN1 gene {36256846;35323929;32232325;36334246}. In reported series, they represent from 1.5% to 3.7% of that group of patients. There is a significant female predominance.

Etiology

MEN4 is caused by heterozygous germline loss-of-function variants in the CDKN1B gene {17030811;23652671}. It is an autosomal dominant disorder with incomplete penetrance in some families, which explains why patients may display either clearly familial or apparently sporadic presentation.

Pathogenesis

The CDKN1B gene (12p13.1) encodes the CDK inhibitor p27KIP1 (hereafter p27). The canonical role of p27 is inhibition of CDK activity. Specifically, nuclear p27 binds to and inhibits the cyclin E / CDK2 and cyclin A / CDK2 complexes that regulate progression from the G1 to the S phase of the cell cycle. The main targets of CDK2 are members of the retinoblastoma family of proteins. When these proteins are phosphorylated by CDK2, they release members of the E2F family, which in turn activate the transcription of genes responsible for G1 to S progression {23652671}. p27 mainly localizes to the nucleus in growth-arrested cells but it is translocated to the cytoplasm when the cells progress through the cell cycle. When in the cytosol, p27 binds cyclin D / CDK4–6 complexes and acts as an essential assembly factor for such complexes. As opposed to cyclin E / CDK2, cyclin D / CDK4–6 complexes remain catalytically active when bound to p27. Sequestering p27 in the cytoplasm prevents the inhibition of nuclear cyclin/CDK complexes, and ultimately promotes cell division {10699961;18354415}. In addition, p27 plays pro-oncogenic roles in the cytoplasm, which are independent of cyclin/CDK inhibition. Indeed, cytosolic p27 plays a role in cell migration and invasion, apoptosis, and autophagy {28687353}.

Although p27 stops cell proliferation when overexpressed, and when deleted in mice promotes tumour formation {8646781;8646779;8646780}, the rarity of somatic variants in human cancers has granted p27 the label of an atypical tumour suppressor. The identification of germline variants in patients with MEN4 established p27 as a bona fide tumour suppressor {17097557}.

The germline p27 variants so far identified in patients with MEN4 span the coding sequence and the 5′ untranslated region of the gene without obvious mutation hotspots. Most of the reported changes in CDKN1B are missense variants, followed by frameshift and nonsense alterations. For some of these variants, functional in vitro studies have been conducted, and the results have shown that they alter the protein’s localization, its tumour suppressive activity, or its binding with known partners. Altogether, the variants so far identified behave as loss-of-function variants and this is in agreement with the tumour suppressive role of p27.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross appearance of the tumours is not distinct from that of sporadic lesions.

Histopathology

Limited data are available on histopathological clues for a MEN4 diagnosis; no clear differences from sporadic cases are reported. The occurrence of multiple tumours of the spectrum is the only known hint {32035641}. Hyperparathyroidism is the most frequent presentation, with either uniglandular or polyglandular disease {28824003}. Pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs) include non-functioning lesions as well as tumours secreting growth hormone, prolactin, or ACTH. Pulmonary, thymic {35323929}, and ileal NETs have been reported in some patients and adrenocortical tumours are rare.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Owing to the variable presentation of MEN4, this diagnosis can only be established by genetic testing. Such testing is recommended in patients with a MEN1 phenotype who are negative for MEN1 variants {20833334}. Testing of patients with apparently sporadic isolated NETs is currently only carried out in research settings because, while deregulation of CDKN1B has been documented in multiple human neoplasms, somatic variants are rare {10699961;24185511}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: documented germline pathogenetic sequence variant of the CDKN1B gene; AND manifestation of pituitary, parathyroid, and/or gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasia.

Desirable: manifestation of pituitary, parathyroid, and/or gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasia in the absence of a pathogenic variant in the MEN1 gene.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Given the small number of cases reported so far, no genotype–phenotype correlations have been established, and cases should be treated in accordance with the clinical guidelines for their individual tumours. Other than ruling out the presence of hyperparathyroidism, there are no data to guide the clinical screening and follow-up of CDKN1B-variant carriers.
