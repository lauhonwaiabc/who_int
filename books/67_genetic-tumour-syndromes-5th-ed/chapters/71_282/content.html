7.1 MicroRNA

RNA regulation is achieved by several means. Small, single-stranded, non-coding RNA molecules containing 21–23 nucleotides known as microRNA are responsible for RNA silencing and post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression.

DICER1 is important in the production of microRNA, as it encodes an enzyme which cleaves the hairpin-structured precursor microRNA to 5′ and 3′ single-stranded mature microRNAs. The lack of 5′ microRNAs leads to overexpression of their target mRNAs, which in the proper cellular context (i.e. embryonic growth, endocrine stimulation) can lead to hyperplasia/neoplasia.

The microprocessor-related tumour predisposition syndromes are caused by heterozygous germline (or constitutional as preferred by some) pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in either DROSHA or its partner, DGCR8, both of which are key elements of the microRNA processor. The microprocessor cleaves primary microRNAs to generate pre-microRNAs, and its pathogenic variants are associated with the formation of a number of different tumour types.
