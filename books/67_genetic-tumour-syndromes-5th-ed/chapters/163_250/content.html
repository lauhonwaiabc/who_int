1.6.3 ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome (ELP1)

Definition

ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline pathogenic sequence variants in the ELP1 gene and characterized by an increased risk of medulloblastoma activated by sonic hedgehog (SHH) during childhood.

MIM numbering

155255 Medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome; MDB

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: ELP1-related medulloblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours develop in the cerebellum.

Clinical features

ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome is characterized by SHH-activated, TP53-wildtype medulloblastoma {32296180}. The median age at diagnosis of SHH-activated medulloblastoma is 6 years, with a reported range of 2–19 years {32296180;33741928;35199222}.

Epidemiology

Germline ELP1 pathogenic sequence variants are estimated to affect 0.1% of the general adult population {32296180}. ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome accounts for 15% of SHH-activated medulloblastoma during childhood and 30% of SHH subgroup 3 medulloblastomas {32296180;35199222}. Patients with ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome can have a family history of medulloblastoma {32296180;34687117}. The lifetime risk of SHH-activated medulloblastoma in ELP1 variant carriers and a wider paediatric and adult tumour spectrum remains currently unknown and warrants prospective, population-based studies. Tumour surveillance recommendations are expected from international study groups.

Etiology

ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome is caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic sequence variants in the ELP1 gene (located on chromosome 9 at position 9q31.3).

Pathogenesis

ELP1-related medulloblastomas are characterized by biallelic inactivation of ELP1 due to somatic loss of chromosome arm 9q {32296180;35199222}. Monosomy 9q is present in all reported tumours, and about 80% of ELP1-related medulloblastomas acquire a somatic sequence variant or focal deletion in the tumour suppressor gene PTCH1 (located on chromosome 9 at position 9q22.32). Based on these findings, a three-step model of tumorigenesis has been proposed for ELP1-related medulloblastomas {32296180}: step 1, heterozygous germline pathogenic ELP1 sequence variant; step 2, loss of the wildtype copy of ELP1 with concurrent monoallelic loss of PTCH1 due to a somatic deletion of chromosome arm 9q; step 3, biallelic inactivation of PTCH1 due to a somatic mutation or focal deletion within the remaining PTCH1 gene copy. ELP1-related medulloblastomas also exhibit somatic alterations that converge on the p53 pathway and include DNA amplification of the PPM1D (31%) and MDM4 (10%) genes, yet are mutually exclusive with somatic and germline TP53 variants {32296180}. ELP1 is the largest subunit of the highly conserved eukaryotic Elongator complex {9355980}, which catalyses translational elongation through transfer RNA (tRNA) modifications at wobble position 34 {15769872;16420352;25151136;5498072}. ELP1-related medulloblastomas are characterized by a destabilized Elongator complex, loss of Elongator-dependent tRNA modifications, codon-dependent dysregulation of protein expression, and an unfolded protein response {32296180}, consistent with Elongator studies in model systems {29497044;26651292;26052047}.

Macroscopic appearance

ELP1-related medulloblastomas are similar in appearance to sporadic medulloblastomas.

Histopathology

Patients with ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome are primarily diagnosed with desmoplastic/nodular medulloblastoma (80%) and less commonly with classic medulloblastoma, large cell / anaplastic medulloblastoma, and medulloblastoma with extensive nodularity {32296180;35199222}.

Loss of ELP1 protein expression and immunohistochemical staining in resected tumour material allows for identification of patients with ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome with high sensitivity and specificity {32296180;35199222}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Evaluation of cerebrospinal fluid cytology is required for staging {35151105;26769585}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Over 40 pathogenic ELP1 sequence variants have been found in patients with SHH-activated medulloblastoma and include frameshift, nonsense, and canonical splice variants {32296180;33741928;34687117;35199222}. Pathogenic missense variants and structural variants have been identified in 10% of cases {32296180}. Pathogenic ELP1 sequence variants detected in resected tumour tissue represent primarily (97%) heterozygous germline variants {32296180}. Monosomy 9q and, frequently, loss of PTCH1 via mutation or focal deletion are present in resected tumour material {32296180;35199222}. Pathogenic germline sequence variants in APC, BRCA2, PALB2, PTCH1, SUFU, GPR161, and TP53 are absent in patients with ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome {32296180;31609649;29753700}. Molecular classification based on DNA methylation profiling of resected tumour material identifies an SHH-activated subgroup 3 medulloblastoma {28609654;28545823;31664194} in over 90% of reported cases {32296180;33741928}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a heterozygous pathogenic germline sequence variant in the ELP1 gene in the context of an SHH-activated, TP53-wildtype medulloblastoma.

Desirable: loss of heterozygosity of the ELP1 gene in tumour material; loss of ELP1 protein expression on immunohistochemical staining in tumour material; a molecular tumour profile consistent with an SHH-activated subgroup 3 medulloblastoma.

Staging

Clinical staging procedures include MRI examinations of the CNS (brain and spinal cord) with a contrast agent. This is complemented by cerebrospinal fluid cytology at the time of diagnosis. The postoperative staging system developed by Chang and others {4983156} is still being used and defines the following degrees of metastatic spread:

  • M0 – no evidence of gross subarachnoid or haematogenous metastasis;
  • M1 – microscopic tumour cells found in the cerebrospinal fluid;
  • M2 – gross nodular seeding demonstrated in the cerebellar/cerebral subarachnoid space or in the third or lateral ventricles;
  • M3 – gross nodular seeding in the spinal subarachnoid space;
  • M4 – metastasis outside the cerebrospinal axis.

Prognosis and prediction

Preliminary data indicate that patients with ELP1-related medulloblastoma predisposition syndrome and SHH-activated medulloblastoma have a favourable clinical outcome (5-year overall survival rate of 92%; n = 17 patients) {32296180}.
