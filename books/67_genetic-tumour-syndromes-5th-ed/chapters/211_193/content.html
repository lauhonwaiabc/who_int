Definition

Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome (VHL) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by pathogenic germline variants of the VHL tumour suppressor gene, located at chromosome 3p25.3, and characterized by the development of haemangioblastoma of the CNS and retina, clear cell renal cell carcinoma (RCC), phaeochromocytoma, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PanNETs), and endolymphatic sac tumours (ELSTs).

193300 Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome; VHLS

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome type 1; von Hippel–Lindau syndrome type 2A; von Hippel–Lindau syndrome type 2B; von Hippel–Lindau syndrome type 2C

Localization

Haemangioblastomas most often involve the retina, cerebellum, and spinal cord, especially the paraspinal nerve roots, but can occur anywhere along the craniospinal axis, including peripheral nerves and even tissues outside the nervous system {15122713;16981244;17219830;24145646;29189208;29941223}. ELST arises from the vestibular aqueduct and may invade through the temporal bone and into the cerebellopontine angle {16322231;25944396}.

Clinical features

Retinal haemangioblastomas manifest earlier than RCCs (mean age 25 years), and thus offer the possibility of an early diagnosis {28650583}.

CNS haemangioblastomas develop mainly in young adults (mean age 29 years). They are predominantly located in the cerebellum, followed by the brain stem and spinal cord {29379961}. Approximately 25% of all cases of CNS haemangioblastoma are associated with hereditary VHL syndrome.

Renal cysts and clear cell RCCs are typically multifocal and bilateral. The mean patient age at manifestation is 37 years (vs 61 years for sporadic clear cell RCC), with a patient age at onset of 16–67 years. There is a 70% chance of developing clear cell RCC by the age of 70 years. Metastatic RCC is the leading cause of death from VHL syndrome.

Adrenal phaeochromocytomas arise in 20% of patients with VHL syndrome, and the mean age of onset is 30 years. Patients with VHL are also at risk of paraganglioma and carotid body tumours.

Pancreatic manifestations are predominantly multiple cysts but also NETs {29748190}.

Other VHL-associated tumours include ELSTs, associated with hearing loss, tinnitus, and vertigo, and epididymal and broad ligament cystadenomas.

Type 1 VHL is characterized by haemangioblastomas and RCCs but rare or absent phaeochromocytomas, and is typically caused by deletions and loss-of-function and missense variants. Type 2A is characterized by haemangioblastomas and phaeochromocytomas, but rarely RCCs, and is caused by missense variants. Type 2B is characterized by a high frequency of haemangioblastomas, RCCs, and phaeochromocytomas, and is mainly caused by missense variants. Type 2C is characterized by phaeochromocytomas but an absence of haemangioblastomas and RCCs and is mainly caused by VHL missense variants.

Epidemiology

VHL syndrome is estimated to have an annual incidence rate of 1 affected individual per 36 000 population.

Etiology

VHL syndrome is caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic sequence variants of the VHL gene at chromosome 3p25.3. Missense variants are the most common, but nonsense variants, microdeletions/insertions, splice-site variants, and large deletions also occur. The heterogeneous clinical manifestations of VHL syndrome are a reflection of the diversity of germline variants.

Pathogenesis

Inactivation of the VHL tumour suppressor gene in affected family members is responsible for their genetic susceptibility to tumour development at various organ sites, but the mechanisms by which the inactivation or loss of the suppressor gene product (VHL protein) causes neoplastic transformation are only partly understood {25533676}. The cell of origin (haemangioblast, stromal cell) is not well defined but current evidence points to a developmentally arrested haemangioblast precursor {14612494}. In accordance with the function of VHL as a tumour suppressor gene, variants are also common in sporadic haemangioblastomas (occurring in as many as 78% of cases) and are found in a high percentage of clear cell RCCs.

The VHL protein has many different functions and is critically involved in protein degradation. The alpha domain of the VHL protein forms a complex with elongin B (TCEB2), elongin C (TCEB1), cullin-2, and RBX1 (in conjunction called the VHL complex), which has ubiquitin ligase activity, thereby targeting cellular proteins for ubiquitination and proteasome-mediated degradation. The alpha domain of the gene involved in the binding to elongin C is frequently mutated in neoplasms associated with VHL syndrome.

The VHL protein plays a key role in cellular oxygen sensing, by ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of hypoxia-inducible factors (HIF1α and HIF2α) {9665461;10861440}, which mediate cellular responses to hypoxia. This leads to loss of VHL function with a pseudohypoxic state characterized by altered expression of genes that drive vascularization, cyst formation, lipid storage, metabolic adaptation, and extramedullary erythropoiesis. The beta domain of the VHL protein interacts with HIF1A. The hydroxylation of HIF1α on proline residues by oxygen-dependent prolyl hydroxylases generates a binding site for the VHL protein. Recruitment of the VHL ubiquitin ligase complex leads to the polyubiquitylation and proteasomal degradation of HIF1α and HIF2α. In the absence of functional VHL, HIF1α and HIF2α accumulate and activate the transcription of several hypoxia-inducible genes, including VEGFA, PDGFB, TGFA, and EPO, by binding to the respective hypoxia-responsive elements in the promoter region (leading to pseudohypoxia). Constitutive overexpression of VEGF-A {7533661;9370235} explains the extraordinary vascularization of neoplasms associated with VHL syndrome due to increased angiogenesis/vasculogenesis, as well as the formation of cysts due to increased vascular permeability (VEGF-A has also been denominated VPF, vascular permeability factor) {11245271}. Increased erythropoietin expression is common in haemangioblastomas {9787178} and is responsible for intratumoural (extramedullary) haematopoiesis and for the paraneoplastic erythrocytosis syndrome that can rarely occur in patients with VHL syndrome. HIF-dependent downregulation of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A leads to enhanced lipid storage, a characteristic of VHL-dependent tumours.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid tumours related to VHL syndrome have the same appearance as their sporadic counterparts, with frequent multifocality being a clue to syndromic disease.

Histopathology

The morphology of individual tumours arising in VHL syndrome is similar to that of their sporadic tumour counterparts. Of note, however, is that ELSTs presenting in the cerebellopontine angle may closely mimic choroid plexus papillomas. Neuroimaging is helpful in this differential because only ELSTs invade and destroy temporal bone.

VHL-related endocrine manifestations have characteristic histological and immunohistochemical features. Clear cell change especially in association with other cytomorphological findings (e.g. thick vascular tumour capsule, myxoid and hyalinized stroma, rich vascular network intermixed with tumour cells displaying variable clear and amphophilic cytoplasm, absence of cytoplasmic hyaline globules) has been regarded as a characteristic feature of VHL-related non-epithelial neuroendocrine neoplasms (phaeochromocytomas and paragangliomas, PPGLs) {12114747;25611110;29239044;35285002}. Membranous CAIX supports VHL syndrome, but can also be found in some succinate dehydrogenase–related PPGLs {23257898;33826547}. α-Inhibin was identified as a novel immunohistochemical biomarker of pseudohypoxia-related tumours, including VHL-related PPGLs {33826547}. Similarly to PPGLs, VHL-related NETs (epithelial neuroendocrine neoplasms) often display characteristic clear cell change {25611110;35294740}. The multifocal nature of PanNETs (> 5 mm) and/or presence of islet dysplasia, NETs (0.5–5 mm), or associated pancreatic cystic disease are harbingers of VHL-related pancreatic disease {25611110}. VHL-related NETs show α-inhibin and/or membranous CAIX expression {25611110;23913169;35294740}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of a VHL germline sequence variant is desirable to confirm the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the clinical diagnosis of VHL syndrome is based on the presence of capillary haemangioblastoma in the CNS or retina and the presence of one of the typical extraneural tumours or a pertinent family history.

Desirable: by genetic testing, a germline VHL pathogenic variant can virtually always be identified.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

The median life expectancy of patients with VHL syndrome is 49 years. Clinical surveillance guidance has been published {24355456;28620007}.
