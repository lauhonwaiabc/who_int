1.3.6 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour predisposition syndrome (KIT, PDGFRA)

Definition

Germline KIT and PDGFRA pathogenic variants cause autosomal dominant syndromes characterized by combinations of gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GISTs), inflammatory fibroid polyps, fibrous tumours, and disorders of mast cells, skin pigmentation, and gastrointestinal tract motility.

MIM numbering

175510 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour, somatic or familial (PDGFRA)

606764 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour, familial (KIT)

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial gastrointestinal stromal tumour syndrome (KIT or PDGFRA related).

Not recommended: gastrointestinal stromal tumour–plus syndrome; Devon polyposis syndrome; intestinal neurofibromatosis / neurofibromatosis 3b.

Subtype(s)

KIT-related gastrointestinal stromal tumour syndrome; PDGFRA-related gastrointestinal stromal tumour syndrome

Localization

In KIT-related syndromes, the GISTs arise throughout the gastrointestinal tract. The GISTs arising in PDGFRA families occur in the stomach, whereas the inflammatory fibroid polyps, fibrous tumours, and gastrointestinal tract fibrosis occur throughout the gastrointestinal tract {27318444;25975287;36802986}.

Clinical features

Patients with pathogenic germline KIT variants present with GISTs throughout the gastrointestinal tract. They are also prone to motility disorders (particularly dysphagia) associated with diffuse interstitial cell of Cajal hyperplasia (ICCH); skin pigmentation disorders, including hyperpigmentation, lentigines, and naevi, particularly around the mouth, genitals, hands, and feet; and mast-cell disorders, such as urticaria pigmentosa and systemic mastocytosis {27437068}. The KIT-related syndrome is not associated with inflammatory fibroid polyps.

In addition to gastric GISTs, the PDGFRA-related syndrome is associated with inflammatory fibroid polyps, distinctive fibrous tumours and areas of mucosal/submucosal or muscularis propria fibrosis, and gastrointestinal tract lipomas. PDGFRA variants are not associated with diffuse ICCH and dysmotility, pigmentation abnormalities, or mast-cell disorders {18670346;27437068;25975287;17566086;29486293}. Some patients with PDGFRA-related syndrome have coarse facial features and large hands, and premature tooth loss has been reported in one kindred {14699510;17087943;29486293}.

Epidemiology

KIT and PDGFRA germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) are rare causes of syndromic GISTs compared with tumour syndromes related to succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) deficiency (see section SDH-deficient tumour syndromes and section Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)). For example in one series of 499 patients with GIST, 16 (3.2%) were associated with SDH syndromes, 7 (1.4%) with NF1, and only 1 (0.2%) with a pathogenic germline KIT variant {36593350}; and at the time of writing, < 60 kindreds have been reported {28314314;37311038;39350996}. While KIT-related tumours demonstrate equal sex distribution, PDGFRA-related tumours have a female predilection (M:F 1:3) {27437068}. Compared with their counterparts with sporadic disease, patients with either KIT- or PDGFRA-related GIST syndromes present on average 10 years earlier {27437068}.

Etiology

These syndromes are caused by GPVs in either KIT or PDGFRA. KIT-related kindreds are 10 times as common as PDGFRA-related kindreds {28314314;37311038;39350996}.

Pathogenesis

As in sporadic GISTs, activating pathogenic variants in KIT and PDGFRA cause ligand-independent kinase activation and therefore tumorigenesis of GISTs. While some GISTs arising in these syndromes also show secondary molecular events, such as loss of heterozygosity, or chromosomal gains or losses, these are not required for tumorigenesis.

The involvement of KIT in the development and maintenance of interstitial cells of Cajal, melanocytes, and mast cells explains the development of ICCH, dysmotility, disorders of pigmentation, and mast-cell diseases.

Telocytes, recently described fibroblast-like CD34+/PDGFRA+ cells (previously termed interstitial Cajal-like cells), which proliferate and grow under the influence of PDGFRA, are reported to be the cell of origin of the fibroid polyps, fibrous tumours, and mucosal/submucosal fibrosis associated with PDGFRA variants {30117724}.

Macroscopic appearance

Other than multifocality, the GISTs and inflammatory fibroid polyps found in these syndromes are grossly indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts. The mucosal/submucosal fibrosis associated with PDGFRA may extend to the serosa or mesentery and be mistaken for a sclerosing malignancy or nonspecific fibrosis {18670346;25975287;30117724}.

Histopathology

The GISTs are identical to their sporadic counterparts and show normal preserved expression of SDHB. KIT-related tumours are usually spindled and distributed throughout the gastrointestinal tract, while PDGFRA-related GISTs are confined to the stomach and more frequently show epithelioid or mixed differentiation {29170162}. Diffuse ICCH is a clue to KIT-related GIST syndrome but does not occur in association with PDGFRA {29170162}. The inflammatory fibroid polyps are similar to their sporadic counterparts but form a continuum with less-cellular fibrous tumours {18670346;25975287;29486293;30117724}. The fibrous tumours are often densely collagenized but contain scattered CD34+ and KIT− spindled cells often with long slender cytoplasmic processes. Discontinuous extension of the fibrosis away from the polyp into the adjacent submucosa, serosa, or mesentery is a clue to separating hereditary from sporadic inflammatory fibrous polyps {29486293}.

Cytology

The cytological features of the GISTs are the same as those of their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

KIT and PDGFRA GPVs are rare, and there are no clear guidelines on who should be offered genetic counselling/testing. There is clearly a higher risk in younger patients with multifocal disease, a family history, and coexistent inflammatory fibroid polyps, or other syndromic features such as skin pigmentation changes or mastocytosis.

Although there seems to be some selection towards more favourable genotypes, the pathogenic variants found in the germline syndromes overlap with those found in tumours in sporadic disease {27437068;23610110}. Therefore, a pragmatic approach to assessing the possibility of these syndromes as the setting of multiple GISTs or inflammatory fibroid polyps is to first sequence the tumours using the standard panels employed for somatic testing of GISTs. If no KIT or PDGFRA variants are found, germline KIT or PDGFRA syndrome is effectively excluded, and therefore an SDH-deficient tumour syndrome and neurofibromatosis 1 become the most likely causes of syndromic disease. If a pathogenic KIT or PDGFRA variant is identified, a decision can be made on whether to proceed to germline testing based on local resourcing and the clinical risk of syndromic disease. If two separate primary GISTs are found to harbour the same KIT or PDGFRA variants, then these syndromes should be actively considered.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: confirmation of GPVs in either KIT or PDGFRA.

Desirable: multifocal GISTs (KIT and PDGFRA); ICCH, pigment disorders, mast-cell disorders, and dysmotility (KIT); inflammatory fibroid polyps and fibrous tumours (PDGFRA).

Staging

The individual tumours are staged according to the relevant Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging schemes.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis and treatment of the GISTs is the same as for sporadic tumours. However, because of the relatively small size and indolence of most of these GISTs and the propensity to multifocal disease, the decision of whether and when to operate is nuanced. The mitotic count and size can be used to predict the prognosis for individual tumours, and if tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy is required, the specifics of the KIT or PDGFRA variant present predict the likelihood of response or primary resistance {23598963;35791894;28710566}. Skin pigmentation abnormalities associated with KIT syndrome have also been reported to improve with tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy {28074523;28710566;32220019}. The inflammatory fibrous polyps and fibrous tumours associated with PDGFRA variants are benign {18670346;25975287;29486293}.
