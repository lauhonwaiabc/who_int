6.2.3 Clear cell meningioma predisposition syndrome (SMARCE1)

Definition

Clear cell meningioma predisposition syndrome (CCMPS) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline pathogenic variants in SMARCE1 leading to increased risk of developing clear cell meningiomas.

MIM numbering

607174 Meningioma, familial, susceptibility to

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial clear cell meningioma; heritable clear cell meningioma.

Not recommended: familial meningioma syndrome; meningioma predisposition syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Clear cell meningioma in the context of CCMPS has a proclivity for the spinal canal and cerebellopontine angle {23377182;25143307;33085974;33319313}.

Clinical features

Development of meningiomas of clear cell subtype, especially in children and young adults, is suggestive of CCMPS and warrants germline testing. Multiple relatives with clear cell meningiomas in one family is highly suggestive of CCMPS. Only single reports mention other tumours in patients with this syndrome, for example, high-grade childhood intraparenchymal brain tumour with features suggestive of atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumour (n = 1) {35164878}. As such, further studies are needed to determine the level of increased risk for non-meningothelial tumours.

Epidemiology

Clear cell meningiomas in general tend to affect younger patients, including children and young adults (mean age in one series: 24 years) {31765868}. However, in some carriers of a germline pathogenic SMARCE1 variant, clear cell meningiomas may only become manifest multiple years after a clear MRI and in elderly patients {33185983}. CCMPS may show reduced penetrance in males {25143307}. Because of the limited number of families reported to date, the penetrance of clear cell meningioma in CCMPS is yet to be determined. Also, further studies are needed to assess the frequency of an underlying SMARCE1 germline variant in adult patients diagnosed with clear cell meningioma.

Etiology

CCMPS results from inactivating germline variants of the SMARCE1 gene (see #37861Table 7.06). No other predisposing variables are known.

Pathogenesis

In clear cell meningiomas, germline plus somatic and biallelic somatic SMARCE1 pathogenic variants are common, with virtually all cases showing loss of nuclear SMARCE1 expression by immunohistochemistry {25143307;28474749}. SMARCE1 protein serves as a subunit of the mammalian SWI/SNF complex. Altered SMARCE1 protein impairs the function of one of the three main complexes of SWI/SNF, the canonical BAF complex, and results in increase of non-canonical BAF complexes containing BRD9, with broad subsequent changes in genes responding to growth factors, tyrosine kinases, and WNT signalling {35681054}.

SMARCE1 is located in the chromosome 17q21.2 region and contains 11 exons spanning 22.8 kb of genomic DNA {23377182}. The SMARCE1 protein is a 57 kDa core subunit of mammalian SWI/SNF chromatin remodelling complexes. These act as master regulators of transcription, using ATP for sliding the nucleosomes along the DNA helix {21654818}. In SMARCE1-related meningiomas, most germline variants are frameshift or immediate stop-gain variants, which are predicted to result in loss of protein expression.

Macroscopic appearance

Clear cell meningiomas in patients with this syndrome are identical to their non-syndromic counterparts.

Histopathology

The histopathology of clear cell meningiomas in patients with CCMPS overlaps with that of their non-syndromic counterparts. The clear cell subtype of meningioma has a predominantly patternless or sheeting architecture containing round to polygonal cells with clear, glycogen-rich cytoplasm and prominent perivascular and interstitial collagen. The perivascular and interstitial collagen occasionally coalesces into large acellular zones of collagen or forms brightly eosinophilic, amianthoid-like collagen. Tumour cells show prominent PAS-positive and diastase-sensitive cytoplasmic glycogen. Generally, whorl formation is vague and psammoma bodies are inconspicuous.

Cytology

The cytology of syndromic clear cell meningiomas is identical to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

DNA sequencing can be used to demonstrate SMARCE1-variant / altered status of a (clear cell) meningioma. Immunohistochemical staining can be used to detect SMARCE1 loss {25143307;28474749} and serve as a tool for selecting cases that require further genetic testing. Methylome profiling can provide information about tumour type in histologically challenging cases since clear cell meningiomas show a distinct methylation pattern {33319313}. In patients with SMARCE1-altered clear cell meningioma and a germline pathogenic variant of this gene, genetic counselling and screening in siblings, parents, and/or offspring is indicated. Furthermore, brain and spine imaging screening are recommended for asymptomatic SMARCE1 pathogenic variant carriers at least every 3 years {33185983}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pathogenic germline variant in the SMARCE1 gene.

Desirable: one or more clear cell meningiomas; positive family history.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Clear cell meningiomas in general are associated with more-aggressive behaviour, including recurrence and occasional cerebrospinal fluid seeding, and have therefore been designated CNS WHO grade 2, pending larger studies to confirm the more-aggressive clinical behaviour {7726360;33319313}.
