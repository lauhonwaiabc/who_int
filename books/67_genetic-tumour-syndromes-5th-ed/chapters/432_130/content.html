4.3.2 NTHL1-related tumour syndrome (NTHL1)

Definition

NTHL1-related tumour syndrome (NTS) is an autosomal recessive syndrome caused by germline (constitutional) biallelic NTHL1 pathogenic variants, characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps and an increased risk of colorectal cancer, breast cancer, and variable other extracolonic malignancies.

MIM numbering

616415 NTHL1-associated polyposis

ICD-11 coding

2E92.40 Polyposis syndrome

Related terminology

Acceptable: NTHL1-associated polyposis; NTHL1 tumour syndrome.

Not recommended: familial adenomatous polyposis 3; endonuclease III–like 1 (NTHL1).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The adenomatous polyposis and main cancer susceptibility affects the colorectum. In women, the breast appears to be one of the most frequent sites of cancer. The gynaecological organs, including the endometrium, also show increased risk of cancer. A substantial proportion of patients are reported with less frequent cancers at multiple other sites.

Clinical features

NTS is associated with adenomatous polyps of the large bowel arising in adulthood, generally by the age of 50 years, with increased risk of colorectal cancer as the major malignant risk {25938944;32239880;31227763}. Tumours at other sites, including endometrium and breast, are also seen, with breast cancer appearing as the most frequent cancer type in women {30753826;35292903}. NTS has been suggested to be a cause of Lynch-like syndrome, given the clinical overlap and the occurrence of acquired somatic mismatch repair gene variants resulting in microsatellite instability and mismatch repair immunohistochemical abnormalities {26559593;27720914}. Similarly to Lynch syndrome (see section Lynch syndrome) and MUTYH-associated polyposis (see section MUTYH-associated polyposis), sebaceous skin neoplasms have also been reported (see section Muir–Torre syndrome). Additional more-variable neoplastic findings include duodenal adenomas, duodenal cancer, prostate cancer, bladder urothelial cancer, psammomatous meningioma, brain tumours, basal cell carcinoma, pancreatic cancer, ovarian cancer, cervical cancer, head-and-neck squamous cell carcinomas, and haematological malignancies, mostly reported in small numbers of cases; the cumulative lifetime risk of developing extracolonic cancers by age 60 years is estimated at 35–78% {32239880;30753826;29105096;31227763}. See #38686Table 5.15.

Epidemiology

NTS is thought to be rarer (about one fifth less common) than MUTYH-associated polyposis, although the exact prevalence is unknown.

Etiology

NTS {25938944;32239880} is a germline DNA repair disorder of base excision repair, caused by recessively inherited (homozygous or compound heterozygous) variants in NTHL1.

Pathogenesis

In normal cells, NTHL1 encodes a DNA glycosylase that repairs a variety of DNA lesions by removing the damaged base, including 5-hydroxycytosine and 5-hydroxyuracil, two products of cytosine oxidation that are mutagenic because they mispair with adenine, resulting in high-frequency C>T transitions {29105096;33087284}. The increased mutation rate caused by NTHL1 deficiency contributes to tumour development. There may be a small increase in risk of developing colorectal cancer or other malignancies in individuals with a heterozygous germline NTHL1 pathogenic variant, but this remains to be confirmed {32239880;32860789}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopic appearances of bowel adenomas and colorectal cancers in NTS are closely similar to those of their sporadic counterparts {25938944}.

Histopathology

The colorectal polyps are mostly adenomas, although some hyperplastic polyps and serrated lesions are seen. Bowel adenocarcinomas in NTS show closely similar appearances to their sporadic colorectal counterparts {32239880}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Adenomatous polyposis is most frequently caused by germline variants in APC, MUTYH, POLE, POLD1, or AXIN2. After exclusion of these, families with adenomatous polyposis (5 to > 100 polyps), some with colorectal cancers, should be investigated for biallelic germline NTHL1 variants. Similarly, in individuals with multiple primary tumours and/or (bilateral) breast cancer at a young age, for which TP53, BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, and ATM germline pathogenic variants have been excluded, NTS should be considered. It is relevant to mention that most patients with NTS with cancer have an extracolonic tumour as the first diagnosed tumour type {30753826;35292903}.

The majority of pathogenic variants in NTHL1 are inactivating variants {32239880}. NM_002528.6:c.268C>T p.(Gln90*) appears to be the most common NTHL1 variant in northern Europe. DNA sequencing of multiple primary tumour types of patients with NTS has shown a mutation signature with high similarity to the Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer (COSMIC) single-base substitution signature SBS30 {28912133;30753826;32239880;33087284;34363023}. Since this mutation signature is rare in sporadic cancers, detection of SBS30 in tumour exome or genome sequencing data is a strong indication of NTHL1 deficiency and NTS.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of germline biallelic NTHL1 pathogenic variants.

Desirable: exclusion of germline pathogenic variants in APC, MUTYH, POLE, POLD1, or AXIN2; presentation with colorectal adenomatous polyps, colorectal cancer, and/or variable extracolonic malignancies.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with NTS present with a wide variety of tumours, and prognosis and outcome data are currently limited. The fact that several patients have developed multiple tumours over a longer period of time may suggest that tumours respond well to treatment, possibly because of the high mutation load in these tumours.
