1.5.12 Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome (GREM1)

Definition

Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome (HMPS) is an autosomal dominant colorectal tumour predisposition syndrome caused by duplications upstream of GREM1, characterized by a mixture of colorectal polyps of different types, including individual polyps with mixed histology, and a predisposition to colorectal adenocarcinoma.

MIM numbering

601288 Polyposis syndrome, hereditary mixed 1; HMPS1

ICD-11 coding

2E92.40 Polyposis syndrome

Related terminology

Not recommended: colorectal cancer, susceptibility to, 4; colorectal cancer, susceptibility to, on chromosome 15.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Several different polyp types occur in the colon and rectum.

Clinical features

Patients with HMPS may develop polyps of more than one type, but also, and characteristically, individual polyps may contain components of more than one histological type {10646855;22561515;23805267}. HMPS is a rare condition, the phenotype of which is emerging and may depend on the underlying variant.

Typically, total polyp count is < 100. Polyps present in early adulthood, but have been observed in patients as young as 10 years of age. Polyp types include adenomas, hyperplastic polyps, inflammatory polyps, serrated polyps/lesions, atypical juvenile polyps, prolapse-type polyps, and lymphoid aggregate–containing polyps. The characteristic polyps with mixed histology can include two, three, or even more of the individual types of histology in a single polyp {9024286;14572586;18807306;27984123}.

Extracolonic features, such as those observed in familial adenomatous polyposis, have not been seen {9024286}. Colorectal adenocarcinoma due to malignant change in polyps is the most frequent cancer type seen in HMPS families. While other gastrointestinal cancers and cancers at other sites are seen in individuals and families, it is not yet possible to say whether these are incidental or due to HMPS.

Epidemiology

HMPS is a rare syndrome with an emerging phenotype and of unknown prevalence, but probably < 1 in 100 000 in populations without a founder effect. The original description in a large Ashkenazi Jewish kindred would be consistent with a greater prevalence in that population {12696020;20407467}.

Etiology

HMPS has been found to be caused by tandem head-to-tail duplications affecting the GREM1 gene that include regulatory elements, leading to increased and ectopic expression of the BMP antagonist GREM1 {22561515;26493165;29804199}. This, in turn, has been proposed to lead to persistence and proliferation of progenitor cells outside of the stem cell niche {27984123;25419707}. In 1 case of early-onset colorectal cancer, but without any reported polyps, a 57 kb duplication encompassing the whole GREM1 gene and part of the adjacent SCG5 gene has been observed {21128281}.

It should be noted that null or missense genetic variants within the GREM1 protein-coding region are associated with a completely different phenotype, in which no tumour predisposition is seen {20610440}.

Although patients with the original 40 kb duplication (NC_000015.9:g.32712558_32712559ins[N[30];32672738_32712558]) found in individuals of Ashkenazi background are the best described, other non-Ashkenazi families are being identified with different variants, such as a 16 kb duplication together with a single nucleotide substitution 76 nucleotides upstream of the GREM1 initiation codon (NC_000015.9:g.[32694019_32710248dup;33010288C>G]) in a Swedish kindred with a phenotype of mostly adenomatous polyps, but with only a few polyps of mixed histology, and a 24 kb duplication (NC_000015.9:g.32718858_32718859ins[TG;32695006_32718858]) in a family with a history suggestive of Lynch syndrome rather than polyposis, the index case of which had 20 polyps found, from age 46 to 63 years, including 11 adenomas and 5 hyperplastic polyps, but none with mixed histology {10092300;26493165;27984123;22561515;28242209;29804199}. In addition, a 57 kb duplication, NC_000015.9:g.(?_32683685)_(32741075_?)dup, which duplicates not just upstream controlling regions but also the entire GREM1 coding sequence,  has been described in an isolated case of sigmoid adenocarcinoma in a patient aged 35 years, without any polyps being reported {21128281}. Hence, cases or families with apparently pure adenomatous, serrated, or even hamartomatous polyposis in which there is a mixture of polyps, or no mixed polyps, or potentially just early-onset colorectal cancer are at risk of delayed or missed diagnosis. An inclusive approach to diagnosis is, therefore, recommended.

Pathogenesis

Polyp formation is due to increased and ectopic expression of GREM1, a BMP antagonist {22561515;25419707}. While HMPS segregates in families as an autosomal dominant trait, no second hit is required as with classic tumour suppressor genes, because the underlying mechanism is one of overactivity of normal (unmutated) GREM1 protein in an inappropriate location and, therefore, loss of the normal allele is not observed as it would lead to a reduction in activity and thus a reduction in cell growth stimulus.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopic appearance is as for the sporadic counterparts {9024286;10646855;22561515;23805267}.

Histopathology

People with HMPS develop a variety of different types of colorectal polyps, including adenomas, hyperplastic polyps, serrated polyps/lesions, inflammatory polyps, atypical juvenile polyps, prolapse-type polyps, and lymphoid aggregate–containing polyps, with appearances similar to those of their sporadic counterparts {9024286;27984123;14572586;26947005;28242209} (see #39004Table 2.32). The most characteristic polyp shows mixed histology within the same polyp, and this can include two, three, or more histological types as described above. There is an increased risk of colorectal adenocarcinoma that is indistinguishable histopathologically from sporadic cancer {9024286;10646855;14572586;18807306;22561515;23805267;27984123}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology involves identification of a duplication affecting GREM1 involving regulatory elements between SCG5 and GREM1 {10646855;22561515;23805267} [[LOVD3 Leiden Open Variation Database. The GREM1 Gene Homepage [Internet]. Leiden (Kingdom of the Netherlands): Global Variome Shared LOVD; 2025 [cited 2025 Aug 5]. Available from: https://databases.lovd.nl/shared/genes/GREM1. Bookref]].

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of a duplication affecting GREM1 involving regulatory elements located between SCG5 and GREM1 is essential for diagnosis.

Desirable: different colorectal polyp types, including individual polyps with mixed histological types, seen endoscopically and confirmed by histopathological diagnosis, sometimes over several colonoscopies.

 

See #39006Table 2.33.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis and prediction is as for the sporadic counterparts.
