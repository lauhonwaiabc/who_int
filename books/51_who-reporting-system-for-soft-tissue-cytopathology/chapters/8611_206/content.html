8.6.11 Sample reports: Malignant

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format depends on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and ROSE are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as “Diagnostic summary” (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Specimen type:

Ultrasound-guided FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

37-year-old female with a left medial thigh mass. MRI shows a 32 mm soft tissue mass deep to the superficial fascia in the posteromedial aspect of semi-membranous muscle, with diffuse homogeneous enhancement.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Medial mid left thigh

Category: Malignant

Diagnosis: Myxoid liposarcoma, provisionally low-grade (see comment below)

 

Material received:

Six air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. 3 mL bloodstained fluid for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These moderately cellular smears show tissue fragments of myxoid stroma containing small capillaries branching and anastomosing in a plexiform pattern, with scattered lipoblasts featuring one or more eccentric clear vacuoles that displace and indent the nucleus, and with small spindle cells with mildly irregular nuclei. In the cell block there is considerable material, and the tumour cells are positive in immunocytochemistry for DDIT3. Confirmatory FISH is underway.

 

Comment: The features of this malignant neoplasm are those of myxoid liposarcoma. High-grade features such as hypercellularity or a significant round cell component are not identified in this limited sample. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.

 

Supplementary report 1:

FISH demonstrates DDIT3 gene rearrangement, confirming the diagnosis of myxoid liposarcoma.

 

(Editor’s note: The cytopathologist has issued a report based on the cytopathology and ICC, and then provided a final integrated report with the FISH.)

Sample report 2

Specimen type:

Ultrasound-guided FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

40-year-old male with a 65 mm right levator scapulae intramuscular neoplastic mass suggestive of sarcoma on CT and MRI.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Right levator scapulae mass

Category: Malignant

Diagnosis: Synovial sarcoma (see comment and supplementary report below)

 

Material received:

Six air-dried Giemsa-stained smears from ROSE, two alcohol-fixed smears. 4 mL pink fluid for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show branching tissue fragments (some containing capillaries); loosely cohesive small tissue fragments; and single cells, which are uniform spindle cells with poorly defined scant cytoplasm, hyperchromatic oval nuclei, and inconspicuous nucleoli. There are no epithelioid cells, glandular structures, round cells, or marked atypia. Occasional mitoses are present. There is no necrosis. Cell block shows spindle cells positive for SS18::SSX and TLE1, supporting a diagnosis of synovial sarcoma. S100 and SOX10 are negative, arguing against neural differentiation, and desmin is negative, arguing against muscle differentiation. CK7 shows patchy positivity, and CD34 is negative.

 

Comment: The overall features of this malignant spindle cell neoplasm support a diagnosis of synovial sarcoma. Molecular testing is underway.

 

Supplementary report 1:

This neoplasm is positive for SS18::SSX1 by NGS, confirming synovial sarcoma.

 

(Editor’s note: The cytopathologist could have issued a report categorizing the lesion as malignant, and then followed up with a supplementary report with the ICC, but has instead decided to wait for the ICC on the cell block. The confirmatory NGS has confirmed the diagnosis, and this is presented in the final integrated report.)

Sample report 3

Specimen type:

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

51-year-old male patient with a firm, palpable 60 mm mass in his right medial lower thigh, of 7 months’ duration. No other lesion elsewhere in his body. CT shows a large, 70 mm heterogeneous mass infiltrating the muscles, with adjacent oedema.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Right medial lower thigh

Category: Malignant

Diagnosis: Suggestive of high-grade sarcoma (see comment below)

 

Material received:

Five air-dried smears, two alcohol-fixed smears. 2 mL bloodstained fluid.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show poorly cohesive tissue fragments and single scattered plump spindle and pleomorphic cells exhibiting marked nuclear enlargement, hyperchromasia, and variation in shape, with prominent nucleoli and scattered mitotic figures, including atypical forms, in a background of inflammatory cells and necrosis. There is no diagnostic material in the cell block.

 

Comment: The features of this malignancy suggest a high-grade sarcoma. Correlation with clinical history and imaging is required. Repeat study (to obtain material for immunocytochemistry) and/or CNB is recommended.

Sample report 4

Specimen type:

CT-guided FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

55-year-old female with pain and lump in central lower back. CT shows large destructive tumour in sacrum with adjacent soft tissue component. MRI shows lobulated mass, hypointense on T1-weighted and heterogeneously hyperintense on T2-weighed images, including postcontrast sequence.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Central lower back

Category: Malignant

Diagnosis: Chordoma

 

Material received:

Six air-dried Giemsa-stained smears from ROSE, two alcohol-fixed smears. 4 mL bloody fluid for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

Moderately cellular smears composed of round to epithelioid cells arranged in minute tissue fragments and cords with abundant fibrillary myxoid matrix. The tumour cells have moderate to abundant granular to vacuolated cytoplasm, and the vacuolated cells are consistent with physaliphorous cells. There is a background of blood.

 

Comment: The features are those of a chordoma. Correlation with the imaging findings at a multidisciplinary meeting has taken place. Confirmatory immunocytochemistry stains are underway on the cell block.

 

Supplementary report 1:

There is considerable material in the cell block, which is positive for pancytokeratin (AE1/AE3) and positive in nuclei for brachyury. There is patchy staining for S100, whereas SOX10, GFAP, and PAX8 are negative.

 

(Editor’s note: In this case, the cytopathologist has provided a categorization and diagnosis based on the cytomorphology and then issued a final report incorporating ICC.)

Sample report 5

Specimen type:

CT-guided FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

11-year-old boy, slow-growing soft tissue mass in left upper arm. Plain radiograph shows a large mass involving muscles and infiltrating humerus. MRI shows 80 mm soft tissue mass isointense on T1-weighted and heterogeneous on T2-weighted images with postcontrast enhancement.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Left upper arm

Category: Malignant

Diagnosis: Ewing sarcoma

 

Material received:

Four air-dried Giemsa-stained smears from ROSE, two alcohol-fixed smears. 3 mL bloody fluid for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

Highly cellular smears with loosely cohesive round cells, including light and dark cells, with a high N:C ratio, against a granular proteinaceous background that appears “tigroid” in the Giemsa smears. The cells show focal rosette formation and contain a scant to moderate amount of cytoplasm with fine cytoplasmic vacuoles in Giemsa-stained smears. Cell block immunocytochemistry shows tumour diffusely positive for NKX2-2 and CD99 (MIC2) (cytoplasmic and membranous) and negative for SATB2.

 

Comment: The cytopathology and immunocytochemistry are diagnostic of Ewing sarcoma.
