5.5.2.5 Glomus tumour

Definition

Glomus tumour (GT) is a mesenchymal neoplasm composed of cells resembling the perivascular modified smooth muscle cells of the normal glomus body.

Clinical features and imaging

GTs mainly affect young adults, with no sex predominance. The most common presentation is as a subcutaneous painful lesion in the lower or upper extremity, especially in the subungual region {11145243; 14080348; 20530151; 11726106; 8732413}. Other presentations occur at a variety of sites, including gastrointestinal tract {11859201; 26078584; 12040654; 11335965; 12768637}, genitourinary tract {19912496; 10750892; 15974822; 28438616}, and lung {10935651}. Deeply located GTs present as a nonspecific mass. Some GTs may behave aggressively {28834810; 11231328; 30314644}. Multifocality is present in 10% of cases.

Histopathology

Tumour cells are uniform and epithelioid to round with a central nucleus. The cytoplasm is lightly eosinophilic, with prominent cell borders {7672784; 3038728}. Malignant GTs show marked nuclear atypia and/or atypical mitotic figures.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Variable cellularity Tightly packed, well-demarcated nests and/or tissue fragments of small, round to polygonal epithelioid cells Distinct cytoplasmic borders and variable cytoplasm Round, hyperchromatic nuclei with homogeneous fine chromatin Magenta (Giemsa) intercellular myxoid material and a few spindle cells

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Cytopathological descriptions of GT are limited {8669195; 23635014; 25076782; 25531999; 21187877; 21938152; 11335965; 12768637; 12040654; 26078584; 11726106}. The epithelioid tumour cell population may appear indistinct.

In superficial localizations, GT should be differentiated from eccrine spiradenoma {21938152}, solitary fibrous tumour, and paraganglioma {21187877}. Eccrine spiradenoma is composed of uniform cells along with basement membrane–like magenta intercellular material {21938152}. In solitary fibrous tumour, smears show tissue fragments of oval to spindle cells with ill-defined granular cytoplasm. In paragangliomas, tumour cells may show nuclear pleomorphism, with delicate intracytoplasmic reddish (Giemsa) granulations {21187877}.

GTs located in the gastrointestinal tract should be differentiated from gastrointestinal stromal tumours, smooth muscle tumours, and neuroendocrine tumours {11335965; 21490829}. The cells of neuroendocrine tumours are isolated or in loose small sheets with frequent pseudorosettes.

Ancillary testing

GTs are positive for SMA, MSA, collagen IV, and h-caldesmon, whereas CD34, CD56, KIT, DOG1, desmin, S100, chromogranin, and synaptophysin are usually negative {11145243; 25614464}.

More than half of GT cases harbour recurrent NOTCH gene family rearrangements, with MIR143::NOTCH1, MIR143::NOTCH2, or MIR143::NOTCH3 fusion genes {23999936}. Detection of NOTCH gene rearrangements and/or BRAF mutations may be a useful diagnostic tool, especially in malignant tumours {28834810; 30556047}.
