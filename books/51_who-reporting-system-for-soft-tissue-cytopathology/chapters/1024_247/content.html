10.2.4 Category: Soft tissue neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential

Definition: Neoplasms in the “Soft tissue neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential” (“STNUMP”) category are mesenchymal neoplasms that cannot be unequivocally diagnosed as benign or malignant on cytopathological analysis and ancillary testing, including tumours with intermediate biological potential and tumours requiring the application of histopathological criteria to determine whether they are benign or malignant.

The category of “Soft tissue neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential” (“STNUMP”) represents a diverse group of neoplasms whose biological behaviour cannot be predicted on an FNAB or CNB, or in some cases on histopathology, even if a definitive diagnosis of a specific tumour type can be made by routine FNAB cytopathology and ancillary testing.

The WHO classification of soft tissue and bone tumours has an “indeterminate” group of neoplasms, which comprises “intermediate (locally aggressive)” neoplasms, such as fibromatosis, which are categorized in the WHO Reporting System for Soft Tissue Cytopathology (WHO System) into the “Benign” category, and “intermediate (rarely metastasizing)” neoplasms, which are categorized into the “STNUMP” category, along with tumours whose criteria for risk stratification for malignancy are based on histopathological parameters that can be applied only to resected specimens.

The categories “Atypical” and “STNUMP” address two fundamentally different diagnostic issues. The category “Atypical” is used when the cytopathologist cannot recognize a specific type of soft tissue neoplasm and so cannot definitively assign the specimen to the “Benign” or “Malignant” category, or when a specific line of differentiation is identified but there are insufficient cytopathological features to warrant categorization as “Suspicious for malignancy”. The category “STNUMP” is used when the cytopathologist is able to diagnose a specific neoplasm, but either definitive criteria cannot be applied for risk stratification and ROM, or the specific tumour type has an inherent but unpredictable risk of metastasis.

Neoplasms in the “STNUMP” category include the following:

First, three tumours of intermediate biological risk: dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, a locally aggressive, frequently recurring fibroblastic neoplasm with a small risk of high-grade fibrosarcomatous transformation; inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, a locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing neoplasm; and angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma, another locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing neoplasm.

Second, myoepithelial neoplasms of soft tissue and perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa), whose criteria for malignancy are best assessed on histopathological features {12960802; 16327428; 24061520; 30001237}.

Third, solitary fibrous tumour, whose risk stratification schemes predicting its risk of malignant behaviour are best applied to CNB and excision specimens rather than to FNAB material {20457867; 28731041}.

Currently, there is insufficient published data associated with the “STNUMP” category to establish a ROM, and estimates of ROM for each of the diverse tumour types based on histopathology have been proposed. A recent single-institution retrospective study of more than 2200 FNAB samples using the WHO System categories showed a ROM of 51.43% {39212584}. Specific FNAB diagnoses with appropriate ancillary testing can establish appropriate management for the patients.

An “STNUMP” categorization requires correlation of the clinical, imaging, cytopathological, and any ancillary test findings. If ancillary testing is not available on the original FNAB, repeat FNAB with CNB, if available, to obtain material for confirmatory ICC, FISH, or NGS is recommended. In most cases, complete resection is usually performed for definitive treatment, and the excised specimen can be assessed for histopathological features that enable risk stratification to be carried out.
