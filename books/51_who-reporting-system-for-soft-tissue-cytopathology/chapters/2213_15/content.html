2.2.1.3 Immunocytochemistry

Many soft tissue neoplasms have distinctive immunophenotypes, and the specific diagnosis of most entities requires ancillary testing that commonly includes a panel of ICC markers {30156774; 30190145; 30709446}. Historically, these markers have been combinations of tissue type–specific markers, actin and intermediate filaments, and cluster of differentiation (CD) surface markers to identify a line of differentiation. However, these stains are largely nonspecific and in many cases cannot be used to render definitive interpretations of soft tissue tumours. The recent expansion in specific molecular alterations has allowed for development of antibodies targeting these tumour-defining molecular events. Many of these novel ICC stains identify overexpression or loss of expression of proteins secondary to underlying gene amplification, gene mutation, or gene fusion events. Some tumour-specific markers have been identified by gene expression profiling studies. More recently developed antibodies now target specific mutations or fusion products and serve as reliable surrogate molecular tests.

Preparation

ICC may be performed on FNAB or exfoliative cytopathology samples using several preparation types, including cell block, direct smears, cytospin, and LBC preparations {8685232; 8872443; 9360054; 11782091; 18318580; 20482720; 21771092; 23244485; 31367220}. Cell blocks may be prepared using several methods {23355202; 25113785; 29909418; 30153355}.

Fixation

Formalin-fixed cell block preparation is the most reliable methodology to perform ICC for soft tissue tumours {8685232; 8872443; 9360054; 11782091; 18318580; 20482720; 21771092; 23244485; 31367220}. Markers typically used for soft tissue tumour formalin-fixed histopathology and cell block specimens are unlikely to be validated for alternative cytopathological preparations. Furthermore, most soft tissue tumours require a panel of markers for appropriate classification, and alternative preparations are unlikely to provide sufficient material for complete ancillary workup.

Controls

Regular tissue controls can be used for ICC on cell block preparations. However, laboratories performing ICC on alternative preparations must ensure that the appropriate validations and controls have been developed for that particular cytopathological preparation and are continually monitored. Control slides may be stored at −70 °C without significant loss of antigenicity.

Antigen retrieval

Antigen retrieval can be performed on alternative cytopathology specimens, such as smears and LBC preparations {22095380; 22434540}. Although some membrane-bound markers may not require retrieval, heat-induced epitope retrieval improves detection, especially for nuclear markers. Protease retrieval may damage epitopes on direct smears.

Performance

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) cell block preparations are the most common FNAB material used for ICC and are largely subject to the same limitations as immunohistochemistry on tissue sections, whereas other cytopathological preparations are constrained by the limited or total lack of validation of cytopathological preparations and lack of appropriate controls {8685232; 8872443; 9360054; 11782091; 18318580; 20482720; 21771092; 23244485; 31367220}. Direct smear preparations suffer from bloody or proteinaceous backgrounds causing nonspecific staining and from thick or 3D tissue fragments. These specimens may benefit from additional washing steps. Membranous and cytoplasmic markers typically show more nonspecific staining in these settings than do nuclear markers. Stained versus unstained air-dried and alcohol-fixed smears show comparable results {2440215; 7539200}.

A subset of antibodies have been reported to be unreliable on non–formalin-fixed preparations, probably due to loss of antigenicity or protein diffusion secondary to alcohol fixation. They include S100, KIT (CD117), calretinin, CD5, inhibin, napsin A, OCT3/4, PAX5, BerEP4, D2-40, and PAX8 {8872443; 26882197}. Although alternative fixation methods have shown comparable ICC expression, formalin fixation provides reproducibility, facilitates standardization, and is available in most laboratories.

Diagnostic applications

Diagnostic applications of the more commonly used markers in soft tissue cytopathology are summarized in #36310Table 2.02, #36311Table 2.03, and #35795Table 2.04, and they are discussed further in the relevant sections. The use of ICC panels requires awareness of the expected immunophenotypes of tumours in a given differential diagnosis that has been established based on the FNAB, as well as of the pitfalls in overlapping or aberrant expression of specific markers. Expression of keratins is seen in many soft tissue neoplasms, including high-grade sarcomas and soft tissue neoplasms with epithelioid morphology. Antibodies for both traditional and lineage-specific transcription factor differentiation-specific markers only identify the line of differentiation and do not distinguish between normal tissue counterparts and benign and malignant neoplasms. However, for some tumour types, the main diagnostic utility of ICC remains determining the line of differentiation, such as for leiomyoma and leiomyosarcoma, which lack recurrent molecular alterations but show expression of the smooth muscle markers SMA, desmin, and caldesmon. Many tumours have distinctive immunophenotypes that can guide appropriate molecular testing once identified. For example, Ewing sarcoma shows diffuse membranous CD99 staining and nuclear staining for NKX2-2 {26847175}, and the diagnosis can subsequently be confirmed by FISH testing to identify EWSR1 rearrangement. Other tumour types have so-called “polyphenotypic” profiles, such as desmoplastic small round cell tumour, which is positive for keratins, EMA, desmin, and the C-terminus of WT1 {10843285; 10989634}. Some highly reliable tumour-specific markers have been identified by gene expression profiling, including DOG1 in gastrointestinal stromal tumour {15215166; 19606013} and MUC4 in low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma {21536545; 21415703}. As stated above, many ICC markers are now available as testing surrogates for underlying molecular alterations.
