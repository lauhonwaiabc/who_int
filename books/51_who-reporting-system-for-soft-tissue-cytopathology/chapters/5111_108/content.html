5.1.1.1 Introduction

The “Atypical” category is used when there are cytopathological features present that are essentially benign, but with occasional features uncommon in benign lesions that raise the remote possibility of a malignancy and thus cannot be ignored. This indeterminate category is included to increase the negative predictive value of a “Benign” categorization.

Whenever atypical features are present, the FNAB is categorized as “Atypical”, even if the overall material shows technical faults and would otherwise be categorized as “Insufficient”, or when suboptimal preparation has resulted in a degree of artefactual or degenerative change but sufficient atypical cytomorphological features are present and a malignancy cannot be entirely excluded. Most of the specimens in this category are likely to be from reactive, inflammatory, or reparative processes, but specific features sufficient for a definitive benign diagnosis are lacking. The category must be used in conjunction with the clinical history, physical examination, and imaging findings. The report should always state that “correlation with clinical and imaging findings is required”.
