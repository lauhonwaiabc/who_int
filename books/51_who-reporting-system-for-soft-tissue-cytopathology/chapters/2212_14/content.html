2.2.1.2 Flow cytometry

Flow cytometry is an ancillary technique widely used to assess haematolymphoid disorders in nodal and extranodal tissues {20132132; 21438908; 32212260}, but flow cytometry has a limited role in the assessment of disorders of other cell lineages. Cell-cycle analysis by determination of nuclear DNA has long been used as a prognostic marker in carcinoma {3309200}. Flow cytometry has also been used to investigate blood for circulating tumour cells {21337491} and fluids such as pleural effusions for carcinoma cells {32506689}. In these cases, the samples contain single abnormal cells in a suspension, which is a requirement of flow cytometry. It is usually straightforward to prepare samples of haematolymphoid disorders into single-cell suspensions, but this is challenging with biopsy material from disorders of soft tissues {28369679}. Therefore, routine clinical flow cytometry is largely limited to the analysis of haematolymphoid disorders, which can involve soft tissues.

In soft tissue, the commonest haematolymphoid tumour is extranodal lymphoma, and about 30% of lymphomas arise in sites that normally lack lymphocytes {27537785}. Plasmacytoma and myeloid sarcoma can also be assessed by flow cytometry {26100534; 17170724}. Haematolymphoid lesions may be suspected because of clinical features or because of the findings of ROSE {30156771}. If flow cytometry is to be performed on an FNAB, it is optimal to take a dedicated pass. Samples must be placed in a tissue culture medium with or without fetal bovine serum, or if such medium is not available, in saline {32606494; 24022851; 33456333}. If flow cytometry is performed 18–24 hours or more after sample collection, commercial or in-house stabilizing solutions should be used {23674509; 32479677}. Some protocols recommend storage at 4 °C pending analysis, while others recommend ambient temperature {32606494; 33456333; 24022851}.

Analysis of FNAB samples by flow cytometry involves preparing preferably tens of thousands of cells in suspension, staining them with fluorochrome-labelled antibodies, and running them through a flow cytometer to determine the amount of label on each cell {26767547}. The antibodies should initially cover key lymphocyte markers. If a suspicious population cannot be identified by these markers, the sample should be assessed for plasma cell and myeloid lineages {17803189}.
