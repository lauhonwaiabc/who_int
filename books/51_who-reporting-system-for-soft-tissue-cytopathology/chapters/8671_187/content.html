8.6.7.1 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a malignant spindle cell neoplasm with limited Schwann cell differentiation, characterized by concurrent inactivating mutations involving three pathways: NF1, CDKN2A/CDKN2B, and PRC2 core components (SUZ12 or EED).

Clinical features and imaging

MPNSTs arise most commonly in patients aged 20–50 years, although they may arise in younger patients, including children, in the context of neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1). MPNST can arise from a peripheral nerve, from a pre-existing benign nerve sheath tumour, or in a patient with NF1; approximately 50% of cases are associated with NF1 {11894862}, and about 10% arise after radiation therapy {7377756}.

Most patients present with an enlarging mass, which may be painful, and patients may experience neuropathic symptoms with intraneural tumours. The extremities, trunk, and head and neck are the most common sites {27158754; 3082508}.

The distinct variant epithelioid MPNST is not associated with NF1 and is characterized by SMARCB1 inactivation {25602794; 30864974}.

Overall, MPNSTs are aggressive, with poor prognosis.

Histopathology

MPNST is composed of fascicles of spindle cells, often interlacing and with alternating areas of hypercellularity and hypocellularity, imparting a marbled appearance. Tumours have characteristic thin-walled and irregular “staghorn” or haemangiopericytoma-like vessels, and many cases show dense perivascular growth of tumour cells. Geographical necrosis and high mitotic activity are common, particularly in high-grade tumours. Tumour cells have spindle, wavy, or serpentine hyperchromatic nuclei and pale eosinophilic cytoplasm. There is nuclear enlargement and mild atypia, but MPNSTs typically show an overall uniformity, with prominent pleomorphism seen in occasional cases. Heterologous differentiation occurs in about 15% of MPNSTs and can be rhabdomyoblastic in the so-called “triton tumour”, osseous, cartilaginous, vascular, or epithelial, which can be glandular or squamous.

There may be infiltration into pre-existing peripheral neve sheath tissue or a precursor neurofibroma, and a spectrum of different degrees of malignant progression within a given tumour may be observed in NF1-associated cases.

Epithelioid MPNST shows predominant epithelioid morphology, with lobulated growth, frequently fibromyxoid stroma, and round nuclei with prominent nucleoli in cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Distinguishing MPNST from other spindle cell sarcomas often requires ancillary testing. In the context of association with a peripheral nerve or schwannoma or neurofibroma, or the setting of NF1 or prior radiation therapy, MPNST should be a chief consideration. MPNSTs arising de novo are more challenging to recognize.

Mimics of MPNST include cellular schwannoma, leiomyosarcoma, synovial sarcoma, solitary fibrous tumour, and dedifferentiated liposarcoma. Cellular schwannoma lacks cytopathological atypia. Leiomyosarcomas arising in soft tissue sites are often associated with a large vessel. ICC is required for other tumours.

Epithelioid MPNST may closely mimic amelanotic malignant melanoma, and ICC is helpful.

Ancillary testing

The majority of MPNSTs are negative for the Schwann cell markers S100, SOX10, and GFAP, but positive patchy and focal staining for any of these markers occurs in 30% of cases {23929265}. Tumours with heterologous differentiation show expression of corresponding markers, such as desmin and myogenin in rhabdomyoblasts and keratin in epithelium. In the differential diagnosis, schwannomas show strong and diffuse staining for S100 and SOX10, which are either negative or focal in MPNST. Leiomyosarcomas express the smooth muscle markers SMA, desmin, and caldesmon. Molecular alterations for which sensitive and specific diagnostic ICC markers are available can differentiate synovial sarcoma with SS18-SSX and SSX, solitary fibrous tumour with STAT6, and dedifferentiated liposarcoma with MDM2 and CDK4.

Epithelioid MPNSTs show strong and diffuse staining for S100 and SOX10, with retained H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3), and they are consistently negative for the melanocytic markers HMB45 and melan-A (MART1), distinguishing them from amelanotic melanoma. Unlike in conventional MPNST, SMARCB1 loss is characteristic in epithelioid MPNST {25602794; 30864974}, but it can also be seen in other epithelioid tumours, including the mimic proximal-type epithelioid sarcoma. Epithelioid sarcoma is negative for S100 and SOX10, positive for keratins and EMA, and frequently positive for CD34.

Loss of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) secondary to PRC2 inactivation can be detected by ICC in MPNST, and complete loss of staining for H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) is seen in 50–80% of all cases, typically in high-grade and radiation-induced tumours {28776579; 26645727; 26585554; 29482987; 28898989}. However, nonspecific partial loss may be seen in a subset of MPNSTs. ATRX loss and an alternative-lengthening-of-telomeres phenotype has been described in a small subset of NF-1 associated MPNSTs {31462295; 29796169}.
