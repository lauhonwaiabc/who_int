8.6.8.4 Clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue

Definition

Clear cell sarcoma (CCS) of soft tissue is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm typically involving deep soft tissue, showing a distinctive nested growth pattern and melanocytic differentiation, and most often characterized by EWSR1::ATF1 fusion.

Clinical features and imaging

CCSs account for 0.8–1% of all sarcomas {17227118; 30210202} and typically occur in young adults, with a peak incidence in the third decade of life; they are slightly more common in females {24560887; 17227118; 30210202; 6614306; 10491522; 6616410}. Most cases present as a slow-growing, often painful or tender soft tissue mass in the lower extremities, especially the foot and ankle, and they are often attached to aponeurotic structures and tendons {24560887; 17227118; 30210202; 6614306; 10491522; 6616410}. Rare sites include head and neck, trunk, and viscera {6614306; 10491522; 6616410}.

MRI shows features of a non-aggressive lesion, such as well-defined margins and homogeneity on T1- and T2-weighted images, without perilesional oedema, satellite nodules, or intratumoral necrosis {15290067; 10855466; 30075493}.

Histopathology

There is an infiltrative growth of uniform epithelioid to fusiform cells divided into nests and fascicles by thin fibrous septa. Tumour cells display limited pleomorphism and pale eosinophilic, rarely clear cytoplasm, with centrally located round to ovoid vesicular nuclei with macronucleoli. Scattered wreath-like multinucleated tumour giant cells and melanin pigment are relatively common {9875133; 17227118; 30210202}. Necrosis is found in one third of cases. The mitotic count is deceptively low for this highly malignant tumour.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Variable but often moderate to high cellularity Dispersed large cells and occasional loose or cohesive tissue fragments with non-overlapping nuclei Finely striped (“tigroid”) background Round, polygonal, or spindle cells with moderate to abundant clear to pale concentric or eccentric cytoplasm and central or displaced nuclei Round to ovoid hyperchromatic nuclei with prominent nucleoli Nuclei with perinucleolar chromatin clearing (“vesicular”, Pap) and occasionally 2–3 smaller nucleoli Rare binucleated or wreath-like multinucleated cells Occasional microacinar tissue fragments Intranuclear cytoplasmic pseudoinclusions and cytoplasmic melanin may be present

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes synovial sarcoma and sarcomas with epithelioid morphology (e.g. epithelioid sarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma), metastatic carcinoma, anaplastic large cell lymphoma, and melanoma. All of these tumours, like CCS, produce highly cellular smears. The tigroid background seen in CCS is also seen in Ewing sarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and anaplastic large cell lymphoma {35481562}. Individual tumour cells of synovial sarcoma are oval to spindle, with hyperchromatic nuclei and indistinct nucleoli {8980353}, and single scattered epithelioid to rhabdoid-like cells are rarely seen in synovial sarcoma {28345284}. Metastatic carcinoma displays crowded 3D tissue fragments and variable papillary and glandular architecture, whereas acinar formation is rarely reported in CCS {11863218; 32356379}. Prominent epithelioid differentiation is uncommon in Ewing sarcoma.

Ancillary testing

Most cases of CCS of soft tissue are characterized by reciprocal translocation t(12;22)(q13;q12) resulting in an EWSR1::ATF1 fusion {2009504; 1373311; 11992546; 9824209; 1486556; 1486557}. A minor subset of cases harbour translocation t(2;22)(q34;q12), which includes the EWSR1::CREB1 fusion {18300804; 19561568; 32356379}.

CCS stains positively for S100, HMB45, melan-A, and SOX10, and it typically expresses MITF-M {11211309}. Synovial sarcoma displays variable keratin and EMA immunostaining. Additionally, synovial sarcoma displays SS18::SSX gene fusion. Epithelioid sarcoma expresses a variable number of epithelial markers and shows loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) in most instances {27145123}. Anaplastic large cell lymphoma expresses CD30 and ALK. Metastatic carcinoma shows epithelial markers.
