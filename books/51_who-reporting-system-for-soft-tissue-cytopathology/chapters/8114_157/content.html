8.1.1.4 Risk of malignancy and diagnostic management recommendations

The ROM for the “Malignant” category ranges from 91% to 100% based on several retrospective studies {32926591; 12451566; 28739192; 19763717; 11190808; 37231794}. A more recent large retrospective study reported a ROM of 97.69%, noting that the ROM varies depending on both the cytomorphological pattern and the WHO Reporting System for Soft Tissue Cytopathology (WHO System) categorization {39212584}.

Clinical and imaging correlation is always necessary in evaluating neoplasms within the “Malignant” category {18325355; 16411083}, and management decisions as to any further diagnostic workup and definitive management should be made by a multidisciplinary team. If FNAB does not provide enough material for diagnosis, or if the cytopathological findings are inconsistent with clinical and imaging assessments, the imaging findings should be carefully reviewed and correlated. If suspicion remains high, repeat FNAB – preferably with ROSE and triaging of material for appropriate ancillary tests – or CNB or simple excision biopsy should be conducted before proceeding with any major surgical or oncological interventions.
