8.2.1.1 Atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma

Definition

Atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma (ALT/WDLPS) is a locally aggressive, non-metastasizing mesenchymal neoplasm composed either entirely or partly of a proliferation of adipocytes showing at least focal nuclear atypia in both adipocytes and stromal cells, and characterized by an amplification of MDM2 and CDK4.

Clinical features and imaging

ALT/WDLPS occurs in middle-aged and elderly patients of both sexes. The ALT designation is applicable to resectable tumours of the extremities occurring as large, deeply located, slowly growing masses most commonly occurring in the thigh and buttock or trunk, shoulder, or back {27020493}, whereas the WDLPS designation is used for prognostically less favourable retroperitoneal or mediastinal tumours in the retroperitoneum and paratesticular region and less commonly in the mediastinum or head and neck. ALT/WDLPS in deep locations and especially in the retroperitoneum is often asymptomatic until it exceeds 200 mm. On CT or MRI, a homogeneous fatty mass is seen, sometimes with pronounced septation but without solid areas {30943944; 34997060}. Curative complete resection is possible in extremity or trunk locations, but the retroperitoneal tumours tend to spread diffusely and recur (often repeatedly) due to the inherent difficulties of a complete resection. ALT/WDLPS carries a risk of dedifferentiation in the recurrent tumours, most commonly in the retroperitoneum – in as many as 20% of cases; in the extremities this risk is 2%.

Histopathology

ALT/WDLPS consists of solid sheets of adipocytes showing variability of cell size and fibrotic septation, with variable nuclear atypia of both the adipocytes and stromal cells. The nuclei of the adipocytes are variably enlarged, showing hyperchromasia and pleomorphism, with occasional multinucleation. Multinucleated cells showing a wreath-like arrangement of hyperchromatic nuclei around an eosinophilic cytoplasm resembling flowers and known as “floret” cells may be encountered. The presence of lipoblasts neither makes nor is required for a diagnosis of ALT/WDLPS [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]]. Occasionally, areas with abundant myxoid matrix may be present. In the inflammatory variant, numerous lymphocytes and plasma cells, as well as neutrophils and eosinophils, may be observed, whereas collagenous regions with atypical stromal cells and sometimes rare atypical adipocytes dominate in the sclerosing subtype. Lipoma-like, inflammatory, and sclerosing areas may all be a part of the same tumour. There are no solid, nodular, or spindle cell regions or pleomorphic areas lacking a lipomatous component in adequately sampled ALT/WDLPS. If these non-lipogenic nodular components are present, mostly with an abrupt transition, the progression to dedifferentiated liposarcoma has occurred.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Variable cellularity Cohesive tissue fragments of adipocytes showing variability of size and nuclear atypia Nuclear atypia of the adipocytes varies from slight hyperchromasia to pronounced pleomorphism and multinucleation “Floret” cells and lipoblasts may be present Lipoblasts have one or more clear vacuoles in cells varying in size, with the vacuoles indenting the nucleus, which often appears scalloped by the vacuoles; there may be more than one nucleus “Floret” cells have a wreath or circumferential arrangement of nuclei around central eosinophilic cytoplasm (Pap) No solid or spindle cell non-adipocytic component

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Typical lipoma is the main differential diagnosis of ALT/WDLPS, and it may show similar clinical and imaging findings. Deeply located large intramuscular lipomas are rare in the extremities and are extremely rare in the retroperitoneum. Spindle cell and pleomorphic lipomas occur mostly in the subcutaneous tissue of the neck and shoulder and usually measure < 50 mm.

Ancillary testing

ICC demonstrates the overexpression of MDM2 or CDK4 in the atypical nuclei of ALT/WDLPS, but this may be difficult to detect in the lipoma-like variant. Lipomas do not carry the 12q13-q15 amplicon and do not usually contain MDM2-positive nuclei on ICC. FISH of the MDM2 gene reliably discriminates between lipoma and lipoma-like ALT/WDLPS {8579124; 26146760; 20679883; 25810689}, whereas ICC has a low sensitivity of 45% in this setting {27508976}.

Overexpression of MDM2 in the nuclei of macrophages found in fat necrosis poses a diagnostic pitfall, but FISH, which may be performed on direct smears and cell blocks, is a reliable method for the detection of the diagnostic amplification of the MDM2 gene {25132684; 30600320}.
