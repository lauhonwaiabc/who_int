8.6.2.2 Infantile fibrosarcoma

Definition

Infantile fibrosarcoma (IFS) is a malignant fibroblastic tumour most commonly occurring in infancy, frequently characterized by an ETV6::NTRK3 fusion.

Clinical features and imaging

IFS is a locally aggressive and rapidly growing tumour that only rarely metastasizes. The superficial or deep soft tissues of the extremities are the most common sites of involvement, followed by the trunk and the head and neck {8159611; 974993}. The typical presentation is rapid growth of a painless mass or exophytic nodule {974993}, and superficial tumours may ulcerate and appear vascular {16918626}. Rarely, tumours may present in intra-abdominal, retroperitoneal, or visceral sites {8159611; 974993; 19917847; 25150723}. One third of cases occur congenitally {974993}. Analogous tumours that arise in the kidney are termed “cellular congenital mesoblastic nephroma”.

Imaging features are nonspecific, and IFS typically appears as a heterogeneously enhancing mass on ultrasound, CT, or MRI, sometimes with a prominent vascular component {31620852; 32980096}. Extensive haemorrhage may be seen, particularly in larger tumours {16918626; 22812410; 24706181}.

Histopathology

IFS most commonly appears as a uniform fascicular or sheet-like proliferation of monomorphic spindle to ovoid cells, but it may show a broad histopathological spectrum {30585824}. Some IFSs may show a haphazard architecture, a more primitive round cell population, or stellate morphology. Most tumours show a characteristic vascular component composed of ectatic, irregularly branching (haemangiopericytoma-like) vessels, which can be numerous. The stroma ranges from myxoid to collagenous, and some examples with less cellularity may resemble fibromatosis or myofibroma {22420727; 30585824}. A prominent chronic inflammatory infiltrate may be seen. IFS frequently shows infiltrative growth into adjacent normal tissue. Mitotic figures can range from rare to numerous, but atypical forms are rare. Necrosis may be present.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Cytopathological findings have not been clearly defined in the rare reports of FNAB of IFS {10439797; 19067284}. However, the cytopathological features of cellular congenital mesoblastic nephroma, a renal tumour morphologically and molecularly identical to IFS, are presumed to be similar {17681013; 19217046; 23417966; 27324404}.

Significant morphological overlap exists between IFS and various benign, intermediate, and malignant paediatric mesenchymal neoplasms {22420727}. Distinction between these entities requires ICC and often molecular studies, as well as correlation with the clinical presentation.

Ancillary testing

The immunoprofile is nonspecific. Tumours variably express SMA, CD34, S100, and desmin. IFS with NTRK rearrangements usually shows diffuse positivity for pan-TRK {22420727; 30585824}. Detection of ETV6::NTRK3 or variant rearrangements by FISH, RT-PCR, or targeted NGS is helpful for confirming the diagnosis.
