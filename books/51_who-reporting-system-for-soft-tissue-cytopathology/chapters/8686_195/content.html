8.6.8.6 Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

Definition

Desmoplastic small round cell tumour (DSRCT) is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm composed of small round tumour cells, associated with prominent stromal desmoplasia, polyphenotypic differentiation, and EWSR1::WT1 gene fusion.

Clinical features and imaging

DSRCTs are aggressive tumours occurring in adolescents and young adults, with a peak incidence in the third decade of life. These tumours usually occur in the abdomen of male patients, with peritoneal seeding, and they can rarely occur in the thoracic, sinonasal, intraosseous, or paratesticular regions {27621277; 32804706}. Patients typically present with extensive tumour involvement at diagnosis, sometimes with ascites or bowel obstruction, progressing to lymph node, liver, lung, and bone metastases in later stages. On abdominal imaging, numerous omental and mesenteric implants are seen, without a primary solid organ mass {23146167}. These tumours have a poor prognosis, with death usually within 2 years of diagnosis and a 5-year survival rate of < 30%, even with multimodality treatment options such as neoadjuvant chemotherapy and aggressive surgery with postoperative chemoradiation. Hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy and immunotherapy have been attempted in these patients, but prognosis remains poor {27955733; 34869013; 32555030}.

Histopathology

DSRCTs show nests of uniform small cells with round to oval nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, and scant cytoplasm within a prominent fibroblastic stroma. These lesions tend to be nodular, ranging from 1–2 mm micronodules up to large 400 mm masses {16683894}. Apoptotic debris, rosettes, eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions, and nuclear moulding can be seen to varying degrees. Variants include rhabdoid, epithelioid, and small cell morphologies.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

High cellularity Loosely cohesive, hyperchromatic round cells with scant to moderate cytoplasm and variable nuclear moulding Metachromatic stromal material in the background of smears and cell block material, but rare in body fluids and liquid based preparations Occasional pseudorosettes Paranuclear cytoplasmic densities, heart/kidney-shaped nuclei, and cytoplasmic vacuolization can be seen

Discussion and differential diagnosis

DSRCT overlaps morphologically with a variety of epithelial, mesenchymal, and lymphoproliferative tumours. The diagnosis can be established on small biopsy, with sufficient cell block material showing the multiphenotypic differentiation, exclusion of other small round cell tumours, and confirmation of the pathognomonic EWSR1::WT1 translocation {15880716; 24639098}. The differential diagnosis includes rhabdomyosarcoma, synovial sarcoma, small cell osteosarcoma, mesenchymal chondrosarcoma, neuroblastoma, rhabdoid tumours, Wilms tumour, round cell sarcomas (including CIC-rearranged sarcomas), and Ewing sarcoma.

Ancillary testing

DSRCTs have unique ICC, showing positivity for epithelial markers (cytokeratins, EMA), myogenic markers (desmin in a punctate perinuclear staining pattern), and neural markers (S100, NSE). CD99 can show cytoplasmic staining that differs from the membranous staining in Ewing sarcoma {12131150}. SALL4 positivity has also been reported in occasional DSRCTs {24525512}. The WT1 (C19) stain is also positive if it detects the C-terminus, due to the in-frame translocation t(11;22)(p13;q12) in these tumours resulting in an EWSR1::WT1 fusion gene, in which the N-terminal region of EWSR1 is fused with the C-terminal region of WT1 {27621277; 10989634}. Wilms tumour is differentiated by showing positive nuclear staining for the WT1 N-terminus. Ewing sarcoma shares the feature of EWSR1 rearrangements, but typically involving other genes (FLI1, ERG, others) {31802230}. The EWSR1::WT1 fusion results in upregulation of the expression of PDGFRA, VEGF, and other proteins related to proliferation {33525546}. NGS has shown rare mutations in TERT, ARID1A, HRAS, and TP53, in addition to overexpression of FGFR4 {33753552}.
