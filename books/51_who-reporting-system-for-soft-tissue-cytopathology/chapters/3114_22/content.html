3.1.1.4 Risk of malignancy and diagnostic management recommendations

Published reports on the ROM for soft tissue FNAB are limited, but the ROM for the “Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic” category is often reported as high, most likely due to sampling error.

In a systematic review with meta-analysis of 13 articles on FNAB of bone and soft tissue lesions, there were 7.7% inadequate samples, with adequacy rates for soft tissue lesions ranging from 38.6% to 94.8% {32622858}. In a recent report of 828 patients, with a 4% inadequate rate, the ROM was 42%, but this included a CNB with each FNAB for all but 2 patients {32926591}. Another study analysed 1114 soft tissue and bone FNAB specimens, most of which had ROSE, with 51% of the FNAB procedures performed without image guidance; the inadequate rate was 3%, and more than half of the 37 inadequate cases were found to be malignant at follow-up {20091838}. A retrospective study of FNAB samples of 301 soft tissue lesions {12451566} found that 22 cases (7%) were inadequate samples, including 9 haemangiomas and 2 malignant tumours, yielding a ROM of 9%. A recent large single-institution retrospective study of 2214 cases showed a ROM of 29.87% for the “Insufficient” category, but it also showed, interestingly, that the ROM for all categories varied with the FNAB cytopathological pattern, from low for lipomatous tumours; to higher for spindle cell, epithelioid, and myxoid tumours; to very high for pleomorphic and round cell tumours {39212584}.

An “Insufficient” categorization should initiate correlation with the clinical and imaging findings. Whenever there is discordance between the clinical, imaging, and cytomorphological findings, a repeat specimen should be requested. If a lesion is suspicious clinically and/or on imaging and the diagnosis remains unresolved after FNAB sampling, CNB or incisional or excisional biopsy should be recommended. The application of guidance to the FNAB decreases the sampling error and is recommended for a lesion that is difficult to distinctly locate by palpation or is deep-seated, and ROSE is recommended to enable immediate progression to CNB at the same patient visit if the FNAB is “Insufficient” {35427924}.
