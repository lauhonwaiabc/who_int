4.5.2.2 Hibernoma

Definition

Hibernoma is a rare, benign lipomatous tumour showing brown fat differentiation.

Clinical features and imaging

Hibernomas account for 1% of all adipocytic tumours and typically occur in young adults. Most cases present as a slow-growing, well-defined, painless subcutaneous mass in the thigh, back, upper trunk, head and neck, or upper extremities. Deep-seated hibernomas account for fewer than 10% of cases and occur in intra-abdominal, retroperitoneal, and intrathoracic locations {11395560; 24248814; 22049958}.

On MRI, classic hibernomas are slightly hypointense, and lipoma-like hibernomas are isointense to subcutaneous fat on T1-weighted images. On T2-weighted MRI, high signal intensity is typical. Hibernomas display marked or moderate lack of homogeneity, with prominent septa and vascular structures {16642344; 26046999; 23385517}. Hibernomas are easily detectable on PET because they contain abundant mitochondria and are highly metabolically active {32211221; 26046999}.

Histopathology

Classic hibernomas are composed of three cell types in varying proportions: large brown fat cells with finely vacuolated and eosinophilic granular cytoplasm and small bland central nuclei, mature adipocytes, and vacuolated lipoblast-like cells. Most hibernomas have a prominent capillary network. Subtypes include the lipoma-like and rare myxoid and spindle cell subtypes {11395560; 24248814}. Hibernomas mimicking atypical lipomatous tumour have been reported, but nuclear atypia and mitoses are absent {29629919}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Moderate to high cellularity Tissue fragments and sheets of polygonal and oval cells with abundant cytoplasm Brown fat hibernoma cells with microvacuolated and granular cytoplasm and small, uniform central nuclei Mix of areas of regular adipocytes and areas of round brown fat cells Numerous capillaries Rare lipoblast-like cells with univacuolated or multivacuolated clear cytoplasm

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma, lipoma with regressive changes, adult rhabdomyoma, granular cell tumour, and fat necrosis. The centrally located, small, bland nucleus helps to distinguish multivacuolated brown fat cells from atypical lipoblasts containing hyperchromatic, irregular, and scalloped nuclei {11391608; 21053560; 12827716} [[Domanski HA, Walther CS. FNA cytology of soft tissue and bone tumors. Basel (Switzerland): Karger; 2017. (Monographs in clinical cytology series; vol. 22).]] {2579013}. Hibernomas with a large proportion of mature adipocytes, histiocytes, and lipophages resembling brown fat can be wrongly diagnosed as common lipoma {11391608} [[Domanski HA, Walther CS. FNA cytology of soft tissue and bone tumors. Basel (Switzerland): Karger; 2017. (Monographs in clinical cytology series; vol. 22).]]. Granular cell tumour and adult rhabdomyoma display a granular rather than microvacuolated cytoplasm and lack adipocytic differentiation [[Domanski HA, Walther CS. FNA cytology of soft tissue and bone tumors. Basel (Switzerland): Karger; 2017. (Monographs in clinical cytology series; vol. 22).]].

Ancillary testing

There is no distinctive ICC for hibernomas. Most hibernomas are characterized by recurrent genetic changes involving structural rearrangements of 11q13 {12800150; 21078971} resulting in MEN1 and AIP codeletion {21078971}.
