8.6.2.4 Neuroblastic tumours

Definition

Neuroblastic tumours comprise a group of tumours of neural crest origin spanning a continuum of maturation and including ganglioneuroma, ganglioneuroblastoma, and neuroblastoma.

Clinical features and imaging

Neuroblastoma is the most common extracranial malignancy of childhood, with the majority of cases diagnosed before 5 years of age and approximately 50% presenting clinically before the age of 2 years, with a median age of 23 months. Neuroblastomas can arise from any site containing sympathetic neural tissue; they most commonly occur in the adrenal gland (~40% of cases), followed by the retroperitoneal, thoracopulmonary, mediastinal, cervical, and pelvic regions. Approximately 70% of patients present with metastatic disease to regional lymph nodes, liver, bones, and lungs. The prognosis of neuroblastoma is correlated with patient age at diagnosis, primary site, histopathology, and grade. In general, children under 1 year of age have the best prognosis {7809504; 10421273; 10421272; 8336186; 19047291}.

The International Neuroblastoma Pathology Classification (INPC) categorizes clinically aggressive and non-aggressive groups of neuroblastomas on the basis of unfavourable and favourable histopathology, respectively, as defined by the presence or absence of Schwannian stroma, differentiating neuroblasts, and maturing and mature ganglion cells {10421273; 10421272; 8336186}. Other prognostic indicators are the mitosis-karyorrhexis index for the Schwannian stroma–poor neuroblastoma category and molecular and ploidy findings {33155698; 25435121}. There are multiple systems for staging neuroblastoma: the International Neuroblastoma Staging System (INSS) is based on surgical resection findings, and the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group Staging System (INRGSS) is based on postsurgical findings, pretreatment imaging, the patient’s age, and the clinical extent of disease {10421273; 19047291}. CT or MRI is used to evaluate the extent of disease {25969636}. Neuroblastoma often presents as a large, nodular, heterogeneous, solid mass involving the adrenal gland or occurring anywhere along the sympathetic chain. The presence of stippled calcification and/or intraspinal extension is useful in differentiating neuroblastoma from other small round cell tumours. Nuclear medicine studies (e.g. MIBG scintigraphy) are also used to detect occult disease and bony metastasis.

Histopathology

By definition, neuroblastomas are composed of > 50% neuroblasts, and ganglioneuromas are composed exclusively of mature ganglion cells, neurites, Schwann cells, and fibrous tissue. Ganglioneuroblastoma contains > 50% ganglioneuromatous elements, with a relatively small neuroblastic component. Neuroblasts are small to medium-sized cells with scant cytoplasm and ill-defined cell borders. The nuclei are round to oval, with fine granular chromatin and indistinct to small nucleoli. The neuroblasts are arranged diffusely or in nests separated by thin fibrovascular septa, with variable amounts of fibrillary neuropil. As cells differentiate towards ganglion cells, the nuclei enlarge and acquire vesicular chromatin and a single prominent nucleolus, with characteristic abundant, largely eccentric eosinophilic amphophilic cytoplasm. Other features of neuroblastoma include variable amounts of neuropil and Schwannian stroma, Homer Wright pseudorosettes, necrosis, and lymphoid cells {7809504; 9140292; 19530234}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of neuroblastoma includes other paediatric round cell tumours. With the exception of Ewing sarcoma, these tumours lack Homer Wright pseudorosettes and neuropil. When present in Ewing sarcoma, neuropil is predominantly associated with pseudorosettes and is otherwise scant, and the cells are more monomorphic than those in neuroblastoma. The cells in lymphoblastic lymphoma are intermediate in size, with scant cytoplasm, and they have distinct cellular borders and round, ovoid, or indented nuclei with finely dispersed chromatin and one or more nucleoli. Of note, cytoplasmic fragments in neuroblastoma may mimic the cytoplasmic fragments (“lymphoglandular bodies”) seen in lymphoblastic lymphoma, although these are usually more numerous. In rhabdomyosarcoma, the nuclei of both the primitive cells and those with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation are more hyperchromatic; if present, tadpole and ribbon-like rhabdomyoblasts help to distinguish rhabdomyosarcoma from neuroblastoma. In contrast to neuroblastoma, CIC-rearranged and BCOR family tumours may have spindle cells, and the round cells are larger and have a greater degree of pleomorphism than those in embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma. BCOR family tumours also have delicate vascular fragments that are absent in neuroblastoma {29300189; 31679010}. The cells in poorly differentiated synovial sarcoma are relatively uniform, larger, and have irregular hyperchromatic nuclei with one or more nucleoli {10888758; 26800124}. In contrast to immature, maturing, and mature ganglion cells, the rhabdoid cells in malignant rhabdoid tumour have distinct cytoplasmic inclusions and round nuclei with macronucleoli and perinucleolar clearing. The presence of maturing and mature ganglion cells and Schwann cells distinguishes ganglioneuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma from other diagnostic considerations. Undifferentiated neuroblastoma lacks pseudorosettes and neuropil, and the distinction of this entity from other paediatric round cell tumours requires ICC and/or molecular studies {10425044; 10934943; 17636492; 26800124; 28718513; 33399252}.

Ancillary testing

By ICC, neuroblastic cells are positive for PHOX2B, NSE, synaptophysin, chromogranin, neurofilament, CD57, and CD56, and they are negative for epithelial markers (EMA and cytokeratin), WT1, CD99, CD45, and muscle markers {1768940; 20216406}. PHOX2B is highly specific and is especially helpful for distinguishing undifferentiated neuroblastoma, in which staining for other neural markers may be lost or focal, from other round cell tumours {34965292}. Schwann cells are positive for S100 and SOX10. Flow cytometric analysis for DNA ploidy and molecular studies for MYCN amplification, 1p deletion, 11q deletion, 17q gain, and abnormalities in ALK and ATRX have prognostic and therapeutic implications {33155698; 25435121; 25386934; 23329364; 22976801; 22416102; 22585002; 26503946; 27760112; 10930802; 21774092; 23979398; 35414524}.
