8.2.1.3 Pleomorphic liposarcoma

Definition

Pleomorphic liposarcoma (PLPS) is a pleomorphic high-grade sarcoma containing a variable number of pleomorphic lipoblasts and lacking any other specific differentiation.

Clinical features and imaging

Pleomorphic liposarcoma occurs over a wide age range, from 18 to 93 years. Patients frequently present with a rapidly growing painless mass, most commonly in the lower extremity (including the thigh), followed by the upper extremity and trunk. Other sites are the retroperitoneum, head and neck, abdomen, pelvis, and spermatic cord {11979090; 15371941}.

On CT and MRI, a well-circumscribed tumour with a negligible amount of fat is noted. The MRI features resemble those of other high-grade sarcomas, including areas of necrosis and haemorrhage {21700993}.

Histopathology

Tumours display varying admixtures of lipogenic and non-lipogenic areas, including pleomorphic sarcomatous, round cell, epithelioid, and spindle cell components, along with tumour necrosis, vascular invasion, and frequent mitotic figures. Rarely, myxofibrosarcoma-like and pericytic growth patterns are noted {11979090; 15371941}. ICC shows S100 expression in areas of lipogenic differentiation in as many as 48% cases {11979090; 15371941}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Moderate to high cellularity Loosely cohesive tissue fragments and scattered single cells displaying marked pleomorphism, as well as stripped nuclei Variable numbers of lipoblasts and cells with fibrillary cytoplasm and microvesicles Epithelioid and spindle cells are seen Inflammatory cells and mitotic figures Lipoproteinaceous background and necrosis Rarely, myxoid background and branching blood vessels have been reported

Reference(s): {24652822; 24227706; 17973999; 17536551; 15108227; 21053578}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

PLPS can be misdiagnosed as a pleomorphic sarcoma, a dedifferentiated liposarcoma, or a well-differentiated liposarcoma. Well-differentiated liposarcoma lacks pleomorphic sarcomatous cells {24652822}. Dedifferentiated liposarcoma with homologous differentiation is difficult to differentiate from pleomorphic liposarcoma {20588177}. Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma and myxofibrosarcoma constitute other differential diagnoses because they display “pseudolipoblasts” {24121178; 28544823; 33041221}. The epithelioid variant of PLPS can mimic carcinoma {10430277; 10578991}. The newly characterized myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma enters the diagnostic consideration in young adults {34168281; 26647907}.

Ancillary testing

There is no pathognomonic genetic alteration, but mutations of TP53 and NF1, as well as copy loss of RB1 (50%), have been reported in PLPS {21598240; 9829856; 20601955}.
