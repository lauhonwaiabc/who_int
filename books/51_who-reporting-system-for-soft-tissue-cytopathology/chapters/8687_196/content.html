8.6.8.7 Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour

Definition

Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour (ERT) is a highly malignant soft tissue tumour, mainly affecting infants and children, that consists of characteristic round or polygonal neoplastic cells with eccentric glassy eosinophilic cytoplasm containing hyaline-like inclusion bodies, as well as macronucleoli. Morphologically and genetically identical tumours also arise in the kidney and brain. The majority of tumours are characterized by biallelic alterations of SMARCB1, leading to loss of expression of SMARCB1 (INI1).

Clinical features and imaging

ERT affects very young infants and neonates. Rare congenital cases have been documented with dissemination of tumour to the placenta {25751458; 31718365}. ERT arises in the deep soft tissues of the extremities; abdominal, pelvic, and retroperitoneal areas; and head and neck region. ERT can also occur in visceral organs. ERT most commonly presents as large tumorous nodules, but it can occasionally manifest as multiple metastases, commonly in the skin. The prognosis for ERT is dismal, with a majority of patients succumbing to metastases or dissemination of tumour {30914466; 34295774}. A subset of patients are predisposed to the development of one or more rhabdoid tumours as part of a predisposition syndrome {25494491; 29397238}.

Histopathology

ERT shows sheets of undifferentiated cells with a rhabdoid appearance. Individual cells have abundant eccentric dense eosinophilic cytoplasm, clear chromatin, and a large nucleolus. There is often a paranuclear dense eosinophilic inclusion or globule that corresponds to intermediate filaments. Necrosis is frequently present, and mitoses are easily identifiable.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Variable cellularity Large cells seen singly and in loosely cohesive tissue fragments High N:C ratio Eccentric pale blue to basophilic (Giemsa) cytoplasm Large nuclei with macronucleoli Nuclear chromatin is fine and clear

Reference(s): {3465147; 2159866; 1322263}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of malignant rhabdoid tumour includes a number of paediatric neoplasms in the “small round blue cell tumour” category, such as rhabdomyosarcoma, desmoplastic round cell sarcoma, Wilms tumour, and Ewing sarcoma, requiring ICC to make a diagnosis.

Ancillary testing

ERT is variably positive for CD99 and markers of neuroendocrine differentiation {8092393; 9930572}. ERT shows loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression {15489652}. Affected individuals have germline mutations or deletions affecting the SMARCB1 locus on chromosome 22 {21208904}. Rhabdoid tumours coexpress vimentin and markers of epithelial differentiation such as EMA or keratin (which frequently presents in a paranuclear pattern).
