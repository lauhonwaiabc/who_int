4.3.1.6A IgG4-related disease

Definition

Immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4)-related disease (IgG4-RD) or IgG4-related sclerosing disease (IgG4-RSD) is a clinicopathological entity marked by IgG4-positive plasma cell infiltration and fibrosis in one or more organs.

Clinical features and imaging

Diagnosis of IgG4-RD requires the characteristic microscopic appearance along with an increased number of IgG4-positive plasma cells and an elevated IgG4:IgG plasma cell ratio in tissue {22596100}. IgG4-RD or IgG4-RSD most commonly affects middle-aged and elderly men. In FNAB, IgG4-RD is mostly encountered in the head and neck region, including the salivary glands, as well as in the lung, the pancreatobiliary tract, and the liver.

Patients generally present with symptoms referable to the formation of a mass that is increasing in volume and that is visible if superficial (e.g. in salivary gland) or that causes organ compression if deeply located (e.g. in the pancreas or in the hepatobiliary region). Fever and constitutional symptoms are uncommon {22596100; 22827716}.

Histopathology

Three major histopathological features are present: a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, fibrosis arranged at least focally in a storiform pattern, and obliterative phlebitis. The presence of two of these three features supports the diagnosis, and the dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate and storiform-type fibrosis are usually present. Phlebitis without obliteration of the lumen and an increased number of eosinophils may also be present {22596100}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Predominance of small lymphocytes with abundant plasma cells, a variable number of eosinophils, and sometimes macrophages Fibroblasts and myofibroblasts are intermingled with the inflammatory infiltrate Occasional tissue fragments showing irregularly arranged spindle cells may be present

Reference(s): {32578252; 22827716; 29924455}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

A firm cytopathological diagnosis of IGg4-RD is not possible on cytomorphology and always requires ICC assessment of IgG and IgG4 positivity in the plasma cells and careful correlation with all clinical and imaging findings. FNAB of patients with IgG4-RD shows a prominent lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate. Smears almost never display storiform-type fibrosis or obliterative phlebitis, but these features may be present in cell blocks.

The differential diagnosis includes all other inflammatory processes that can develop with pseudonodular masses in superficial and deep locations. The presence of granulomas, giant cells, and abundant neutrophils suggests an inflammatory or infectious process and not IgG4-RD.

Ancillary testing

ICC to assess the number of IgG4-positive plasma cells and the IgG4:IgG plasma cell ratio is absolutely essential to support the diagnosis of IgG4-RD. The cut-off point for the number of IgG4-positive plasma cells per mm2 varies between different organs; in small specimens, a cut-off point of > 4 IgG4-positive plasma cells per mm2 has been proposed. In addition, an IgG4:IgG plasma cell ratio of > 40% has been proposed as a cut-off value in any organ. However, even if both cut-off values are exceeded, the diagnosis of IgG4-RD always requires careful correlation with all clinical and imaging findings {22596100}.
