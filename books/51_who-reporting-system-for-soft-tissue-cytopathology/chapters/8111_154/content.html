8.1.1.1 Introduction

The diagnosis of malignancy is dependent in part on the quality of the material provided for assessment, in part on the nature and location of the lesion, and in part on the cytopathologist’s experience and confidence. ICC, FISH, and molecular analysis for specific mutations are also important for the diagnosis of malignancy in some soft tissue tumours.

The “Malignant” category is used when unequivocal cytomorphological features of malignancy are present. No single cytopathological feature is diagnostic of malignancy, but the presence of round cells, prominent nuclear atypia, marked abnormalities of N:C ratio, atypical mitotic figures, marked variability in cell size and shape, necrosis, and hypercellularity are highly suggestive of malignancy.

Non-lymphoid proliferations composed of round or oval cells from soft tissue neoplasms are almost always malignant, whereas tumours composed of pleomorphic cells are frequently malignant. Tumours composed of bland uniform spindle cells lacking nuclear atypia and mitotic figures are often benign but require careful analysis and correlation with imaging and clinical features to avoid false negative and false positive diagnoses. Correlation with histopathological features in CNB is also helpful in avoiding a false positive diagnosis. Hypercellular specimens may be malignant, whereas a hypocellular specimen favours a benign diagnosis. Some soft tissue malignancies have characteristic molecular features; for example, MDM2 amplification is particularly helpful in establishing malignancy in fat tumours. Other sarcomas are associated with specific translocations, including ASPSCR1::TFE3 in clear cell sarcoma, FUS::CREB3L2 in low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, and FUS::DD1T3 in myxoid liposarcoma {24411016; 24138725}.

After confirmation of malignancy, cytopathological examination should establish the type of malignancy by differentiating between sarcomas, metastatic carcinomas, melanoma, and lymphomas. When possible, further subtyping into a specific sarcoma type or at least designation of the sarcoma as low-grade or high-grade should be performed, and ICC and molecular analysis can aid in the specific identification of some sarcoma types.
