6.2.2 Sample reports: STNUMP

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format depends on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and ROSE are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as “Diagnostic summary” (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Specimen type:

CT-guided FNAB and CNB

Clinical and imaging information:

54-year-old female, right retroperitoneal mass.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Right retroperitoneum

Category: Soft tissue neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential

Diagnosis: ALK-positive spindle and epithelioid neoplasm (see comment below)

 

Material received:

Eight air-dried Giemsa-stained slides from ROSE, three alcohol-fixed slides. 4 mL bloodstained fluid for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These moderately cellular smears show uniform spindle cells with tapered cytoplasm and oval to coffee-bean nuclei, with scattered larger polygonal cells with large nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant cytoplasm, in a background of lymphocytes and plasma cells. There is no necrosis. The cell block shows considerable similar material. Immunocytochemistry shows that the spindle cells are positive for ALK and negative for AE1/AE3 pancytokeratin, claudin-4, CD30, CD45RB, ERG, DOG1, desmin, SMA, S100, SOX10, CD34, and MDM2.

 

Comment: The features suggest a myofibroblastic tumour. It is noted that the CNB shows limited but similar material. FISH to assess for ALK gene rearrangements is underway and will be reported in a supplementary report.

 

Supplementary report 1:

ALK rearrangement is detected by FISH, supporting the diagnosis of inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour.

 

(Editor’s note: In this case, the cytopathologist has waited to include the ICC on the cell block rather than issuing a cytopathology report to be later followed up with a supplementary report. FISH has confirmed the diagnosis and has been reported in a supplementary final integrated report. They have also mentioned the concurrent CNB, which provided limited material.)

Sample report 2

Specimen type:

CT-guided FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

43-year-old male, left presacral 40 mm soft tissue mass.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Presacral mass

Category: Soft tissue neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential

Diagnosis: Solitary fibrous tumour (see comment below)

 

Material received:

Nine air-dried Giemsa-stained smears from ROSE, three alcohol-fixed smears. 4 mL bloodstained fluid processed for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These moderately cellular smears show uniform spindle cells in tissue fragments and as single cells in the background. The spindle cells show fine cytoplasmic processes and nuclei with fine granular chromatin and no nucleoli. Some fat and tissue fragments that vary from minute, collagenous, and hypocellular to myxoid are also present. There is no necrosis and mitoses are not seen. There is considerable similar material in the cell block. The spindle cells are positive for CD34 and STAT6, and negative for desmin, S100, SMA, and MDM2.

 

Comment: The overall features support the diagnosis of solitary fibrous tumour. Cellular atypia, mitoses, and necrosis are not seen in this small sample of a large tumour, and risk stratification to assess metastatic potential based on a multivariate risk stratification model for solitary fibrous tumour (see reference below) can be performed only on resection specimens.

 

Reference: Demicco EG, Wagner MJ, Maki RG, et al. Risk assessment in solitary fibrous tumors: validation and refinement of a risk stratification model. Mod Pathol. 2017 Oct;30(10):1433–42 {28731041}

 

(Editor’s note: In this case, the cytopathologist has issued a single integrated report with ICC and the final diagnosis.)
