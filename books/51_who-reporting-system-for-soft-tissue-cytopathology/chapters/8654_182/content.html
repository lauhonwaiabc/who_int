8.6.5.4 Spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

Spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma (RMS) is a type of RMS that has fascicular spindle cell and/or sclerosing morphology.

Clinical features and imaging

“Fusion-associated spindle and sclerosing rhabdomyosarcomas”, including infantile cases characterized by a recurrent VGLL2-related fusion {26501226}, and “MYOD1-mutant spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma” {35929754}, have replaced the single group “spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma”, based on morphology, genetic changes, and importantly clinical outcomes {27562493; 31383960} in the forthcoming sixth-edition WHO classification of soft tissue and bone tumours. The varying FNAB findings of these two new entities are yet to be fully elucidated, and the following sections are based on the fifth edition at this stage.

Spindle cell / sclerosing RMS accounts for 3–10% of all RMS cases. It affects infants, children, and adults. The tumours usually present as a rapidly growing, painless soft tissue mass, with symptoms related to local compression at the tumour site. The head and neck region is the most common site of involvement, followed by the extremities. Rarely, other locations are affected, such as viscera (uterus, prostate), retroperitoneum, or bone {16006807; 1599014; 30270691; 24009636; 11860548; 30720533; 12218574; 29431183; 25197394; 23320845; 29758589; 28839951; 10834531; 26823695}.

Histopathology

Spindle cell RMS is characterized by cellular fascicles of spindle cells with an intersecting or herringbone growth pattern, resembling leiomyosarcoma or fibrosarcoma. The spindle neoplastic cells have pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and blunt, ovoid, or fusiform, centrally located nuclei with small inconspicuous nucleoli. Mitotic figures, nuclear atypia, and hyperchromatic nuclei are often present. Primitive undifferentiated areas with round cells and hyperchromatic nuclei may also be present focally. Sclerosing RMSs show prominent hyalinizing sclerosis, with tumour cells arranged in cords, nests, microalveoli, or trabeculae in a pseudovascular growth pattern, with prominent intervening sclerosis noted {30181563; 16006807; 1599014; 8434703; 10834531}. The recently described intraosseous spindle cell RMSs show, apart from the typical spindle cell morphology, areas of distinctly epithelioid cells arranges in sheets and fascicles {29431183; 29758589; 30720533; 31383960}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma
Low cellularity Tissue fragments of varying size consisting of round to polygonal cells with round to oval nuclei and variable amount of ill-defined cytoplasm Wisps of intervening metachromatic stroma between the cells may be seen in Giemsa-stained smears
Spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma
Moderate cellularity Loose and tightly cohesive tissue fragments showing fascicles of spindle cells with oval to spindle hyperchromatic nuclei and a minimal to moderate amount of ill-defined bluish to vacuolated cytoplasm (Giemsa) Cellular stromal fragments and a variable number of macrophages

Reference(s): {35929754}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes synovial sarcoma, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, and various malignant spindle and round cell tumours {35929754}. The lack of arborizing vascular structures is useful in ruling out a synovial sarcoma despite the presence of relatively monomorphic round to oval cells {9500652}. Leiomyosarcomas display a variable number of spindle cells with blunt-ended nuclei and/or epithelioid cells. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour is a spindle cell sarcoma that may show heterologous rhabdomyoblastic differentiation, often closely mimicking spindle cell RMS and distinguished by ICC {3461649; 22434579}. The presence of spindle nuclei is helpful in largely ruling out Ewing sarcoma and other round cell malignancies, and ICC can be diagnostic.

Ancillary testing

Spindle cell / sclerosing RMS is characterized in ICC by variable but often diffuse expression of desmin and only focal expression of myogenin (MYF4) in most cases. MYOD1 staining can be focal or diffuse in spindle cell RMS, but the new entity in the forthcoming sixth-edition WHO classification of soft tissue and bone tumours, “MYOD1-mutant spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma”, usually shows diffuse MYOD1 positivity. A subset of cases of the recently described intraosseous spindle cell RMS can also show positivity for keratin and ALK {33318583}. Synovial sarcoma harbours SS18::SSX fusions, which can be detected by FISH for SS18 or ICC for SS18::SSX and SSX. Leiomyosarcoma is positive for desmin, but it is also positive for SMA and caldesmon and negative for myogenin {16900474}. Spindle cell RMS can be differentiated from malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour by its staining for skeletal muscle markers and negativity for Schwannian markers, as well as the expression of S100, SOX10, GFAP, and H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) {31107719}.
