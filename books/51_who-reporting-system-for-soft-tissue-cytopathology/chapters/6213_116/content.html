6.2.1.3 Solitary fibrous tumour

Definition

Solitary fibrous tumour (SFT) is a spindle cell neoplasm with fibroblastic differentiation, characterized by prominent, branching, thin-walled dilated (“staghorn”) vasculature and NAB2::STAT6 gene rearrangement.

Clinical features and imaging

SFT presents as a painless mass arising in the superficial or deep soft tissues of the extremities, abdomen, pelvis, pleura, meninges, retroperitoneum, trunk, head and neck, and (rarely) visceral organs. SFTs have been described over a wide age range (median: 50–60 years) and with equal sex distribution. Rarely, they present with a hypoglycaemic paraneoplastic syndrome, called Doege–Potter syndrome {22575866; 24360568; 26924782; 28731041; 28838212; 28877055; 29039030; 30206022}.

Histopathology

SFTs tend to be well-circumscribed, lobulated masses. They show a wide histopathological spectrum, with variable cellularity and haphazard growth of fibroblast-like spindle cells with pale cytoplasm, as well as variable paucicellular fibrotic, collagenous keloidal stroma. They contain characteristic thin-walled dilated vessels showing a branching “staghorn” or haemangiopericytoma-like vasculature. Subtypes may contain mature adipocytes and are known as fat-forming or lipomatous SFT {7793472; 10524520; 11014579}, or they contain multinucleated giant cells {10895819} or myxoid stroma {10349983}. Rarely, they may undergo dedifferentiation to high-grade sarcoma {19718788; 22613995}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes desmoid-type fibromatosis, low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, neurogenic tumours (including peripheral nerve sheath tumours), vascular tumours, breast myofibroblastoma, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP), and synovial sarcoma. The differential for fat-forming SFT includes well-differentiated liposarcoma.

The primary diagnosis of SFT on FNAB is difficult due to the cytomorphological overlap with other entities in the differential diagnosis. Definitive diagnosis typically requires the use of ancillary testing on a cell block or concurrent core biopsy. Risk stratification for SFT cannot be performed on cytopathology material and requires evaluation of the resection specimen.

Ancillary testing

By ICC, SFT expresses CD34, BCL2, and CD99, although these markers are nonspecific, but nuclear positivity for STAT6 is a sensitive and specific surrogate for the fusion in SFT {24030747; 24702701; 25873501; 28914981; 34049841}. Identification of NAB2::STAT6 is difficult on conventional cytogenetics and FISH due to the proximity of the gene partners on chromosome 12q, and the gene fusion can be better characterized by PCR and NGS {23313952; 23313954; 23761323}.

Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma is positive for MUC4. Peripheral nerve sheath tumours, such as schwannoma and neurofibroma, express S100 and/or SOX10. Desmoid-type fibromatosis shows nuclear expression of β-catenin. Synovial sarcomas may be positive for keratin and/or EMA, and they also express TLE1, although this may be diffusely positive in a subset of SFTs. However, synovial sarcoma is positive for SSX1::SS18, SSX2::SS18, or SSX4::SS18 in the vast majority of cases, and ICC for SSX C-terminus or SSX::SS18 fusion–specific antibodies can be used to support the diagnosis.

CD34 expression may be seen in DFSP, vascular tumours, and a subset of breast myofibroblastomas. In difficult cases, STAT6 can differentiate SFT from DFSP, and positive COL1A1::PDGFB supports DFSP. Vascular tumours express other vascular markers, such as CD31, ERG, and FLI1, in addition to CD34. Myofibroblastomas are positive for desmin, and a subset show loss of RB1 expression by ICC. Finally, MDM2, which is positive in well-differentiated liposarcoma, is negative in SFT, although a subset of liposarcomas express STAT6 by ICC.
