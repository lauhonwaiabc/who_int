8.6.8.10 Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma

Definition

Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS) shows no identifiable line of differentiation when analysed with currently available technology. It is a heterogeneous group and a diagnosis of exclusion, although genetic subgroups (particularly in the round cell group) have emerged. Dedifferentiated types of specific soft tissue sarcomas are excluded from this category.

Clinical features and imaging

UPS predominantly affects older adults. It is common in deep somatic soft tissues but may present at any anatomical location. Frequently characterized by rapid growth, it has an otherwise non-distinctive clinical presentation. UPS was previously called undifferentiated sarcoma and was categorized as a tumour of uncertain differentiation in the fifth edition of the WHO classification of soft tissue tumours. It is now categorized as a fibroblastic and myofibroblastic neoplasm.

Histopathology

Typically, UPS is an overtly malignant, high-grade sarcoma composed of pleomorphic cells in a storiform to other non-distinctive pattern. Bizarre, multinucleated tumour giant cells are common. Tumour cell nuclei demonstrate marked variation in size and shape, hyperchromasia, and membrane irregularities {30709449; 34602314}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Clinical factors inform the differential diagnosis, including anatomical site {30340082}. Dedifferentiated liposarcoma and intimal sarcoma are considerations for retroperitoneal and great vessel tumours, respectively {12640106; 34602314}. In a patient with a history of carcinoma or melanoma, a dedifferentiated form of the prior tumour should be considered {34602314; 30709449}. A dedifferentiated component of a specific type of mesenchymal tumour, such as leiomyosarcoma, solitary fibrous tumour, or gastrointestinal stroma tumour, should be excluded. In the context of prior radiation therapy, a radiation-associated sarcoma is considered given that at least 25% of these are undifferentiated {20308666; 3179948}.

For some tumours, including pleomorphic liposarcoma with its lipoblasts, extraskeletal osteosarcoma showing bone matrix, and myxofibrosarcoma with its myxoid matrix and characteristic vasculature, the characteristic diagnostic features may be limited in extent and distribution, and these cases can be problematic to distinguish with a sampling technique such as FNAB or CNB {30340082; 30709449; 33041221}.

Although most tumours in the differential are high-grade sarcomas, cellular pleomorphism is also a feature of several benign neoplasms, such as pleomorphic lipoma and “ancient” schwannoma. Neoplasms of intermediate biological potential should also be considered {34602314}, including pleomorphic dermal sarcoma and superficial CD34-positive fibroblastic tumour, which enter the differential in superficial sites {31828431; 23887307}.

The diagnosis of UPS is one of exclusion, so although it can be suggested on FNAB as a diagnostic possibility, definitive diagnosis requires ICC and in many cases excision biopsy.

Ancillary testing

UPS demonstrates complex karyotypes with extensive genomic rearrangements and no specific immunophenotype or molecular signature {29100075}. Therefore, ICC and molecular studies are required to exclude sarcomas of specific types and lines of differentiation {30709449; 30340082; 34602314}. MDM2 and CDK4 amplification or protein overexpression by ICC supports a diagnosis of intimal sarcoma or dedifferentiated liposarcoma, respectively {12640106; 34602314}.
