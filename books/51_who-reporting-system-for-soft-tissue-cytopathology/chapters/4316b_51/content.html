4.3.1.6B Elastofibroma

Definition

Elastofibroma is a benign, ill-defined proliferation of fibroelastic tissue with excessive abnormal elastic fibres.

Clinical features and imaging

Elastofibroma is a soft tissue proliferation of uncertain origin that occurs predominantly in elderly individuals about 70 years of age, particularly women {7116305; 33952447; 32913617; 31233334; 17280612}. Although the exact prevalence of elastofibroma is unknown, in larger studies, the lesions were reported in 0.8–2.73% of elderly individuals by CT, and an incidence rate of 13–17% has been reported in autopsy studies {31031128; 9762978; 35110340; 1124654}. The lesion commonly presents as a slowly growing unilateral or occasionally bilateral mass, ranging from 20 to 150 mm, located between the subscapular area and the chest wall {33952447; 32913617}. There have been reports of rare multiple lesions and lesions in atypical locations, including different parts of the thoracic wall, extremities, neck, joints, mediastinum, and viscera {32913617; 17280612; 31914985}.

MRI of elastofibroma, which has a higher diagnostic confidence than either ultrasound or CT, reveals a double-convex, lens-shaped soft tissue mass with a low signal intensity, similar to that of skeletal muscle, interlaced with strands of fat on both T1-weighted and T2-weighted sequences {33952447; 28920444; 19539441}. Elastofibromas are easily detectable on PET-CT due to their hypermetabolic nature {31293317; 34658562}.

Histopathology

Elastofibroma is composed of abundant hypocellular collagenous stroma with an admixture of bland fibroblasts, mature adipocytes, and numerous disrupted elastic fibres within the collagenous stroma.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Scant to moderate cellularity Fat tissue intermingled with collagen fibres and bland spindle cells Loosely cohesive tissue fragments or dispersed uniform spindle cells Elastic fibres are present as linear (braid-like) and globular bodies with a shell-like and stellate appearance and serrated “moth-eaten” edges, best seen in the Pap stain

Reference(s): {11992374; 1636353; 12552956; 23554149; 21630483; 14648789; 11074644; 8050332; 18500010; 17536569; 20422214}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The pathogenesis of elastofibroma is still unclear and a matter of debate {3552953; 32913617; 19132384; 1842374; 25820560; 12165800; 25119171}. The main diagnostic feature is the presence of elastic fibres with serrated borders, corresponding to faulty elastin fibrillogenesis. These structures are usually easy to find in alcohol-fixed Pap-stained smears but are very subtle or invisible in air-dried Giemsa-stained smears {23554149; 14648789}. The differential diagnosis includes extra-abdominal desmoid fibromatosis, spindle cell lipoma, and nuchal fibroma.

Ancillary testing

The elastin Verhoeff–Van Gieson stain is positive in cell block material, showing fragmented abnormal elastic fibres.
