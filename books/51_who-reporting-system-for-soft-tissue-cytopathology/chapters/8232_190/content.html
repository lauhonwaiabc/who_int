8.2.3.2 Synovial sarcoma

Definition

Synovial sarcoma (SS) is a monomorphic spindle cell sarcoma showing variable epithelial differentiation, characterized by specific SS18::SSX1, SS18::SSX2, or SS18::SSX4 fusions.

Clinical features and imaging

SS presents as a painful or painless mass over a wide age range (median: 30–40 years) and shows an equal sex distribution {11782370; 19514087}. SSs most often occur in the deep soft tissue of the extremities, but they also arise in the trunk and abdomen, head and neck, mediastinum, retroperitoneum, and visceral organs {19514087}. Imaging shows stippled, spiculated calcifications in 30% of cases {20858835; 23169746}.

Histopathology

SS is classified as monophasic, biphasic, or poorly differentiated. Monophasic SS shows uniform spindle cells with indistinct cytoplasm arranged in sheets and fascicles that may show whorling or herringbone patterns. Biphasic SS shows a mix of similar spindle cell proliferation and focal epithelial differentiation that includes cords, nests, glands, and papillae. Poorly differentiated SS shows round cells or a large epithelioid pattern with prominent nucleoli. Background stromal tissue may be scant to abundant, dense and collagenized, or focally myxoid. SS often shows irregular, dilated, thin-walled, so-called “staghorn” vessels, imparting the previously known “haemangiopericytoma” pattern, and calcifications are variably present. Mitotic figures are readily identified and are increased in poorly differentiated SS.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The specific diagnosis of monophasic SS on cytopathology alone may be challenging, but cellular FNAB smears from SS can be categorized as “Malignant” {12203876}. A soft tissue sarcoma with biphasic morphology, in a patient without neurofibromatosis, is consistent with biphasic SS. The primary diagnosis of poorly differentiated SS is especially challenging {17559565; 28345284}, and definitive classification typically requires the use of ancillary studies to differentiate from cytopathological mimics.

The differential diagnosis for monophasic SS includes malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST), solitary fibrous tumour (SFT), dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, gastrointestinal stromal tumour, and thymoma. MPNST occurs in the setting of neurofibromatosis, in a background of neurofibroma, or arising from a peripheral nerve. Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans arises superficially.

The differential diagnosis for biphasic SS includes MPNST with epithelial differentiation, myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue, epithelioid sarcoma, and metastatic carcinoma, and ICC is often required. Poorly differentiated SS must be distinguished from small round cell sarcomas including Ewing sarcoma, CIC-rearranged sarcoma, sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations, rhabdomyosarcoma, and lymphoma, and this requires ICC.

Ancillary testing

By ICC, SS typically expresses EMA and/or keratins, including pankeratins, CK7, and CK19. They may also express S100, BCL2, and CD99, but these markers are nonspecific. In the differential diagnosis, MPNSTs focally express S100 or SOX10, but a subset of cases show complete loss of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3). SS often shows weak to patchy positivity for H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) and rarely complete loss of expression. SFT may also express CD99 and BCL2, but STAT6 can be used as a surrogate for the NAB2::STAT6 fusion characteristic of SFT. TLE1 is diffusely and strongly positive in SS nuclei in most cases, but TLE1 may also be expressed in MPNST and SFT {17255769; 19363472; 21571956}. Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans typically expresses CD34, which is negative in SS, whereas gastrointestinal stromal tumour expresses KIT (CD117), DOG1, and CD34. Myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue often show expression of epithelial markers; neurogenic markers, such as S100 and SOX10; and myogenic markers, including SMA, calponin, desmin, and p63. A subset show loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression. Myoepithelial tumours may harbour EWSR1 rearrangements with various partners. Epithelioid sarcoma can also show epithelial differentiation by ICC, loss of SMARCB1 (INI1), and positivity for CD34.

Many of the differential diagnoses of poorly differentiated SS express CD99. Ewing sarcomas show diffuse strong membranous staining with CD99 and nuclear NKX2-2. They harbour EWSR1 or FUS rearrangements. In CIC-rearranged sarcomas, CD99 expression is often multifocal, and they may also express WT1 and ETV4. Sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations also expresses multifocal CD99 positivity, but it additionally expresses SATB2, BCOR, and cyclin B3 (CCNB3) in tumours that harbour BCOR::CCNB3 fusions. SS also shows expression of BCOR, so molecular testing is typically required to confirm the presence of SS18 fusion. Rhabdomyosarcoma expresses desmin, myogenin, and MYOD1, and the alveolar subtype harbours PAX3::FOXO1 or PAX7::FOXO1 fusion.

The SS18::SSX fusion–specific antibody is a surrogate for fusions of SS18 with SSX1, SSX2, or SSX4; both it and the SSX C-terminus antibody are nuclear stains, sensitive and specific for SS {32141887}. By cytogenetics, SS harbours a t(X;18) translocation {1379714}. The fusion can also be demonstrated by FISH for SS18 {8579122; 15208645; 17334349} or PCR and NGS to identify SS18::SSX1, SSX2, or SSX4 fusion partners {15364967; 11782370; 17334349}.
