4.1.1.1 Introduction

A “Benign” categorization is applied when a sample is adequate for interpretation and shows unequivocal features of a benign lesion. This may represent a specific benign neoplasm or inflammation process.

The “Benign” category of soft tissue tumours includes an assortment of neoplasms that are further subclassified based on distinctive cytomorphological features that manifest as an identifiable “pattern”, including specific types of stroma or matrix constituting adipocytic, myxoid, and chondro-osseous patterns; a prominent cellular feature such as inflammation or multinucleate giant cells; or a specific cell shape, as in spindle or epithelioid patterns.

The fifth-edition WHO classification of soft tissue tumours recognizes an “intermediate” category of soft tissue lesions based on their biological potential [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]], and this is further subdivided into “locally aggressive” neoplasms such as deep or aggressive fibromatosis, a neoplasm that is locally aggressive but will not metastasize, and “rarely metastasizing” neoplasms that are occasionally aggressive or have a very low but nevertheless real and documented propensity for metastasis. In this first WHO Reporting System for Soft Tissue Cytopathology (WHO System), the “intermediate” lesions of the WHO classification are divided and categorized into two distinct categories: “locally aggressive” lesions such as fibromatosis into the “Benign” category, and “rarely metastasizing” tumours such as angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma into the “Soft tissue neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential” (“STNUMP”) category. The “STNUMP” category acknowledges that with selected neoplasms, the cytopathological and often the histopathological and ICC features are not always a reliable predictor of the behaviour of the neoplasm, and that some unpredictable neoplasms, although most commonly behaving in a benign fashion, may have a small potential for malignant behaviour.
