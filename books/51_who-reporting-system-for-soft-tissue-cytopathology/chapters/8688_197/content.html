8.6.8.8 PEComa

Definition

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumours (PEComas) are mesenchymal neoplasms composed of perivascular epithelioid cells, which are distinctive epithelioid cells that are often closely associated with blood vessel walls and that express both melanocytic and smooth muscle markers on ICC.

Clinical features and imaging

PEComas occur most often in middle-aged individuals, with a female predominance, and they consist of a family of related tumours that include angiomyolipoma (AML) and lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM). Tumours may arise from any part of the body, including the kidney, lung, liver, pancreas, gastrointestinal tract, gynaecological tract, and soft tissues {16359539}. PEComas are commonly seen as well-circumscribed masses {31921981; 30417984}, but imaging is nonspecific, overlapping with other more common tumours at the corresponding site. Specific imaging may be seen in some scenarios, such as pulmonary LAM demonstrating diffuse abundant cysts in bilateral lungs, or renal AML with high fat content.

Histopathology

PEComas are composed of variable proportions of epithelioid and spindle cells, which are characterized by ovoid to round nuclei with clear to eosinophilic granular cytoplasm with well-defined borders. Tumour cells are present as solid sheets, expansile nests, and cords. Perivascular distribution with radial arrangement of tumour cells surrounding a vascular lumen may be seen, but this is not a required diagnostic feature. Thin and delicate vessels in a sinusoidal pattern are common. Stromal hyalinization may be present; when it is extensive, this subtype is called sclerosing PEComa {21293289}. Giant cells and melanin pigment may be present. By convention, PEComas with predominantly clear, epithelioid cells may be referred to as “clear cell” tumours, and those composed predominantly of plump spindle cells that are arranged around dilated lymphatics are designated as LAM or lymphangiomyoma if present as a localized mass. AML is morphologically distinct, typically composed of smooth muscle, fat, and thick-walled vessels in varying proportions.

The criteria for malignancy are not well-defined in PEComa, although cases with aggressive clinical behaviour tend to exhibit large size (> 50 mm), an infiltrative growth pattern, lymphovascular invasion, significant cytopathological atypia, necrosis, and mitotic activity {16327428}. Organ system–specific modifications to these criteria have been proposed and continue to be revised as data accumulates for these rare entities {24418852; 30001237; 21263237}. Given these limitations, malignant potential should be reported cautiously in cytopathological specimens, unless it is a metastatic lesion in a patient with a known history.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of PEComa varies depending on the spindle or epithelioid cell type and tumour location. Given PEComas’ expression of melanocytic markers, the differential includes melanoma in every site, but smooth muscle ICC is positive in PEComa and negative in melanoma. PEComas with predominantly epithelioid morphology may be confused with carcinomas with clear cytoplasm, such as clear cell renal cell carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, and clear cell carcinoma of Müllerian origin, but carcinomas are distinguished by diffuse cytokeratin expression and lack of smooth muscle and melanocytic marker expression.

PEComas with spindle cells may be confused with true smooth muscle neoplasms, and the cytomorphological evaluation is critical for this distinction, because smooth muscle neoplasms show cigar-shaped nuclei, eosinophilic fibrillary cytoplasm, and a predominance of thick-walled vessels without a delicate capillary network.

Ancillary testing

The hallmark of PEComa is coexpression of melanocytic markers – including HMB45, melan-A (MART1), and MITF – and smooth muscle markers – including desmin, caldesmon, and SMA – although the extent and intensity of expression may be quite variable. Cathepsin K, a protease regulated by MITF, may also be positive {30001237}. Melanocytic markers tend to be more strongly expressed in the epithelioid cells, and smooth muscle markers are more prominent in spindle cells. PEComas are typically negative for S100, SOX10, CD10, and cytokeratin. Melanocytic markers are usually negative in smooth muscle neoplasms, but aberrant HMB45 positivity has been reported {17197924}, making the interpretation of ICC less straightforward. Gastrointestinal stromal tumours may also be considered in the differential, and ICC is helpful in this scenario because gastrointestinal stromal tumours are positive for KIT (CD117) and DOG1 but negative for melanocytic markers and also usually negative for smooth muscle markers.

There are two well-defined molecular subgroups of PEComa: those with TSC1/TSC2 alterations that result in activation of the mTOR pathway and those with TFE3 fusions that result in a predominance of epithelioid and clear cell cytomorphology {25651471}. TFE3-rearranged PEComas strongly express TFE3 by ICC and tend to have little or no MITF or myogenic marker expression.
