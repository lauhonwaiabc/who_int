6.2.1.2 Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans

Definition

Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) is a superficial, locally aggressive fibroblastic neoplasm, with cellular storiform and spindle cell features and harbouring a COL1A1::PDGFB or related fusion.

Clinical features and imaging

DFSP presents most commonly as plaque-like to nodular or multinodular cutaneous masses in young to middle-aged adults (most commonly 20–30 years of age) {13919964; 14799050}. Rarely, they present as an atrophic depressed area of skin {10025975} or a subcutaneous mass {18645303}. Related tumours include giant cell fibroblastoma and hybrid DFSP–giant cell fibroblastoma, which are seen in paediatric patients {12502925; 17349565; 1939782; 21429521}. DFSPs often show slow, persistent growth, unless they undergo fibrosarcomatous transformation. They occur most commonly on the trunk, proximal extremities, and head and neck region. Less commonly they occur in the perineum or genital skin {29140881}, breast {30014607}, or distal extremities {29240584}.

Histopathology

Classic DFSP is characterized by a cellular spindle cell infiltration within the dermis and subcutaneous fat. Cytopathologically, tumour cells are uniform elongate spindle cells arranged in a storiform or whorled pattern. Tumour cells typically surround skin appendages and do not involve the epidermis. Characteristically, they invade into subcutaneous fat, surrounding individual adipocytes and imparting a “honeycomb” appearance to the deep edge of invasion. Subtypes include pigmented DFSP with intermixed pigmented melanocytes {3901787}; myxoid DFSP, which shows prominent myxoid stroma, nodular growth, and prominent vessels with mural fibrosis {17721193; 17890911}; and DFSP with myoid differentiation, which shows nests of cells with myofibroblastic differentiation and eosinophilic hyalinized stroma {8720984}. Importantly, DFSP with fibrosarcomatous transformation shows a transition to a fascicular or herringbone growth pattern, increased cellularity, nuclear atypia, and mitotic activity {9591728; 10935653; 16625088}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Variable cellularity Tissue fragments of cohesive spindle cells showing a storiform architecture within a collagenous matrix Background of single cells and stripped nuclei Many cells with poorly defined cytoplasm Occasionally bipolar cytoplasmic processes in cells with better-defined cytoplasm Mild to moderate nuclear atypia, but uniform chromatin and typically lacking nucleoli May have myxoid or metachromatic matrix (Giemsa) Rarely tissue fragments of adipose tissue infiltrated by spindle tumour cells

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Clinical features may be helpful in distinguishing DFSP from other spindle cell neoplasms. A spectrum of benign and malignant cellular spindle cell neoplasms of soft tissue constitute the differential diagnoses for DFSP, including benign fibrous histiocytoma, peripheral nerve sheath tumours (including schwannoma, neurofibroma, and soft tissue perineurioma), nodular fasciitis, solitary fibrous tumour, “fat-poor” and “fat-free” variants of spindle cell lipoma, low-grade malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, and adult fibrosarcoma {12478683; 15048962; 15830369}.

Benign fibrous histiocytoma tends to present as smaller, pigmented dermal nodules, and smears show an admixture of fibroblasts and epithelioid, xanthomatous, and multinucleated histiocytes, helping to distinguish fibrous histiocytoma from DFSP {15540171}. Schwannomas are characterized by tissue fragments of different sizes with irregular borders (“jigsaw puzzle pieces”), with scant dispersed cells and occasional nuclear palisading and Verocay bodies in the tissue fragments. The spindle cells of schwannoma have elongate, slender, and “boomerang” or “fish-hook” nuclei {16680779}, with the cells within a fibrillary stroma.

Nodular fasciitis typically presents as a rapidly growing subcutaneous mass, and FNAB smears shows a haphazard distribution of single cells and small myxoid fragments of spindle cells and plump ganglion-like cells, often associated with scattered inflammatory cells {8050331; 31741577}.

Smears of solitary fibrous tumour often have a bloody background {9126139}. Spindle cell lipoma presents in the posterior neck or upper back and shows bright eosinophilic fragments of collagen matrix, which helps to differentiate it from DFSP {11748578}.

Smears in low-grade malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour are composed of spindle cells with wavy nuclei and comma-shaped naked nuclei, as well as polymorphous oval to rounded cells {12203877}. Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma may occasionally show cytopathological features overlapping those of DFSP {18637810}.

Adult fibrosarcoma is a vanishingly rare entity and was a histopathological diagnosis of exclusion {20829680}. It is not longer included as a specific neoplasm in the sixth edition of the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series.

Ancillary testing

DFSP is characterized by diffuse strong expression of CD34 {9129699}, which is lost in areas of fibrosarcomatous DFSP {9591728}. Myoid nodules express SMA. Schwannoma and neurofibroma express S100 and SOX10, which are negative in DFSP. Soft tissue perineurioma and neurofibroma may be positive for CD34, but they also express other perineurial markers, GLUT1, claudin-1, and EMA (which is also expressed in 20% of DFSPs) {12426982}. Solitary fibrous tumours also express CD34 but also express nuclear STAT6 {28914981}. Both DFSP and spindle cell lipoma express CD34. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours do not express CD34. A limited panel of antibodies, such as MUC4, EMA, SMA, and CD34, as well as molecular tests to confirm FUS rearrangements in low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, can be helpful for reaching the correct diagnosis. 

Cytogenetic analysis has identified supernumerary ring chromosomes {7495279; 7757993; 8076341; 8697429; 8978765} derived from translocation t(17;22) {7541645} in DFSP. Rare variant translocations t(2;17) and t(9;22) have also been described {8055483; 10198616}. The most common gene fusion, COL1A1::PDGFB {8988177}, can be identified on PCR or NGS {17950782}. Break-apart FISH may be used to identify PDGFB rearrangements. DFSPs with alternative translocations have been shown to harbour COL6A3::PDGFD or EMILIN2::PDGFD {29955147; 30014607; 34628665}. In difficult cases, the diagnosis of nodular fasciitis can be confirmed by the presence of USP6 fusions.
