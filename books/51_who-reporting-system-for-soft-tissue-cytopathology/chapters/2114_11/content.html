2.1.1.4 Cell preparation methods

FNAB smears are the most common cytopathological specimens procured from soft tissue lesions by palpation of superficial masses or using imaging guidance in deeply sited lesions {17941014; 11038426}. Other common preparations are centrifugation smears, cytospin/cytocentrifuge smears, and LBC, any of which can be used, especially for a sample from a cystic or partly cystic lesion or a body cavity effusion fluid that may show involvement by soft tissue sarcomas {31693303; 34233079}. In addition, cell blocks and touch smears from CNB can be useful diagnostic specimens and valuable resources for ancillary testing {11038426; 32417160}.

Direct FNAB smears

FNAB material varies in consistency depending on the nature of the lesion sampled. Cystic lesions usually yield fluid that should be processed differently than samples from more solid lesions. If FNAB yields fluid from a large cystic lesion, a repeat FNAB using ultrasound or other imaging guidance to sample solid areas, if any, should be performed. The fluid can be fixed either in ethanol, in the same volume of ethanol as cyst fluid sample, or in a commercially available LBC medium, or the fluid can be processed by cytocentrifuging followed by direct smearing of the deposit for air-dried and alcohol-fixed smears. Post-FNAB needle rinses can also be handled in the same ways.

A variety of smearing techniques are available, which can be used depending on the consistency of the FNAB material. The commonly used technique is to place a drop of material near the frosted end of the slide and use a second slide oriented perpendicular to the first slide as a spreader to gently spread the material down the length of the slide. An alternative technique may be employed if the sample is very thin or bloody, where the diagnostic material is placed in the centre of the slide and another slide is placed longitudinally on the first slide and used to spread the material to produce smears on both slides. Care should be taken to apply gentle and constant pressure while making smears to avoid crushing of cells and to produce evenly spread smears.

The number of FNABs performed on any given lesion depends on many factors, including the operator’s expertise, the relative accessibility of the lesion, and the need for image guidance for needle placement. Usually, two to four passes are performed on a given lesion. A small amount of the specimen is placed onto a glass slide and smeared, and the remainder of the specimen can be collected for LBC or cell block preparation. A minimum of two smears can be prepared from the sample on the slide. One of the slides is immediately placed in 96% ethanol for several minutes for fixation and subsequently stained with Pap or H&E, while the other is thoroughly air-dried, stained using the Giemsa stain, and used immediately to assess adequacy and make a provisional diagnostic evaluation using ROSE.

Visual inspection of the material and the suspected diagnosis enables triaging of the sample at ROSE or on arrival in the laboratory. Haemolytic and mucolytic agents are used to pretreat haemorrhagic and mucinous specimens. Sparsely cellular specimens can be processed by cell concentration methods.

Cytospin preparations

Cytospin preparations are prepared if scant and/or sparsely cellular fluid is produced from the lesion. Body cavity specimens are usually thin and clear and should be spun down at a high-speed cytospin to deposit a monolayer of cells onto the slide. If the fluid is turbid, thick, and opalescent, smears can be prepared from the sediment of simple centrifugation.

Touch imprint preparations

Touch imprints of CNB are easily prepared and can be particularly helpful in hypercellular tumours such as malignant small round cell tumours of soft tissues {32417160}. Touch imprint smears are prepared by gently dabbing or rolling the core biopsy of the tissue on the glass slide to cover at least 50–60% of the slide surface. The slide is immediately fixed in 96% ethanol or can be air-dried for Giemsa staining and ROSE. Tumour cells can be procured from touch imprints for downstream molecular analysis or other ancillary techniques {23576371}. However, vigorous touch preparations may lose tumour cellularity, cause crush artefact of the CNB, and result in reduced DNA content in CNB {25521802}.

LBC offers the transportation of the FNAB material in a stable collecting medium {25427721}. Several commercially available platforms are available, and the specimen is collected into company-specific vials of patented preservative and fixative to generate LBC slides after processing on specific platforms {31693303}. LBC slide preparations lack the pattern recognition features of direct smears used routinely by cytopathologists and diminish any background.

Cell blocks

Cell blocks can be prepared from FNAB or body cavity fluids, facilitated by ROSE. A dedicated pass can be taken for cell block preparation based on the initial assessment of the lesion. Several methods can be employed, ranging from simple centrifugation, removal of the supernatant, and then use of the sedimentation pellet; to adjuvant-based techniques; to automated cell blocks {25113785; 30153355; 29318761}. Cell blocks are vital resources for performing ancillary techniques such as ICC, in situ hybridization, and molecular testing. It is essential to have a cell block in soft tissue tumour cytopathology, especially in the current era of a growing number of targetable genetic abnormalities in soft tissue tumours.
