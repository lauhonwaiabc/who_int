8.6.6.1B Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma is a high-grade pleomorphic sarcoma, usually of adults, composed of bizarre brightly eosinophilic polygonal, round, and spindle cells that display skeletal muscle differentiation.

Clinical features and imaging

These are tumours of adults and are most common in the sixth to seventh decades of life, with a mean patient age of 72 years; they occur more frequently in men than in women, with an M:F ratio of 1.8:1 {26504053}. These tumours arise in the deep soft tissue. Although most often occurring in the lower extremities, they can also occur at other anatomical sites. Patients present with a rapidly growing, variably painful soft tissue mass {11406662}. These are highly aggressive sarcomas, with a median survival time of 7.3 months in the largest study to date, in which about 80% of patients died of disease {26504053}. Patients with superficial tumours (~20%) had a favourable outcome. Metastases to lungs are common {26504053; 11406662; 8333559}.

Histopathology

Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcomas are usually large, well-marginated tumours {26504053} with a pseudocapsule and a whitish fleshy cut surface, often with necrosis. The tumours are composed of sheets of large, atypical and frequently multinucleated polygonal, spindle, or rhabdoid cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm. Nuclei often show marked variation in size and shape, with hyperchromasia and prominent nucleoli {8333559; 11406662}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

High cellularity Dispersed single cells and tissue fragments Large pleomorphic and rhabdoid cells with copious eccentric basophilic cytoplasm and round to irregular pleomorphic nuclei Multinucleation Background of haemorrhage and necrosis Mitoses, including atypical forms Evidence of skeletal muscle differentiation; cross-striations in the eccentric cytoplasm not common

Reference(s): {19790247; 28024111; 32159876}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes other pleomorphic sarcomas, such as undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, and other rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes with anaplasia. Non-mesenchymal neoplasms, such as sarcomatoid carcinoma, melanoma, and anaplastic large cell lymphoma, can show overlapping cytomorphology, and ICC is required.

Ancillary testing

ICC shows strong desmin expression and often limited expression of MYOD1 and myogenin. Molecular analysis has shown complex karyotypes with numerous copy-number alterations, but no specific molecular alterations have been identified {34773670; 12550764; 19480930}.
