3.1.1.3 Discussion and background

Reported rates of the “Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic” category vary widely {12451566; 32926591; 31611506; 32622858}, influenced by the criteria used to define adequacy, the type of procedure, imaging and clinical findings, and the skills of the person performing the procedure. The definition is not based on the number of cells, because no strictly numerical cut-off value can adequately reflect the wide variety of tissues sampled; the broad differential diagnosis of inflammatory, degenerative, and neoplastic lesions; and the need for correlation of the cytopathological material findings with the clinical and imaging features of the lesion. Using a definition of “no or too few cells”, the “Insufficient” frequency rate for soft tissue lesions has been reported as about 3% {11190808}. The rate was 4% with a definition of “when the quality or quantity of the diagnostic material was insufficient for evaluation” or if there was a discrepancy in the triple test of the cytomorphological, clinical, and imaging provisional diagnoses {32926591}. One recent study suggested a definition of adequacy as “5 clusters of 10 unobscured cells on all slides”, but no rationale for this number was provided [[Pandey G, Jalaly F. Fine needle aspiration cytology of soft tissue lesion: our institutional experience. IOSR J Dent Med Sci. 2021;20(4):1–4. doi:10.9790/0853-2004020104.]]. In a meta-analysis of 13 reports on the adequacy of bone and soft tissue FNAB, the range of adequate specimens for soft tissue lesions was 39–95% {32622858}, somewhat lower, but not statistically significantly so, than for bone samples at 83–95%.

FNAB samples of soft tissue lesions may be hypocellular, particularly vascular lesions, lipomas, or ganglion cysts, and the background material and clinical correlation may imply an adequate preparation even though there are very few cells, as for a typical lipoma. But in the imaging setting of a clearly defined mass, rather than a lipoma, low cellularity suggests an inadequate sample. Site also influences concepts of adequacy; for example, the differential diagnosis of deeply located, intramuscular, and especially large fatty tumours includes lipoma-like atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma, and the requirement for adequate material shifts not only in regard to the amount and features of the material on the FNAB smears, but also as to the availability of material for studies to determine MDM2 amplification status {32926591}.

FNAB smears consisting exclusively of blood cannot be considered diagnostic. So the diagnosis of haemangioma requires correlation with imaging {12451566}, although tissue fragments containing blood vessels may occasionally be diagnostic of haemangioma and can exclude other well-vascularised lesions such as solitary fibrous tumour {9784986}.

Among other mesenchymal tumours, the high content of collagen-rich sclerotic, extracellular matrix, as found for example in desmoid fibromatosis or low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, is often associated with low cellularity due to the inherent properties of the tumour {17260203; 17477374}, and CNB is often required {35427924; 15918176}.
