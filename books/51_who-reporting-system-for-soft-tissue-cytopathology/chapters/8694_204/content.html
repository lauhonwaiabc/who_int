8.6.9.4 Sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations

Definition

Several groups of primitive round cell sarcomas show BCOR genetic alterations, resulting in oncogenic activation and BCOR overexpression. Although these pathological entities show distinct clinical presentations, there is overlap with regard to morphology, immunoprofile, and gene expression, suggesting a shared pathogenesis. The first group is characterized by sarcomas with BCOR-related gene fusions, most frequently BCOR::CCNB3. The second group shows BCOR internal tandem duplication (ITD), which has been described in infantile undifferentiated round cell sarcomas and primitive myxoid mesenchymal tumours of infancy.

Clinical features and imaging

BCOR::CCNB3 sarcomas have a striking predilection for children, with > 90% of patients aged < 20 years {29300189; 28877060}, and a male predominance, with an M:F ratio of 4.5:1 {28420319; 28197724; 24805859}. They arise more often in bone than in soft tissue (ratio: 1.5:1), with a predilection for the pelvis, lower extremities, and paraspinal region {22387997; 24805859}. Sarcomas with alternative BCOR gene rearrangements occur in a wider age range {26752546}. Sarcomas with BCOR ITD and primitive myxoid mesenchymal tumour of infancy occur in the first year of life or are present at birth {16538060; 26945340}, and they usually arise in the soft tissues of the trunk, retroperitoneum, or head and neck {26945340}. Osseous lesions often show cortical destruction and extension into the soft tissues {25176412; 24805859}.

Histopathology

BCOR family tumours show considerable morphological overlap. Patterns include sheets or vague nests of uniform round to ovoid cells, solid areas with plump spindle cells arrayed in short fascicles, and hypocellular areas with short spindle cells in a myxoid matrix {28877060; 29300189; 24805859; 27228320; 25360585; 16538060; 29430000; 26945340}. A rich network of delicate vessels is typically present. Cells are deceptively bland, with fine chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. Mitoses are variable.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of BCOR family tumours includes various primitive round and spindle cell mesenchymal tumours. In Ewing sarcoma, cells are usually more monotonous, pseudorosettes may be present, spindle cells are rare or absent, and myxoid matrix is absent {29300189; 31679010; 34265123}. Although synovial sarcoma can closely mimic BCOR family tumours cytopathologically, both Ewing sarcoma and synovial sarcoma lack the small, delicate vascular fragments seen in BCOR family tumours {34265123}. CIC-rearranged sarcomas often have prominent nucleoli and more pronounced anisonucleosis {23834194; 26800124; 29300189; 33399252}. Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma comprises predominantly single cells with atypical, hyperchromatic nuclei with coarse chromatin and prominent nucleoli; large, multinucleated wreath-like cells with abundant cytoplasm may also be present {26800124; 31679010}. Given the cytomorphological overlap between BCOR family tumours and other mesenchymal tumours, definitive diagnosis requires ICC and/or molecular studies.

Ancillary testing

Tumours with BCOR gene alterations show strong, diffuse nuclear staining for BCOR in ICC, and most are positive for SATB2, TLE1, and cyclin D1. In contrast to other BCOR family tumours, BCOR::CCNB3 sarcomas also express cyclin B3 {24805859; 28877060; 26037154}, and CD99 is positive in approximately 50% of cases {29300189}. Various molecular techniques can be used to detect BCOR rearrangements and BCOR ITD.
