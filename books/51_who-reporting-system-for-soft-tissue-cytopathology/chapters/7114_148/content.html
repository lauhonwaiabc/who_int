7.1.1.4 Risk of malignancy and diagnostic management recommendations

The category has a high positive predictive value for malignancy, with a reported ROM of 71–80% {20091838; 32926591}, and in one recent retrospective single-institution study of more than 2200 cases, the ROM was 68.42% based on using the six categories of the WHO System {39212584}. The authors of the study made the interesting observation that the ROM varies not only with categorization into the six categories but also with the FNAB pattern seen, from 3% for lipomatous tumours, which include one of the most common FNAB targets (superficial lipoma), to 62.78% for spindle cell, 78.15% for epithelioid, 84.58% for myxoid, 94.26% for pleomorphic, and 100% for round cell tumours. Further research around this finding will be worthwhile to guide cytopathologists and clinicians.

Clinical and imaging findings should always be correlated with the cytopathological report and the results of any ancillary testing available, ideally in a multidisciplinary meeting. If there are discrepancies, repeat FNAB should be recommended to confirm the malignancy, preferably with ROSE to effectively triage material for cell block, ICC, FISH, or NGS, along with CNB whenever available. In some situations, incisional biopsy with or without frozen section or a surgical excision biopsy may be required.
