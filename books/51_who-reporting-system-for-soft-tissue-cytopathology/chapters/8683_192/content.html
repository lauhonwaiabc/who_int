8.6.8.3 Alveolar soft part sarcoma

Definition

Alveolar soft part sarcoma (ASPS) is a rare neoplasm of uncertain histogenesis predominantly affecting the deep soft tissues of the extremities, featuring variably discohesive epithelioid cells arranged in nests resulting in a distinct alveolar appearance, with a characteristic translocation, der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25) that results in ASPSCR1::TFE3 gene fusion.

Clinical features and imaging

ASPS presents as a slowly growing, painless soft tissue mass {24605193}. Vaginal bleeding is common in tumours involving the female genital tract. Proptosis is a presenting condition in cases of tumours involving the orbit. The disease can be localized or metastatic. A significant percentage of patients present with or develop metastatic lesions, frequently in the lungs {26804150; 22173238}.

On imaging, ASPS appears as a lobulated mass with flow voids, conspicuous peripheral blood vessels, and internal nodularity {25415714}. Hypervascularity is seen on postcontrast-enhanced CT {2750619}.

Histopathology

ASPS is composed of large, polygonal tumour cells with well-defined cell borders, central to eccentric nuclei, and prominent nucleoli, arranged in an organoid and a uniform nesting pattern with loosely cohesive cells, imparting an alveolar growth pattern. The tumour nests are surrounded by capillary vessels, leading to a branching haemangiopericytoma-like vasculature. Variable features include cytoplasmic vacuolization, rhabdoid-like cells, multinucleation, myxoid stroma, cystic change, pleomorphic cells, xanthomatous cells, mitotic figures, and dystrophic calcification {17071801; 22173238; 15735860}.

The tumour cells contain DPAS-positive rhomboid or rod-shaped intracytoplasmic crystals and granules that may be focally or diffusely present {22173238}. These are immunopositive for MCT1 and CD147 {11943706}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

High cellularity Cohesive tissue fragments, some of which appear alveolar, and scattered cells with stripped nuclei in the clean background Polygonal cells with central to eccentric round nuclei, perinucleolar chromatin clearing (“vesicular”, Pap), and prominent nucleoli Abundant, well-defined to ill-defined, granular to finely vacuolated, concentric or eccentric cytoplasm Variable papilliform tissue fragments, binucleate to multinucleate giant cells, intracytoplasmic rod-like crystals, and a thin fibrillary stroma

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnoses include renal cell carcinoma, granular cell tumour, clear cell sarcoma, epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, epithelioid sarcoma, perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa), alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, and paraganglioma {1879268; 19787801; 22173238; 26693959; 32853443}.

Renal cell carcinoma displays cytomorphological features overlapping with those of ASPS, and the finding of normal kidneys on imaging is crucial in confirming a diagnosis of ASPS over metastatic renal cell carcinoma. Granular cell tumours, unless malignant, generally lack pleomorphic nuclei and prominent nucleoli, in contrast to ASPS. Similar to ASPS, epithelioid sarcoma and clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue display epithelioid or polygonal cells with moderate to abundant cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli; the prominent nucleoli are more consistently seen in clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue, which also includes some spindle cells. Plentiful intracytoplasmic granules and crystals are rarely seen within these tumours, unlike in ASPS. Epithelioid haemangioendotheliomas also have polygonal cells, but they have grooved nuclei and intracytoplasmic lumina containing red blood cells. Alveolar rhabdomyosarcomas contain epithelioid or polygonal cells with binucleate and multinucleate forms, including giant cells with a wreath-like circular arrangement of nuclei. Paragangliomas also display features overlapping with those of ASPS, including nuclear anisokaryosis with abundant granular cytoplasm and intracytoplasmic inclusions. These tumours are suspected in sites such as the abdomen and neck.

Ancillary testing

ASPS show S100 negativity, which is useful in ruling out a granular cell tumour. HMB45 and melan-A negativity rule out melanoma, clear cell sarcoma of soft parts, and PEComa {32356379; 33162379}. Negative immunostaining for desmin, myogenin, and MYOD1 is useful in ruling out a rhabdomyosarcoma. Synaptophysin and chromogranin negativity rules out a paraganglioma. TFE3 constitutes a highly sensitive marker for a diagnosis of ASPS; however, it is also positive in translocation-related renal cell carcinomas, neuroendocrine tumours, and granular cell tumours {22173238}. PAX8 is positively expressed in most renal cell carcinomas, unlike in ASPS {25315900}. ASPS cells express TFE3 and cathepsin K {18382356; 18176180; 21602817}, but despite high sensitivity, these markers are not entirely specific, because they are also expressed in certain renal cell carcinomas {12766578; 22173238}.

ASPS has a characteristic ASPSCR1::TFE3 gene fusion, as a result of a recurrent, non-reciprocal translocation, der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25). TFE3 gene rearrangement can be assessed by FISH, or ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion by RT-PCR or NGS. These are also found in certain renal cell carcinomas {11438465; 23828314}.
