8.2.1.2 Dedifferentiated liposarcoma

Definition

Dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS) is an atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma (ALT/WDLPS) showing progression, either in the primary or in a recurrence, to a usually non-lipogenic sarcoma of variable histopathological grade, in most cases characterized by amplification of MDM2 and CDK4.

Clinical features and imaging

DDLPS occurs most commonly in middle-aged to elderly adults, without sex predilection. Patients frequently present with a large painless mass, most commonly in the retroperitoneum and abdominal cavity. Other sites include the spermatic cord, deep soft tissue of proximal extremities, trunk, and head and neck region. MRI shows coexisting lipogenic and non-lipogenic components, and the latter should be targeted by FNAB {34362013; 27209201}.

Histopathology

The classic histopathological feature is transition, abrupt or gradual, from ALT/WDLPS to non-lipogenic sarcoma with a wide range of morphological patterns, but the well-differentiated lipogenic component may be absent {9060596; 30857767}. Common patterns include cellular pleomorphic, myxofibrosarcoma-like, fascicular spindle cell, fibromatosis-like, and a storiform meningothelial-like pattern. Other less common patterns include inflammatory cell–rich or inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour–like, a predominantly epithelioid morphology, and even a nested pattern resembling paraganglioma. Many tumours display mixed patterns {30709444; 30852045; 9839165; 21317707; 15214821}. Heterologous elements showing skeletal muscle, bone, cartilage, and vascular or smooth muscle differentiation can occur {17895758; 16012847; 33154322}. Some cases exhibit homologous lipoblastic differentiation, resembling pleomorphic liposarcoma {20588177}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Various spindle and pleomorphic sarcomas constitute a very broad range of differential diagnoses, depending on the cytomorphological pattern and location. The most common mimics are undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, myxofibrosarcoma, and pleomorphic liposarcoma {19574885; 24227706; 24525499}, and ICC is usually required, with or without FISH and NGS.

Ancillary testing

MDM2 amplification, along with co-amplification of CDK4, is considered pathognomonic for ALT/WDLPS. Nuclear expression of MDM2 by ICC and/or MDM2 gene amplification detected by FISH or NGS is desirable in establishing the diagnosis of DDLPS {16160477; 22301498; 30857767; 24227706}. Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, myxofibrosarcoma, and pleomorphic liposarcoma lack MDM2 amplification in most cases. Given that MDM2 gene amplification is also a hallmark of intimal sarcoma (an aggressive sarcoma arising in the wall of large vessels), tumour location and the presence of a lipogenic component warrant significant consideration of DDLPS {34312479; 11213836}.

DDLPS with loss of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) expression is difficult to differentiate from a malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, especially because a small subset of these tumours exhibit MDM2 expression and/or MDM2 amplification {29309298}. DDLPS with heterologous differentiation may lead to erroneous diagnoses, such as extraskeletal osteosarcoma and spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma, and homologous differentiation can lead to misdiagnosis as pleomorphic liposarcoma {17895758; 16012847; 33154322}.
