8.1.1.3 Discussion and background

The “Malignant” category encompasses all cases in which the material is unequivocally malignant, characterized either by cellular smears with distinctly malignant cytomorphological features or by smears showing key cytopathological features diagnostic of a specific malignant tumour or tumour group. The “Malignant” category must be used in conjunction with clinical history, physical examination, and imaging findings. The report should almost always state that “correlation with clinical and imaging findings is required”.

This category includes both primary low- or high-grade sarcomas and secondary tumours occurring in the soft tissues. In addition to the “Malignant” diagnosis, distinguishing primary sarcoma from metastatic lesions of non-mesenchymal origin in the initial diagnostic process has significant impact on further clinical management. Clinical data and information about previous malignancies, together with the cytopathological features of the FNAB smears, are usually sufficient to render a correct diagnosis in many cases of secondary malignancies {32298511; 9800130; 19763717}. However, when a mass in soft tissue is the first manifestation of a carcinoma, melanoma, or lymphoma, ancillary tests are often necessary to determine the site of the primary malignant lesion and to assist with potential treatment targets.

Regarding primary soft tissue sarcomas, if a specific tumour diagnosis cannot be rendered, the case should at least be categorized as “Malignant”, and a differential diagnosis is required. In some cases, diagnosis of high-grade or low-grade sarcoma can provide a diagnostic level sufficient to plan surgical treatment and further management. However, for some types of sarcomas, optimal treatment includes neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy, which typically requires a specific histopathological type and grade of sarcoma. In such cases ancillary testing of FNAB material and CNB may be essential for the accurate determination of the precise histopathological diagnosis {31868988; 32303388; 32298511; 30156774}. Small round cell neoplasms are a typical example of this necessity; the categorization of small round cell neoplasms as “Malignant” based on the FNAB smears is usually straightforward, but these entities share many cytopathological features and can be difficult to specifically diagnose on the basis of cytomorphology alone {35417916; 34515032}. Because most small round cell sarcomas are characterized by specific patterns of protein expression or genetic changes, ancillary tests are essential to reach a specific histopathological diagnosis {32654990; 34515032; 35417916}. Similarly, myxoid soft tissue neoplasms often pose diagnostic challenges in FNAB examination due to the overlapping cytopathological features of many entities {37231794; 33041221}. Although the sensitivity and specificity for detecting malignancy in this group of neoplasms is high {37231794}, additional ICC or molecular tests can assist in establishing a definitive diagnosis for certain specific entities {33168472; 35878421}.
