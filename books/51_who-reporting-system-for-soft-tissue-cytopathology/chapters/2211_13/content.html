2.2.1.1 Introduction: The role of ancillary testing

Soft tissue tumours are a group of more than 100 mesenchymal entities characterized by a wide range of microscopic patterns, including spindle, myxoid, epithelioid, and pleomorphic, and they often show pronounced intratumoral heterogeneity. The research findings of recent decades have deepened our understanding of the pathogenesis of both benign and malignant soft tissue tumours, and they have revealed a plethora of etiologically important molecular aberrations [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]]. Some of these aberrations are highly specific to particular entities, such as the STAT6::NAB2 translocation in solitary fibrous tumour and rearrangement of the SS18 gene in synovial sarcoma, whereas others occur in more than one clinically and histopathologically well-defined entity, for example EWSR1::ATF1 in atypical fibrous histiocytoma and clear cell sarcoma, or the ALK gene rearrangement in inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, anaplastic lymphoma, and some lung adenocarcinomas. Reliable surrogate ICC markers have been developed for many of the genetic aberrations, facilitating diagnosis and based on high-quality cytopathological samples {30709446; 30156774; 30190145}.

FNAB of soft tissue masses is a minimally invasive diagnostic method, but it has the inherent limitation of small sample size, both as measured in absolute values and in relation to the volume of the studied mass. Sensitive and specific ancillary methods allow the detection of specific diagnostic tumour properties from this limited material {26780171; 30709446; 30156774; 30190145; 30600320}. Not only can ICC characterize the line of differentiation, such as by the detection of epithelial, myogenic, neural, or endothelial antigens, but it can also demonstrate, with high accuracy, the changes caused at the protein level by specific genetic aberrations, for example STAT6 rearrangement, KIT mutation, MDM2 amplification, or SMARCB1 (INI1) inactivation {33608696; 30709446; 30156774; 30190145}. The precise detection of specific mutations either to confirm the ICC findings or to detect mutations that lack an ICC surrogate can be performed with more laborious, time-consuming, and costly molecular methods, i.e. cytogenetic analysis, FISH, PCR, or NGS {16557276; 32828707; 30417957}. ICC and FISH have the advantage of short turnaround times, and NGS provides the advantage of comprehensiveness and, with various platforms, a comparable turnaround time.

All methods applicable to histopathological samples of soft tissue tumours are also applicable to cytopathological material, with the additional advantage of smears for FISH analysis lacking truncation artefact, i.e. whole cells and nuclei are present on the cytopathological slides rather than only the sectioned incomplete cell on the slide of a paraffin-embedded section.

The prerequisite for the application of any ancillary test to FNAB material is that there be sufficient material, quantitatively as well as qualitatively, and that clinical and imaging information be available {25487166; 26780171}. The triaging of the FNAB for the application of ancillary methods in the context of a soft tissue tumour should be planned before and during the collection of the FNAB, based on the differential diagnosis and ROSE. Direct smears and cell block are sometimes underestimated in their value in the diagnostic workup of soft tissue masses as a source of material {26780171; 32303388; 24045541}.
