4.5.3.3 Intramuscular myxoma and juxta-articular myxoma

Definition

Intramuscular myxoma is a benign hypocellular soft tissue neoplasm composed of bland spindle cells embedded in a usually abundant myxoid and hypovascular stroma. Juxta-articular myxoma typically arises in the vicinity of a large joint and lacks GNAS mutation.

Clinical features and imaging

Patients usually present with a painless, slowly growing soft tissue mass involving the muscles of the thigh, shoulder, buttocks, and upper arm, typically in adults aged 40–70 years, with a female predominance {14253111; 3524794}. On MRI, the tumours are well circumscribed with slightly lobulated edges, and they are homogeneously hypointense on T1-weighted images and extremely hyperintense on T2-weighted images {25615761; 25019435}. Most intramuscular myxomas are sporadic, but a minority present as multiple lesions and are associated with fibrous dysplasia of bone, a condition called Mazabraud syndrome {4709710; 30653046}.

Histopathology

Intramuscular myxoma is composed of uniform, bland spindle to stellate cells separated by abundant extracellular, hypovascular myxoid stroma. At the periphery, infiltration of tumour cells into surrounding skeletal muscle fibres is often present, which should not be mistaken for a sign of malignancy. Areas may show increased cellularity and slightly conspicuous capillary to occasional thick-walled, poorly formed short “stubby” vessels; if this pattern is predominant, the diagnosis of “cellular myxoma” may be rendered. Cytopathological atypia such as nuclear pleomorphism and hyperchromasia, mitoses, and necrosis are absent {4036856; 9777984; 11532040}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of intramuscular myxoma is broad and includes any soft tissue tumour that may show hypocellular myxoid material and low-grade spindle cells, including ganglion cyst, juxta-articular myxoma, nodular fasciitis, soft tissue perineurioma with prominent myxoid stroma, low-grade myxofibrosarcoma, low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, and myxoid liposarcoma with pulmonary oedema-like areas {27159449; 10459049; 33168472}.

Ganglion cyst is a degenerative soft tissue lesion that commonly occurs in the hands and wrists as a result of trauma. It contains only abundant granular myxoid material and occasional histiocytes, and it lacks the spindle, stellate, myxoma cells {8989538; 12115304}. Juxta-articular myxoma is a benign myxoid lesion arising adjacent to large joints. Although morphologically resembling intramuscular or cellular myxoma, juxta-articular myxoma appears to occur commonly in men and lacks GNAS mutations {11942570}. The distinction between soft tissue perineurioma with its slenderer perineural cells and intramuscular/cellular myxoma is difficult and relies on ICC. Both myxofibrosarcoma and myxoid liposarcoma have a more complex vascular component, and low-grade myxofibrosarcoma and low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma have more notable nuclear atypia.

Ancillary testing

ICC is not helpful to establish the diagnosis of intramuscular myxoma, but it is essential to exclude its mimics. Tumour cells are consistently negative for MUC4 and may be positive for CD34, EMA, and rarely SMA {27159449; 33168472}. Soft tissue perineurioma expresses EMA and claudin-1, allowing distinction from intramuscular myxomas. Many intramuscular myxomas harbour activating mutations in codon 201 of GNAS, which can be detected by NGS and can be diagnostically helpful in distinguishing cellular myxoma from low-grade myxofibrosarcoma {19287459; 19320777; 30111377}.
