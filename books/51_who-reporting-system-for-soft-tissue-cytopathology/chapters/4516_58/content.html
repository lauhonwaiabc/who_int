4.5.1.6 Myofibroblastoma

Definition

Myofibroblastoma (MFB) is a benign spindle cell neoplasm occurring in the breast and extramammary sites, composed of myofibroblastic cells, bands of hyalinized collagen, and usually an adipocytic component.

Clinical features and imaging

The neoplasm is classified as mammary MFB when in the breast, as extramammary MFB when subcutaneous, as intranodal or palisaded MFB when arising in a lymph node, and as superficial MFB when involving external gynaecological sites {3037930; 26523539; 11474286; 34196064; 17284119}.

Mammary MFB was originally thought to be more frequent in the male breast, but subsequent studies demonstrated an equal sex distribution, with cases occurring especially frequently in older men and postmenopausal women {3037930; 26523539}. Intranodal or palisaded MFBs usually occur in middle-aged men in their fifth or sixth decade of life, but they have a wide age range {17284119; 2712186; 2712187}. Mammary MFB generally presents as a well-circumscribed soft tissue mass, ranging from 20 mm to 130 mm, although lesions in the breast are usually 40 mm or less and extramammary sites usually 60 mm or less. Intranodal MFBs also present as a solitary circumscribed nodule up to 50 mm in diameter. The inguinal lymph nodes are most commonly involved, but a few cases have been reported in submandibular lymph nodes and rarely in other sites {17284119; 2712187}. Ultrasound, mammography, and MRI of the breast reveal a well-circumscribed homogeneous mass that can be confused with a fibroadenoma. Other sites, again, demonstrate a round nodule.

Histopathology

Mammary and subcutaneous MFB consists of a spindle cell proliferation arranged in irregular fascicles {3037930; 26523539; 11474286}. The spindle cells are elongated and bland and lack any significant atypia. The spindle cells usually infiltrate bands of fibrous tissue, and focal myxoid change can be present. Admixed adipose tissue can be seen, which can cause the lesion to be confused with a spindle cell lipoma. The spindle cells have elongated nuclei and scant pale to slightly eosinophilic cytoplasm, which can resemble smooth muscle cells. Mitotic activity is low, averaging less than one mitotic figure per mm2, and atypical mitotic figures are not seen. Mammary MFB can occasionally demonstrate a spectrum of morphological appearances, with cellular, infiltrative, epithelioid, decidua-like, collagenized/fibrous, lipomatous, and myxoid subtypes {3037930}. Rarely, MFB of the breast can have bizarre mononucleated and binucleated cells that can be a potential pitfall for a malignant diagnosis, especially in CNB or FNAB specimens. The term “bizarre cell myofibroblastoma of the breast” has been proposed for this variant {34196064}.

Intranodal palisaded MFB consists of a cellular spindle cell proliferation that generally replaces the lymph node, although a thin peripheral rim of lymphoid tissue can be present {17284119; 2712186; 2712187}. The spindle cells are arranged in short intersecting or criss-crossing fascicles and can demonstrate subtle nuclear palisading imparting a Schwannian appearance, which can potentially lead to misdiagnosis as a schwannoma. The spindle cells are uniform, with elongated round to oval to slightly pointy-ended nuclei and surrounding pale eosinophilic cytoplasm. There is minimal cytopathological atypia, and mitotic figures are rare. The characteristic feature of intranodal palisaded MFB is the so-called amianthoid fibre, which is a stellate to globular eosinophilic extracellular band of collagen that can calcify. Admixed blood vessels, lymphocytes, red blood cells, and haemosiderin deposits can also be seen {2712187}. Slit-like extracellular spaces containing erythrocytes can be present, simulating a Kaposi sarcoma–like appearance.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of MFB is broad and encompasses a variety of reactive and neoplastic spindle cell lesions. For mammary MFB, the differential diagnosis includes solitary fibrous tumour.

Fibromatosis has long fascicles of spindle cells, in contrast to the indistinct short spindle cells of MFB, and fibromatosis is negative for desmin and positive for β-catenin, whereas MFB is positive for desmin and negative for β-catenin. Other entities to be considered in the differential diagnosis include spindle cell lipoma, which has ropy collagen in contrast to the usually diffuse collagen seen in mammary MFB, as well as spindle cell liposarcoma and low-grade malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, where the clinical features, cytomorphological features lacking atypia, and ICC make the diagnosis. A low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma can also enter into the differential diagnosis, but this neoplasm has cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and moderate to severe nuclear atypia.

The differential diagnosis of intranodal palisaded MFB again includes a number of spindle cell lesions, including metastatic spindle cell carcinoma and melanoma. The metastatic malignancies demonstrate a greater degree of cellularity and nuclear atypia, and ICC assists. Benign metastasizing leiomyoma has cells with more oval and eosinophilic cytoplasm, although they do share staining for smooth muscle markers with intranodal MFB.

Ancillary testing

Mammary MFBs express CD34 and desmin, with loss of nuclear RB1 due to deletion or rearrangement of 13q14. SMA and EMA are positive in fewer than half of cases. Both solitary fibrous tumour and MFB can be CD34-positive, but mammary MFB is also desmin-positive, whereas solitary fibrous tumour is negative for desmin and positive for STAT6. In addition, MFB demonstrates loss of RB1, which is positive in solitary fibrous tumour.

Intranodal palisaded MFBs are positive for SMA and MSA. Nuclear β-catenin and cyclin D1 positivity can also be seen, but unlike in mammary MFB, desmin is infrequently positive, and CD34 is negative. Spindle cell carcinomas are cytokeratin-positive, and melanomas are positive for melan-A (MART1), SOX10, and S100. Intranodal schwannomas are S100-positive, and dendritic cell sarcomas stain for dendritic markers such as CD21 and CD35.
