8.6.9.3 CIC-rearranged sarcoma

Definition

CIC-rearranged sarcoma is a high-grade round cell undifferentiated sarcoma defined by CIC-related gene fusions, most often CIC::DUX4.

Clinical features and imaging

CIC-rearranged sarcomas present as mass lesions and have been described in most sites, including the soft tissue of the extremities, head and neck, and retroperitoneum (80–85% of cases), visceral organs (10–15%), and rarely bone (< 5%). There is a wide age range, with a median patient age of 25–35 years, and a slight male predominance {26685084; 27079694; 28346326}. Imaging shows aggressive mass lesions but nonspecific imaging characteristics. By CT, tumours show isodense to hypodense attenuation, and they are metabolically active by FDG PET. MRI shows mass contrast enhancement, necrosis, perilesional oedema, and fluid-fluid level {32840647}.

Histopathology

Tumours are composed of sheets of uniform undifferentiated small round cells with areas of lobular growth separated by fibrous stroma. Variant morphologies, such as epithelioid, rhabdoid, and spindle, can be seen in 10–20% of cases. Necrosis is common, and a subset of cases show myxoid stroma {26685084; 27079694; 28346326}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of CIC-rearranged sarcoma includes Ewing sarcoma, BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma, alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, poorly differentiated synovial sarcoma, and desmoplastic round cell tumour, as well as carcinomas and lymphomas. The cells in Ewing sarcoma are usually singly dispersed or in small tissue fragments, and they are uniform with scant cytoplasm, round nuclei, fine pale chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli {26800124}. Giemsa smears may have a striped or “tigroid” background. BCOR::CCNB3 sarcomas tend to be more pleomorphic and may have spindle cells and myxoid stroma {29300189; 31679010; 34265123}. Alveolar rhabdomyosarcomas have atypical, hyperchromatic nuclei with coarse chromatin and prominent nucleoli; large, multinucleated wreath-like cells with abundant cytoplasm may also be present {26800124}. Poorly differentiated round cell synovial sarcomas have round to short spindle cells with hyperchromatic nuclei with granular chromatin, and nuclear moulding may be present {10888758; 26800124}. Desmoplastic round cell tumour is characterized by granular chromatin and nuclear moulding, and pseudorosettes and fragments of metachromatic stroma may be present {26800124}. Given the cytomorphological overlap between these tumours, definitive diagnosis requires ICC and molecular testing {23834194; 26800124; 28718513; 33399252}.

Ancillary testing

CIC-rearranged sarcomas show strong, diffuse nuclear staining for ETV4 and WT1 {24723486; 26685084; 27562494}. Most show patchy, variable staining for CD99, although diffuse membranous staining is present in some tumours {28346326}. They are usually negative for NKX2-2, S100, cytokeratin, EMA, and desmin, although rare tumours are focally positive for one or more of these markers {24723486; 26685084; 27562494}. SMARCB1 (INI1) is retained. Nuclear staining for NUT is present in tumours with CIC::NUTM1 rearrangements {30407212}. CIC rearrangements can be demonstrated by FISH and other molecular genetic techniques {22072439; 28233365; 28493604}.
