4.5.1.5 Neurofibroma

Definition

Neurofibroma is a benign peripheral nerve sheath tumour consisting of differentiated Schwann cells, perineurial/perineurial-like cells, fibroblasts, mast cells, and residual interspersed myelinated and non-myelinated axons embedded in a myxoid and collagenous extracellular matrix.

Clinical features and imaging

Sporadic neurofibromas present as superficial tumours of skin and subcutaneous tissue associated with small superficial nerves or less commonly involving medium to major nerve trunks as deep-seated neoplasms. Cutaneous neurofibromas are typically asymptomatic, mobile, and without specific anatomical distribution. Larger or deep-seated tumours present with neuropathic changes including motor and/or sensory deficits in the nerve distribution. Patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) present with multiple cutaneous and deep-seated neurofibromas that often involve paraspinal nerve trunks and are large and plexiform. Patients with NF1 also present with other cutaneous findings, including café-au-lait spots and axillary and inguinal freckling, as well as Lisch nodules (pigmented nodules of the iris), optic gliomas, and dysplastic lesions of bone such as sphenoid dysplasia and cortical thinning. Sporadic neurofibromas present after the second decade of life and without sex predilection. Diffuse and plexiform neurofibromatosis in patients with NF1 may present congenitally.

The imaging features of neurofibroma are dependent on the morphological subtype. Nodular neurofibroma shows superficial tumours that may be multiple in patients with NF1 {3101136; 10489179; 10525761; 12540444}. Diffuse-type neurofibromas show infiltration of subcutaneous tissues on cross-sectional imaging. Plexiform neurofibromas have characteristic long, multinodular enlargement of large nerves and nerve roots, especially the lumbosacral and brachial plexus. They are often bilateral and involve multiple levels. They may cause bone remodelling of the vertebrae when arising in nerve roots. They are isointense to hyperintense on T2-weighted MRI. The target sign of peripheral hyperintensity and central hypointensity/isointensity is suggestive of a peripheral nerve sheath tumour.

Histopathology

Neurofibromas can show nodular, diffuse, or plexiform growth patterns {7528474}. Nodular neurofibromas show a well-circumscribed but unencapsulated tumour with uniform distribution of bland-appearing spindle cells, with a combination of Schwann cells and fibroblasts in a background of myxoid to fibrotic stroma. The collagen shows wiry strands, imparting a fragmented or “shredded carrot” appearance. There is often an intermixed population of mast cells. By ICC, neurofibromas contain a mixture of perineurial/perineurial-like cells and small axons.

Diffuse-type neurofibromas have similar histomorphology but lack circumscription, are often large, and infiltrate into the surrounding dermis and subcutaneous tissue. Plexiform neurofibromas are typically large and multinodular, with each nodule surrounded by perineurium. They often involve larger nerves or a nerve plexus and have a “bag of worms” or “ropy” gross appearance.

Alternative morphology is rarely seen in neurofibromas and includes pseudomeissnerian corpuscles, melanin deposition, sclerosis, metaplastic bone, and rarely epithelioid change.

Cytopathological atypia may be seen, such as degenerative atypia showing nuclear enlargement and hyperchromasia. Atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential is a histopathological diagnosis that requires the presence of at least two of the following features: cytopathological atypia, hypercellularity, loss of classic neurofibroma architecture, and mitotic activity.

Low cellularity Small tissue fragments of cohesive spindle cells with curved, comma, bent, or wavy nuclei May have occasional stripped nuclei Myxoid or fibromyxoid collagenous matrix

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The cytopathological differential diagnosis of neurofibroma includes schwannoma, spindle cell lipoma, solitary fibrous tumour, intramuscular myxoma, and low-grade malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours. Schwannomas usually produce more cellular smears than do neurofibromas, but distinguishing schwannomas from neurofibromas requires clinical correlation because the spindle cells of these tumours may be indistinguishable, and the Schwann cells of each stain with S100 and SOX10. Neurofibromas may contain intermixed perineurial/perineurial-like cells; these may also be present at the periphery of schwannomas.

Spindle cell lipomas typically occur in the neck and upper back and show more-cellular smears composed of blunt spindle cells arranged in cohesive tissue fragments and as single cells with intermixed adipocytes.

Solitary fibrous tumours show well-circumscribed neoplasms with variably cellular smears, often with prominent single cells in a bloody background.

Intramuscular myxomas produce very hypocellular smears with prominent myxoid matrix, and any cellular component is negative for S100 and SOX10.

Low-grade variants of malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour are very difficult to distinguish from neurofibromas showing cytopathological atypia due to ancient change.

Ancillary testing

In neurofibromas, the Schwann cells are positive for S100 and SOX10, and perineurial/perineurial-like cells are positive for EMA, GLUT1, claudin-1, and CD34 (which also highlights intermixed fibroblasts). Axons can be seen with neurofilament ICC. CD34 is positive in both spindle cell lipomas and neurofibroma, as well as in solitary fibrous tumours, which are characteristically positive for nuclear STAT6.

Staining for S100 and SOX10 is often strong in neurofibromas, whereas expression is focal or absent in malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour. Loss of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) by ICC is extremely helpful in the interpretation of malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour but occurs only rarely in low-grade variants.

Molecular testing is only rarely used. NGS to identify underlying mutations in NF1 may be performed, but the large number of exons, presence of several pseudogenes, and lack of mutation hotspots with many pathogenic mutations and deletions pose challenges {25074460}.
