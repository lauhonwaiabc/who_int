8.6.5.2 Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS) is a malignant neoplasm composed of a monomorphic population of primitive round cells showing skeletal muscle differentiation. The presence of either a PAX3::FOXO1 or a PAX7::FOXO1 fusion gene is detected in the majority of cases.

Clinical features and imaging

ARMS occurs across all ages, with a peak incidence in people aged 10–25 years; it arises most commonly in the deep soft tissue of extremities, followed by head and neck, paraspinal, and perineal regions, and rarely in the breast {6620082; 11835233; 26756883; 31032134}. Patients present with a rapidly growing malignant soft tissue mass. Nearly 30% of patients present with locoregional or distant metastasis {18467730; 22378628; 25989287}. Tumours occurring in the head and neck region involving the meninges lead to cranial nerve defects {21873599}; those in the paraspinal region can cause compression of the spinal nerves {22948994}, and those occurring in the pelvis result in symptoms related to bowel obstruction {24045440}.

Histopathology

ARMS is a cellular tumour composed of primitive small round cells, arranged in nests, with intervening fibrovascular septa. Loss of cohesion of the cells in the centre creates an alveolar growth pattern. Some cases show a compact arrangement of tumour cells leading to a solid-type growth pattern with pseudocystic spaces and interspersed mitotic figures. Individual tumour cells contain scant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic nuclei; there is a variable number of cells with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation, and wreath-like multinucleated giant cells may be present. Rarely, cells with vacuolated cytoplasm due to intracytoplasmic glycogen are noted {11353062; 25989287; 26756883; 28521080; 24113309}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

High cellularity Single scattered intact cells and loosely cohesive tissue fragments Monomorphic round cells, with scant to moderate, blue to finely vacuolated cytoplasm (Giemsa) and hyperchromatic nuclei Variable numbers of rhabdomyoblastic cells may be present, showing large round to oval nuclei and moderate to abundant blue cytoplasm (Giemsa) or greenish cytoplasm (Pap) Binucleate and multinucleated forms, including with wreath-like circular arrangement of nuclei, are frequently seen Scattered mitotic figures and bare nuclei are often present

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Various round cell tumours constitute the differential diagnoses. Ewing sarcomas show a dual population of poorly differentiated round cells, including small, dark cells and large, sometimes finely vacuolated cells. Rhabdoid tumours show features overlapping those of ARMS, with individual tumour cells displaying characteristic perinuclear intracytoplasmic globular inclusions. Poorly differentiated synovial sarcomas are composed of round to ovoid cells with tapering cytoplasm, seen individually and in tissue fragments. Rarely, anaplastic large cell lymphoma can be misdiagnosed as ARMS, and ICC greatly assists. Differentiating ARMS from embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS) can be difficult on smears, but ARMS is characterized by relative hypercellularity and monomorphic small round cells in discohesive tissue fragments, with a variable number of multinucleated giant cells. On the other hand, smears of ERMS display large tissue fragments with eosinophilic material and scattered cells, including round, tadpole, and ribbon forms {9861531; 10934943; 17636492; 17973999}.

Ancillary testing

ARMS is positive for desmin, myogenin, and MYOD1, indicative of skeletal muscle differentiation. Myogenin expression is typically diffuse and strong in ARMS, unlike in ERMS {10666368; 15536621; 30098515}. Certain cases of ARMS show aberrant immunoexpression of epithelial and neuroendocrine markers {18487991; 30098515}.

Ewing sarcomas are positive for CD99 (MIC2) (membranous) and NKX2-2, and negative for desmin, myogenin, and MYOD1. Ewing sarcoma is characterized by t(11;22)(q24;q12) and t(21;22)(q22;q12) translocations in most cases, unlike ARMS {17636492; 17973999; 26847175}. Rhabdoid tumours display loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) {15489652}. Poorly differentiated synovial sarcomas invariably display characteristic t(X;18)(p11.2;q11.2) translocation {12203876}. Desmoplastic small round cell tumour and lymphoblastic lymphoma can be differentiated with specific immunostains. CD45 (LCA), CD30, and ALK are useful in separating ARMS from anaplastic large cell lymphoma {20306994}.

ARMS is characterized by t(2;13)(q35;q14) and t(1;13)(p36;q14) chromosomal translocations, leading to PAX3::FOXO1 and PAX7::FOXO1 fusions, respectively, in 50–88.8% of cases {7530783; 9164192}. The translocation t(2;13) is relatively more common than t(1;13) translocation and has been reported to have a poorer prognosis in some studies {12039929}. However, in a subsequent study, the PAX::FKHR fusion type was not found to be a significant predictor for survival {21254373}. FISH can be used for the detection of FOXO1, using a break-apart probe {19704258}. RNA sequencing can be used to determine the gene fusions.
