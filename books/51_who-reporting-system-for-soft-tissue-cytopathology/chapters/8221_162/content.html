8.2.2.1 Myxoid liposarcoma

Definition

Myxoid liposarcoma (MLPS) is a malignant tumour composed of uniform round to ovoid cells with a variable number of small lipoblasts set in a myxoid stroma with a branching capillary vasculature, with a pathognomonic translocation of FUS::DDIT3 or EWSR1::DDIT3 fusion.

Clinical features and imaging

MLPS occurs in a wide age range of patients, but young to middle-aged adults are most commonly affected [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]]. Rare cases of MLPS have been documented in children and adolescents {19194281}. MLPS is typically located in the extremities and rarely in the trunk or chest wall, and the deep soft tissue compartment of the thigh is the single most common site {8608528}. Affected individuals often present with a history of a painless lump or mass of variable duration.

The prognosis of MLPS is dependent on a number of factors, including patient age, tumour location, adequacy of tumour resection, margin status, and degree of tumour differentiation {31721459; 29977616; 25583234}. MLPS differentiation is determined by the “round cell” component within a tumour, with > 5% round cell regions portending a poorer prognosis {8554106; 8608528; 11751490; 17510918; 22052112}. Local recurrence or metastases occurs in as many as 60% of cases {17510918; 22190864}. MLPS has a tendency to metastasize to unusual sites, including other soft tissue locations, which may account for its occasional presentation as multifocal disease {10914725}.

MLPS has a variable appearance on imaging. It is usually lobulated with discrete borders, and necrosis within the tumour results in a heterogeneous appearance. Like other tumours with myxoid stroma, MLPS tends to show high signal intensity on T2-weighted MRI.

Histopathology

MLPS displays a spectrum, from a low-grade tumour with abundant myxoid material and low cellularity to a high-grade densely cellular tumour made up of small primitive round cells (previously known as “round cell liposarcoma”). MLPS is usually a multinodular lesion with central hypocellular regions and increased concentration of tumour cells near the periphery of individual lobules. The tumour is composed of abundant extracellular myxoid matrix material containing a variable number of branching capillary vessels arranged in a plexiform pattern. Individual tumour cells vary in shape, from spindle primitive cells to lipoblasts in various states of maturity. Nuclear atypia is usually minimal, and mitoses are seldom seen. Variations on classic morphology include chondroid metaplasia, stromal hyalinization, and foci of mature lipomatous areas {22261448}.

In some MLPSs, myxoid areas transition to a higher-grade component, with distinctive round cells with increased N:C ratio producing dense cellularity and less myxoid background, fewer vascular arcades, and fewer individual lipoblasts. If present, these higher-grade foci tend to concentrate at the periphery of the tumour lobules, but some MLPSs with a higher percentage of round cells may show dispersion throughout the entire tumour.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Low-grade myxoid liposarcoma
Variable cellularity Abundant extracellular myxoid stroma in tissue fragments Myxoid tissue fragments contain small anastomosing capillary vessels in a plexiform or arcade pattern Small, uniform spindle to ovoid cells with slightly irregular nuclear membranes and small prominent nucleoli Small and large lipoblasts with one or more eccentric clear cytoplasmic vacuoles and a displaced nucleus
High-grade (round cell) liposarcoma
High cellularity Dispersed population of small round cells with very high N:C ratio Inconspicuous matrix material and vascular tissue fragments Mild cellular and nuclear pleomorphism Irregular nuclear membranes, multiple prominent small nucleoli, and occasional intranuclear pseudoinclusions Rare lipoblasts

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of MLPS includes other entities with myxoid stroma, including myxoma, myxofibrosarcoma, extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma, myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma, and lipoblastoma {33168472; 10896330}.

Intramuscular and juxta-articular myxomas are hypocellular, have very rare small uniform spindle cells, and lack the vascular plexiform fragments that are the diagnostic feature of MLPS. Myxofibrosarcoma tends to be more cellular than MLPS and has obvious cellular pleomorphism, a feature not associated with MLPS. Myxofibrosarcoma can often have small vascular fragments identifiable, but the plexiform type is not seen. Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma shows substantial overlap with MLPS on cytopathology, with both having myxoid material and vascular fragments with associated tumour cells, but lipoblasts are not seen in extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma. Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma has a myxoid background, but it can be eliminated from the differential diagnosis because it is a neoplasm restricted to the distal extremities, a site that is very unlikely for MLPS. Finally, lipoblastoma is a lesion that occurs almost exclusively in very young children.

Ancillary testing

MLPS is characterized by a balanced translocation involving the DDIT3 gene on chromosome 12, with either FUS on chromosome 16 or less frequently EWSR1 on chromosome 22. Detection of a fusion transcript involving the DDIT3 gene locus is by FISH break-apart probe, which is a sensitive and specific test for MLPS {22089349}.

ICC is useful in distinguishing the round cell type of high-grade MLPS from other small round blue cell tumours, with positive DDIT3 in the nuclei serving as a sensitive and specific marker for both the low-grade and high-grade forms of MLPS {33731886; 32815829}.
