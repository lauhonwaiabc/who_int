6.1.1.3 Discussion and background

Prediction of the likelihood of a neoplasm giving rise to local recurrences or distant metastases is a principal goal in the pathological evaluation of soft tissue neoplasms. Cytopathological and histopathological evaluation can accurately separate benign from malignant neoplasms using morphological criteria and ancillary studies for the majority of tumours. However, it is well established that there are neoplasms that rarely metastasize, and the risk of metastasis is unpredictable on the basis of histopathological parameters; the fifth-edition WHO classification of soft tissue and bone tumours categorizes these as “intermediate (locally aggressive)” and “intermediate (rarely metastasizing)” tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]]. Specifically, in the latter category, these are locally aggressive tumours with frequent recurrences, and they show a well-documented ability to give rise to distant metastases, albeit rarely and unpredictably.

The categories “Atypical” and “STNUMP” address two fundamentally different diagnostic issues. The category “Atypical” is used when the cytopathologist cannot recognize a specific type of soft tissue neoplasm and so cannot definitively assign the specimen to the “Benign” or “Malignant” category, or when a specific line of differentiation is identified but there are not sufficient cytopathological features to warrant assignment to the “Suspicious for malignancy” category. The category “STNUMP” is used when the cytopathologist is able to recognize a specific neoplasm or line of differentiation, but either definitive criteria cannot be applied for risk stratification or assessment of malignancy, or the tumour type has an inherent but unpredictable risk of metastasis.

This chapter includes a broad range of soft tissue neoplasms that are commonly encountered in routine practice. Three tumours of intermediate biological risk are included: dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, a locally aggressive, frequently recurring fibroblastic neoplasm with a small risk of high-grade fibrosarcomatous transformation; inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, a locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing neoplasm; and angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma, another locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing neoplasm.

Myoepithelial neoplasms of soft tissue and perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa) are also included in this chapter, because the criteria for malignancy for these neoplasms are best assessed on histopathological features {12960802; 16327428; 24061520; 30001237}.

Solitary fibrous tumour and gastrointestinal stromal tumour are also included in this diagnostic category, because risk stratification schemes predicting their risk of malignant behaviour are best applied to CNB and excision specimens rather than FNAB material {20457867; 28731041}.
