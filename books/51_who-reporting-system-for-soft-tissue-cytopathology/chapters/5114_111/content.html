5.1.1.4 Risk of malignancy and diagnostic management recommendations

This category includes reactive, inflammatory, or reparative processes that lack the specific criteria that would allow a definitive benign diagnosis, as well as cases in which the sample lacks sufficient qualitative or quantitative features to differentiate between a non-neoplastic and a neoplastic lesion. In addition, atypical specimens may represent a benign lesion or normal tissue in which there has been suboptimal preparation resulting in a degree of artefactual degenerative change, but in which there are sufficient atypical morphological features such that a malignancy cannot be entirely excluded.

The ROM has been reported as 46% in one study of soft tissue and bone lesions {32926591}. A recent single-institution retrospective review of more than 2200 cases categorized using the WHO System reported a ROM of 39.62%, and it was observed that the ROM varied across all categories when the pattern was taken into account; that is, ROM varied from 3% for lipomatous tumours to 62.78% for spindle cell, 78.15% for epithelioid, 84.58% for myxoid, 94.26% for pleomorphic, and 100% for round cell tumours {39212584}.

Correlation with clinical findings, imaging, and any available ancillary tests on FNAB material are essential once a soft tissue FNAB is categorized as “Atypical”, and discussion by a multidisciplinary team is recommended. If these findings indicate that the process is highly likely to be a benign neoplasm or a reactive or inflammatory process, then observation and clinical follow-up may be considered, but in most cases, and in particular any cases where there is discrepancy in the triple approach, a repeat FNAB is recommended, ideally with ROSE and where available CNB. Incisional biopsy with frozen section or excisional biopsy may be considered if the imaging and clinical suspicion is high.
