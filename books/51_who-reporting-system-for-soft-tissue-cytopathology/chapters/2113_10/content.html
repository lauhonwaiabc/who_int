2.1.1.3 Rapid onsite evaluation

ROSE is a clinical service, offered by cytopathologists, supervised pathology trainees, or well-trained cytotechnologists, that provides immediate feedback regarding an FNAB or touch imprint of a CNB, with the goal of optimizing patient care. ROSE provides numerous benefits, including decreasing the number of passes required for an adequate sample, improving the yield for diagnostic accuracy, optimizing sample triage and allocation of material for ancillary tests, decreasing the likelihood of the need for additional repeat or more invasive diagnostic procedures, and minimizing the delay in diagnosis {21882357; 23429365;24844295}.

The rarity of soft tissue tumours and the cytomorphological overlap between lesions often creates challenges in their accurate diagnosis, as well as in the grading of sarcomas {17442232}. The accuracy of FNAB in distinguishing benign from malignant soft tissue tumours and sarcoma from other malignancies has been shown to be comparable to that of surgical biopsies, with up to 96% sensitivity and 98% specificity {21938143; 11335959; 20091838}. However, tumour heterogeneity, as well as the presence of haemorrhage and/or necrosis in large soft tissue tumours, may result in an insufficient or suboptimal yield. With small or deep-seated lesions, reactive cells surrounding the lesion can mimic neoplasia. The ROM in cases of non-diagnostic FNAB from bone and soft tissue lesions is reported to be as high as 42% in one study {32926591}.

These issues highlight the importance of ROSE to minimize the likelihood of an inadequate FNAB and to alert the proceduralist to the possible need for additional sampling. The principal rapid stains used for onsite evaluation include Giemsa, toluidine blue, ultrafast Pap, and rapid H&E. Method selection is often influenced by technical considerations, speed, and cost, as well as the operator’s preference {30585436; 31962321}. Triage with ROSE based on the direct smears also allows for allocation of sampled material for routine cytomorphological evaluation, liquid medium preparations, and cell block material for supplemental ancillary studies; for example, a small round cell pattern may warrant triage for molecular testing as well as additional tests including flow cytometry to exclude non-Hodgkin lymphoma {24045541; 14676638}. Touch preparations of CNB carry a risk of damaging fragile cellular material but offer benefits similar to those of ROSE. ICC is the most commonly used technique for the evaluation of tissue type of origin and differentiation, and it is applicable to FNAB material; LBC preparations; cell block or CNB sections; and molecular genetic assays, including cytogenetics, FISH, PCR, and NGS, which are increasingly being employed in soft tissue lesions. However, limited cellularity often requires the prioritization of the samples, and requests for more material at the FNAB procedure can be addressed by ROSE
