7.1.1.1 Introduction

The “Suspicious for malignancy” category is used when cytopathological features are present that suggest a malignant diagnosis but these features are insufficient in number or specificity to permit a definitive diagnosis. Atypical cells may show features suggestive of malignancy but do not allow a confident diagnosis of malignancy due to suboptimal preparation, smearing, preservation, or staining. Such specimens cannot be regarded as “Inadequate/Insufficient/Non-diagnostic”. Specimens in the “Suspicious for malignancy” category are generally from lesions that yield hypocellular smears or from neoplasms with a continuum of low-risk to high-risk features.

There is likely to be wide interobserver variability in this category. The diagnosis of malignancy is dependent in part on the cytopathologist’s experience and confidence, in part on the quality of the material for assessment, and in part on the nature of the lesion. The nature of the lesion is a particular problem in soft tissue neoplasms, which may show considerable intratumoural morphological heterogeneity, as well as overlap between lesions.
