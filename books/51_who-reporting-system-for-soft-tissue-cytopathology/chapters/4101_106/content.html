4.10.1 Sample reports: Benign

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format depends on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and ROSE are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as “Diagnostic summary” (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Specimen type:

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

44-year-old female. Soft 40 mm lump in right thigh, 1-year duration. MRI shows well-defined, encapsulated, expansile, homogeneously intense 44 mm lesion with thin septa in the adductor muscle.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Right mid-thigh adductor muscle

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Consistent with a lipoma

 

Material received:

Three air-dried Giemsa slides and one alcohol-fixed slide. 3 mL pink fluid with possible fatty flecks on its surface received, but there was no pellet on spinning down for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These mildly cellular smears show tissue fragments consisting of mature adipocytes and occasional single adipocytes in a lipoproteinaceous background, consistent with a lipoma.

Sample report 2

Specimen type:

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

45-year-old male. Superficial soft tissue 20 mm swelling inner aspect of right thigh. Nonspecific findings on ultrasound.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Inner aspect of lower right thigh

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Spindle cells and fat (see comment below)

 

Material received:

Two air-dried slides, one alcohol-fixed slide. No fluid for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These moderately cellular smears show tissue fragments of fat and spindle cells with bland elongated nuclei seen singly and in minute tissue fragments.

 

Comment: The features are benign but not diagnostic of a specific lesion, and the findings may not represent the targeted lesion. Correlation with clinical and imaging findings is recommended, and repeat FNAB should be considered.

 

(Editor’s note: The lesion has no specific clinical or ultrasound findings. Some cytopathologists may categorize the findings as “Non-diagnostic”; others, as illustrated here, categorize the findings as “Benign”, report what they see on the smears, and add the caveat that “the findings may not represent the targeted lesion”.)

Sample report 3

Specimen type:

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

35-year-old female. History of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and bone marrow transplant. Now enlarging superficial left paraspinal mass on back in T10 region growing from 7 mm to 13 mm in 3 months.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: FNAB of superficial lesion left T10

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Nodular fasciitis (see Supplementary reports 1 and 2 below)

 

Material received:

Four air-dried Giemsa-stained smears from ROSE and two alcohol-fixed smears. 2 mL of bloodstained fluid prepared for cell block (rather than for original request for flow cytometry).

 

Microscopic description:

These moderately cellular smears show scattered myxoid tissue fragments containing mildly pleomorphic spindle cells and occasional larger cells with a low to moderate N:C ratio and oval large nuclei with prominent single nucleoli. Similar single spindle and larger cells are present in the background, with occasional lymphocytes. The cell block shows similar material.

 

Comment: The features are consistent with nodular fasciitis. Confirmatory immunocytochemistry and molecular testing for USP6 gene rearrangements have been ordered.

 

Supplementary report 1:

On cell block, the tumour is diffusely positive for SMA, and H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) expression is retained. Desmin, caldesmon, S100P, SOX10, CD34, MDM2, ALK, and β-catenin are negative.

 

Supplementary report 2:

RNA sequencing–based fusion profiling demonstrated the MYH9::USP6 fusion, confirming the diagnosis of nodular fasciitis.

 

(Editor’s note: In this case, the initial categorization as “Benign” and diagnosis as “consistent with nodular fasciitis” have been issued with a note that ancillary tests are underway. The ICC findings and then the molecular results have then been issued as two supplementary reports in one integrated report, and the final diagnosis has been modified to reflect the additional ancillary test results.)

Sample report 4

Specimen type:

EUS-guided FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

70-year-old female. History of skin cancers and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia, now found to have an 18 × 12 mm bilobed hypoechoic submucosal lesion in the gastric cardia.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: EUS-guided FNAB gastric cardia

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Leiomyoma (see comment below)

 

Material received:

Six air-dried Giemsa smears from ROSE, three alcohol-fixed slides for Pap staining. 4 mL bloodstained fluid spun down for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show large tissue fragments made up of bland spindle cells with a fascicular architecture and elongated ovoid nuclei, in a bloody background. No mitoses are seen. There is no necrosis. The cell block sections show similar bland spindle cells with elongated nuclei and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm in fascicles with small vessels. Immunocytochemistry shows the tumour cells to be diffusely positive for desmin, SMA, and h-caldesmon. S100 is negative. Both DOG1 and KIT (CD117) highlight occasional admixed interstitial cells of Cajal.

 

Comment: Overall, the findings best support a diagnosis of leiomyoma with an increased number of interstitial cells of Cajal. The presence of increased interstitial cells of Cajal, which are positive for KIT (CD117) and DOG1 by immunocytochemistry, in a leiomyoma may be a result of entrapment and/or hyperplasia and is a finding recognized as a mimic of gastrointestinal stromal tumour (see reference below). Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.

 

Reference: Deshpande A, Nelson D, Corless CL, et al. Leiomyoma of the gastrointestinal tract with interstitial cells of Cajal: a mimic of gastrointestinal stromal tumour. Am J Surg Pathol. 2014 Jan;38(1):72–7 {24145645}

 

(Editor’s note: In this case, the cytopathologist has not issued a cytopathological report but rather waited until the ICC was available to make the specific diagnosis and issue the report as a final report.)

Sample report 5

Specimen type:

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

25-year-old male. Slow-growing superficial lump upper arm. MRI shows well-defined, lobulated lesion right forearm with hyperintensity on T2-weighted images. Note: The FNAB caused intense pain, and only one pass was performed.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Upper right arm

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Schwannoma

 

Material received:

Two air-dried slides and one alcohol-fixed slide. 2 mL of pink fluid received and processed for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

Moderately cellular smears showing spindle cells in branching, anastomosing tissue fragments and scattered singly, in a relatively clean background with occasional scattered lymphocytes. The tumour cells have elongated, tapering, often angled “fishhook” nuclei and occasional intranuclear inclusions, with a moderate amount of pale spindle cytoplasm. The cell block shows similar features, and the tumour cells are diffusely positive for S100 and SOX10.

 

(Editor’s note: In this case, the cytopathologist has not issued a cytopathology report but rather waited to include the ICC on the cell block in one integrated final report.)

Sample report 6

Specimen type:

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

25-year-old female patient with a well-defined lesion on the dorsum of wrist of 6 months’ duration.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Dorsum of left wrist

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Ganglion cyst

 

Material received:

Four air-dried slides and one alcohol-fixed slide.

 

Microscopic description:

A large amount of thick proteinaceous material is present, with occasional macrophages with foamy cytoplasm.

 

Sample report 7

Specimen type:

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information:

36-year-old male. Left anterior axillary fold 15 mm firm fixed lesion. Ultrasound shows spiculated lesion fixed to fascia.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Left anterior axillary fold

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Granular cell tumour

 

Material received:

Three air-dried smears and two alcohol-fixed smears. 3 mL pink fluid for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

Moderately cellular smears composed of cohesive sheets and scattered polygonal cells containing abundant granular cytoplasm and small round nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli. There is no material in the cell block.

 

Comment: The features are those of a granular cell tumour. Correlation with clinical and imaging findings is recommended, and if clinical suspicion is high, repeat FNAB (to obtain material for immunoperoxidase studies) or CNB is recommended.
