4.5.1.3 Leiomyoma

Definition

Leiomyomas are benign smooth muscle tumours that occur in the deep soft tissue or the abdominal-retroperitoneal cavity or in various viscera.

Clinical features and imaging

Leiomyomas commonly occur in deep dermis or skeletal muscle in extremities, or they arise in the muscularis propria of the gastrointestinal tract or the urinary tract. ER-positive leiomyomas in women can occur in the abdominal-retroperitoneal cavity or abdominal wall. Leiomyomas are typically detected radiologically as a well-circumscribed mass {11688572; 11684951; 10680889; 11688571; 20154594; 12653572}. The tumour size ranges widely, from < 10 mm for a submucosal leiomyoma in the gastrointestinal tract up to > 300 mm for a retroperitoneal leiomyoma {32622859; 11684951; 24384850}. Leiomyomas in the gastrointestinal tract are much more commonly seen in the oesophagus than in the stomach. These submucosal leiomyomas can be successfully sampled by endoscopic FNAB {32622859; 31213256; 31012545}.

Histopathology

Most tumours show a circumscribed bland spindle cell proliferation arranged in intersecting fascicles. The spindle cells have eosinophilic cytoplasm and slender nuclei with blunt ends, without cytopathological atypia such as nuclear enlargement, pleomorphism, or hyperchromasia. Mitoses are exceedingly rare, and necrosis is absent. Degenerative changes such as hyalinization, calcifications, and myxoid or cystic change are not uncommon {24384850}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Typically low, occasionally high, cellularity Large cohesive tissue fragments with smooth edges and variable cellularity, consisting of spindle cells Bland slender spindle cells with blunt-ended nuclei showing vesicular chromatin and without cell borders (Pap) Extremely clean background without stroma or single cells Lack of nuclear atypia, mitotic figures, or necrosis

Reference(s): {18208801; 12760289; 31012545; 8498132; 32622859}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Leiomyoma can be suspected based on cytopathology and unequivocally diagnosed with confirming ICC on cell blocks.

The differential diagnosis depends partly on the tumour location and includes most bland spindle cell neoplasms. For subepithelial intramural leiomyomas in the gastrointestinal tract, common considerations include gastrointestinal stromal tumour, peripheral nerve sheath tumour, solitary fibrous tumour, glomus tumour, desmoid fibromatosis, perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa), and even normal muscular propria. For superficial cutaneous lesions, angioleiomyoma and smooth muscle hamartoma enter the differential diagnosis. Ancillary ICC is necessary to distinguish leiomyoma from its mimics.

Sharing identical ICC, well-differentiated leiomyosarcoma is the most important differential diagnosis. Although the presence of nuclear pleomorphism, conspicuous mitoses, and increased single cells in the background suggest leiomyosarcoma, the distinction between leiomyoma with degenerative nuclear atypia and low-grade leiomyosarcoma is extremely difficult {8498132; 9143825; 16900474; 12619091}. Therefore, the term “smooth muscle neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential” may be used when the distinction between the two entities is not possible on needle biopsy {24384850}.

Ancillary testing

By ICC, leiomyomas express desmin, SMA, and/or h-caldesmon. In addition, leiomyomas (especially in distal oesophagus and rectum) may contain scattered increased numbers of interstitial cells of Cajal, which are usually highlighted by DOG1 and KIT (CD117) ICC {10680889; 11688571}. ER and PR are typically positive in retroperitoneal abdominal leiomyomas, especially in women {11684951}. In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) can be diagnostically helpful in identifying EBV-associated smooth muscle tumours in immunocompromised patients {24384850}.
