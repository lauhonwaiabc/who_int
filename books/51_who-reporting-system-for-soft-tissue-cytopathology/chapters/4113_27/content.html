4.1.1.3 Discussion and background

Benign soft tissue lesions greatly outnumber aggressive or malignant tumours, including sarcomas, by an estimated factor of 100 to 1 {6670522}. For the most part, benign soft tissue tumours mimic their associated normal tissue counterparts. Lipoma, for example, bears such a similar appearance to normal fat that it is often almost impossible to distinguish the two on cytomorphology. Objective cytopathology features such as uniformity, homogeneity, monomorphic features, and mitotic inactivity are generally descriptors associated with benign or very low risk neoplasms. Many of the lesions included in this FNAB category are not neoplastic, such as focal fat necrosis, mass-forming inflammatory processes, and ganglion cysts, but they are included because they fall into the differential diagnosis of neoplastic lesions clinically and on imaging, and they are frequently sampled by FNAB.

Many benign lesions are easily recognizable on FNAB and often produce low to modest cellularity with a uniform population of individual cells without significant pleomorphism or atypia. There are notable exceptions such as nodular fasciitis, which can be alarmingly cellular and show extensive cellular pleomorphism; myositis ossificans with marked nuclear pleomorphism; and nerve sheath tumours and leiomyomas that can show focal nuclear atypia.

Benign does not necessarily mean inconsequential, because benign tumours can cause significant morbidity by obstruction, impingement, or other physical mechanisms. In addition, not all benign tumours are easily removed. For example, vascular neoplasms and malformations can be large and infiltrative, necessitating difficult and often multistage removal. Fibromatosis, a locally aggressive tumour, has historically been treated surgically but is difficult to eradicate, with patients often enduring multiple excisions, deformities, and even limb loss, and the treatment paradigm for this entity has largely shifted to a wait-and-see approach, with consideration of medical therapies {28439797}. In addition, some benign lesions may be transient or self-limited, such as nodular fasciitis {21826056}.
