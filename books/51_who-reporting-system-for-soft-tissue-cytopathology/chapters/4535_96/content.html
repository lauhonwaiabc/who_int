4.5.3.5 Perineurioma

Definition

Perineuriomas are benign peripheral nerve sheath tumours composed entirely of perineurial cells.

Clinical features and imaging

Perineuriomas typically present as a painless mass in the dermis or the subcutaneous or deep soft tissue, or they can be intraneural or intramucosal {9414186; 11012740; 15370128; 15958848}. They typically occur in the extremities or trunk, and rarely in the head and neck or viscera. They occur with an almost equal sex distribution and a median age of 41–46 years. The sclerosing variant of perineurioma occurs at median age of 26 years {9414186; 12836021}.

On MRI, intraneural perineuriomas show enlargement of the affected nerve, with proximal and distal tapering; they are hypointense to isointense on T1-weighted images and hyperintense on T2-weighted images, with marked homogeneous contrast enhancement {29192862; 24210319; 35713690}.

Histopathology

Most tumours are circumscribed, with storiform, whorled, and/or fascicular growth patterns. Cellularity varies, with areas that are collagenous or mixed collagenous and myxoid stroma {9414186; 11012740; 15370128; 15958848}. Mitoses are typically rare, with a mean of 1 mitosis per 2 mm2. Rare atypical perineuriomas show pleomorphism, marked hypercellularity, or infiltration into surrounding tissue. Rarely they can show reticular {11257623; 16133356} or plexiform architecture {10936896; 16133356}. Sclerosing perineurioma shows thick collagen deposition, with epithelioid tumour cells arranged as cords, trabeculae, or whorls {9414186; 12836021}. Only extremely rarely do malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours show perineurial differentiation {9808129}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Low cellularity but can be variable Collagenous to myxoid background Bland elongated cells with bipolar cytoplasmic processes

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The cytopathological features of perineurioma are very nonspecific, and it is therefore impossible to make the diagnosis based on FNAB alone. The differential diagnoses depend partly on the subtype of perineurioma. They include low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, neurofibroma, cellular myxoma, and dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, and ICC is required.

Ancillary testing

Perineuriomas express EMA, which ranges from weak and focal to strong and diffuse {15958848}. Claudin-1 and GLUT1 are also often positive {12459629; 12836021}. CD34 is expressed in about 60% of soft tissue perineuriomas {15958848}. Staining for S100, SOX10, and GFAP is negative. In addition to EMA, sclerosing perineuriomas may show limited staining for keratin {9414186}. Sclerosing perineurioma overlaps morphologically with sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma. Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcomas may express EMA and claudin-1, mimicking perineurioma. They do, however, express MUC4, which is also seen in the related sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma, and testing for FUS::CREB3L2 or FUS::CREB3L1 fusions can assist in diagnosing low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma / sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma.

Cellular myxomas are typically positive for CD34 and may be positive for SMA. They typically do not express claudin-1 or GLUT1. In difficult cases, testing for GNAS mutations helps classify intramuscular myxomas.

Neurofibromas may contain intermixed perineurial cells but are more diffusely positive for S100. Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans strongly expresses CD34 but is typically more cellular at FNAB, with rare cases showing myxoid stroma. COL1A1::PDGFB fusion may be helpful in some cases.

Molecular testing for perineurioma of soft tissue is only rarely undertaken. Tumours have been associated with deletion of 22q12 and mutations in NF2 {9042282; 30303818; 11290539}, similar to schwannomas and meningiomas.
