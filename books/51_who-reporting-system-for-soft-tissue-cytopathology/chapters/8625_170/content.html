8.6.2.5 Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma

Definition

Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma (LGFMS) is a malignant fibroblastic neoplasm characterized by alternating collagenous and myxoid areas, deceptively bland spindle cells with a whorling growth pattern, and arcades of small blood vessels, and by FUS::CREB3L2 or FUS::CREB3L1 fusions.

Clinical features and imaging

Presentation is with a painless mass, over a wide age range (median: 30–45 years) and with a slight male predilection {7797200; 9414192; 11023096; 21921785}. LGFMS most often arises in the deep soft tissues of the extremities {7797200; 9414192; 11023096; 17721195; 21550274}, head and neck {9414192; 11023096}, mediastinum {17721195}, and retroperitoneum {7797200; 11023096; 17721195; 21921785; 29385413}. Tumours rarely arise in the superficial soft tissues {15644777; 21550274} or within visceral organs {21677541}. On imaging, LGFMS typically appears as well-circumscribed masses, hypodense to isodense to muscle on CT and showing variably hypointense and hyperintense foci distributed in nodular or gyriform patterns on MRI {22576969; 26204295}.

Histopathology

LGFMS is composed of fibrous, collagenous hypocellular areas that abruptly transition into cellular myxoid areas. This mosaic pattern, with abrupt transition between the collagenous and myxoid areas, can be recognized on CNB. In addition, tumour areas may show larger collagenous rosettes. The tumour cells are spindle, bland, and uniform, arranged in short fascicles or a storiform pattern. Mitotic activity is low, and there is no necrosis. Peripheral arcades of arteriole-sized blood vessels may be present {7797200; 9414192; 11023096; 15644777; 17721195; 21550274; 21921785}. In some tumours, areas of sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma may be present, composed of monomorphic epithelioid cells arranged in cords, nests, and sheets, surrounded by hyalinized sclerotic stromal collagen {17721195; 22982887; 24441665; 25231134}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

A primary diagnosis of LGFMS cannot be made solely on cytopathological features {10227597; 18637810}, and these tumours are likely to be placed into the “Atypical” category. The differential diagnosis is wide and includes spindle cell neoplasms such as peripheral nerve sheath tumours, schwannoma, neurofibroma, and perineurioma, as well as desmoid-type fibromatosis, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans with myxoid differentiation, solitary fibrous tumour, and low-grade myxofibrosarcoma, as well as intramuscular myxoma.

Other differential diagnoses include myxoid liposarcoma and extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma. Myxoid liposarcoma may be differentiated by the presence of lipoblasts, and round cell and spindle cell variants of myxoid liposarcoma show higher cellularity and atypia than LGFMS, although NGS can be of great assistance. Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans with myxoid changes is a superficial tumour; the distinction from LGFMS may be difficult, requiring ICC. Cellular myxoma is an intermediate lesion between intramuscular myxoma and LGFMS, showing minimally atypical cells with some elongated nuclei in a myxoid background.

Sclerosing epithelioid fibromyxoid sarcoma should be differentiated from malignant melanoma and mucinous carcinoma {33168472; 15108227; 19899126}.

The differential diagnosis of hybrid tumours or predominantly sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma include metastatic carcinoma, epithelioid vascular tumours, ossifying fibromyxoid tumour, biphasic synovial sarcoma, and extraskeletal osteosarcoma. A definitive diagnosis requires MUC4 ICC {21415703}.

Ancillary testing

LGFMS and subsets of sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma share cytomorphological features, and they are diffusely and strongly positive in the cytoplasm for MUC4 ICC and share molecular characteristics {17721195; 22982887; 24441665; 25231134; 21415703}. They may be positive for EMA and SMA {17721195; 21415703}. Cytogenetic testing reveals t(7;16) or t(11;16) translocations and supernumerary ring chromosomes. FUS rearrangements can be detected by FISH, or FUS::CREB3L1 or FUS::CREB3L2 fusion by PCR/NGS-based techniques {17721195}.
