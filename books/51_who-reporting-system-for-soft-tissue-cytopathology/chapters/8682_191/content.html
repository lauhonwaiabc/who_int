8.6.8.2 Epithelioid sarcoma

Definition

Epithelioid sarcoma (ES) is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm that exhibits partial or complete epithelioid cytomorphology and immunophenotype and is characterized by loss of SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47) in most cases.

Clinical features and imaging

Two clinicopathological subtypes are recognized. Classic or distal ES presents in the form of single or multiple, usually painless and firm, indurated superficial nodules in the upper extremities. Often, the lesions present as non-healing ulcers, mimicking inflammatory lesions of the skin. Proximal-type ES presents as a deep soft tissue mass, usually in the proximal truncal regions of the perineum and genitalia {4014539; 9042279; 11454997; 34379944; 17341294}.

There are no specific features on imaging. These tumours appear as infiltrating lesions {12107573}. The proximal type displays a multinodular configuration. Imaging is also useful in detecting metastatic lesions in the lymph nodes {12239049}.

Histopathology

Classic-type ES is composed of nodules of atypical cells with epithelioid to fusiform shapes, variable numbers of mitotic figures, and central necrosis, leading to a granuloma-like appearance {4014539; 17341294}. Dystrophic calcification and metaplastic bone formation are uncommonly seen {4014539; 17341294}. The proximal type displays a multinodular and diffuse arrangement of large, pleomorphic cells with rhabdoid-like morphology. Focal necrosis and haemorrhage, leading to a pseudoangiosarcomatous appearance, can be seen {9042279; 11454997; 17341294; 18607629}. The hybrid form includes tumours with variable components of classic and proximal subtypes {19033866}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

High cellularity Loosely cohesive tissue fragments and sheets and single cells Polygonal or epithelioid and spindle cells with moderate to abundant, well-defined cytoplasm Perinucleolar chromatin clearing (“vesicular” chromatin, Pap) and distinct nucleoli Perinuclear, intracytoplasmic inclusions, leading to a plasmacytoid or rhabdoid-like appearance, particularly in the proximal subtype Variable multinucleated giant cells, cytoplasmic vacuoles, and metachromatic stroma

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnoses include carcinoma, synovial sarcoma, epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, epithelioid angiosarcoma, epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, and melanoma {8356950; 27145123}.

Ancillary testing

CD34 positivity and loss of SMARCB1 are useful in the distinction of ES from metastatic carcinoma. CD34 positivity separates ES from a synovial sarcoma, malignant rhabdoid tumour, and myoepithelial carcinoma {16704491}. Loss of SMARCB1 is also useful in differentiating ES from epithelioid haemangioendothelioma and angiosarcoma {24075062}. Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour shows diffuse S100 immunoreactivity and loss of SMARCB1 in 27–67% cases {25602794; 28452074}. Loss of SMARCB1 and negative immunoexpression of HMB45 and melan-A are useful in ruling out melanoma. ES and anaplastic large cell lymphoma are both positive for EMA, but loss of SMARCB1 is indicative of ES, whereas CD30, CD45 (LCA), and ALK immunoreactivity are indicative of anaplastic large cell lymphoma {20306994}.

Most cases show homozygous or heterozygous deletion of SMARCB1 (INI1) {23060122}. ES displays immunopositivity for various epithelial markers, such as low- and high-molecular-weight cytokeratins and EMA {9042279; 10452506; 11454997; 17341294}. More than 50% of cases show CD34 immunopositivity {10452506; 18607629}. Loss of SMARCB1 protein is reported in > 80% cases of ES {18973917; 19033866; 24075062}. Certain classic-type ES cases display ERG immunopositivity {23774169; 25479928}.
