11.1.0.3 Thyroglossal duct cyst

Definition

Thyroglossal duct cyst (TGDC) is persistent cystic dilatation of the thyroglossal duct in the anterior neck, commonly in the midline.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DA05.Y Other specified cysts of oral or facial-neck region & Thyroglossal cyst

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority (> 95%) of TGDCs occur in the midline neck, at or just below the level of the hyoid bone (75% are infrahyoid), with 2–4% just at the tongue base {22192899; 23458940; 27161104}. Rarely, TGDC may be seen within the thyroid gland (pyramidal lobe; isthmus) {9731820}. The lateral (non-midline) neck cases are usually displaced by recurrent infections or prior surgery {27161104}.

Clinical features

Patients generally present with a mobile, midline, infrahyoid, painless neck mass that moves upward with swallowing {19248939; 21237423; 22338239; 27161104}. An associated skin fistula (in ~10% of patients) is twice as common in paediatric patients as adult patients {19248939; 22338239; 27161104}. A malignancy may be identified in about 3% of cases {1745980; 9121176; 11510730; 23318645; 27161104}.

Imaging

By imaging, a midline round or elongated cystic lesion, sometimes with irregular walls, septa, and solid components, may expand into or destroy the hyoid bone {22338239; 23689821; 30717638; 30725193}. The amount of thyroid follicular tissue is usually insufficient to be detected on radioisotope studies. Nodular soft tissue excrescences may suggest carcinoma.

Epidemiology

About 2.23 per 100 000 people in the general population have TGDC, representing 60–70% of cases of congenital neck cysts {27161104}. Furthermore, in adult patients who underwent neck ultrasound, 0.9% were noted to have a TGDC {30467213}, and 7% of adult laryngectomy specimens demonstrated thyroglossal duct remnants {850454}. There is a wide age range (0.8–87.0 years), with a median age of 32 years {27161104; 23676810}, but with bimodal peaks in the first and fifth decades of life {21237423; 23458940; 27161104}. TGDC shows an equal sex distribution overall, although in the paediatric age group it is slightly more common in boys, and in adults it is slightly more common in women {21237423; 23676810; 24332664; 25577275; 27161104}. In patients with carcinoma, women are more frequently affected (F:M ratio: 2.3:1), with a mean age at presentation of 40 years {11510730; 21453651; 23318645; 24457400; 27704385; 28322121}, although paediatric patients can be affected {12764621; 27704385; 28293858}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The thyroglossal duct is an embryonic developmental structure whose remnant remains anywhere along the thyroid anlage migration pathway from the tongue foramen caecum to a final pretracheal position. Normal involution between 7 and 10 weeks’ gestation may anomalously persist, with dilatation resulting in a TGDC {27161104; 25577275; 23689821; 17720342; 7169333}.

Macroscopic appearance

A smooth-walled cystic structure includes clear, mucinous fluid to purulent material when secondarily infected. The size ranges from 2 to 85 mm (median: 25 mm), with cysts smaller in paediatric patients than in adult patients {18314017; 27161104}.

Histopathology

There is an epithelial-lined cyst adjacent to the hyoid bone, containing respiratory epithelium, squamous epithelium, or a mixture of both (50% of cases show both), with squamous epithelium alone being more common in inflamed cases {27161104}. Some cases lack an epithelial lining. Cysts rupture frequently (65%), inciting fibrosis, with histiocytes, cholesterol clefts, and foreign body giant cell reaction. Thyroid follicular epithelium, commonly localized in the cyst wall as several millimetres of tissue, is identified in about 70% of cases {27161104; 11404612}. Mucoserous salivary gland tissue (15%) and cartilage (< 1%) may also be identified.

Malignant neoplasms are identified in about 3% of TGDCs {1745980; 25577275; 27704385; 27161104; 28293858; 29938764}, with papillary thyroid carcinoma accounting for > 95% of malignancies {1745980; 11510730; 19565286; 21453651; 23318645; 25577275; 27704385; 27161104; 28322121; 28293858} and with other carcinoma types rarely detected {9413065; 21512669}. Soft tissue involvement is common (60%). Some patients may show concurrent malignancy in thyroidectomies performed as part of management {19565286; 21453651; 24457400; 25577275; 27704385; 27161104; 30091370}. C cells originating from the ultimobranchial apparatus are not seen in TGDC, which originates from the thyroid diverticulum, located near the foramen caecum at the base of the tongue {27161104; 28536894}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes epidermal inclusion cyst, dermoid cyst, branchial cleft cyst, bronchogenic cyst, cervical thymic cyst, and metastatic papillary thyroid carcinoma, which can usually be separated on the basis of the anatomical site of involvement, imaging findings, and pertinent histological features.

Cytology

Smears from TGDCs are often non-diagnostic, with a low cellularity and more inflammatory cells than epithelial cells {12049106; 19248939; 22277268; 25577275; 27161104}. The epithelial cells are usually squamous or respiratory, seldom containing thyroid follicular cells. Smears obtained from firm, fixed lesions may show nuclear features of papillary thyroid carcinoma when malignancy is concurrently present {25577275; 27704385; 27161104}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a perihyoidal location.

Desirable: a midline lesion; respiratory or squamous epithelium–lined cyst with associated thyroid follicular epithelium.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence is higher in patients with infected cysts as well as those not managed by a Sistrunk procedure, with perihyoidal thyroglossal duct remnants resulting in persistent clinical disease {30194760; 30388395; 23331819; 17165669}. Infection, rupture, haemorrhage, and fistula formation are recognized complications {19248939; 22249624; 23676810; 24332664; 29521676; 30388395}. When carcinoma is identified, the outcome is excellent, although staging is not presently required for extrathyroidal sites {19565286; 27161104; 32042395}.
