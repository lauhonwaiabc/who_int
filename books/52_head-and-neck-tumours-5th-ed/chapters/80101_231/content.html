8.0.10.1 Ewing sarcoma

Definition

Ewing sarcoma is a primitive small round cell sarcoma defined by fusions involving members of the FET and ETS gene families.

ICD-O coding

9364/3 Ewing sarcoma

9364/3 Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B52.3 Ewing sarcoma of soft tissue

2B52.Y Ewing sarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of other specified sites

Related terminology

Not recommended: primitive neuroectodermal tumour; peripheral neuroectodermal tumour.

Subtype(s)

Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma

Localization

Overall, 1–9% of Ewing sarcomas arise in head and neck sites, of which approximately 40% involve skull and facial bones, 30% are centred in soft tissue, and 9% affect the mandible {19841575; 28056517}. However, most cases of the adamantinoma-like subtype reported to date involve the head and neck, including the salivary glands, thyroid gland, and sinonasal tract {26034869; 30285997; 31950471}.

Clinical features

Patients with Ewing sarcoma present with a palpable mass or mass effect, including pain, swelling, bleeding, or pathological fracture.

Epidemiology

Approximately 50% of head and neck Ewing sarcomas arise in patients aged < 18 years, and 85% occur in the first four decades of life, with a slight male predominance similar to that of tumours at other sites outside the head and neck {28056517; 31774563}.

Etiology

Ewing sarcoma generally arises sporadically, and no risk factors are known.

Pathogenesis

Ewing sarcomas arise as a result of reciprocal translocations between members of the FET gene family, predominantly encompassing EWSR1 or FUS, and the ETS gene family, most commonly including FLI1, ERG, ETV1, ETV4, or FEV {3163261; 8022439; 8162068; 7700648; 8834175; 9121764; 12907633; 17620387}. These genes encode transcription factors contributing to oncogenic transformation via widespread gene activation and repression {27246176}.

Macroscopic appearance

Ewing sarcoma specimens are solid with a soft, fleshy, grey-white cut surface.

Histopathology

Ewing sarcoma is composed of hypercellular sheets and nests of uniform small round cells with a scant amount of eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm, indistinct cytoplasmic borders, and occasional rosette formation. Tumour nuclei are generally round to oval with delicate stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. A subset of cases, known as atypical Ewing sarcoma, can show larger cells with hyperchromatic, angulated nuclei; coarse chromatin; and prominent nucleoli.

Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma is a subtype of Ewing sarcoma that shows a lobulated architecture, peripheral palisading, and a myxoid to hyalinized stroma; there is consistent immunohistochemical evidence of squamous differentiation, although keratin pearls are not seen in every case.

Immunohistochemistry

Ewing sarcoma consistently shows diffuse membranous positivity for CD99 and nuclear positivity for NKX2-2 {1848471; 29661713; 26847175}. Although NKX2-2 is the more specific marker overall, it stains several mimics in the head and neck, particularly tumours with neuroendocrine differentiation {28616785}. FLI1 and ERG are expressed in cases with corresponding gene fusions, although their specificity is limited {11117787; 19841938; 22766791}. Neuroendocrine marker positivity can be seen in 50% of Ewing sarcomas, and 30% show some cytokeratin expression {33610950; 16006796}. The adamantinoma-like subtype of Ewing sarcoma consistently demonstrates diffuse cytokeratin, p63, and p40 positivity {16006796}.

Differential diagnosis

Ewing sarcoma overlaps with many head and neck small blue round cell tumours, including olfactory neuroblastoma, melanoma, lymphoma, and various carcinomas. A broad immunohistochemical panel including CD99 and/or NKX2-2 can facilitate the correct diagnosis, although molecular confirmation is frequently necessary. Although less common in the head and neck, tumours previously known as Ewing-like sarcomas, including those with CIC and BCOR fusions, should also be ruled out via immunohistochemistry and molecular studies {28337592; 28777154}.

Cytology

FNA demonstrates a hypercellular population of monotonous round cells frequently including a mix of smaller and larger cells and occasionally showing rosette formation {22180234}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic confirmation is recommended for diagnosis in the head and neck, given the overlap with other small blue round cell tumours. About 85–90% of cases show t(11;22)(q24;q12), resulting in EWSR1::FLI1 fusion {3163261}, and 5–10% of cases show t(21;22)(q22;q12), resulting in EWSR1::ERG fusion {10561219}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: malignancy with a small round cell morphology; membranous CD99 expression.

Desirable: molecular confirmation of EWSR1 or FUS rearrangement.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for head and neck sarcomas.

Prognosis and prediction

The 5-year survival rate is 70% for localized disease but just 30% for cases with metastases at presentation {26304893}. Tumours in the head and neck have better survival than those at other sites, probably because they are smaller and have a lower metastatic rate {28056517}. The presence of 100% necrosis after chemotherapy is associated with better survival {27482030}.
