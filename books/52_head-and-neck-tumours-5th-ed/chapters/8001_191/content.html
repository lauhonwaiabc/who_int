8.0.0.1 Soft tissue tumours: Introduction

Many different soft tissue tumours arise in the soft tissues and mucosa-covered surfaces of the head and neck region. Some mesenchymal neoplasms more commonly arise in other parts of the body; some, such as spindle cell lipoma, have a predilection for the head and neck region; and others are unique to specific head and neck sites. The most significant change from the fourth edition of the WHO classification of head and neck tumours is the removal of most soft tissue tumours from each anatomical site and the collation of the diagnostic information in one chapter for all relevant head and neck sites. Selected tumours specific to, or most often found in, particular sites are covered in site-specific chapters (see #28610Box 9.01). Most of the bone lesions are presented in section Chapter 7: Odontogenic and maxillofacial bone tumours.

The fifth edition of the WHO classification of soft tissue and bone tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]] provides a more detailed account of each soft tissue tumour. In this chapter, the authors provide the key etiological, pathogenetic, diagnostic, and prognostic information on each neoplasm. Where appropriate, information specific to head and neck neoplasms is emphasized. The classification remains primarily morphological, but immunocytochemistry and other molecular techniques are often useful for confirming a diagnosis (e.g. solitary fibrous tumour) or resolving a differential diagnosis. Molecular techniques are increasingly valuable for identifying targets for specific treatments.
