8.0.6.1 Rhabdomyoma

Definition

Rhabdomyoma is a benign soft tissue neoplasm showing skeletal muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8900/0 Rhabdomyoma, NOS

8904/0 Adult-type rhabdomyoma

8903/0 Fetal-type rhabdomyoma

ICD-11 coding

2E86.2 & XH8WG9 Rhabdomyoma & Rhabdomyoma, NOS

2E86.2 & XH4729 Rhabdomyoma & Fetal rhabdomyoma

2E86.2 & XH4BG5 Rhabdomyoma & Adult rhabdomyoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: extracardiac rhabdomyoma; rhabdomyomatous hamartoma.

Subtype(s)

Adult-type rhabdomyoma (ARM); fetal-type rhabdomyoma (FRM)

Localization

ARM and FRM arise predominantly in the head and neck, with the parapharyngeal space, salivary glands, larynx, mouth, and soft tissue of the neck most commonly affected {31950473}.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a slowly growing mass, but rapid growth does not exclude the diagnosis {31950473}. ARM may be multifocal {2974590; 32773112; 8505039}. Symptoms are site-dependent and include hoarseness, airway obstruction, and dysphagia {31950473}.

Epidemiology

The age range of patients with ARM is broad (median age: 60 years); the M:F ratio is 3:1. The median age of patients with FRM is 4.5 years, ranging from birth (congenital) to 60 years; the M:F ratio is 5:3 {31950473; 8319954; 8505039}.

Etiology

Some cases are syndrome-related (FRM: naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome; ARM: Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome) {16294371; 29744825}.

Pathogenesis

Mutations in the sonic hedgehog pathway genes are often involved {23780909; 16294371}. FLCN can be altered in some cases {29744825}.

Macroscopic appearance

ARMs are circumscribed, lobulated, soft, and tan to red-brown, with a fleshy cut surface. FRMs are circumscribed, soft, and grey-white, with a glistening cut surface. The median tumour size for both is 30 mm {8505039; 8319954}.

Histopathology

ARM is composed of large polygonal cells having abundant eosinophilic, granular, or vacuolated cytoplasm (spider cells) with focal cross-striation or crystalline (rod-like or jackstraw-like) inclusions. The small round nuclei are centrally or peripherally located, with vesicular chromatin and distinct nucleoli {8505039; 31950473}.

FRM shows immature rhabdomyoblasts including primitive round to spindle cells and myotube-like rhabdomyoblasts with eosinophilic cytoplasm and cross-striations set in a myxoid matrix {8319954}.

FRM can be confused with embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma. Features favouring FRM are a superficial location, circumscription without infiltrative margins, a lack of cellular atypia, absence of a cambium layer, and a paucity of mitotic figures {19421668; 31950473; 8319954}.

Cytology

ARM shows large polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic or vacuolated cytoplasm and peripherally located nuclei {29131558; 21053579}. In FRM, bland spindle cells and rhabdomyoblasts with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm are seen {19421668}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

ARM: large polygonal skeletal muscle cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm and round, centrally or peripherally located nuclei with nucleoli.

FRM: rhabdomyoblasts in different stages, without atypia and with few mitotic figures.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The recommended treatment is complete excision. Recurrence is often attributed to incomplete removal. There is no metastatic potential {19421668; 31950473; 8505039}.
