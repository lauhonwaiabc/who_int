11.1.0.1 Ranula

Definition

Ranula is a pseudocyst that develops from the extravasation of mucin, typically located in the floor of the mouth, adjacent to the sublingual gland.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DA04.5 Mucocele of salivary gland

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucocoele; retention cyst; mucus extravasation.

Subtype(s)

Simple or oral ranula; plunging or cervical ranula {15356464; 22038368; 32109322; 32809690}

Localization

Ranula typically occurs in the lateral side of the floor of the mouth, in association with an excretory duct of the sublingual gland. The plunging type of ranula passes around the posterior border of the mylohyoid muscle or through a hiatus in the muscle {15356464; 20054853; 22038368; 32809690}.

Clinical features

Simple ranula presents as a painless mass in the floor of the mouth, whereas a plunging ranula presents as a neck swelling. Ranulas are mostly unilateral and unifocal but can cross the midline {15356464; 22038368; 32109322; 32809690}.

Imaging

CT and MRI images of a plunging ranula may demonstrate extension of the lesion into the submandibular space (tail sign) {31334858}.

Epidemiology

Ranulas are rare and have a frequency of 0.2 cases per 1000 people. The lesions affect people of any age but are more common in teenagers and young adults. No sex predilection has been reported {12618979; 32809690}.

Etiology

The most common etiology is trauma to the sublingual gland, which is a spontaneous secretor that produces a continuous flow of mucin {15356464; 20054853; 22038368; 32809690}. An association between ranulas and primary Sjögren syndrome has been reported {28964279; 31141241; 32803681}.

Pathogenesis

Not relevant

Macroscopic appearance

The lesion appears as a blue and fluctuant dome-shaped mass that resembles a male frog’s vocal sac. It ranges from 1 to 40 mm in size {3343879; 32809690}.

Histopathology

Ranula is a cyst-like structure consisting of mucin surrounded by fibroconnective or granulation tissue that frequently contains histiocytes and is devoid of an epithelial lining. Thrombi and blood-filled vessels can be seen in the surrounding wall or the adjacent salivary gland.

Differential diagnosis

The entities include mucoepidermoid carcinoma, dermoid cyst, thyroglossal duct cyst, branchial cleft cyst, and a vascular malformation. The presence of mucin and the absence of an epithelial lining help to differentiate ranula from other cystic and vascular lesions {15356464; 22038368; 32809690}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cyst-like structure containing mucin surrounded by fibrous or granulation tissue, without an epithelial lining.

Desirable: imaging showing extension of the lesion into the submandibular space (tail sign) (in selected cases).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Both simple and plunging ranulas are treated by complete surgical excision of the gland with the removal of the adjacent traumatized salivary duct. Recurrence may occur due to inadequate excision {15635557; 20594633; 21757145; 32087991}.
