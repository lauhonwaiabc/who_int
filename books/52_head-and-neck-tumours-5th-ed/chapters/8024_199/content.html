8.0.2.4 Desmoid fibromatosis

Definition

Desmoid fibromatosis is a locally aggressive, non-metastasizing myofibroblastic neoplasm with infiltrative growth and a propensity for local recurrence.

ICD-O coding

8821/1 Desmoid-type fibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH13Z3 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Desmoid-type fibromatosis (aggressive fibromatosis)

Related terminology

Acceptable: aggressive/deep fibromatosis; desmoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Soft tissues of the neck / mandibular region are commonly involved. Less frequently affected are the scalp, face, paranasal sinuses, nasopharynx, oral cavity, and larynx {31950474; 31059930}. Multifocality may be seen {29705714}.

Clinical features

Patients usually report no pain. Lesions can be large (> 50 mm) and become symptomatic by forming a mass and by invading bone, nerves, and vasculature {31950474; 24206198; 31059930}.

Epidemiology

Fibromatosis of the head and neck is rare, accounting for as many as 15% of all extra-abdominal desmoids in adults and 35% in children {31950474; 31059930; 27988199}. Patients have a median age of 37–39 years. The disease is more common in women than men.

Etiology

There are multiple causes, including genetic events and external factors such as trauma {31950474}.

Pathogenesis

Desmoid fibromatosis derives from mesenchymal progenitor cells. Mutations in CTNNB1 or (less frequently) APC stabilize β-catenin, which activates the WNT/APC/β-catenin pathway {29705714; 20841474}.

Macroscopic appearance

Fibromatosis forms a poorly delineated, firm, grey-white lesion with a coarse trabecular cut surface {24206198; 31950474}.

Histopathology

Neoplasms are infiltrative and composed of poorly defined long fascicles of uniform myofibroblasts with slender, tapering, stellate or oval nuclei with open chromatin. Mitotic figures are variably present and often scarce. There is inconspicuous cytoplasm. The background is most often collagenous, sometimes showing coarse bundles, but can be also strikingly myxoid, blurring the classic architecture. Parallel with the fascicles are small vessels with perivascular oedema. Immunohistochemically, SMA and nuclear β-catenin are variably expressed, with the latter showing about 80% positivity {31950474; 29705714; 24206198}.

Cytology

FNA shows clusters of fibroblasts without atypia.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The finding of hotspot somatic mutations in exon 3 of CTNNB1 may be helpful in small biopsies or in selected cases if morphology is inconclusive {31950474}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: long fascicles of monomorphic myofibroblasts with small, tapered nuclei and inconspicuous cytoplasm.

Desirable: nuclear expression of β-catenin; CTNNB1 hotspot or (alternatively) APC mutation (in selected cases).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The course is variable and unpredictable, with progression, stable disease, and spontaneous regression. An individual approach is mandatory. A wait-and-see policy is advocated in asymptomatic patients. Systemic treatments comprise antihormonal or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory therapies, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and chemotherapy. Recurrence after surgery does not correlate with margin status. The question of whether tumours harbouring CTNNB1 p.S45F mutations are associated with a greater risk of local recurrence is controversial {31950474; 29705714; 31059930; 32004793}.
