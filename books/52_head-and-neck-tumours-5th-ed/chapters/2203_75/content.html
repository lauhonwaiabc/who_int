2.2.0.3 Salivary gland anlage tumour

Definition

Salivary gland anlage tumour is a predominantly infantile nasopharyngeal lesion with biphasic squamoid epithelial and myoepithelial components continuous with the surface epithelium, resembling developing salivary gland.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2E90.6 Benign neoplasm of nasopharynx

Related terminology

Not recommended: congenital pleomorphic adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Salivary gland anlage tumours occur in the posterior nasal septum or posterior nasopharyngeal wall.

Clinical features

Patients present with feeding difficulty and respiratory distress due to nasal airway obstruction {15656947}. Before birth, salivary gland anlage tumour may be associated with polyhydramnios {25149159}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 40 examples of salivary gland anlage tumour have been reported {7402665; 8279626; 15656947; 19025657; 19112069; 19289716; 20704478; 21391752; 22896937; 25149159}. The affected patients are infants (diagnosed by 3 months of age) and there is a male predilection. An in utero case has been reported {25149159}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Whole-exome sequencing of 3 cases showed no plausible driver mutations {30598408}, suggestive of a possible hamartomatous origin for these tumours.

Macroscopic appearance

Salivary gland anlage tumour is a polypoid to pedunculated, smooth, tan-brown mass with a solid to microcystic cut surface {15656947}.

Histopathology

Salivary gland anlage tumour is characterized by a complex network of tubules and ducts. Submucosal ducts and solid and cystic squamous nests with variable keratinization are continuous with the surface squamous epithelium. The subepithelial stroma tends to be hypocellular, with more cellular stromal nodules in the centre of the polyp. Cellular atypia and mitoses are absent {15656947}.

Immunohistochemistry

The myoepithelial nature of the stromal cells is confirmed by positive immunohistochemistry for cytokeratins and SMA.

Cytology

Cytological examination is rarely required, but if done, it may reveal cohesive tissue fragments (some with duct-like structures) of uniform cells with small oval nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, and sparse cytoplasm with indistinct borders. A component of metaplastic squamous epithelium may be present {9181318}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a polypoid lesion with a complex network of ducts and solid-cystic squamous nests, showing continuity with surface squamous epithelium; hypocellular to cellular spindle cell stroma.

Desirable: spindly cells positive for cytokeratins and SMA (in selected cases).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

There have been no reports of recurrence after excision.
