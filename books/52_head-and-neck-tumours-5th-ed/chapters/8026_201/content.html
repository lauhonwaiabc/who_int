8.0.2.6 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour (IMT) is a distinctive, rarely metastasizing neoplasm composed of myofibroblastic and fibroblastic spindle cells accompanied by an inflammatory infiltrate of plasma cells, lymphocytes, and/or eosinophils.

ICD-O coding

8825/1 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

8825/1 Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH66Z0 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Myofibroblastic tumour, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflammatory pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma

Localization

Approximately 15–25% of IMTs develop in the head and neck {7611533; 31690781}, most commonly the larynx (true vocal cords), sinonasal tract (maxillary sinus), and oral cavity {26276782; 24756612; 22014665; 18022129; 8635024}. Other sites include the trachea, pharynx, orbit, skull base, salivary glands, neck, and skin {7611533; 21042957; 31690781}.

Clinical features

Patients with laryngeal tumours present with hoarseness and dysphonia {18022129; 8635024}, whereas those with sinonasal and skull base tumours experience nasal obstruction, pain, visual changes, and cranial nerve palsies {24756612}. Constitutional symptoms are uncommon {22014665; 30410939; 31950474; 24756612}.

Imaging

Imaging shows a nonspecific lobulated soft tissue mass with variable calcification, postcontrast enhancement, and local invasion {22974570; 24756612; 30410939; 27153788}.

Epidemiology

A wide age range of patients is affected (0–81 years). Head and neck IMTs commonly affect adults {26276782; 24756612; 27443585; 30410939; 31690781}. No consistent sex predilection is observed {21042957; 22014665}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

As many as 60% of tumours have an ALK gene (2p23) rearrangement with a wide variety of fusion partners. Among head and neck IMTs, ALK fusion with TIMP3 has been most commonly detected, and ROS1 fusion can be rarely seen {31215130; 34001695}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are usually < 50 mm, firm, and fleshy, with a tan-white, whorled to myxoid cut surface, occasionally showing haemorrhage and calcification.

Histopathology

Tumours are submucosal and may be ulcerated, ranging from circumscribed to infiltrative. Three principal patterns may coexist: a myxoid fasciitis or granulation tissue–like pattern, a hypercellular spindle cell pattern, and a hypocellular fibrous (fibromatosis-like) pattern {7611533; 17414097; 31950474}. The myofibroblasts have eosinophilic cytoplasm and vesicular, open chromatin with several small nucleoli. A ganglion-like appearance, intranuclear cytoplasmic inclusions, or granular cell change may be seen. The spindle cells are associated with variable inflammatory infiltrate and fibromyxoid stroma. Necrosis is infrequent. Mitotic activity is variable but generally low, without atypical forms {7611533; 24756612; 17414097}. The epithelioid subtype is primarily intra-abdominal.

By immunohistochemistry, most head and neck IMTs, especially in patients < 40 years of age, express ALK, correlating with ALK gene rearrangements {26469330; 26647767; 27443585}. ROS1 may show cytoplasmic reactivity in ROS1-rearranged IMT {26647767; 25612511}. The spindle cells generally show a patchy, variably intense reactivity for SMA, MSA, calponin, and desmin, with focal keratin reactivity in 30% of cases {7611533; 31950474}.

The pathological differential diagnosis of IMT is broad and depends on the predominant histological pattern and site. In particular, distinction from spindle cell squamous cell carcinoma is crucial; finding areas of squamous dysplasia or differentiated squamous cell carcinoma is helpful {33723761}. Laryngeal IMT should not be confused with vocal cord polyp {26276782; 31215130}. IgG4-expressing plasma cell infiltration in IMT is usually much lower than in IgG4-related disease {19718789; 26469330; 30451733}.

Cytology

The cytology of IMT features bland spindle cells with oval nuclei and small nucleoli, with ganglion-like cells possible, in a background of lymphocytes and plasma cells {20730898; 27087029}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular assays for ALK, ROS1, and other gene rearrangements may be used, particularly if targeted therapy is being considered {26647767; 30002191; 31690781}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a loose or compact fascicular proliferation of spindle cells with myofibroblastic differentiation, fibrous or myxoid stroma, and associated chronic inflammatory cell infiltrate.

Desirable: demonstration of ALK or ROS1 expression by immunohistochemistry; rearrangement of ALK or other implicated receptor tyrosine kinases by molecular methods (in selected cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The natural history of IMT is variable. Laryngeal IMTs generally follow a benign course {18022129; 8635024; 26276782}, whereas sinonasal and oral cavity IMTs are more aggressive {21042957; 29643133; 24756612}. Overall, approximately 25% of IMTs recur, related to anatomical site and resectability {24756612; 27443585; 30410939; 7611533}. Distant metastases are rare (< 5%) and may be associated with ALK-negative IMT. Death from disease is unusual (~10%), but reliable prognostic indicators are lacking {17414097}. Targeted therapies (i.e. ALK inhibitors) may improve outcome {28787259; 29286567; 31690781}.
