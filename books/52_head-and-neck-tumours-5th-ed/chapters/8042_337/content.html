8.0.4.2 GLI1-altered soft tissue tumour

Definition

GLI1-altered soft tissue tumour is a mesenchymal neoplasm of uncertain histogenesis, characterized by an epithelioid morphology and GLI1 alterations.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites

Related terminology

Acceptable: epithelioid neoplasm with GLI1 rearrangement.

Not recommended: haemangiopericytoma of tongue; pericytoma with t(7;12).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours may involve soft tissue, bone, or viscera. Approximately 40% occur in the head and neck, most arising in the tongue and oropharynx, with less common locations including the submandibular gland and the soft tissues of the neck {15111311; 29309307; 31189998; 31934916; 35933574}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a slow-growing, painless mass {8163277; 31567194}.

Epidemiology

This is a rare tumour occurring over a broad age range but most commonly presenting in young adults (median age: 37 years, range: 1–83 years). There is no overt sex predilection {15111311; 31934916}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

GLI1-altered soft tissue tumours have a heterogeneous molecular pathogenesis and can be broadly divided into two groups. About two thirds of cases are defined by gene fusions {15111311}. The remaining one third arise from amplification involving GLI1, often with coamplification of neighbouring genes {31189998}. Tumours with GLI1 rearrangement can be further subdivided on the basis of their fusion partner. The most common fusion is ACTB::GLI1, with PTCH1::GLI1, MALAT1::GLI1, and DERA::GLI1 representing rare events {15111311; 31189998; 31934916; 33067875}. Various GLI1 exons have been reported as breakpoints {15111311; 29309307}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range in size from 8 to ≥ 100 mm but tend to be smaller in the head and neck region. They are generally circumscribed and, on cut section, are white-yellow, fleshy, and may be solid and/or cystic {15111311; 29309307; 31567194; 33067875; 32132020}.

Histopathology

Morphologically there is a spectrum of architectural and cytological findings. Tumours are frequently multilobulated, with margins that may be well demarcated or infiltrative {15111311}. Architecturally the cells are generally arranged in nests and sheets; occasionally they may have fascicles, pseudorosettes, cords and/or reticular patterns {15111311; 29309307; 31189998; 31934916}. The cells are small to medium-sized and range from epithelioid to ovoid to spindled, with variable eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm {29309307; 26980027; 31189998}. The nuclei are round to ovoid and relatively monomorphic, with pinpoint nucleoli; mitotic activity is variable (0–5 mitoses/mm2) {29309307; 31189998; 31934916}. A prominent capillary-sized vasculature is often present. The intervening stroma may be focally myxoid or hyalinized {15111311; 29309307; 31189998; 31934916}. Protrusion into vascular spaces is common {15111311; 31934916}. Necrosis is rarely encountered {31934916}. The morphology and ultrastructural findings are suggestive of pericytic differentiation {15111311}, but this is not entirely supported by immunohistochemistry.

These neoplasms lack a consistent immunophenotype. Although positive for vimentin, these tumours show variable, often patchy/focal or weak, immunoreactivity for SMA, S100, CD10, CD56, CD99, p16, and EMA {15111311; 29309307; 26980027; 31189998; 31934916; 32132020; 35933574}. Focal expression of h-caldesmon and keratin has been reported {31567194; 31189998}. Tumours are generally negative for SOX10, HMB45, GFAP, CD34, and p63 {29309307; 31189998}. The lack of expression of chromogranin and synaptophysin {31934916} may assist in their distinction from neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs). Tumours with GLI1 amplification often have concomitant overexpression of CDK4, MDM2, and STAT6 {31934916}.

Cytology

Rarely reported {35514197}

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The presence of a GLI1 rearrangement or amplification, in the appropriate clinical and histomorphology context, is diagnostic. GLI1 rearrangement is not disease-defining and has been reported in plexiform fibromyxoma {27101025} and gastroblastoma {28731043}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: neoplasm arranged in nests and sheets of epithelioid to ovoid cells; prominent capillary vasculature; monomorphic round to ovoid nuclei; demonstration of GLI1 rearrangement or amplification.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most tumours pursue an indolent clinical course when completely excised. A subset is complicated by local or distant recurrence. Distant metastasis has been reported in approximately 20% of cases and includes spread to lung, lymph node, soft tissue and bone, and brain {29309307; 31189998; 31567194; 31934916}.
