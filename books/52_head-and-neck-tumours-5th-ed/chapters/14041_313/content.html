14.0.4.1 Li-Fraumeni syndrome

Definition

Li–Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) is an autosomal dominant cancer predisposition syndrome caused by constitutional pathogenic sequence variants of the TP53 gene and characterized by a predominance of soft tissue sarcomas, osteosarcoma, breast cancer, brain tumours, and adrenocortical carcinoma.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

151623 Li–Fraumeni syndrome; LFS

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Not recommended: sarcoma family syndrome of Li and Fraumeni.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LFS is associated with cancers of the breast, soft tissue, bone, brain, adrenal glands, and female genital tract. Reported cases in the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) LFS database with respect to the head and neck region involved the larynx, the parotid gland, the nasal cavity, and the nasopharynx; oral cancer may also occur.

Clinical features

LFS is characterized by the early onset of a broad spectrum of cancers and a high lifetime cancer risk. Breast and adrenocortical carcinomas, brain tumours (particularly choroid plexus carcinoma), leukaemia, and soft tissue and bone sarcomas are considered to be the core tumours, which constitute about 70% of LFS-related neoplasms {29076966}. Cancers of the head and neck region are very uncommon. The expanded clinical definition allows a broader recognition of individuals and families with LFS (see #40185Box 16.03) {26014290; 32457520}.

Epidemiology

Head and neck tumours and larynx tumours are very rare, accounting for 0.31% and 0.12% of cases, respectively (8 and 3 of 2591 included tumours, respectively) in version R20 (July 2019) of the TP53 Database [[The TP53 Database [Internet]. Bethesda (Maryland): National Cancer Institute (NCI) of the National Institutes of Health; 2019. Version R20, July 2019. Available from: https://tp53.isb-cgc.org.]] {27328919}.

Etiology

LFS is caused by constitutional pathogenic sequence variants of the gene TP53 {1978757; 2259385}. A broad spectrum of mutations involving the coding regions of the gene have been found in families with LFS, but 20–40% of individuals with LFS and the majority of families with LFS-like syndrome lack detectable mutations {14583457; 12619118}. The lack of 100% concordance between TP53 mutations and the classic LFS phenotype may be explained in several ways, including posttranslational alterations, complete deletion, the effects of modifier genes, and alterations of other genes influencing the phenotype generated by the presence of specific germline alterations {21779515}. Mutations may occur at specific hotspot codons that either interfere with DNA binding or disrupt the structure of the binding surface, thus interfering with its ability to modulate the transcription of target genes {20182602}. Missense mutations lead to a codon change, posing challenges to the functional interpretation of new variants {23161690}. Further mutations may occur outside the DNA-binding domain (DBD) and include rearrangements and deletions {27984644}. Future studies with novel sequencing technologies such as next-generation sequencing may be able to uncover a higher number of mutations in TP53, as well as mutations in other genes, in LFS and LFS-like syndrome {28509937}.

Pathogenesis

The activation of TP53, one of the most prominent tumour suppressor genes, activates protective cellular processes including cell-cycle arrest, apoptosis, and senescence to prevent the propagation of genetically altered cells {11099028}. Under normal conditions, p53 is maintained at low levels as a result of rapid turnover mediated by MDM2, its main negative regulator {28254861}. Cells from individuals with LFS exhibit genomic instability, telomere dysfunction, and spontaneous immortalization {23587008}. Mutant p53 isoforms vary in their abilities to inhibit wildtype p53 in a dominant negative fashion {12826609; 17311302}. Some even exhibit overt oncogenic qualities, although this gain-of-function mechanism is poorly understood {30224644}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not clinically relevant

Histopathology

Most tumours are squamous cell carcinomas, particularly those occurring in the larynx, pharynx, and oral cavity {27328919; 11120478}. Various sarcomas appear to be the most common head and neck malignancies in paediatric patients with LFS. In particular, rhabdomyosarcoma, mandibular osteosarcoma, synovial cell sarcoma, and pleomorphic myxoid liposarcoma have been reported {33971750; 32959210}.

Cytology

The cytopathology of LFS-associated tumours is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Guidelines for the selection of candidates for TP53 germline testing have recently been updated (see #40185Box 16.03).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: constitutional pathogenic sequence variants in TP53; appropriate family history.

Staging

Staging is performed according to the affected organ sites.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis and therapy varies according to the tumour types encountered.

Recommendation 1

All patients who meet the modified Chompret criteria should be tested for constitutional pathogenic TP53 variants:

·     Familial presentation: proband with a TP53 core tumour (breast cancer, soft tissue sarcoma, osteosarcoma, CNS tumour, adrenocortical carcinoma) before the age of 46 years AND at least one first-degree or second-degree relative with a core tumour before the age of 56 years; or

·     Multiple primitive tumours: proband with multiple tumours, including two TP53 core tumours, the first of which occurred before the age of 46 years, irrespective of family history; or

·     Rare tumours: patient with adrenocortical carcinoma, choroid plexus carcinoma, or rhabdomyosarcoma of embryonal anaplastic subtype, irrespective of family history; or

·     Very early onset breast cancer: breast cancer before the age of 31 years, irrespective of family history

Recommendation 2

Children and adolescents should be tested for constitutional pathogenic TP53 variants if presenting with:

·     Hypodiploid acute lymphoblastic leukaemia; or

·     Otherwise unexplained SHH-driven medulloblastoma; or

·     Jaw osteosarcoma

Recommendation 3

Patients who develop a second primary tumour within the radiotherapy field of a first core TP53 tumour that occurred before the age of 46 years should be tested for constitutional pathogenic TP53 variants.

Recommendation 4

a.   Patients older than 46 years presenting with breast cancer without personal or familial history fulfilling the Chompret criteria should not be tested for constitutional pathogenic TP53 variants.

b.  Any patient presenting with isolated breast cancer and not fulfilling the Chompret criteria, in whom a disease-causing TP53 variant has been identified, should be referred to an expert multidisciplinary team for discussion.

Recommendation 5

Children with any cancer from southern and south-eastern Brazilian families should be tested for the p.R337H Brazilian founder constitutional pathogenic TP53 variant.

aTesting for disease-causing TP53 variants should be performed before starting treatment in order to avoid in variant carriers, if possible, radiotherapy and genotoxic chemotherapy and to prioritize surgical treatments.
