8.0.2.5 Solitary fibrous tumour

Definition

Solitary fibrous tumour (SFT) is a fibroblastic tumour characterized by a prominent, branching, thin-walled, dilated (staghorn) vasculature and most commonly associated with NAB2::STAT6 rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

8815/0 Solitary fibrous tumour, benign

8815/1 Solitary fibrous tumour, NOS

8815/1 Lipomatous solitary fibrous tumour

8815/1 Giant cell–rich solitary fibrous tumour

8815/3 Solitary fibrous tumour, malignant

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH7E62 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Solitary fibrous tumour, NOS

2B5Y & XH1HP3 Other specified malignant mesenchymal neoplasms & Solitary fibrous tumour, malignant

Related terminology

Acceptable: extrapleural solitary fibrous tumour.

Not recommended: fibrous mesothelioma; haemangiopericytoma; giant cell angiofibroma; benign solitary fibrous tumour; glomangiopericytoma.

Subtype(s)

Lipomatous solitary fibrous tumour; giant cell–rich solitary fibrous tumour

Localization

These occur most commonly in the orbit, followed by the nasal cavity (~50%) and/or the paranasal sinuses; however, any site including the salivary glands, larynx, and oral cavity may be affected {26924782; 31206727; 28838212; 32529350; 29282671; 22002440; 20614325; 28877055; 26026772}.

Clinical features

Patients with SFT usually present with a painless, slow-growing mass. There may be visual changes, pain, and proptosis for orbital tumours {32529350}; obstructive symptoms, epistaxis, headaches, and discharge for sinonasal tumours {29282671}; and hoarseness, foreign body sensation, breathing difficulty, and voice changes for laryngeal lesions {20614325}. Paraneoplastic syndrome has been reported {16025074}.

Imaging

CT shows well-circumscribed tumours that strongly enhance with contrast. Tumours show enhancement with gadolinium contrast on T1-weighted MRI {17337142; 23413243}.

Epidemiology

About 15% of SFTs develop in the head and neck {11920476; 26924782; 31206727}. There is a male predominance (M:F ratio: 6:1) for laryngeal tumours, and an equal sex distribution and broad age range at presentation (20–80 years, median: ~50 years) for tumours at other sites {22002440; 29282671; 32529350; 31206727}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

SFTs almost always show a recurrent NAB2::STAT6 gene fusion {24513261; 24807787}. NAB2 fuses with STAT6 as a result of a paracentric inversion at chromosomal region 12q13.

Macroscopic appearance

In cavities, there is usually a polypoid, firm-fibrous, grey-white mass, possibly attached by a stalk. In soft tissue, the same firm masses are circumscribed, but unencapsulated if benign. Malignant tumours tend to be infiltrative and may have necrosis. The median size is smallest in the larynx (25 mm), intermediate in the salivary gland (40 mm), and largest in sinonasal tract sites (50 mm) {20614325; 32529350; 23413243; 29282671; 22002440}.

Histopathology

SFTs are unencapsulated masses containing a patternless distribution of hypocellular and hypercellular areas, intermingled with loose to dense keloid-like collagen. Sheets of uniform, bland, syncytial spindled cells are separated by dilated, angulated vascular spaces. Mitoses, although uncommon, aid in risk stratification. Myxoid change, fibrosis, and interstitial mast cells may be seen. Biologically aggressive features include increased cellularity, infiltrative borders, marked pleomorphism, tumour necrosis, and increased mitoses {26924782; 28838212; 31206727; 32529350}.

The lipomatous subtype contains mature adipocytes; the giant cell–rich subtype has tumour giant cells; both of these types are rare in the head and neck {28877055}.

Cells show nuclear reactivity for STAT6, a highly specific and sensitive marker {24030747; 24625420; 24977739; 25873501; 26811389}, along with CD34, BCL2, and CD99. Desmin, S100, SOX10, actins, and β-catenin are usually negative {32529350; 29282671; 22002440; 24977739; 25873501}. STAT6 and CD34 may be lost in malignant tumours {27189111; 30623305}.

Within the sinonasal tract, the differential diagnosis includes glomangiopericytoma, peripheral nerve sheath tumours, leiomyoma, desmoid-type fibromatosis, and biphenotypic sinonasal sarcoma; within the salivary gland, orbit, and oral cavity, the differential diagnosis is with myoepithelioma, pleomorphic adenoma, and nodular fasciitis.

Cytology

Smears are cellular, with loosely cohesive clusters around blood vessels or dispersed cells with bare nuclei {32686237; 15192959}. Round, oval, and spindled cells have scant cytoplasm with isomorphic ovoid nuclei {15192959}. Wispy collagenous stroma may be identified {32686237; 15192959; 28469319}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Because FISH is difficult to interpret, owing to the small size of the inversion, STAT6 immunohistochemistry and NAB2::STAT6 fusion analysis are more reliable {24030747; 30623305}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindled to ovoid cells arranged around a branching and hyalinized vasculature; variable stromal collagen deposition; nuclear STAT6 expression by immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: demonstration of NAB2::STAT6 gene fusion (in selected cases).

Staging

Multivariable risk stratification models are used instead of staging {31206727; 32529350}.

Prognosis and prediction

The majority are benign. Multiple risk models with variable clinicopathological features (age, size, cellularity, mitoses, necrosis, pleomorphism) can predict aggressive behaviour {22575866; 26924782; 28731041; 28838212}, although only one is optimized for orbital tumours {32529350}, and none for other head and neck sites. Recurrences are seen (in < 10% of cases), often with positive margins {29282671}, and metastases are rare. Secondary TERT promoter mutations and TP53 deletions or mutations {22026427; 26022454; 29985536} may be associated with more aggressive disease. Lungs, bones, and liver are the most frequent sites for metastasis.
