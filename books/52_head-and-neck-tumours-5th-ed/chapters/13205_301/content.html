13.2.0.5 Middle ear squamous cell carcinoma

Definition

Middle ear squamous cell carcinoma (MeSCC) is a carcinoma showing squamous differentiation arising from metaplastic squamous epithelium lining the middle ear.

ICD-O coding

8070/3 Squamous cell carcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C21.1 Squamous cell carcinoma of middle ear

Related terminology

Not recommended: epidermoid carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) arising primarily in the middle ear is rare. Direct extension from SCC of the pinna, nasopharynx, and/or the sinonasal tract is more common but is not discussed here {32366754} (see #24237Table 8.01).

Clinical features

Presenting findings include hearing loss, otorrhoea, otalgia, bleeding, tinnitus, vertigo, and facial nerve palsy {20513031; 26472473; 23553471; 24121777; 28692593}.

Epidemiology

MeSCC affects approximately 0.017 in 100 000 individuals {19373882; 25081186; 26472473; 28692593}. The mean age at presentation is 60 years (range: 21–89 years), with an equal sex distribution {15925819; 20513031; 24121777}. MeSCC is rarely bilateral {10077254; 11667995}.

Etiology

Predisposing factors include chronic otitis media and irradiation {25932267; 31890889}. Viral etiology and genetic alterations (such as DEK::AFF2) have been described {21493437; 32366754}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Late presentation is common, with tumour filling the middle ear and extending into mastoid air spaces {15925819; 24121777; 25081186; 26472473; 28692593}.

Histopathology

The histological features and immunohistochemical profile of MeSCC are similar to those of SCC at other sites, with pleomorphic epithelial cells arranged in sheets with a pavemented appearance, increased N:C ratio, and coarse nuclear chromatin. Most tumours are keratinizing and well to moderately differentiated. Non-keratinizing tumours may be EBV-associated or harbour genetic alterations (such as DEK::AFF2 rearrangements) {21493437; 32366754; 34049316}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunoreactivity for pancytokeratin, p40, p63, and CK5/6 is noted.

Differential diagnosis

Direct extension from external ear, nasopharynx, or sinonasal tract tumours should be excluded with clinical and radiological correlation, with EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER), NUT immunohistochemistry, or molecular testing for genetic rearrangements aiding in classification {21493437; 28060373; 34049316; 34108636}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically performed

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an infiltrating squamous epithelial proliferation with malignant cytological and architectural features; clinical exclusion of direct extension from adjacent sites.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for head and neck cutaneous SCC, although expert-derived modifications are suggested {30500288} [[Thompson LDR, Gupta R, Sandison A, et al. Ear and temporal bone tumours: histopathology reporting guide. 1st ed. Sydney (Australia): International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting; 2018.]].

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is generally poor because of advanced disease at presentation and delayed diagnosis {31890889}. Outcome is related to stage at presentation {23507994}, and the reported 5-year survival rate is between 25% (for high-volume disease) and 83% (for low-volume disease) {23202152; 23553471; 24121777; 24492135; 26472473}. Direct intracranial extension carries a poor prognosis. Nodal metastases are uncommon, affecting level I and III neck lymph nodes when identified {20513031}; distant metastases are rare.
