14.0.4.5 Bloom syndrome

Definition

Bloom syndrome is an autosomal recessive chromosomal breakage syndrome due to loss-of-function variants in the BLM gene, with distinct clinical features and the development of tumours including oropharyngeal carcinoma.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

210900 Bloom syndrome; BLM

ICD-11 coding

4A01.31 DNA repair defects other than combined T-cell or B-cell immunodeficiencies

Related terminology

Not recommended: Bloom–Torre–Machacek syndrome; congenital telangiectatic erythema.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

None

Clinical features

The most prominent and constant physical feature is the small size (height, weight, and head size). Also common are a lack of subcutaneous fat tissue, and a rash, most commonly on the cheeks and nose, usually first appearing during infancy after sun exposure {28846287}. A high-pitched voice and distinctive facial features (long, narrow face; prominent nose and ears; small lower jaw) can be observed. Most affected individuals have normal intellectual ability. Men tend to be infertile (azoospermia and oligospermia), whereas women may be fertile but often have early menopause. Individuals with Bloom syndrome have mild immune system abnormalities (often low IgM and IgA levels) leading to recurrent infections of the upper respiratory tract, ears, and lungs during infancy and are at greater-than-normal risk of chronic obstructive lung disease and diabetes mellitus resembling the adult-onset type. In addition, they are 150–300 times as likely to develop cancers as are normal individuals (66% solid tumours, 33% leukaemia/lymphoma). Colorectal cancer is the most common tumour in the Bloom syndrome registry (29 cases), followed by skin, breast, and oropharyngeal cancer (24 cases each) {17407155}.

Epidemiology

Bloom syndrome, first described in New York City in 1954 by dermatologist David Bloom, is extremely rare, with about 283 cases reported to the Bloom Syndrome Registry {17407155}. Although the overall prevalence is unknown, in the Ashkenazi Jewish population it is estimated to be approximately 1 case per 48 000 births. A founder mutation known as BLM(Ash) is present in approximately 1 in 100 people of Ashkenazi Jewish background. There are also founder mutations in the Slavic and Hispanic populations.

Etiology

Caused by mutations in the gene BLM, on 15q26.1, which encodes a member of the RECQ family of DNA helicases.

Pathogenesis

Loss of BLM function results in the accumulation of errors during DNA replication and increased chromosomal rearrangements and breakages, leading to genetic instability and predisposing individuals to cancer {33736941}.

Macroscopic appearance

Nonspecific

Diagnostic molecular pathology

When Bloom syndrome is clinically suspected, the detection of biallelic pathogenic variants in the BLM gene and/or the identification of an increased frequency of sister-chromatid exchanges in specialized cytogenetic studies may help establish the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinical features including small size and distinctive facial features; BLM mutation.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Cancer and related complications are the leading cause of death, on average occurring in the third decade of life {20301572}. Sun exposure to the face and other exposed areas should be avoided, particularly in infancy and early childhood, and exposure to ionizing radiation should be minimized. Cancer screening and surveillance are recommended {18359209}.
