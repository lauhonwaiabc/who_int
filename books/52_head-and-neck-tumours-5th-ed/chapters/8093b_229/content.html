8.0.9.3B Undifferentiated sarcoma

Definition

Undifferentiated sarcoma is a heterogeneous group of neoplasms without any identifiable line of differentiation as analysed by currently available techniques.

ICD-O coding

8805/3 Undifferentiated sarcoma

8801/3 Spindle cell sarcoma, undifferentiated

8802/3 Pleomorphic sarcoma, undifferentiated

8803/3 Round cell sarcoma, undifferentiated

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH73J4 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Giant cell sarcoma

2B5F.2 & XH0947 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Malignant fibrous histiocytoma

2B5F.2 & XH6HY6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Undifferentiated sarcoma

2B5F.2 & XH85G7 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Small cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant fibrous histiocytoma.

Subtype(s)

Spindle cell sarcoma, undifferentiated; pleomorphic sarcoma, undifferentiated; round cell sarcoma, undifferentiated

Localization

Lesions arise in the sinonasal tract, upper aerodigestive system, parotid gland region, and soft tissue of the neck {12778019; 25186315; 18853446; 23812657; 28629789}. They are usually subcutaneous or submucosal but occasionally arise in bone {12778019}.

Clinical features

There are no characteristic clinical features, although rapid growth is common. Rarely, regional or distant metastases are reported {12778019}.

Epidemiology

Undifferentiated sarcoma occurs in adults, with no sex predilection. Because of reporting differences, the incidence cannot be reliably determined {18343096; 25186315; 18853446; 23812657; 28629789}.

Etiology

Although the etiology is unknown in most tumours, radiation is a risk factor {12778019; 18853446}.

Pathogenesis

Undifferentiated sarcoma shows extensive and complex genetic aberrations {30889380; 20217954}. Genomic profiling may reveal that some seemingly undifferentiated sarcomas can be classified more specifically {19290004; 15221942; 12640106; 21412072}. See the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of this series for additional details [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]].

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours present as multilobulated grey-white, fleshy masses. The cut surface often shows haemorrhagic, myxoid, and/or necrotic changes {207408; 23812657; 28629789}.

Histopathology

Tumours are composed of an admixture of spindle, epithelioid, and pleomorphic cells set in a variably collagenized extracellular matrix. Cellularity varies. Pleomorphism, frequent mitotic figures (including atypical forms), histiocyte-like cells, and foamy cells, as well as giant tumour cells with enlarged, polylobulated nuclei, are commonly observed. Areas of tumour necrosis are often seen. Specific features of known tumour types are absent. The three subtypes listed above probably represent histological patterns, rather than distinctive subtypes.

Immunohistochemically, there may be limited SMA reactivity, but h-caldesmon, desmin, S100, and epithelial markers are not expressed. Histiocytic antigens do not aid interpretation.

Undifferentiated sarcoma is a diagnosis of exclusion. All other potential mimics should be ruled out, including rhabdomyosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, liposarcoma, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, high-grade myxofibrosarcoma, high-grade osteosarcoma, other distinctly characterized round cell sarcomas, carcinoma, melanoma, and lymphoma {20217954}.

Cytology

Not clinically reliable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: malignancy with pleomorphic high-grade morphology; exclusion of other entities.

Desirable: an absence of distinctive molecular aberrations (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Intermediate survival rates (60–70%) are reported {1624288; 25186315}. Previous radiation exposure is reported as an adverse prognostic factor for disease-free survival {18853446}.
