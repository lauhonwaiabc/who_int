14.0.6.1 Brooke-Spiegler syndrome

Definition

Brooke–Spiegler syndrome (BSS) is an inherited autosomal dominant disease characterized by multiple spiradenomas, cylindromas, spiradenocylindromas, and trichoepitheliomas (cribriform trichoblastomas), with salivary gland involvement by basal cell adenoma (BCA), membranous type.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

605041 Brooke–Spiegler syndrome; BRSS

132700 Cylindromatosis, familial

ICD-11 coding

2F22 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial cylindromatosis; CYLD cutaneous syndrome; multiple familial trichoepithelioma.

Not recommended: turban tumours; skin–salivary gland tumour diathesis.

Subtype(s)

Multiple familial trichoepithelioma

Localization

Spiradenoma, cylindroma, and spiradenocylindroma mainly involve the skin of the head and neck area. Trichoepitheliomas predominantly affect the nasolabial folds. Salivary gland tumours mostly occur in the parotid gland (membranous-type BCA), whereas submandibular glands are rarely affected {19455704}. Extremely rare is the occurrence of mammary cylindroma {15252315}.

A phenotypic variant of BSS is multiple familial trichoepithelioma (MFT, MIM number: 601606), characterized by the development of trichoepitheliomas without other tumours {19730223; 23249834}.

Clinical features

Spiradenomas, cylindromas, and spiradenocylindromas occur as multiple (dozens to hundreds), often coalescing, variably sized (mostly 5–30 mm) nodules that may be skin-coloured, reddish, or blue. Trichoepitheliomas are bilateral, small (2–10 mm), often confluent, skin-coloured papules. Cutaneous tumours demonstrate growth throughout life, and their number increases with age. Rapid growth, ulceration, and bleeding, especially in older patients, usually indicate malignant transformation, especially for cylindroma, spiradenoma, and spiradenocylindroma (~5–10% of cases). Parotid neoplasms present as a freely movable mass usually measuring 5–50 mm in diameter. Multifocal unilateral involvement of the parotid gland as well as bilateral metachronous and synchronous parotid neoplasms can be seen. Facial nerve paralyses or visual alterations are rare {19194280; 19730223; 23249834}.

Epidemiology

BSS and MFT are rare, but their exact incidence rates are unknown. About 1–2% of patients with BSS develop salivary gland tumours, whereas patients with MFT appear to have skin lesions only. Cutaneous tumours usually occur around puberty, whereas salivary gland involvement is usually seen after the age of 40 years {23249834}.

Etiology

BSS results from a constitutional, heterozygous pathogenic sequence variant in CYLD in a proband, manifesting with multiple skin tumours and membranous-type BCA {31093340; 32298062}.

Pathogenesis

Most patients (80–85% of those with the classic BSS phenotype and 40–50% of those with the MFT phenotype) harbour constitutional pathogenic sequence variants in the CYLD gene, a tumour suppressor gene located on chromosome 16q12-q13 {10835629; 26329847; 16922728}. More than 100 CYLD mutations have been reported. Most mutations (~50%) are frameshift, about 25% are nonsense, about 15% are missense, and about 10% are putative splice-site mutations and are predicted to result in truncated proteins. Most mutations involve exons 9–20, whereas exons 4–8 are spared {20132422; 20502185}. Other rare mechanisms include large deletions in CYLD and mutations in intronic sequences leading to intronic exonization {19668078; 25347032}. Cutaneous neoplasms demonstrate a large spectrum of somatic mutations representing both loss of heterozygosity and sequence alterations in CYLD {26861065}.

Macroscopic appearance

Benign cutaneous and salivary gland tumours are usually circumscribed, unencapsulated, ovoid to round nodules with a smooth surface. In larger lesions (especially spiradenomas), the cut surface may show foci of haemorrhage and cystic change {22978388}.

Membranous-type BCAs in BSS are usually multicentric and bilateral. These tumours have a solid homogeneous tan cut surface {25141971}.

Histopathology

Salivary gland involvement in this syndrome is characterized by the presence of multicentric BCA, which appears identical to cutaneous cylindroma. These tumours are usually bilateral/multicentric, well circumscribed, and characteristically of the membranous-type growth pattern. The tumour nests show the characteristic peripheral palisading of dark cells, with paler cells located more centrally within these nests. The cells have vesicular nuclei. The cell nests are separated by a dense hyaline stroma {19455704; 28060371}. By immunohistochemistry, both epithelial and myoepithelial markers are present {23209336; 24206768}.

Membranous-type BCAs are distinguished from cellular pleomorphic adenomas by their consistent basaloid appearance, peripheral palisading, and the presence of thick hyaline basement membranes. BCAs are distinguished from basal cell adenocarcinoma by the absence of invasion {25141971}.

Spiradenoma, cylindroma, and spiradenocylindroma are related tumours, usually well demarcated, multinodular, or uninodular, with regular or somewhat pushing borders. Spiradenoma is composed of small basaloid cells arranged in a trabecular, reticular, or solid fashion intermixed with paler cells and lymphocytes (an integral component of the tumour). A few tubular structures are often present. Droplets of PAS-positive hyaline basal membrane material can be seen. Cylindroma in most cases displays a jigsaw puzzle (mosaic-like) arrangement of islands of basaloid cells surrounded by variably thickened eosinophilic basement membrane material. The peripheral cells often show palisading and are usually darker than the centrally located cells. Intratumoural lymphocytes are absent or very few in number. Spiradenocylindroma combines areas of cylindroma and spiradenoma that are either closely intermingled or sharply segregated. The ratio between spiradenomatous and cylindromatous components varies among cases.

Malignant neoplasms evolving from cutaneous tumours include low-grade and high-grade lesions with a basaloid morphology resembling salivary gland basal cell adenocarcinomas. Less common are adenocarcinoma NOS and sarcomatoid (metaplastic) carcinoma {19633533; 22588548}.

Cytology

Membranous-type BCA is composed of monomorphic small basaloid cells with scant cytoplasm in a clean background and with a dense hyaline stroma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of a constitutional heterozygous pathogenic variant in CYLD establishes the diagnosis. The children of an individual with the variant have a 50% chance of inheriting it {32298062}. No genotype–phenotype correlations have been found with respect to the severity of the disease, the possibility of malignant transformation, and the development of extracutaneous lesions {15854031}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the diagnosis of BSS is established in a proband with multiple skin tumours (multiple inherited and histologically confirmed cutaneous cylindromas, spiradenomas, spiradenocylindromas, and/or trichoepitheliomas); and/or identification of a constitutional heterozygous pathogenic variant in CYLD by molecular genetic testing.

Desirable: the syndrome should be suspected with a salivary gland multicentric, membranous-type BCA in a patient with single cylindroma, trichoepithelioma, or spiradenoma; cases with few such lesions (≤ 4) are usually sporadic and not associated with a germline CYLD mutation {22588548}.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Salivary gland membranous-type BCA is benign. Spiradenoma, cylindroma, and spiradenocylindroma are benign lesions. Malignant tumours developing from these neoplasms may show local aggressive growth or distant metastases. Because of their rarity and microscopic heterogeneity, the exact prognosis is unknown.
