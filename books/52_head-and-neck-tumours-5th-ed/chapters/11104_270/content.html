11.1.0.4 Dermoid and teratoid cysts

Definition

A dermoid cyst is a developmental cystic lesion composed of ectodermal and mesodermal tissues, typically including epidermis-like squamous epithelium and cutaneous adnexal structures. A teratoid cyst includes endodermal tissues.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DA05.Y Other specified cysts of oral or facial-neck region & Dermoid cyst of mouth

Related terminology

Acceptable: dermoid; cystic dermoid; cystic teratoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately 7% of all dermoid cysts are found in the head and neck region {32884763}, most commonly in the periorbital region, as well as in the orbit, ear, dorsum of the nose, floor of the mouth / submental region, and the mid-neck region.

Clinical features

Patients present with a nonspecific, firm, palpable mass. They may present with a cutaneous pit secreting sebaceous material and with protruding hair follicles.

Imaging

Imaging studies are recommended for ruling out osseous or intracranial extension. In the floor of the mouth / submandibular region, imaging may reveal a well-defined lesion, classically containing intracystic corpuscles (the sack-of-marbles sign), that is hypodense on CT, hyperintense on T1-weighted MRI and low-signal on T2-weighted MRI due to lipid content. Occasionally these “marbles” may be calcified. Such imaging features permit differentiation from simple cystic lesions (e.g. ranula) in these anatomical sites {21918039}.

Epidemiology

There is an equivocal sex predilection (a possible slight female preponderance). These cysts are often present from birth, with approximately 70% diagnosed in patients aged < 5 years {25439551; 30466230}.

Etiology

Dermoid and teratoid cysts are developmental cystic lesions. There is no reported genetic etiology.

Pathogenesis

The cysts most commonly arise due to entrapped ectoderm and mesoderm along planes of embryonic fusion.

Macroscopic appearance

These are cystic lesions as large as 120 mm and commonly filled with keratinaceous debris {32884763}.

Histopathology

The dermoid cyst wall is composed of keratinized, epidermis-like squamous epithelium. Abundant desquamated keratin or hair follicles may be noted within the cystic lumen. Cutaneous adnexal structures are present in the underlying connective tissues and possibly attached to the cyst wall, and they may include hair follicles or sebaceous, eccrine, or apocrine glandular elements. Teratoid cyst includes endodermal tissues, to potentially include gastrointestinal or respiratory epithelium, or glandular tissues to include pancreatic or salivary tissue.

Cytology

The cysts may contain desquamated anucleate and nucleated squamous epithelial cells and keratin debris {10352915}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

Dermoid cyst: a squamous epithelium–lined cystic lesion with associated adnexal structures, possibly including hair follicles and glandular elements (sebaceous, eccrine, and/or apocrine).

Teratoid cyst: same as above, but including endodermal tissues such as respiratory or gastrointestinal mucosa.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence is unusual.
