8.0.2.7 Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

Definition

Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma is a rarely metastasizing mesenchymal neoplasm, often having fibromatosis-like features, which tends to arise in the head and neck region.

ICD-O coding

8825/3 Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH2668 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: myofibrosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common site is the tongue, followed by the larynx and gingiva {33302922}. Rarely these tumours arise in the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses. They are mostly deeply seated; however, an origin in subcutaneous and submucosal sites does not exclude the diagnosis. Origin in the bone, notably the gnathic bones, can occur {31950474; 33302922; 9777985; 15173943; 24132324}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a slowly growing, painless mass. The average tumour size is 40 mm {9777985; 33302922}.

Epidemiology

Patients can be affected at any age (median: 42 years) {33302922; 9777985; 15173943}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Complex genetic aberrations have been identified, but the pathogenesis is unknown {10362810; 31950474}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are poorly marginated, grey-white, and firm, with a fibrous cut surface {9777985}.

Histopathology

The pattern is that of a cellular, fibromatosis-like or fibrosarcoma-like lesion composed of fascicles or broad sheets of cells, with or without focal herringbone or storiform arrangement. Checkerboard-like infiltration of the adjacent skeletal muscle is a key diagnostic feature. The atypical myofibroblastic cells have enlarged, tapered, hyperchromatic nuclei and a scant or moderate amount of cytoplasm. Mitotic figures are variably present. The background can be collagenous or myxoid. Transformation into high-grade sarcoma has been reported {9777985; 24132324; 15173943}. There is a myofibroblastic immunophenotype showing variable expression of SMA, desmin, calponin, and CD34. H-caldesmon is reported to be focally expressed in a few cases. Expression of β-catenin does not rule out this tumour type {9777985; 15173943; 31950474; 17711447}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant {31950474}

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffusely infiltrative growth, often between skeletal muscle fibres; cellular fascicles of spindle cells with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm; at least focally moderate nuclear atypia; variable expression of SMA and/or desmin.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrences are common, but metastatic spread to lung, soft tissue, and bone, often after a long interval, occurs rarely {9777985}.
