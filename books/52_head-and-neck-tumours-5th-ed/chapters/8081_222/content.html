8.0.8.1 Neurofibroma

Definition

Neurofibroma is a benign peripheral nerve sheath tumour consisting of differentiated Schwann cells, fibroblasts, perineurial-like cells, and residual interspersed axons set in a fibromyxoid matrix.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Ancient neurofibroma; cellular neurofibroma; plexiform neurofibroma; atypical neurofibroma

Localization

Neurofibromas most commonly affect the skin, but deep neck lesions are seen (carotid space, brachial plexus, posterior/paraspinal). Any head and neck site may be affected {25503638; 27402223; 24315214; 23601771; 7127260}. Intraorally, the tongue is the most common location {25654048; 33196600; 31675118; 31598201; 31111316; 29931661; 25503638}. Diffuse neurofibromas arise in the skin and subcutis, and plexiform neurofibromas arise superficially or deep along nerves with the orbitotemporal region most commonly affected {33196600}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a slowly growing, often circumscribed, and sometimes painful mass. Motor or sensory symptoms develop when major nerve trunks are affected. Disfigurement and compression of vital structures may be seen. Multiple and plexiform neurofibromas are frequently associated with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) {22606860; 9267819; 22420725}.

Epidemiology

About 15% of neurofibromas arise in the head and neck region {33196600; 33723760}. Neurofibroma is the most common benign peripheral nerve sheath tumour. It affects patients of all ages, but most commonly arises in the second to fourth decades of life {22420725; 22327363; 33196600}. All ethnic groups are affected and there is no sex predilection {25654048}. Plexiform neurofibroma, specifically in patients with NF1, presents in childhood {33196600; 33723760}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Inactivating mutations of the tumour suppressor gene NF1, encoding neurofibromin (NF1), are the sole recurring abnormality identified in the Schwann cell population {22420725; 33723760}, although microenvironmental factors also play a role in tumour development {30728335; 29596064; 27171146}.

Specific criteria for atypical neurofibroma / atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential are usually seen in the setting of NF1 and are strongly associated with deletions of CDKN2A/CDKN2B {21987445; 26857854; 30722027; 29774626}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions are nodular, with a tan-white, glistening cut surface. Occasionally, an associated nerve can be identified {22327363; 22420725}. Plexiform neurofibromas show a complex, tortuous, and convoluted shape {33723760}. Diffuse neurofibromas are plaque-like lesions {22327363}.

Histopathology

Neurofibromas are localized intraneurally and/or extraneurally. They are most often nodular and uncommonly diffuse or plexiform. Lesions are unencapsulated and characterized by a random distribution of spindly cells in a myxoid to collagenous stroma. Nuclei are wavy and tapered. The cytoplasm is inconspicuous. Mitotic figures are usually absent. Entrapped ganglion cells may also be seen. The collagen bundles resemble shredded carrots. Mast cells are frequently found {22327363; 33723760; 33588442}.

Subtypes

Diffuse neurofibromas show Meissner corpuscles and can entrap pre-existent structures {22327363}.

Plexiform neurofibroma shows involvement of several cross-sections of peripheral nerve fascicles surrounded by perineurium in a localized area.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemically, neurofibromas show a heterogeneous and variable expression of S100 and SOX10, interspersed with fibroblasts or perineurial cells positive for CD34 and EMA, respectively. NFP highlights entrapped axons {22420725; 22495377; 22327363}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnoses include schwannoma, traumatic neuroma, palisaded encapsulated neuroma, mucosal neuroma of multiple endocrine neoplasia, desmoplastic melanoma, and dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (when superficially located) {22327363}.

Increased mitotic figures, increased cellularity, and pleomorphism may suggest malignant transformation {33588442}.

Cytology

FNA specimens are often paucicellular due to the collagen matrix, but a mucin-rich background and small spindled cells with thin, wavy nuclei can aid in diagnosis {10459049; 21938166}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an unencapsulated, low-cellularity spindle cell neoplasm associated with a variably fibromyxoid background with shredded carrot–like collagen.

Desirable: expression of S100, SOX10, CD34, EMA, NFP in an appropriate cell population (in selected cases).

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours are consistently benign, but neurofibromas in patients with NF1 have the potential for malignant transformation {22327363; 23036231; 25929351; 22420725}.
