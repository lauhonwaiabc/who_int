15.0.1.1 Metastases to the head and neck region

Definition

Metastatic tumours are neoplasms that involve various head and neck sites as a result of lymphatic or vascular spread from non-contiguous primary malignancies. Haematolymphoid tumours are excluded by definition.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2D60.0 Malignant neoplasm metastasis in lymph nodes of head, face, or neck

2E2Z Malignant neoplasm metastasis, unspecified

2D8Y Malignant neoplasm metastasis in parotid gland and in oral cavity

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Neck metastases are to cervical lymph nodes; soft tissues metastases rarely occur, particularly for non-epithelial neoplasms. The most common squamous cell carcinoma metastases to the neck depend on anatomical site: level II from the oropharynx, and level III from the larynx. Upper and mid neck node metastases arise from other head and neck primary sites; lower neck metastases are from infraclavicular primary tumours. The Virchow node (left supraclavicular) receives metastases from abdominal and pelvic malignancies {7646330}. Thyroid carcinomas metastasize to the central compartment before the lateral cervical nodes {23148663}. Local extension by metastatic malignancy into adjacent soft tissues occurs most often with lymph node deposits originating from a variety of cancers where extranodal extension is a known adverse prognostic factor {12570052}.

Oral cavity metastases occur with similar frequency in oral soft tissues and jawbones {31570205}. The tooth-bearing gingiva is most commonly involved, although any subsite can be affected {18061527; 23646853; 27891304; 31570205}.

Major salivary gland metastases involve the parotid most commonly {16444748; 17113505; 29549903}, with other sites rarely involved {20596975}. A high percentage of metastases are to intraglandular and periglandular lymph nodes rather than the glandular parenchyma.

In the nasopharynx, the posterior nasopharyngeal wall and the fossa of Rosenmüller are affected {23924557; 26541404; 32728442}.

Pharynx metastases are mostly to the base of the tongue and the palatine tonsils {668170; 20596975}, and usually on the left {668170}, although bilateral cases occur {1624294; 17657505}.

Sinonasal tract metastases frequently involve the maxillary sinus followed by the sphenoid, ethmoid, and frontal sinuses, with involvement of multiple paranasal sites synchronously or metachronously being possible {11602931}.

In the ear, the petrous apex is most commonly affected (in one third of cases), followed by the internal auditory canal, mastoid, middle ear, and external auditory canal {1869438; 1919860; 1991061; 2995524; 4328134; 4938079; 7964154; 8892576; 9509102; 10680873; 11945183; 15768802; 18754073; 24077267; 26545469; 26545474; 29909612}.

Mucosal involvement is more common in larynx metastases; spread to the laryngeal cartilages is usually to ossified areas {26743607}.

Metastases to maxillofacial bones involve the mandible (molar area) ≥ 3 times as often as the maxilla; both jaws are affected in 5% of cases {7815371; 17138711; 25409855; 23798834; 19304027}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation is highly variable and dependent on the anatomical subsite involved. Most metastatic deposits present as a mass.

Neck nodes / soft tissue: Painful and non-painful, single or multiple, solid and/or cystic masses.

Oral cavity: An exophytic, ulcerated and non-ulcerated, asymptomatic or symptomatic mass with swelling, pain, bleeding, toothache, altered tooth mobility, numbness with bone involvement, and halitosis {31146958}. Metastases are an inflammatory mimic when associated with dental implants {22670251; 23646853; 27720649}.

Salivary glands: A preauricular, facial, or submandibular mass, with facial pain, trismus, tenderness, numbness, and facial nerve paralysis {12820326; 20091686; 26245749}.

Pharynx: Swelling, dysphagia, nasal obstruction, tonsillitis, sinusitis, postnasal drip, pain, cranial nerve motor deficits {32728442}, and asphyxiation {21841958}.

Sinonasal tract: Nasal obstruction, headache, epistaxis, facial pain, visual disturbances, facial swelling, and cranial nerve deficits.

Ear / temporal bone: Hearing loss, facial palsy and/or ear pain {21854691}, haemorrhage or compression of adjacent structures {379358; 2019918; 9509102; 11945183}, and Collet–Sicard syndrome (paralysis of cranial nerves IX–XII) {7407020; 15885949; 16929891}. One third of cases are detected incidentally during primary tumour staging.

Larynx: Dysphonia, persistent cough, difficulty swallowing, haemoptysis, and sore throat.

Maxillofacial bones: Pain (70%), paraesthesia, swelling, exophytic growth, and mobile teeth {7815371; 17138711; 25409855}.

Epidemiology

Head/neck metastases may occur after a short or prolonged (years) interval after the initial discovery of the primary tumour {27844410; 30273434; 27218239}. True carcinoma of unknown primary accounts for < 3% of head and neck cancers {25804376; 10799723; 17657782; 33244460}.

Neck lymph node metastases mostly originate from head and neck sites {23870760}; cystic nodal metastasis predicts a tonsil, tongue base, or thyroid primary {25804376; 12478648}. Metastatic carcinoma of unknown primary is more common in older patients (aged 55–65 years) and men {22034046}. Breast and lung carcinomas appear in supraclavicular nodes {10208667; 27844410}; pelvic/intra-abdominal metastases favour the Virchow node {7646330}. Less common carcinomas include those of the cervix, oesophagus, ovary, and pancreas {10208667}.

Oral cavity metastases (1% of all oral malignancies) {18061527} are the sentinel event in about one third of cases {23646853; 25409855}. Tumour type is sex-dependent (breast, kidney, and skin carcinoma in women, and lung carcinoma in men {27891304; 31570205}), and most patients are in their sixth or seventh decade of life {18061527}. Tongue metastases are from lung and renal cell tumours {27891304}.

Salivary gland metastases account for 4–24% of salivary gland tumours {28001329}, are increasing in incidence {22281605; 28872155}, and affect male patients predominantly. Parotid metastases arise from cutaneous head/neck malignancies (squamous cell carcinoma, melanoma, Merkel cell carcinoma, and basal cell carcinoma {3562340; 28534378; 33544379}; melanoma is second only to squamous carcinoma {17141071; 28872155}). Less common metastases include sebaceous carcinoma {25521100} and carcinomas of the lung, breast, kidney, stomach, nasopharynx, and prostate {9578259; 19186459; 25129420; 26416080; 29212872; 29449729}.

About 1% of pharyngeal malignancies are metastases {668170; 20596975; 23110013}; they are typically associated with disseminated disease {17331151; 20596975} and affect middle-aged adults {20596975}.

Sinonasal tract metastasis is rare {22705221; 27218239}. It occurs more frequently in elderly patients and is slightly more common in male patients. Renal cell carcinoma is the most common, followed by carcinomas of the lung, breast, prostate, and thyroid {11602931; 20596975; 27649110; 30273434}. Metastatic hepatocellular carcinoma and gastrointestinal adenocarcinoma are common in certain geographical areas, such as Taiwan, China {31010194}.

Metastases to the ear / temporal bone are rare (< 2%, increasing to 20% in autopsy studies {379358}) and include breast and uterine carcinoma in women, and prostate adenocarcinoma in men {1869438; 1919860; 1991061; 2995524; 4328134; 4938079; 7964154; 8892576; 9509102; 10680873; 11945183; 15768802; 18754073; 24077267; 26545469; 26545474; 29909612}.

Metastases account for < 0.5% of laryngeal malignancies. They occur more frequently with increasing age and are twice as common in male patients. Melanoma is the most common, followed by carcinomas of the kidney, lung, breast, colon, and prostate. Metastatic sarcoma is extremely rare {3655540; 20596975; 21500157; 26743607; 30119910}.

Breast carcinoma is the most common malignancy to metastasize to maxillofacial bones (< 1% of head/neck metastatic bone disease), followed by metastatic lung, colorectal, and renal cancers {7815371; 17138711; 25409855}.

Etiology

HPV and EBV are provable carcinogenic factors and represent a significant etiological component of carcinoma of unknown primary {33244460}. Of these, HPV-associated oropharyngeal cancer is the predominant virus-induced head and neck cancer in North America and much of Europe {34066342; 31893516}. EBV infection exists in nearly 100% of non-keratinizing nasopharyngeal carcinomas and is widely predominant in southern China {28893937}. A statistically significant association exists between HCV infection and cancers of the oral cavity, oropharynx, and larynx {32599500}.

Pathogenesis

Pathways of metastatic spread include haematogenous dissemination via the pulmonary circulation or via the paravertebral plexus of Batson, bypassing the lungs {17857618}. Lymphatic spread is either by anterograde flow or, in the case of the palatine tonsil that lacks afferent vessels, retrograde flow {443718}. Another possible route is direct implantation after local trauma associated with instrumentation (e.g. endoscopy) {443718; 8730375; 17657505}.

As the most common cause of head and neck metastases, squamous carcinoma is a multistep process caused by genomic instability secondary to a variety of key genetic pathways, leading to epithelial dysplasia, followed by the penetration of an epithelial basement membrane and the invasion of the angiolymphatic system, thus empowering malignant cells to metastatically disseminate {33243986; 34353444; 34518141}. Tumour dissemination seems to depend on both pre-existing lymphatics and lymphangiogenesis within the tumour or at the tumour periphery {17160713}.

Macroscopic appearance

Metastases range from microscopic to large, clinically evident masses. They may be single or multiple, ulcerated, and solid or cystic (or both), and they may possess variable necrosis, haemorrhage, and/or calcification {18383529; 31145296}.

Histopathology

Correlation with clinical, imaging, and serological findings is generally required along with tumour histopathology for a definitive diagnosis. Histopathology frequently mirrors the primary tumour phenotype; grade progression may occur. Metastatic squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and melanoma are most frequent. Selective and targeted immunohistochemistry may be required to support a specific phenotype or an origin from a specific anatomical site (see #27219Table 14.01). High-grade transformation (dedifferentiation) is a component of some neoplasms producing metastases whose histopathology differs from the primary tumour {21876843; 23821210; 26245749}.

Cytology

FNA functions as a highly accurate, efficient, and cost-effective triage and diagnostic tool. This is particularly true for salivary gland, lymph node, and soft tissue masses {7646330; 17041956; 24935698; 25656853; 28001329; 28032725; 28371507; 33017519}. Because of its high sensitivity (83–97%) and specificity (91–100%) for the diagnosis of metastatic lesions {18528906; 22034046; 30488579}, FNA is often recommended as the initial diagnostic modality {22034046}. A false negative rate as high as 42% may be seen with cystic lesions {22034046}. Cytological features are dependent on tumour type. Ancillary studies (immunohistochemistry, in situ hybridization, and molecular analysis) can frequently be performed from FNA specimens {24478259; 28727266; 30600323; 32507626}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Selective molecular testing may confirm specific tumour types.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a malignant neoplasm at a region that is distant from a primary tumour site.

Desirable: a known primary tumour; clinical, imaging, and serological correlation (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for the primary site.

Prognosis and prediction

Metastatic disease, particularly with extranodal extension {2030629; 12570052; 26514096; 31077394}, is indicative of high clinical stage, poor prognosis, and generally reduced survival {12244407; 15805872; 20592390; 20596975; 20596978; 23646853; 26743607}.
