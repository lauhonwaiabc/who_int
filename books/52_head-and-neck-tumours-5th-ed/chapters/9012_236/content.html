9.0.1.2 IgG4-related disease

Definition

IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a systemic, immune-mediated, mass-forming lesion characterized by an IgG4+ plasma cell–rich lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis, and (typically) elevated serum IgG4.

ICD-O coding

9760/1 IgG4-related disease

ICD-11 coding

4A43.0 IgG4-related disease

Related terminology

Not recommended: IgG4-related autoimmune disease; IgG4-related sclerosing disease; Küttner tumour; pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

IgG4-RD affects single or multiple organs simultaneously or metachronously (see #24693Table 10.02). In the head and neck region, the orbit and submandibular salivary gland are most frequently affected, followed by the cervical lymph nodes, skin, and soft tissue. The upper aerodigestive tract (sinonasal area, oral cavity, laryngopharyngeal mucosa), ear, skull base, and thyroid can be affected {30612117; 27658185; 26686205; 33392765; 33167472; 25204708; 24115385}.

Clinical features

The presentation is subacute. Fatigue and weight loss are common, but fever is unusual. Pre-existing allergic symptoms are common. Clinical manifestations in the head and neck region include mass lesions (submandibular mass) and localized effects in the affected sites such as nasal obstruction, facial swelling, pain, epistaxis, destruction of nasal structures, dysphonia, dyspnoea, stridor, hearing loss, vestibular dysfunction, and headache {26194097; 26686205; 33192112; 33167472; 33325728; 24682733}.

Serum IgG4 is elevated in the majority of cases; this may correlate with the number of organs involved and disease activity, but it is not very specific for IgG4-RD. Tests for serum IgG4:IgG ratio and IgG4:IgG RNA ratio may perform better {32546500; 24651618; 22536256; 32552502}. Mild peripheral eosinophilia and elevation of IgE, ESR, and antinuclear antibodies, as well as positive rheumatoid factor and hypocomplementaemia, are common, but disease-specific markers such as antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA), SSA, SSB, and dDNA are negative.

Imaging

Imaging studies are useful to confirm the primary site of the disease and to detect multiorgan involvement. FDG avidity may reflect disease activity {33516733}.

Epidemiology

IgG4-RD is uncommon. It typically affects patients in late adulthood (fifth and sixth decades of life) with an M:F ratio of 1.6:1 for the head and neck region and 4:1 for other sites. Paediatric cases exist but are rare {32546500; 27012661}. No environmental or racial factors have been found.

Etiology

IgG4-RD is associated with chronic antigen stimulation by self-antigens (ANXA11, laminin-511, galectin-3, prohibitin) or unidentified antigens in genetically susceptible individuals with impaired immunological tolerance {29852256; 28765476; 30089633; 25932630; 31104539; 30081155}.

Pathogenesis

Recent studies suggest that chronic antigen stimulation and a breach of immunotolerance leads to oligoclonal expansion of circulating plasmablasts and CD4+ cytotoxic T cells. Increased T2 follicular helper cells promote plasmablast differentiation and IgG4 isotype switching, and they contribute to germinal-centre formation in the affected sites {28166682; 24815737; 27411315}. CD4+ cytotoxic T cells, IgG4+ plasmablasts, and the locally recruited M2 macrophages in the affected sites express profibrotic cytokines leading to fibroblastic activation and collagen production {32939060}. IgG4 probably plays an anti-inflammatory role in an unsuccessful attempt to dampen the aberrant immune response.

Macroscopic appearance

The involved salivary gland shows a lobulated tan cut surface. Mucosal lesions in the upper aerodigestive tract feature an exophytic granular or nodular mass, usually with an intact surface.

Histopathology

A lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate rich in IgG4+ plasma cells and storiform fibrosis are cardinal features of IgG4-RD, where the former often predominates in the early (inflammatory) phase of the disease and the latter in the late (fibrotic) phase. Elevation of both the number of IgG4+ cells (see #24693Table 10.02) and the IgG4+:IgG+ ratio (> 40%) is required to render this feature specific {22596100}. Obliterative phlebitis is characterized by occlusion of the venous lumen by a fibroinflammatory infiltrate, but this feature is less frequently encountered in the head and neck region {23068303; 21107087}. Necrosis, abscess, neutrophilic infiltration, necrotizing vasculitis, and granulomas are uncommon in IgG4-RD. However, perifollicular granulomas may be seen {32889888}.

The salivary gland lesions are non-circumscribed and show patchy atrophy of the acini, periductal fibrosis, a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, reactive lymphoid follicles, and an eosinophilic infiltrate. Focal involvement of the gland is the norm. The lobular architecture is accentuated by fibrosis of the interlobular septa.

Lesions in the upper aerodigestive tract show a mucosa expanded with IgG4+ cell–rich lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate and storiform fibrosis, but obliterative phlebitis is uncommon. Destruction of the surrounding bone is common, and perineural extension can be seen {21546462}. Inner ear lesions can result from extension of middle ear disease or involvement by skull base pachymeningitis {33325728; 31841234}.

Eosinophilic angiocentric fibrosis is a rare entity characterized by eosinophilic vasculitis, lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, and concentric perivascular stromal fibrosis {25065665; 30289267}. Some cases show increased IgG4+ plasma cells and serum IgG4 {21502911; 32342944}. Whether it may represent an IgG4-RD is still controversial because of its lack of response to steroids and inconsistent elevation of the IgG4+:IgG+ cell ratio in the reported cases {33064012; 27281484}.

Cytology

FNAs of lesions involved by IgG4-RD are usually of low to moderate cellularity. In the salivary gland, there may be scattered ductal structures, often enveloped by collagen bundles, a paucity or absence of acini, and isolated fragments of fibrous stroma in a background rich in a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate {11791589; 27666423}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with increased IgG4+ cells (see #24693Table 10.02) and an IgG4+:IgG+ ratio of > 40%, with storiform fibrosis; exclusion of known entities such as ANCA-associated vasculitis, rheumatoid arthritis, multicentric Castleman disease, Rosai–Dorfman disease, inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, and chronic infection.

Desirable: obliterative phlebitis; clinically compatible features (IgG4-RD in other body sites, elevated serum IgG4, and response to steroid/rituximab therapy).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

IgG4-RD follows a remitting and relapsing course. Although highly responsive to steroids, it can result in organ dysfunction and even death if not properly diagnosed and treated {25155229; 26453089; 17414116}. Localized disease such as IgG4-related sialadenitis may be controlled by surgery with or without steroids. Multiorgan disease, high baseline IgG4, IgE, and peripheral eosinophilia are risk factors for relapse. Maintenance therapy with steroids, immunomodulators, and biological agents like rituximab has been advocated {33689549}. An increased incidence of malignancy, including carcinoma and lymphoma, has been observed in patients with IgG4-RD {25155229; 31872350; 26472416; 32188869; 28544157}, but the causal relationship with cancer is still controversial {25881845; 24492683; 32552502}.

Orbit, including lacrimal glands

 

 

 

IgG4-related ophthalmic disease, IgG4-related orbital inflammation, IgG4-related orbital myositis (orbital pseudotumour, idiopathic fibroinflammatory disease, idiopathic orbital inflammation)

> 400/mm2

Unilateral or bilateral painless orbital swelling, proptosis, and diplopia; rarely eye pain and visual impairment

Contrast-enhancing soft tissue mass; preferentially affecting lateral rectus muscle, in contrast to Graves ophthalmopathy; bilateral infraorbital nerve involvement on imaging is pathognomonic of IgG4-related ophthalmic disease {33068267; 33402162; 17414116}

Salivary glands

 

 

 

IgG4-related sialadenitis (Küttner tumour, chronic sclerosing sialadenitis)

> 400/mm2

Unilateral or bilateral painless swelling most commonly involving submandibular gland; occasionally simultaneous bilateral lacrimal, parotid, and submandibular gland involvement (Mikulicz disease); xerostomia is usually mild and responds well to steroids, in contrast to Sjögren syndrome; sialography normal {26194097}

Retroperitoneum, mediastinum, and aorta

 

 

 

IgG4-related retroperitoneal fibrosis (Ormond disease)

 

> 120/mm2

Asymptomatic or nonspecific constitutional symptoms (fatigue, anorexia, fever, weight loss), low back or flank pain, leg oedema, abnormal renal function test

Imaging shows soft tissue mass over paravertebral area and pelvis, frequently involving abdominal aorta and iliac arteries (IgG4-related periaortitis) and entrapping ureter, causing hydronephrosis {31942203; 30754935}
 

IgG4-related mediastinal fibrosis

> 120/mm2

Thoracic counterpart of retroperitoneal fibrosis and often manifests simultaneously; frequently involves thoracic aorta (IgG4-related aortitis/periaortitis)

Asymptomatic or dyspnoea, haemoptysis, dysphagia, and chest pain {29851832}
 

IgG4-related aortitis/periaortitis/periarteritis

> 200/mm2

Most commonly involves infrarenal abdominal aorta and iliac arteries in the context of retroperitoneal fibrosis or thoracic aorta, less commonly other medium-sized arteries (coronary and mesenteric arteries); asymptomatic or low back pain, some may present with aortic aneurysm or dissection

Imaging shows vessel wall thickening (media and adventitia), mass-like lesion with contrast enhancement with or without luminal dilatation (aneurysm) or reduction; PET-CT useful to assess disease activity and detect asymptomatic lesions {33121641; 31942203}

Lymph node

 

 

 

IgG4-related lymphadenopathy

> 400/mm2

Incidental lymphadenopathy on imaging, painless non-tender lymph nodes, often adjacent to organs affected by IgG4-related disease, local mass effects (hydronephrosis, lower limb swelling for pelvic lymph nodes)

Lymph nodes usually mildly enlarged (mean: 20 mm) with homogeneous enhancement and no calcification or necrosis {18344866; 30558692}
