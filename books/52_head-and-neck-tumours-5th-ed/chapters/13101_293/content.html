13.1.0.1 Ceruminous adenoma

Definition

Ceruminous adenoma is a benign neoplasm of the apocrine cerumen-producing glands of the external auditory canal (EAC).

ICD-O coding

8420/0 Ceruminous adenoma

8420/0 Ceruminous pleomorphic adenoma

8420/0 Ceruminous syringocystadenoma papilliferum

ICD-11 coding

2F00.Y & XH7AL8 Other specified benign neoplasm of middle ear or respiratory system & Ceruminous adenoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: ceruminoma; ceruminomata; adenoma ceruminalis; cylindroma; eccrine cylindroma; aural hidradenoma.

Subtype(s)

Ceruminous pleomorphic adenoma; ceruminous syringocystadenoma papilliferum

Localization

Tumours arise from dermal ceruminous glands confined to the outer half of the EAC. Direct extension of pleomorphic adenoma from adjacent structures should be excluded {15104293; 21792797}.

Clinical features

Symptoms include an outer EAC mass with intact tympanic membrane, conductive hearing loss, otorrhoea or discharge, and otalgia {6243462; 6300574; 14102073; 15104293}. Imaging identifies an EAC soft tissue–density mass and excludes direct extension from adjacent sites {16168591; 23351401}.

Epidemiology

Ceruminous adenoma represents < 1% of all primary ear neoplasms {6243462; 6300574; 15104293}, with an equal sex distribution and a wide age range (12–85 years), with a peak in the sixth decade of life {6243462; 6300574; 15104293}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Ceruminous adenoma is a unilateral, polypoid, grey, skin-covered mass measuring 10–40 mm {6243462; 6300574; 15104293}.

Histopathology

Ceruminous adenomas are circumscribed and usually unencapsulated, and they lack invasion. Surface pagetoid-type extension, ulceration, and pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia {6402199} may be seen.

They are predominantly glandular, often with cystic change, and extend into the surrounding stroma. Solid back-to-back glands and papillary patterns may be identified. The dual cell population (comprising inner/luminal epithelial cells and outer basal/myoepithelial cells) is bland, with low to moderate cellularity. Luminal cells are cuboidal to columnar, with ample eosinophilic cytoplasm, showing apical snouting (apocrine). Isolated tumour cells show cytoplasmic golden-yellow/brown pigment granules. Myoepithelial cells are often spindled with clear cytoplasm. Mitoses may be increased (to as many as 4 mitoses/2 mm2), especially around ulceration or previous trauma/biopsy. Atypical mitoses and comedonecrosis are absent. Concurrent cholesteatoma may occur.

Subtypes

Tumours with chondromyxoid stroma represent ceruminous pleomorphic adenoma {32443830}, often with metaplastic elements {2545327; 7517760; 10349387; 16398681; 18314029; 24908278}.

Ceruminous syringocystadenoma papilliferum shows papillary projections lined by a dual cell population, with a variably dense stromal plasma cell infiltrate {15104293; 16168591; 28490727}.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic cells react strongly and diffusely with pancytokeratin; luminal cells express CK7, EMA, and KIT (CD117); basal cells express myoepithelial/basal cell markers {2545327; 15104293; 22623086}. GATA3 is usually positive in luminal or apocrine cells.

Differential diagnosis

The most important differential is ceruminous adenocarcinoma. It is important to exclude direct extension of salivary gland tumours. Paraganglioma and middle ear neuroendocrine tumour (MeNET) are distinctive.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not performed

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a lateral EAC location; low/moderately cellular tumour; bland, glandular proliferation of a biphasic population of cells; luminal apocrine morphology with decapitation.

Desirable: cytoplasmic golden-yellow/brown pigment granules.

Staging

Not performed

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrences develop after incomplete excision {6243462; 6300574; 15104293; 32443830}.
