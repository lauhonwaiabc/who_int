9.0.4.3 Primary mucosal CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder

Definition

CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder (TLPD) is a neoplastic proliferation of large, CD30-positive T cells arising in the oral cavity or occasionally in other mucosal sites in the head and neck. This entity constitutes a clinicopathological spectrum of lymphoproliferative lesions, analogous to the spectrum observed in primary cutaneous CD30-positive TLPD.

ICD-O coding

9718/3 Primary mucosal CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The proliferation typically occurs in the oral cavity or tongue, but similar lesions have been described in the nasopharynx, conjunctiva, and orbit {8833302; 22388754; 8332573; 24332333}.

Clinical features

Patients with CD30-positive TLPD typically present with a mass lesion, often with ulceration. Spontaneous regression may occur {9415340}. Clinical history and staging are important for excluding secondary involvement by a systemic lymphoma.

Cases with both mucosal and skin involvement should be classified as mucosal involvement by primary cutaneous CD30-positive TLPD {22388754; 24749749; 33269496}.

Epidemiology

There is a male predominance (M:F ratio: 2:1). The disorder primarily affects adults, with a mean patient age in the sixth decade of life {22388754; 24332333}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Presentation after dental implants has been reported {26261101}.

Pathogenesis

Clonal TR gene rearrangements have been detected in most cases {22388754; 24332333}. Occasional cases carry rearrangements of DUSP22 on 6p25.3, similar to the rearrangements observed in some primary cutaneous CD30-positive TLPDs and ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphomas {22388754}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour typically appears as a nodule, mucosal ulceration, or both {24332333}.

Histopathology

Primary mucosal CD30-positive TLPD demonstrates a morphological spectrum similar to that observed in primary cutaneous cases. The neoplastic cells are large atypical lymphoid cells with pleomorphic nuclei and abundant cytoplasm. Cells resembling the hallmark cells of anaplastic large cell lymphoma are often seen. Most cases show a diffuse or sheet-like growth pattern. A mixed inflammatory background may be present, including areas with prominent eosinophils or neutrophils {14750239; 22388754; 24332333}. Some cases of traumatic ulcerative granuloma with stromal eosinophilia may represent the indolent end of the spectrum of CD30-positive TLPD {16938660}.

By definition, CD30 is positive, and staining is strong and uniform. The large lymphoid cells typically show a T-cell phenotype but often demonstrate loss of one or more pan–T-cell antigens. CD4 is expressed more frequently than CD8. Cytotoxic markers (i.e. TIA1, granzyme B, and perforin) are often expressed, and EMA may be positive. CD56, ALK, and EBV are negative {24332333}. The EBV-positive cases that have been reported in children most likely represent chronic active EBV infection instead {26420252}.

This disorder must be distinguished from reactive inflammatory conditions of the oral cavity and from secondary involvement by systemic anaplastic large cell lymphoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

TR gene rearrangement studies may be helpful in establishing clonality {22388754; 24332333}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an infiltrate of large, atypical, pleomorphic lymphoid cells in a mucosal site; variable inflammatory background; strong and uniform staining for CD30; demonstration of T-lineage markers and/or molecular evidence of T-cell clonality; exclusion of systemic lymphoma.

Staging

Patients should have disease limited to the presenting mucosal site (stage IE by Lugano staging). Clinical staging is essential to exclude mucosal involvement by systemic anaplastic large cell lymphoma {22388754}.

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases show complete resolution with local therapy (excision with or without radiotherapy), with or without the addition of systemic chemotherapy {18387994; 22388754; 24332333}. Occasional cases show spontaneous regression.
