1.3.0.9 Germ cell tumours of the head and neck

Definition

Teratoma in the head and neck is a germ cell tumour defined by the presence of mature or immature tissues derived from all three germ cell layers. Extragonadal malignant germ cell tumours encompass a spectrum of malignant germ cell neoplasms identical to those occurring in the gonads.

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign teratoma; immature teratoma; teratoma, postpubertal type; teratoma, prepubertal type.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Head and neck teratomas and malignant germ cell tumours are extremely rare. They are described most frequently in the sinonasal tract (maxillary sinus and nasal cavity) and nasopharynx {27437234; 26881568; 23975485; 18598836; 17902152; 9251738; 9078942; 2982716}. Rarely reported sites include the oral cavity, oropharynx, ear and temporal bone, neck, and salivary gland {31620699; 33128732; 29113273; 22560784; 18937314; 18302061; 12953777; 9251738; 9078942; 1399316; 1690172}.

Clinical features

Both tumours generally present as mass lesions, with additional symptoms referrable to the site affected, including nasal obstruction and facial deformity {1690172; 2982716}. Associations include stillbirth, prematurity, fetal malpresentation, and maternal polyhydramnios for teratomas {1690172; 2982716}. Congenital abnormalities may be concurrently present. Aicardi and Dandy–Walker syndromes have been associated with head and neck yolk sac tumours {31640397; 9715538}. Serum AFP or β-hCG levels may be elevated, particularly the former for yolk sac tumours {9853671}. Teratomas may undergo malignant transformation {21319013; 2982716}.

Imaging

Imaging demonstrates a heterogeneous cystic and solid mass with variable amounts of fat and calcifications, which may suggest the diagnosis {31640397; 23975485; 23009771; 21319013}.

Epidemiology

Teratomas of the head and neck account for a small proportion of teratomas overall, and malignant germ cell tumours are extremely uncommon {26881568; 18937314; 1690172; 2982716}. The majority of teratomas occur in neonates and older infants, whereas the majority of malignant germ cell tumours develop in adults, although children may be affected. There is no sex bias {35073631; 33128732; 18937314; 9078942; 1690172; 2982716}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours generally have a smooth but lobulated exterior and a heterogeneous, multiloculated, solid to cystic cut surface, containing variable amounts of serous, mucinous, glairy, or haemorrhagic fluid. Tumours measure as large as 70 mm {9078942; 2982716}.

Histopathology

Teratomas are defined by the presence of organoid mature or immature tissues from all embryonic germ cell layers (endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm). Tumours display multiple cystic spaces lined by a variety of epithelial types, including simple or stratified squamous epithelium, often associated with skin adnexal structures, pseudostratified and ciliated respiratory epithelium, mucin-producing intestinal epithelium, and transitional epithelium. Teratomas may also contain a significant component of neural tissue, encompassing both mature glial elements, retinal anlage, choroid plexus, or immature neuroectodermal tissue composed of small to medium-sized cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and scant fibrillary cytoplasm arranged in sheets and rosettes. When a neural tissue component is predominant, immature teratoma is diagnosed {1690172}. A variety of mesenchymal elements including skeletal muscle, smooth muscle, cartilage, and bone can be identified. Solid organ parenchyma including pancreatic, liver, or lung tissue is rare.

Carcinoma may be seen as a somatic malignancy in a teratoma {31442873; 3511885}, referred to as teratoma with malignant transformation.

The most common head and neck malignant extragonadal germ cell tumour is yolk sac tumour, which recapitulates embryonic yolk sac of the gonads with a reticular/microcystic pattern or the classic round or elongated tumour cell–lined papillae with a large central vessel, protruding into a cystic space surrounded by tumour cells (Schiller–Duval bodies). Choriocarcinoma, embryonal carcinoma, and seminoma are extremely rare {35073631; 33128732; 1690172; 9078942; 2982716}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry is usually unnecessary for diagnosis, with the various teratoma components displaying staining characteristics of their constituent tissues. Yolk sac tumours are reactive for AFP, α1-antitrypsin, SALL4 (but negative for OCT3/4), glypican-3 (GPC3), LIN28A, and pankeratins, with focal coexpression of CDX2 and GATA3 in many cases {29753846; 26772394; 19365862; 1708402}, whereas choriocarcinoma is positive for β-hCG {33128732}.

Differential diagnosis

SMARCB1-deficient carcinomas (including adenocarcinoma) in the sinonasal tract may show yolk sac differentiation but are considered within the spectrum of SWI/SNF complex–deficient sinonasal carcinomas {35257325; 33824593; 34420180; 31468350}. Teratocarcinosarcoma may show SALL4 and β-catenin immunoreactivity but shows distinct histological features of malignant squamous or glandular tissues along with mesenchymal and primitive neural elements, shown to be SMARCA4-deficient {34106411; 32520761}. Limited sampling may result in misdiagnosis, depending on which component(s) are sampled. Metastatic disease to the head and neck from a distant primary site must be considered and excluded {21119432}. Teratocarcinosarcoma is covered in section Teratocarcinosarcoma .

Cytology

FNA is rarely performed, but it may contain only cyst contents or be interpreted as “insufficient” with only normal elements seen (mature teratoma).

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: involvement of a head and neck site; for teratoma: proliferation of tissue from all three germ layers, usually mature tissues, foci of immature neuroectodermal tissues may be seen; for malignant germ cell tumour: most commonly yolk sac tumour; exclusion of SWI/SNF complex–deficient sinonasal carcinomas and teratocarcinosarcoma.

Desirable: exclusion of a primary in another site.

Staging

Not available

Prognosis and prediction

Teratomas can cause death due to airway compression and impaired development of other vital structures. There is a poor outcome for malignant germ cell tumours irrespective of site {35073631; 31620699; 31442873; 18302061; 1690172; 9078942; 2982716}, although syndrome association and multimodal therapies may yield a better outcome {31640397}.
