8.0.8.4 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a malignant spindle cell neoplasm with limited Schwannian differentiation, often arising from a peripheral nerve, from a pre-existing benign nerve sheath tumour, or in a patient with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1).

ICD-O coding

9540/3 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, NOS

9542/3 Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B5E & XH2XP8 Malignant nerve sheath tumour of peripheral nerves or autonomic nervous system, primary site & Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

2B5E & XH4V81 Malignant nerve sheath tumour of peripheral nerves or autonomic nervous system, primary site & Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, epithelioid

2B5E & XH2VV8 Malignant nerve sheath tumour of peripheral nerves or autonomic nervous system, primary site & Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant schwannoma; neurofibrosarcoma; neurogenic sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid MPNST

Localization

The head and neck region is the second most common location after the trunk and extremities {27158754; 3082508}. Tumours in this region occur most commonly in the soft tissue of the neck {24685259; 28762137}.

Clinical features

Patients with MPNST typically present between the ages of 20 and 50 years, with NF1-associated tumours arising at younger ages. Patients present with an enlarging mass that may be painful. MPNST arising within a nerve is often associated with neuropathic symptoms.

Epidemiology

MPNST is rare, accounting for 5–10% of all soft tissue sarcomas in the head and neck {30779403}.

Etiology

Subsets are associated with radiation (~10%) {7377756} or NF1 (~50%).

Pathogenesis

MPNSTs have complex karyotypes and frequent concurrent inactivating mutations (in NF1, CDKN2A/CDKN2B, and PRC2 core components [EED or SUZ12]) involving three pathways {25240281; 25119042; 25305755; 30722027}. As many as 80% of high-grade MPNSTs exhibit complete loss of PRC2 activity and complete loss of H3K27me3 by immunohistochemistry {26645727; 25240281; 26585554}.

Epithelioid MPNSTs are molecularly distinct, characterized by frequent SMARCB1 gene inactivation {30864974}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most MPNSTs are > 50 mm. Intraneural tumours show fusiform enlargement of the involved nerve. MPNST shows a tan-white, fleshy cut surface, often with haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

MPNST shows interlacing fascicles of spindle-shaped cells, often with alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas. A haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern is characteristic, and denser perivascular growth is common. There are often areas of geographical necrosis and high mitotic activity. Nuclei are hyperchromatic and spindled to serpentine, whereas the cytoplasm is pale and eosinophilic. Occasional cases show prominent pleomorphism. Approximately 15% of MPNSTs show heterologous differentiation, which may be rhabdomyoblastic (triton tumour), osseous, cartilaginous, or vascular, as well as epithelial (glandular or squamous).

Epithelioid MPNST is composed of lobules of epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, sometimes embedded in an abundant myxoid or hyalinized stroma {1746681}. It is not associated with NF1 and rarely arises ex schwannoma {25602794}.

MPNSTs show limited staining for S100 (< 50% of tumours), SOX10 (< 70%), and GFAP (20–30%) {23929265}. Complete loss of staining for H3K27me3 is seen in 50–80% of all MPNSTs, with loss being more frequent in high-grade and radiation-induced MPNSTs {28776579; 26645727; 26585554; 29482987}. Unlike conventional MPNST, epithelioid MPNST shows strong and diffuse staining for S100 and SOX10, with retained H3K27me3 and loss of SMARCB1 expression {30864974}.

Cytology

Microscopic examination shows highly cellular smears with a mix of dispersed cells and clusters of interlacing fascicles in a fibrillar background, with oval to spindled nuclei within cells showing ill-defined cytoplasmic borders. Mitoses and necrosis may be found {20307006}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In diagnostically challenging cases, DNA methylation profiling can identify a specific tumour signature of MPNST {33479225}. Other supporting molecular data include mutations in NF1 and in SUZ12 or EED.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cell sarcoma with fascicular growth, alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas, and tapered or buckled nuclei; epithelioid morphology and diffuse positivity for S100 and SOX10 (in epithelioid MPNST).

Desirable: association with a pre-existing large nerve, neurofibroma, NF1, or radiation; variable positivity for S100, SOX10, or GFAP; loss of H3K27me3 by immunohistochemistry; loss of SMARCB1 expression (in epithelioid MPNST).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for head and neck sarcomas.

Prognosis and prediction

MPNST shows aggressive behaviour with a poor prognosis. For head and neck MPNST, 2- and 5-year disease-specific survival rates are 50% and 30%, respectively {28762137}. Adverse prognostic factors include a tumour size of > 50 mm, local recurrence, positive surgical margins, and high-grade morphology (including MPNST with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation). NF1-associated and radiation-induced MPNSTs are associated with 5-year survival rates of approximately 35% and 23%, respectively {27158754; 31078939}. Epithelioid MPNSTs are less aggressive {25602794}.
