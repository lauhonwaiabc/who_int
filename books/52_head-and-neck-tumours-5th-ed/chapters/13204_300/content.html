13.2.0.4 Middle ear adenocarcinoma

Definition

Middle ear adenocarcinoma is a locally aggressive primary epithelial malignancy arising from the middle ear, showing glandular differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8260/3 Middle ear adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C21.0 Adenocarcinoma of middle ear

Related terminology

Acceptable: papillary adenocarcinoma of middle ear; low-grade adenocarcinoma.

Not recommended: endolymphatic sac tumour (distinct tumour category); aggressive papillary tumour of middle ear.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumour extensively fills the middle ear and/or tympanic cavity, expanding into the petrous bone apex, sometimes involving the cerebellopontine angle and cerebellum {18633929}.

Clinical features

Described mostly in case reports {8692411; 11307613; 14738614; 17167601; 18633929; 32940914}, symptoms are often of a long duration and nonspecific; they include hearing loss, otalgia, vestibular dysfunction, and otorrhoea.

Epidemiology

The age range at presentation is broad (16–55 years, median: 33 years, mean: 34 years), but given the long symptom duration, the age at onset may be younger. There is a slight female predilection {8692411; 11307613; 14738614; 17167601; 18633929; 32940914}. Isolated cases have been associated with von Hippel–Lindau syndrome.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

VHL mutations have been described in association with aggressive papillary tumours involving middle ear but that involved the endolymphatic sac. Tumours confined to the middle ear without involvement of endolymphatic sac and negative for VHL mutation likely represent a subset of adenocarcinoma distinct from endolymphatic sac tumour. Mouse models of histologically similar tumours showed EGFR mutations {26027747}. Oncocytic and papillary tumours have been reported to have MKRN1::BRAF fusion {32940914}. Tumours resembling salivary polymorphous low-grade adenocarcinoma have been described, but PRKD1, PRKD2, or PRKD3 mutation was not proved {34097084}.

Macroscopic appearance

The middle ear cleft and mastoid air cells are usually filled with a bone-destructive tumour.

Histopathology

Tumours show a cystic-papillary glandular pattern with a complex architecture including papillae lying loosely or infiltrating fibrous connective tissue. The papillae are usually lined by a single layer of low cuboidal to columnar epithelial cells with uniform nuclei, eosinophilic cytoplasm, and indistinct cell borders. Thyroid follicle–like areas may be present, similar to those seen in endolymphatic sac tumour. Appearances similar to oncocytic, mucinous, or polymorphous adenocarcinoma, or even to salivary duct carcinoma, suggest a wide morphological spectrum in these tumours {32940914; 34097084}.

Immunohistochemistry and differential diagnosis

Tumour cells are positive for CK7, EMA, and (sometimes) S100. PAX8 and CAIX are negative, unlike in endolymphatic sac tumour {30291511; 32940914}, which is the principal differential consideration. Metastatic carcinomas (lung, thyroid, colon) should be excluded.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: imaging confirmation of middle ear localization; a bone-destructive adenocarcinoma, often with papillary growth; exclusion of metastases.

Staging

Without standardized staging, International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting (ICCR) minimum data set reporting is encouraged {30500288} [[Thompson LDR, Gupta R, Sandison A, et al. Ear and temporal bone tumours: histopathology reporting guide. 1st ed. Sydney (Australia): International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting; 2018.]].

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis depends on the size of the lesion at presentation and the adequacy of the surgical excision.
