8.0.2.8 Epithelioid haemangioma

Definition

Epithelioid haemangioma is a benign vascular neoplasm of well-formed blood vessels with plump epithelioid endothelial cells and a variable eosinophil infiltrate. It is often associated with FOS and FOSB fusions.

ICD-O coding

9125/0 Epithelioid haemangioma

9125/0 Cellular epithelioid haemangioma

9125/0 Atypical epithelioid haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.0Y & XH10T4 Other specified neoplastic haemangioma & Epithelioid haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiolymphoid hyperplasia with eosinophilia; histiocytoid haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

Cellular epithelioid haemangioma; atypical epithelioid haemangioma

Localization

Sites involved include the cutaneous and superficial soft tissues of the forehead, preauricular region, and scalp {4008683; 22015125; 26135557; 26685720; 29266025}.

Clinical features

Epithelioid haemangiomas form slowly growing, indolent, erythematous and violaceous nodules that are often pruritic or painful {30277937}, and which are multifocal in about 15% of cases {26685720; 29266025}.

Epidemiology

These lesions develop over a broad age distribution, with a peak in the fourth decade of life, and without a sex predilection {7364594; 23845664; 24676051; 26685720; 26135557}.

Etiology

Vessel damage or trauma are suggested as etiological factors for cutaneous lesions, whereas molecular alterations are detected in soft tissue tumours {26135557}.

Pathogenesis

Chromosomal translocations leading to FOS or FOSB rearrangements drive tumorigenesis in a significant proportion of tumours {25043949; 26173738; 26135557; 29150442}.

Macroscopic appearance

Epithelioid haemangiomas form pale-pink to red-brown nodules, sometimes coalescing into plaques.

Histopathology

There is a circumscribed, nodular to lobular proliferation of variably sized vessels lined by distinctive plump epithelioid endothelial cells. The vessels may show a gradient of maturation towards the periphery, ranging from lobular to irregular distribution. The endothelial cells are epithelioid with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, often with intracytoplasmic vacuoles (blister cells). The hobnailed monolayer of endothelial cells creates a bumpy or tombstone-like appearance. Lymphocytes and eosinophils are invariably present, but to a variable degree. Mitotic figures are inconspicuous. Atypical tumours are infiltrative and solid, with an increased cellularity, tumour necrosis, and fewer spaces, but they are rare in the head and neck {25043949}. Immunohistochemical expression of FOSB may be seen (~50% of cases), even when the gene fusion is absent {28009608; 29527734}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant, although reported {12685201}

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobular architecture with vasoformation; lining endothelial cells are epithelioid, with eosinophilic cytoplasm; loose stroma, often with eosinophils.

Desirable: demonstration of FOS or FOSB rearrangements or expression (in selected cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours may show locoregional growth {24676051; 26685720}.
