8.0.3.5 Angiosarcoma

Definition

Angiosarcoma is a malignant neoplasm that variably recapitulates the morphological and immunohistochemical features of endothelial cells.

ICD-O coding

9120/3 Angiosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B56.Y & XH6264 Angiosarcoma, other specified primary site & Haemangiosarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: epithelioid haemangioendothelioma; malignant haemangioendothelioma; malignant angioendothelioma; haemangiosarcoma; haemangioblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The oral cavity and nasal cavity / maxillary sinus are the most frequently affected mucosal sites in the head and neck region, most commonly with a single site of disease {20614274; 3294314; 11520569; 28478092}.

Clinical features

Presenting signs and symptoms are nonspecific and usually of short duration (mean: ∼10 months). For sinonasal tumours, common symptoms are recurrent epistaxis and obstruction {20614274} along with nasal discharge, an enlarging mass, sinusitis, epiphora, pain, diplopia, and headaches {17987727}. Infiltration often leads to invasion of vital structures and bone erosion. On T2-weighted MRI, tumours show contrast enhancement or a bright signal. Angiography reveals tumour extent and feeder vessel(s), which may facilitate presurgical embolization {20614274}. Lymph node and distant metastases are uncommon at initial presentation.

Epidemiology

A male predominance of the tumours has been noted {28723012}. Tumours develop most commonly in elderly patients (oral cavity) and in patients in their fifth decade of life (sinonasal tract).

Etiology

Rarely, radiation or environmental exposure to vinyl chloride and coal dust have been associated with these tumours {3294314; 28723012}. Telangiectasia, trauma, and chronic oedema have also been considered as possible etiological factors {25698587; 12360047}.

Pathogenesis

Angiosarcomas are a genetically heterogeneous group of tumours, often incorporating mutations affecting the MAPK pathway genes (KRAS, HRAS, NRAS, BRAF, MAPK1, and NF1) {26440310}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours can be as large as 80 mm (mean: 39 mm); paranasal sinus tumours are typically larger than sinonasal cavity tumours (68 mm vs 22 mm). The tumours are nodular to polypoid, soft, friable, purple to red, and often ulcerated, with associated haemorrhage and necrosis {20614274; 9630175; 12640107}.

Histopathology

Angiosarcomas develop below an intact, uninvolved epithelium, with vasoformative neoplastic cells expanding into soft tissue and bone, frequently accompanied by necrosis, haemorrhage, and secondary ulceration. The tortuous, irregular, freely anastomosing vascular channels can create cleft-like spaces, rudimentary vessels, and capillary-sized vessels, as well as cavernous spaces filled with erythrocytes and lined by plump, enlarged, atypical, spindled or epithelioid endothelial cells protruding into the vascular spaces in multiple layers or papillae. The nuclei are enlarged and pleomorphic with heavy chromatin distribution, irregular nuclear contours, and prominent nucleoli. Mitoses, including atypical forms, are easily identified {20614274; 17987727}. Extensive haemorrhage and clot formation may obscure the tumour.

Tumours express CD31 (in a typical cell membranous pattern) and ERG, whereas CD34, FLI1, podoplanin (recognized by D2-40), and actin are inconsistently expressed. Focal keratin expression (particularly in the epithelioid pattern) can be misleading and suggest carcinoma {21959309; 33964187; 12640107}. Cutaneous angiosarcoma can look rather bland and may therefore be mistaken for a benign vascular tumour {29766535}; mucosal melanoma, spindle cell squamous cell carcinoma, and myoepithelial carcinoma must also be considered. Grading is not applied to sinonasal angiosarcoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No identified genetic findings aid in diagnosis at present.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: anastomosing vascular spaces lined by atypical endothelial cells; infiltrative growth; endothelial multilayering; marked nuclear atypia; brisk mitotic activity.

Desirable: expression of endothelial markers (preferably CD31 and ERG) by immunohistochemistry (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging is not applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrences occur in about 40% of cases, and the overall survival rate is about 60% {25698587}. Old age, metastatic disease, and poor performance status are predictors of a poor outcome. Metastatic spread occurs most commonly to the lung, liver, spleen, and bone (marrow). Specific etiological factors may be associated with shorter survival {17987727}.
