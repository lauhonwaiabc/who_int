12.1.2.3 Merkel cell carcinoma

Definition

Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) is a high-grade primary cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC).

ICD-O coding

8247/3 Merkel cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C34 & XH81N8 Cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma & Merkel cell carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: primary neuroendocrine carcinoma of skin.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MCC most commonly arises on sun-exposed sites. A review of 14 414 patients with MCC demonstrated that 43% of cases developed on the head/neck {27198511}.

Clinical features

MCC presents as a rapidly growing, often violaceous papule or nodule. The broad clinical differential diagnosis includes basal cell and squamous cell carcinoma, lymphoma, atypical fibroxanthoma, and melanoma. The majority (65%) of patients present with disease localized to the skin, 26% with regional lymph node metastases, and 8% with distant metastases {27198511}.

Epidemiology

MCC most commonly affects older, White, non-Hispanic men. A population-based survey of 11 028 cases from 21 countries showed the highest rates in Australia, New Zealand, and the USA {29533867}. The incidence of MCC (number of cases per 100 000 person-years) has increased significantly in the USA, from 0.15 in 1986, to 0.44 in 2001, to 0.79 in 2011 {15611998; 26215243; 29102486}.

Etiology

Risk factors for MCC development include male sex, old age, chronic sun exposure, and immunosuppression. Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) infection and ultraviolet (UV) radiation–induced mutagenesis reflect two distinct etiologies of MCC. Asymptomatic MCPyV infection occurs early in life, with seropositivity rates of 60–70% {28893943}.

Pathogenesis

Clonally integrated MCPyV expresses the viral oncoproteins large T-antigen (LT) and small T-antigen {25681708; 28802497; 29072302; 30417062} and is responsible for 60–80% of cases {18202256; 27815175}. Truncated LT binds and inactivates RB1, allowing cell-cycle progression. Virus-negative MCC possesses a significantly higher mutation burden than MCPyV-positive MCC, predominantly UV radiation signature mutations affecting TP53, RB1, and NOTCH family members {26215243; 26238782; 26655088; 26627015; 31399473}. MCPyV-positive MCCs predominate in areas with high ambient UV radiation, whereas MCPyV-negative MCCs predominate in low-exposure areas {30417062}.

Macroscopic appearance

MCC presents as a solitary flesh-coloured or erythematous dermal nodule or plaque-like mass, possibly with ulceration and/or haemorrhage.

Histopathology

Scanning magnification shows a densely cellular dermal malignancy with frequent extension into the subcutis, infiltrative or pushing borders, and variably dense lymphohistiocytic inflammatory infiltrate. Intraepidermal MCC is rare {27198511; 28802497}. Tumour cells with scant cytoplasm, round to oval nuclei, and finely granular chromatin are arranged as sheets, cords, and trabeculae. Mitotic figures and apoptotic bodies are common. Tumour cells are usually intermediate in size, but large cell and small cell morphologies may occur {28802497; 29556962}. MCC may show divergent differentiation, most commonly glandular {8751563; 23530585; 25699980} or squamous {9810922; 19609205; 19702685; 21453956; 23664542; 23530585; 26541273}, and less commonly sarcomatoid or neuroblastic {28802497}. Distinct foci of invasive squamous cell carcinoma, found in as many as 15% of cases, may represent collision phenomenon rather than transdifferentiation {30417062}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical studies are essential to confirm the diagnosis of MCC. The most specific marker is CK20, which highlights most MCCs (~88%) {9042291; 10027519; 11175640; 11127923; 16625069; 30067951; 31201352} with characteristic perinuclear dots. MCCs show neuroendocrine differentiation, including INSM1 (virtually 100%) {35551625}, synaptophysin (92%), and chromogranin A (84%). NFP is expressed in about 80% of MCCs. Immunohistochemical detection of MCPyV LT (CM2B4) is sensitive (88%) and specific (94%) {27815175}. A subset of MCPyV-negative MCCs demonstrate divergent immunophenotypic findings (positivity for thyroid transcription factor 1 [TTF1], or negativity for NFP and/or CK20) {30067951; 31201352}. CK20-negative MCCs may be diagnosed by NFP positivity and TTF1 negativity; MCPyV staining has limited sensitivity for CK20-negative tumours {25394777}. A meta-analysis identified frequent expression of PAX5 (~90%), TdT (~65%), and KIT (CD117) (63%) in MCC {28693804}. SATB2 is also expressed in MCCs {30349028; 31233624}. Expression of B-cell markers is associated with MCPyV {32134459}. These are all important pitfalls, because detection of these biomarkers and MCPyV can assist in the distinction of MCC from other NECs {30349028}.

Cytology

FNA typically reveals a cellular proliferation of malignant cells with crush artefact. The tumour cells contain scant cytoplasm, variable nuclear size and shape, finely granular chromatin, indistinct nucleoli, and frequent nuclear moulding {24678011}. Mitoses and apoptotic bodies are common.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular studies are not typically used in the diagnosis of MCC, although viral gene expression and UV radiation signature mutation detection have enabled distinction from histological mimics {30986802; 32445471}. Immunohistochemical detection of MCPyV LT (CM2B4) is sensitive (88%) and specific (94%) {27815175}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a rapidly growing skin lesion; a highly cellular dermal malignancy composed of cells with round to oval nuclei and finely granular chromatin; immunohistochemistry showing cytokeratin (e.g. CK20) positivity with dot-like perinuclear staining and markers of neuroendocrine differentiation.

Desirable: expression of B-cell markers and/or MCPyV (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Five-year overall survival rates are about 51% for patients with localized disease, 35% for those with regional lymph node metastases, and 14% when distant metastases are present. In node-negative patients, T stage robustly predicts 5-year overall survival rate (55.8% for pT1, 41.1% for pT2/pT3, and 31.8% for pT4) {27198511}, although recent data suggest that survival rates are substantially higher {32103415}. Cases with clinically evident lymph node metastases have a worse prognosis than those with clinically occult lymph node involvement {27198511}, and the pattern and extent of sentinel lymph node involvement are additional prognostic factors {27166398; 31094923}. Patients with clinically evident regional lymph node disease without a known primary MCC are distinguished from patients with a known primary MCC (stage IIIA vs IIIB, respectively) and have a better prognosis {22030017; 23182060; 23835211}.

Additional prognostic features of the primary tumour include tumour thickness, pattern of growth, lymphovascular invasion, and tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, all of which are independently associated with patient survival {18798233; 21422430; 22467679; 27166398}.

MCPyV-positive MCCs may have better survival outcomes than MCPyV-negative MCCs {19535775; 21642382; 22261808; 22795182; 27815175; 28763479}, although this is controversial {21422430; 22882157} and MCPyV status does not predict survival independently of stage {27815175}. p63 expression correlates with shorter survival in MCC {17599745; 21765392; 22795182; 24746200}, but not independently of stage {24225752}. A Ki-67 labelling index of ≥ 55% {32696301} and TERT methylation {33909215} were associated with a bad prognosis.

Integrity of the host immune system is both a risk factor and a prognostic factor in MCC {10609948; 11853800; 18280333; 22120659}. Immunocompromised patients have worse survival than immunocompetent patients {23190897}. Increased expression of immune-related genes such as CD8A and granzyme genes and higher levels of tumour-infiltrative CD8+ T cells independently correlate with longer survival {21422430; 22467679; 27166398}. Furthermore, in MCPyV-positive MCCs, decreased expression of immune markers including granzyme and IDO1 correlated with shorter survival, whereas in MCPyV-negative MCCs, expression of several genes including UBE2C correlated with shorter survival {33547200}.
