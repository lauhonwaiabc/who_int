12.1.1.2 Middle ear neuroendocrine tumour

Definition

Middle ear neuroendocrine tumour (MeNET) is a neoplasm arising from the middle ear mucosa, showing epithelial and neuroendocrine differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8240/3 Middle ear neuroendocrine tumour

ICD-11 coding

2C21.Y & XH8DS0 Other specified malignant neoplasm of middle ear & Neuroendocrine tumour, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: middle ear adenoma; neuroendocrine adenoma of the middle ear; carcinoid tumour; adenomatous tumour of the middle ear; adenocarcinoid; amphicrine tumour. (See section the introduction to this chapter, for more discussion on terminology.)

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Any part of the middle ear may be affected, with extension into the mastoid, Eustachian tube, or external auditory canal via ruptured tympanic membrane {12011260; 22623086; 24766278; 30001283; 34041698}.

Clinical features

Symptoms include hearing changes, fullness or ear pressure, and tinnitus. Otoscopy usually reveals a grey-white or fibrotic mass behind an intact tympanic membrane {12011260; 22623086; 24766278; 30001283; 34041698}.

Imaging

CT and MRI delineate extent and tumour location, including ossicular chain involvement {12011260; 11214650; 28680503}. Functional imaging (such as with 68Ga-DOTATATE) targets SSTR (especially SSTR2 and SSTR5 located on the tumour cell membrane), which can be used for detection or follow-up monitoring {15709214; 17515757; 33273780; 30001283}.

Epidemiology

MeNET accounts for < 2% of all middle ear tumours. Both sexes are equally affected, most commonly in the fifth decade of life, but there is a broad age range {12011260; 19303146; 22623086; 24766278; 30001283; 34041698}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of MeNET is still under investigation. There is evidence of an origin from L cells within the middle ear mucosa {1031337; 12011260; 34041698}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is white, grey, or red-brown, with a soft or rubbery consistency. It is unencapsulated, frequently entrapping and destroying the ossicles {12011260}.

Histopathology

Tumours exhibit cuboidal to columnar cells with distinct cell borders and eosinophilic cytoplasm. The small, central, hyperchromatic nuclei rarely contain nucleoli. The tumours show many patterns that infiltrate the stroma, including a glandular configuration in a fibrous stroma; back-to-back, pseudorosette, trabecular, and sheet-like patterns; and discohesive single cell infiltration. Many have prominent swirling of tumour cell ribbons. Mucins can be demonstrated within the gland lumen. Some tumours show significant nuclear pleomorphism and increased mitotic activity.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic cells coexpress keratins (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2) and neuroendocrine markers (synaptophysin, chromogranin) along with the transcription factors INSM1, insulin gene enhancer protein ISL1 (islet-1), and SATB2; they may coexpress CDX2 and GATA3 focally but are negative for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1). The most common hormones produced are glucagon, PP, and PYY, but serotonin is also expressed {12011260; 24766278; 29438167; 34041698}. Recent studies suggest a single cell proliferation rather than a dual cell proliferation as traditionally identified, with no myoepithelial cells {22623086; 34041698}. The Ki-67 labelling index is usually low (< 2%), but a Ki-67 index of > 20% has been reported, correlating with aggressive behaviour, which suggests grading similar to that for all neuroendocrine tumours (NETs): G1, G2, and G3 {30140036; 34041698}, with cut-off points under investigation.

Cytology

Rarely clinically applied, but reported {29731914; 33044790}

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a middle ear epithelial neoplasm arranged in variable patterns, with neuroendocrine differentiation.

Desirable: epithelial and neuroendocrine immunoreactivity.

Staging

There is currently no staging system, but expert-derived proposals have been introduced {30001283; 30500288} [[Thompson LDR, Gupta R, Sandison A, et al. Ear and temporal bone tumours: histopathology reporting guide. 1st ed. Sydney (Australia): International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting; 2018.]].

Prognosis and prediction

About 15–20% of patients develop recurrence {12011260; 28547535; 33605503}, specifically when the ossicles remain. In well documented, non-manipulated, non-irradiated tumours primarily of the middle ear, metastasis is very rare and may involve cervical nodes, bone, and liver {22777694; 28547535; 33044790; 34041698}; it is more frequent with a high Ki-67 proliferation index.
