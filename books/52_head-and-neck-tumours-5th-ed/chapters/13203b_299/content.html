13.2.0.3B Endolymphatic sac tumour

Definition

Endolymphatic sac tumour (ELST) is an epithelial neoplasm associated with the endolymphatic sac/duct, often demonstrating an association with von Hippel–Lindau syndrome (VHL).

ICD-O coding

8140/3 Endolymphatic sac tumour

ICD-11 coding

2C21.Y & XH85C2 Other specified malignant neoplasm of middle ear & Endolymphatic sac tumour

Related terminology

Not recommended: papillary endolymphatic sac tumour; aggressive papillary adenoma; aggressive papillary middle ear tumour; low-grade papillary adenomatous tumour of temporal bone; endolymphatic sac papillary tumour; low-grade papillary adenocarcinoma; endolymphatic sac carcinoma; low-grade carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ELSTs are believed to arise in association with the endolymphatic sac/duct {16322231; 20614260}. Bilateral tumours are nearly always VHL-associated {29482989}.

Clinical features

Ipsilateral progressive hearing loss, tinnitus, facial nerve palsy, and vertigo are common presenting findings {15796386; 21451430; 23070752; 25650230; 25867206; 29482989; 30291511; 32068496}. A long symptom duration (4 years) suggests slow tumour growth {2804921; 7630290; 14710902; 15035284; 23070752; 30291511}.

Imaging

Comprehensive evaluation includes a combination of MRI and CT. T1-weighted MRI shows a hyperintense (hypervascular) heterogeneous mass, and CT shows a permeative, destructive, multilocular lytic lesion centred on the endolymphatic sac {9051037; 11942507; 15190140; 15796386; 17544692; 17991063; 20351605; 21167761; 22367728; 30466638; 31197530; 32068496}.

Epidemiology

Data from the International Endolymphatic Sac Tumor Registry {25867206} show a 3.6% prevalence of ELSTs in VHL. VHL constitutional pathogenic variants were identified in 39% of apparently sporadic ELSTs, showing that ELSTs are the initial presentation of VHL in 32% of patients. These findings support genetic testing in all patients with ELSTs {20351605; 20495761; 21451430; 25650230; 25867206}, and all patients with VHL should be radiographically screened for ELSTs {15035284; 15190140; 23070752}. There is a wide age range at presentation (10–88 years), but the majority of patients present between 30 and 40 years of age, although patients with VHL are younger {30291511}. The M:F ratio is 1:1.6 {2804921; 15035284; 15190140; 23070752; 30291511}.

Etiology

Constitutional pathogenic variants of the tumour suppressor gene VHL are usually detected in these patients, although sporadic cases may lack such abnormalities {11085513; 15035285; 18423895}. Somatic and germline (constitutional) pathogenic variant analysis of ELSTs has been performed, with many VHL mutations and allelic deletions identified {9214679; 10932304; 11085513; 15796386; 16322231; 20351605; 20495761; 20850701}.

Pathogenesis

VHL is an autosomal dominant–inherited disease, caused by constitutional pathogenic variants in the tumour suppressor gene VHL (3p25). When inherited, a second mutation affecting the wildtype allele will result in a cell with no functional VHL protein, at risk for tumour development {16322231; 20151405; 21386872}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range in size from a few millimetres to as large as 100 mm (mean: 30 mm); they are usually larger in older patients. Tumours are unencapsulated, destructive lesions, with bone invasion and remodelling {7936748; 20614260; 30291511}.

Histopathology

The tumours are papillary and/or cystic, commonly with bone invasion or remodelling. A single layer of low cuboidal to columnar epithelial cells are arranged in simple, coarse, broad, interdigitating papillary projections with fibrovascular cores, showing limited branching, found within cystic spaces. Cystic spaces contain serum, secretions, and/or erythrocytes. Fibrosis may be present. The acinar spaces are filled with inspissated material with similarity to thyroid colloid. The cytoplasm is ample, clear, and vacuolated, and cell membranes are indistinct. The nuclei are uniformly small, round, and hyperchromatic, with coarse nuclear chromatin and small nucleoli {2804921; 7936748; 9145719; 16778477; 18423895; 20614260; 21167761; 30291511}. Pleomorphism, increased mitotic figures, and necrosis are inconspicuous.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic cells are positive for pancytokeratin, CK7, HIF1α, EMA, GLUT1, CAIX (membranous), and PAX8 (nuclear), with limited S100, GFAP, and vimentin staining, and they are negative for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), thyroglobulin, PSA, CD10, AMACR (P504S), p63, synaptophysin, GATA3, and RCCm {7630290; 7936748; 9023246; 11455007; 15035285; 16778477; 18423895; 30291511}.

Differential diagnosis

The major differential diagnoses include metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma and papillary or follicular thyroid carcinoma, along with choroid plexus papilloma, papillary ependymoma, papillary meningioma, paraganglioma, and middle ear neuroendocrine tumour (MeNET) {25944396; 30291511}. The diagnosis of metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma, especially when in a patient with VHL, can be quite challenging, but immunohistochemistry studies aid in distinction {30291511}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: endolymphatic sac/duct location; simple, coarse, broad, interdigitating papillary projections lined by low cuboidal to columnar epithelial cells with clear or slightly eosinophilic cytoplasm surrounding round nuclei with coarse chromatin.

Desirable: in selected cases, positivity for pancytokeratin, CAIX, and PAX8, and negativity for TTF1, GATA3, and RCCm.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is dependent on the extent of the disease, with recurrences common after incomplete excision. Metastases are not reported {15354011; 17315832; 17544692; 21451430; 22367728; 23685713; 24662627; 25650230; 29923043; 32068496}. Overall outcome is often dependent on other stigmata of VHL {2804921; 16778477}.
