11.1.0.5 Lymphoepithelial cyst

Definition

Lymphoepithelial cyst is a benign acquired epithelial-lined cystic lesion associated with dense lymphoid stroma.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DA05.Y Lymphoepithelial cyst of mouth

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign lymphoepithelial cyst, HIV-related sialadenitis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lymphoepithelial cysts affect multiple locations, including the parotid, lateral neck, and oral cavity, particularly the floor of the mouth {13608417; 28936296}.

Clinical features

In immunocompetent individuals, lymphoepithelial cysts present as a unilateral painless mass. Patients with HIV infection may develop bilateral disease {3393663; 31456841}.

Imaging

Imaging is useful for confirming their simple cystic nature and delineating their anatomical location. Lymphoepithelial cysts appear as well-circumscribed T2-hyperintense and T1-weighted low-signal lesions on MRI, which may demonstrate thin peripheral postcontrast enhancement. On ultrasound, the lesions are hypoechoic/anechoic with well-defined margins. In HIV-related cases, enlarged adenoidal tissue and cervical lymph nodes are also often noted.

Epidemiology

Lymphoepithelial cysts arise over a broad age range, with a peak in the fourth to fifth decade of life {7535539; 9496829; 20303054}. They have classically been described in AIDS and are reported to occur in as many as 6% and 10% of HIV-positive adults and children, respectively {1407984; 3393663}. Cystic lesions with a similar morphology also occur in middle-aged immunocompetent individuals and patients with Sjögren syndrome {10335942; 31456841}.

Etiology

The etiology is unclear. It has been postulated that lymphoepithelial cysts arise from epithelial inclusions located in intraparotid and/or periparotid lymph nodes that subsequently undergo cystic dilatation due to obstruction {3393663; 6809660}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions may be unilocular or multilocular, with a mean size of 25 mm {29491615}.

Histopathology

Lymphoepithelial cyst is a dilated cystic structure lined by squamous epithelium with variable degrees of maturation and/or columnar epithelium. The epithelium lacks cytomorphological evidence of malignancy. The cyst wall exhibits a dense lymphoid infiltrate with well-developed lymphoid follicles, and the epithelium is commonly permeated by lymphocytes. In the setting of AIDS and Sjögren syndrome, the background parenchyma may show features of lymphoepithelial sialadenitis. In HIV-positive patients, immunoreactivity for the major HIV-1 core protein p24 can be seen in follicular dendritic cells and interfollicular multinucleated giant cells within the lymphoid stroma adjacent to the cyst {8394048; 28963391}.

Differential diagnosis

Branchial cleft cysts and isolated lymphoepithelial cysts have striking overlapping morphology. Because both lesions have a benign clinical course, the distinction between them has no practical implications. Cystic salivary gland neoplasms with tumour-associated lymphoid proliferation can mimic a lymphoepithelial cyst.

Cytology

Smears show a mixed lymphoid population and rare benign epithelial elements in a background of proteinaceous fluid {2294703; 10450101; 12357495}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dilated cystic structure; benign epithelial lining; dense lymphoid stroma in cyst wall.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Surgical management is curative for isolated lesions, whereas cysts in the setting of AIDS resolve with highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) or radiotherapy {21237435; 23532713; 31456841}.
