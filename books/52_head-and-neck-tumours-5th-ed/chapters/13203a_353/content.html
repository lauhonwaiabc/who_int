13.2.0.3A Exostosis (osteoma) of the ear

Definition

Exostosis (osteoma) of the ear is a slowly progressive benign growth of bony cortex secondary to chronic irritation {13904891}.

ICD-O coding

9180/0 Osteoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

AA40.Y Other specified acquired deformity of external auditory canal & Osteoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Exostosis of the ear arises in the bony cortex of the external auditory canal (EAC) {30521295} and rarely in the middle ear {16243740}.

Clinical features

Patients with exostoses of the EAC and middle ear are generally asymptomatic but can present with conductive hearing loss {21310100; 25087466}. Patients with exostosis of the EAC can present with recurrent otitis externa, otalgia, and cerumen impaction {21310100}. Exostoses or bone spurs arising in the EAC are also called “surfer’s ear” {30521295}.

Imaging

EAC exostoses are best assessed on high-resolution CT and usually present as bilateral, broad-based, convex thickenings of cortical bone, medial to the isthmus. This is in contrast to EAC osteoma, which is usually unilateral, may be pedunculated, and originates in the lateral half of the EAC.

Epidemiology

Exostosis of the EAC affects 630 per 100 000 individuals {12075223}. The frequency is higher in those with regular aquatic exposure, such as from surfing, diving, swimming, kayaking, and other maritime activities {30521295; 32033062}. Exostosis of the EAC has been identified in prehistoric humans and is used anthropologically to identify cultures with an aquatic lifestyle {27834109}. In contrast, exostosis of the middle ear is extremely rare and has been described in dry/desert areas in both modern and ancient societies {16243740}.

Etiology

Exostosis is most likely to be due to chronic irritation of the bone, particularly in the EAC where the cutaneous lining is very thin. The incidence is proportional to the frequency and duration of irritant exposure {25772761}. Multiple osteomas may be associated with familial adenomatous polyposis {31070935}.

Pathogenesis

Chronic irritation due to cold temperature, water, pH, and physical irritants leads to vasodilatation, inflammation, and stimulation of osteoblastic activity leading to bone formation {13904891}.

Macroscopic appearance

Exostoses appear as broad-based bony proliferations resembling normal bone cortex, although in most instances pathologists receive multiple fragments.

Histopathology

Histologically, exostoses are composed of lamellar bone. Anastomosing trabeculae are present, with interspersed vascular tissues {31070935}. Of note, marrow spaces are absent.

Cytology

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: normal/mature-appearing lamellar bony cortex in the appropriate radiological context.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Exostoses of the EAC and the middle ear are benign lesions, usually managed conservatively with a good outcome.
