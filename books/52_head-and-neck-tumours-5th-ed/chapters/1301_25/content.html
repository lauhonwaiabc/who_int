1.3.0.1 Sinonasal ameloblastoma

Definition

Sinonasal ameloblastoma (SA) is a benign but locally aggressive extragnathic sinonasal tract epithelial odontogenic neoplasm composed of ameloblast-like cells and stellate reticulum.

ICD-O coding

9310/0 Ameloblastoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E83.0 & XH1SV4 Benign osteogenic tumours of bone or articular cartilage of skull or face & Ameloblastoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SA affects the nasal cavity (septum, lateral wall), paranasal sinuses, or both {9477098; 23243540}.

Clinical features

Patients commonly present with nasal obstruction and/or epistaxis {9477098}. With tumour progression, extension into the nasal vestibule {3927244; 22507424} or orbitofacial deformity may develop {3927244; 9477098}.

Imaging

Imaging studies reveal a solid rather than multicystic appearance, with remodelled and/or destroyed sinus bony walls {22507424}. Radiographic evidence of an intraosseous maxillary alveolar bone component precludes a diagnosis of SA {9477098}.

Epidemiology

SA is very rare, with a strong male predilection. Patients are adults in their sixth to seventh decade of life, notably older than those with its gnathic counterparts {9477098; 29846904}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Although not studied in sinonasal tract tumours, BRAF activating mutations are most common in jaw tumours, followed by mutually exclusive RAS mutations {24374844; 24859340; 24993163; 30216733}, with SMO mutation being a common co-occurring mutation {24859340; 24993163}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface is solid with limited cystic change {9477098; 29846904}.

Histopathology

SA is a basaloid epithelial neoplasm commonly arranged in a plexiform or follicular pattern, with a loosely arranged stellate reticulum–like component centrally. Peripheral cells palisade with reverse polarity and subnuclear vacuolization. Direct surface epithelial continuity may be identified {9477098}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes salivary gland neoplasms, skin basal cell carcinoma, and basaloid squamous cell carcinoma. Craniopharyngioma is separable with clinicoradiographic correlation.

Cytology

Hypercellular smears show tissue fragments of small basaloid cells with hyperchromatic round nuclei and peripheral palisading, and distinct larger cells with more open chromatin. There may be occasional stromal fragments with spindle cells with elongated nuclei and abundant clear cytoplasm {9250285}. Keratin and cholesterol crystals are not seen {32841532}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sinonasal location; radiographic exclusion of jaw origin; ameloblastic-type epithelium with central stellate reticulum–like material.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Morbidity depends on tumour size and sites of tumour extension. Local recurrences may occur, but no distant metastases are reported {9477098}.
