8.0.5.3 EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour

Definition

EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour is a tumour with smooth muscle differentiation that is associated with EBV infection in the setting of immunosuppression.

ICD-O coding

8897/1 EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7Y & XH00B4 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of other specified site & Smooth muscle tumour, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: smooth muscle tumour in immunocompromised patient.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour in the head and neck region is very rare. It occurs in the larynx, oropharynx, and eye/orbit more frequently than in the nasal cavity {30120720; 20690046; 33891274}. Some patients show multifocal disease throughout the body, thought to result from independent monoclonal events related to EBV infection rather than from metastasis {16330945}.

Clinical features

Patients have usually had a kidney transplantation, HIV infection, malnutrition, or other disease/event causing immunodeficiency before the emergence of the tumour. Dysphonia, hoarseness, dyspnoea, cough, and pain may result from airway narrowing.

Epidemiology

The age range is wide (8–59 years). EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour shows no sex predilection, and it tends to occur in patients of Asian ethnicity {33891274}. Patients with primary (inherited) immunodeficiency tend to be children {30120720; 29535735}.

Etiology

The etiology involves EBV infection in the setting of T-lymphocyte immunosuppression.

Pathogenesis

Mechanisms of the role of EBV infection in tumorigenesis are not fully understood. The EBV type III latency pattern is suggested to be involved, with activation of the mTOR/AKT pathway {19706821; 23682851}.

Macroscopic appearance

Laryngeal mucosal tumours are polypoid.

Histopathology

The tumour comprises intersecting fascicles of spindle cells with ample eosinophilic cytoplasm and blunt-ended nuclei. Some tumours also contain epithelioid cells with open nuclear chromatin in box-shaped to oval nuclei {33891274}. Nuclear atypia or increased mitotic activity can be seen; necrosis is uncommon except in HIV-positive patients. By immunohistochemistry, tumour cells are positive for SMA, h-caldesmon, and SMMHC. Strong nuclear positivity for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) by in situ hybridization allows distinction from other SMA-expressing tumours {25027306}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of EBV infection, usually by EBER in situ hybridization, is required.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a clinical history of immunosuppression; a tumour with smooth muscle differentiation; evidence of EBV infection.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour shows indolent behaviour and can be removed conservatively without radical resection. Prognosis is mainly dependent on the immune condition of the patient. Some posttransplant cases respond to reduced immunosuppression.
