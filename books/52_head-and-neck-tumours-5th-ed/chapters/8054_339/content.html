8.0.5.4 Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential

Definition

Smooth muscle tumours of uncertain malignant potential (STUMPs) show morphological features that exceed the criteria for leiomyoma or its subtypes, yet are insufficient for a diagnosis of leiomyosarcoma, and behave in a malignant fashion in only a minority of cases.

ICD-O coding

8897/1 Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential

ICD-11 coding

2F7Y & XH00B4 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of other specified site & Smooth muscle tumour, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Rare examples of STUMP have been reported in the sinonasal tract {12653572; 27879303}, but leiomyoma and leiomyosarcoma are much more common {2242259; 29270859}.

Clinical features

Sinonasal tumours present with nasal obstruction and facial pain.

Epidemiology

Epidemiological data are limited to single case reports. Patients with STUMP are aged 20–44 years, with both sexes affected {12653572; 27879303}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

STUMPs typically form an ulcerated submucosal mass with a fleshy, pale-tan appearance.

Histopathology

These tumours form interlacing fascicles and bundles of smooth muscle cells with blunt-ended nuclei and ample eosinophilic cytoplasm, show slightly increased cellularity, mild to moderate pleomorphism, and a mitotic count of ≤ 4 mitoses/2 mm2 {12653572; 27879303}. Tumour cells blend with the musculature of the tunica media of vessels {12653572}. Erosion or invasion of surrounding bone or cartilage may be present {12653572}. Isolated pleomorphism is generally seen in symplastic leiomyoma {12401830}.

Immunocytochemistry is positive for SMA, MSA, and desmin, with no nuclear expression of β-catenin, HMB45, SOX10, S100, or keratins {27137987}.

The differential diagnosis includes leiomyoma, leiomyosarcoma, biphenotypic sinonasal sarcoma, glomangiopericytoma, peripheral nerve sheath tumour, perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa), and meningioma. The modest cellularity, moderate pleomorphism, and lower mitotic count are usually sufficient to make the distinction from sarcoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a smooth muscle tumour with mild to moderate pleomorphism, increased cellularity, and a mitotic count of ≤ 4 mitoses/2 mm2; no tumour necrosis.

Desirable: desmin, SMA, and/or caldesmon (in selected cases).

Staging

Not performed

Prognosis and prediction

STUMPs are more likely than leiomyomas to recur locally {12653572; 27879303}.
