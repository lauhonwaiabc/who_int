8.0.1.2 Liposarcomas

Definition

Liposarcomas are a heterogeneous group of malignant adipocytic neoplasms with type-dependent features and include atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma (ALT/WDLPS); dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS); myxoid liposarcoma (MLPS); pleomorphic liposarcoma (PLPS); and myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma (MPLPS).

Related terminology

Not recommended: for ALT/WDLPS: atypical lipoma; lipoma-like liposarcoma; for MPLPS: pleomorphic myxoid liposarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

About 80% of liposarcomas in this site involve the subcutaneous tissue of the face, neck, and scalp {23728920}. MLPS may metastasize to unusual sites, including the head and neck region {22473934}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with a mass. Symptoms are site- and size-dependent {12379747}.

Epidemiology

Liposarcomas account for 2–9% of sarcomas in the head and neck. The mean patient age is 60 years. Male patients (76%) outnumber female patients, and White patients (> 80%) outnumber Black and Asian patients. The most common subtype is ALT/WDLPS, followed by MLPS, PLPS, and DDLPS {23728920}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ALT/WDLPS and DDLPS: Amplified sequences of 12q14-15, including MDM2, TSPAN31, HMGA2, CDK4, YEATS4, CPM, FRS2, and STAT6 {31501988; 24457460}.

MLPS: The driver mutation is FUS::DDIT3 or EWSR1::DDIT3 {7566973; 8637704}.

PLPS: Complex numerical chromosome aberrations, including loss of RB1, TP53, and NF1 {31501988}.

MPLPS: Complex numerical chromosome aberrations, including loss of RB1; mutations in KMT2D {26647907}.

Macroscopic appearance

Liposarcomas form (multi)nodular fatty (ALT/WDLPS) or grey-white firm tumours with a variable fatty component (DDLPS, PLPS). Myxoid/gelatinous areas can be observed in all lesions. Necrotic areas are mainly found in high-grade lesions {31950475}.

Histopathology

ALT/WDLPS consists of atypical adipocytes with enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei. Irregular septa contain atypical stromal cells with hyperchromatic nuclei. Lipoblasts may be present {12379747}. MDM2 and CDK4 provide strong immunohistochemical support for the diagnosis {22301498}.

DDLPS shows a variable, spindle cell sarcomatous pattern often combined with a well-differentiated liposarcoma component. Lipoblastic, rhabdomyoblastic, or osteogenic differentiation may occur {12379747; 22301498; 24457460; 31950475}.

MLPSs are arranged in lobules consisting of a variable number of prelipoblasts and lipoblasts in a myxoid background with a characteristic plexiform capillary network. Adipocytes may be present. The lobules often show peripheral condensation of primitive cells. High-grade MLPSs show > 5% solid sheets of undifferentiated mainly round cells {31950475}. DDIT3 immunohistochemistry is a surrogate marker for the fusion gene {33465826}.

PLPS is a high-grade sarcoma defined by the presence of atypical lipoblasts in an otherwise high-grade sarcoma with spindle cell and/or epithelioid cell features. A dedifferentiated liposarcoma may be excluded by expression of MDM2/CDK4 or amplification of MDM2. RB1 can be absent {31950475}.

MPLPS shows mixed features of MLPS and PLPS, with atypical and bland-looking lipoblasts, variable pleomorphic stromal cells, myxoid matrix, and less vascularity {31501988; 19194281}.

Cytology

Cytological features of the subtypes of liposarcoma reflect their histological composition {19899126}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

ALT/WDLPS and DDLPS are associated with MDM2 amplification. MLPS is associated with FUS/EWSR1::DDIT3 fusions. MPLPS and PLPS are associated with RB1 deletion.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

ALT/WDLPS: atypical stromal cells in irregular septa; adipocytes may show (subtle) atypia.

DDLPS: non-adipogenic sarcoma and often atypical fat cells.

MLPS: prelipoblasts (round or spindle cells) and lipoblasts; chicken-wire vasculature; myxoid background; round cell hypercellular areas > 5% in high-grade lesions.

PLPS: pleomorphic lipoblasts in an otherwise high-grade sarcoma NOS.

MPLPS: mixed features of PLPS and MLPS.

Desirable: appropriate immunocytochemistry or molecular features (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging is not clinically relevant in ALT/WDLPS. For other subtypes, staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Liposarcomas of the head and neck are usually early-stage and low-grade. Survival is significantly better for liposarcoma of the head and neck than other sites {23728920}. However, the prognosis depends on the subtype and localization.

ALT/WDLPS: May recur if incompletely excised {24800932}.

DDLPS: Often recurs and shows metastatic potential in a small subset {24800932}.

MLPS: Recurrence and metastatic disease depend on grade. Most tumours behave as low-grade sarcomas. When metastasized, the outcome is worse {22190864}.

PLPS, MPLPS: Often show aggressive behaviour, with metastases and a fatal outcome {24800932; 31501988; 31950475}. Superficial lesions often have a good prognosis {31950475}.
