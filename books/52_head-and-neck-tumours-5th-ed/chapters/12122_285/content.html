12.1.2.2 Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

Definition

Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC) is a poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) composed of cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, vesicular chromatin, and prominent nucleoli.

ICD-O coding

8013/3 Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2B6A.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of oropharynx

2B6B.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of nasopharynx

2B68.Z Malignant neoplasms of submandibular or sublingual glands, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma, large cell type; neuroendocrine carcinoma, large cell type.

Not recommended: grade 3 neuroendocrine carcinoma; large cell neuroendocrine tumour; high-grade neuroendocrine neoplasm.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LCNECs occur with similar frequency in the larynx, oropharynx, and sinonasal tract {22024350; 26735857}. In the larynx, > 80% of LCNECs arise in the supraglottis {24596175; 20589486}. Additional tumours are rarely reported in the hypopharynx, nasopharynx, and salivary glands {10824928; 15882834; 22718848}.

Clinical features

Presenting symptoms correspond to anatomical site. In the larynx or oropharynx, LCNECs often manifest with hoarseness or dysphagia. Sinonasal tract tumours frequently cause mass effect including nasal obstruction, epistaxis, or anosmia. Regional or distant metastases are identified at presentation in 60–80% of laryngeal LCNECs {20589486; 24596175; 31437725; 34401980}.

Epidemiology

LCNECs are uncommon laryngeal neoplasms {24596175; 26183520; 34401980}. The exact prevalence of LCNEC in the sinonasal tract is difficult to determine because of the variable terminology used, but they are relatively rare. Across head and neck sites, LCNEC has a median age of 60–64 years and an M:F ratio of 1.5–4:1 {20589486; 22082601; 26735857; 31437725; 34401980}.

Etiology

Risk factors for LCNEC are also dependent on the site of origin. Laryngeal LCNECs are associated with tobacco use in > 90% of cases {24596175}. A subset of oropharyngeal and sinonasal LCNECs harbour high-risk HPV, although most patients also have a history of smoking {26735857}. Rare cases of nasopharyngeal LCNEC are EBV-positive {26064748; 29302900}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of LCNEC in the larynx, oral cavity, and hypopharynx is not well established. By immunohistochemistry, tumours show frequent loss of RB1 and p53 overexpression, raising parallels with a subset of lung LCNEC {22082601; 22718848; 27392929; 33433884}. In oropharyngeal and sinonasal LCNEC where HPV is detected, RNA in situ hybridization confirms the presence of transcriptionally active virus, suggesting a driver role for viral oncoproteins {26735857}. In the sinonasal tract, a significant proportion of LCNECs have recurrent IDH2 mutations similar to those frequently seen in sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma (SNUC). They also share a global methylation phenotype and pathway activation profile with SNUC, raising the possibility that IDH2-mutated LCNEC and SNUC represent a phenotypic spectrum of a single entity {28493366; 30332658; 31186531; 34265800}. Although large cell morphology and positivity for neuroendocrine markers are also seen in a subset of sinonasal tumours that show immunohistochemical loss of SMARCA4, such tumours are now included within the spectrum of SWI/SNF complex–deficient sinonasal carcinoma rather than LCNEC {31934917}.

Macroscopic appearance

LCNECs are generally large and highly infiltrative, and they form fleshy submucosal masses with frequent ulceration and necrosis.

Histopathology

LCNEC shows nested, organoid, or trabecular growth with frequent peripheral palisading, rosette formation, and central comedonecrosis. The tumour cells are generally larger than the diameter of three lymphocytes and have abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Nuclei have coarse to speckled chromatin, often with a single prominent nucleolus. The mitotic rate is > 10 mitoses/2 mm2 but is generally much higher.

Immunohistochemistry

LCNECs should be positive for cytokeratin, particularly low-molecular-weight cocktails such as CAM5.2. The expression of first-generation neuroendocrine differentiation markers (e.g. synaptophysin and chromogranin A) may be variable with a dot-like staining pattern {20589486; 22718848; 27529763}. For this reason, the use of second-generation neuroendocrine differentiation biomarkers, especially INSM1, has been shown to be useful in the diagnostic workup of neoplasms with variable synaptophysin and/or focal/absent chromogranin A expression {29438167; 31186531; 31857137; 32813226; 33786701}. The use of nonspecific biomarkers alone (e.g. CD56, CD57, and NSE) is discouraged. Thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) expression is present in a minority of cases {22718848}, and focal positivity for p63 or p40 can be seen {22718848}. The role of Ki-67 for grading is not well established, but LCNECs have a Ki-67 index of > 20%, and often > 50% {22082601; 34401980}. Abnormal p53 expression {22082601; 27392929} and global RB1 loss {27392929; 34401980} can occur. p16 may be overexpressed in LCNEC regardless of high-risk HPV status {27392929}.

Differential diagnosis

In all sites, LCNEC can show morphological overlap with small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (SCNEC), basaloid and non-keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma, and solid adenoid cystic carcinoma. In the sinonasal tract, it should also be distinguished from olfactory neuroblastoma, NUT carcinoma, and SWI/SNF complex–deficient sinonasal carcinomas. LCNEC can be distinguished from SNUC, but IDH2-mutant tumours in both categories may represent a phenotypic spectrum, which can be recognized by mutant IDH2 immunohistochemistry {29683816; 30206411; 31186531; 33017591}.

Cytology

FNA of LCNEC shows hypercellular smears composed of large cells with ample cytoplasm, large nuclei, and vesicular chromatin with frequent large nucleoli {30475447}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Routine high-risk HPV RNA in situ hybridization testing is not recommended {27392929; 29251996}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a high-grade malignancy with nested, organoid, or trabecular growth of neuroendocrine-type cells with abundant cytoplasm and round nuclei with prominent nucleoli; > 10 mitoses/2 mm2; immunohistochemical evidence of diffuse neuroendocrine differentiation; positive for ≥ 1 cytokeratin.

Desirable: peripheral palisading, rosette formation, or comedonecrosis; Ki-67 proliferation index > 20% (often much higher).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for the appropriate anatomical site.

Prognosis and prediction

Laryngeal LCNEC has a poor prognosis, with 5-year survival rates of 10–15% {20589486; 22082601; 24596175; 26611246}. Outcomes in sinonasal LCNEC are unclear overall, although the subgroup with IDH2 mutations have a similar prognosis to that of IDH2-mutated SNUC, with a 3-year survival rate of 53% {31186531}. HPV positivity is not associated with improved prognosis in LCNEC {26735857}. The prognostic implications of EBV-positive LCNEC are not yet clear.
