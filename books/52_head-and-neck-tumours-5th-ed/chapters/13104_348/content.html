13.1.0.4 Chondrodermatitis nodularis chronica helicis

Definition

Chondrodermatitis nodularis chronica helicis (CNCH) is a non-neoplastic, inflammatory, degenerative process of the external ear, characterized by necrobiotic changes in the dermis and perichondrium.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

AA12 Chondrodermatitis nodularis

Related terminology

Acceptable: chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis.

Not recommended: Winkler disease; chondrodermatitis chronica auriculae.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The helix is affected twice as often as the antihelix, but any part of the auricle may be affected {17524165; 27399947}, with the right affected more than the left {13079290; 13124280; 21276202; 24206803}.

Clinical features

Patients present clinically with an intensely painful nodule. The pain is usually nocturnal and magnified by any ear pressure {13079290; 13124280; 21276202; 24206803; 30505787}. CNCH begins as a reddish, round, indurated nodule, which develops a central crater containing crust-like to horny material, with atrophic adjacent skin. Drainage of purulent material is common. Dermoscopy shows a global configuration (daisy pattern) of thick white lines, radially arranged and converging on a central rounded lump {29926164; 30775149}.

Epidemiology

CNCH is a relatively common external ear skin lesion {13079290; 17524165; 27399947} with a male predilection (M:F ratio: 2:1) {17524165; 21276202; 24206803; 27399947}. Most patients are in their seventh decade of life {13079290; 17524165; 21276202; 24206803; 27399947}. Young patients may have an underlying systemic disease causing ischaemic necrobiosis {15811116}.

Etiology

Exposure to sunlight or cold predisposes individuals to CNCH {24206803}.

Pathogenesis

Dermal microtrauma (e.g. due to helmets, headsets, actinic damage, frostbite) combined with the relatively tenuous aural vascularity {19413613; 29805936} leads to subepidermal injury, and with reduced repair capacity and collagen degeneration in the elderly, the lesion becomes persistent {13079290; 15811116}. Systemic diseases associated with microangiopathy may predispose individuals to CNCH {13079290; 15811116; 21276202}.

Macroscopic appearance

CNCH appears as a small central crater, usually 4–15 mm, containing necrotic material.

Histopathology

A central cup-shaped crater is filled with necrotic debris, fibrin, and a variable number of inflammatory cells. A parakeratotic and hyperkeratotic crust is usually present, surrounded by acanthotic epithelium often with acute inflammation. An inverted funnel–shaped defect is present in the dermis, with the collagen underlying the crater appearing homogeneous and eosinophilic, admixed with fibrin. There is superficial cartilage erosion with loss of cartilage basophilia, associated with fibrosis and chondrocyte dropout causing smudgy cartilage degeneration. Rarely, devitalized cartilage may extrude through the crater.

Differential diagnosis

CNCH is very commonly confused clinically with skin neoplasms (squamous or basal cell carcinoma), and rarely with relapsing polychondritis or granuloma annulare.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cup-shaped crater with necrotic debris and fibrin extending to involve the superficial cartilage.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrences occur in as many as 35% of patients and usually develop within a year {15149500; 19116517; 24684884; 27399947}.
