9.0.3.3 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) NOS is a diffuse proliferation of neoplastic large B-lymphoid cells. This is a morphologically and molecularly heterogeneous entity that does not meet the diagnostic criteria of specific large B-cell lymphoma neoplasms.

ICD-O coding

9680/3 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2A81.Z Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

About 60% of head and neck DLBCL cases involve lymph nodes; many of these patients have systemic disease. About 40% of DLBCL cases are localized to the head and neck, with a nearly even split in terms of lymph node and extranodal involvement. Any extranodal site may be involved. The Waldeyer ring, particularly the palatine tonsil, is the most common site {26113048; 28772206}. DLBCL is the most common type of lymphoma involving the head and neck region {22700993; 26118762; 28772206}. DLBCL of the thyroid is considered in the WHO classification of endocrine tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours [Internet; beta version ahead of print]. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/53.]].

Clinical features

Patients with DLBCL at any site often present with a mass. Site-specific symptoms include altered vision, sinus or nasal cavity obstruction (often mimicking an upper respiratory infection), an oropharyngeal ulcer with or without pain or bleeding, enlarged tonsil(s), or hoarseness. A subset of patients have B-type symptoms.

Epidemiology

Lymphomas represent about 5–10% of all malignant neoplasms that involve the head and neck, and DLBCL is the most common type of lymphoma. DLBCL can involve nodal or extranodal sites. Most patients are in their sixth or seventh decade of life, and the M:F ratio in most studies is 1.2–1.6:1 {28772206}.

Etiology

The etiology is largely unknown. In the salivary glands, DLBCL is often preceded by extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue, associated with the autoimmune disease Sjögren syndrome {32153241}.

Pathogenesis

DLBCL is biologically heterogeneous, and gene expression profiling can divide cases into germinal-centre B-cell, activated B-cell, and unclassified subtypes. More recent studies of DLBCL using high throughput methods to assess gene expression, copy-number alterations, and gene mutations have shown activation of various cellular pathways {31648986}, and several genetic subtypes of DLBCL have been proposed {32289277; 33249557}. Molecular studies focused on DLBCL of the head and neck are few. Translocations that occur in nodal DLBCL, such as those involving MYC, BCL2, and BCL6, also occur in head and neck DLBCL, more commonly in nodal cases.

Macroscopic appearance

DLBCL often forms a soft, fish-flesh mass. Necrosis may be visible.

Histopathology

Head and neck DLBCL is composed of centroblasts or, less often, immunoblasts arranged in a diffuse pattern. Some aggressive neoplasms exhibit a starry-sky pattern. Mitotic figures are common. Coagulative necrosis may be present.

Immunophenotypic studies show that DLBCL cells are positive for pan–B-cell antigens and negative for pan–T-cell antigens. Ki-67 often shows a high proliferation index ranging from 60% to 90%. About 50–75% of cases are of non–germinal-centre B-cell type, usually positive for IRF4 (MUM1) and FOXP1. About 25–50% of cases are of germinal-centre B-cell type, usually positive for CD10, BCL6, LMO2, or GCET1 {22700993; 28772206}. In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is negative. Large B-cell lymphoma with IRF4 rearrangement should be considered, especially if the lymphoma cells are positive for IRF4 (MUM1) and CD10.

Cytology

Cytological preparations typically show sheets of large, discohesive cells with lymphoid morphology that may be associated with necrosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Interphase cytogenetic testing is strongly recommended to exclude the diagnosis of DLBCL with MYC and BCL2 rearrangements. If MYC rearrangement or simultaneous MYC and BCL6 rearrangement is detected, the case should be reported as DLBCL-NOS in younger patients. Localization to the head and neck region with strong and uniform IRF4 (MUM1) expression suggests large B-cell lymphoma with IRF4 rearrangement; IRF4 rearrangement should be evaluated in such cases, and if present excludes DLBCL-NOS. Gene expression profiling is the gold standard for cell-of-origin determination. Detailed mutation spectra as obtained by targeted panel and/or whole exome/genome sequencing currently have very limited consequences and little role in routine practice, but this may change as more knowledge and diagnostic assays become available.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: head and neck region; diffuse proliferation of large malignant lymphoid cells; B-cell lineage; exclusion of other specific entities of large B-cell lymphoma.

Staging

Lymphomas are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}. Most patients with extranodal DLBCL of the head and neck present with stage IE disease; some patients have involvement of regional lymph nodes (stage II).

Prognosis and prediction

Patients are treated with systemic immunochemotherapy, often with involved-field radiotherapy {17429099; 31793408}. Clinical stage and the International Prognostic Index (IPI) are useful for assessing prognosis. Patients with extranodal DLBCL of the head and neck have a better prognosis than patients with solely nodal disease or those with extranodal DLBCL at other sites {24273125; 29861074}.
