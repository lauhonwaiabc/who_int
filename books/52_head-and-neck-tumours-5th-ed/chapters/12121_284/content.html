12.1.2.1 Small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

Definition

Small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (SCNEC) is a poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) composed of epithelial cells with scant cytoplasm, hyperchromatic nuclei, finely granular chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli, a high mitotic count, and frequent necrosis.

Related terminology

Acceptable: neuroendocrine carcinoma, small cell type; poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma, small cell type.

Not recommended: oat cell carcinoma; small cell undifferentiated carcinoma; grade 3 neuroendocrine carcinoma; high-grade neuroendocrine neoplasm.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately 60% of head and neck SCNECs arise in the larynx, the majority in the supraglottis {27859290}. About 35% occur in the sinonasal tract, most frequently affecting the nasal cavity {26880574; 27859290}. Fewer than 5% involve the oropharynx, oral cavity, nasopharynx, or major salivary glands {20659699; 26258144; 27859290}.

Clinical features

Symptoms depend on the site of origin. In the larynx, tumours frequently manifest with hoarseness or dysphagia. Sinonasal tumours often show mass effect with nasal obstruction or epistaxis. About 50–70% of all SCNECs have regional or distant metastasis at the time of presentation {1653928; 27938993; 31437725}. Paraneoplastic syndromes rarely occur {17727081}.

Epidemiology

SCNEC accounts for 40–50% of laryngeal neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) {24596175; 26183520}. Its prevalence in the sinonasal tract is not well established. Laryngeal SCNEC has a mean age of 59–62 years and an M:F ratio of 1.6–3.4:1 {6299507; 24596175; 27859290; 31437725}, whereas sinonasal SCNEC has a mean age of 51–58 years and an M:F ratio of 1–2.2:1 {9712424; 16526967; 26880574; 27859290; 27938993}.

Etiology

Risk factors correspond to anatomical site. More than 90% of laryngeal SCNECs are associated with tobacco use, but this association is not strong in the sinonasal tract {31437725; 25992139}. The majority of oropharyngeal SCNECs are positive for high-risk HPV, although patients often have a history of smoking as well {21997688; 22301491}. A subset of sinonasal SCNECs also harbour high-risk HPV {23095507; 26229021}. There is a disproportionate incidence of HPV18 involvement in SCNEC, although HPV16 probably remains the prevalent genotype {21997688; 23095507; 31550725; 33232848}. With the exception of rare SCNECs that arise ex pleomorphic adenoma {22736150; 33741286}, most salivary gland tumours previously classified as SCNEC carry Merkel cell polyomavirus or ultraviolet (UV) radiation signature mutations and are best classified as metastatic Merkel cell carcinoma {22204708; 25457888; 30986802}.

Pathogenesis

The molecular basis of laryngeal, oropharyngeal, and oral SCNECs has not been fully evaluated. Immunohistochemistry demonstrates consistent RB1 loss and p53 overexpression, similarly to lung SCNEC {27392929; 22082601; 33433884}. RNA in situ hybridization highlights transcriptional activity in HPV-associated SCNEC, suggesting that viral oncoproteins drive these neoplasms {29556964}. Emerging molecular data on sinonasal SCNECs suggest that they arise along unique pathways with recurrent ARID1A mutations {31186531}. Although some sinonasal tumours with immunohistochemical SMARCA4 loss have small cell morphology and express neuroendocrine markers, such tumours are now regarded as SMARCA4-deficient sinonasal carcinoma rather than SCNEC {31934917}.

Macroscopic appearance

SCNECs are generally large and highly infiltrative, and they form fleshy submucosal masses with frequent ulceration and necrosis.

Histopathology

SCNEC grows in sheets and nests, with occasional trabeculae, peripheral palisading, or rosettes. Tumour cells are usually smaller than the diameter of three lymphocytes, with scant cytoplasm and indistinct cellular borders. They have large hyperchromatic nuclei, finely granular to stippled chromatin, and absent or inconspicuous nucleoli with frequent nuclear moulding. The mitotic count is > 10 mitoses/2 mm2 but is often much higher, with numerous apoptotic bodies and necrosis. Crush artefact with extravasated DNA coating blood vessels (Azzopardi phenomenon) is also common. Approximately 5% of laryngeal SCNECs but as many as 80% of oropharyngeal SCNECs occur in combination with another histological tumour type, usually squamous cell carcinoma {24596175; 21997688}.

Immunohistochemistry

SCNEC should be positive for cytokeratin, particularly low-molecular-weight cocktails such as CAM5.2 and frequently in a perinuclear dot-like distribution {9712424; 20090218}. The expression of first-generation neuroendocrine differentiation markers (e.g. synaptophysin and chromogranin A) may be variable with a dot-like staining pattern {9712424; 27529763}. For this reason, the use of second-generation neuroendocrine differentiation biomarkers, especially INSM1 (a nuclear transcription factor that regulates neuroendocrine differentiation), has been shown to be useful in the diagnostic workup of neoplasms with variable synaptophysin and/or focal/absent chromogranin A expression {29438167; 32813226; 31857137; 33786701}. The use of nonspecific biomarkers alone (e.g. CD56, CD57, and NSE) is discouraged; results should be interpreted in conjunction with diffuse INSM1 expression or diffuse synaptophysin and chromogranin A expression. Thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) is positive in a subset of cases {10976695}. The role of Ki-67 for grading head and neck SCNECs is not well established, but most have a Ki-67 index of > 20% (often > 70%) {22082601; 22699626}. Abnormal p53 expression {22082601; 27392929} and global RB1 loss {27392929} are common. p16 is often overexpressed in SCNEC regardless of HPV status {27392929; 29251996}.

Differential diagnosis

In all sites, SCNEC displays histological overlap with large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC) (abundant cytoplasm / prominent nucleoli), well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) with crush artefact (< 10 mitoses/2 mm2 and < 20% Ki-67 with no aberrant p53 and RB1 expression), basaloid squamous cell carcinoma (squamous pearls / surface dysplasia and p63/p40 positivity; lacking diffuse INSM1), and solid adenoid cystic carcinoma (neuroendocrine marker–negative). Sinonasal SCNEC should be distinguished immunohistochemically from many small blue round cell tumours, including olfactory neuroblastoma (cytokeratin-negative), lymphoma (CD45-positive), melanoma (S100/SOX10-positive), rhabdomyosarcoma (desmin/myogenin-positive), Ewing sarcoma (positive for CD99/NKX2-2), NUT carcinoma (NUTM1-positive), SMARCB1-deficient sinonasal carcinoma (SMARCB1 [INI1] loss), SMARCA4-deficient sinonasal carcinoma (SMARCA4 [BRG1] loss), and sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma (neuroendocrine marker–weak/patchy to negative).

Cytology

FNA specimens show hypercellular sheets of small blue round cells with nuclear moulding, numerous mitotic figures and apoptotic bodies, and powdery salt-and-pepper chromatin with background crush artefact and necrotic debris {30468701; 30475447}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing is not needed for the diagnosis of SCNEC. Routine high-risk HPV RNA in situ hybridization testing is not recommended {27392929; 29251996}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a high-grade carcinoma with minimal cytoplasm, hyperchromatic moulded nuclei, finely granular chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli; > 10 mitoses/2 mm2; immunohistochemical evidence of diffuse neuroendocrine differentiation; immunohistochemical reactivity for ≥ 1 cytokeratin.

Desirable: cell size smaller than the diameter of 3 lymphocytes; prominent apoptotic bodies and necrosis; paranuclear dot-like cytokeratin reactivity; Ki-67 proliferation index > 20% (often > 70%).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for the appropriate anatomical site.

Prognosis and prediction

In the larynx, as many as 90% of SCNECs develop distant metastases, and the 5-year survival rate is 5–20% {1653928; 22082601; 24596175}. Sinonasal SCNECs have better outcomes, with 5-year survival rates of 40–50% {26880574; 27859290; 27938993}. Regardless of site, lower-stage tumours are associated with longer survival {26183520; 27859290}. HPV-related pathogenesis does not improve prognosis in SCNEC {23095507; 26229021}.
