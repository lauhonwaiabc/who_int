9.0.1.3 EBV-positive mucocutaneous ulcer

Definition

Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)-positive mucocutaneous ulcer (EBVMCU) is a self-limiting lymphoproliferative disorder with a polymorphous lymphoid infiltrate including Hodgkin/Reed–Sternberg (HRS)-like cells that typically involves mucosal and cutaneous sites in immunocompromised patients.

ICD-O coding

9680/1 EBV-positive mucocutaneous ulcer

ICD-11 coding

ME60.Z & XH3SG2 Skin lesion of unspecified nature & EBV-positive mucocutaneous ulcer

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

EBVMCU involves mucosal sites including the oral mucosa, tonsils, palate, gastrointestinal tract, and skin {20154586; 19889052; 22769419; 25007145; 28395107; 30418184; 32561847; 33739792}. Rarely, regional lymph nodes may be involved {20154586; 28395107}.

Clinical features

EBVMCU manifests as well-circumscribed, often painful ulcers in mucosal or cutaneous sites, without systemic symptoms, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, or bone marrow involvement {20154586; 19889052; 22769419; 25007145; 28395107; 30418184; 32561847; 33739792}. Multifocal lesions within one anatomical region may be present in a subset of patients {20154586; 30418184}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of EBVMCU is unknown. It typically occurs in immunosuppressed patients, including elderly patients; and secondary to autoimmune, iatrogenic, HIV, and posttransplant settings {20154586; 19889052; 22769419; 25007145; 28395107; 30418184; 32561847; 33739792; 22236092; 24334644}.

Etiology

EBV is present in all cases and probably etiologically associated.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is not well understood and is probably related to alterations in the cytotoxic T-cell repertoire and functionality in response to EBV infection in immunosuppressed patients. IGH gene rearrangements are detected in 50% of cases; rare TR (TCR) gene rearrangements may be present {20154586}.

Macroscopic appearance

EBVMCU causes a shallow ulcer without mass formation.

Histopathology

Mucosal and cutaneous lesions show well-circumscribed ulcers with a polymorphous infiltrate at the base, containing lymphoid cells ranging from small to large, variable numbers of immunoblasts, plasma cells, and HRS-like cells. Vascular destruction, thrombosis, and necrosis are often prominent; the overlying epithelium may show pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia {20154586; 25007145; 28395107; 19889052; 22236092; 30082493; 30418184; 32561847}. Small cell infiltrates predominate in some cases {33739792}.

Immunohistochemistry shows a mixture of CD20+ and CD3+ lymphoid cells. CD30 is commonly expressed, whereas CD15 is rare. CD8+ T cells may predominate in some cases. Small T cells typically form a boundary with uninvolved adjacent tissue {20154586; 25007145; 28395107; 30418184}.

The differential diagnosis includes EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and classic Hodgkin lymphoma. The circumscription and localization of EBVMCU makes it easily distinguished from lymphomas, although the diagnosis may be challenging in small or core needle biopsies {20154586; 25007145; 28395107; 30082493; 30418184; 32561847; 33739792}.

Cytology

Cytology shows a polymorphous lymphoid infiltrate with necrosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: setting confirmed or highly suspicious for immune deficiency/dysregulation; a well-circumscribed shallow ulcer in mucosal or cutaneous sites, with a polymorphous lymphoid infiltrate; atypical large cells positive for CD20 (variable), CD30 (variable), and PAX5; EBV+ in tissue (EBV viral load measurements in blood are not sufficient for diagnosis).

Desirable: band of CD3+ T cells at the periphery and at the base of the lesion.

Staging

Formal staging procedures for EBVMCU are not indicated; however, when there is clinical suspicion for more extensive lymphoproliferative disorders in a patient with known or suspected immune deficiency/dysregulation, including suspected inborn errors of immunity, staging work-up should be performed to exclude additional and/or more aggressive lymphoproliferative disorders at other sites. Imaging should also be undertaken to exclude mass lesions or deep-seated lesions.

Prognosis and prediction

The majority of cases regress spontaneously or respond to the withdrawal of immunosuppression (if feasible) or to localized therapy. Occasional cases may have a relapsing and remitting course without progression {20154586; 25007145; 28395107; 22769419; 19889052; 24456137}.
