9.0.4.1 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue

Definition

Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (EMZL) is an indolent mature B-cell neoplasm with architectural and cytological features similar to those of reactive mucosa-associated lymphoid tissues occurring in certain extranodal sites.

ICD-O coding

9699/3 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A85.3 Extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma, primary site excluding stomach or skin

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Head and neck EMZL most frequently affects the ocular adnexa (60%) and salivary glands (30–40%) {31757436}. Involvement of the Waldeyer ring {12712477; 18234544}, oral cavity {18158571; 17604660}, larynx {33198051}, and sinonasal tract {26094364; 16320028; 19378323; 10555004} is rare (see #24166Table 10.03). EMZL of the thyroid is considered in the WHO classification of endocrine tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours [Internet; beta version ahead of print]. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/53.]].

Clinical features

Most patients present with painless enlarging masses. Salivary gland lesions occasionally cause pain or facial nerve paralysis {31584614}. Laryngeal tumours may cause hoarseness and cough {33198051; 25280040}. B symptoms are rare.

Epidemiology

The head and neck region is the second most common primary site for EMZL, after the gastrointestinal tract. Most patients are middle-aged and older adults (median age in the seventh decade of life), and there is a slight female preponderance among ocular adnexal lymphoma cases {17255761}. Salivary gland EMZL has a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:2–3) {24417667}, due to association with Sjögren syndrome {24733777; 21075560; 21867583}. The incidence rates of ocular adnexa and salivary gland EMZL in the USA are 0.14 and 0.09–0.10 cases per 100 000 person-years, respectively; the incidence is lower for other head and neck sites {24417667}.

Etiology

EMZL often arises in association with chronic antigenic stimulation related to infection or an autoimmune disorder. Patients with Sjögren syndrome have a 5- to 20-fold increased risk of developing lymphoma {25316606; 18984412}. HCV infection may be a predisposing factor in some salivary gland {17071937} and ocular adnexal EMZLs, especially in Italy {32088088}. Chlamydia psittaci is reported as a possible etiological agent in some series of ocular adnexal lymphoma {15100336}, but not in others {17454602}. Small numbers of EMZL cases arise in association with IgG4-related disease {18580683; 11717546; 29285637}. Occasional laryngeal EMZLs occur in patients with autoimmune disease {25280040}. Rare EMZLs arise in iatrogenically immunosuppressed patients {29957733}.

Pathogenesis

Chronic inflammation leads to the development of clonal B-cell populations that may acquire additional genetic abnormalities and progress to overt lymphoma {20408860; 25316606; 29674500; 30065018}.

IGH and IGL genes are clonally rearranged and show mutated variable regions. Genetic changes vary by site; they result in NF-κB activation. Trisomy 3 and trisomy 18 are common in head and neck EMZLs {17052360}. A subset of cases show t(14;18)(q32;q21) (IGH::MALT1), and t(11;18)(q21;q21) (BIRC3::MALT1) is found in EMZLs in some sites but is rare in head and neck EMZLs {15356642; 18158571}. Inactivating mutations, deletions, and promoter hypermethylation of TNFAIP3 (A20) (a negative regulator of NF-κB) are described, mainly in translocation-negative EMZLs {19006194; 19907439}. Mutated TBL1XR1 and GPR34 are common in salivary EMZLs {29674500; 30065018}. MYD88 p.L265P mutation is rare in EMZL and should raise the possibility of lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma, particularly in cases with marrow involvement and serum M component {22944768; 22808296; 28682481}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are non-circumscribed, firm, and tan-coloured. Interspersed cysts formed by dilated ducts are common in salivary gland EMZL.

Histopathology

EMZLs are composed of small lymphocytes and marginal zone cells with small to medium-sized, slightly irregular nuclei, smooth chromatin, and pale cytoplasm {31912792}. Monocytoid cells with abundant pale cytoplasm are sometimes present and are conspicuous in salivary gland EMZLs. Occasional large lymphoid cells are interspersed. Plasmacytic differentiation occurs in one third of cases, sometimes with Dutcher bodies and infrequently with amyloid deposition {22180478; 18158571}. Remnants of reactive follicles are common, sometimes with follicular colonization. Well-formed lymphoepithelial lesions are commonly found in salivary gland EMZLs, and occasionally in lacrimal gland EMZLs. In the Waldeyer ring, a diagnosis of EMZL may be difficult to establish because crypt epithelium is normally heavily infiltrated by lymphoid cells.

Neoplastic cells express B-lineage markers (CD20, CD22, PAX5), usually IgM, occasionally IgG or IgA, and rarely IgD. They are typically negative for CD5, CD10, BCL6, CD23, cyclin D1, SOX11, and EBV (except for rare EBV+ cases in immunosuppressed patients) {29957733}. IRTA1, a marker of marginal zone cell differentiation, is positive in the majority of cases {22716304}. Expression of CD43 or T-bet (TBX21) on B cells or demonstration of immunoglobulin light chain monotypia by B cells (usually by flow cytometry) or, in cases with plasmacytic differentiation, in plasma cells, can aid in the diagnosis of lymphoma. Salivary gland EMZLs frequently express stereotypic B-cell receptors {32182401; 28682481}, often with rheumatoid factor activity {32182401}. A small subset of EMZLs (< 4%) that express CD5 may be associated with more widespread disease and more aggressive behaviour {17255761; 11697516; 22406370}. Foci of solid or sheet-like growth of large B cells, unassociated with follicular dendritic cell meshworks, represent progression to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.

The differential diagnosis includes other B-cell lymphomas and reactive hyperplasia, including atypical marginal zone hyperplasia (see section Reactive lymphoid hyperplasia) {15256428}.

Cytology

FNAB, when combined with flow cytometry, can be a valuable tool in establishing a diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

IGH genes are clonally rearranged.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: architectural effacement or distortion by a small B-cell proliferation lacking CD5 and CD10 expression.

Desirable: demonstration of monotypic lymphoid and/or plasma cells (or clonally rearranged IG genes), lymphoepithelial lesions, and remnants of underlying reactive lymphoid follicles; presence of lymphoepithelial sialadenitis and/or Sjögren syndrome in salivary gland cases.

Staging

Most lymphomas are localized at presentation {24521680}. Lymph nodes and other extranodal sites may be involved {31733094; 17255761}.

Lymphoid neoplasms are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

Spread to lymph nodes and other mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue sites is not uncommon, but death due to lymphoma is rare {21075560; 24733777} [[Ferry JA, Fung CY, Lucarelli MJ, et al. Ocular adnexal lymphoma: long-term outcome, patterns of failure and prognostic factors in 174 patients. J Hematopathol. 2021 Mar;14:41–52. doi:10.1007/s12308-020-00424-6.]]. The prognosis is more favourable with localized disease and a low-risk MALT Lymphoma International Prognostic Index (MALT-IPI) score {31733094}. Large cell transformation is associated with more aggressive behaviour {10577857}.
