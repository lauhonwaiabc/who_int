13.0.0.1 Ear tumours: Introduction

The ear comprises three distinct regions/compartments: the external ear, the middle ear / temporal bone, and the inner ear. The classification of ear tumours remains similar to that in the previous edition yet is evolving on the basis of emerging molecular discoveries linked to histomorphology and immunohistochemistry.

The most common tumour of the external ear region is (cutaneous) squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). Site-specific external canal neoplasms develop from ceruminous glands and include ceruminous adenoma and ceruminous adenocarcinoma, which both include salivary gland–type neoplasms (e.g. ceruminous pleomorphic adenoma, ceruminous adenoid cystic carcinoma, and ceruminous mucoepidermoid carcinoma). Tumour-like conditions that enter into the differential diagnosis have been included (such as chondrodermatitis nodular chronica helicis and auricular pseudocyst). Exostosis (osteoma) has been added because of the unique findings within the external auditory canal.

Selected tumours of the middle and inner ear have new terminology and expanded definitions. Meningioma has been combined with other extraneuraxial sites and is included as one of the sections on other sinonasal tumours. Aggressive papillary tumour (APT), included in the previous edition, is included within middle ear adenocarcinoma, although it remains controversial whether it is an independent tumour or a subtype of endolymphatic sac tumour (ELST). “Low-grade adenocarcinoma” and “papillary adenocarcinoma of middle ear” are related terms that are preferred to “aggressive papillary tumour” (which is no longer recommended). The tumour arises in the middle ear without involvement of the apical petrous temporal bone and is therefore unrelated to ELST {18633929; 32940914}. Middle ear adenocarcinomas represent a heterogeneous spectrum of distinct tumours with histological, immunohistochemical, and molecular alterations (MKRN1::BRAF fusion) distinctly different from those of middle ear neuroendocrine tumour (MeNET) and ELST {32940914}. The emerging DEK::AFF2 squamous cell carcinoma represents a distinct non-keratinizing SCC that may affect the middle ear / temporal bone {32366754} but is more common in the sinonasal tract {33002918}, and shows histological features of SCC (and therefore, for this edition, is considered a subtype of SCC).

The tumour formerly classified as middle ear adenoma is also a controversial tumour. These tumours have been referred to as carcinoid tumour {16148713} and neuroendocrine adenoma of the middle ear {12011260}. Given their predominant neuroendocrine features, the designation of MeNET has been proposed in line with recent WHO neuroendocrine terminology harmonization across all anatomical sites {30140036}. A recent publication {34041698}, having identified tumours composed of cells comparable to normal intestinal L cells resembling hindgut neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), proposed the name “middle ear NET” and recommended classification within the broader category of neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs). Previous studies proposed admixtures of epithelial non-neuroendocrine elements, making them possibly mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNENs), but diffuse INSM1 immunoreactivity suggests a single cell type. Given their site specificity, however, the tumours have been catalogued within this chapter for this edition.
