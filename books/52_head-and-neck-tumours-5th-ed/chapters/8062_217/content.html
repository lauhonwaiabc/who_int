8.0.6.2 Rhabdomyosarcomas

Definition

Rhabdomyosarcoma is a family of malignant mesenchymal tumours with skeletal muscle differentiation and includes embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS), alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS), pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma, and spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma (SCSRMS).

Related terminology

Not recommended: myosarcoma; malignant rhabdomyoma.

Subtype(s)

Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma; alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma; spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma; pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma

Localization

Within the head and neck (excluding parameningeal disease), rhabdomyosarcoma affects the orbit/eyelid, nasopharynx, ear and temporal bone, and sinonasal tract (in order of frequency), albeit with differences in histological type and age at presentation {1247965; 17346806; 23743294; 28875443; 30055718}.

Clinical features

Diagnosis is often delayed, because symptoms are nonspecific. Metastatic disease is frequently present at diagnosis (25–30%) {28521080}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 35–40% of all rhabdomyosarcomas are located in the head and neck {20718921}, and rhabdomyosarcoma is the most common sinonasal sarcoma {7838548; 12110339; 23743294; 25186315; 28875443}. See the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of this series for additional details.

Etiology

ERMS (but no other subtype) is associated with radiation and several tumour predisposition syndromes {20186103; 27345568; 27617148; 31697451; 33372952}.

Pathogenesis

Most ARMSs harbour a PAX3 or PAX7::FOXO1 fusion {8098985; 8187070; 8275086}. PAX3 and PAX7 are transcription factors with essential roles in myogenesis {26424495}, and the fusion proteins activate many downstream target genes, including MET, ALK, MYCN, and MYOD1 {8643596; 20663909; 30617281}. Less common fusions include PAX3 with FOXO4, NCOA1, and FGFR1 {12183429; 21666686; 24436047}. SCSRMSs separate into three genetic groups: NCOA2 and/or VGLL2 rearrangement, MYOD1 mutation, and no identifiable genetic alteration {23463663; 30181563}. Skull/mandibular intraosseous RMS with spindle and epithelioid phenotype shows EWSR1::TFCP2 or FUS::TFCP2 {33382123}. As many as 50% of ERMSs have RAS pathway mutations {30617281}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are usually polypoid, fleshy, gelatinous to firm, poorly circumscribed, and infiltrative masses {10420698; 25267746}. Necrosis and haemorrhage may be seen. Botryoid ERMS shows multiple grape-like polypoid masses. Mastoid tumours tend to be smaller (< 30 mm) than their extremity counterparts.

Histopathology

ERMS

ERMS shows primitive round to spindle cells with scant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic nuclei, and it may have scattered rhabdomyoblasts. Botryoid ERMS typically shows linear aggregates of tumour cells close to the mucosa (cambium layer), with a cellularity gradient {666648; 9724344; 17443282; 18985676; 26646016}.

ARMS

ARMS typically shows fibrovascular septa separating cellular nests of small, monomorphic, round cells with scant cytoplasm, which tend to coalesce in the centre, with a dyscohesive periphery. The solid pattern shows sheets of tumour cells but lacks fibrovascular septa {28521080; 24113309; 25186315; 16006807; 28875443}.

SCSRMS

SCSRMS consists of a fasciculate proliferation of spindle cells that have elongated nuclei and pale, indistinct cytoplasm, with interspersed spindled or polygonal rhabdomyoblasts that have abundant, brightly eosinophilic cytoplasm {31950473}.

Immunohistochemistry

The skeletal muscle–specific proteins myogenin (MYF4) and MYOD1 are variably expressed both within and between tumours, the proportion of positive cells reflecting the degree of differentiation {16861966}. Occasional expression of keratin, neuroendocrine markers, S100, and ALK may be observed {2455782; 16006807; 18973919; 23307059; 28875443; 33382123}. A panel of markers is essential to avoid misdiagnosis.

Differential diagnosis

The major differential diagnoses depend on the pattern of the tumour, and metastatic tumours must be excluded {18606509}. Fetal rhabdomyoma, Ewing sarcoma, haematolymphoid tumours, melanoma, carcinoma, and neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) must be excluded.

See section Rhabdomyosarcoma with TFCP2 rearrangement for details of intraosseous rhabdomyosarcoma with EWSR1 or FUS::TFCP2 fusion.

Cytology

Smears are highly cellular, with primitive, uniform, round, spindled, and stellate cells with scattered rhabdomyoblasts, sometimes in a tigroid background {16680771; 17636492; 24599626}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PAX3, PAX7, NCOA2, or VGLL2 rearrangement and MYOD1 mutation are seen in selected cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

ERMS: primitive round to spindle cell morphology.

ARMS: monomorphic, primitive-appearing round cells dyscohesively arranged in a nested to solid pattern.

SCSRMS: spindled morphology with/without sclerotic collagenous matrix.

Desirable: rhabdomyoblasts; positive immunohistochemistry for myogenin/MYOD1; detection of supporting genetic alterations (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging is via the Intergroup Rhabdomyosarcoma Study Group (IRSG) stratification {9724344; 15059650; 18521303}.

Prognosis and prediction

Risk stratification predictive of outcome has been addressed with surgicopathological staging and fusion-based classification (IRSG grouping system) {24326270; 30617281; 30373318}. Overall, rhabdomyosarcomas are considered a systemic disease, with specific sites showing a relatively poor prognosis (5-year survival rate: 40–45%) {25186315; 22777866; 16025051; 28875443}. Age and tumour stage (most adult patients have stage IV disease at presentation) are the most important risk factors, with children and female patients having a better outcome than adults or male patients {31456361; 23743294; 23424037}. Patients with fusion-positive ARMS have a worse outcome than those with fusion-negative ARMS and ERMS, with a worse prognosis for tumours with a PAX3::FOXO1 fusion than for those with a PAX7::FOXO1 fusion {24326270; 30617281; 30373318; 23526739; 20351326; 22454413; 22447499; 28035744; 31456361}.
