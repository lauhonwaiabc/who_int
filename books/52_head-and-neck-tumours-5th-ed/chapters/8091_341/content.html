8.0.9.1 Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumour

Definition

Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumours (PMTs) are distinctive neoplasms that cause tumour-induced osteomalacia, usually through the production of FGF23.

ICD-O coding

8990/0 Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumour, NOS

8990/3 Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumour, malignant

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH9T96 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumour, benign

2F7C & XH3B27 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumour, malignant

Related terminology

Acceptable: phosphaturic mesenchymal tumour, mixed connective tissue type.

Subtype(s)

Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumour, malignant

Localization

PMTs may arise in any soft tissue or osseous location {14707860}. After the appendicular skeleton, the head and neck region is the second most common site, with about half of the cases arising in the paranasal sinuses, followed in frequency by the mandible and maxilla; uncommon sites are the floor of the mouth, pharynx, larynx, tongue, and temporal bone {31301876; 31505461; 30206408}.

Clinical features

Most PMTs are small lesions, and patients commonly present with a long history of muscle weakness, fatigue, bone pain, and progressive fractures {33151412; 28450684}. Radionuclide scans (especially 68Ga-DOTATATE PET-CT) are useful in detecting occult PMTs {33709632}. Rare non-phosphaturic PMTs are also reported {31301876; 29514106}.

Epidemiology

PMTs are exceedingly rare; the true incidence is unknown. Fewer than 500 cases have been reported {14707860; 31301876; 30206408}. Tumours affect middle-aged adults with an equal sex distribution and are rarely detected in children or the elderly {31301876; 28614212; 26009620}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

PMTs cause tumour-induced osteomalacia through the production of phosphatonins, most often FGF23. FGF23 inhibits the renal reabsorption of phosphates, inducing phosphaturia, hypophosphataemia, and tumour-induced osteomalacia. Binding of FGF23 to its receptor, FGFR1, activates signalling pathways that upregulate FGF23 production in an autocrine/paracrine loop, which potentially drives PMT tumorigenesis {25319834; 27443518; 31301876}. Roughly 50% of PMTs harbour FN1::FGFR1 fusions or (less often) FN1::FGF1 fusions {25319834; 27443518}. Upregulation of Klotho expression may underlie the pathogenesis of fusion-negative tumours {31792355}. Non-phosphaturic PMTs are probably found earlier or produce less FGF23 {31301876; 27443518; 29514106}.

Macroscopic appearance

PMTs usually are nondescript soft tissue or bone masses. Some may be calcified or haemorrhagic, or contain fat {14707860; 28614212}.

Histopathology

Classic PMTs are composed of bland, ovoid, spindle to stellate cells embedded in a richly vascularized stroma, and they contain the pathognomonic basophilic, smudgy matrix with grungy or flocculent calcification {14707860; 28614212; 31301876} (see #24513Box 9.02). Head and neck PMTs tend to contain adipose tissue, show minimal/absent calcified matrix, are more myoid (particularly sinonasal), and contain prominent thick-walled blood vessels {14707860; 28614212}. Alveolar PMT may induce epithelial (odontogenic) proliferation {30206408; 32049812}.

Extremely rare malignant PMTs show cytological anaplasia, an increased mitotic rate, and necrosis, usually with a sarcomatous appearance {14707860; 26759148}.

The immunophenotype of PMT is nonspecific, including variable expression of CD56, ERG1, SATB2, SSTR2A, CD99, and podoplanin (recognized by D2-40). Chromogenic in situ hybridization for FGF23 mRNA is much more sensitive and specific than FGF23 immunohistochemistry {25025444; 31301876; 14707860; 28614212; 30206408; 26464698; 24060005; 28858396} (see #24514Box 9.03).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FN1::FGFR1 or (infrequently) FN1::FGF1 fusions are identified in > 50% cases and are confirmatory but not essential for diagnosis {33151412; 27443518; 25319834; 31301876; 31792355}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the clinicopathological constellation of a bland spindle cell tumour with a basophilic, smudgy matrix with or without grungy calcification and/or vascularized stroma, along with clinical evidence of phosphate wasting and/or osteomalacia that is reversed by the complete removal of the tumour.

Desirable: FGF23 overexpression in tissues, or elevated levels in the serum (in selected cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The vast majority of PMTs are clinically and histologically benign. Complete tumour excision brings a dramatic resolution of osteomalacia {14707860; 30206408}. Extremely rare instances of malignant PMT may metastasize and cause death. FGFR1 fusions may be a potential marker for targeted use of FGFR1–3 inhibitors {32905668}.

Classic features:

·    Ovoid to spindle to stellate cells

·    Basophilic, smudgy matrix with grungy or flocculent calcification

·    Prominent vascular network

May be present:

·    Myxoid or chondromyxoid stroma

·    Mature adipose tissue

·    Flower-like crystals (or oxalate-like, slate-grey)

·    Microcysts with basophilic secretions

·    Osseous PMTs: epithelioid cells with trabecular/endocrine growth patterns

·    Osteoclastic giant cells (giant cell granuloma–like)

·    Permeative growth within bony trabeculae in osseous PMT (mimics malignant infiltration)

·    Hyaline cartilage (focal)

·    Osteoid deposits (osteoblastoma-like, ossifying fibroma–like)

·    Aneurysmal bone cyst–like areas

·    Fat and thick-walled blood vessels (angiomyolipoma-like)

·    Dermatofibrosarcoma-like infiltration (in subcutis)

Head and neck PMTs:

·    Large amounts of mature adipose tissue common

·    Minimal or absent calcified matrix

·    Myoid-appearing spindle cells (glomoid-appearing)

·    Prominent thick-walled blood vessels (pericytoma-like or haemangioma-like)

·    Alveolar PMT: epithelial nests (possibly from odontogenic rests) admixed with spindle mesenchymal cells may be seen

Malignant PMT:

·    Usually in a recurrent setting

·    Pre-existing component of benign PMT usually identified in the primary tumour

·    Sarcomatous features, resembling adult-type fibrosarcoma or undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma

·    High cellularity, high nuclear grade, necrosis, and increased mitotic rate

·    Matrix production is minimal to absent

 

Positive:

·    SATB2

·    ERG

·    CD56

·    SSTR2A

·    FGF23

·    Podoplanin (D2-40)

·    Vimentin

·    NSE

·    S100

·    BCL2

·    DMP1

·    AE1/AE3 (in the epithelial nests of alveolar PMTs)

Negative:

·    HMB45

·    Melan-A

·    STAT6

·    Desmin

·    KSHV/HHV8
