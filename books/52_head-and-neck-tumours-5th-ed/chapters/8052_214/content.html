8.0.5.2 Leiomyosarcoma

Definition

Leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is a malignant neoplasm composed of cells showing smooth muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8890/3 Leiomyosarcoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B58.Y & XH7ED4 Leiomyosarcoma, other specified primary site & Leiomyosarcoma, NOS

2B58.Z & XH7ED4 Leiomyosarcoma, unspecified primary site & Leiomyosarcoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Subcutaneous and deep neck soft tissues are involved more frequently than mucosal locations; of the latter, the sinonasal tract and oral cavity are the most commonly affected {23434173; 12653572}. Organ-specific cases are rare {21729441; 23434173; 24220466; 29395283}.

Clinical features

Symptoms are those of a mass lesion {23434173; 29270859}. Metastatic LMSs to the head and neck are rare {23434173; 2242259; 27015437}.

Epidemiology

Primary head and neck LMSs are very rare, representing 0.01% of all tumours {2242259; 12653572; 23434173; 29270859; 29588301}. There is an equal sex distribution, and LMS occurs over a wide age range (20–86 years), most commonly in the fifth to sixth decade of life {2242259; 12653572; 23434173; 29270859}.

Etiology

Radiation exposure, immunosuppression, and tumour predisposition syndromes (Li–Fraumeni syndrome, retinoblastoma) are predisposing factors {20803265; 21837677; 23674091; 29220301; 29270859}. Sinonasal LMS may arise after radiotherapy {29395283}.

Pathogenesis

Specific evaluation of head and neck LMS is limited {8431912}, but LMSs show extensive genomic instability, with near-universal inactivation of TP53 and RB1 {21125665; 26692951; 29100075; 29321523}. Head and neck LMSs do not show the driver mutations of MED12 seen in genital LMS {22768200}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are polypoid, firm to fleshy, usually poorly defined, and unencapsulated. Cut sections show a whorled and whitish or tan-grey appearance with areas of haemorrhage, cystic degeneration, and necrosis {23434173; 29270859}.

Histopathology

LMSs are composed of spindle cells arranged in compact interlacing fascicles. Tumour nuclei are oval to elongated (cigar-shaped) and frequently blunt-ended, with a perinuclear vacuole compressing the nucleus. Atypia varies, with enlarged nuclei and hyperchromasia, and sometimes obvious nucleoli. Cytoplasm is eosinophilic or clear. An epithelioid cytomorphology is rarely seen. Osteoclastic and pleomorphic giant cells may occur. Tumours may have a myxoid background and scattered inflammatory cells, and they rarely show dystrophic or psammomatous calcification. Mitotic activity is usually brisk (> 4 mitoses/2 mm2), and comedonecrosis is common {12653572; 2242259; 29270859}. High-grade transformation may be present. Neoplastic cells show strong reactivity for SMA, desmin, and h-caldesmon {29270859}. RB1 is commonly lost, and tumours do not express EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) {2242259; 23434173; 29270859}. The differential diagnosis is primarily with leiomyoma, smooth muscle tumours of uncertain malignant potential, biphenotypic sinonasal sarcoma, perivascular epithelioid cell tumour, spindle cell squamous cell carcinoma, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, and metastatic tumours.

Cytology

Plump spindled cells with cigar-shaped nuclei, fibrillar granular cytoplasm with perinuclear vacuoles, atypia, and possibly mitoses may be seen in FNA smears {21325797; 29395283; 31043260}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fascicles of eosinophilic spindled cells with blunt-ended nuclei showing variable pleomorphism and mitoses.

Desirable: immunolabelling for SMA, desmin, and/or caldesmon (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for head and neck sarcoma.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is often poor (5-year overall survival rate: 20%) due to late presentation and difficulties achieving tumour-free margins {16369174; 29270859}. High-grade tumours have a worse survival rate than low-grade tumours (53% vs 88%) {22777866}. Distant metastasis, often to the lungs, occurs in as many as 50% of cases, often after a prolonged interval {16369174; 23434173; 29270859}.
