9.0.6.3 Rosai-Dorfman disease

Definition

Rosai–Dorfman disease (RDD) is a histiocytic disease with large histiocytes containing hypochromatic nuclei, usually showing emperipolesis of small lymphocytes, S100 positivity, and (often) genetic mutations in the MAPK/ERK pathway.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease

ICD-11 coding

EK92 Histiocytoses of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Acceptable: Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease.

Not recommended: sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RDD most often involves lymph nodes, particularly in the cervical region. About 30–40% of patients have extranodal disease. In the head and neck, the nasal cavity, paranasal sinuses, orbit, and oral cavity are most commonly involved. Less common sites include the salivary glands, soft tissues, and facial bones {8491488}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with painless, slow-growing lymphadenopathy or an extranodal mass. About one third of patients have B-type symptoms.

Epidemiology

RDD has a worldwide distribution. The median age of patients is in the third decade of life, with a wide age range {2180012}. In the USA, there are about 100 new cases per year {29720485}. The disease affects people of all ethnicities but is more common in African Americans.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown, but at least a subset of cases appear to be neoplastic. Assessment of AR (HUMARA) in a small number of cases has shown that RDD is polyclonal {8547085}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is incompletely understood. A subset of cases (~50%) carry gene mutations in the MAPK/ERK pathway and appear to be neoplastic {28664935; 29720485}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lymph nodes are enlarged and matted, often forming multinodular masses.

Histopathology

Lymph nodes show expanded sinuses filled by large histiocytes with plate-like cytoplasm containing central hypochromatic nuclei and distinct, round nucleoli. The histiocytes usually show emperipolesis manifested by multiple small lymphocytes within the histiocyte cytoplasm. Between sinuses, the lymph node contains many small lymphocytes and plasma cells. At low-power magnification, the impression is that of light and dark zones or a striated appearance. Neutrophils and (less often) microabscesses can be present. Fibrosis can be marked, imparting a storiform pattern. Cytological atypia, multinucleation, and mitoses are uncommon. Eosinophils are absent. In lymph nodes, rare RDD cases can be associated with lymphoma of various types {30323237}. Lymphoma-associated RDD is usually small and focally involves lymph nodes.

At extranodal sites, the striated appearance as seen at low power is less common, and emperipolesis can be rare or absent. The mixed inflammatory infiltrate, including plasma cells, lymphocytes, and neutrophils, with variable numbers of histiocytes, can make the diagnosis of RDD challenging. Entities in the differential diagnosis of extranodal RDD include infectious processes, IgG4-related sclerosing diseases, and other histiocytic disorders.

The histiocytes of RDD are strongly positive for S100 and OCT2 {2180012; 28664935; 33177341}. The histiocytes are also commonly positive for phosphorylated ERK or cyclin D1, indicating activation of the MAPK/ERK pathway {30323237; 31172509}. RDD histiocytes are weakly positive or negative for histiocyte-associated antigens such as CD4, CD11c, CD68, and CD163 {28664935}, and are negative for CD1a, CD207 (langerin), and pan–B-cell and T-cell antigens. Background small lymphocytes represent a mixture of T and B cells. Plasma cells are polytypic, and IgG4-positive cells can be numerous.

Cytology

Aspiration smears show many lymphocytes, plasma cells, and large histiocytes, often with emperipolesis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

As many as 50% of RDD cases have mutations in the MAPK/ERK pathway, including KRAS, NRAS, MAP2K1, ARAF, CSF1R, and (rarely) BRAF p.V600E {28664935; 29720485; 31172509; 32601132}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large histiocytes with a characteristic morphology; S100 positivity.

Desirable: emperipolesis; MAPK/ERK pathway mutations (in selected cases).

Staging

No staging system has been developed. Most patients have unilateral or bilateral lymphadenopathy or localized extranodal disease.

Prognosis and prediction

Most patients have asymptomatic disease and observation is adequate. About 50% of patients undergo complete resolution. A subset of patients have persistent disease, or RDD can relapse, requiring therapy that may include complete excision, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy. In this patient subset, inhibitors of MEK (encoded by MAP2K1) may be helpful. Rare patients with RDD have familial disease. Workup to exclude H syndrome (SLC29A3 mutation) or autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (FAS [TNFRSF6] mutation) may be indicated {20140240}.
