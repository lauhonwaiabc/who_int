9.0.3.8 Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma

Definition

Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma (PTFL) is a localized, nodal mature B-cell lymphoma occurring predominantly in the paediatric and adolescent age group. It is characterized by a clonal proliferation of germinal-centre B cells with a pure follicular growth pattern; altered lymph node architecture; a high proliferation index; and an absence of BCL2, BCL6, and IRF4 rearrangements.

ICD-O coding

9690/3 Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A80.4 Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Usually, lymph nodes of the head and neck region are involved {22855608}. Lymph nodes from other non–head and neck sites can be involved, but much less often {27325104; 31686194}.

Clinical features

Patients present with isolated, asymptomatic lymphadenopathy. As currently defined, disseminated disease excludes a diagnosis of PTFL.

Epidemiology

Patients are usually children or young adults with a median age in the second decade of life. There is a marked male predominance (M:F ratio: ≥ 10:1). PTFL can occur in adults.

Etiology

There are no known risk factors

Pathogenesis

Mutations in MAPK pathway genes (usually MAP2K1; uncommonly MAPK1 or RRAS) occur in about 50% of cases {27325104; 28533310}. TNFRSF14 is mutated in about 50% of cases, and IRF8 p.K66R occurs in about 15% of cases and may be specific for PTFL {28533310}. Mutations of chromatin-modifying genes usually present in classic follicular lymphoma, such as KMT2D, CREBBP, and EZH2, are infrequent in PTFL {27325104; 28533310}. PFTLs lack BCL2, BCL6, MYC, and IRF4 rearrangements {22855608; 27325104; 28533310}.

Macroscopic appearance

The involved nodes show a fish-flesh cut surface.

Histopathology

The lymph node architecture is partially or completely replaced by large, expansile follicles of lymphoma that may be round or serpiginous {22855608; 27325104; 31686194}. These follicles often have a prominent starry-sky pattern and may be surrounded by underdeveloped mantle zones, or mantle zones may be absent. The follicles are usually composed of intermediate-sized cells with blastoid features and multiple nucleoli. A subset of PTFL cases have typical centroblasts. Mitotic figures are usually numerous. As currently defined, areas of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma exclude a diagnosis of PTFL.

Flow cytometry immunophenotyping studies show a monotypic B-cell population positive for CD10 and negative for CD5. Immunohistochemical analysis shows that these neoplasms express pan–B-cell markers (e.g. CD20, PAX5) and germinal-centre B cell–associated markers including CD10, BCL6, LMO2, stathmin, and LLT1, and are negative for T-cell antigens {22855608; 31686194}. FOXP1 is positive in most cases and has been proposed as a useful marker to distinguish PTFL from reactive follicular hyperplasia {31686194}. BCL2 is negative or weakly and variably positive in 40–50% of PTFL cases. Ki-67 is usually > 30%.

Cytology

Smears show sheets of intermediate-sized cells with blastoid nuclear features and/or centroblasts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PTFL cases carry clonal immunoglobulin heavy chain and light chain rearrangements, and there is no evidence of clonal TR gene rearrangements.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: absence of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma or disseminated disease; paediatric and young adult age group (usually age < 40 years, most 2–25 years); a follicular pattern; expression of germinal-centre B-cell antigens; a monotypic B-cell population by flow cytometry or monoclonal IG gene rearrangement shown by PCR analysis; absence of BCL2, BCL6, MYC, and IRF4 rearrangements.

Desirable: MAPK pathway gene mutations (usually MAP2K1) and TNFRSF14 mutations.

Staging

The Lugano system is used to stage lymphomas and has been adopted by the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}. Patients with PTFL present with localized disease.

Prognosis and prediction

Most patients achieve complete remission after surgical excision of the involved lymph node and additional therapy is not required.
