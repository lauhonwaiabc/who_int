4.0.2.1 Intercalated duct adenoma and hyperplasia

Definition

Intercalated duct adenoma and hyperplasia are benign salivary ductal proliferations resembling bilayered (epithelial and myoepithelial) intercalated ducts.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2E91.Z Benign neoplasm of unspecified major salivary glands

Related terminology

Not recommended: adenomatous ductal proliferation.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority (85%) of cases arise in the parotid gland, followed by the submandibular gland (11%) and the oral cavity (4%) {7868091; 10971702; 11174600; 12072816; 19542868; 22271513; 22460810; 25497834}.

Clinical features

Most cases are incidental, identified concurrently with other salivary gland lesions (e.g. basal cell adenoma, epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma) {10971702; 19542868; 24299520; 25497834}.

Epidemiology

Intercalated duct adenoma and hyperplasia are rarely reported {7868091; 10971702; 11174600; 12072816; 19542868; 22271513; 22460810; 24299520; 25497834}. There is an M:F ratio of 2:3, occurring in middle age (mean age: 52 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Adenomas have been shown to harbour hotspot HRAS pathogenic variants, while hyperplasia often has CTNNB1 mutations {36480093}.

Macroscopic appearance

Intercalated duct adenoma and hyperplasia are rarely detected macroscopically. Most lesions are < 5 mm {19542868}.

Histopathology

Intercalated duct lesions manifest as a nodular, occasionally multinodular, proliferation of cuboidal ductal cells with attenuated myoepithelial cells, sometimes containing acinic cells. Intercalated duct lesions are subdivided into adenoma when well circumscribed or encapsulated, and hyperplasia when blending with adjacent acini {19542868; 36480093}.

Differential diagnosis

If well circumscribed, it should be distinguished from striated duct adenoma (which lacks myoepithelial cells) {19542868; 21054495}.

Cytology

Smears show cohesive large tissue fragments of epithelial cells with high N:C ratios and bland oval nuclei, with admixed thin stromal strands {26477034}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a monotonous proliferation of cuboidal ductal cells surrounded by myoepithelial cells, sometimes with acinic cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most are incidental lesions, with an excellent prognosis.
