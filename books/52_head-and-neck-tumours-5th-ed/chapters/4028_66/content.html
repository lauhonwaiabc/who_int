4.0.2.8 Warthin tumour

Definition

Warthin tumour is a benign salivary gland tumour composed of oncocytic epithelial cells lining ductal, papillary, and cystic structures in a lymphoid stroma.

ICD-O coding

8561/0 Warthin tumour

8561/0 Infarcted/metaplastic Warthin tumour

ICD-11 coding

2E91.Z & XA5T23 & XH9ZB2 Benign neoplasm of major salivary glands, unspecified & Salivary gland apparatus & Adenolymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: adenolymphoma; papillary cystadenoma lymphomatosum; cystadenolymphoma.

Subtype(s)

Infarcted/metaplastic Warthin tumour

Localization

Tumours are almost exclusively restricted to parotid glands, especially the inferior pole, and periparotid lymph nodes {9578258; 8431414}; < 1% are found in the deep lobe {28013343}. Tumours are multifocal in 12–20% of patients, either synchronously or metachronously, and are bilateral in 5–17% {11755819; 17050316}. They may be associated with other salivary tumour types {2917323; 32331963}.

Clinical features

Patients present with painless, slow-growing, and fluctuant swellings. Pain or facial nerve palsy may occur in metaplastic/infarcted variants {2743609; 3458128}.

Imaging

Characteristic imaging findings include multifocality, parotid tail location, cystic change, and avidity on PET-CT {25523505}.

Epidemiology

Warthin tumour accounts for 5–20% of all salivary tumours, except in Black Africans, in whom it is rare {24455085}. The mean age at diagnosis is 62 years (range: 12–92 years) {6304434}. The M:F ratio has shifted in Europe and North America, from 10:1 in 1953 {6304434} to almost equal nowadays {3458128}; however, a 2018 study found that the 10:1 ratio remains true in China {29627151}.

Etiology

Warthin tumours, especially when bilateral, have been linked strongly to cigarette smoking {8678050; 17050316; 18361448}. Other suggested risk factors include radiation exposure in atomic bomb survivors {9118025}, and autoimmune diseases {9288224; 25814196}, especially thyroiditis. Some studies have suggested a role for IgG4 {24565204} and various viruses, but this is still unproven {29224806}.

Pathogenesis

Warthin tumour probably arises from salivary ductal inclusions in parotid lymph nodes {23868565; 32865726}. Clonality studies have suggested a non-neoplastic nature {11112212; 15861216}; however, a subset of metaplastic Warthin tumours have been found to harbour KRAS codon 12 mutations {36752878}, which may suggest the existence of a different subtype with a neoplastic nature.

Macroscopic appearance

Most are well-circumscribed spherical-oval masses, 20–50 mm in diameter, although one example measured 200 mm {31742077}. Solid areas and multiple cysts with papillary projections are apparent on cut surface.

Histopathology

Warthin tumour is composed of varying proportions of papillary-cystic structures lined by bilayered oncocytic epithelial cells, surrounded by a lymphoid stroma including germinal centres. The epithelial component comprises inner columnar and outer cuboidal cells. Limited foci of squamous, mucous, ciliated, and sebaceous cells can be present.

Subtype

In the infarcted/metaplastic subtype of Warthin tumour, the bilayered epithelium is replaced by squamous metaplastic epithelium without atypia. Mucinous metaplasia may also be present {24121173; 10583558}. The infarcted/metaplastic subtype, which may follow FNA or trauma, shows areas of necrosis, in which a ghost papillary architecture may be discerned (highlighted with reticulin staining). There is usually marked squamous (less often, mucinous) metaplasia and a stromal reaction, including granuloma formation {2743609; 10583558; 10631719}.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis of the metaplastic Warthin tumour subtype is Warthin-like mucoepidermoid carcinoma; however, Warthin tumours lack MAML2 gene rearrangement {24121173; 32222825}.

Cytology

Smears show a characteristic triad of small cohesive sheets of oncocytes (with abundant granular cytoplasm, well-defined cytoplasmic margins, and central rounded nuclei with a prominent single nucleolus), numerous lymphocytes, and a smaller number of larger lymphoid cells, with granular background debris {9099541}. The proportion of the three components can vary. Diagnostic challenges occur when there are metaplastic changes, including squamous and mucinous metaplasia raising a differential diagnosis with mucoepidermoid carcinoma, which lacks tissue fragments of bland oncocytic cells but may have a cystic background and a lymphoid component {9285198; 32767837}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not directly relevant, but an absence of MAML2 alteration can exclude mucoepidermoid carcinoma {24121173}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed mass with spaces lined by papillary bilayered oncocytic cells; lymphoid stroma.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Complete surgical excision with an adequate margin is usually curative {25543869}. Uncommon local recurrences are probably due to multifocal tumours or inadequate excision {18550337}. Malignant transformation in Warthin tumour is rare; a few examples have been reported of both epithelial {7965888; 11026102; 11683932; 15455232; 21877991; 29627151} and lymphoid neoplasms {16045788; 26119391; 26169920}.
