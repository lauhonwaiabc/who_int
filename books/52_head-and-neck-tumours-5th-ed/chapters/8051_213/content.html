8.0.5.1 Leiomyoma and angioleiomyoma

Definition

Leiomyoma is a benign tumour with smooth muscle differentiation. Angioleiomyoma is a pericytic neoplasm comprising a proliferation of well-differentiated smooth muscle cells surrounding vascular channels.

ICD-O coding

8890/0 Leiomyoma, NOS

8894/0 Angioleiomyoma

ICD-11 coding

2E86.1 Leiomyoma of other or unspecified sites

Related terminology

Not recommended: myoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common sites for leiomyoma in the head and neck region are the oral cavity (lips, tongue, buccal mucosa, palate), sinonasal tract, and mandible {24250094; 26094018; 26682845}. They are extraordinarily rare in the sinonasal tract, predominantly involving the nasal cavity and more rarely the paranasal sinuses. In the sinonasal tract they mostly present as angioleiomyoma {26047608; 12653572; 24384850}.

Clinical features

At most sites the tumours cause soft tissue swellings, whereas mucosal lesions may cause nasal or airway obstruction, bleeding, and (rarely) pain {26047608; 12653572; 24384850}.

Epidemiology

Only about 1% of all leiomyomas are located in the head and neck region {24250094}. Of these, about 12–15% present as angioleiomyomas {26047608; 1122481; 12653572; 24250094}. Adults are most often affected, with an equal sex distribution.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There is no evidence that leiomyomas of the head and neck region share the driver mutations (MED12, HMGA2 rearrangements) seen in genital and retroperitoneal leiomyomas {22223266; 28591699}.

Macroscopic appearance

Leiomyomas are polypoid, nodular, and usually sharply demarcated, with a white to tan trabecular cut surface covered by a capsule or normal mucosa {30885006}.

Histopathology

Leiomyomas are subepithelial with infrequent mucosal ulceration. Bland, spindled tumour cells are arranged in intersecting fascicles. Cells are oval to elongate, with cigar-shaped nuclei, without atypia, and with eosinophilic fibrillary cytoplasm. Mitoses are absent to sparse.

Angioleiomyoma, the most common smooth muscle tumour in the head and neck region, shows a prominent vasculature surrounded by smooth muscle cells intimately associated with vessels {24384850}. Calcification, ossification, fatty metaplasia, or myxohyaline degeneration may be seen, probably indicating longstanding lesions. A fatty component is more common in male patients and older patients. Although it has a smooth muscle component, angioleiomyoma is formally classified as a pericytic (perivascular) tumour (see the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume).

Immunohistochemically, lesions express smooth muscle markers (α-SMA, MSA, desmin, and caldesmon) but are negative for HMB45, SOX10, and S100 {26047608; 12653572; 24111893}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour with smooth muscle differentiation with very rare or absent mitoses and lacking nuclear atypia and necrosis; angioleiomyoma has a prominent vascular component.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Leiomyomas and angioleiomyomas have an excellent prognosis.
