13.1.0.6 Middle ear papilloma

Definition

Middle ear papilloma is a benign surface epithelial neoplasm defined by its resemblance to sinonasal papilloma, showing a multilayered epithelium with mucocytes and transmigrating neutrophils.

ICD-O coding

8121/0 Middle ear papilloma

8121/1 Inverted sinonasal-type papilloma

8121/0 Oncocytic sinonasal-type papilloma

8121/0 Exophytic sinonasal-type papilloma

ICD-11 coding

2F00.Y & XH7YQ5 Other specified benign neoplasm of middle ear or respiratory system & Squamous cell papilloma, inverted

Related terminology

Not recommended: Schneiderian papilloma; papilloma; transitional papilloma; fungiform papilloma; eosinophilic papilloma.

Subtype(s)

Inverted sinonasal-type papilloma; oncocytic sinonasal-type papilloma; exophytic sinonasal-type papilloma

Localization

Most temporal bone sinonasal-type papillomas (TBSPs) are identified unilaterally in the middle ear, but the external auditory canal, mastoid bone, Eustachian tube, and skull base are commonly concurrently affected {8615588; 11802052; 12759269; 12737292; 25959041; 31428494; 34021464}. About 50% of patients show concurrent sinonasal tract / nasopharyngeal involvement. Middle ear papilloma must be distinguished from cutaneous-type papilloma {20596973}.

Clinical features

Patients present with hearing loss, otorrhoea, a mass, otalgia, tinnitus, and otitis media; nerve symptoms (motor or sensory) are also reported. Tympanic membrane rupture is frequent, and concurrent Eustachian tube involvement is common {34021464}.

Imaging

Imaging studies document disease extent for surgical planning, while frequently confirming concurrent sinonasal tract disease {20875193; 21817933; 26698908; 29764790; 22565660; 25564042; 34021464}.

Epidemiology

TBSP is very rare. There is an equal sex distribution and a broad age range (19–81 years, median: 56 years) at presentation {34021464}. When the disease is exclusively TBSP, there is a female predominance, and patients are less likely to develop carcinoma (19% vs 50%) than patients with concurrent sinonasal tract disease. Male patients develop carcinoma more frequently than female patients (M:F ratio: 1.7:1) {34021464}.

Etiology

In a few reported cases, low-risk HPV has been identified {19863319; 20073603; 25724573}.

Pathogenesis

Embryologically, Schneiderian membrane–derived epithelium lines the tympanic cavity and Eustachian tube. Whether primary or secondary, tumour development may be viral or by another mechanism. Neither EGFR nor KRAS mutations have been documented in TBSP {29145573; 27234382}. Emerging data suggest that oncocytic-type papilloma may be distinct {32940914}. In rare cases, transcriptionally active high-risk HPV is detected {25724573; 20981655}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions are fragmented, multilobulated, grape-like, or polypoid masses within and often filling the middle ear. Although they are physically exophytic, the majority are of the inverted type histologically.

Histopathology

The vast majority are of the inverted subtype, showing a markedly thick, inverted (endophytic) growth of non-keratinizing transitional cells. The inverted areas are contained by an intact basement membrane. Intraepithelial microcysts containing macrophages, mucin, and cell debris are present, along with transepithelial inflammatory cells. Tumour cells lack pleomorphism, tumour necrosis, atypical mitoses, and bone destruction. Mitotic figures are usually easily identified. Marked pleomorphism, tumour necrosis, destructive infiltration of bone, and atypical mitoses are features of malignant transformation to squamous cell carcinoma, identified in about a third of patients {34021464}.

Subtypes

Tall, columnar stratified cells with abundant oncocytically altered cytoplasm are seen in the rare oncocytic papilloma category. The exophytic subtype is extremely rare.

Differential diagnosis

Tumours must be separated from middle ear neuroendocrine tumour (MeNET), cholesteatoma, and squamous cell carcinoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multilayered transitional epithelial proliferation with transepithelial inflammatory cells; absence of pleomorphism, necrosis, and increased mitotic figures.

Staging

Not performed

Prognosis and prediction

There is a very high local recurrence risk (80%), along with a significant malignant transformation risk (33%), the latter especially when concurrent sinonasal tract papilloma/carcinoma is identified {34021464}. About 10% of patients die from the disease {11802052; 23306579; 26698908; 34021464}. Radical rather than conservative treatment is associated with a lower risk of local recurrence.
