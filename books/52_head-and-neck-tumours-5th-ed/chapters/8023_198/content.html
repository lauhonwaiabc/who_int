8.0.2.3 Nodular fasciitis

Definition

Nodular fasciitis (NF) is a transient neoplasm typically found in subcutaneous tissue, composed of bland myofibroblasts and usually harbouring USP6 rearrangements.

ICD-O coding

8828/0 Nodular fasciitis

8828/0 Intravascular fasciitis

8828/0 Cranial fasciitis

ICD-11 coding

FB51.2 & XH5LM1 Pseudosarcomatous fibromatosis & Nodular fasciitis

Related terminology

Not recommended: pseudosarcomatous fasciitis.

Subtype(s)

Intravascular fasciitis; cranial fasciitis

Localization

NF typically develops in the subcutis; dermal and deep localization is rare. Any anatomical site can be involved, including the face, neck, oral cavity, orbit, parotid, and ear. Intravascular fasciitis occurs in small to medium-sized vessels {6462780; 11510001; 20716998; 19469872; 31950474; 27686647; 33000481}.

Clinical features

Nodular fasciitis grows rapidly and usually has a preoperative duration of ≤ 2–3 months. It usually measures ≤ 30 mm on excision {31950474}. Extension into adjacent structures does not exclude the diagnosis {32192385; 9923933; 27686647; 31950474}.

Epidemiology

As many as 25% of NFs occur in the head and neck region, affecting all age groups, with a peak incidence in the third and fourth decades of life. There is no sex predilection. Intravascular and cranial fasciitis are rare. Cranial fasciitis develops predominantly in infants aged < 2 years, more commonly in boys {31950474; 6462780}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The identification of recurrent USP6 gene rearrangements as driver mutations has established the clonal neoplastic nature {12550774; 21826056; 28752842}. Several fusion partners lead by promoter switch to transcriptional activation of USP6. Activated pathways include NF-κB, WNT/β-catenin, and JAK-1/STAT3 {32635781}.

See the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of this series for additional information.

Macroscopic appearance

NF is unencapsulated and circumscribed or infiltrative. The cut surface varies from myxoid to fibrous, occasionally with central cystic change {31950474}.

Histopathology

NF (including subtypes) is composed of plump, spindle-shaped cells lacking nuclear pleomorphism. Mitotic figures may be plentiful, without atypical forms. There is typically a tissue culture–like architecture. Cellular areas often show a storiform growth pattern. The stroma may be variably myxoid with microcystic stromal changes or fibrous, sometimes with keloidal collagen. There are extravasated erythrocytes, lymphocytes, and osteoclast-like giant cells. Small vessels may be numerous, occasionally resembling granulation tissue. Extension into adjacent structures may be present {1928550; 6462780; 31950474}.

Lesional cells express SMA and MSA; focal desmin positivity is occasionally found {1928550; 31950474}.

Cytology

Bland spindle cells with unipolar curved / bipolar processes, lacking relevant atypia. A tissue culture–like appearance and myxoid stroma can be appreciated {30311731}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In selected cases, USP6 rearrangement may confirm the diagnosis {28752842}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a bland myofibroblastic proliferation with a tissue culture–like growth pattern.

Desirable: assessment of USP6 rearrangement in selected cases.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence is rare. Metastatic disease is exceptional {31950474; 27113271}.
