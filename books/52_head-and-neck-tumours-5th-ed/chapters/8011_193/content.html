8.0.1.1 Lipomas

Definition

Lipomas are a family of benign mesenchymal tumours composed predominantly of adipocytes admixed with other cellular constituents and include conventional lipoma, spindle cell lipoma and pleomorphic lipoma (SCL/PL), atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumour (ASCLT/APLT), dysplastic lipoma, fibrolipoma, chondrolipoma, angiolipoma, and intramuscular lipoma.

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Conventional lipoma in the head and neck is less common than at many other body sites and rarely involves intraoral, hypopharyngeal, or laryngeal sites {32851988}. The buccal mucosa, lower lip, and tongue are the most commonly affected sites {32851988; 31041916}. SCL/PLs and dysplastic lipomas occur mainly in the posterior neck, and less frequently in the oral cavity, scalp, and face {31291740}. Dysplastic lipoma occurs preferentially in the upper back, shoulders, and posterior neck {30001242}.

Clinical features

Lipoma typically presents as a slowly growing, asymptomatic, circumscribed mass < 5 mm in diameter {21447447}; some cases are associated with lipomatosis {18644522; 19192164}.

Occasionally, multiple lesions of SCL/PL are seen; these cases may be familial {9422314; 12522375}. About 15% of dysplastic lipomas are multifocal, and occasionally they occur after retinoblastoma {28807343; 30001242}.

Epidemiology

Lipomas are more common in men than women. They occur in all age groups but most commonly arise in the fifth to sixth decades of life and are rare in children {24800932}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

HMGA2 rearrangements are associated with conventional lipoma {10747931; 15593017; 28139834}. RB1 deletion is detected in the majority of SCL/PLs {12494468}. Dysplastic lipoma harbours RB1 deletions and a subset of cases occur after retinoblastoma {28807343; 30001242}. A vascular anomaly is considered the underlying mechanism in intramuscular lipoma and may explain its tendency to recur.

ASCLT/APLT: Deletion/losses of 13q14, including RB1, RCBTB2, DLEU1, and ITM2B {31501988}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lipomas are well circumscribed and encapsulated, with a yellowish cut surface. Intramuscular and facial skin lipomas may show infiltrating borders {25219904}. Spindle cell lipoma (SCL) and atypical spindle cell lipoma (ASCL) are variably yellow, greyish-white, and myxoid, depending on the proportion of the constituent elements.

Histopathology

Conventional lipoma displays adipocytes lacking atypia.

SCL/PL tumours represent the morphological spectrum of a single neoplasm. SCL is a benign adipocytic tumour composed of variable amounts of mature adipocytes, bland spindle cells, and ropy collagen. Pleomorphic lipoma also contains pleomorphic and multinucleated floret-like giant cells {25319950}. Mitoses are rare, but single atypical-looking mitoses may be present, as well as lipoblasts {28546131; 28843712}. Skeletal muscle involvement is seen in 36% of facial SCLs {25219904}.

ASCLT/APLT shows, in addition to the classic features of spindle cell and pleomorphic lipoma, atypical stromal and fat cells with a higher degree of atypia. Infiltration into the surrounding tissues occurs variably {27879515; 28877053; 33782225}.

Dysplastic lipomas show striking anisocytosis of adipocytes but little or no atypia. They lack the stromal component and atypical pleomorphic cells of SCL/PLs {30001242}.

Fibrolipoma combines mature fat cells with prominent intermingled paucicellular collagenous component. Chondrolipoma is characterized by foci of mature cartilage. Angiolipoma contains a variable vascular component with intermingled or confluent capillary-size vessels frequently associated with fibrin microthrombi. Intramuscular lipoma shows infiltrating mature adipocytes within skeletal muscle with frequent thick-walled vessels.

Degeneration-related changes have resulted in the misclassification of submucosal lingual lipomas as atypical lipomatous tumours, but all lack MDM2 amplification {29748845}. Rare (low-fat) tumours may lack adipocytes.

Cytology

The smears from SCLs show a mixture of adipocytes and uniform spindle cells often set in a myxoid stroma. Fragments of brightly eosinophilic collagen fibres are present. Pleomorphic lipoma shows multinucleated giant cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and a moderate amount of cytoplasm {11748578}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

MDM2 assessment may rule out atypical lipomatous tumours in traumatized lesions {29748845}.

Demonstration of loss of RB1 can be helpful in selected cases to confirm SCL/PL and ASCLT/APLT {12494468; 21563233}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a neoplasm composed either exclusively of mature adipocytes or combined with stromal elements; for ASCLT/APLT: spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma morphology with atypia of stromal cells and fat cells, with or without lipoblasts.

Desirable: absence of MDM2 amplification (traumatized and atypical-looking lesions).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lipomas are cured by simple excision. Recurrence is rare. Non-destructive recurrence has been reported in 15–20% of dysplastic lipomas after non-radical resection. Malignant transformation has not been observed.

SCL/PL is a benign tumour that is adequately treated with conservative excision. Local recurrence is rare, even with incomplete resection {1192370; 7459800; 998247}.

ASCLT/APLT: Recurrence is rare {33782225; 27879715}.
