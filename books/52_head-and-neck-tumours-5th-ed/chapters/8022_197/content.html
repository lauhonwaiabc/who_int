8.0.2.2 Myofibroma

Definition

Myofibroma is a benign mesenchymal neoplasm of perivascular myoid cells that forms a spectrum with myopericytoma.

ICD-O coding

8824/0 Myopericytoma

ICD-11 coding

2E84.Y & XH0953 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of other specified sites & Myofibroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: congenital generalized fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

Infantile myofibroma; infantile myofibromatosis; solitary myofibroma; adult myofibroma

Localization

The head and neck, upper extremities, trunk, and (rarely) deep soft tissue and bones are the main sites affected by myofibromas {22486319}. Widespread visceral involvement characterizes the generalized form.

Clinical features

Myofibroma presents as a small, well-circumscribed but not encapsulated, superficial dermal or subcutaneous violaceous nodule, ranging in size from < 10 mm to several centimetres {22486319}. Three clinicopathological settings are recognized: solitary, multicentric, and generalized. Multicentric disease shows a female predilection.

Epidemiology

Myofibroma may be present at birth, appear in the first 2 years of life, or arise in adults, with a male predominance.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Somatic and germline PDGFRB mutations are responsible for solitary and familial myofibroma/myofibromatosis, respectively {28505006; 27776010}.

Macroscopic appearance

Myofibroma forms a well-circumscribed but not encapsulated dermal or subcutaneous violaceous nodule of one to several centimetres, with a firm, fibrous consistency.

Histopathology

Biphasic growth of alternating bundles of primitive basophilic, small, rounded to plump spindled cells and eosinophilic myoid cells associated with prominent thin-walled pericytoma-like vessels with frequent myointimal nodular proliferations (vascular balls) {16330949; 9591720; 8067513}. The stroma is characteristically myxohyaline and may show ischaemic-type necrosis and calcification. Mitotic activity is low, but highly mitotically active lesions may be encountered. Atypical mitoses are lacking. The neoplastic cells express α-SMA, but h-caldesmon is absent or only focally positive. Desmin is negative.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of somatic or germline PDGFRB mutations may be helpful in selected cases. Infantile myofibromatosis lacks the ETV6::NTRK3 and LMNA::NTRK1 gene fusions found in infantile fibrosarcoma {10895816; 26863915}. SRF::RELA fusions are detected in a subset of cellular myofibroma but not in conventional myofibroma {28248815}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: alternating bundles of primitive, darkly stained, rounded to plump cells and mature eosinophilic myoid cells associated with prominent thin-walled pericytoma-like vessels and myointimal nodular proliferations (vascular balls) within a myxohyaline stroma.

Desirable: PDGFRB mutations in myopericytoma and myofibroma; SRF::RELA gene fusions in cellular/atypical myofibroma (in selected cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Myofibroma does not recur after excision. The generalized form is frequently fatal because of extensive visceral involvement and limited surgical possibilities. Atypical histology (increased cellularity, high mitotic activity, absent myoid nodules, infiltrating borders, intravascular growth, perineural invasion, entrapment of nerves) does not seem to adversely influence outcome in the absence of atypical mitoses and cytological atypia {24921644}.
