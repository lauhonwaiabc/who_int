11.2.0.2 Heterotopia-associated carcinoma

Definition

Heterotopia-associated carcinoma is a primary malignant epithelial neoplasm arising from histologically normal, congenitally displaced tissue in the head and neck, most commonly thyroid and salivary tissue.

ICD-O coding

8010/3 Heterotopia-associated carcinoma

8010/3 Thyroid carcinoma

8010/3 Salivary carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D42 & XH26N1 Malignant neoplasms of ill-defined sites & Heterotopia-associated carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: ectopia-associated carcinoma; choristoma-associated carcinoma; accessory tissue–associated carcinoma; primary intranodal carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

Thyroid carcinoma; salivary carcinoma

Localization

Heterotopia-associated thyroid carcinomas arise in the midline anterior neck, along the path of the thyroglossal duct from the tongue to the pyramidal thyroid {21752893}. Heterotopia-associated salivary carcinomas most commonly arise in intraparotid, periparotid, and cervical lymph nodes or at extranodal sites along the sternocleidomastoid muscle {1710048; 15742261}. However, thorough radiographic and/or pathological evaluation is necessary to exclude an unusual presentation of an orthotopic primary neoplasm or a metastasis {26691539}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with an asymptomatic neck mass, with rare patients reporting pain or dysphagia {28322121}.

Epidemiology

Heterotopia-associated carcinomas are rare. Approximately 1–3% of thyroglossal duct cysts develop carcinoma, occurring more commonly in female patients (M:F ratio: 1:2–3) and spanning a wide age range (9–83 years), with an average age of 39 years {9121176; 27704385; 28322121}. Heterotopia-associated salivary carcinomas also demonstrate a wide age range (7–81 years), with a female predominance seen in acinic cell carcinoma {1710048; 33177745}.

Etiology

Heterotopia-associated carcinomas arise from malpositioned embryological remnants, but the cause is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The average tumour size is 10–40 mm, although some tumours grow to larger sizes {27704385; 28322121; 33177745}.

Histopathology

The most common carcinoma arising from heterotopic thyroid tissue is papillary thyroid carcinoma, with rare reports of follicular, oncocytic, or squamous cell carcinoma {28322121}. Heterotopia-associated papillary thyroid carcinoma demonstrates identical nuclear features to those seen in primary thyroid tumours. Architecture may be papillary or follicular, and the tumour may proliferate into the lumen of a cyst or infiltrate into the surrounding tissue {27704385}.

Heterotopia-associated salivary carcinomas display the same morphological features as their orthotopic counterparts. To differentiate from orthotopic carcinomas with tumour-associated lymphoid proliferation, heterotopia-associated salivary carcinomas arising in lymph nodes should be separate from the salivary gland and confined to nodal tissue with a subcapsular sinus or hilum {33177745}. Mucoepidermoid and acinic cell carcinoma are the most frequent heterotopia-associated salivary carcinomas {1710048; 15742261}. A subset of salivary intraductal carcinomas arising within intraparotid lymph nodes has also been described {32661669}.

For both heterotopia-associated thyroid and salivary carcinomas, identification of adjacent non-neoplastic heterotopic tissue supports the diagnosis, but this is not always present.

Differential diagnosis

For the differential diagnosis of heterotopia-associated papillary thyroid carcinoma, see #26577Table 12.02 .

For the differential diagnosis of heterotopia-associated salivary carcinoma, see #26578Table 12.03 .

Cytology

FNA of carcinomas arising in heterotopic tissue shows the same morphological features as that of primary carcinomas. For heterotopia-associated thyroid carcinomas, there is a higher risk of a false negative result due to aspiration of paucicellular cyst fluid only {27161104; 28322121}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The most common mutations reported in thyroglossal duct cyst papillary thyroid carcinoma are BRAF p.V600E {24777145}. Heterotopic mucoepidermoid carcinomas harbour MAML2 rearrangements, similarly to their orthotopic counterparts {21590088}. Salivary intraductal carcinomas arising within intraparotid lymph nodes harbour RET and ALK rearrangements {32661669}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a carcinoma arising in a known location for heterotopic tissue; clinical and/or pathological exclusion of a prior or concurrent orthotopic carcinoma.

Desirable: adjacent benign heterotopic tissue.

Staging

No formal staging criteria exist for heterotopia-associated carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of heterotopia-associated papillary thyroid carcinoma is excellent, with a recurrence rate of < 5% and mortality rate of < 1% for surgically treated thyroglossal duct carcinoma {27704385; 28322121}. Metastases to lateral cervical lymph nodes are seen in 10–20% of patients {28322121}. Synchronous primary thyroid carcinomas are identified in 25–35% of patients, comparable to the incidence reported for occult incidental thyroid carcinomas in autopsy studies {11870661; 9121176; 27704385}. Age ≥ 45 years and soft tissue infiltration are associated with an increased risk of locoregional recurrence {27704385}. Squamous cell carcinoma arising in a thyroglossal duct cyst is rare and associated with a worse prognosis {29623203}.

For heterotopia-associated salivary carcinomas, high-grade histopathology is associated with a more aggressive clinical course {7260848; 15742261}. Metastases to cervical lymph nodes are predominantly seen in high-grade carcinomas or low-grade tumours with high-grade transformation {1710048; 33177745}.

Diagnosis

Location

Distinguishing morphological features

Benign thyroid inclusion

Intranodal; midline, paramidline, or central neck

·    Isolated, few or small foci of bland, simple follicles without cytological atypia or architectural complexity

·    Limited to one lymph node

·    Capsular or immediately subcapsular location within lymph node

·    Wedge-shaped arrangement, wider at capsule and narrower towards cortex

·    Absence of psammomatous calcifications, fibrosis, and desmoplasia

·    Absence of radiographic and/or pathological abnormalities in the thyroid gland

·    Absence of pathogenic mutations (e.g. BRAF p.V600E)

Thyroglossal duct cyst

Midline anterior neck, above thyroid and below hyoid bone

·    Smooth-walled dilated cyst lined by respiratory or non-keratinizing squamous epithelium

·    Bland, simple thyroid follicles without cytological atypia present in wall

Pyramidal lobe papillary thyroid carcinoma

Superior midline thyroid, in contiguity with thyroid parenchyma

·    Located adjacent to benign orthotopic thyroid tissue

·    Absence of lymph nodal architecture

·    Papillary structures or infiltrative follicular growth

·    Nuclear features of papillary thyroid carcinoma

Metastatic papillary thyroid carcinoma involving a lymph node

Intranodal; lateral or central neck

·    Residual lymph nodal structures (capsule, subcapsular sinus, and/or hilum)

·    Primary site often but not always identified clinically, radiographically, and/or postoperatively within orthotopic (ipsilateral) thyroid gland

·    Numerous intraparenchymal-based follicles

·    Often more than one lymph node involved

·    Papillary or follicular architecture

·    Well-developed nuclear features of papillary thyroid carcinoma, although fully developed nuclear features may be absent

Heterotopia-associated papillary thyroid carcinoma

Midline anterior neck, above thyroid

·    Presence of benign heterotopic tissue

·    Absence of lymph nodal architecture

·    Absence of radiographic and/or pathological abnormalities in the thyroid gland

·    Papillary structures or infiltrative follicular growth

·    Nuclear features of papillary thyroid carcinoma

Diagnosis

Distinguishing morphological features

Benign intranodal salivary inclusion

·     Bland acini and intercalated, striated, or intralobular ducts with intact myoepithelial/basal cells

·     Intranodal location with subcapsular sinus

Primary salivary neoplasm with tumour-associated lymphoid proliferation

·     Neoplasm arising within salivary parenchyma

·     Absence of nodal subcapsular sinus or hilum

·     Absence of CAM5.2-reactive extrafollicular reticulum cells {31541432}

Metastatic salivary carcinoma involving a lymph node

·     Morphological features of malignancy, including cytological atypia and/or architectural complexity

·     Presence of nodal subcapsular sinus or hilum

·     CAM5.2-immunoreactive extrafollicular reticulum cells {31541432}

·     Primary site identified clinically or radiographically within major or minor salivary glands

Heterotopia-associated salivary carcinoma

·     Morphological features of malignancy, including cytological atypia and/or architectural complexity

·     Separate from the salivary gland

·     Entirely embedded within nodal tissue with a subcapsular sinus or hilum

·     Absence of radiographic and/or pathological abnormalities in the major and minor salivary glands

·     Adjacent benign heterotopic tissue
