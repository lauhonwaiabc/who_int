8.0.3.2 Lymphangioma

Definition

Lymphangioma is a benign vascular lesion composed of a localized collection of dilated lymphatic channels.

ICD-O coding

9170/0 Lymphangioma, NOS

9170/0 Mixed lymphangioma

9173/0 Cystic lymphangioma

9173/0 Microcystic lymphangioma

9173/0 Macrocystic lymphangioma

ICD-11 coding

LA90.12 & XH9MR8 Lymphatic malformations of certain specified sites & Lymphangioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: cystic hygroma; vascular malformation; lymphatic malformation; lymphangiomatous polyps.

Subtype(s)

Microcystic lymphangioma and macrocystic lymphangioma (depends on cyst diameter; cut-off point: 10 mm); mixed lymphangioma {30819600}

Localization

Although the skin and subcutaneous tissue of the head and neck region are the most common sites for lymphangiomas, they are only occasionally reported in the oral cavity and salivary glands. Intraoral lymphangiomas arise most commonly on the dorsum of the tongue, followed by the palate, buccal mucosa, gingiva, and lips {31823215}.

Clinical features

Clinical behaviour varies, with erratic growth, progression, or even spontaneous regression during the first two decades of life. Symptoms are related to lesion size and perturbation of surrounding structures {22420724; 30819600}.

Epidemiology

Lymphangiomas are relatively uncommon and usually diagnosed in infancy and early childhood; however, there is a broad age range {30677207; 22658544; 30819600}.

Etiology

These lesions are caused by developmental errors in the vascular system due to mosaic somatic mutations arising during embryogenesis or, more rarely, due to constitutive pathogenic variants {31950476}.

Pathogenesis

Activating mutations, mainly in PIK3CA, result in hyperactive PI3K/AKT signalling {22658544; 21793738; 31950476}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions are sponge-like and not always delineated. They can be pedunculated or, more rarely, sessile.

Histopathology

Variably sized, irregular, thin-walled, fluid-filled spaces are lined by lymphatic endothelium and surrounded by a stroma of fibrous, smooth muscle, and adipose tissue along with lymphocytes {22420724}. Red blood cells and organized thrombi may be seen in the lumina after trauma. Communication with the venous system is sometimes seen. The endothelial cells show expression of CD31 and (rarely) CD34 {11048808}. They also express podoplanin (recognized by D2-40), PROX1, VEGFR3, and LYVE1 {19098468; 24283866; 22420724}. The walls of the lymphatic vessels and/or the surrounding layer show expression of SMA {11048808; 21605441}.

Cytology

Aspiration produces a lymphocyte-rich yellow fluid {30819600}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Mutation analysis for PIK3CA in selected cases may be helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: variably sized, mainly thin-walled lymphatic channels, often with aggregates of lymphocytes.

Desirable: expression of vascular markers and podoplanin (recognized by D2-40) (in selected cases).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lymphangiomas are benign, but because of their propensity for involvement of deeper tissue planes, recurrence after surgical resection may occur {22420724}.
