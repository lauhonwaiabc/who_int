8.0.8.2 Schwannoma

Definition

Schwannoma is a nerve sheath tumour composed entirely or nearly entirely of differentiated neoplastic Schwann cells.

ICD-O coding

9560/0 Schwannoma, NOS

9560/0 Ancient schwannoma

9560/0 Cellular schwannoma

9560/0 Plexiform schwannoma

9560/0 Epithelioid schwannoma

9560/0 Microcystic reticular schwannoma

ICD-11 coding

2F38 & XH98Z3 Benign neoplasm of other or unspecified sites & Schwannoma (neurilemoma)

Related terminology

Not recommended: neurilemoma, neurinoma.

Subtype(s)

Ancient schwannoma; cellular schwannoma; plexiform schwannoma; epithelioid schwannoma; microcystic reticular schwannoma

Localization

Extracranial, non-vestibular schwannomas arise anywhere in the head and neck region. Intraorally, the tongue is most commonly affected {22606860; 25654048; 24384632; 31485983}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a slowly growing, sometimes painful mass. Other signs depend largely on size, nerve of origin, and localization {22606860; 7127260; 9267819; 22420725; 33723760}. CT and MRI show unilateral, well-circumscribed, expansile tumours that are oval, round, or fusiform, with mottled central lucency and peripheral postcontrast enhancement, often with cystic changes. Bone erosion and remodelling is occasionally present {7127260; 22621416; 22954739; 24717879}.

Epidemiology

As many as 40% of schwannomas occur in the head and neck {33723760; 24384632}. The majority of cases affect adults in their second to fifth decade of life, with an equal sex distribution {9267819; 24384632}.

Etiology

Most lesions are sporadic {22327363; 22606860}. Multiple and plexiform schwannomas suggest an association with neurofibromatosis type 2 or schwannomatosis {33723760}.

Neurofibromatosis type 2 is an autosomal dominant genetic condition caused by mutations in the tumour suppressor gene NF2 and characterized by the development of schwannomas (including bilateral vestibular schwannomas), meningiomas, ependymomas, and low-grade gliomas {33723760}.

Schwannomatosis is characterized by spinal and peripheral schwannomas and meningiomas, usually without vestibular schwannomas {33723760; 26848914}. It is usually sporadic (as many as 85% of cases) but is also inherited {33588442}.

Pathogenesis

NF2 inactivating mutation is the most common genetic finding in sporadic or familial schwannomas {33588442}.

Schwannomatosis is associated with SMARCB1 (INI1) and LZTR1 mutations {33723760}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions are nodular with a tan-white, glistening cut surface, and they can measure as large as 75 mm. Occasionally, an associated nerve can be identified {22327363; 22420725; 24384632}. Plexiform schwannomas appear tortuous to convoluted {33723760}.

Histopathology

Schwannomas are commonly well-circumscribed, encapsulated, spindle cell tumours, occasionally encroaching on adjacent structures. Most show compact hypercellular Antoni A areas with occasional typical nuclear palisading (degenerative/ancient change) (Verocay bodies) alternating with loosely arranged myxoid Antoni B foci. The elongated, slightly polymorphic spindle cells possess oval, tapered, or buckled nuclei and poorly defined eosinophilic cytoplasm. Intranuclear cytoplasmic inclusions, nuclear pleomorphism, and mitoses may exist. Antoni B zones contain a cobweb-like network of cell processes together with lipid-laden histiocytes and thick-walled, hyalinized blood vessels. Microcystic/reticular changes may be seen. Peripheral lymphoid aggregates are common {9267819; 22327363}.

Subtypes

The cellular subtype shows predominantly the hypercellular Antoni A pattern, forming long fascicles. Mitotic activity and necrosis may be present {9267819}.

Plexiform schwannomas have a thin fibrous capsule and an intraneural multinodular/plexiform architecture. Cell morphology resembles classic schwannomas {33723760}.

Epithelioid schwannomas are (multi)nodular and encapsulated, and characterized by epithelioid cells arranged in trabeculae, loose nodules, and cohesive nests in a collagenous, myxohyaline, or myxoid stroma. Degenerative features and mitoses may be present {1746681}.

Immunohistochemistry

Diffuse immunoreactivity for S100 and SOX10 is usual {9267819; 22420725; 22495377; 22327363; 25724000}. An absence of SMARCB1 (INI1) is observed in 40% of epithelioid schwannomas, and a mosaic staining pattern is typical for schwannomatosis {28368924; 33723760}. EMA outlines perineurial cells peripherally, and NFP may highlight entrapped axons at the periphery. CD34 is usually positive only in subcapsular and septal areas {33588442}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes nodular fasciitis, neurofibroma, solitary fibrous tumour, (angio)leiomyoma, desmoid-type fibromatosis, meningioma, glomangiopericytoma, biphenotypic sinonasal sarcoma, mucosal melanoma, solitary circumscribed neuroma, mucosal neuroma of multiple endocrine neoplasia, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, synovial sarcoma, spindle cell squamous cell carcinoma, and leiomyosarcoma {9267819; 11073336; 22002440; 25472697; 19644540}.

Cytology

Aspirate smears are typically cellular, with cohesive syncytial fragments of bland spindle cells. Within the fragments, variably wavy/convoluted nuclei with tapered edges and fibrillary cytoplasm are seen. Degenerative atypia may be present {17277622; 23737794; 29413661; 30457183; 30620436; 31043260}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: hypercellular spindled cell areas and loose hypocellular myxoid areas; spindle cells with buckled, elongated, fusiform nuclei, occasionally palisaded.

Desirable: hyalinized blood vessel walls; S100/SOX10 in selected cases.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Schwannomas are benign. Multifocality may mimic recurrence or malignancy {22327363; 9267819; 22420725; 22606860; 24384632}.
