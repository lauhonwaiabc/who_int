9.0.3.1 Mantle cell lymphoma

Definition

Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) is a mature B-cell neoplasm usually composed of monomorphic, small, irregular lymphoid cells that express monotypic immunoglobulin, B-cell antigens, and usually CD5. It is associated with CCND family rearrangements, most commonly of CCND1.

ICD-O coding

9673/3 Mantle cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A85.5 Mantle cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: centrocytic lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MCL most often involves the lymph nodes, but extranodal involvement occurs commonly. The head and neck region is the second most common extranodal site involved by MCL, in about 2–4% of patients {23341329}. The Waldeyer ring, most often the tonsils, is the most frequent site, but MCL also involves the nasopharynx, orbital region, salivary glands, and skin of the head and neck {24851070; 34137303}. About 40% of patients with head and neck involvement have stage I or II disease {23341329}.

Clinical features

Patients may present with pain or difficulty in swallowing due to a mass. Orbital and skin lesions may present with a nodule.

Epidemiology

The incidence of MCL is 0.5 cases per 100 000 person-years {21945518}. MCL is most common in older adults, with a median age in the seventh decade of life. The M:F ratio is 2–3:1 {18615506; 21945518; 24763513}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The t(11;14)(q13;q32) translocation, resulting in cyclin D1 overexpression, is thought to be a founding or early event {33783838}. Cyclin D1 drives the cell cycle at the G1- to S-phase transition. Rare cyclin D1–negative cases may have CCND2 or rarely CCND3 translocations, partnered with IGH, IGK, or IGL {23255553; 30538135}. Conventional cytogenetic analysis has shown many additional abnormalities and a complex karyotype in most MCL cases {11764078}. MCL also shows numerous copy-number changes {32584970}. MYC rearrangements occur in a small subset of cases and are probably late events in pathogenesis {32273477; 33528622}. The most common gene mutations in MCL involve ATM (40–75%), TP53 (20–40%), CDKN2A (20–30%), KMT2D (15%), and NOTCH1/2 (5–15%) {32598477}. In sum, these many abnormalities impair the cell cycle, apoptosis, and DNA repair {33783838}.

Macroscopic appearance

MCL has a fish-flesh cut surface.

Histopathology

MCL most often shows a diffuse pattern (~75%), but vaguely nodular (~25%) and (rarely) pure mantle zone (~1%) patterns occur {16173960}. In conventional MCL, the lymphoma cells are uniform and small with irregular nuclear contours. Intermixed single epithelioid histiocytes are seen in about 75% of cases, and naked germinal centres can be present. Aggressive subtypes of MCL represent 15–20% of cases. Blastoid subtypes are composed of intermediate-sized cells with immature chromatin and a high mitotic rate, mimicking lymphoblasts. Pleomorphic subtypes are composed of intermediate-sized and large cells with more prominent nucleoli, mimicking (in part) large cell lymphoma.

The neoplastic B cells of MCL are typically positive for surface immunoglobulin, pan–B-cell antigens, CD5, BCL2, cyclin D1, and SOX11, and negative for CD10, CD23, CD200, BCL6, and LEF1 {16173960; 31054894}. A small minority of cases lack CD5 or express CD10 and/or BCL6 {33528622}. A few cases lack expression of cyclin D1 or SOX11, but not both {16123218; 17934069}. Cyclin D1–negative MCL cases have a similar immunophenotype, including SOX11 expression {23255553}.

Cytology

In conventional cases, smears show monomorphic small lymphoid cells often associated with epithelioid histiocytes.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

IGH::CCND1 rearrangement, t(11;14)(q13;q32), is characteristic of MCL and is found in > 95% of cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lymphoma cells of B-lineage with a consistent immunophenotype, including cyclin D1 expression and/or evidence of CCND1 rearrangement and/or SOX11 expression.

Desirable: CCND2 rearrangement (in cases of cyclin D1–negative MCL).

Staging

Lymphomas are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

The Mantle Cell Lymphoma International Prognostic Index (MIPI) is used to predict prognosis {26926679}. The tumour proliferation rate, most easily assessed by Ki-67, is the most important pathological predictor of prognosis and is often added to the MIPI {26926679; 33783838}.
