7.2.0.3 Metastasizing ameloblastoma

Definition

Metastasizing ameloblastoma (MA) is an ameloblastoma that has metastasized despite a benign histopathological appearance in the primary and metastatic foci.

ICD-O coding

9310/3 Ameloblastoma, metastasizing

ICD-11 coding

2E83.0 & XH96J9 Benign osteogenic tumours of bone or articular cartilage of skull or face & Ameloblastoma, metastasizing

2E83.1 & XH96J9 Benign osteogenic tumours of bone or articular cartilage of lower jaw & Ameloblastoma, metastasizing

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign metastasizing ameloblastoma.

Not recommended: malignant ameloblastoma; malignant adamantinoma; metastatic ameloblastoma; atypical ameloblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The primary tumour develops more frequently in the mandible than in the maxilla {32659412; 33394372}. The most common sites of metastasis are the lungs, followed by the cervical lymph nodes {33394372}, with distant metastasis in approximately 68% of MAs {32659412}. Infrequently reported metastatic sites include bone, brain, liver, and pericardium {15891309; 24152624; 29747056; 30775320; 33394372}.

Clinical features

Symptoms vary by site; some patients are asymptomatic. Pulmonary metastases may cause dry cough, haemoptysis, and dyspnoea {28944145; 32132332}. Metastasis may be diagnosed concurrently with the primary tumour or after a variable, often long, latent period (range: 0–45 years; mean: 11 years) {20970910; 33394372}. Metastases retain the benign ameloblastoma growth pattern.

Epidemiology

Incidence data are only available combined with ameloblastic carcinoma, which is more common; the combined annual cumulative incidence is 1.79 cases per 10 million person-years {25692490}. MA has a mean age at diagnosis of 45 years and a slight male predominance {32659412; 33394372}.

Etiology

MA harbours BRAF p.V600E mutations {25475564}. Risk factors for metastasis include large size, rapid enlargement, protracted clinical course, inadequate removal, and multiple recurrences at the primary site {32659412}.

Pathogenesis

Metastasis may result from haematogenous spread during surgical treatment of the primary {32659412}. Alternatively, metastasis could be driven by additional genetic alterations without histopathological changes {32659412}, but no putative molecular markers of metastatic behaviour have been identified {31169204}.

Macroscopic appearance

Features are identical to those of conventional ameloblastoma.

Histopathology

MA exhibits identical histopathological features to conventional ameloblastoma, with no specific features predicting metastasis. Among reported MA cases, the plexiform histological pattern is most common {20970910; 33394372}.

Cytology

FNAC has been employed in some cases for evaluating metastatic deposits, although cytological features alone are not sufficient for conclusive diagnosis {12804548; 19446199}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign conventional ameloblastoma in the primary tumour and metastasis; no cytological atypia or features of malignancy in the primary tumour or metastasis.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

MA has an overall mean survival time of 5 years on systematic review {33394372}. Surgery is the mainstay of treatment; radiotherapy and chemotherapy have no proven benefit {21459020; 32659412}. BRAF-targeted therapy has been reported in limited cases {25475564; 31340860}. Recurrence occurs in about one quarter of cases and is associated with increased mortality {32659412}.
