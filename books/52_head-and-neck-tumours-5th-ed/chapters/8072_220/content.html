8.0.7.2 Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma

Definition

Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma is an aggressive malignant mesenchymal tumour of uncertain differentiation, characterized by NR4A3 gene rearrangement, an abundant myxoid matrix, a multilobular architecture, and uniform cells arranged in cords, clusters, and reticular networks. Despite the name, there is no evidence of cartilaginous differentiation.

ICD-O coding

9231/3 Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH9344 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Myxoid chondrosarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: NR4A3-rearranged myxoid sarcoma (provisional).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common head and neck sites are the orbit, intracranial sites, and the sinonasal tract. The orbit is the third most common site after the meninges and lower extremities. Case reports have described tumours in the eyelid, parapharyngeal space, and buccal space {29381948; 31909032}.

Clinical features

Patients may present with an enlarging deep-seated soft tissue mass associated with pain, diplopia, or headache. Intracranial tumours may cause symptoms related to raised intracranial pressure.

Epidemiology

Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma is rare (< 1% of all soft tissue sarcomas). The M:F ratio is 2:1, and the disease usually affects adults in their fourth or fifth decade of life. Orbital extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma is extremely rare {27162461}. Few cases are described in the paediatric age group {28638563}. Intracranial extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcomas have been reported to show a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:3) and a slightly younger age at presentation (median: 39 years) {29381948}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Molecular characterization of the t(9;22) and t(9;17) variant translocations show NR4A3 gene fusions with either EWSR1 at 22q12.2 or TAF15 at 17q12 {10602520; 8634690; 10602519; 10537274}. The NR4A3 fusions, present in > 90% of extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcomas {15998372; 12378528; 12598313}, are specific to extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma and may therefore be considered a hallmark of this disease. See the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of this series for additional details.

Macroscopic appearance

Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma forms well-circumscribed, lobulated, thinly encapsulated tumours with a soft to rubbery tan/white cut surface, with or without microcalcifications and haemorrhage {4261659}.

Histopathology

Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma is multinodular, with fibrous septa separating hypocellular lobules with abundant pale-blue myxoid or chondromyxoid matrix. Well-formed hyaline cartilage is virtually never seen, and the stroma is strikingly hypovascular. Characteristically, the cells form cords, small clusters, and complex trabecular or cribriform arrays. They contain modest amounts of deeply eosinophilic to vacuolated cytoplasm and uniform round/oval nuclei; delicate elongated cytoplasmic processes are common. Chromatin is evenly distributed, often with a small, inconspicuous nucleolus. Mitoses are infrequent. Some tumours have prominent rhabdoid cytoplasmic inclusions. Rare cases are hypercellular with decreased myxoid matrix and a higher-grade, often epithelioid, cytomorphology.

S100 is positive in as many as 20% of cases, and KIT (CD117, c-KIT) is positive in as many as 30%. Synaptophysin and NSE may be positive {10895826; 11679947; 10955458}. Tumours with rhabdoid features are often negative for SMARCB1 (INI1). Tumours are very rarely keratin-positive and are negative for GFAP and muscle markers.

Cytology

Smears may be cellular or paucicellular with a myxochondroid background matrix. Two cell populations are observed, oval and spindled, occurring singly or in cords and clusters, with pale blue cytoplasm and with or without nuclear grooves and small inclusions. Multinucleated cells may be present. The differential diagnosis includes other blue round cell tumours, including rhabdomyosarcoma, synovial sarcoma, and Ewing sarcoma {30407856}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of the NR4A3 gene rearrangement may be helpful in selected cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a multilobular tumour; generally bland cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm disposed in strands or cords in a predominantly myxoid stroma.

Desirable: NR4A3 rearrangement (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for head and neck sarcomas.

Prognosis and prediction

Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma is associated with long-term overall survival rates of 82–90% (5-year), 65–70% (10-year), and 58% (15-year) in large retrospective series {18951519; 10366145}. However, there are high rates of distant metastases and death from disease. Lung metastases are common at presentation in high-grade tumours {18951519; 10366145; 1451062}. Poor prognostic indicators include an older age at presentation, a large tumour, and a proximal location {10366145; 10955458}. Adverse histology includes increased cellularity and pleomorphism {9781944; 10469211} and rhabdoid cells {10955458; 11493979}. Worse outcomes are associated with variant non-EWSR1 gene fusions than with EWSR1::NR4A3 {24746215}.
