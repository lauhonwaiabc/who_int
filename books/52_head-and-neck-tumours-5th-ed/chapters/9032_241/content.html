9.0.3.2 Follicular lymphoma

Definition

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is a neoplasm of germinal-centre B cells, composed of a mixture of centrocytes and centroblasts, that usually has a follicular growth pattern (at least partially).

ICD-O coding

9690/3 Follicular lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A80.Z Follicular lymphoma, unspecified

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Typical FL in the head and neck region is most often seen in older individuals and usually involves the cervical lymph nodes and the Waldeyer ring {9704731}. Rarely, FL can involve the salivary glands, the ocular adnexal region, and other locations {9704731; 11583402; 17786716; 16984613}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with painless enlarging lymph nodes or masses (see #26080Table 10.04).

Epidemiology

FL accounts for about 20–30% of all lymphomas {32586570}, and the incidence of this disease has increased in recent years {31548815}. The highest incidence rates are reported in the USA and western Europe; the incidence is lower in low- and middle-income countries than in high-income countries {27354024}. FL predominantly affects adults, with a median age of 65 years. There is no sex predilection. FL is 2–3 times as common in White populations as it is in Black populations {32586570}.

Etiology

Lifestyle and occupational risk factors have been associated with a higher risk of FL {25174024}.

Pathogenesis

FL is thought to derive from FL-like B cells that undergo the t(14;18)(q32;q21) BCL2 rearrangement in the bone marrow and circulate in the blood {17043145}. These circulating B cells are long-lived because of BCL2 overexpression. The circulating t(14;18) BCL2-rearranged B cells undergo several passages through the germinal centre, where they acquire additional genetic hits via somatic hypermutation, eventually leading to FL {25384217}. Localized and systemic FL differ in their gene expression profiles {31697802}.

Macroscopic appearance

Involved tissues have a fish-flesh appearance, which may be vaguely nodular.

Histopathology

Typical FL cases show numerous and crowded follicles of a uniform size consisting of centrocytes and centroblasts. These follicles lack a starry-sky pattern and polarization. Rare cases exhibit a predominantly diffuse pattern of growth, but these are usually encountered in inguinal lymph nodes. FLs are optionally graded according to conventional criteria, i.e. by counting or estimating the absolute number of centroblasts in neoplastic follicles per HPF of 0.159 mm in diameter and 0.458 mm2 in area (see #39421Table A in the foreword, for HPF conversion). Grade 1/2 FL (FL1/FL2) cases contain 0–15 centroblasts and grade 3A (FL3A) > 15 centroblasts per HPF of 0.159 mm in diameter and 0.458 mm2 in area. Follicular large B-cell lymphoma (formerly termed “follicular lymphoma, grade 3B” [FL3B]) cases consist exclusively of centroblasts and are rare. If a diffuse growth pattern is present in addition, these cases should be classified as diffuse large B-cell lymphoma with grade 3B follicular component.

As a rule, the tumour cells are positive for CD20 and germinal-centre markers such as CD10, BCL6, LMO2, and HGAL. BCL2 is expressed in 85% of FL1/FL2 cases and 60% of FL3A cases. Follicular large B-cell lymphoma (formerly FL3B) cases are more often negative for CD10 and BCL2 and may coexpress IRF4 (MUM1). The proliferation index in typical FL1 and FL2 cases is low, but FL3 may show Ki-67 indexes as high as 70–100% {11986240}.

See #26080Table 10.04 for the differential diagnosis.

Cytology

Smears show a mixture of centrocytes and centroblasts. Ancillary studies can be performed on FNA fluid specimens or cell blocks.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FL involving head and neck lymph nodes or tonsils in systemic disease harbours the t(14;18)(q32;q21) translocation in 85% of cases and has a characteristic mutation pattern involving genes such as KMT2D, TNFRSF14, CREBBP, and EZH2. BCL6 may be rearranged in BCL2 translocation–positive or BCL2 translocation–negative cases. Follicular large B-cell lymphoma (formerly FL3B) is often negative for the BCL2 gene translocation but may show BCL6 rearrangement {21659362}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: presence of an atypical lymphoid proliferation of follicular-centre cells with a corresponding immunophenotype and/or genotype.

Desirable: follicular pattern.

Staging

Lymphomas are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

Stage and the Follicular Lymphoma International Prognostic Index (FLIPI) are major prognostic determinants. More recently, gene expression profiling and the establishment of clinicogenetic risk models have proved useful in the prognostication of patients {29475724; 26256760}.

Tumour grade has some prognostic impact. FL grades 1, 2, and 3A are clinically indolent, whereas FL grade 3B often exhibits more aggressive behaviour and usually requires diffuse large B-cell lymphoma–like chemotherapy.
