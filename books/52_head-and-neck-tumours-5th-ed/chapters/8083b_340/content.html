8.0.8.3B Neuromas

Definition

Neuromas constitute a diverse group of peripheral nerve sheath tumours, some reactive and hyperplastic, that include traumatic neuroma (TN), solitary circumscribed neuroma (SCN), multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2B (MEN2B)-associated mucosal neuroma, oral pseudoperineurioma (OPP), and PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum (PROS)-associated neuroma (PIK3CA neuroma).

Related terminology

Acceptable: for solitary circumscribed neuroma: palisaded encapsulated neuroma.

Not recommended: for traumatic neuroma: amputation neuroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

TN occurs in areas of nerve injury including bones. SCN frequently affects facial skin and palate/gingiva. Most OPPs occur on the tongue. PIK3CA neuromas affect the tongue, lips, and cheek.

Clinical features

Neuromas manifest as single nodules (TN, SCN) or multiple nodules (PIK3CA neuroma, rarely in SCN) of < 10 mm with the exception of TN. Many but not all TNs are painful; all other neuromas are asymptomatic. Multiple mucosal neuromas unrelated to MEN2B and PROS have been described {26708403}.

Epidemiology

TN: Middle-aged adults; more frequent in women.

SCN: Fifth to seventh decades of life; twice as common in men.

OPP: Young female patients.

Mucosal neuromas: Usually the first sign of MEN2B and of PIK3CA-related orofacial overgrowth.

Etiology

TNs are the result of accidental or iatrogenic nerve injury or amputation. Although SCNs are considered hyperplastic lesions, trauma maybe a possible cause for oral SCN, whereas cutaneous lesions have been associated with acne. MEN2B- and PIK3CA-related neuromas are caused by germline RET and somatic PIK3CA mutations, respectively. Solitary OPPs are considered the result of trauma. Multiple OPPs can be encountered in PROS.

Pathogenesis

TN: Exuberant proliferation of disorganized peripheral nerve fascicles as part of nerve regeneration after nerve injury.

SCN: Intraneural fascicular Schwann cell proliferation.

MEN2B and PIK3CA neuromas: Enlargement of peripheral nerve bundles as part of RET germline mutations and PIK3CA somatic mutations, respectively.

OPP: Perineurial proliferation as part of apparent nerve injury or nerve enlargement as seen in PROS.

Macroscopic appearance

The size of TN varies. SCN and PROS-associated neuromas are small smooth nodules.

Histopathology

TN: Disorganized Schwann cell fascicles surrounded by perineurium sprouting from severed nerve {30011306}.

SCN: Fungating, round, multinodular, or plexiform in the superficial dermis / lamina propria, characterized by intersecting Schwann cell fascicles with nerve axons frequently revealing cracking artefact {20237984}.

OPP: Nerve fascicles showing complete or incomplete perineurium featuring thin, internally located, perineurial cell pseudo–onion bulbs {23645379}.

PIK3CA neuroma: Enlarged nerves with frequently hyperplastic perineurium or OPP. May share features with MEN2B necessitating clinicopathological correlation {28665924; 32770747}.

Cytology

Not described

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PIK3CA variants in the majority of PIK3CA neuromas

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

TN: haphazardly arranged nerve fascicles.

SCN: a fungating, multilobular/plexiform, circumscribed, partially encapsulated fascicular proliferation.

OPP: pseudo–onion bulbs.

PIK3CA neuromas: enlarged nerves; thickened perineurium; OPP.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is excellent for all types described.
