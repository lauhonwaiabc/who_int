14.0.4.4 Ataxia-telangiectasia

Definition

Ataxia–telangiectasia (AT) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by pathogenic constitutional homozygous or compound heterozygous pathogenic variants of the ATM gene, leading to different diseases in multiple systems.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

208900 Ataxia–telangiectasia; AT

ICD-11 coding

4A01.31 DNA repair defects other than combined T-cell or B-cell immunodeficiencies

Related terminology

Not recommended: Louis–Bar syndrome.

Subtype(s)

Variant ataxia–telangiectasia

Localization

Neurological manifestations and conjunctival telangiectases are important features of the disorder. Haematolymphoid and solid malignancies involve a variety of anatomical compartments, including the head and neck region.

Clinical features

Patients develop progressive cerebellar ataxia associated with cerebellar degeneration in early childhood, telangiectases in sun-exposed areas (bulbar conjunctiva, bridge of nose), other progressive neurological symptoms, immunosuppression with deficiency of cellular immunity, recurrent sinopulmonary infections, hypogonadism, insulin resistance, and malignancies. Lymphoid neoplasms of the B- and T-cell lineage predominate in patients with AT {31537806} and may present in the head and neck, with involvement of the Waldeyer ring. Myeloid neoplasms are distinctly uncommon. Other solid tumours developing in these patients may involve the brain, gastrointestinal tract, endocrine system, parotid gland {11764097}, and liver.

Variant AT is associated with milder disease and protracted manifestations often identified in adulthood. Telangiectases are often absent in these patients {19535770; 22345219; 30549301}.

Epidemiology

AT has an incidence of approximately 1 case per 40 000–100 000 births {33194896}. The disorder is encountered in all geographical locations, with variable prevalence.

Etiology

AT is caused by homozygous pathogenic sequence variants involving ATM, located in chromosome region 11q22.3. In a small proportion of cases, mutations in MRE11 (MRE11A) in chromosome region 11q21 are associated with a variant AT–like disorder.

Pathogenesis

The ATM gene is large, comprising 66 exons, and pathogenic mutations are scattered throughout it with no distinct hotspots {12552559}. Most constitutional pathogenic sequence variants are truncating and result in classic phenotypes. Missense and in-frame mutations associated with residual protein levels may account for milder phenotypes, referred to as variant AT. ATM encodes a serine/threonine protein kinase of about 300 kDa with sequence homology to the PI3K family but DNA repair functions. It is a nuclear protein that plays an important role in the repair of double-stranded chromosome breaks. Affected patients demonstrate hypersensitivity to ionizing irradiation. Activated ATM protein monomers are recruited to areas of DNA damage. The MRN complex is required for the optimal activation of ATM at areas of double-stranded DNA breaks, whereas several protein kinases (e.g. CHK2) and p53 key substrates are phosphorylated by ATM. Other functions of ATM include regulation of the cell cycle, apoptosis, telomere maintenance, response to oxidative stress, mitochondrial homeostasis, and insulin signalling. Heterozygous ATM pathogenic variant carriers account for a small fraction of hereditary breast, ovarian, and pancreatic cancers {22585167; 33471991}.

Macroscopic appearance

No difference is observed in the setting of AT compared with the tumours’ sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

No difference is observed in the setting of AT compared with the tumours’ sporadic counterparts.

Cytology

No difference is observed in the setting of AT compared with the tumours’ sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Deletions near or involving ATM have been reported in subsets of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) {9829743}. In one study, hypermethylation of the ATM promoter was detected in 25% of HNSCCs {14744748}, and this was associated with decreased expression in cell lines in vitro. In a targeted sequencing study, putative somatic pathogenic variants were detected in 6 of 10 (60%) high-risk cutaneous HNSCCs {28984303}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic neurological (ataxia, cerebellar degeneration) and non-neurological manifestations (telangiectases), which may be absent in young children; elevated AFP in serum (in > 95% of patients); decreased immunoglobulins (IgA and IgE, selective IgG subtypes); lymphopenia; increased chromosome breakage after exposure of blood cells to X-rays; ATM constitutional pathogenic sequence variant.

Staging

See individual tumour types.

Prognosis and prediction

The lifetime risk of malignancy in patients with AT is about 25%. Cancer and respiratory insufficiency are leading causes of death in these patients.
