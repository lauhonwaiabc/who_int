13.1.0.3 Squamous cell carcinoma of the external auditory canal

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the external auditory canal (EAC) is a carcinoma showing squamous differentiation arising from the keratinizing squamous epithelium lining the EAC.

ICD-O coding

8070/3 Squamous cell carcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C31 & XA1RS6 & XH0945 Squamous carcinoma of skin & Head and neck & Squamous cell carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Origin within the EAC instead of extension from adjacent pinna or periauricular skin sites {30069837; 32311775}

Clinical features

Chronic otorrhoea, otalgia, bleeding, hearing loss, otitis externa or otitis media, and facial palsy are nonspecific symptoms {33216374}. An EAC mass, although rare, is more suggestive of the diagnosis {11734117; 22926989; 30069837}.

Imaging

High-resolution CT of the temporal bone and contrast-enhanced MRI are complementary modalities in the preoperative assessment of EAC SCC {27639864}. CT allows detection of bone erosion and delineates the surgical anatomy, whereas MRI demonstrates the extent of soft tissue, intracranial, and nodal involvement. PET-CT is also useful in the detection of nodal involvement and distant metastases, and for monitoring for residual/recurrent disease.

Epidemiology

EAC SCC affects 0.16 per 100 000 individuals and accounts for 0.2% of head and neck malignancies {10912706; 23845289}. It presents in the sixth to seventh decades of life, with an equal sex distribution {4842612; 33568243}.

Etiology

EAC SCC is associated with longstanding chronic suppurative otitis media and irradiation {18948828; 21765382; 31890889}. Rarely, sinonasal-like papillomas may transform into SCC {22470050; 25564042; 31890383; 34021464}.

Pathogenesis

The genetic profile is complex, with amplification of chromosomal arms 3q, 5p, and 8q; 3p deletions; and TP53, CDKN2A, NOTCH, and FAT alterations, similarly to SCC at other sites {32500594}. Solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation damage may be seen in pinna SCC. Genetic alterations from carcinogens such as tobacco and alcohol or with viral etiologies are not seen.

Macroscopic appearance

Ulceration of an EAC mass is common.

Histopathology

The morphological appearance and immunohistochemical profile are similar to those of SCCs of other cutaneous sites. Carcinoma in situ may be seen in the adjacent epithelium, and concurrent sinonasal-type papilloma may also be noted. Cholesteatoma and chronic otitis may be seen in some cases {10828803; 31130732}. Most tumours are well differentiated and keratinizing {23507994; 23845289; 25401454; 25654948; 28858932}. Poorly differentiated non-keratinizing tumours may be seen, with further evaluation as technically or clinically appropriate.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunoreactivity for pancytokeratins, p40, p63, and CK5/6 is useful in separating the tumours from other lesions in the differential diagnosis.

Differential diagnosis

Distinction from cholesteatoma, pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia, cutaneous basal cell carcinoma, and cutaneous melanoma is important. Direct extension to the EAC from an SCC of the pinna or another nearby cutaneous site should be excluded. Documenting direct extension from salivary gland, nasopharynx, or sinonasal tract tumours is important for management. In poorly differentiated non-keratinizing tumours, translocation-driven tumours (BRD4::NUTM1, DEK::AFF2, EWSR1::FLI1, EWSR1::ERG) should be considered {32366754}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infiltrating squamous proliferation with architectural and cytological features of carcinoma; origin from the pinna should be excluded.

Desirable: immunohistochemical confirmation of squamous lineage can be useful in poorly differentiated tumours (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for head and neck cutaneous SCC, although modifications are suggested {30500288}.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are locally aggressive, with nodal metastases. Factors like large tumour size, depth of invasion > 8 mm, soft tissue extension, lymphovascular or perineural involvement, incomplete resection, and nodal metastases portend a poor prognosis {19381105; 23845289; 24843224; 30500288; 32367152; 33216374}.
