9.0.4.4 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL) is an extranodal lymphoma of NK- or T-cell lineage, characterized by vascular damage and destruction, prominent necrosis, cytotoxic phenotype, and association with EBV.

ICD-O coding

9719/3 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

9719/3 Nasal lymphoma, involving the nasal and/or paranasal region

9719/3 Extranasal lymphoma, arising from the skin, gastrointestinal tract, testis, or soft tissue

ICD-11 coding

2A90.6 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: lethal midline granuloma; malignant midline reticulosis; polymorphic reticulosis; angiocentric immunoproliferative lesion; angiocentric lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

Nasal lymphoma (involving the nasal and/or paranasal region); extranasal lymphoma (arising from the skin, gastrointestinal tract, testis, or soft tissue)

Localization

About 85% of ENKTLs occur in the upper aerodigestive tract, with the nasal cavity being the most common site of involvement {16380410; 23232851; 24438142; 25554090}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with nasal obstruction, epistaxis, and necrotizing lesions of the nose or hard palate. Extensive inflammation associated with a tumour results in periorbital oedema, proptosis, and diplopia.

Epidemiology

ENKTL affects adults, with a median age of 43–53 years and a male predominance {21854649; 32105608; 32658985; 32891786}. The disease rarely affects children and adolescents {26712870}. The prevalence of ENKTL varies depending on ethnicity and geographical location. ENKTL accounts for 0.7–17% of malignant lymphoma cases in eastern Asian countries {18251781; 20806229; 21854649; 29661730; 30311404} and for 0.5–7.8% of cases in Central and South American countries {22263570; 23086753; 30900209}, but for < 0.1% of cases in the USA {19029440; 27618563; 27778143}.

Etiology

The etiology of ENKTL is poorly understood, other than its association with EBV exposure. Epidemiological data suggest that genetic or environmental factors predispose some individuals to the disease. Genome-wide association studies in an Asian population identified SNPs on IL18RAP (rs13015714) on chromosome 2q12.1, HLA-DRB1 (rs9271588) on chromosome 6p21.3, and HLA-DPB1 (rs9277378) on chromosome 6 that were associated with an increased risk of ENKTL {27470079; 31879220}.

Pathogenesis

ENKTL demonstrates complex genomic alterations; the most consistently detected chromosomal aberration is deletion of 6q21-25 {16049916; 19965620; 29097495}, which harbours tumour suppressor genes including PRDM1, PTPRK, and FOXO3 {19194464; 21690554; 25612622}. Recurrent mutations identified in ENKTL include those of STAT3, JAK3, BCOR, ARID1A, TP53, MGA, and DDX3X {11733360; 22705984; 25980440; 26192917}. Deregulation of signalling pathways and genes (NF-κB, BIRC5, MYC, RUNX3, AURKA, EZH2, and CD274 [PDL1]), epigenetic alterations, and microRNA deregulation have been identified {21052088; 21294123; 21610143; 21921041; 23529930; 23963825; 25760809; 27737703; 28119527; 28349165; 29386059; 30299211; 32183952}. Recently, three ENKTL molecular subtypes have been proposed, named TSIM, MB, and HEA. The TSIM subtype is associated with JAK/STAT activation, NK-cell origin, and PDL1 overexpression; the MB subtype shows MYC overexpression and a poor outcome; and the HEA subtype is characterized by epigenetic changes, NF-κB activation, and T-cell origin {32183952}.

Macroscopic appearance

There is destructive growth with ulceration and necrosis.

Histopathology

ENKTL exhibits a polymorphous infiltration of neoplastic cells mixed with inflammatory cells. The cytomorphology of the tumour cells varies and is most often composed of medium-sized cells or a mixture of small and large lymphocytes with hyperchromatic and irregular nuclei. Angiocentric growth and extensive necrosis of tumour tissue are found in most cases of ENKTL. In the nasal cavity, tumours are often accompanied by pseudoepitheliomatous proliferation of overlying squamous mucosa.

The cellular lineage of the tumour cells is either NK or T cell {25554090; 23232851; 22314189}. The typical immunophenotype of ENKTL is CD20−, sCD3−, cCD3+, CD4−, CD5−, CD8−, and CD56+, and expression of cytotoxic molecules such as TIA1, granzyme B, and perforin. A minority of cases can be sCD3+, CD8+, or CD56−. T-cell receptor antigen is usually negative and frequently lost, even in T-lineage ENKTLs {25554090; 27015135}. Therapeutic targets including CD30, PDL1, and CD38 are commonly expressed {29052238; 26770954; 31653980; 27696202; 32766875; 28188133; 25865943}.

Differential diagnosis

EBV-negative extranodal cytotoxic T-cell lymphoma should be diagnosed as peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS. Nodal cytotoxic T-cell or NK-cell lymphoma with EBV association is diagnosed as EBV-positive nodal T- and NK-cell lymphoma. See the Haematolymphoid tumours volume for additional details on the differential diagnosis [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]].

Cytology

ENKTL rarely presents in effusion and shows anaplastic tumour cells with fine to coarse azurophilic granules {32020785}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) in situ hybridization reveals EBV infection in virtually all tumour cells. TR genes are clonal in as many as 40% of cases {15195017; 22314189; 27015135}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a malignant neoplasm in an extranodal location; EBV positivity in cytotoxic T or NK tumour cells.

Staging

Most patients (76%) present with Lugano stage I or II disease {32066865; 33054461}.

Prognosis and prediction

The 5-year overall survival rates of patients diagnosed with nasal lymphoma and extranasal lymphoma are 54% and 34%, respectively {26873565; 32105608; 33054461}. Patients aged > 60 years and those with stage III or IV disease, distant lymph node involvement, extranasal lymphoma, and a higher level of circulating EBV DNA have a significantly worse prognosis {26873565; 32105608; 33718104}.
