13.1.0.5 Cystic chondromalacia

Definition

Cystic chondromalacia is a degenerative pseudocystic lesion of the auricular cartilaginous plate.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

AA41.Y Other specified acquired deformity of pinna & Benign idiopathic cystic chondromalacia of the pinna

Related terminology

Acceptable: auricular pseudocyst.

Not recommended: auricular seroma; otoseroma; intracartilaginous cyst; auricular endochondral pseudocyst.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ear helix, scaphoid fossa, concha, or triangular fossa {30470633}

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a unilateral, fusiform, painless, slightly fluctuant swelling {1556479; 3755327; 5904038; 6508627; 15068513}; rare bilateral lesions may be asynchronous {2354120; 3083365; 6508627; 15068513}. Lesions present rapidly (< 12 weeks) and lack overlying skin changes. A rare association with atopic eczema is reported, perhaps related to rubbing or repeated microtrauma {2354120}.

Epidemiology

Auricular pseudocyst tends to be more common in young men (mean age: 35 years; M:F ratio: 9:1), although either sex and all ages may be affected {1556479; 3755327; 5904038; 6508627; 15068513; 30564784; 30921629}. It has been described in all ethnic groups, although an increased incidence is reported in Chinese patients {1556479; 3083365; 5904038; 6508627; 15068513; 23575386; 30564784}.

Etiology

The etiology is undefined. Possibilities include ischaemic necrosis (related to repetitive minor trauma), abnormal release of lysosomal enzymes by chondrocytes, and an embryological fusion defect {3755327}. Trauma may be accounted for by contact sports, movement disorders (such as [Friedreich] ataxia, spasticity, or dyskinesia), or earphone or mobile phone use {1556479; 3755327; 5904038; 6508627; 15068513; 23473281; 26693094}.

Pathogenesis

IL-1, a mediator of inflammation, induces IL-6 (stimulates chondrocyte proliferation) and stimulates proteases and PGE2 production by chondrocytes, leading to decreased extracellular matrix formation, and thus may contribute to intracartilaginous cleft development {15341921; 21178846}. LDH isoenzymes are elevated compared with autogenous blood {23575386}, suggesting a disrupted cartilage origin {23575386; 26693094}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions can be as large as 40 mm (mean: 20 mm). Unroofing procedures may show a central intracartilaginous cleft. Incision or aspiration may yield viscous, clear to olive oil–coloured fluid {3755327; 15235361}.

Histopathology

The cartilaginous plate contains a central cleft without epithelium (pseudocyst). The contour of the cleft may be irregular, associated with a rim of fibrous tissue or granulation tissue with plump fibroblasts. Mucoid material, haemosiderin deposits, granulation tissue, and fibrosis are variably present, depending on lesion duration or prior procedure {1556479; 3755327; 5904038; 6508627; 12619465; 15235361; 30470633; 25536139; 25255358}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential includes relapsing polychondritis, chondrodermatitis nodularis chronica helicis, and traumatic perichondritis.

Cytology

FNA is sometimes performed, showing acellular smears of granular proteinaceous material {10945913}. Fluid LDH levels may be supportive in the correct clinical setting {30921629}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: degenerated central ear cartilage, with a spectrum of haemorrhage, granulation tissue, and fibrosis.

Staging

Not performed

Prognosis and prediction

Cosmetic concerns often prompt therapeutic intervention, to avoid infection, auricular chondritis, and further deformity. Recurrence is seen in about 1–5% of cases {15068513}.
