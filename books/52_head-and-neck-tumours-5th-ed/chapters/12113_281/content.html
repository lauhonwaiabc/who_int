12.1.1.3 Ectopic or invasive PitNET / pituitary adenoma

Definition

Ectopic or invasive pituitary neuroendocrine tumour (PitNET) / pituitary adenoma is a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumour (NET) composed of adenohypophysial cells occurring within the sinonasal tract.

Related terminology

Subtype(s)

PitNET subtypes are outlined in #25089Table 15.02 .

Localization

Invasion into the sinonasal tract from the sella is much more common than for truly ectopic PitNETs {28009611; 29434339}. Ectopic PitNETs arise in the sphenoid sinus or upper nasopharynx {22430769; 28009611; 10773251; 15274075; 3017137} and less frequently in the ethmoid sinus and nasal bridge {22430769}.

Clinical features

Symptoms may be attributed to endocrinopathies, mass effect, or both. Cushing syndrome, acromegaly, and amenorrhoea/galactorrhoea are the most common endocrine manifestations {8550790; 12114673; 12170097; 12611618}. Mass effects include obstruction, sinusitis, rhinorrhoea, and headache {22430769; 28009611}. Laboratory investigation for elevated pituitary hormones, with and without stimulation or suppression testing, informs functional status as part of the classification. Familial or syndromic cases may require investigation {19153518; 19407507; 19945022}.

Imaging

Preoperative imaging studies determine the tumour size and extent, and exclude direct extension from the sella, a common occurrence {28009611}. 68Ga-DOTATATE PET shows increased uptake {30797906}.

Epidemiology

NETs of the sinonasal tract account for about 3% of all sinonasal tract tumours, and PitNETs account for a significant proportion, especially in the sphenoid sinus {10773251; 15274075; 22430769}, indicating that direct extension is much more common than ectopic disease. Patients with ectopic disease present over a broad age range (2–84 years), with the majority in their sixth decade of life. There is a slight female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:1.3) {22430769; 28009611}.

Etiology

The majority of PitNETs occur sporadically {1977759}. A minority of tumours are associated with familial predisposition syndromes, implicating specific germline mutations in the development of these tumours {25905184; 32201880; 31658440}.

Pathogenesis

Among sporadic tumours, the most common recurrent somatic mutations that drive tumorigenesis affect GNAS in as many as 40% of somatotroph tumours {17514647; 1977758; 2549426}, and USP8 and USP48 in approximately 50% of corticotroph tumours {25485838; 25675982; 25942478; 26578638; 28505279; 28982703; 29957855; 31222332; 30093687}. Novel mutations associated with sporadic tumours include those of CABLES1 {26695862; 28533356} in corticotroph tumours, and ATRX or TP53 {18026859; 33106857} in PitNETs with metastasis. Apart from these rare events, recurrent molecular alterations have not been identified and instead epigenetic alterations may be relevant to tumorigenesis {20060434; 28711607; 29396598; 31139150; 16873443; 18544619}. Chromosomal alterations are common, which is unusual for tumours with largely indolent behaviour {28486603}.

Macroscopic appearance

Ectopic PitNETs range in size from 8 to 80 mm (mean: 35 mm) {3017137; 22430769; 28009611}. Generally removed as fragments, they have no specific gross features.

Histopathology

PitNETs are generally monomorphic, with cells arranged in a variety of histological patterns, including diffuse, glandular, papillary, and trabecular arrangements. Cytologically, tumour cells may be acidophilic, basophilic, or chromophobic; however, these tinctorial characteristics are nonspecific. In addition to general neuroendocrine differentiation markers, PitNETs are also typically diffusely positive for INSM1. PitNETs reflect their derivation from six adenohypophysial cell types with multiple subtypes of each. Some PitNETs are composed of less differentiated cells, such as acidophil stem cell and immature PIT1-lineage tumours (formerly known as silent subtype 3 adenomas or poorly differentiated PIT1-lineage PitNETs) {26743473; 28766057}. Null cell tumours fail to demonstrate evidence of differentiation into SF1, TPIT, or PIT1 lineage {31401793; 29434339; 25403448}. Cells may have densely or sparsely granulated cytoplasm according to the tumour type (see #25089Table 15.02), explaining the variable histological tinctorial qualities. Nuclei tend to be bland with regularly distributed chromatin. Mitotic activity is generally low. The presence of extensive cellular pleomorphism, brisk mitotic activity, lymphatic or vascular invasion, atypical mitoses, and necrosis is not typical and should prompt consideration of alternate diagnoses. Calcification or ossification is rare, occurring in occasional lactotroph and thyrotroph tumours. Infiltration of bone/cartilage is common, and minor mucoserous glands may be surrounded or displaced by tumours.

Hormone immunohistochemistry alone can be misleading because of nonspecific or diffusion-type staining; therefore, the use of stains for pituitary transcription factors along with hormone profiling is required to ensure accurate tumour characterization {32813227}. These tumours frequently have trapped non-tumorous elements that can complicate interpretation {32813227}, further emphasizing the importance of thorough assessment. The distinction of multiple synchronous PitNETs of distinct lineages requires the use of transcription factors {30215160}. For these reasons, the current WHO classification of PitNETs requires the routine use of transcription factors (PIT1, SF1, TPIT, GATA3, and ERα) and antibodies against adenohypophysial hormones (ACTH, growth hormone, prolactin, TSH-β, LH-β, FSH-β, and the alpha subunit of glycoprotein hormones), as well as other biomarkers including but not limited to keratins (e.g. CAM5.2, CK8/18) {29453601; 32813227; 34017065; 29434339} (see #25089Table 15.02) [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours [Internet; beta version ahead of print]. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/53.]]. Like for other NETs, the Ki-67 proliferation index should be assessed using either manual counting or automated image analysis nuclear algorithms {29434339; 34017065}. When metastatic disease occurs, the term “PitNET with metastasis” is preferred; the use of “adenoma” and “carcinoma” creates a problem because most patients present with an initial primary tumour and the development of metastases would require a change in the initial diagnosis {34017065} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours [Internet; beta version ahead of print]. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/53.]]. There is no single morphological feature or biomarker that can accurately predict metastatic spread {34017065; 22822048; 33433883}. There is also no widely accepted grading system for PitNETs.

The distinction of PitNETs from other tumours and the detailed histological subtyping of a PitNET based on its transcription factor lineage, cell type, and (in some instances) subtype as determined by other characteristics are clinically critical, because accurate classification provides risk stratification and predicts differential responses to therapies {34017065; 32813227; 26177314; 24144285; 29434339}.

The differential diagnosis includes paraganglioma, olfactory neuroblastoma, hypothalamic neurocytoma, sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma, teratocarcinosarcoma, primary sinonasal well-differentiated NET, melanoma, meningioma, metastatic tumours, and even rhabdomyosarcoma {28060373; 30217041; 30379651}. Many sinonasal tract tumours may coexpress neuroendocrine markers, including sinonasal adenocarcinomas, sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma, and Ewing sarcoma, among others, but morphological features with specific biomarker reactivity are required for this distinction.

Cytology

In cytological preparations, PitNETs exhibit hypercellularity and a homogeneous appearance. Because there is a relative lack of stroma compared to normal, the cells of tumours do not aggregate in large numbers and are seen predominantly as individual cells and small tissue fragments. Papillary formations may be seen. These tumours cannot be distinguished from other NETs without immunohistochemistry.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an epithelial well-differentiated neuroendocrine neoplasm (i.e. a NET) with immunohistochemical evidence of neuroendocrine differentiation; immunohistochemical confirmation of adenohypophysial differentiation using pituitary transcription factors and hormones.

Desirable: CAM5.2 or CK8/18 positive; Ki-67 proliferation index.

Staging

There is no formal staging system that has been universally applied for pituitary/sellar neoplasms, and any proposals would not apply to ectopic tumours. However, by definition sellar tumours presenting as a sinonasal mass would have a high-stage classification using the Knosp classification {8232800}.

A pathology reporting template for pituitary tumours was introduced in 2011 {21526962}, and this was followed by the European Pituitary Pathology Group (EPPG) proposal in 2019 {31578606}. The International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting (ICCR) dataset for CNS tumours includes tumours of the anterior pituitary [[Louis DN, Brandner S, Brat D, et al. Tumours of the central nervous system (CNS) reporting guide. 1st ed. Sydney, Australia: International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting; 2018.]] and may be adapted for use in the context of ectopic tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

PitNETs continue to grow without intervention, and those with hormone hypersecretion cause medical complications that can be lethal. Prognosis is dependent on tumour subtype and response to therapy {24144285}. Recurrences may develop and are more likely when incompletely excised initially {7857023; 19945022; 22430769}. Complete tumour resection is challenging given the tumour location. Exceptionally, metastatic behaviour may be seen {3095506; 7857023; 12170097; 26891865}.
