7.2.2.1 Ameloblastic carcinoma

Definition

Ameloblastic carcinoma (AC) is a primary odontogenic carcinoma histologically resembling ameloblastoma.

ICD-O coding

9270/3 Ameloblastic carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5J & XH4M89 Malignant miscellaneous tumours of bone or articular cartilage of other or unspecified sites & Odontogenic tumour, malignant

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant ameloblastoma; dedifferentiated ameloblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

AC is an intraosseous jaw carcinoma with a mandibular predilection, usually arising posteriorly {28600599; 26768075; 31444935}. Most ACs arise de novo, but there may be pre-existing ameloblastoma {6366686; 17368379; 25436009}. Extraosseous AC is extremely rare {11120479}.

Clinical features

Patients present with jaw or orofacial swelling, sometimes with pain and paraesthesia {28600599; 31444935}, and later with tooth mobility and extension into the oral mucosa and trismus {19800270; 26768075; 31444935}. Rarely, AC is associated with hypercalcaemia of malignancy {11113817}.

Imaging

Contrast-enhanced CT shows a poorly defined expanding lesion with medullary and cortical bone destruction {19800270}. MRI delineates soft tissue or marrow extension. Moderate FDG uptake on PET-CT helps detect metastasis {20841465; 25436009}.

Epidemiology

AC accounts for ≤ 2% of all odontogenic tumours {15660091; 17156974; 30448231} and 30% of malignant odontogenic tumours {22385300}. A wide age range is affected, with a median age of 49 years {28600599}, and there is a male predilection {17448710; 25013517; 28600599}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Some ACs develop in longstanding untreated ameloblastoma {26768075} or in locally recurrent ameloblastoma after surgery and/or irradiation {6366686; 24906942; 11113817}.

Pathogenesis

BRAF p.V600E mutations have been reported in up to 40% of ACs {25854168; 26306423; 30889301; 33176823}.

Macroscopic appearance

AC is a solid mass with irregular borders and limited cystic change {20841465; 26238174}.

Histopathology

AC resembles ameloblastoma and has variable features of malignancy. The architecture ranges from ameloblastoma-like follicular or plexiform patterns to solid, compact sheets of basaloid epithelium or anastomosing strands with a loss of stellate reticulum. Peripheral palisading, nuclear reverse polarity, and stellate reticulum must be evident, at least focally. The threshold for diagnosis of malignancy is poorly defined, but ACs usually show some of the following: at least moderate cellular or nuclear atypia, increased mitotic activity, increased N:C ratio, hyperchromatic vesicular nuclei, crowding of basal cells and expansion of the basal cell compartment with disordered polarity, and expansion of atypical cells into stellate reticulum. Central necrosis in islands is a helpful finding, but it may be subtle with apoptosis. Extension into the mucosa and infiltration into the medullary space do not distinguish benign ameloblastoma from AC. Increased mitotic activity or Ki-67 proliferative fraction cannot define AC, and emphasis is placed on identifying a combination of architectural and cytomorphological abnormalities.

Minor differentiation patterns include squamous, clear cell, spindle cell, and pseudosarcomatous morphology. Combinations are possible, and these features vary in extent {17448710; 19800270; 24906942; 27195964; 30928327}. Dispersed invasion and perineural infiltration are uncommon in AC {27195964; 30500289; 33176823}, in contrast to other odontogenic malignant neoplasms {18753945; 23715163}.

Benign ameloblastoma may be present as a precursor lesion {23099227; 24906942}.

Immunohistochemistry

Unlike ameloblastoma, AC shows expression of p53 {27195964}, SMA, and SOX2 {19150605; 24603057}, but these are not validated as arbiters of malignancy.

Differential diagnosis

This includes benign ameloblastoma, especially maxillary examples that can show high cellularity. Basaloid AC must be separated from basal cell ameloblastoma, basaloid squamous cell carcinoma, and adamantinoma-like Ewing tumour {26034869; 31895100}. AC with spindle cell changes warrants the exclusion of spindle cell / sarcomatoid squamous cell carcinoma, carcinosarcoma, and other spindle cell malignancies {26238174; 30928327}. AC with clear cell features must be separated from clear cell odontogenic carcinoma {23715163}. A primary intraosseous carcinoma lacking ameloblastoma features is better categorized as primary intraosseous carcinoma.

Cytology

FNAB may detect possible metastasis in known cases {20841465}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRAF p.V600E mutations {25854168; 33176823} and TP53 mutation {24906942} are of undefined diagnostic value. EWSR1 translocation indicates clear cell odontogenic carcinoma rather than AC with clear cell phenotype {23715163}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a histological resemblance to ameloblastoma; cytological atypia.

Desirable: an intraosseous jaw tumour; tumour necrosis; BRAF mutations (in a subset); an associated ameloblastoma precursor.

Staging

Although there is no defined eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system staging guidance, the use of International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting (ICCR) minimum data set reporting is encouraged {30500289}.

Prognosis and prediction

Radical surgical resection is the treatment of choice, with radiotherapy for non-surgical candidates, recurrent/metastatic disease, and/or inadequate surgical margins {30393089; 26796877}. AC recurs locally in up to 40% of cases {19800270; 25436009}, and metastases to cervical nodes occur in up to 13% of patients {28600599; 30509051; 33176823}. Distant metastasis develops in one third of cases, most commonly in the lung, followed by the brain, liver, and bone {17942338; 28600599}, worsening the prognosis {19800270; 31444935}. The 5-year overall survival rate is 70%, better after complete surgical resection {28600599}. Origin in a benign precursor {6366686; 17368379; 25436009} and histopathological patterns do not affect the clinical course {29362117}. Novel treatment considerations include proton beam therapy {30240599} and BRAF-targeting agents {26796877}.
