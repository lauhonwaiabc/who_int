9.0.5.1 Hodgkin lymphoma

Definition

Classic Hodgkin lymphoma (CHL) is a neoplasm derived from germinal-centre B cells, characterized by a low fraction of tumour cells (Hodgkin/Reed–Sternberg [HRS] cells) embedded in a reactive microenvironment rich in immune cells. The large neoplastic cells show a defective B-cell expression programme.

Nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL) is a germinal centre–derived B-cell neoplasm composed of scattered large neoplastic B cells with multilobated nuclei (LP cells) within nodules dominated by mantle zone B cells and follicular dendritic cells.

Related terminology

Acceptable: Hodgkin disease.

Subtype(s)

Classic Hodgkin lymphoma (nodular sclerosis, mixed cellularity, lymphocyte-rich, and lymphocyte depletion); Hodgkin lymphoma, nodular lymphocyte-predominant type

Localization

Hodgkin lymphoma is usually a node-based disease. Fewer than 1% of cases involve extranodal sites in the head and neck region {20564093; 7503365; 15981804; 33478377}. Most commonly, the Waldeyer ring is affected, including the nasopharynx and tonsils. It is frequently associated with cervical lymphadenopathy. Hodgkin lymphoma in the parotid usually arises from intraparotid lymph nodes {25929348}. Hodgkin lymphoma of the thyroid is considered in the WHO classification of endocrine tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours [Internet; beta version ahead of print]. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/53.]].

Clinical features

Waldeyer ring involvement often causes local symptoms including difficulties in swallowing or obstructed nasal respiration {12373352}. Fewer than 20% of patients with Hodgkin lymphoma manifesting at these sites have B symptoms.

Epidemiology

Hodgkin lymphoma accounts for 15–25% of malignant lymphomas, with NLPHL representing 3–8% of all Hodgkin lymphoma cases. Overall, Hodgkin lymphoma shows slight male preponderance; this is more marked (M:F ratio: 2.4:1) for NLPHL {8922237; 10071266}.

Etiology

The etiology of Hodgkin lymphoma is unknown, but a proportion of cases are associated with EBV {8836402; 33686198}.

Pathogenesis

HRS cells are germinal-centre B cells with somatically hypermutated IG genes, an absence of immunoglobulin expression, and a lack of a B-cell gene expression programme, whereas LP cells retain their B-cell phenotype {10637266; 12393731; 27496304}. HRS cells show constitutive activation of NF-κB and other protumorigenic signalling pathways and produce a variety of cytokines and chemokines responsible for the inflammatory infiltrate and the generation of a protective microenvironment {21368758; 21483001; 27496304}.

Macroscopic appearance

CHL nodular sclerosis may show gross nodularity and fibrotic bands, whereas other subtypes exhibit a whitish, homogeneous cut surface.

Histopathology

CHL and NLPHL are essentially different diseases, each with its own pathogenesis, morphological spectrum, and immunophenotype.

The main feature of CHL is the presence of characteristic HRS cells and variants in a mixed inflammatory infiltrate. The cellular composition of the background varies depending on the subtype {27496304}. Classic Reed–Sternberg cells are binucleated or multinucleated large cells with nuclei with open chromatin and large, eosinophilic nucleoli. The mononuclear cells are called Hodgkin cells. In nodular sclerosis CHL, the lacunar cell is characterized by clear haloes surrounding the frequently lobulated nuclei with less prominent nucleoli. CHL in the Waldeyer ring shows a higher frequency of mixed-cellularity and lymphocyte-rich subtypes than primary nodal disease {7503365; 15981804; 20564093}. HRS cells in CHL express CD30 and CD15 in almost 100% and 75–85% of cases, respectively. They are positive for IRF4 (MUM1) and weakly express PAX5, whereas other B-cell markers are either negative or weakly positive in occasional tumour cells. LMP1 is expressed in EBV+ cases. The background lymphoid population is dominated by T cells, except for lymphocyte-rich CHL, which exhibits small B cells in a nodular arrangement {10961891; 30407610}.

NLPHL is characterized by rare, large, atypical LP cells with frequently lobulated nuclei in a background of small lymphocytes usually arranged in a macronodular fashion {30407610}. LP cells in NLPHL show a retained B-cell programme and express CD20, CD79a, B-cell transcription factors (PAX5, OCT2, BOB1, and MEF2B), and (variably) EMA. The background usually is dominated by small B cells. LP cells are rosetted by PD1+/CD57+ T follicular helper (TFH) cells {30407610}.

Differential diagnosis

HRS-like cells can occur in a variety of non-Hodgkin lymphomas and reactive conditions. In the Waldeyer ring, infectious mononucleosis and EBV-associated mucocutaneous ulcer are important differential diagnoses of EBV+ CHL {29518976}. NLPHL needs to be distinguished from lymphocyte-rich CHL and small B-cell lymphomas {10961891; 30407610}. Distinction from melanoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, and sarcoma is usually straightforward.

Cytology

FNA specimens show HRS or LP cells in a mixed inflammatory background, but primary diagnosis requires a tissue biopsy {11842377}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular studies are not required and are of limited diagnostic use.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Classic Hodgkin lymphoma

Essential: primary nodal or mediastinal presentation; HRS cells with the typical immunophenotype (CD30+, CD15+/−, PAX5+ [weak], CD20 negative/ weak/ heterogeneous, ALK−) in an appropriate inflammatory background.

Nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma

Essential: primary nodal presentation; LP cells with preserved B-cell phenotype in a nodular (at least focally) background with many small lymphocytes, histiocytes, and follicular dendritic cells, often with typical rosetting of CD57+/PD1+ T cells.

Staging

Lymphomas are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

CHL and NLPHL are essentially different diseases, each with its own clinical behaviour and treatment requirements. With modern therapy regimens, 80–90% of patients with Hodgkin lymphoma can be cured {29194581}. Stage is the most important predictor of outcome {25113753}.
