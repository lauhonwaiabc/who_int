8.0.3.3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma (EHE) is a malignant, fusion gene–associated vascular neoplasm composed of epithelioid endothelial cells in a distinctive myxohyaline stroma.

ICD-O coding

9133/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, NOS

9133/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion

9133/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with YAP1::TFE3 fusion

ICD-11 coding

2B5Y & XH9GF8 Other specified malignant mesenchymal neoplasms & Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant epithelioid haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

EHE with WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion; EHE with YAP1::TFE3 fusion

Localization

These are rare tumours of the head and neck region arising typically in soft tissue of the neck, oral cavity, salivary glands, or bone {24986479; 26298095; 16301153; 21584898; 10846129}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a slowly growing mass, which is sometimes painful because of vascular occlusion. Other symptoms are site- and size-dependent. There is a propensity for lymph node metastases {31950476; 31463731; 26298095; 16301153}.

Epidemiology

There is a wide age range of patients, from children to the elderly, with young to middle-aged adults being mainly affected {22420724; 24986479; 31950476; 31463731}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

As a specific driver mutation, the WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion gene due to the chromosomal translocation t(1;3)(p36;q25) occurs in most EHEs {11342784; 21584898; 21885404; 25961935}. Rare cases display a YAP1::TFE3 fusion due to a t(X;11)(p11;q13) translocation {23737213; 24986479}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours form a (multi)nodular mass, typically showing a pale, solid cut surface, sometimes with haemorrhage {24986479; 26298095; 31950476}.

Histopathology

Bland-looking epithelioid, spindle, and histiocytoid endothelial cells are arranged in short cords and strands in a myxohyaline stroma {33776722}. They show abundant hyaline cytoplasm with subtle intracytoplasmic lumina. Striking nuclear atypia is seen in approximately 30% of tumours. Mitotic activity is usually low. Multicellular vascular channels are sometimes present, with brightly eosinophilic cytoplasm and a solid growth, especially in YAP1::TFE3-associated lesions. Immunohistochemically, CD31 and ERG are consistently expressed, and epithelial antigens may be expressed. CAMTA1 or TFE3 is expressed, corresponding to the fusion genes {7093931; 23737213; 21584898; 24986479; 31950476; 31463731}.

Differential diagnoses are extensive and include epithelioid haemangioma, epithelioid angiosarcoma, myoepithelial tumours, sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma, epithelioid sarcoma, carcinomas, pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma, chordoma, chondrosarcoma, and extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma {7093931; 31950476; 31463731; 24986479}.

Cytology

Cytological examination may show clusters of epithelioid and spindle cells with round to ovoid nuclei, occasional vacuolated cytoplasm, and scant mitotic figures {32797671}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular identification of the WWTR1::CAMTA1 or YAP1::TFE3 fusion may be helpful in selected cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an infiltrative lesion composed of strands, cords, and nests of bland-looking epithelioid, spindle, and histiocytoid cells with typical glassy cytoplasm and subtle intracytoplasmic lumina, in a myxohyaline background.

Desirable: immunohistochemical positivity for CD31, ERG, and CAMTA1 or TFE3.

Staging

Staging is not applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Clinical behaviour and prognosis depend on the primary site. Most cases behave in an indolent manner, but there is a risk of metastatic disease and a significant mortality rate (as high as 45%) {31950476; 31463731}.
