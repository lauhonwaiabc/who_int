11.2.0.1 Branchioma

Definition

Branchioma is a benign neoplasm composed of an admixture of spindled cells, epithelial islands, and adipocytes, generally affecting the lower anterior neck.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

XH0707 Ectopic hamartomatous thymoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: biphenotypic branchioma.

Not recommended: ectopic hamartomatous thymoma; branchial anlage mixed tumour; thymic anlage tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Branchioma principally affects the lower anterior neck, proximate to the suprasternoclavicular area {15371953; 27428736; 28879635; 29995801; 32026292}. It is rarely presternal {9720942; 26763841}.

Clinical features

Patients most commonly present with a slow-growing subfascial suprasternoclavicular swelling, which is left-sided more often than right-sided {6742312; 1693594; 2050369; 15371953; 27428736; 28879635; 29995801; 32026292}.

Epidemiology

This very rare tumour is identified in a wide age range (19–89 years, median: 40 years) and has a male predilection (M:F ratio: 3.4:1) {6742312; 1693594; 2050369; 15371953; 27194678; 27428736; 28879635; 29995801; 32026292}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Few cases have been studied, with next-generation sequencing analysis revealing a hotspot mutation in HRAS (p.Q61K) without other known fusions (specifically PLAG1 rearrangement) in 1 case {23339366; 32026292}. The tumour is considered to arise from misplaced branchial pouch derivatives, containing tissues from two primordial layers (mesoderm and endoderm: biphasic) {32026292}. The branchial apparatus embryologically gives rise to a very complex and coordinated development of the neck structures, including fat, myoepithelial cells, and epithelial cells, supporting a branchial origin.

Macroscopic appearance

Branchioma is a lobular to multilobular mass, ranging in size from 14 to 190 mm (mean: 50 mm) {32026292}. It shows a grey-white to yellow solid cut surface.

Histopathology

Branchioma is typically well circumscribed or encapsulated, with a haphazard proliferation of spindled cells, epithelial islands, and adipocytes, in variable proportions. The epithelial cells form non-keratinizing squamoid islands, anastomosing cords, cysts, or glandular structures. The glands are often surrounded by myoepithelial cells. Skin adnexal differentiation (including sebaceous, eccrine, and apocrine elements) may occur {17574947}. Plump spindled cells show fascicular, storiform, or lattice-like growth, often merging into the epithelial component. They possess bland-looking elongated nuclei and light-staining cytoplasm. Myoid features may occasionally be prominent {2076891; 8496655}. Fat cells are haphazardly present, sometimes associated with lymphocytes. Among the epithelial and spindled cell components, there are delicate spindly stromal cells with small nuclei and scant cytoplasm. Rarely, carcinoma may develop in the epithelial component, but it is generally contained macroscopically {8130171; 8857650; 32026292}.

Immunohistochemistry

The epithelial component is immunoreactive for pancytokeratin; the squamoid islands are also positive for CK5/6, p63, and p40. The plump spindly cells are positive for pancytokeratin, CK5/6, p63, p40, and SMA, indicating myoepithelial differentiation {1693594; 12390415; 15371953; 16640545; 17574947; 27428736; 28879635; 32026292}. The delicate spindly cells are negative for pancytokeratin and positive for CD34.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant, although reported {10097715; 17718153}

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a solitary well-circumscribed mass located in the suprasternoclavicular area; haphazard proliferation of benign spindled cells, epithelial islands, and adipocytes, in the absence of primitive elements.

Staging

Not performed

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence is rare {6742312; 15371953; 26617926; 28879635; 32026292}. Even with a supervening carcinoma component, death from disease has not been reported.
