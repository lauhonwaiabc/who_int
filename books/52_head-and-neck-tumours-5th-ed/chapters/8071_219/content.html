8.0.7.1 Chondroma

Definition

Chondroma is a benign soft tissue neoplasm, unrelated to bone or synovium, and composed of chondrocytes that produce hyaline or myxoid cartilage.

ICD-O coding

9220/0 Chondroma, NOS

9220/0 Chondroblastoma-like soft tissue chondroma

ICD-11 coding

2E89.1 & XH0NS4 Benign tumours of uncertain differentiation, soft tissue & Chondroma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: chondroma of soft parts; extraskeletal chondroma.

Subtype(s)

Chondroblastoma-like soft tissue chondroma

Localization

These tumours usually arise in the hands and feet (80%). An origin in the head and neck is uncommon {19349148}. Case reports describe lesions in the preauricular region and auricle {24653926; 22844293}, cheek {15955665; 8229872}, parotid gland {16487372}, lip {29115679}, tongue {30845089; 15617977; 6590720}, neck and parapharyngeal space {8587816; 17008056}, and dura {25250651}.

Clinical features

These are painless, firm, slowly growing masses {26297064}. Lesions have a high signal on T2-weighted MRI. There may be ring-like or spiculated calcification.

Epidemiology

Patients are usually middle-aged (average: 34.5 years) and are more frequently male than female (M:F ratio: 1.5:1) {76505}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic analyses reveal several different aberrations, including rearrangements of 12q13-q15 and trisomy 5, and in a set of tumours with rearrangements of 12q13-q15, a truncated or full-length HMGA2 transcript was found {8402563; 10748295; 11793371; 14614053; 25250651}. In about 50% of the cases, FN1 gene rearrangements were reported, with FGFR1 and FGFR2 as fusion partners {31273315}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions are well-circumscribed, solid, nodular masses, 10–20 mm in size, with a grey-blue, sometimes myxoid cut surface.

Histopathology

Chondromas are nodular tumours that are sometimes thinly encapsulated and are composed of mature hyaline or myxoid cartilage, with lobules delineated by fibrous septate. Cartilage may be hypercellular, and the usually small, hyperchromatic nuclei may be large, with coarse chromatin, small nucleoli, and no more than moderate pleomorphism. Calcification or endochondral ossification may occur. Degenerative changes include fibrosis, mucinous change, and cyst formation {24653926}. The chondroblastoma-like subtype is hypercellular, with epithelioid chondrocytes showing grooved or cleaved nuclei and individual cell calcification {11342780}. Mitoses are few or absent.

Cytology

Cytology is rarely performed. Smears may show variably sized clusters and single cells in a myxofibrillary matrix. Nuclei are oval to spindled, with moderate variation in size, and with finely dispersed chromatin and small, indistinct nucleoli. Cytoplasm may be abundant and vacuolated and cell margins indistinct. No multinucleation or mitoses are seen {11213511}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a soft tissue mass composed of nodules of cartilage; hyaline or myxoid matrix that may calcify; cytologically bland chondrocytes; few mitoses.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign tumours with no recurrence after complete excision.
