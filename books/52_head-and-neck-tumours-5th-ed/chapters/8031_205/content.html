8.0.3.1 Haemangioma

Definition

Haemangioma is a benign vascular neoplasm.

ICD-O coding

9120/0 Haemangioma, NOS

9131/0 Capillary haemangioma

9121/0 Cavernous haemangioma

9131/0 Lobular capillary haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.0Y & XH5AW4 Other specified neoplastic haemangioma & Haemangioma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: lobular capillary haemangioma; capillary haemangioma; cavernous haemangioma.

Not recommended: pyogenic granuloma; pregnancy epulis; histiocytoid haemangioma, epithelioid haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

Capillary haemangioma; cavernous haemangioma; lobular capillary haemangioma

Localization

The oral cavity (gingiva and lips) and sinonasal tract (anterior septum) mucosae are the most commonly affected head and neck sites {1595597; 1642875; 6307911; 7435775; 11685962; 16955784; 17478135; 19203815; 20417014; 23184353; 30673134}, with rare reports in other organ sites {29106877; 11115277; 25439553; 22503446; 21766313}.

Clinical features

Oral lesions present as a solitary, red haemorrhagic papule, whereas unilateral epistaxis and/or an obstructive painless mass is seen in the sinonasal tract {7435775; 10827405; 16539297; 16859060; 17478135; 19203815; 20417014; 23184353; 25207171; 28153757; 28389160; 29019747; 29977856}. A compressible mass is noted in major salivary gland tumours {25439553; 22503446; 21766313; 11115277}.

Imaging

Imaging studies show an intensely enhancing tumour surrounded by a hypoattenuating peripheral rim, often with bone remodelling in sinonasal locations {1642875; 20007721; 23703148}.

Epidemiology

Mucosal haemangiomas account for about 10% of head and neck haemangiomas and about 25% of non-epithelial sinonasal tract neoplasms {4362952; 7435775; 16955784; 23184353}. Haemangiomas occur in patients of all ages, with a median of 40 years. Incidence peaks in boys and adolescent males, and in pregnant women. Haemangiomas of the head and neck account for about 7% of all benign tumours in children. The sex distribution is equal in patients aged > 40 years {9425482; 4362952; 6307911; 1642875; 7435775; 23184353}. Salivary gland haemangiomas develop almost exclusively in neonates and children, with girls affected more frequently than boys (M:F ratio: ~1:2) {8419878; 11115277; 29106877; 25439553; 22503446; 21766313}.

Etiology

Lobular capillary haemangioma is associated with mucosal injury, hormonal factors (pregnancy and oral contraceptive use) {21745433; 22713445; 16955784; 30628110}, and selective BRAF inhibitors {25251629; 27116335}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown for head and neck tumours. Nevertheless, the hypothesis that infantile (congenital) haemangiomas are proposed to originate from ectopic placenta cells has recently been supported by their abundant expression of the C19MC cluster of microRNA genes {22374805; 27660822}.

Macroscopic appearance

The mean size is < 15 mm, but examples as large as 80 mm have been reported {16955784; 23184353}. Gross appearances range from a diffuse flat mass to a bulging, polypoid nodule. The lesions are soft, often with secondary surface changes {9425482; 4362952; 6307911; 1642875; 7435775}.

Histopathology

Haemangiomas of the sinonasal tract are primarily divided into capillary and cavernous types. Other subtypes are reported rarely {1642875; 7435775; 16955784; 1497117; 23184353}.

Lobular capillary haemangioma is a circumscribed proliferation of capillaries with plump endothelial cells surrounded by pericytes in a fibromyxoid stroma, arranged in one or more lobules which may show high cellularity. Each lobule has a large central vein surrounded by small capillaries, with an overlying collarette of epithelium (often ulcerated or atrophic). Mitoses are often identified and lack atypia.

Cavernous haemangiomas are composed of multiple large, cystic, thin-walled, blood-filled spaces lined by endothelial cells and separated by scant connective tissue stroma, and they may be considered vascular malformations.

The neoplastic cells show CD34, CD31, ERG, FLI1, and von Willebrand factor (factor VIII–related antigen) immunopositivity, with variable expression of ER and PR {16955784; 1497117; 23184353}. KSHV/HHV8 is negative.

Tumours should be distinguished from granulation tissue, reactive or malformative vascular polyps, papillary endothelial hyperplasia, glomus tumour, angiofibroma, glomangiopericytoma, lymphoma, and angiosarcoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Lobular capillary haemangioma

Essential: a polypoid, lobular, circumscribed anastomosing network of capillaries with plump endothelial cells and often a proliferation of pericytes in a fibromyxoid stroma arranged in one or more lobules.

Desirable: vascular immunohistochemistry markers (in selected cases).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Multiple recurrences (up to 45%) are more common in children and may develop if the lesional bed is not completely eradicated {17478135; 23184353}. Spontaneous regression is documented in paediatric and postpartum patients {22852124; 23075363; 23184353; 25181659; 27144115; 27271813; 29019747; 29215342; 30319828; 30673134}.
