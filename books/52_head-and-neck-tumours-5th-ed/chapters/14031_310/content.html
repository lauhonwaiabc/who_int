14.0.3.1 Familial paraganglioma syndromes

Definition

Familial paraganglioma syndromes are a group of inherited cancer syndromes characterized by the presence of paragangliomas.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

See #27513Table 16.03 .

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial paraganglioma-phaeochromocytoma syndromes; hereditary paraganglioma-phaeochromocytoma syndromes; hereditary phaeochromocytoma-paraganglioma.

Subtype(s)

Familial paraganglioma syndromes are shown in #27513Table 16.03 .

Localization

Parasympathetic-derived paragangliomas commonly arise in the head and neck region, including in the carotid body and along cervical branches of the glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves {24642075}. Familial paragangliomas may be multifocal; they occur anywhere in the body. Sympathetic-derived paragangliomas are usually intra-adrenal (phaeochromocytoma), retroperitoneal, or para-aortic (arising at the inferior mesenteric artery and above the aortic bifurcation).

Clinical features

Clinical manifestations may be caused by catecholamine excess and/or mass effects. In the head and neck, mass effects predominate.

Symptoms of epinephrine/norepinephrine excess include sweating, palpitation, and anxiety; signs include hypertension and tachycardia. These are generally associated with sympathetic paragangliomas. Parasympathetic paragangliomas may secrete dopamine with minimal clinical manifestations, and others (mainly those of the head and neck) are non-secretory. There is strong genotype–phenotype correlation in the catecholamine profile. Tumours with pseudohypoxic pathogenesis are cluster 1 tumours, which tend to be clinically silent and non-secretory or dopamine-secreting; cluster 2 comprises tumours with kinase signalling and rare phaeochromocytomas with WNT pathway activation that are usually functional.

Cluster 1 tumours express SSTR and are well visualized with 68Ga-DOTATATE PET-CT or the less sensitive indium-labelled somatostatin. Cluster 2 tumours can be imaged with FDOPA PET-CT or iobenguane (metaiodobenzylguanidine; 123I-MIBG).

Epidemiology

Overall, about 40% of paragangliomas are hereditary. A younger age at presentation, multiple tumours, and extra-adrenal tumours are significantly associated with the presence of a constitutional pathogenic sequence variant {12000816}.

Etiology

The etiology is attributed to the inheritance of genetic alterations that predispose individuals to tumour development {28162975; 29239044}.

See #27513Table 16.03 .

Macroscopic appearance

Familial paragangliomas are often multifocal. Adrenal disease in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 is often bilateral and grossly multinodular.

Histopathology

The morphology of most familial paragangliomas overlaps that of sporadic paragangliomas. Some features may be suggestive of a genetic predisposition. Some succinate dehydrogenase (SDH)-associated sympathetic paragangliomas consist of monotonous cells with vacuolated eosinophilic cytoplasm {31789631}. Some have a distinct pseudorosette pattern {24659481}. SDH-related paragangliomas from the head and neck usually have small cells with clear cytoplasm. Unlike sporadic paragangliomas, SDH-associated tumours are rarely associated with a spindled morphology or densely granular cytoplasm {31789631}. Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome–associated tumours may have clear cells with vacuolated cytoplasm and stromal oedema {2114747}.

Immunohistochemistry identifies neuroendocrine markers (nuclear INSM1 and cytoplasmic synaptophysin and chromogranin) in tumour cells. S100 and SOX10 highlight sustentacular cells. Paragangliomas express nuclear GATA3 and cytoplasmic tyrosine hydroxylase. Most paragangliomas are immunonegative for cytokeratins {31442521}. Loss of SDHB immunoreactivity in tumour cells with granular cytoplasmic staining of stromal cells supports the diagnosis of SDH-related disease {16103922; 20236688; 19576851}; in tumours that lack SDHB immunoreactivity, loss of SDHA staining identifies patients with SDHA mutations {16103922; 21752896}. Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome–associated tumours have membranous positivity for CAIX. Tumours of the pseudohypoxic type express inhibin. FH mutations are characterized by a loss of expression of fumarate hydratase, which is highly specific but imperfectly sensitive, and aberrant expression of 2-succinocysteine, which is highly sensitive but imperfectly specific {24334767}.

Cytology

Cytological diagnosis of paragangliomas is challenging, but the use of immunohistochemistry may assist in the diagnosis and in the determination of familial disease {29575826}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic testing is required to confirm the mutation responsible for the disease.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the presence of ≥ 1 paraganglioma in a kindred or individual, or a confirmed constitutional pathogenic sequence variant in a susceptibility gene.

Desirable: loss of SDHB immunoreactivity has a high predictive value for SDHB, SDHC, or SDHD mutations.

Staging

Although the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system is applied to phaeochromocytomas and sympathetic paragangliomas, no TNM system is published for extra-adrenal paragangliomas in the head and neck. International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting (ICCR) and other expert-initiated reporting guidelines have been introduced {24476517; 32407815}.

Prognosis and prediction

Most paragangliomas can be surgically resected; however, large tumours and some head and neck tumour locations may preclude complete excision. Familial lesions are often multifocal; the presentations may be asynchronous, mimicking metastasis. Catecholamine profile, SDHB mutation, and somatic ATRX or TERT promoter mutations increase the risk of metastasis {30301828; 31705439}. Five-year overall survival rates in patients with metastatic paraganglioma range from 50% to 70% {28746746}.
