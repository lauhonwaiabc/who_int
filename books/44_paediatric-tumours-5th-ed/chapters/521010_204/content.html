5.2.10.10 Subungual exostosis

Definition

Subungual exostosis is a benign osteocartilaginous tumour of the bone surface occurring in the distal phalanx of a digit, underlying the nail bed.

ICD-O coding

9213/0 Subungual exostosis

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Not recommended: Dupuytren exostosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority of cases (nearly 80%) of subungual exostosis occur on the dorsal or medial aspect of the distal phalanx of the great toe, usually underlying the nail bed {422611; 3284396}, although occasionally it can arise in a periungual location without disturbance of the nail itself {15252309}. The remaining cases involve the distal phalanges of other toes or fingers {29704255}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with painful subungual lesions involving a distal phalanx, of a few months’ to years’ duration. The toenail may be eroded and elevated at presentation, and ulceration and infection may occur. On imaging, a small bony protuberance is seen attached to the dorsum or side of a distal phalangeal bone surface, with no continuity between the lesion and the medullary cavity of the native bone.

Epidemiology

Subungual exostosis often arises in young patients, typically in the second and third decades of life, with > 50% of patients aged < 18 years (average age: 25.7 years). No sex predilection has been reported {24146360}.

Etiology

Some reports indicate that chronic irritation, prior trauma, or infection are predisposing factors for developing subungual exostosis {15252309; 15875652}, suggesting that the pathogenesis is an inflammatory or reactive growth of cellular fibrous tissue and metaplastic cartilage that undergoes enchondral ossification. Recurrent clonal cytogenetic abnormalities, however, suggest a true neoplastic process.

Pathogenesis

A recurrent chromosomal translocation, t(X;6)(q24-q26;q15-q21), in analysed cases of subungual exostosis –often the only change – strongly suggests that it is a neoplastic lesion caused by rearrangement of genes in the two breakpoints {9885985; 15252309; 16284948}. The breakpoints are located in the collagen gene COL12A1 on chromosome 6 and in the vicinity of IRS4 on the X chromosome. The translocation does not result in any fusion transcripts; however, this rearrangement results in the increased expression of the IRS4 gene, which encodes an insulin receptor substrate, presumably due to disruption and/or exchange of regulatory sequences with its translocation partner, the COL12A1 gene. The exact role of IRS4 is still poorly understood, but IRS proteins are known to act as mediators of signalling from receptors, such as insulin receptor and IGF1R, and thus have an important effect on cell growth and survival {20340132}.

Macroscopic appearance

The lesions are typically small (5–12 mm) and consist of single or multiple fragments of bone and cartilage.

Histopathology

Subungual exostosis is composed of a central core of trabecular bone surrounded by a thin cap composed of hyaline cartilage and focally by fibrocartilage or fibrous tissue. A hypertrophic zone is often thin or lacking within the hyaline cartilage, and columniation is poor. Enchondral ossification with gradual progression to woven and lamellar bone is typically observed; however, the cartilaginous cap may be absent, and bone may form directly from spindle cells. The cellularity of the cartilage and fibrocartilage is variable and minor focal atypia may be identified, including binucleation and a high N:C ratio. Early lesions may lack attachment to the underlying bone {3284396}. Proliferating fibroblasts are present in direct continuity with the nail bed, sometimes embedded in a thick, collagenous matrix. A loose myxoid stroma may be focally present in continuity with areas of cartilaginous differentiation.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a small osteocartilaginous lesion on the surface of a distal phalangeal bone, with no continuity between the lesion and the medullary cavity of the underlying bone.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Subungual exostosis is benign, and local excision is curative. Recurrences may occur after incomplete excision {3284396; 24146360} and can be managed by re-excision.
