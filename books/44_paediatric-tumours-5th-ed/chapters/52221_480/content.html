5.2.2.2.1 Fibrous hamartoma of infancy

Definition

Fibrous hamartoma of infancy (FHI) is a soft tissue neoplasm of infants and young children, showing a characteristic organoid, triphasic morphology with bundles of bland fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells, nodules of primitive mesenchyme, and mature adipose tissue.

ICD-O coding

8992/0 Fibrous hamartoma of infancy

ICD-11 coding

LC2Y Other specified hamartomata derived from dermal connective tissue

Related terminology

Not recommended: subdermal fibromatous tumour of infancy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

FHI commonly involves the axillae, trunk, upper extremities, and genital region. Other locations include the scalp, foot, hand, orbit, and buttock {28059097; 24525510}.

Clinical features

Patients with FHI present with a painless solitary subcutaneous mass, sometimes with overlying skin discolouration, oedema, hypertrichosis, and tethering {28059097}. Large lesions and multiple tumours in the same patient can be encountered. FHI has been reported in patients with tuberous sclerosis and William syndrome {28059097; 19319513; 17326745}.

Epidemiology

FHI is rare, generally diagnosed by 2 years of age, with a male predominance. It is congenital in 15–25% of cases {24525510}. Occurrence in older children is possible {28059097}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Recurrent EGFR exon 20 insertion/duplication mutations have been demonstrated in these lesions {27631514}.

Macroscopic appearance

FHI is poorly circumscribed and ranges in size from 4 to 170 mm (mean: 30 mm), with a variable amount of adipose tissue and grey-tan fibrous tissue {28059097}.

Histopathology

FHI has a characteristic triphasic organoid pattern with fascicles of bland fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells admixed with mature adipose tissue and myxoid nodules containing primitive mesenchymal cells with rounded to stellate nuclei. One quarter of cases can show areas resembling giant cell fibroblastoma and composed of hyalinized zones containing collagen with cracking artefact forming slit-like spaces lined by flattened tumour cells. Mitotic figures are infrequent, and necrosis is typically absent. Interspersed inflammatory cells can be seen. Sarcomatous morphology has been reported in two tumours {28059097}.

By immunohistochemistry, FHI shows variable expression of SMA in the fibroblastic areas and in primitive mesenchyme. S100 is expressed in the adipocytic component. Primitive mesenchyme and areas with giant cell fibroblastoma–like morphology are CD34-positive. Minimal to moderate EGFR protein expression has been reported, mostly in the primitive mesenchyme {27631514}.

Cytology

FNAB-produced smears show bland fibroblasts in a collagenous matrix and mature fat without nuclear atypia or mitoses {18499982}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

EGFR exon 20 insertion/duplication mutations have been identified cases of FHI by targeted next-generation sequencing and Sanger sequencing {27631514}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic triphasic morphology with bundles of bland fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells, nodules of primitive mesenchyme, and mature adipose tissue.

Desirable: EGFR expression.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most patients are cured with simple excision, although roughly 15% of cases have been reported to locally recur if incompletely excised.
