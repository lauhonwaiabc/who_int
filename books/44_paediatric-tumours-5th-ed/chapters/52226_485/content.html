5.2.2.2.6 Lipofibromatosis

Definition

Lipofibromatosis (LPF) is a rare paediatric soft tissue tumour with a distinctive admixture of mature fat, fascicles of bland spindled cells, and lipoblast-like cells.

ICD-O coding

8851/1 Lipofibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

FB51.Y & XH4QB6 Other specified fibroblastic disorders & Lipofibromatosis

Related terminology

Not recommended: infantile/juvenile fibromatosis (non-desmoid type).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LPF involves the hands and feet, although it can occur in other locations {11075850}.

Clinical features

LPF typically manifests as a slowly growing subcutaneous mass {11075850}.

Epidemiology

LPF occurs more often in boys than in girls, with a wide age range (birth to 14 years). As many as 20% of reported cases have been congenital {11075850; 30310176}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

LPF may represent a morphological pattern common to a variety of locally recurring paediatric tumours of the extremities, rather than a single specific entity. The presence of FN1::EGF or FN1::TGFA fusions suggests that some cases probably represent early or non-calcified calcifying aponeurotic fibromas. Moreover, identification of fusions involving genes encoding EGFR (HER1) ligands (EGF, HBEGF, TGFA) or EGFR itself, or genes encoding other receptor tyrosine kinases (ROS1, RET, PDGFRB), suggests that activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway might be implicated in the pathogenesis of LPF {30310176}.

Cytogenetic abnormalities have been described in one case showing a translocation {18036401}.

Macroscopic appearance

The firm, rubbery, poorly demarcated mass has a white or yellow cut surface. Most are 10–70 mm in diameter, although very large tumours involving the entire extremity have been reported {11075850; 30310176; 19461290}.

Histopathology

LPF is composed of fascicles of mature fibrous tissue with bland, uniform, elongated nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm traversing mature adipose tissue. The adipose tissue may form a major proportion of the lesion. In newborn patients, the fat lobules may have an immature appearance with a myxoid matrix. Small collections of univacuolated cells may be present in the fibroblastic component, where melanin-laden cells have been occasionally documented. LPF entraps skeletal muscle and subcutaneous structures. Mitoses are rare and necrosis is absent. Immunohistochemically, the fibroblasts show variable expression of CD34 and SMA, and the fat is S100-positive. The uncommon pigmented cells express melanocytic markers {11075850; 18236073}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of the FN1::EGF gene fusion may be helpful in cases of calcifying aponeurotic fibromas lacking calcification {30310176}, although this is not required for routine diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: histology showing an admixture of mature fat, short fascicles of bland spindled cells, and lipoblast-like cells; exclusion of other entities with an LPF-like pattern (e.g. calcifying aponeurotic fibroma, infantile fibrosarcoma).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

LPF has a high rate of local recurrence but no metastatic potential. Congenital onset, male sex, acral location, mitotic activity in the fibroblastic component, and incomplete excision may be risk factors for recurrence {11075850}.
