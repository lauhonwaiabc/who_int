2.3.1.1 B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma

Definition

B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma is a neoplasm of precursor lymphoid cells committed to the B-cell lineage involving bone marrow and blood (B-lymphoblastic leukaemia, B-ALL) and occasionally occurring with primary involvement of nodal or extranodal sites (B-lymphoblastic lymphoma, B-LBL).

ICD-O coding

See #32041Table 2.07 .

ICD-11 coding

See #32041Table 2.07 .

Related terminology

Acceptable: B-cell lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma.

Not recommended: precursor B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma; B-cell acute lymphoid leukaemia; B-lymphoblastic lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

See #32041Table 2.07 .

Localization

B-ALL arises in bone marrow and/or peripheral blood or as an extramedullary mass with bone marrow involvement. By definition, B-LBL involves extramedullary sites such as lymph nodes and lymph, with little to no bone marrow involvement.

Clinical features

Patients with B-ALL can present with symptoms of cytopenias, bone pain, and/or extramedullary masses. B-LBL typically manifests as extramedullary masses (often adenopathy or skin nodules).

Epidemiology

Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) is the most common paediatric malignancy, and the incidence of ALL is higher in high-income countries than in low- and middle-income countries {24488779; 15698386}. In the USA from 2013 to 2017, the incidence in children (age: < 20 years) was approximately 3.5 new cases per 100 000 person-years, with a male predominance (M:F ratio: 1.3:1) [[Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) Program [Internet]. Bethesda (MD): National Cancer Institute; 2020. SEER Explorer. Available from: https://seer.cancer.gov/explorer/.]]. Peak incidence occurs between the ages of 1 and 4 years. B-ALL constitutes approximately 85% of ALL cases {23523389}. The prevalence of different subtypes varies depending on age (see #32041Table 2.07).

Etiology

The initiating causes of most B-ALLs are unknown, although there are some predisposing genetic risk factors. Children with Down syndrome have a 20-fold increased risk of ALL {26699264}, and many have CRLF2 rearrangement {22441210}. Children with Li–Fraumeni syndrome have an increased risk of B-ALL; low hypodiploid B-ALL (32–39 chromosomes) is strongly associated with germline TP53 mutations {23334668}. Germline variants in haematopoietic transcription factors, including ETV6 {26522332; 25807284; 26102509; 25581430}, PAX5 {24013638; 24287434}, and IKZF1 {29881510}, predispose children to B-ALL.

Pathogenesis

The development of B-ALL, like other cancers, occurs secondary to cumulative disruptions of key cell regulatory pathways {21376230; 29395053; 32565969}. Certain regulatory pathways appear to play a greater role than others in B-ALL because most dysregulations involve genes for self-renewal, cell proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation, or their signalling pathways. {25184049; 23612374; 30697448; 31367767; 32247396}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

With extensive involvement, the bone marrow and mass biopsies show dense infiltrate of small to medium-sized mononuclear cells consistent with blasts. In skin, the lymphoblasts diffusely infiltrate the dermis and subcutaneous adipose tissue while sparing the epidermis. Some cases show a starry-sky pattern.

Cytology

The term “lymphoma” is used when there is a mass lesion with no or minimal evidence of blood and marrow involvement (< 25% blasts in the peripheral blood and bone marrow). Without a mass, there is no specified required blast percentage for leukaemia; in general, it should be ≥ 20%.

Two morphological subtypes of B-ALL lymphoblasts (L1 and L2) are seen on Wright-stained peripheral blood or bone marrow smears {188440; 6938236}. These subtypes have no correlation with the genetic subtype or prognosis. Approximately 10% of cases of B-ALL with L1 lymphoblasts have coarse azurophilic (pale pink) granules that can be associated with BCR::ABL1 or Philadelphia (Ph) chromosome–like mutations {19926587; 28521628}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

B-ALLs with recurrent genetic abnormalities are defined by their characteristic chromosomal structural abnormalities using conventional karyotyping or FISH (see #32042Table 2.08). Patients with hypodiploid B-ALL should undergo germline TP53 mutation testing {32457520}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

  • For B-ALL: ≥ 20% B-cell lymphoblasts by flow cytometry, with B-cell lineage markers
  • For B-LBL: at histology, effaced lymph node architecture or diffuse infiltration of an organ by a monomorphic population of blasts with a B immunophenotype (positive for CD19, CD22, cCD79a, and/or PAX5) and markers of immaturity (positive for TdT, CD34, and/or CD10) but negative for surface immunoglobulin (although CD34 and TdT expression may be absent in rare cases, posing a diagnostic challenge {29187045})
  • For both B-ALL and B-LBL: identification of specific recurrent genetic abnormalities

Desirable:

  • Immunophenotypic profiles associated with specific recurrent genetic abnormalities (see #32041Table 2.07, p.XXX) {9359509; 12094248; 9823952; 6958337}

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic factors for B-ALL include age, sex, race, white blood cell count, CNS involvement, and cytogenetic/molecular findings; this last factor is the basis for B-ALL subclassification. Currently, the most reliable predictor is the detection of minimal residual disease during therapy, using flow cytometry, RT-PCR, or next-generation sequencing {20304809; 18388178; 23074282; 22593176}. The 5-year overall survival rate approaches 90%, but high-risk disease or persistent minimal residual disease requires allogeneic bone marrow transplantation {32247396}. B-ALL with strong CD45 expression can have a poor prognosis {23911702}.

A subset of B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma cases with specific recurrent genetic abnormalities show distinctive clinical and phenotypic properties with prognostic implications and are classified according to their genetic abnormalities.
