4.2.1.1 Epibulbar choristoma

Definition

Epibulbar choristoma is a benign congenital developmental lesion of histologically mature tissue in an abnormal location. Different subtypes can be distinguished: epibulbar dermoid is composed of dermal and epidermal elements, dermolipoma additionally contains a large amount of adipose tissue, and complex choristoma contains two or more ectopic tissues normally absent from the conjunctiva and caruncle.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.06 Epibulbar choristoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: dermoid tumour; mixed dermoid.

Subtype(s)

Epibulbar dermoid; dermolipoma; complex choristoma

Localization

Epibulbar choristomas are typically located in the superior or inferior temporal quadrants of the epibulbar compartment. If extensive, they can occupy the entire temporal epibulbar/conjunctival quadrant and may also involve parts of the cornea. They are occasionally found mainly on the cornea, with a minimal conjunctival component at the limbus {15350332}.

Bilateral involvement is rare, but it can occur in syndromic patients {9709758; 27405313}.

Clinical features

Dermoids are usually inferotemporal limbal, whitish, well-defined, firm lesions with cilia (eyelash hairs). They can sometimes be bilobed. A lipid line can be seen in the cornea in limbal dermoids.

Dermolipomas are superotemporal yellowish, soft, non-mobile lesions that may contain cilia. They are usually less well defined than a dermoid and can be pedunculated or extend into the orbit.

The features of complex choristomas depend on their underlying tissue composition.

Epidemiology

Epibulbar choristomas are rare. The prevalence in the population is unknown, but in one large series they constituted 2% of melanocytic and non-melanocytic conjunctival lesions {15350332}.

Etiology

Epibulbar choristomas are developmental anomalies. They are frequently observed in patients with Goldenhar syndrome or linear naevus sebaceus syndrome {3200959; 25772967; 3684203}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features (above).

Histopathology

Dermoids are composed of interweaving dermal-type collagen bundles covered by squamous epithelium (keratinized or non-keratinized, with or without goblet cells). Dermal appendages, in particular cilia with associated sebaceous glands and sweat glands, may be present. Variable amounts of fat can be observed.

Dermolipomas additionally contain a large amount of adipose tissue. Skin appendages, in particular cilia with associated sebaceous glands and sweat glands, are found less frequently and are less numerous than in epibulbar dermoids.

In complex choristomas, various additional heterotopic tissues can be found; lacrimal glands and cartilage are commonly encountered. Other components include smooth muscle {9709758; 2655139} and nerve fascicles {9709758}; more rarely there may be myxomatous tissue {21885965}, bone {3681614}, striated muscle {2655139}, immature tooth, and brain {166560}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: conjunctival/corneal localization; dermal-type collagen bundles and various components of mature epidermal tissue (adipose tissue for dermolipoma; additional components for complex choristoma); absence of malignant changes.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Malignant transformation has not been described. Debulking or excision is sometimes necessary to mitigate the risk of amblyopia associated with corneal lesions, inadequate lid closure, or dellen, or for cosmetic reasons {19512907; 7933633}.
