5.2.2.7 Paediatric NTRK-rearranged spindle cell neoplasm

Definition

This is an emerging family of rare spindle cell tumours showing a wide morphological spectrum, from lipofibromatosis-like lesions (lipofibromatosis-like neural tumour) to infantile fibrosarcoma–like lesions, and harbouring NTRK1, NTRK2, or NTRK3 gene rearrangements or other gene alterations (such as RAF1, BRAF, or RET) implicated in receptor tyrosine kinase pathway activation. Our understanding of this tumour type is rapidly evolving; additional genetic alterations may be discovered in future studies.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F7C Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: NTRK-rearranged mesenchymal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The superficial soft tissues of the extremities are the most common sites of disease; other locations include soft tissues of the trunk, scalp, or gastrointestinal tract {32506523; 31994201; 30276917; 32669612}.

Clinical features

Patients with these tumours most frequently present with a palpable mass.

Epidemiology

The largest series combined demonstrate a wide age distribution (range: 0–77 years; overall median: 19 years) {27259011; 30276917; 32506523}. In the paediatric population, > 55% of cases occur in the first year of life {31994201; 30585824}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Oncogenesis occurs through the activation of kinase signalling, most commonly through the formation of chimeric proteins involving NTRK1 and various partners {27259011; 30276917}. Other cases involve fusions of NTRK2, NTRK3, RAF1, and BRAF. Rare cases have been described harbouring fusions of RET, ROS1, or ALK {31994201; 27259011}. The same spectrum of genetic aberrations leading to kinase activation are present in infantile fibrosarcoma, raising the possibility of a common/related pathway of oncogenesis {30585824; 28877062; 29915264}. Additionally, tumours with infantile fibrosarcoma–like morphology cluster with RAF1 tumours with CD34 and/or S100 expression by RNA unsupervised hierarchical cluster analysis, again suggesting that these may be related entities and/or share a common pathogenesis {30276917; 28877062}.

Macroscopic appearance

This is not yet defined.

Histopathology

A broad morphological spectrum has been described, including tumours reminiscent of infantile fibrosarcoma, inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, lipofibromatosis, and malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour. The main histological patterns are summarized in #19869Table 6.04 {32243019; 30585824}. The most consistent feature is haphazardly arranged primitive/monomorphic spindle cells {28877062; 31994201; 27259011; 30276917; 31219820}. Some cases have distinctive stromal and/or perivascular hyalinization {30276917; 27259011; 30585824; 30938880}. Within this spectrum are tumours with low to high cellularity and a range of mitotic counts. By immunohistochemistry, many tumours show variable coexpression of CD34 and S100 {27259011}. In those tumours with NTRK gene fusions, pan-TRK expression is also present {29863809; 29683818}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Testing for NTRK gene or downstream effector molecule (BRAF) fusions or alterations may be useful {30276917; 30585824; 31994201}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: haphazardly arranged monomorphic primitive spindle cells; characteristic histological pattern(s) as summarized in #19869Table 6.04 ; fusions or activating alterations of receptor tyrosine kinase pathway genes (e.g. NTRK1, NTRK2, NTRK3, RET, RAF1, BRAF); exclusion of well-established pathological entities such as inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour and infantile fibrosarcoma.

Desirable: expression of CD34 and/or S100.

Staging

None validated

Prognosis and prediction

Paediatric tumours can recur locally and have a low rate of metastasis {30276917; 27259011; 30585824}. Limited data are available to determine whether histological features (increased cellularity, mitoses, necrosis) convey biological significance in these tumours. Some studies that included paediatric and adult patients suggest that the histology is of biological significance {30276917}; however, others do not {30585824}. Further studies are needed to define the prognostic role of histological parameters according to age. Given the recent advances in tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapies, the natural history and treatment regimens for these tumours are likely to change over time. Given the spectrum of genetic alterations that these tumours may contain, it is recommended that the pathology report clearly denote which fusion or activating mutation is present, because this may influence treatment.
