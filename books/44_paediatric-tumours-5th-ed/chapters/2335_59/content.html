2.3.3.5 Primary cutaneous CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorders

Definition

Primary cutaneous CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorders comprise lymphomatoid papulosis (LyP) (a recurrent, self-healing disease) and cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphoma (C-ALCL). These entities represent two ends of a spectrum of disease, with some cases showing overlapping clinical and histopathological features (borderline cases).

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Primary mucosal CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder; lymphomatoid papulosis types A–E; lymphomatoid papulosis with DUSP22 locus rearrangement

Localization

LyP usually affects the trunk and extremities. Rarely, mucosal lesions may be present.

C-ALCL affects the skin, with no site predilection.

Clinical features

Patients with LyP present with a variable number (few to hundreds) of reddish papular, papulonecrotic, nodular lesions in various stages of evolution. The lesions disappear after weeks, months, or even years {15023774}. The typical clinical course of waxing and waning lesions is crucial for the differential diagnosis.

Most patients with C-ALCL present with a solitary, frequently necrotic nodule or tumour. Rare multifocal cases have been reported {10845893}.

In patients with primary C-ALCL, contrast-enhanced CT (with or without PET) or whole-body integrated PET should be performed {21841159}.

Epidemiology

LyP represents about 48% of cutaneous haematological diseases in children aged < 15 years {21445946; 27138554}. However, its frequency might be underestimated because of its spontaneous resolution {24749749}. Type A is the most frequent subtype (accounting for 37% of cases of LyP in a recent paediatric series), and types B and C are less numerous (26% and 19%, respectively). Types D, E, and F are rare {32172217}. Cases with overlapping features (type A with type B, and type E with type F) have also been reported {32172217}.

C-ALCL in children is very rare {22773605; 15719203}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. C-ALCL may occur in patients who have received a transplant or in HIV-positive patients {20881832; 19694826}.

Pathogenesis

LyP shows a clonal rearrangement of the TR genes in 40–60% of cases. Rearrangement of DUSP22::IRF4 on 6p25.3 has been identified in a subset of LyP cases in adults. Atopy and staphylococcal superantigens have been pathogenetically linked to LyP {32049976}.

A subset of C-ALCL shows a t(2;5) translocation resulting in an ALK fusion {15165197; 22773605}. The incidence of DUSP22 or TP63 fusions in adult C-ALCL is unknown {30630983}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most cases are diagnosed by punch/incisional biopsies. Excisional biopsies are rare and show papular, nodular, or tumoural lesions (see Clinical features, above).

Histopathology

LyP

Type A LyP shows a wedge-shaped dermal infiltrate of neutrophils, eosinophils, small lymphocytes, and scattered atypical lymphocytes with larger nuclei, isolated or in small clusters. Multinucleated cells with Reed–Sternberg cell–like features may be present {18929914; 24749749; 27138554}.

Type B LyP shows an epidermotropic infiltrate of small atypical lymphocytes with cerebriform nuclei.

Type C LyP shows a nodular infiltrate composed of large lymphocytes with abundant cytoplasm, pleomorphism, and mitotic figures, simulating large cell anaplastic lymphoma.

Type D LyP shows an epidermotropic infiltrate of atypical small to medium-sized cells {32172217; 26763888}. Deep dermal and perivascular infiltrates may be seen.

Type E LyP shows an angiocentric and angiodestructive infiltrate of medium-sized pleomorphic lymphocytes, with frequent vascular occlusion, necrosis, and ulceration {23026936; 32172217}.

DUSP22::IRF4-rearranged LyP shows a biphasic growth pattern, with intraepidermal small cerebriform lymphocytes and larger lymphocytes in the dermis {23648461}.

Unusual rare morphological patterns include follicular, syringotropic, granulomatous, and spindle cell LyP {27993440}.

Immunophenotype

The atypical cells are CD30+ and CD3+. In LyP types A–C, cells are frequently CD4+, although CD8+ dominant forms appear to be more frequent in the paediatric age, especially in type C. A subset may be double-negative. Type D–E LyP cells are CD8+. In all types, the expression of CD7 and CD5 is variably reduced. TIA1 and granzyme B are most common in type B, type C, and CD8-predominant LyP.

Differential diagnosis

In all subtypes of LyP, the typical clinical course of waxing and waning lesions is crucial for the differential diagnosis.

C-ALCL

This tumour shows sheets of large pleomorphic lymphoid cells, with variable epidermotropism, angiocentricity, and angiodestruction. A prominent neutrophil infiltrate is seen in the neutrophilic pattern {20881832; 19694826}. DUSP22-rearranged C-ALCLs are more monomorphic. The cells express CD30 and have an activated T-cell immunophenotype, with variable loss of CD2, CD5, CD7, and CD3. C-ALCLs with ALK fusions are ALK-positive {22773605; 15719203}.

Differential diagnosis

For conventional C-ALCL, see #19837Table 2.11 . The neutrophilic pattern may mimic pyoderma gangrenosum and other non-neoplastic conditions.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

TR gene rearrangement can be useful in determining clonality in C-ALCL and LyP. DUSP22::IRF4 rearrangement is present in selected C-ALCLs, and in a subtype of LyP recognizable by its characteristic histology.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

LyP

Essential: a clinicopathological diagnosis combining clinical features (self-healing lesions that evolve and disappear after some weeks or months) and histology (atypical T cells, CD30 positivity); exclusion of infectious and inflammatory skin diseases.

Desirable: clonal rearrangement of TR genes.

C-ALCL

Essential: clinical appearance of a solitary necrotic nodule or tumour; histological appearance of sheets of large pleomorphic lymphoid cells, with variable epidermotropism, angiocentricity, and angiodestruction.

Desirable: no clinical history or evidence of mycosis fungoides (in such cases, the diagnosis of mycosis fungoides with CD30+ large cell transformation is more likely).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

LyP is a self-healing disease with frequent residual atrophic scars. The prognosis of the disease is benign if the patient does not develop a second lymphoma {21841159}. Although no progression to a second lymphoma was observed in one study of young patients {11251571}, 5–9% of paediatric patients with LyP present with a lymphoma before/at diagnosis or, more frequently, at a variable interval (even years) after diagnosis. Long follow-up is required {15023774; 27138554; 24749749}.

The prognosis of C-ALCL is excellent for both ALK-negative and ALK-positive cases, with spontaneous regression in a subset {21841159; 22773605}.
