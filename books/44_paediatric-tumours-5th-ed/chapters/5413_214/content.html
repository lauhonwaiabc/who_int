5.4.1.3 Chondromesenchymal hamartoma of the chest wall

Definition

Chondromesenchymal hamartoma of the chest wall is a benign tumour composed of cartilage, spindle cells, bone, and blood-filled cystic spaces.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F01 Benign neoplasm of intrathoracic organs

Related terminology

Not recommended: chest wall hamartoma; cartilaginous hamartoma; cartilaginous hamartoma of the chest wall; (benign) mesenchymoma; vascular and giant chondromatous hamartoma of the chest wall.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lesions arise within the medullary cavity or on the surface (mostly lateral and posterior regions) of a rib and may extend into multiple contiguous ribs. Bilateral or multicentric tumours have been reported {3969297; 8861490; 11756727; 16703172; 18181344}.

Clinical features

Infants may be asymptomatic or present with respiratory distress as a result of rib deformation and involvement of the chest cavity {3763502; 8166533; 8291008; 1409151; 18181344}. The mass may be palpable, or it may be detected incidentally on a chest radiograph for an upper respiratory tract infection {8166533; 8861490; 11756727; 18416338}.

Imaging

Plain radiography shows a well-defined lytic lesion with a sclerotic margin and an extrapleural soft tissue mass. The lesion has a solid and cystic appearance, more evident on CT. Haemorrhagic cavities with a secondary aneurysmal bone cyst–like appearance are noticed more on T2-weighted MRI {1409151; 11756727; 24574377}. The lesions appear as heterogeneous masses on T1- and T2-weighted images {11079622}.

Epidemiology

Chondromesenchymal hamartoma of the chest wall is extremely rare, with approximately 100 cases reported in the literature {18376284}; it is usually congenital and almost all cases are diagnosed in infants {8166533; 11756727; 11528603; 3763502; 3969297; 8291008; 8861490; 1409151; 16703172}. Rare cases in older children or adults have been reported {8782339; 30374636; 32398154}. The M:F ratio is 2:1

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

A well-circumscribed multinodular to lobulated mass, usually 50–100 mm. The cut surface shows blood-filled multicystic spaces, along with solid areas containing cartilage and bone {8291008; 18181344; 16703172}.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of nodules of hyaline cartilage, often with enchondral ossification, surrounding blood-filled spaces. The walls of the cystic spaces contain reactive bone, spindle cells, and osteoclastic giant cells, reminiscent of an aneurysmal bone cyst. The spindle cells lack cytological atypia and nuclear hyperchromasia {3763502; 1409151; 8291008; 11079622; 16703172; 18181344}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-defined solid-cystic lesion arising from the rib, composed of hyaline cartilage, blood-filled cystic spaces, woven bone, benign spindle cells, and scattered osteoclastic giant cells.

Desirable: presentation during infancy or early childhood.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Chondromesenchymal hamartoma of the chest wall is a benign tumour. Rare deaths due to respiratory insufficiency have been reported {18181344; 3763502; 1409151; 8166533; 16703172}. The treatment is surgical resection. Scoliosis and deformities may be complications. Spontaneous regression may occur {11517112}; therefore, asymptomatic patients can be treated conservatively {21129530}.
