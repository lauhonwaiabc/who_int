5.2.10.9 Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

Definition

Desmoplastic small round cell tumour (DSRCT) is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm composed of small round tumour cells associated with prominent stromal desmoplasia, polyphenotypic differentiation, and EWSR1::WT1 gene fusion.

ICD-O coding

8806/3 Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH5SN6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: intra-abdominal desmoplastic round cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The vast majority of DSRCTs are in the abdominal cavity. Tumours are frequently in the retroperitoneum, pelvis, omentum, and mesentery, and they commonly have multiple serosal implants. Extra-abdominal DSRCTs are rare and mainly restricted to the thoracic cavity or paratesticular regions {9042290}. Exceptional cases may occur in the limbs, head and neck, kidney, or brain {17414105}.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with widespread abdominal/peritoneal involvement {2473463; 1709557}. Clinical features (e.g. pain, abdominal distention, a palpable mass, an acute abdomen, ascites, and organ obstruction) are usually related to the primary site of presentation.

Epidemiology

DSRCT primarily affects children and young adults {1709557}. There is a striking male predominance and a peak incidence in the third decade of life, although it occurs over a wide age range (from the first to the fifth decade of life).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

DSRCT is characterized by recurrent chromosomal translocations, t(11;22)(p13;q12) {1314522; 7687454; 8374894}, which fuse the EWSR1 gene (on 22q12.2) with the Wilms tumour transcription factor gene, WT1 (on 11p13) {8187063; 7495283; 7862627}. These translocations usually join EWSR1 exons 1–7 (encoding the potential transcription-modulating domain) in-frame with WT1 exons 8–10 (encoding the last three zinc fingers of the DNA-binding domain). Rare translocations that retain additional EWSR1 exons also occur. EWSR1::WT1 transcription regulates the expression levels of various genes – PDGFA, IL2RB, the exocytosis regulator BAIAP3, TSPAN7 (TALLA-1), MLF1, IGF1R, CCN2, and LRRC15 {32703985; 15826834} – and activates the neural reprogramming factor ASCL1 to induce neural gene expression and partial neural differentiation {24934812}. Interestingly, the mesothelial lining of body cavities, the most common site of DSRCT, also expresses WT1. During embryogenesis, WT1 is expressed at high levels in intermediate mesoderm–derived tissues, primarily those undergoing mesenchymal–epithelial transition {1722569; 8758208; 2164159}. This pattern recapitulates the epithelial differentiation observed in DSRCT.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients with DSRCTs typically present with bulky multinodular masses. Tumours often comprise a dominant solid mass and multiple smaller accompanying nodules or serosal implants. The cut surface is firm, homogeneous, and grey or white-tan. Foci of necrosis or haemorrhage can be present.

Histopathology

DSRCTs often comprise sharply demarcated nests of undifferentiated small cells and a surrounding desmoplastic stroma. The nests can vary in size and shape from tiny cords to large, irregularly shaped confluent sheets. Necrosis is common in the centre of larger nests. Haemorrhage and cystic degeneration can also be present. In some DSRCTs, the tumour cells are arranged into tubular, glandular, trabecular, or rosette-like structures. DSRCTs typically have uniform, small, round or oval cells; rarely, they have epithelioid or spindled cells. The tumour cells have hyperchromatic nuclei, scant cytoplasm, and indistinct cytoplasmic borders. Some tumours have larger cells with greater nuclear pleomorphism. DSRCT cells may have intracytoplasmic (rhabdoid-like) inclusions or signet-ring cell–like intracytoplasmic vacuoles. Tumours usually have numerous mitotic figures. The stroma of DSRCT is made of fibroblasts or myofibroblasts embedded in a collagenous, loose, or myxoid matrix {9738572; 9808123}.

DSRCTs have a polyphenotypic immunohistochemical profile, expressing epithelial, muscular, and neural markers. Most tumours are positive for desmin, cytokeratins, and EMA, but they are consistently negative for myogenin and MYOD1. Desmin expression is cytoplasmic and distinctly dot-like. Antibodies that specifically target the C-terminus (not the N-terminus) can detect tumour cell nuclear WT1 expression {10843285; 10989634}. The stromal cells frequently express SMA.

Cytology

Cytological specimens are cellular. The tumour cells have a high N:C ratio, granular chromatin, and nuclear moulding. Pseudorosettes are common {11836699}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

DSRCT diagnosis is confirmed by detecting the EWSR1::WT1 fusion transcript. Fusion detection is especially useful for identifying DSRCTs disguised by atypical clinical or histological features {9738572}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primitive-appearing tumour cells that are immunopositive for cytokeratin and desmin and immunonegative for skeletal muscle–associated markers (e.g. myogenin and MYOD1).

Desirable: identification of a t(11;22)(p13;q12) translocation or an EWSR1::WT1 fusion transcript; tumour cell immunoreactivity for a C-terminal WT1-targeted antibody; desmoplastic stroma.

Staging

No widely accepted staging system has yet been adopted.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for patients with DSRCT remains extremely poor, even with aggressive multimodality treatment. The 5-year overall survival rate is about 25%, and few (if any) patients are cured {29871905; 15868593; 22162435}. New therapeutic opportunities may come from potentially targetable genes recently identified by transcriptome analysis, such as IGF2, FGFR4, CD200, and CD276 {32703985}.
