5.4.3.2A Aneurysmal bone cyst

Definition

Aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC) is a benign neoplasm of bone containing multiloculated blood-filled cystic spaces.

ICD-O coding

9260/0 Aneurysmal bone cyst

ICD-11 coding

FB80.6 & XH23E0 Aneurysmal bone cyst & Aneurysmal bone cyst

Related terminology

Not recommended: giant cell reparative granuloma of small bones; giant cell lesion of small bones.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ABC can affect any bone but usually arises in the metaphysis of long bones, especially the femur, tibia, and humerus, and the posterior elements of vertebral bodies. Rare tumours occur in the soft tissues {1626294; 11756770; 30603771; 32157177}.

Clinical features

The most common signs and symptoms are pain and swelling, which are rarely due to fracture. In the vertebrae, ABC can compress nerves or the spinal cord and cause neurological symptoms.

Imaging

On radiographs and CT, ABC appears as a lytic, expansile lesion with well-defined margins. Most tumours contain a thin shell of subperiosteal reactive bone. On MRI, characteristic fluid-fluid levels created by the different signal intensities of the cyst contents are present. On bone scan, ABCs show increased peripheral radiotracer uptake, with a central photopenic area.

Epidemiology

ABC affects patients of all ages, but 80% of cases occur during the first two decades of life. Both sexes are equally affected {2835141; 1591685}. The estimated incidence is 0.15 cases per 1 million person-years {10379320}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ABCs harbour gene rearrangements of the USP6 gene at chromosome band 17p13.2, in approximately 70% of cases. The most common translocation, t(16;17)(q22;p13) {10825009; 15026324; 10502326; 1777412; 11106078}, leads to the fusion of CDH11 with USP6, with resultant upregulation of USP6 transcription {15026324; 15735689}. The other fusion partners of USP6 include THRAP3 (TRAP150), CNBP (ZNF9), OMD, COL1A1, CTNNB1, STAT3, FOSL2, EIF1, SPARC, PAFAH1B1, USP9X, RUNX2, ASAP1, FAT1, SAR1A, and TNC {15735689; 28449806; 30553465; 27910166; 30767316; 32011035}. USP6 rearrangements also occur in solid ABCs of the small bones of the hands and feet, but not in the giant cell granulomas of the jaws {24742829}.

Macroscopic appearance

ABC is a well-defined, multiloculated lesion, composed of blood-filled cystic spaces separated by tan-white, gritty, sponge-like septa. More solid areas can be present. The peripheral soft tissue component is surrounded by a thin shell of reactive bone.

Histopathology

ABC contains blood-filled cystic spaces separated by fibrous septa. The fibrous septa are composed of a moderately dense cellular proliferation of bland fibroblasts, with scattered osteoclast-type giant cells and reactive woven bone rimmed by osteoblasts. The woven bone frequently follows the contours of the fibrous septa. Mitoses are present and can be numerous; however, atypical mitoses are absent. The solid subtype of ABC has similar components {8431852; 6850506}.

The differential diagnosis includes ABC-like areas consequent to haemorrhagic changes, associated with benign and (less frequently) malignant bone tumours (formerly termed “secondary aneurysmal bone cysts”) {7863874; 2835141; 1591685}. Telangiectatic osteosarcoma may mimic ABC at imaging and macroscopically, but microscopically, malignant cells within fibrous septa are seen.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

USP6 rearrangement in ABC can be detected by FISH. The percentage of lesional cells carrying the rearrangement can sometimes be low.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a multicystic lesion with fluid-fluid levels on imaging; histology showing a cyst wall composed of fibroblasts, osteoclast-like giant cells, haemosiderin, and new bone formation.

Desirable: demonstration of USP6 rearrangement.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

ABC may recur in 20–70% of cases. Spontaneous regression after incomplete removal is very unusual. The presence or absence of USP6 fusion does not affect the prognosis.
