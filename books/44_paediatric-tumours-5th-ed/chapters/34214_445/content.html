3.4.2.1.4 Inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevus

Definition

Inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevus (IJCN) is a distinctive compound naevus that appears in children and adolescents and has associated inflammation.

ICD-O coding

8770/0 Inflamed juvenile naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH8WP0 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevus

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflamed naevus of puberty and young adulthood.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

IJCNs are perilimbal and may also involve the limbus itself {11801498}.

Clinical features

IJCN is an elevated, well-circumscribed, usually amelanotic nodular lesion. IJCN may vary in the degree of pigmentation during growth, where the lesion may appear orange/red in parts, due to vascularization and increased pigmentation. The naevus usually shows cystic structures and is freely movable, except when it involves the limbus. IJCNs frequently cause undue concern because they can grow rapidly, appear vascularized, and can have congested feeding vessels {18985522; 18300811; 11801498; 33603334}.

Epidemiology

IJCN is seen in children and adolescents at a mean age of 13 years, and in most series there is a male predominance. Patients may have atopic dermatitis, asthma, bilateral papillary conjunctivitis, and vernal conjunctivitis {12036989; 18985522; 18300811; 11801498; 33603334; 21128776; 25171709}.

Etiology

The etiology of IJCN is unknown, but it is a compound naevus with associated eosinophilic and/or chronic inflammation. The cystic component may increase in size by the accumulation of mucin, and together with the inflammatory reaction this may induce the growth of the naevus at an early age. Alternatively, this process might constitute a direct immune response induced by the naevus itself {12036989; 25171709}.

Pathogenesis

IJCN arises from neural crest–derived melanocytes accompanied by an inflammatory infiltrate that expresses IL-17 and VEGF and is usually presumed to be of the allergic type {26615952; 12036989}.

Genetic profile

Naevi in patients aged < 18 years, with larger basal diameter, cysts, and amelanosis, show more NRAS p.Q61R mutations than BRAF p.V600E mutations (by immunohistochemistry) {29332123}. A BRAF c.1799T>A (p.V600E) mutation was identified in 1 of 4 IJCNs {16123397}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features (above).

Histopathology

IJCN shares features with conjunctival compound naevus, including a triangular or V-shaped architecture seen at low power, with the junctional component extending beyond the stromal component, and with cystic and solid epithelial rests in most cases. The cystic component often contains goblet cells, and PAS staining highlights this component {25171709}. Prominent lymphocytic inflammation at the base of the naevus is characteristic. Well-defined germinal centres may be present. Plasma cells (including a high proportion of IgG4-positive cells) and eosinophils are seen in about three quarters of IJCNs. Eosinophils usually percolate the superficial epithelium {25171709}. Periods of rapid growth result from inflammatory infiltration and cystic enlargement by mucin production and accumulation {25171709; 2657539; 22139382; 11801498}. IJCNs show apparent disordered growth, such as prominent confluent junctional nests and a lack of (or incomplete) maturation or paradoxical (reverse) maturation, in which the nuclear and overall size of the naevus cells is greater in the subepithelial component than in the junctional region. This may lead to misdiagnosis as a premalignant or malignant melanocytic lesion. The junctional component often extends horizontally beyond the subepithelial component {18985522; 18300811; 21128776}. Mitotic figures may be present but are rare, and the Ki-67 proliferation index is low. IJCN is positive for HMB45 antigen in the intraepithelial component, and in the superficial layer in the stromal component only. Immunostaining for CD45 (LCA), CD3, and CD20 reveals a mixed population of B cells and T cells {25171709; 18985522; 18300811}.

Differential diagnosis

The clinical appearance and rapid growth of IJCN often raise concern for conjunctival melanoma, which is very rare in children and adolescents. The histological patterns may also raise suspicion for melanoma; however, the young age of the patient, the architecture, the epithelial inclusions, the absence of ulceration, and the HMB45 immunohistochemistry staining pattern of IJCN are indicators of a benign lesion (see #23262Table 5.01). Amelanotic IJCN should also be differentiated from the salmon-patch appearance of conjunctival lymphoproliferative lesions, including conjunctival lymphoma {25171709; 2657539; 11801498; 21128776}. Plasma cells may be abundant and some are IgG4-positive, but they do not meet the full diagnostic criteria for IgG4-related disease {25171709}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: preserved naevus architecture (V-shaped) with a predominant intraepithelial/junctional component extending along epithelial inclusions in the stroma; a marked inflammatory component (lymphocytes, plasma cells, and eosinophils); absence of atypical mitosis, ulceration, or necrosis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

IJCN rarely recurs if excised, and it is rarely the source of conjunctival melanoma {18300811; 11801498}.
