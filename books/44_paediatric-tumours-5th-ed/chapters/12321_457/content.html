12.3.2.1 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

Definition

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) is a malignant salivary gland neoplasm characterized by mucous, intermediate, and epidermoid (squamoid) tumour cells forming cystic and solid growth patterns, usually associated with MAML2 rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

8430/3 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

8430/3 Oncocytic mucoepidermoid carcinoma

8430/3 Warthin-like mucoepidermoid carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2B67.0 & XH1J36 Adenocarcinoma of parotid gland & Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

2B68.0 & XH1J36 Adenocarcinoma of submandibular or sublingual glands & Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: mucoepidermoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

Oncocytic mucoepidermoid carcinoma; Warthin-like mucoepidermoid carcinoma; central intraosseous mucoepidermoid carcinoma

Localization

Tumours in paediatric patients most commonly affect the parotid gland {26614703; 28739497}, followed by the submandibular gland and the minor salivary glands in the oral cavity (especially the palate) {26614703; 28739497; 17263868}, with rare central (intraosseous) cases.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a solitary painless mass or mucosal ulceration, which is present for a mean duration of about 1 year {32565266; 10964053}. Some cystic masses may fluctuate, clinically mimicking a mucocoele.

Epidemiology

Although rare, MEC is the most common salivary gland malignancy in paediatric patients {28139061; 28739497; 26614703}. It most commonly affects adolescents (reported median ages: 12–15 years) {26221861; 28739497; 16783829}. There is no sex predilection {16783829; 21284057}.

Etiology

Treatment with chemotherapy and/or radiation (usually for leukaemia, lymphoma, or sarcoma) may place children at a higher risk of developing a MEC, from several years later (for chemoradiation) to decades later (for radiation alone) {21280198}, although tumour characteristics are similar to those of non–treatment-associated tumours. Increased occurrence of childhood MEC has been reported after nuclear disasters and chemotherapy {30772619}.

Pathogenesis

The majority of MECs are characterized by the t(11;19)(q21;p13) translocation, which results in the CRTC1::MAML2 fusion, followed in frequency by the t(11;15)(q21;q26) translocation, which results in the CRTC3::MAML2 fusion {12539049; 27299797; 18050304; 23035786; 23018873; 20588178}. There is no association with tumour grade and/or patient outcome and fusion status {31535311; 31442522; 28438292; 27340278; 26796488}. TP53 mutations are commonly identified, more often in intermediate- and high-grade tumours {27340278}.

Macroscopic appearance

MEC typically manifests as a well-defined or infiltrative cystic or multicystic white to tan mass, frequently with viscous content.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of epidermoid, intermediate, and mucinous cells in varying proportions. The mucinous cells contain intracytoplasmic mucin compressing a peripherally located nucleus. The epidermoid cells are polygonal, have abundant eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm, and (rarely) may show small foci of overt keratinization (extensive keratinization is not seen in MEC). The intermediate (in size) cells are round to oval and are larger than normal basal cells but smaller than epidermoid cells. Clear cell {26297211}, oncocytic {33002921}, Warthin-like {26457352; 28877061}, and ciliated {28877061} patterns/subtypes of MEC have been described.

Immunohistochemistry

Mucicarmine or PAS stains highlight mucous cells. Immunohistochemically, clusters of epidermoid cells are highlighted by p63, p40, and CK5/6, whereas CEA(m) and EMA (MUC1) highlight mucocytes {26230372}. S100 and SOX10 are negative.

Grading

Grading is defined by the growth pattern and cellular features {20588178; 11420454; 31021855}. Low-grade tumours are well circumscribed and cystic, whereas intermediate-grade tumours are well circumscribed and mixed cystic and solid. High-grade tumours are typically infiltrative and solid. Scant mucous cells, increased mitosis, and profound nuclear anaplasia are characteristic of high-grade tumours.

Differential diagnosis

Pleomorphic adenoma with mucinous metaplasia, necrotizing sialometaplasia, lymphoepithelial cysts, mucocoele, Warthin tumour, and clear cell carcinoma can mimic MEC {32729300; 27769871}.

Cytology

The cellularity of aspirates varies with tumour grade. Low-grade tumours (common in paediatric patients) are hypocellular, with an abundant mucinous background, macrophages, and debris, admixed with scattered sheets of epidermoid and mucous cells {32526741}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The presence of MAML2 rearrangements (and rarely other gene fusions) is an important diagnostic marker {27299797}; their absence raises the possibility of other lesions in the differential diagnosis (mucocoele, chronic sialadenitis, lymphoepithelial cyst, sclerosing polycystic adenoma {27299797; 21243374; 16434888; 31605313}).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mixture of mucous, intermediate, and epidermoid cells.

Desirable: MAML2 rearrangement (in selected cases).

Staging

MECs are staged using the TNM system as published by the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC).

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis in paediatric patients is excellent {16618905; 10964053; 16783829; 28739497}, with 5- and 10-year overall survival rates of 98% and 94%, respectively {21284057}. Nearly all patients who develop distant metastasis have high-grade tumours {10964053; 16783829; 21284057; 28739497}.
