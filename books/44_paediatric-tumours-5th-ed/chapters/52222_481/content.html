5.2.2.2.2 Inclusion body infantile digital fibromatosis

Definition

Inclusion body fibromatosis is a benign myofibroblastic tumour with recurrent potential, characterized by intracytoplasmic eosinophilic inclusions.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: infantile digital fibroma/fibromatosis; recurring digital fibrous tumour(s) of childhood; recurring digital fibroma(s) of childhood/infancy.

Subtype(s)

Classic (digital) inclusion body fibromatosis; non-classic (extradigital) inclusion body fibromatosis

Localization

Classically, these tumours arise from the dorsal/dorsolateral distal to mid portion of the fingers and toes, although they rarely involve the thumb, great toe, hand, or foot; extra-digital locations (extremities, tongue, breast) are rare {18830128; 840560; 3892466}.

Clinical features

The typical presentation is a painless, dome-shaped to polypoid cutaneous lesion that can occur multiply (multinodular or metachronous on ipsilateral digits) {4344937; 840560; 3835952; 18830128}.

Epidemiology

Tumours arise equally in boys and girls aged < 5 years; most occur during the first year of life and about 30% are congenital, although adult tumours are rare {18830128; 3892466; 840560; 3835952}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are small (< 20 mm), nodular, firm, and rubbery, with a fibrous, off-white to grey cut surface {840560; 3835952; 18830128}.

Histopathology

These are dermal-based lesions composed of spindled myofibroblastic cells arranged in short to medium-length fascicles, sometimes with a whorled or storiform arrangement. The fascicles are often arranged perpendicularly to the epidermis, surrounding skin adnexal structures, with variable collagen content {4344937; 3892466}. The nuclei are cytologically bland and there are typically intracytoplasmic pale-pink to red inclusions that may be paranuclear and can indent the nucleus {14322942; 3892466}. Trichrome and Movat’s pentachrome stains can help highlight these inclusions, staining them red and pink, respectively {18830128}. The inclusions are composed of actin-rich myofilaments that stain for SMA, calponin, and sometimes caldesmon {18830128; 3811906; 6253042; 1310240; 24117680}. Multinucleated giant cells can occasionally be seen in superficial dermis {1946077}.

Cytology

Plump spindled to polygonal cells and a collagenous matrix are seen, occasionally with scattered intracytoplasmic inclusions {21986179}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a bland, dermal-based, myofibroblastic spindle cell tumour with intracytoplasmic pink-red inclusions.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours have a high risk of recurrence (as high as 75%) but are cured with complete surgical excision {215963; 3892466; 18830128; 8583050; 9493986; 17273037; 21276743}. Spontaneous regression has also been described {17542881}.
