5.2.2.5 EWSR1::SMAD3-rearranged fibroblastic tumour

Definition

EWSR1::SMAD3-rearranged fibroblastic tumour is a benign, locally recurring fibroblastic tumour with a characteristic clinical, morphological, immunohistochemical, and genetic profile.

ICD-O coding

8810/0 EWSR1::SMAD3-rearranged fibroblastic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: acral fibroblastic spindle cell neoplasm.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

EWSR1::SMAD3-rearranged fibroblastic tumours occur in the dermis and subcutis of the distal extremities, mainly in acral sites {29309308; 29957732}. One bone tumour, located in the tibia, has been reported so far {32710151}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a superficially located, often slow-growing lesion at the distal extremities, mainly in acral sites {29309308; 29957732}. The tumour in the tibia caused pain and diffuse swelling {32710151}.

Epidemiology

There is a strong female preponderance. Patient ages range from 1 to 70 years, with two lesions described in children so far {29309308; 29957732}. The neoplasm of the tibia was in an adult {32710151}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Tumorigenesis is driven by EWSR1::SMAD3 fusion, resulting from a t(15;22)(q22.33;q12.2) translocation. SMAD3, a transcriptional regulator, is involved in the TGF-β/SMAD signalling pathway for fibroblasts producing extracellular matrix proteins. FN1 mRNA upregulation is similar to that in other fibroblastic tumours. ERG overexpression appears to be unique to this tumour type and is not seen in other paediatric fibroblastic lesions {29309308; 29957732}.

Macroscopic appearance

EWSR1::SMAD3-rearranged fibroblastic tumours are relatively circumscribed nodular or multinodular lesions with a white-grey cut surface {29957732}.

Histopathology

Lesions are located in the dermis and subcutis. They show a nodular or multinodular growth pattern with infiltrative margins. There is commonly a biphasic appearance, with high cellularity at the periphery and variable central hyalinization. The spindle cells are arranged in intersecting fascicles. Nuclei are monomorphic and elongated. The cytoplasm is inconspicuous. Calcification may occur in the hyalinized areas, possibly as a sign of a longstanding lesion. ERG expression is a consistent immunohistochemical finding, unlike in other fibroblastic lesions, and other markers are negative (CD34, SMA, desmin, caldesmon, S100, SOX10, MUC4, β-catenin) {29309308; 29957732}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of a fusion of EWSR1 (exon 7) with SMAD3 (exon 5), usually by RT-PCR, next-generation sequencing techniques, or FISH (the latter commonly for EWSR1), is required for diagnosis {29309308; 29957732}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a superficially located infiltrating tumour, commonly in the distal extremities / acral sites, consisting of monomorphic spindle cells with central hyalinization; nuclear ERG expression; detection of an EWSR1::SMAD3 fusion gene.

Staging

Staging is not clinically relevant as far as we know, because this lesion is at worst locally aggressive {29309308; 29957732}.

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence has been described in 60% of the soft tissue cases and occurs after incomplete excision. The prognosis is excellent {29309308; 29957732; 32710151}.
