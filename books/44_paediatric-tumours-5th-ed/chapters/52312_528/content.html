5.2.3.12 Venous malformations

Definition

Venous malformations are malformed and/or excessive venous channels of any calibre, resulting from an error in vasculogenesis.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA90.2Z Peripheral venous malformation, unspecified

Related terminology

Not recommended: for common venous malformation: venous haemangioma; venous angioma; cavernous haemangioma; for verrucous venous malformation: verrucous haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

Common venous malformation (CVM); blue rubber bleb naevus syndrome (BRBNS); glomuvenous malformation (GVM); verrucous venous malformation (VVM); genuine diffuse phlebectasia of Bockenheimer (GDPB)

Localization

CVM can occur in any tissue plane and can be focal, multifocal, or diffuse, sometimes involving an entire limb (GDPB). BRBNS has a predilection for the palms, soles, and trunk; GVM, for the torso and extremities; VVM, for the lower extremities; and GDPB, for the upper extremities.

Clinical features

CVM is a soft, compressible mass, frequently with a cutaneous bluish blush, involving the subcutaneous fat and (often) the dermis and underlying soft tissue. BRBNS manifests as multiple raised blue papules that increase in number over time; rarely, a congenital large mass may additionally be present. GVM is single or multiple, nodular or plaque-like, and pink to bluish-purple. VVM commonly occurs in the lower extremities as single or multiple deep red macular stains that become progressively hyperkeratotic. In GDPB, there is marked ectasia of superficial and deep veins, including extensive involvement of musculature and sometimes bone {26055853; 27607317}.

Epidemiology

All entities affect both sexes, and the lesion may be evident at birth. Most are sporadic.

Etiology

Most CVMs are sporadic, with a mutation in TEK (TIE2) or PIK3CA {29217067}. BRBNS is marked by dual (cis) TEK mutations within the C-terminal domain {27519652}. VVM is associated with MAP3K3 (ERK) mutations causing upregulation of RAS/MAPK signalling {25728774}.

Pathogenesis

Mutations in TEK (TIE2) or PIK3CA cause upregulation of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling, which is essential for angiogenesis and vasculogenesis {27519652; 29217067}. MAP3K3 (ERK) mutations cause upregulation of RAS/MAPK signalling {25728774}.

Macroscopic appearance

The sectioned appearance of all types, except for GDPB, is most often unencapsulated, red, and spongiform with intermingled native tissue.

Histopathology

In CVM, abnormal venous channels vary in number, calibre, shape, and muscularization; they are often anastomotic, and they contain organizing thrombi. BRBNS typically has closely apposed, small, abnormal and often spongiform veins and organizing thrombi. VVM has dispersed or clustered channels with the morphology of large capillaries or venules involving the dermis and hypodermis; rarely, it is predominantly or wholly restricted to the hypodermis. In VVM the endothelium is focally or diffusely immunopositive for GLUT1 and focally immunopositive for D2-40 and PROX1 {26055853; 27607322}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In certain cases, demonstration of mutations in TEK, PIK3CA, or MAP2K1 may be useful for confirming the type of malformation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for CVM: dysmorphic venous channels; for BRBNS: dysmorphic venous channels; for VVM: predominant capillary-type channels with focal or diffuse endothelial immunopositivity for GLUT1 and focal immunopositivity for GLUT1 and D2-40.

Desirable: for CVM: somatic TEK or PIK3CA mutation; for BRBNS: somatic TEK mutation; for VVM: somatic MAP2K1 mutation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Sclerotherapy and/or operative excision are usually applied in CVM. BRBNS and VVM lesions may require excision. A response to sirolimus has been reported in BRBNS {31879989}.
