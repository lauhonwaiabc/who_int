5.2.6.3C Deep angiomyxoma

Definition

Deep angiomyxoma is a bland-looking myxoid tumour occurring in the deep soft tissues, typically of the lower genital tract.

ICD-O coding

8841/0 Deep angiomyxoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH4V74 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Aggressive angiomyxoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: myxoma; aggressive angiomyxoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Deep angiomyxoma usually occurs in the vulva {8159617; 26881549; 27984053} and spermatic cord {11261819; 12548375; 15776247}, whereas the penis and greater omentum are rarely affected {28161377; 25022396}.

Clinical features

Vulvar tumours may mimic a labial cyst or clitoromegaly {8159617; 26881549; 27984053}. In male patients, enlargement of the spermatic cord or a scrotal protruding mass, mimicking a hernia or a hydrocoele, is seen {11261819; 12548375; 15776247}.

Epidemiology

Deep angiomyxoma is rare in children and both sexes are affected, with a wide age range (from infancy to adolescence) {28161377; 15776247}.

Etiology

In adults, various rearrangements involving HMGA2 (12q13-q15) have been reported {17654722; 20551826}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Deep angiomyxoma appears as a myxoid mass with, at least focally, incompletely circumscribed margins and/or a cerebriform surface {11261819; 12548375}. Necrosis and/or haemorrhage are absent. Tumours vary in size from a few centimetres to as large as 170 mm {12548375}.

Histopathology

Deep angiomyxoma is composed of bland-looking, short, spindled and stellate cells set in an abundant myxoid stroma with numerous capillary-like and medium-sized to large blood vessels. Perivascular hyalinization, hypercellular areas, and/or fibrous areas are often seen. Bundles of mature smooth muscle cells, sometimes radiating from the wall of the larger vessels, are variably interspersed in the stroma. Infiltration of the surrounding tissues is common. Immunohistochemically the neoplastic cells often express desmin and variably express CD34 and SMA [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.fr/588.]].

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a bland-looking myxoid tumour with numerous thin- to thick-walled blood vessels.

Desirable: bundles of smooth muscle cells variably interspersed in the stroma; diffuse desmin immunoreactivity.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

No local recurrence has been reported in children after surgical excision.
