2.3.2.9 Mediastinal grey zone lymphoma

Definition

Mediastinal grey zone lymphoma (MGZL) is a B-cell lymphoma with clinical, morphological, immunophenotypic, and molecular features that overlap with those of classic Hodgkin lymphoma (CHL) – especially nodular sclerosis CHL (NSCHL) – and primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL).

ICD-O coding

9596/3 Mediastinal grey zone lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A86.2 Mediastinal grey-zone lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: Hodgkin-like anaplastic large cell lymphoma; B-cell lymphoma, unclassifiable, with features intermediate between diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and classic Hodgkin lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common presentation is with a large mediastinal mass, with or without involvement of adjacent nodes. Primary extramediastinal (nodal or extranodal) grey zone lymphomas have also been reported but remain controversial, and this diagnosis should only be considered in (clinical) research settings.

Clinical features

Patients have symptoms related to a large mediastinal mass {32037692}.

Epidemiology

MGZL is extremely rare in children and adolescents, representing < 4% of all mature B-cell lymphomas in the paediatric age group {20971819; 32037692}. In adults this lymphoma usually arises in young patients (age range: 20–40 years). There is a male predominance, which appears to be present also in paediatric cases.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown, but gene expression profiling and genetic studies in adults have shown close links to PMBCL and NSCHL {32516416; 32961552}. MGZL is infrequently positive for EBV, and EBV positivity should prompt the suspicion of EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) {29296913}.

Pathogenesis

Clonal IG rearrangement is often positive. The tumour cells show many of the genetic aberrations reported in PMBCL and NSCHL {21700770}. In particular, gains at 2p16.1 (REL locus) and alterations at 9p24.1 involving the JAK2/PDCD1L2 (PDL2) locus are common {21822207}. Increased expression of PDL1 (CD274) may occur as a result. Some genetic and epigenetic differences from both CHL and PMBCL have been reported {21454882; 16874743}. More recent gene expression profiling and mutation analysis studies {32516416; 32961552} have shown that EBV-negative MGZL shares genetic features with NSCHL and PMBCL, whereas grey zone lymphoma with primary extramediastinal presentation has markedly different genetic aspects and partly overlaps with DLBCL. EBV-positive MGZL cases also differ significantly from EBV-negative cases and from tumours without thymic niche involvement.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour has a tan, fish-flesh appearance, often with extensive areas of necrosis. Areas of fibrosis may also be present.

Histopathology

The spectrum of MGZL ranges from cases with a morphology more closely related to CHL but with a strong B-cell phenotype to cases in which the morphology is more closely related to PMBCL but with a CHL-like immunophenotype (also encompassing cases with intermediate morphology and phenotype) {30540571}.

The tumour is composed of pleomorphic cells with confluent, sheet-like growth; the cells may resemble lacunar and Hodgkin cells. The cells are larger and more pleomorphic than in typical PMBCL, although some centroblast-like cells may be present. Focal or diffuse fibrotic stroma may be present, but well-defined fibrous bands are usually absent and their presence suggests a diagnosis of CHL {29296913}. A characteristic feature is the broad spectrum of cytological appearances, often varying from area to area within the same tumour. The background inflammatory infiltrate is less evident than in CHL, although scattered eosinophils, lymphocytes, and histiocytes may be present.

Composite lymphomas, with synchronous or metachronous PMBCL and CHL, should not be diagnosed as grey zone lymphomas.

Immunophenotype

The diagnosis of this lymphoma is frequently challenging because of the variable cytomorphological and immunophenotypic features {31352983; 26447896; 27758822}.

The B-cell programme is often preserved, with strong expression of one or more B-cell surface markers (CD19, CD20, CD79a) and B-cell transcription factors (PAX5, OCT2, BOB1); however, there is a lack of agreement on the precise degree of preservation of the B-cell programme required in individual cases.

Usually, MGZLs with CHL morphology are characterized by CD30 positivity with varying intensity and should have uniform, strong expression of CD20 and PAX5 associated with strong immunoreactivity for at least one additional B-cell marker (CD19, CD79a, BOB1, OCT2).

MGZLs with PMBCL morphology show strong and uniform positive expression of CD30 and partial or complete loss of B-cell markers or strong CD15 expression.

Other frequently expressed markers include CD23 and IRF4 (MUM1). Surface and cytoplasmic immunoglobulins are absent. BCL6 is variable, and CD10 and ALK are negative. The background lymphocytes are predominantly CD3+/CD4+, as seen in CHL.

EBV is usually negative: the diagnosis of EBV+ grey zone lymphoma is controversial, and (especially in extrathymic sites) most cases are better classified as EBV+ DLBCL {29296913}, whereas rare EBV+ MGZL may indeed exist {32516416; 32961552}.

Differential diagnosis

The diagnosis of MGZL should only be considered if the criteria for the diagnosis of other entities (especially CHL, PMBCL, and EBV+ DLBCL, but also B-cell lymphoproliferative disorders and T-cell lymphoma with HRS-like cells) are not met {29296913; 25999451; 23598959}.

Cytology

Cytological features are not reliable for distinguishing this tumour from other mediastinal lymphomas.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

MGZLs are B-cell lymphomas, and IG rearrangements can usually be demonstrated. Other mutation and gene expression features overlap with those of NSCHL and/or PMBCL and are currently not required for the diagnosis but can be useful for research purposes.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential for MGZL with CHL-like morphology:

  • Confluent growth of pleomorphic cells within a variably abundant microenvironment and dense fibrotic stroma
  • Strong uniform expression of CD20 and PAX5
  • Strong uniform expression of at least one additional B-cell marker (CD19, CD79a, BOB1, OCT2)
  • Positive expression of CD30, with variable intensity

Essential for MGZL with PMBCL-like morphology:

  • Monomorphic sheets of medium-sized to large neoplastic cells within a variably dense fibrotic stroma
  • Strong uniform expression of CD30 and partial or complete loss of B-cell markers, or strong CD15 expression

Desirable:

  • Because the complex histological features are often not reliably identifiable in core needle biopsies, a larger biopsy is required for the diagnosis
  • Absence of EBV

Staging

Staging is based on the St. Jude classification {7414342}, which has been recently updated {25940716}.

Prognosis and prediction

MGZLs in adults tend to have more aggressive behaviour than PMBCL. The outcome in children is not firmly established because of the rarity of these cases, but it is probably also an aggressive disease in this age group {32037692}.
