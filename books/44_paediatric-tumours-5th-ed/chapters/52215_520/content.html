5.2.2.15 Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva

Definition

Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP) is a heritable condition of progressive soft tissue metamorphosis to bone, resulting in immobility and premature death.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

135100 Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva; FOP

ICD-11 coding

FB31.1 Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva

Related terminology

Not recommended: myositis ossificans progressiva.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

FOP typically initially affects the dorsal axial and proximal regions, with later involvement of appendicular and caudal regions {31053156}.

Clinical features

FOP is usually associated with congenital malformations of the great toes (bilateral hallux valgus, malformed first metatarsal bone) and cervical–vertebral fusions.

Episodes of painful soft tissue swelling occur during the first decade of life, accompanied by relentless and progressive ossification of soft tissues. Traumas can accelerate the process, so the suspicion of FOP is a contraindication to biopsy. Progressive ossification of the chest wall causes respiratory insufficiency and death {31053156}.

Epidemiology

FOP affects 1 in 2 million people, with no racial or geographical preponderance {32988985}.

Etiology

FOP is associated with a germline activating mutation of the ACVR1 gene {32730934}.

Pathogenesis

Most cases result from a de novo germline activating mutation of ACVR1, which encodes a BMP receptor {32730934; 16642017; 16831905; 32988985}.

Macroscopic appearance

Soft tissue masses precede heterotopic ossification.

Histopathology

Early lesions of FOP are nonspecific vascular loose fibrous tissue proliferations with variable inflammation, which over time become more collagenized {11521229}. As the lesions mature, cartilage appears, followed by endochondral ossification. FOP cannot be distinguished histologically from localized myositis ossificans.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing is necessary to demonstrate a germline mutation of ACVR1 {32730934}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: congenital great toe malformations and rapidly progressive soft tissue masses preceding heterotopic ossification; demonstration of an ACVR1 mutation.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

FOP is progressively disabling, leading to wheelchair use within the second decade of life. The median survival time is 56 years {20194327}.
