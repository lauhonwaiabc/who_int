5.4.2.4 Chondroblastoma

Definition

Chondroblastoma is a benign tumour of bone that typically affects epiphyseal or apophyseal regions, composed of chondroblastic cells and islands of eosinophilic chondroid matrix.

ICD-O coding

9230/0 Chondroblastoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E82.Z & XH4NK2 Benign chondrogenic tumours, site unspecified & Chondroblastoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately 75% of chondroblastomas involve the epiphyseal (subchondral) region of long bones. The most common sites are the proximal and distal femur, proximal tibia, and proximal humerus {2793161; 4023729; 19723993}. Other sites include the talus, calcaneus, patella, and pelvic bones. Less frequently affected are the ribs, vertebrae, other small bones of the hands and feet, and craniofacial bones {5051664; 2793161}.

Clinical features

The most common symptom is pain, which may be longstanding. Local tenderness, joint effusion, impaired movement, and muscle atrophy may be found as presenting symptoms {2793161; 4023729; 19723993}.

Imaging

Radiologically, a well-defined intramedullary central or eccentric lucent lesion is seen in an epiphyseal site or in an epiphyseal equivalent (apophysis, sesamoid). There is usually a thin sclerotic margin and matrix mineralization. Larger lesions can cross the growth plate and involve the metaphysis {4712761}. Trabeculation, cortical erosion, and expansion may be seen, the last of which is more common in flat bones {5051664}. A periosteal reaction, when present, is usually metaphyseal, adjacent to a long bone tumour, and a neocortex can form around expansile lesions {5051664}. The lobular morphology, sclerotic margin, and mineralization are more clearly seen using CT. MRI shows a heterogeneous lesion, which frequently shows intermediate signal intensity on fluid-sensitive sequences and may contain fluid-fluid levels {19936740}. Prominent surrounding reactive change is usually seen, including marrow oedema-like hyperintensity on MRI and synovitis or effusion in the adjacent joint.

Epidemiology

Chondroblastomas account for < 1% of all bone tumours. Most occur in the immature skeleton, in patients aged 10–25 years, although they may arise at any age. In the paediatric population, the mean age at presentation is approximately 12 years {19723993}. There is a male predominance (M:F ratio: 2:1).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

More than 96% of chondroblastomas are associated with histone H3.3 alterations. Owing to early cleavage of the N-terminal methionine in most histones, the description of histone sequence alterations follows its own nomenclature and differs from Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS)-compliant numbering by omitting this first amino acid {33779999}. The characteristic c.110A>T p.K37M (K36M) substitution is more frequent in the H3-3B (H3F3B) gene on chromosome 17 {24162739; 27499898}, with the mutation occurring in the H3-3A gene in fewer cases. Chondroblastomas share with giant cell tumour of bone the predilection for a subarticular/epiphyseal location and histone H3.3 alterations. Specific substitutions are associated with different tumour types – p.K37M (K36M) with chondroblastoma and p.G35W (G34W) with giant cell tumours of bone – raising the possibility that specific substitutions provide a growth advantage to specific cell types {24162739; 30582658; 28505000}. The p.K37M (K36M) substitution of H3.3 inhibits the H3 p.K36 methyltransferases NSD2 (MMSET) and SETD2, which results in reduced global H3 p.K36me {27229140; 27474439}. These epigenetic pathway alterations probably block mesenchymal differentiation and influence tumorigenesis in chondroblastoma {30098026; 25453099; 30101054}.

Macroscopic appearance

Chondroblastomas are usually well-defined eccentric lesions surrounded by a thin rim of sclerosis. The features vary from soft to rubbery with gritty and haemorrhagic areas. Blood-filled spaces may be seen in some cases, representing secondary cyst-like change {5051664; 5445403}.

Histopathology

Histologically, chondroblastomas are composed of sheets of ovoid to polygonal cells with distinct cytoplasmic borders, grooved nuclei, and eosinophilic cytoplasm {14880556; 5051664}. There are interspersed osteoclast-like giant cells and islands of eosinophilic chondroid matrix. Basophilic hyaline cartilage is infrequent. Pericellular lace-like or chicken-wire calcification is a common finding in non-decalcified specimens. Aneurysmal bone cyst–like changes are seen in as many as 30% of cases {19723993}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry using an antibody against H3.3 K36M (H3-3A:c.110A>T p.K37M [K36M]) shows diffuse nuclear expression in > 96% of cases and is helpful in small biopsies or in cases with extensive aneurysmal bone cyst–like change {26844533; 26457357}. SOX9, S100, cytokeratin, and DOG1, although not specific, may be positive {19801163; 2579018; 1692005; 17727486}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes aneurysmal bone cyst, giant cell tumour of bone, clear cell chondrosarcoma, chondromyxoid fibroma, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, and an extremely rare subtype of osteosarcoma: chondroblastoma-like osteosarcoma {10192265}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The vast majority of chondroblastomas harbour a c.110A>T p.K37M (K36M) substitution on one of the genes that encode histone H3.3, including H3-3B on chromosome 17 (in > 95% of cases) or, less frequently, H3-3A on chromosome 1 {24162739; 30582658; 26844533}.

IDH1 and IDH2 substitutions are not seen in chondroblastomas {22074484; 21598255}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sheets of chondroblastic cells; islands of eosinophilic chondroid matrix; osteoclast-like giant cells.

Desirable: imaging features compatible with an epiphyseal/apophyseal lesion; H3.3 p.K37M (K36M) immunoreactivity (for confirmation of cases with unusual features).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Chondroblastomas are benign tumours that in > 80% of cases are successfully treated with surgical curettage with or without adjuvant treatment of the surgical bed {30418383; 15972914}. Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) may be used {19304917}. Recurrence rates vary with the site of disease, being lower for cases in long bones and higher for those in flat and craniofacial bones (range: 10–18%) {30418383; 15972914; 3604981; 8253461}. There are no reliable histological parameters predicting local recurrence {8253461; 12913842; 28971570}. In exceptional cases, benign lung metastasis may develop {16007463}.
