5.2.9.9 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a spindle cell sarcoma arising from a peripheral nerve or from a pre-existing benign nerve sheath tumour, or in patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1). Outside these settings, the diagnosis is based on morphological, immunophenotypic, or molecular features suggesting Schwannian differentiation.

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant schwannoma; neurofibrosarcoma; neurogenic sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, epithelioid

Localization

Most frequently affected are the trunk and extremities, because MPNSTs tend to arise within large spinal nerves, particularly the lumbosacral and brachial plexuses; less commonly, MPNSTs affect the head and neck region {16293873; 12632346; 31889416}.

Clinical features

Like in adults, MPNSTs in children occur as enlarging soft tissue masses, often causing pain, dysaesthesia, or other neurological symptoms {1530109; 16293873; 31889416}. Most tumours are large (> 50 mm) and often extend into adjacent structures {31241238}. MRI features that help distinguish MPNST from benign nerve sheath tumours include peripheral enhancement, peritumoural oedema, intratumoural heterogeneity or lobulation, and infiltrative margins, but their sensitivity is about 60% {27276118}. FDG PET is a useful high-sensitivity modality for differentiating benign peripheral nerve sheath tumour from MPNST {22645095; 29220302}, but false negative and positive results have been reported {22407214; 23242059}.

Epidemiology

In children, MPNSTs are very rare, representing only 8.6% of all MPNSTs (including those in adults) in the SEER Program database {31386019}. They typically affect teenagers {16293873; 21145727; 31241238}. The incidence of NF1 tends to be higher in children with MPNST than in adults with MPNST {31889416}.

Etiology

MPNSTs are associated with NF1 and irradiation. Isolated epithelioid MPNSTs have been reported in patients with NF1 {25602794}.

Pathogenesis

Although biallelic inactivation of NF1 is present at high frequency both in conventional MPNSTs and in their benign precursors, inactivating mutations of CDKN2A and/or CDKN2B and (at a lower frequency) of TP53 preferentially characterize MPNST and atypical neurofibromas {25240281; 31023785; 32666581; 30722027}. Further, loss-of-function mutations of genes encoding the core components of PRC2, either EED or SUZ12, are found in the majority of conventional MPNSTs but not in their precursors; there is a higher frequency of PRC2 inactivation in sporadic and irradiation-associated MPNSTs than in NF1-associated MPNSTs {25240281; 25305755; 25119042; 30722027}. PRC2 usually establishes and maintains H3 p.K27me2 and H3 p.K27me3 in the genome, regulates chromatin structure, and represses the transcription of target genes; when PRC2 is inactivated, loss of its products H3 p.K27me2 and H3 p.K27me3 reprogrammes the chromatin landscape and allows for aberrant redistribution of H3 p.K27ac, which in turn promotes the aberrant transcriptional activation of target genes {25240281; 26857854; 31023785; 32666581}. These genes are involved in developmental processes, and their expression may account for the not uncommon occurrence of heterologous elements in MPNST {25240281}.

Epithelioid MPNSTs, distinct from conventional MPNSTs, are driven by SMARCB1 gene inactivation in about 80% of cases. Identical SMARCB1 alterations are also found in approximately 40% of epithelioid schwannomas, which are considered the putative precursors to epithelioid MPNSTs {30864974}.

Macroscopic appearance

MPNSTs are large fusiform or oval masses, often showing a defined pseudocapsule; they are generally > 50 mm in size, although tumours as small as 10 mm have been reported in children {1530109; 12632346}. The cut surface is fleshy and whitish to grey, with frequent areas of necrosis and haemorrhage.

Histopathology

The majority of MPNSTs consist of fascicles of spindle cells with pale cytoplasm and tapered, wavy, hyperchromatic nuclei. Densely cellular areas alternate with myxoid hypocellular areas, creating a distinctive marbled pattern. Perivascular cell condensation forming cuffs, sometimes with endoluminal protrusions, is characteristic. Geographical necrosis (often with a perivascular-sparing pattern) and numerous mitoses are present. A haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern is often seen. A herringbone or storiform growth pattern are sometimes encountered and may be reminiscent of other spindle cell sarcomas. Extensive pleomorphism is not a typical feature, but it may occur. A residual nerve trunk and the association with a neurofibroma, especially plexiform neurofibroma, are important diagnostic clues. In MPNST arising in the setting of NF1, the distinction between low-grade MPNST, atypical neurofibroma, and atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential may be challenging. Diagnostic criteria have been proposed to help in these differential diagnoses {28551330}. Heterologous elements from divergent mesenchymal or epithelial differentiation are rarely observed in paediatric MPNST; when they are present, it is typically in the context of NF1-associated MPNST {6432304; 6235165; 8202648; 15735851}. Rhabdomyosarcomatous MPNST, also known as malignant triton tumours, features scattered rhabdomyoblasts. At the extreme end of this spectrum are rare sarcomas showing an exclusively rhabdomyosarcomatous morphology, absence of H3 p.K27me3 immunostaining, and MPNST molecular alterations (e.g. mutation in NF1, CDKN2A, or SUZ12) {31107719}. MPNSTs with epithelial differentiation display benign glands with or without mucin secretion {8078812; 8252477}.

About 15% of epithelioid MPNSTs occur in children {1746681; 26096054}. Compared with conventional MPNST, a superficial location is more frequent. Epithelioid MPNSTs may occasionally arise in a pre-existing neurofibroma or schwannoma (conventional or epithelioid) {25602794}. Epithelioid MPNSTs are frequently multinodular, with plump epithelioid cells showing abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli, within a variable myxoid or hyalinized stroma.

Immunostaining for S100 is patchy or focal in about 50% of conventional MPNSTs, with higher expression in low-grade tumours {30137667}. SOX10 is expressed in 70–84% of MPNSTs {23929265; 30137667}. Complete loss of H3 p.K27me3 immunostaining is found in about 40% of tumours {26990975; 32666581} – more frequently in tumours with classic morphology (~75%), and less frequently in low-grade or rhabdomyosarcomatous MPNSTs and in paraspinal locations {26857854; 32666581}. Myogenic markers, including desmin, MSA, MYOD1, and myogenin, can be used to verify the presence of rhabdomyoblasts. Melanocytic markers, i.e. melan-A and tyrosinase, may be rarely expressed (14% and 6% respectively), representing a pitfall for misdiagnosis as spindle cell melanoma {30137667}. Epithelioid MPNST displays strong and diffuse staining for S100 and an absence of melanocytic markers; immunostaining of H3 p.K27me3 is retained, whereas SMARCB1 is lost in as many as 70% of cases {25602794}. Loss of SMARCB1 is also observed in a subgroup of epithelioid schwannoma and cannot be used to differentiate these two entities {26752543; 28368924}.

Like in adults, synovial sarcoma (the most frequent paediatric sarcoma other than rhabdomyosarcoma) is the main differential diagnosis for MPNST in children, and it may rarely arise within a peripheral nerve {14685256; 23929265}. For low-grade MPNST, kinase-rearranged mesenchymal tumours should be considered mainly in lesions with patchy S100 expression in the absence of SOX10 expression {30276917; 30585824}. For MPNST with heterologous elements, rhabdomyosarcoma (which may focally express S100), immature teratoma, and DICER1 malignant teratoid tumour {31296931} enter into the differential. For epithelioid MPNSTs, rhabdoid tumours and proximal-type epithelioid sarcomas should be considered {16353216}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

It is important to exclude the entities that enter into the differential and have recurrent molecular alterations (e.g. SS18 [SYT] rearrangement, kinase fusions). Identification of MPNST molecular alterations (e.g. NF1 inactivating mutations) in the appropriate clinicopathological context may improve diagnostic accuracy.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sarcoma arising from a peripheral nerve or in the context of a pre-existing neurofibroma or in a patient with NF1; a sarcoma consisting of fascicles of monomorphic spindle cells with alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas, geographical tumour necrosis, and numerous mitoses; a spindle cell sarcoma with heterologous elements; a spindle cell sarcoma with nerve sheath differentiation demonstrated by immunohistochemistry (SOX10, S100) or loss of H3 p.K27me3 or with MPNST molecular signature; for epithelioid MPNST: a tumour consisting of epithelioid cells with prominent nucleoli showing diffuse expression of S100, absence of melanocytic markers, and SMARCB1 loss.

Desirable: for epithelioid MPNST: an associated schwannoma.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Paediatric MPNSTs are aggressive sarcomas with 5-year event-free and overall survival rates of 37% and 51%, respectively {16293873}. Localized disease tends to have a better prognosis (event-free survival: 53%; overall survival: 62%) {31241238}. MPNSTs recur locally in > 50% of patients; less frequently, they metastasize to the lungs, brain, liver, bone, or soft tissue within 16 months of diagnosis. Intergroup Rhabdomyosarcoma Study IRS-III stage (residual macroscopic disease) and NF1 are associated with worse event-free survival {31241238}. Large size, a non-extremity site, and NF1 are associated with a worse overall survival {16293873; 1530109; 31241238; 31889416}. The prognostic role of histological grade is controversial.

Proposed definition

NF

A benign Schwann cell neoplasm with thin and often wavy nuclei, wispy cell processes, and a myxoid to collagenous (shredded-carrots) matrix; immunohistochemistry includes extensive but not diffuse S100 and SOX10 positivity and a lattice-like CD34+ fibroblastic network

Plexiform NF

Cellular NF

ANNUBP

A Schwann cell neoplasm with at least 2 of the following 4 features: cytological atypia, loss of neurofibroma architecture, hypercellularity, < 1.5 mitoses/mm2 (< 3 mitoses/10 HPFa)

MPNST, low-grade

A tumour with features of ANNUBP but with mitotic counts of 1.5–4.5 mitoses/mm2 (3–9 mitoses/10 HPFa) and no necrosis

MPNST, high-grade

MPNST with ≥ 5 mitoses/mm2 (≥ 10 mitoses/10 HPFa) or 1.5–4.5 mitoses/mm2 (3–9 mitoses/10 HPFa) combined with necrosis
