2.3.2.7 Lymphomatoid granulomatosis

Definition

Lymphomatoid granulomatosis is an angiodestructive and angiocentric lymphoproliferative disorder composed of EBV+ B cells in a T cell–rich infiltrate that involves extranodal sites.

ICD-O coding

9766/1 Lymphomatoid granulomatosis, grade 1

9766/1 Lymphomatoid granulomatosis, grade 2

9766/3 Lymphomatoid granulomatosis, grade 3

ICD-11 coding

2A81.3 Lymphomatoid granulomatosis

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiocentric immunoproliferative lesion.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In adults, pulmonary involvement occurs in all patients. Other sites include CNS (in 40% of cases), skin (in 34%), kidney (in 19%), liver (in 17%) and other extranodal sites {25321327; 27521314; 21107080}. Lymph node and bone marrow are usually not involved {3785978}. A recent review including 49 paediatric patients (aged < 18 years) demonstrated lung involvement in 82%, CNS in 51%, liver in 25%, skin in 22%, lymph nodes in 16%, kidney in 13%, and spleen in 8% {24390446}.

Clinical features

Lymphomatoid granulomatosis is extremely rare in children. Initial symptoms depend on the localization of the disease, with cough/dyspnoea being most frequent. Neurological symptoms, hepatosplenomegaly, and lymphadenopathy appear to be more frequent in children than in adults {24390446}.

Epidemiology

Lymphomatoid granulomatosis is a rare EBV-driven lymphoproliferative disorder. It typically occurs in adulthood, but it may be also seen in children with inborn errors of immunity.

Etiology

Lymphomatoid granulomatosis is an EBV-associated B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder with a dense T-cell infiltrate.

Pathogenesis

Lymphomatoid granulomatosis arises in patients with underlying immune dysfunction (even in cases of no known primary immunodeficiency), particularly a functional defect in CD8+ cytotoxic T cells {6978760; 8639820; 9354680; 24390446}. The defective immune surveillance of EBV-infected B cells may lead to the development of the disease, with a mechanism similar to that seen in posttransplant lymphoproliferative diseases {32107539; 31352986}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pulmonary nodules are the most frequent presentation, but an interstitial pattern may also occur. Larger nodules may cavitate, and the differential diagnosis includes infection processes, especially in children with primary immunodeficiency {28293550}.

Histopathology

Lymphomatoid granulomatosis is characterized by a lymphohistiocytic infiltrate with an angiocentric and angiodestructive growth pattern {21107080; 25321327}. Most of the infiltrate is composed of lymphocytes admixed with histiocytes, plasma cells, and immunoblasts. Within the nodules, central necrosis is frequent. Despite the name of the lesion, well-formed granulomas are not seen. By immunohistochemical staining, the majority of lymphocytes are T cells and the large atypical cells are B cells, and the latter are positive for EBV by in situ hybridization. Three grades are recognized, providing guidance to the clinical approach. In grade 1 lesions, lymphoid cells show minimal cytological atypia, and large EBV+ cells are rare. In grade 2 lesions, large EBV+ cells are present (40–400 cells/mm2, equating to 5–50 cells/HPF of 0.12 mm2 in area). Necrosis can be present. In grade 3 lesions there are more EBV+ cells (> 400 cells/mm2, equating to > 50 cells/HPF of 0.12 mm2 in area). Necrosis is common and can be extensive. The large cells may form clusters; however, if they sheet out, a diagnosis of EBV+ diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is more appropriate.

Immunophenotype

The large atypical cells are positive for CD20 and EBV using EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) probes by in situ hybridization. There can be variable expression of CD30, but CD15 is negative. The majority of T cells are CD4-positive {25321327}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

IG gene rearrangement may be identified in most grade 2 and grade 3 lesions. Different rearrangements can be identified from different lesions or from different sites. Grade 1 lesions may be polyclonal, because of the paucity of atypical cells. A restricted pattern can be seen in TRG rearrangements, which is not indicative of a T-cell phenotype of the neoplastic cells, but is related to a skewed immune response {25321327}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: presence of a polymorphous lymphoid infiltrate with angiocentricity; transmural lymphoid infiltration of small to medium-sized arteries by small T cells; presence of a variable number of EBV-positive large B cells by in situ hybridization.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis in adults is variable {32107539}. Well-documented cases in the paediatric population are rare, and outcome cannot be extrapolated.
