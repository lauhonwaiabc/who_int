5.2.2.13 Fibrous histiocytoma

Definition

Fibrous histiocytoma (FH) is a neoplasm composed of fibroblastic and histiocytic cells arranged in sheets or short fascicles, often accompanied by a varying number of inflammatory cells, foam cells, and siderophages. Epithelioid FH is an entity separate from FH, characterized by exophytic growth, a distinctive epithelioid morphology, and frequent ALK rearrangement.

Related terminology

Acceptable: dermatofibroma.

Subtype(s)

Cellular fibrous histiocytoma; aneurysmal fibrous histiocytoma; atypical fibrous histiocytoma

Localization

Although any part of the skin can be affected, FH and epithelioid FH mostly involve the extremities, followed by the trunk {6296802}. An eruptive pattern has been described in the setting of immunosuppression {4763427}. Approximately 90% of deep FHs occur in the subcutis; rarely, they occur in subfascial soft tissue sites. Rare cases have also been reported in the retroperitoneum, mediastinum, and pelvis {18300816}.

Clinical features

Cutaneous FHs (dermatofibromas) are elevated or pedunculated red-brown or blue-black lesions measuring < 10 mm to a few centimetres in diameter. Rarely, they may form a depressed area in the skin (atrophic dermatofibroma) {1810988}.

Deep FH manifests as a slow-growing, painless mass.

Epidemiology

Although all morphological subtypes have been documented in children and adolescents, only dermatofibroma, cellular FH, and deep FH occur with any notable frequency in this age group {32246988}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Fusions involving genes encoding PKC isoforms have been identified across all morphological subtypes of FH {26432191; 24721208; 26121314}.

The majority of epithelioid FH cases show ALK gene rearrangement {25857825}.

Macroscopic appearance

FH lesions are relatively well circumscribed, with a vaguely nodular appearance.

Histopathology

Cutaneous FH (dermatofibroma) is composed of short spindle cells arranged in a haphazard manner. Tumour cellularity is often higher in the centre and reduced in the periphery, where the tumour interdigitates with dermal collagen (collagen trapping). Variable numbers of foamy histiocytes, multinucleated (including Touton) giant cells, and chronic inflammatory cells may be present. Dermatofibroma is mostly centred in the dermis, but minimal penetration of the subcutis may be seen. Dermatofibroma usually spares the papillary dermis (grenz zone) and shows hyperplasia of overlying epidermis with hyperpigmentation of basal keratinocytes and elongation of rete ridges.

Cellular FH shows frequent subcutaneous extension (in 30% of cases) and is characterized by increased cellularity and a fascicular growth pattern, as well as a relative paucity of giant cells, siderophages, and foamy macrophages. Typical mitotic figures may be apparent. About 10% of cases may undergo spontaneous central necrosis {8017561}.

Aneurysmal FH is characterized by central blood-filled spaces lined by lesional cells, in a background of FH, often resembling cellular FH {7607620}.

Atypical FH contains pleomorphic spindle cells showing nuclear hyperchromasia and often bizarre nuclei in a background of FH. Necrosis and atypical mitotic figures may be seen {11756767}.

Deep FH shows morphological features similar to those of cutaneous FH, but may show atypical features. However, it usually shows a more prominent storiform pattern, stromal hyalinization, and fewer secondary elements {18300816}.

Epithelioid FH is well circumscribed, often with a surrounding epidermal collarette and composed of epithelioid tumour cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm containing vesicular nuclei and small nucleoli.

A subset of tumours express SMA and desmin, whereas CD34 (often patchy and at the periphery of the tumour) is seen in 5% of cases. Factor XIIIa highlights the background population of dendritic cells {32246988}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: classic FH morphology.

Desirable: for epithelioid FH: demonstration of ALK overexpression by immunohistochemistry or of ALK gene rearrangements by FISH (in selected cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Other than for specific subtypes, excision is curative and recurrences are exceptional. Cellular, aneurysmal, and atypical FH recur locally in 20% of cases. Metastases are exceedingly rare {10874670; 7607620; 11756767}.
