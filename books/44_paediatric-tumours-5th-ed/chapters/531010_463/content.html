5.3.10.10 Undifferentiated sarcomas

Definition

Undifferentiated soft tissue sarcomas show no identifiable line of differentiation when analysed using currently available technology. This is a heterogeneous group and represents a diagnosis of exclusion. Not included are dedifferentiated types of specific soft tissue sarcomas.

ICD-O coding

8805/3 Undifferentiated sarcoma, NOS

8801/3 Undifferentiated spindle cell sarcoma

8802/3 Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma

8804/3 Undifferentiated epithelioid sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH0947 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Malignant fibrous histiocytoma

2B5F.2 & XH6HY6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Undifferentiated sarcoma

2B5F.2 & XH85G7 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Small cell sarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Undifferentiated spindle cell sarcoma; undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma; undifferentiated epithelioid sarcoma

Localization

Undifferentiated sarcomas can occur at any anatomical location. However, in the paediatric population the most common location seems to be the head and neck region, followed by the trunk and the limbs {20055602}.

Clinical features

Undifferentiated sarcomas have no characteristic clinical features that allow their distinction from other sarcoma types. Because most tumours are high-grade, rapid growth is often reported.

Epidemiology

Paediatric undifferentiated sarcomas are currently regarded as exceedingly rare, and their proportion among paediatric sarcomas has in fact decreased as a consequence of improved diagnostic techniques and the identification of distinct tumour entities. Currently, undifferentiated sarcomas account for approximately 5–10% of all paediatric sarcomas {19647855; 20055602}. Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas have been reported as secondary cancers in childhood cancer survivors or in the context of tumour predisposition syndromes {20634481; 20055602}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Undifferentiated sarcomas typically display complex genomic profiles. Cytogenetic analyses show complex karyotypes with near-haploid to hyperoctaploid chromosome numbers {10362810; 25516889}. Array-based {19290004; 21085701} and massively parallel sequencing studies {29100075; 30889380} in adults have shown structural rearrangements and copy-number changes involving most chromosomes. Whole-genome sequencing has revealed whole-genome duplications and chromothripsis accompanied by telomere dysfunction in undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas {30889380}. Data from adult populations show common targets of deletions and genetic disruptions in CDKN2A, CDKN2B, PTEN, TP53, and RB1 {29100075; 30889380; 10748288; 20884963; 21125665; 11103795}. RB1 and TP53 are among the few genes that show recurrent mutations. Driver gene fusions have not been found in undifferentiated sarcomas {29100075}.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross appearance is rather nonspecific. The cut surface is most often fleshy with areas of necrosis and haemorrhage.

Histopathology

Undifferentiated sarcomas may be broadly divided into pleomorphic, spindle cell, and epithelioid subsets, but the only common denominator is the lack of an identifiable line of differentiation. Pleomorphic undifferentiated sarcoma is composed of a patternless proliferation of pleomorphic, often multinucleated, bizarre giant cells. Spindle cell undifferentiated sarcoma is composed of spindle cells featuring tapering nuclei, organized most often in a fascicular growth pattern. The epithelioid subtype can mimic metastatic carcinoma or melanoma and is composed of neoplastic cells featuring abundant polygonal cytoplasm harbouring large vesicular nuclei. In all subtypes, necrosis is very common and high mitotic activity with numerous atypical mitoses is most often observed. Immunohistochemistry plays a major role in proving the absence of a specific line of differentiation.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Undifferentiated sarcomas are characterized by numerous complex genomic alterations without a recurrent genetic abnormality that could be used for diagnostic purposes. Diagnostic molecular techniques such as cytogenetics and next-generation DNA and RNA sequencing play an important role in identifying specific molecular entities with round or spindle cell morphology – tumours that would otherwise have been classified as undifferentiated sarcoma. Cytogenetic/molecular studies of CDK4 and MDM2 gene amplification may also help separate dedifferentiated liposarcomas from undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cell, pleomorphic, and epithelioid morphology in the absence of immunohistochemical or molecular features of specific differentiation.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

Given the extreme rarity of these tumours in the paediatric population, data are very limited. However, the vast majority of undifferentiated sarcomas are high-grade and therefore associated with a dismal prognosis. In the paediatric population, a metastatic rate of 30% and a tumour-related overall mortality rate of 44% have been reported {21963167}. It seems, however, that the overall survival rate is higher in children and adolescents than in adults {31287163; 19647855; 20055602}.
