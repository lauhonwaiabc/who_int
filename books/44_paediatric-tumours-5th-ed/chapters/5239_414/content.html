5.2.3.9 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma (EHE) is a malignant vascular neoplasm composed of epithelioid endothelial cells in a myxohyaline stroma.

ICD-O coding

9133/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, NOS

9133/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion

9133/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with YAP1::TFE3 fusion

ICD-11 coding

2B5Y & XH9GF8 Other specified malignant mesenchymal neoplasms & Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant epithelioid haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion; epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with YAP1::TFE3 fusion

Localization

Almost any organ can be affected. Most cases arise in somatic soft tissues, bone, lung, and liver {31724797; 20333551; 31814291}.

Clinical features

Presentation varies depending on location. Patients with soft tissue EHE usually present with a painful mass. A high incidence of multiorgan disease is reported in children {31724797}.

Epidemiology

EHE is very rare in children. It occurs at a median age of 13.8 years in this population and has no sex predilection {31724797}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

EHE is characterized by a t(1;3)(p36;q25) translocation resulting in a WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion {21885404; 21584898}. The resulting WWTR1 (TAZ)::CAMTA1 chimeric protein disrupts the Hippo pathway, and the protein is located in the nucleus, with constitutive activation {25961935}. An alternative fusion, YAP1::TFE3, is detected in rare cases {23737213}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface is typically white and firm. Origin from a vessel may be seen.

Histopathology

EHE is an infiltrative tumour composed of cords and nests of bland epithelioid tumour cells with round nuclei, eosinophilic cytoplasm, and variable intracytoplasmic vacuoles in a myxohyaline stroma. Angiocentric growth, which can be occlusive, is seen in some cases {7093931; 9130982; 24986479}. A small subset of tumours have nuclear atypia and more mitotic activity. By immunohistochemistry, they are typically positive for ERG, CD31, and CAMTA1, and they are variably positive for keratins, SMA, CD34, and podoplanin {26414223; 31662570}. Immunoreactivity for cytokeratin is seen in a minority {9130982}. Nuclear expression of TFE3 is seen in most cases, irrespective of TFE3 rearrangement {24986479}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of the WWTR1::CAMTA1 or YAP1::TFE3 fusions {21885404; 21584898}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cords and nests of epithelioid cells in a myxohyaline stroma; immunoreactivity for endothelial markers, CAMTA1, or TFE3.

Desirable: molecular confirmation of WWTR1::CAMTA1 or YAP1::TFE3.

Staging

For somatic tumours, the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system is used. AJCC staging is not applicable for abdominal or thoracic tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

EHE is unpredictable; some cases have an indolent course and others are aggressive. Metastasis is seen in as many as 21% of cases and mortality in as many as 17% {18551749; 9130982}. In adults, stratification into high-risk and low-risk groups based on a combination of mitotic activity and tumour size has shown a worse prognosis for patients with both high mitotic activity (> 3 mitoses/10 mm2, equating to > 3 mitoses/50 HPF of 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area) and large tumour size (> 30 mm) than for patients with neither feature (respective 5-year disease-specific survival rates: 59% vs 100%) {18551749}. Atypical histological features are not predictive {18551749}.
