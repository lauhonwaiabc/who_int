5.2.2.11 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Definition

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour (PFHT) is a rare dermal and subcutaneous neoplasm composed of biphasic nodules of histiocytoid cells and spindled myofibroblasts arranged in a plexiform architecture.

ICD-O coding

8835/1 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH4GL1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PFHTs have a strong predilection for the upper extremities, followed by the lower extremities. Other sites less frequently involved include the trunk / abdominal wall and the head and neck region {17870015; 2847569; 1723956}.

Clinical features

Patients with PFHT present with a slow-growing tumour or plaque measuring about 25 mm (range: 5–80 mm) {10366148; 30145610}.

Epidemiology

PFHT shows a wide age range, with a predilection for children and young adults (mean age: ~15 years, range: 1–77 years); 50–70% of cases occur in patients aged < 20 years {2847569; 10366148; 17870015}. There is a slight female predominance {2847569; 10366148; 17870015; 30145610}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

PFHT is an infiltrative / poorly defined mass at the dermal–subcutaneous junction.

Histopathology

PFHT is localized to the dermal–subcutaneous junction, with infiltration of subcutaneous adipose tissue and skeletal muscle. The tumour is characterized by a plexiform or multinodular arrangement of nodules of histiocytoid cells with a variable number of admixed multinucleated, osteoclastic-like giant cells, connected by fascicles of spindled fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells. Other histological changes include chronic inflammation, metaplastic bone, haemorrhage, and haemosiderin deposition. Mild to moderate cytological atypia may be present; marked pleomorphism is rare. The mitotic count is typically low; however, occasional cases contain atypical forms {10366148; 17870015}. Lymphovascular or perineural invasion may rarely be seen {2847569; 10366148; 17870015}. SMA is expressed in the (myo)fibroblastic component.

Differential diagnosis

Those tumours with an overabundance of the (myo)fibroblastic component (e.g. fibroblastic PFHT) may mimic fibromatosis, the recently described plexiform myofibroblastoma {32618598}, plexiform neurofibroma, and fibrous hamartoma of infancy. A prominent fibrohistiocytic pattern may be reminiscent of dermatofibroma, which lacks the plexiform pattern {22420728}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nodules of histiocytoid cells and often osteoclastic-like giant cells; fascicles of (myo)fibroblastic spindle cells; plexiform architecture.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are associated with a risk of local recurrence (12–38%), with rare lymph node metastases {2847569; 10366148; 17870015}. Distant metastasis is extremely rare {9130995; 10366148}.
