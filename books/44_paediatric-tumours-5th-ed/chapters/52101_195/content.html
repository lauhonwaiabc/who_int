5.2.10.1 Clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue

Definition

Clear cell sarcoma (CCS) of soft tissue is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm with melanocytic differentiation and a distinctive nested growth pattern, most often characterized by EWSR1::ATF1 fusion.

ICD-O coding

9044/3 Clear cell sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH77N6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Clear cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant melanoma of soft parts.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CCS most commonly occurs as a deep-seated lesion involving the extremities. The vast majority of cases (as many as 70% in paediatric patients) arise in the distal lower extremities and are frequently associated with tendons, aponeuroses, and fascial structures, with a predilection for the feet, ankles, and knees {6614306; 6616410; 1463095; 10491522; 12115360}. CCS has also been reported in the head and neck, pelvis, abdomen, trunk, mediastinum, skin, oral mucosa, and viscera including the lung and pancreas {27264732; 29401145; 29072181; 28086809; 12115360; 25973121}. CCS may also arise from the gastrointestinal tract {15810965}; however, CCS-like tumour of the gastrointestinal tract (malignant gastrointestinal neuroectodermal tumour), a malignant gastrointestinal neoplasm that has some similarities with CCS, most likely represents a distinct entity, characterized by the presence of neuroectodermal differentiation without melanocytic differentiation {22592145}. CCS may also rarely arise from the kidney {12115360} and should be distinguished from clear cell sarcoma of the kidney, a distinct paediatric malignant renal neoplasm.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a palpable mass that grows insidiously over months or years, with a reported median of 10 months between the onset of symptoms and diagnosis in paediatric patients {12115360}. It may be associated with pain or tenderness in approximately one third to half of the patients {6614306; 6616410; 1463095; 10491522}.

Epidemiology

CCS most commonly affects young people, with a peak incidence in the third to fourth decades of life {6614306; 6616410; 1463095; 10491522}. CCS is less common in children, with approximately 2% of cases having been reported in patients aged < 10 years {12115360}. In the general population there are slightly more female patients with CCS than male patients {14332545; 6614306; 6616410; 1463095; 10491522}; however, in the paediatric population a 2:1 male predominance has been reported {12115360}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

As many as 90% of CCS cases harbour the translocation t(12;22)(q13;q12), resulting in an EWSR1::ATF1 gene rearrangement, most often fusing exon 8 of EWSR1 to exon 4 of ATF1 (type 1 fusion) {2009504; 1373311; 8401579; 11992546}. This fusion results in the acquisition of cAMP independence of the transcriptional activator ATF1 and consequent constitutive activation of the MITF promoter, which is responsible for melanocytic differentiation in CCS {7753552; 16766266}. A small subset of cases harbour the translocation t(2;22)(q34;12), resulting in EWSR1::CREB1 fusion {18300804}; ATF1 and CREB1 are functionally similar transcription factors.

Macroscopic appearance

The median tumour size is 20–50 mm {14332545; 6614306}. The tumours often have a lobulated, variably circumscribed or infiltrative appearance, with a firm, tan or white cut surface.

Histopathology

CCS is composed of short fascicles and nests of relatively uniform epithelioid or plump spindle cells with vesicular nuclei, prominent central nucleoli, and pale eosinophilic cytoplasm. Despite the name, clear cell features are uncommon. The nests are separated by dense fibrous stroma. Occasional distinctive wreath-like multinucleated giant cells are observed in more than half of all cases. Fine granular melanin pigment can occasionally be seen. Mitotic activity is generally low. Cutaneous CCS may mimic a melanoma, especially in rare cases with intraepidermal growth {31688004}. By immunohistochemistry, S100, SOX10, HMB45, melan-A, and MITF are consistently expressed {18300804; 25724000}.

Cytology

Aspiration specimens show round to polygonal cells with prominent nucleoli but no significant cytological atypia {8143535}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of EWSR1 rearrangement or EWSR1::ATF1 fusion may be helpful to differentiate CCS from melanoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nests and short fascicles of plump spindle cells with pale cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli, admixed with occasional wreath-like giant cells; the tumour cells express melanocytic markers including S100, SOX10, melan-A, and HMB45.

Desirable: demonstration of EWSR1 rearrangement or EWSR1::ATF1 fusion.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

CCS is a highly aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis. Recurrence rates reach 40%, and distant metastases occur in approximately 50% of patients {6616410; 10491522; 1463095; 18300804; 2295060}. The most common sites of distant metastases are the lung and lymph node, but bone involvement may also occur in the paediatric population {12115360}. In paediatric patients, the 5-year event-free and overall survival rates are both approximately 65% {12115360}.
