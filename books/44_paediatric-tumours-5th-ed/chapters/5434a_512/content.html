5.4.3.4A Simple bone cyst

Definition

Simple bone cyst (SBC) is an intramedullary, usually unilocular, cystic bone lesion filled with serous or serosanguineous fluid.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

FB80.5 Solitary bone cyst

Related terminology

Acceptable: solitary bone cyst.

Not recommended: unicameral bone cyst.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SBCs generally arise in the appendicular skeleton (proximal humerus: 50%, proximal femur: 25%) and rarely in the pelvis, calcaneus, or acral bones.

Clinical features

Symptoms associated with SBC are pain, swelling, limitation of motion, and pathological fracture.

Epidemiology

SBCs affect patients during the first two decades of life. The M:F ratio is 2:1.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Curetted samples of SBC consist of tan membranous fibrous tissue admixed with cancellous bone and blood.

Histopathology

SBC is composed of a thin wall of fibrous tissue. Fibrin-like collagenous deposits can be seen on the cyst surface and within the wall, often mineralized and organized in cementum-like structures; chronic inflammation, multinucleated giant cells, haemosiderin, cholesterol clefts, and reactive woven bone can also be present, especially if there has been a pathological fracture {22034879}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a simple, usually unilocular, fibrous-walled cyst.

Desirable: fibrin-like deposits that may organize and mineralize, resulting in cementum-like structures.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

SBC can recur locally (10–20% of cases) {16205152}. Spontaneous resolution after pathological fracture has also been reported {30702640}.
