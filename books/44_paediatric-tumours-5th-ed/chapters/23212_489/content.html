2.3.2.12 High-grade B-cell lymphoma with 11q aberration

Definition

High-grade B-cell lymphoma with 11q aberration (HGBCL-11q) has the morphology and immunophenotype of Burkitt lymphoma, but it lacks MYC translocations and harbours a characteristic chromosome 11q gain/loss pattern. Despite morphological, immunophenotypic, and clinical similarities with Burkitt lymphoma, HGBCL-11q is considered a molecularly distinct tumour type.

ICD-O coding

9687/3 High-grade B-cell lymphoma with 11q aberration

ICD-11 coding

2A85.6 & XH8NN2 Burkitt lymphoma including Burkitt leukaemia & Burkitt-like lymphoma with 11q aberration

Related terminology

Acceptable: large B-cell lymphoma with 11q aberration.

Not recommended: MYC-negative Burkitt lymphoma; Burkitt-like lymphoma with 11q aberration.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most (but not all) cases of HGBCL-11q occur as localized and nodal or extranodal disease {30733272; 32239695}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are mass-related. There are no B symptoms {30733272; 32239695}. LBCL-11q may occur in immunocompromised patients {25795716}.

Epidemiology

Unknown

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The mutation landscape of these tumours differs from that of Burkitt lymphoma because the ID3/TCF3 pathway, which is one of the biological hallmarks of BL, seems unaffected {30567752; 30733272}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Morphologically, HGBCL-11q may be indistinguishable from a typical Burkitt lymphoma; however, it may show more cellular pleomorphism, some variation in nuclear shape, larger nucleoli, or an absence of starry-sky pattern {24398325}. The basic immunophenotype is also similar to that of Burkitt lymphoma: usually it has a mature B-cell phenotype with expression of CD10 and BCL6, a high Ki-67 proliferation index (≥ 90%), and BCL2 is negative (with a few exceptions) {24398325}. There may be variable, sometimes high, expression of MYC protein despite the absence of MYC rearrangement {29327714}.

By immunohistochemistry, HGBCL-11q is LMO2+ and sometimes CD56+ (Burkitt lymphoma is usually negative for these markers); in addition, flow cytometry detection of CD16+/CD56+ in the absence of CD38higher or CD8+ are highly characteristic of HGBCL-11q {29327714}.

Cytology

The tumours have vacuolated cytoplasm (known as L3 morphology).

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The definitive diagnosis of HGBCL-11q relies on the detection of the typical molecular aberration by high-resolution array-based comparative genomic hybridization, with a minimal region of gain in 11q23.3 and a minimal region of loss in 11q24.1-qter {24398325}. A subset of cases show no gain and only the telomeric loss or solely telomeric loss of heterozygosity {24398325}, but rare cases with loss of heterozygosity and no telomeric loss are not detected. Interphase FISH is commonly used as a diagnostic test {29272887; 29327714}. The presence of 11q aberrations is not specific for the disease entity {29272887; 32239695; 26776268}. The mutation landscape of HGBCL-11q is different from that of Burkitt lymphoma: mutations in ID3, TCF3, CCND3, SMARCA4, and MYC are rare, and mutations in GNA13 and TTN are frequent {30567752}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour with the morphology and immunophenotype of Burkitt lymphoma; absence of MYC translocations; chromosome 11q gain/loss pattern.

Staging

Staging is performed according to the revised International Pediatric Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma Staging System (IPNHLSS) {25940716}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is favourable when treated according to protocols for Burkitt lymphoma {32239695}.
