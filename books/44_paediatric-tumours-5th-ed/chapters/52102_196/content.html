5.2.10.2 Alveolar soft part sarcoma

Definition

Alveolar soft part sarcoma (ASPS) is a rare tumour of uncertain histogenesis predominantly affecting the deep soft tissues of the extremities, featuring variably discohesive epithelioid cells arranged in nests, resulting in a distinct alveolar appearance. It is characterized by a specific translocation, der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25), which results in ASPSCR1::TFE3 gene fusion.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ASPS most commonly involves the muscles of the buttocks and thighs, followed by the trunk and internal organs. Primaries in the head and neck, particularly in the tongue or orbit, are observed in children {7076238; 15500657}. Uncommon locations include bone/spine {10583555}, bladder {17001165}, mediastinum {9416489}, and the female genital tract {8598329}.

Clinical features

Patients with ASPS present with a slow-growing, painless, intramuscular mass with haemorrhage {24605193}. MRI is notable for flow voids, large vessels, and moderate to intense postcontrast enhancement {26231949}.

Epidemiology

ASPS accounts for < 1% of all soft tissue sarcomas. Patient ages range from 1 to 78 years (median: 25 years); almost two thirds of patients are aged < 30 years. There is a female predominance {2642727; 12837977; 11169942; 8531857; 15500657; 29350467}.

Etiology

ASPSCR1::TFE3 reciprocal translocation appears to be the initiating genetic event. No cases have been reported after irradiation or with a germline cancer predisposition syndrome.

Pathogenesis

ASPS is defined by a specific chromosomal alteration, der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25). The der(X) resulting from the t(X;17)(p11.2;q25) is rarely retained; the der(17)t(X;17) can also be add(17)(q25), if the quality of the banding is not sufficient to allow for positive identification of the additional material from the short arm of the X chromosome. This translocation results in fusion of the transcription factor gene TFE3 (from Xp11) with ASPSCR1 (ASPL) at 17q25.3 {11244503}. Depending on the TFE3 intron involved in the genomic rearrangement, ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion transcripts can differ by the presence or absence of a TFE3 exon. The ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion protein localizes to the nucleus, where it functions as an aberrant transcription factor, causing c-Met overexpression and activation of c-Met signalling, rendering ASPS cells sensitive to c-Met inhibition in vitro and in vivo {17283122; 23288701}. A mouse model of ASPS driven by the conditional expression of an ASPSCR1::TFE3 transgene highlighted the exquisite dependence of ASPS on lactate for growth {25453902}, which may be linked to its frequent occurrence in muscle, with high expression of the lactate transporter MCT1 and its associated protein, CD147 {11943706}. ASPSCR1::TFE3 translocation is the initiating genetic event {11244503}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are nodular and circumscribed, measuring 12–240 mm (median: 65 mm) {11169942}. The centre of larger tumours may be haemorrhagic or necrotic.

Histopathology

ASPS is composed of large polygonal cells with eosinophilic granular cytoplasm, an eccentric vesicular nucleus with a cookie-bite indentation, and a prominent nucleolus, arranged in an alveolar pattern of organoid nests with central discohesion. A rich sinusoidal capillary vasculature encircles tumour nests. Vascular invasion is common. Cytoplasmic clearing, rhabdoid cells, a pseudoglandular pattern, central necrosis, and cystic change may be observed {15500657}. A solid pattern is observed in very young patients, with more alveolar growth in older children and young adults, especially in lingual tumours {15500657}. Rod-like/rhomboid intracytoplasmic crystalline structures may be identified by PAS with diastase resistance {11943706; 15500657} and may stain for MCT1 and CD147 {14886902; 11943706; 21835426}. ASPS demonstrates nuclear TFE3 {12766578}. Cathepsin K and occasional calretinin {21602817} or desmin {15500657} may be observed.

Cytology

Cytological preparations reveal large single cells with granularity or vacuoles {19787801}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

TFE3 rearrangement or ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion transcripts may be diagnostically helpful {30139549}. The presence of the ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion appears to be highly specific and sensitive for ASPS among sarcomas; the same gene fusion can be identified in Xp11.2 translocation renal cell carcinomas of young patients {11438465}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: eosinophilic polygonal cells in an organoid/nested pattern often encircled by rich sinusoidal capillaries; strong and diffuse nuclear TFE3 expression by immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: intracytoplasmic PAS-positive, diastase-resistant crystals; demonstration of TFE3 gene rearrangement or ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion (in selected cases).

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system can be used.

Prognosis and prediction

Although ASPS is not formally graded, it is regarded as high-grade. Local recurrence occurs in 11–50% of cases {26804150}. Metastases involve lung, liver, bone, brain, and (rarely) lymph nodes, often ≥ 10 years after diagnosis {29350467; 20593242; 11169942; 2642727}. In children and adolescents, the 5-year event-free survival rate is approximately 95% {29286582} and is especially high with novel therapy. Negative prognostic factors include older age, tumour size > 100 mm, distant metastasis at diagnosis, and a primary site in the trunk {26804150}. Tumours detected early and resected completely (including lingual tumours) have a better prognosis {8531857; 15500657}. A recent clinical trial of the c-Met inhibitor crizotinib demonstrated disease stabilization in most patients with ASPS, with a few showing tumour shrinkage {29216400}. Tumour response or disease stabilization is seen in > 50% of cases treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors {30347044}.
