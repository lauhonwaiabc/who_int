5.2.10.8 Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation

Definition

Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation (BPOP) is a benign lesion of the bone surface, composed of an admixture of cartilage, bone, and spindle cells.

ICD-O coding

9212/0 Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation

ICD-11 coding

2E82.Z & XH23J5 Benign chondrogenic tumours, site unspecified & Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation

Related terminology

Not recommended: Nora lesion.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Small bones of the distal extremities (mainly the metacarpals and middle phalanges) are affected, with hands four times more commonly affected than feet {32611484}. Involvement of distal phalanges is uncommon. Long tubular bones may be affected {8317609}. Craniofacial bone involvement has been reported.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a painless mass of a few months’ to several years’ duration. Pain and rapid growth have been reported {21533526}. Radiologically, BPOP is a well-marginated ossified mass arising from the cortical bone without periosteal reaction, flaring of the underlying cortex, or corticomedullary continuity with affected bone. Exceptional cases may show corticomedullary continuity {17437102}.

Epidemiology

BPOP has a wide age distribution. Most patients are in their third or fourth decade of life, and there is no sex predilection {15252309}.

Etiology

A history of trauma is reported in a subset of patients {8317609}, and it has been hypothesized by some that BPOP is part of a spectrum encompassing reactive processes, including fibro-osseous tumour of digits and turret exostosis. However, the presence of recurrent cytogenetic abnormalities in some cases suggests that it is a neoplastic process.

Pathogenesis

Recurrent cytogenetic abnormalities, including t(1;17)(q32;q21), inv(7)(q22q32), and inv(6)(p25q15), have been reported {15343507; 15926071; 22075515; 21509435; 24412018}, suggesting that BPOP is a neoplastic process.

Macroscopic appearance

BPOP is an exophytic bony lesion with a cartilaginous surface, usually measuring 10–30 mm.

Histopathology

BPOP is composed of three distinct components with varying degrees of representation: (1) hypercellular cartilage containing frequently enlarged, bizarre, binucleated chondrocytes; (2) cancellous bone undergoing maturation via enchondral ossification, with calcified cartilage having a characteristic basophilic tinctorial quality (known as blue bone); and (3) spindle cell stroma without cytological atypia. Myxoid and fibrocartilaginous tissue may be seen. Atypical mitoses are not seen.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an exophytic bone lesion composed of disorganized, hypercellular cartilage containing atypical chondrocytes, spindle cells, and bone (including characteristic blue bone).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

BPOP is a benign lesion, with no capacity to metastasize. It is treated with simple excision. Recurrences occur in approximately 50% of cases and are managed by re-excision.
