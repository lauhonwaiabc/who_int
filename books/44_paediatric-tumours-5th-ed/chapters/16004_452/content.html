16.0.0.4 Ganglioneuroblastoma, nodular (and other composite neuroblastic tumours)

Definition

Nodular ganglioneuroblastoma (GNBn) is a peripheral neuroblastic tumour (pNT) composed of one or more macroscopically visible neuroblastic nodules within a ganglioneuromatous background.

ICD-O coding

9490/3 Ganglioneuroblastoma, nodular

ICD-11 coding

2A00.21 & XH77W7 Mixed neuronal-glial tumours & Ganglioneuroblastoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: composite ganglioneuroblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GNBn occurs in the same location as other pNTs – in the adrenal glands and in the abdominal, thoracic, pelvic, and cervical sympathetic chains.

Clinical features

Patients with GNBn usually present with a localized abdominal mass with associated pain, distention, and fever; however, they may also present with widespread metastatic disease {19530234; 28465480}.

Epidemiology

GNBn accounts for 5–10% of pNTs {28465480; 22137163}. Patients with GNBn are generally older (83% aged > 18 months) than those with neuroblastoma, and some are even adults {28465480; 24633299}.

Etiology

The etiology of GNBn is identical to that of other pNTs (see section Neuroblastoma).

Pathogenesis

The initiating events leading to the development of GNBn are unknown; it has been postulated that the neuroblastic nodule is a clonal proliferation arising from additional acquired genetic aberrations, but evidence has been contradictory {25081755; 8108298}.

Macroscopic appearance

GNBn appears as a firm yellow-tan tumour, usually with a single red haemorrhagic or necrotic nodule; less frequently there may be multiple nodules. Less common patterns included in the GNBn category lack a visible nodule in the primary tumour and are only identifiable as GNBn due to metastasis of the unseen neuroblastomatous component {14601099}.

Histopathology

Nodular ganglioneuroblastoma

The diagnosis of GNBn relies upon the identification of a nodular neuroblastic component and a non-nodular Schwannian stroma–rich/dominant component. An abrupt demarcation (pushing border or fibrous pseudocapsule formation) characterizes the interface between these two components. The nodule contains poorly differentiated, undifferentiated, or (less frequently) differentiating neuroblasts without discernible Schwann cells; the mitosis-karyorrhexis index can be low, intermediate, or high {22137163}. The surrounding tumour is either ganglioneuroma or intermixed ganglioneuroblastoma. When the nodule outgrows the rest of the tumour, only a small amount of ganglioneuromatous component may be present at the periphery.

GNBn, historically categorized with the unfavourable histology group {11745303}, is now subcategorized as favourable or unfavourable according to the histopathology of the neuroblastic component, using the same International Neuroblastoma Pathology Classification (INPC) criteria as are used for neuroblastoma (see section Neuroblastoma) {14601099}. When multiple nodules are present, each one should be classified independently.

pNTs are generally heterogeneous and GNBn is even more so, posing a potential problem when limited biopsies are performed. A single core needle biopsy could easily miss a small nodule within a larger GNBn, resulting in misdiagnosis as a ganglioneuroma or ganglioneuroblastoma and possible undertreatment of the patient. Therefore, thorough tumour sampling is essential during both biopsy and gross pathological examination.

Other composite neuroblastic tumours

These have been described where multiple distinct neuroblastic components are present but the background is not ganglioneuromatous {24108545; 17535087}. They have discrete, circumscribed areas with clear differences not only histologically but also genetically (MYCN amplification) and immunohistochemically. One of the future challenges in classification will be to formally describe and identify prognostic factors for these pNTs with multiple clones with specific cytomorphological and/or genetic characteristics.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

GNBn is usually non–MYCN-amplified (> 90% of cases) {28465480; 22137163; 11745206}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: grossly distinct nodule(s) with pushing borders; a microscopically distinct neuroblastic nodule within a Schwannian stroma–dominant ganglioneuromatous or Schwannian stroma–rich ganglioneuroblastomatous background; INPC classification of the nodule with the least favourable histology according to the degree of neuroblastic differentiation, mitosis-karyorrhexis index, and patient age.

Desirable: determination of MYCN amplification status and assessment of other genetic characteristics of the neuroblastic component.

Staging

The International Neuroblastoma Risk Group Staging System (INRGSS) is used for pNTs and depends on the presurgical determination of the presence or absence of image-defined risk factors {19047290}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of patients with GNBn can be determined by the analysis of age-linked histopathological features in the nodule(s) and is in most cases (~60–83%) unfavourable {14601099; 22137163}. Each neuroblastic nodule needs to be separately classified using the INPC system; the clinical behaviour appears to be dictated by the nodule with the least favourable histology.
