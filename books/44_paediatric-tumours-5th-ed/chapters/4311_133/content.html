4.3.1.1 Diffuse choroidal neurofibroma and ganglioneuroma

Definition

Neurofibromas are benign peripheral nerve sheath tumours composed of Schwann cells with intermixed perineurial cells, fibroblasts, mast cells, and axons in a variably myxoid to collagenous extracellular matrix. When involving the choroid, a diffuse pattern of growth by Schwann cells is present and there are typically no stromal changes. Ganglioneuroma of the choroid differs from neurofibroma in that it additionally contains a variable number of mature ganglion cells.

Related terminology

Not recommended: ganglioneuronal hamartoma; choroidal hamartoma.

Subtype(s)

Ganglioneuroma of the choroid

Localization

In the choroid, diffuse/multifocal choroidal involvement is typical, and there is a predilection for the posterior pole {25990002}. Associated involvement of the ciliary nerves is variable.

Clinical features

Patients with neurofibroma of the choroid may present with a pale diffuse choroidal mass. Studies using modern imaging techniques (i.e. indocyanine green angiography, infrared fundus autofluorescence, and enhanced depth imaging optical coherence tomography) suggest that choroidal involvement is very common in neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) and might be a potential diagnostic criterion for the disease {25990002; 21963267; 11041400}, even in the presence of a normal fundus examination and colour photography. MRI is helpful for identifying choroidal tumours in patients with NF1, revealing circumscribed or diffuse thickening of the choroid {21574161}. Choroidal neurofibroma has been reported with congenital iris ectropion and glaucoma {1955961}. Ciliary body involvement may lead to anteriorly displaced ciliary processes, whereas ectropion uveae is secondary to the endothelialization of the iridocorneal angle {22480745}. Of note, choroidal melanoma has also been reported in patients with NF1 {9596516}.

Epidemiology

Choroidal lesions in the setting of NF1 may develop in paediatric patients, and they appear to increase in frequency with age.

Etiology

Almost all neurofibromas result from allelic loss and inactivation of the NF1 gene region located on chromosome arm 17q {28436162}.

Pathogenesis

Patients with NF1 are predisposed to the development of uveal lesions, and the number of choroidal nodules is increased in the presence of truncating NF1 mutations {27716896}. One reported example developed in a patient with germline PTEN mutation (Cowden syndrome) {28868283}.

Macroscopic appearance

Diffuse expansion of the choroid by a non-pigmented infiltrate is typically seen.

Histopathology

Neurofibroma is characterized by a haphazard proliferation of Schwann cells and fibroblasts {2039037}. Mast cells are often present as well. In the uveal tract, histopathology often reveals diffuse thickening by a proliferation of Schwann cells, fibroblasts, and choroidal melanocytes. This thickening is massive in some cases; parts of the choroid may be several times as thick as the sclera. Ovoid bodies that resemble tactile corpuscles are distinctive, composed of Schwann cells arranged in lamellae as demonstrated by electron microscopy {6816197}. Ganglion cells, which are a normal constituent of the choroid, may be numerous in some cases. The term “ganglioneuroma” has been applied to these lesions. Bilateral cases have been reported {32454990}. The differential diagnosis is limited, because the clinical and histological features are distinctive.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

NF1 gene inactivation is expected in patients with NF1, whereas PTEN mutations are expected in the context of the very rare cases associated with Cowden syndrome {28868283}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a diffuse or multifocal choroidal mass showing haphazard proliferation of Schwann cells and fibroblasts by histology.

Desirable: NF1 gene inactivation is usually present.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Diffuse uveal tract abnormalities are typically found incidentally during histopathological examination of blind, painful, buphthalmic eyes enucleated from patients with NF1 {27171204}. Malignant degeneration is not a feature, but it may develop in larger coexisting plexiform neurofibromas.
