16.0.0.1 Peripheral neuroblastic tumours: Introduction

Tumours arising from the sympathoadrenal lineage of neural crest–derived tissues are collectively known as peripheral neuroblastic tumours (pNTs). “Neuroblastoma” is often used as an omnibus term for all types of pNTs. However, it is recommended that the term be used to designate a specific set of tumours in this group, characterized by the grade of neuroblastic differentiation and the degree of Schwannian stromal development. The International Neuroblastoma Pathology Committee defines four categories of pNTs: neuroblastoma (Schwannian stroma–poor); ganglioneuroblastoma, intermixed (Schwannian stroma–rich); ganglioneuroblastoma, nodular (composite, Schwannian stroma–rich/stroma–dominant and stroma–poor); and ganglioneuroma (Schwannian stroma–dominant) {10421272}. It is believed that all ganglioneuromas were once neuroblastomas in the early stages of their development [[Willis RA. Chapter 11: The embryonic tumours and teratomas. In: The borderland of embryology and pathology. 2nd ed. London (UK): Butterworth & Co.; 1962. pp. 410–54.]].

pNTs are clinically diverse tumours resulting from defects in molecular mechanisms that regulate normal sympathetic nervous system development {29623426}. Many molecular markers have been identified and correlated with prognosis. Recent advances in tumour biology and normal embryology have increased our understanding of the functions of these markers and enhanced efforts to find actionable targets for precision medicine. An important shift in understanding normal development has occurred with the recent recognition that sympathetic neurons and chromaffin cells in the adrenal medulla {28684471} or extra-adrenal paraganglia {30740044} do not have an immediate common precursor. Whereas sympathetic ganglia arise by direct migration of precursors from the neural crest, chromaffin cells arise predominantly from neural crest cells termed Schwann cell precursors, which remain in place near the dorsal root ganglia, migrate along sensory axons to join with preganglionic sympathetic axons, and use those axons for guidance to reach their destinations. Schwann cell precursors may serve as a reservoir of neural crest–like cells after the neural crest no longer exists {29454705}. At the same time, small numbers of bridge cells {28971249} may further contribute to cellular diversification and expansion by giving rise to neurons in paraganglia and chromaffin cells in ganglia {30740044}. These developmental findings may help to explain characteristics of this disease associated with the frequency distribution of anatomical site and patient age, provide insights into how tumours mature, and lead to new approaches by pathologists and clinicians to improve patient care.
