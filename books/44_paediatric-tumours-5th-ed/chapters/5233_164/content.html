5.2.3.3 Intramuscular vascular anomalies

Definition

Intramuscular vascular anomalies are composed of abnormal capillary and venous channels and/or of channels with arteriovenous communications, sometimes extending beyond the muscular compartment. Some anomalies are associated with significant overgrowth of other connective tissue elements.

ICD-O coding

9132/0 Intramuscular haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

LA90.2Z Peripheral venous malformation, unspecified

LA90.3Z Peripheral arteriovenous malformation, unspecified

2E92.40 PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome

Related terminology

Not recommended: intramuscular haemangioma; intramuscular angioma; intramuscular angiolipoma.

Acceptable: for intramuscular fast-flow vascular anomaly: intramuscular haemangioma, small vessel type; intramuscular capillary-type haemangioma; intramuscular haemangioma, capillary type.

Subtype(s)

Intramuscular haemangioma; venous malformation (VM); fibroadipose vascular anomaly (FAVA); intramuscular fast-flow vascular anomaly (IFVA); arteriovenous malformation (AVM); PTEN hamartoma of soft tissue (PHOST)

Localization

These are intramuscular lesions, mostly involving the extremities and often crossing fascial planes to involve adjacent muscles, subcutaneous fat, or bone.

Clinical features

Localized discomfort is a prime symptom, particularly with FAVA. VM and FAVA have slow flow, whereas IFVA, AVM, and PHOST demonstrate fast flow on imaging. Multifocal small capillary stains are seen anywhere in capillary malformation–arteriovenous malformation disorders (CM-AVM1 and CM-AVM2), which also frequently manifest as Parkes Weber syndrome {31300548}. Characteristic phenotypic features are present in most individuals with PHOST {22446940}.

Epidemiology

These lesions predominantly affect older children and adolescents.

Etiology

Somatic mutations are present in TEK or PIK3CA in most VMs {29217067}, in PIK3CA in most FAVAs {25681199}, and in MAP2K1 or KRAS in most IFVAs {31486960}. Many AVMs are associated with germline or somatic mutations in RASA1 (CM-AVM1) and germline mutations in EPHB4 (CM-AVM2) {31300548}. Germline mutations in PTEN are present in most PHOSTs {22446940}.

Pathogenesis

RAS/MAPK signalling, acting in concert with PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling, is essential for vascular development. Loss of EPHB4 or RASA1 function results in constitutive activation of RAS/MAPK and AKT/mTOR signalling, causing abnormal endothelial differentiation and disordered vascular development {28687708}.

Macroscopic appearance

VM is usually well defined and spongiform, with minimal intervening fibroadipose tissue or skeletal muscle. FAVA is most often infiltrative, with vascular nodules embedded within abundant fibroadipose tissue. IFVA is commonly well defined, with faint red vascular zones and variable fat. In AVM, large channels and some fibroadipose tissue permeate skeletal muscle. PHOST is poorly delimited and has red vascular zones, large veins, and variable fibroadipose tissue.

Histopathology

VM is an admixture of spongiform, variably sized, dysmorphic venous channels and minimal interposed skeletal muscle or fibroadipose tissue {12447041}.

FAVA has abundant fibroadipose tissue with nodular aggregates of back-to-back thin-walled venous channels sometimes seen as diverticula of larger dysmorphic veins. Nerves are often encased by fibrous tissue and infiltrated by abnormal venous channels. Lymphoid aggregates are common and some lesions have metaplastic bone, small foci of dysmorphic lymphatic channels, and clusters of vessels of indeterminate lineage with coiling, thick walls, concentric muscular layers, and tiny lumina {31279623; 31181028}.

IFVA manifests as cords, aggregates, lobules, and occasionally focally anastomosing vessels with mostly capillary-type morphology. Veins are enlarged, many having irregularly thickened walls. Arteries may show enlargement with loss of internal elastic lamina, intimal cushions, and medial muscular hyperplasia. Vessels of indeterminate type may be present. Adipose tissue varies in amount {31486960}.

AVMs are infiltrative, with small, medium, and large arteries showing loss of internal elastic lamina; veins of similar calibre with thickened walls and intimal hyperplasia; channels of indeterminate type; and occasionally obvious arteriovenous communications. Fat and fibrous tissues tend to be minimal {31486960}.

PHOST has a variably fibroadipocytic and myxoid stroma and nodules of variably muscularized dysmorphic venous channels with complex lumina and circumjacent arteries. Other features are enlarged arteries (with occasionally evident arteriovenous communication); a diffuse increase in small arteries, veins, and indeterminate-type vessels; small discrete myxoid foci containing small arteries exhibiting rapid arborization, lymphoid aggregates, and (less commonly) metaplastic bone; and clusters of vessels of indeterminate lineage with coiling, thick walls, concentric muscular layers, and tiny lumina {22446940}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of somatic mutations (TEK, PIK3CA, MAP2K1, KRAS) and germline and/or somatic mutations (RASA1, EPHB4, PTEN) may be helpful in diagnosing some patients.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

VM

Essential: abnormally formed venous channels including a spongiform pattern.

Desirable: demonstration of somatic TEK or PIK3CA mutation.

FAVA

Essential: nodular aggregates of back-to-back thin-walled venous channels; abnormal veins; abundant fibroadipose tissue.

Desirable: demonstration of somatic PIK3CA mutation.

IFVA

Essential: congeries of small vessels; enlarged, thick-walled veins; often, a modified arterial mural structure.

Desirable: demonstration of somatic MAP2K1 or KRAS mutation.

AVM

Essential: arteries with loss of internal elastic lamina; thick-walled veins; indeterminate-type vessels.

Desirable: demonstration of germline and/or somatic mutations in RASA1 or EPHB4.

PHOST

Essential: complex vascular nodules; large arteries and veins; numerous small vessels of indeterminate type.

Desirable: demonstration of germline or (rarely) somatic PTEN mutation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Depending on circumstances, treatments have included the following: for VM, sclerotherapy, excision, and targeted therapy; for IFVA, embolization, excision, and targeted therapy; for FAVA, sclerotherapy, cryotherapy, and excision; and for PHOST, embolization and excision.
