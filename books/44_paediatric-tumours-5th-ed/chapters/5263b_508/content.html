5.2.6.3B Superficial angiomyxoma

Definition

Superficial angiomyxoma is a bland-looking myxoid dermal tumour with a lobular/multinodular configuration.

ICD-O coding

8841/0 Superficial angiomyxoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH58A9 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Superficial angiomyxoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: cutaneous myxoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Superficial angiomyxomas have been reported at different sites, including the genital and head/neck regions {21504453; 32739529; 28272665; 23783153}. Congenital cases are rare, and multiple lesions are usually associated with Carney complex {12532046}.

Clinical features

Superficial angiomyxomas produce a painless cutaneous or subcutaneous papule or nodule.

Epidemiology

Superficial angiomyxoma is rare in children {21504453; 32739529; 28272665; 23783153; 9536546}.

Etiology

The etiology of single lesions is unknown. Multiple lesions may be associated with Carney complex.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours appear as circumscribed myxoid dermal nodules.

Histopathology

Superficial angiomyxoma is composed of bland-looking spindled to stellate cells set in an abundant myxoid stroma with capillary-like vessels that exhibit, at least focally, an arborizing/plexiform configuration. A lobular/multinodular appearance, due to the presence of fibrous septa, is a typical feature. Entrapped cystic epithelial structures may be seen. CD34 is diffusely expressed.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dermal myxoid tumour with a lobular/multinodular configuration.

Desirable: arborizing/plexiform capillary-like vessels.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

No local recurrence has been reported in children.
