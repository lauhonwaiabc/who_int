4.4.1.3 Medulloepithelioma

Definition

Medulloepithelioma is a rare primary embryonal neuroepithelial neoplasm of the eye, characterized by cords of multilayered neuroepithelial cells resembling the medullary epithelium of the optic cup.

ICD-O coding

9501/0 Benign non-teratoid medulloepithelioma

9502/0 Benign teratoid medulloepithelioma

9501/3 Malignant non-teratoid medulloepithelioma

9502/3 Malignant teratoid medulloepithelioma

ICD-11 coding

2D06.1 Malignant medulloepithelioma of ciliary body

Related terminology

Not recommended: diktyoma; teratoneuroma.

Subtype(s)

Benign non-teratoid medulloepithelioma; benign teratoid medulloepithelioma; malignant non-teratoid medulloepithelioma; malignant teratoid medulloepithelioma

Localization

Most medulloepitheliomas occur on the inner surface of the ciliary body. Rare retinal and optic nerve medulloepitheliomas have been reported {20497433; 4827430; 12045984; 14974835}.

Clinical features

Patients with medulloepithelioma present most often with blindness or visual loss (27%), leukocoria (20%), strabismus (12%), or a red and painful eye (20%) {23796765}. Intratumoural cysts, which may be helpful diagnostic clues, are present in 61% of patients. Lens notching or lens coloboma caused by segmental loss of zonular fibres is present in 20% {23796765; 25136238}. A neoplastic cyclitic membrane is present in half {21505878; 25136238}. There is secondary glaucoma in 44% of cases and iris neovascularization in 51%. Ophthalmoscopy reveals a fleshy, pink, tan, white-grey, or yellow ciliary body mass.

Diagnosis and treatment are often delayed. In one series, the average age at presentation was about 4 years, but the average age at diagnosis and treatment was 5 years {655220}. Before diagnosis, some patients have been treated for cataract, glaucoma, or uveitis. Extraocular extension of tumour cells may have occurred after misguided tube shunt surgery for neovascular glaucoma {23699787}. In some cases, the tumour is found incidentally when a blind, painful eye is enucleated or eviscerated. An epibulbar mass may be present in cases with extraocular extension. Enucleation is the standard treatment for advanced ciliary body medulloepithelioma. Small or medium-sized tumours have been treated successfully with plaque brachytherapy {30375203; 25863421}. The tumour often recurs after attempted local resection.

Epidemiology

Most medulloepitheliomas occur in children aged 2–5 years although rare cases in adults have been reported (patient age range: < 1–66 years) {23218809}, and 63% of patients are female {23796765}. There is no racial predilection.

Etiology

Most intraocular medulloepitheliomas occur sporadically and are not associated with congenital malformation or cytogenic abnormality.

Ciliary body medulloepithelioma occurs in 1–3% of patients with the pleuropulmonary blastoma family tumour predisposition syndrome caused by germline mutations in DICER1 {21156700; 25032694; 23613510; 30339877}. Somatic mutations in DICER1 and KMT2D are frequent in intraocular medulloepithelioma {26841698}.

Pathogenesis

Medulloepitheliomas have classically been considered congenital tumours that are thought to arise from anlagen of embryonic medullary epithelium {655220; 9003333; 25545484}. The tumour and its multilaminar medullary epithelium appear to arise from a simple premedullary epithelial monolayer of S100-positive cells {25174896}. Positive staining for CRX and the neuronal marker NeuN suggests that the most anterior layers of the optic cup retain the potential for retinal and neuroglial differentiation. Intraocular medulloepithelioma shares histomorphological features with CNS medulloepithelioma, a rare entity currently grouped with several other Ewing sarcomas as embryonal tumours with multilayered rosettes. Both ocular and CNS medulloepitheliomas express LIN28A, but the intraocular tumours show numerous copy-number aberrations that involve either whole chromosomes or chromosomal arms. They show no amplification of the C19MC cluster of microRNA and have strikingly different DNA methylation patterns compared to CNS tumours, suggesting that the ocular and CNS neoplasms do not share common origins or biological behaviours {26183384; 25748578; 26959146}. Cytogenetic analysis of intraocular medulloepithelioma showed recurrent aberrations in chromosomes 1p, 4, 8, and 16p {26183384}. Intraocular medulloepithelioma has occurred in patients with DICER1-related pleuropulmonary blastoma familial tumour predisposition syndrome. Recurrent somatic DICER1 mutations have been reported in some cases of sporadic intraocular medulloepithelioma without pleuropulmonary blastoma syndrome {25791583; 26841698; 25807141}. MicroRNA profiling in intraocular medulloepitheliomas shows significantly upregulated and downregulated microRNAs. The microRNA dysregulation profile is similar to that seen in glioblastoma and retinoblastoma {25807141}. Recurrent mutations in the histone methyltransferase gene KMT2D also have been reported {26841698}. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of DNA methylation showed that intraocular medulloepitheliomas have distinct signatures from intracranial medulloepithelioma {26183384}. The targetable epigenetic tumour protein EZH2 is enriched in intraocular medulloepithelioma {27842164}.

Macroscopic appearance

Medulloepithelioma appears as a pink, tan, white-grey, or yellow mass that often contains cystic cavities on the inner surface of the ciliary body.

Histopathology

Embryonal medulloepithelioma is centred on the ciliary body and typically rests on its inner surface. The iris and anterior chamber are involved in some cases. The mean tumour size is 11 mm (range: 2–30 mm). The tumour is composed of anastomosing cords, bands, and ribbons of polarized neuroepithelial cells that resemble embryonic medullary epithelium. A net-like pattern of delicate interweaving strands of neuroepithelium characterizes the rare diktyomatous pattern. The polarized neuroepithelial elements often are surrounded by pools of hyaluronidase-sensitive mucopolysaccharide (called neoplastic vitreous). The myxoid matrix abuts a thin basal lamina on the inner surface of the neuroepithelium, and the lumina of the neuroepithelial clefts and rosettes contain zonulae adherentes analogous to the retina’s external limiting membrane {695223}. Intralesional cystoid spaces are present in about half of the tumours, and one third contain foci of intrinsic pigmentation {23796765; 11831926}. Basophilic sheets of poorly differentiated neuroblastic cells resembling retinoblastoma are a histopathological criteria for malignancy. Such areas may contain Homer Wright or Flexner–Wintersteiner rosettes. Additional criteria for malignancy include invasion of the uvea, cornea, sclera, or optic nerve; extraocular extension; sarcomatous change in areas of heterologous tissue; pleomorphism; atypia; and mitotic activity. About 66–80% of medulloepitheliomas are classified as malignant {655220; 23796765}.

Because the current criteria for malignancy correlate poorly with clinical behaviour, and molecular studies have disclosed no differences between histologically benign and malignant medulloepithelioma, a simple grading scheme has been proposed to replace the current dichotomous classification {27239464}. The proposed scheme comprises CNS WHO grade 1 tumours, CNS WHO grade 2 tumours (tumour progression evidenced as pleomorphism, increased mitotic activity, and local invasion), and CNS WHO grade 3 tumours (transformation of the tumour to one with metastatic potential, with extrascleral extension or metastasis). Metastatic disease only occurs in cases with extraocular extension.

Heteroplastic elements including hyaline cartilage, muscle, myoblasts, and brain-like tissue are found in 37% of cases {23796765}. Medulloepitheliomas with heteroplastic elements are designated as teratoid. About one fifth of benign medulloepitheliomas and approximately half of malignant medulloepitheliomas are teratoid {655220; 23796765}. Hyaline cartilage is the most common heteroplastic element. The presence of heteroplastic elements has no effect on prognosis. Sheets of medulloepithelioma can grow posteriorly on the inner surface of the retina, reaching the posterior pole of the eye.

The non-teratoid component of medulloepithelioma typically is immunoreactive for vimentin and NSE. Limited and conflicting results have been reported for chromogranin, synaptophysin, GFAP, S100, and HMB45. Immunoreactivity for pancytokeratin and CK18, with no reactivity for CK7, CK20, or EMA, has been described {25251824; 22288972}. Almost all medulloepitheliomas stain positively for LIN28A and the intensity of staining correlates with aggressive behaviour {25748578}. LIN28A staining is specific for medulloepithelioma; it is uniformly negative in retinoblastoma.

Cytology

Benign cases show cohesive neuroepithelial cells, in strips or glandular formations, with abundant cytoplasm and no mitosis or necrosis. Pap staining highlights the contents of the neoplastic glands. Malignant tumours comprise mitotically active small blue cells with occasional rosettes. Differentiation from retinoblastoma can be challenging. Compared to retinoblastoma, specimens are usually less cellular and necrosis is absent {8465629}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular pathology is not relevant in the diagnosis of medulloepithelioma; most cases are readily diagnosed using routine light microscopy.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour on the inner surface of the ciliary body; bands of polarized neuroepithelial cells; pools of hyaluronic acid; heteroplastic cartilage, muscle, or brain-like tissue in teratoid tumours.

Desirable: positive nuclear staining for RB1 differentiates malignant non-teratoid tumours from retinoblastoma; immunoreactivity for LIN28A.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Metastasis and fatalities can occur when there is extensive extraocular extension {23796765}. The prognosis is excellent if enucleation is performed and the tumour is totally confined to the eye.
