5.4.3.4C Osteofibrous dysplasia

Definition

Osteofibrous dysplasia (OFD) is a benign fibro-osseous tumour of long bone that typically arises in the anterior cortex of the tibia and/or fibula during childhood.

ICD-O coding

9261/0 Osteofibrous dysplasia

ICD-11 coding

2E83.5 & XH6M86 Benign osteogenic tumours of bone or articular cartilage of limbs & Ossifying fibroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: ossifying fibroma of long bones; Kempson–Campanacci lesion.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

OFD typically affects the proximal anterior tibia and/or fibula. Multifocal and bilateral lesions may be present in some cases. Exceptional cases involving other bones have been reported {28249983; 2743266}.

Clinical features

OFD can cause pain, swelling, or bowing deformity.

Epidemiology

OFD is an extremely rare bone tumour, accounting for 0.2% of all primary bone tumours. OFD typically affects patients in the first two decades of life (rarely those aged > 15 years).

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Rare familial cases have been reported {26637977}.

Pathogenesis

FISH analysis has demonstrated recurrent trisomies of chromosomes 7, 8, and 12 in a minority of spindle-shaped cells (< 10%), but not in osteoblasts or osteoclasts, in OFD {9242211; 18300815}. Causative molecular genetic aberrations have not been identified. Germline mutations involving MET (encoding a receptor tyrosine kinase) are seen in hereditary forms of OFD {26637977}. OFD has recently been identified in two cutaneous/skeletal mosaic RASopathies {32662096}.

Macroscopic appearance

OFD appears as a solid, white or yellow, gritty mass confined to the cortical bone with intact overlying periosteum; minimal involvement of the medullary cavity can occur.

Histopathology

OFD shows irregular trabeculae of woven bone with prominent osteoblastic rimming surrounded by a fibrous stroma similar to that of fibrous dysplasia. Towards the periphery of the lesion, the woven bone typically merges with pre-existing lamellar bone, giving the tumour a zonal architecture.

Immunohistochemistry

OFD may show scattered single cells positive for cytokeratin (typically CK14, CK19, CK5, and CK17) {7694513}, p63, and S100 {21674157}.

Differential diagnosis

Compared with OFD, fibrous dysplasia displays a highly cellular and collagenous stroma with woven bone lacking prominent osteoblastic rimming; immunohistochemically, keratin-positive epithelial cells are absent. GNAS gene mutations, present in most cases of fibrous dysplasia, are absent in OFD. The distinction of OFD from OFD-like adamantinoma depends on the more prominent extent of the epithelial component in OFD-like adamantinoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: imaging consistent with OFD; almost exclusive involvement of the cortex of the tibia and/or fibula; a fibro-osseous tumour with prominent osteoblastic rimming of irregular trabeculae of woven bone; absent/single cytokeratin-positive cells.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

OFD usually progresses during the first decade of life but may spontaneously regress after puberty. Therefore, surgical treatment can be limited to cases of pseudoarthrosis, impending pathological fracture, or other severe symptoms.
