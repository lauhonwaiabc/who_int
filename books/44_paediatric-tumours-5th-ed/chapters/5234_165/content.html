5.2.3.4 Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma and retiform haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma (PILA) and retiform haemangioendothelioma (RHE) are locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing lymphatic tumours with hobnail endothelia, which may represent a spectrum rather than different types.

ICD-O coding

9135/1 Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma

9136/1 Retiform haemangioendothelioma

ICD-11 coding

2B56.1 & XH4SY7 Angiosarcoma of skin & Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma

2B56.1 & XH64U8 Angiosarcoma of skin & Retiform haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: Dabska tumour; endovascular papillary angioendothelioma; hobnail haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PILA and RHE usually affect the superficial soft tissue of the lower extremities {10478659}. Cases of PILA have been reported in the oral cavity, spleen, and bone {15061715; 18090926; 29423171}.

Clinical features

PILA and RHE manifest as slow-growing, cutaneous, pink-violaceous nodules or plaques. With both PILA and RHE, a concurrent lymphatic malformation (lymphangioma) or lymphoedema may be observed {21915027; 10478659; 27730656}.

Epidemiology

With < 50 cases of each reported, PILA occurs predominantly in children and young adults, whereas RHE occurs mainly in adolescents and young adults {21238627; 9274963; 25484427; 8291650; 8764941}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

PILA and RHE are poorly defined infiltrative tumours, usually involving the dermis and subcutis. Sizes range from 10 to 400 mm, with a mean of 70 mm for PILA, which is often a more extensive lesion, whereas in most cases RHE is small and measures < 30 mm.

Histopathology

PILA is composed of columnar, matchstick-like endothelial cells. Papillary tufts of these hobnail endothelial cells surrounding hyaline cores protrude into the lumen of dilated lymphatic spaces; lymphatics may be slit-like with intraluminal glomeruloid endothelial proliferations. Proteinaceous fluid and intraluminal and stromal lymphocytes support the lymphatic phenotype and association. There should not be significant endothelial atypia or mitoses; such cases would instead be classified as angiosarcoma. RHE is also poorly circumscribed with hobnail cells and a lymphatic appearance that tends to have a densely sclerotic stroma and an arborizing pattern that resembles normal rete testis. RHE can be one component of composite haemangioendothelioma.

Both PILA and RHE stain for lymphatic markers (PILA stains for podoplanin [recognized by D2-40], PROX1, VEGFR3, or VEGFR2; RHE stains for PROX1 and occasionally D2-40) as well as endothelial markers (CD31, CD34, and ERG). SMA should be positive around the larger lymphatic spaces and may be absent within the papillary tufting {32031140; 22067331; 10478659; 19674202; 24282657}. The presence of SMA also separates these entities from angiosarcoma.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for PILA: poorly defined dissecting lymphatic spaces with intraluminal papillary columnar cell tufting, with no atypia or mitoses; the lymphatic phenotype is evidenced by morphological intraluminal proteinaceous fluid and stromal and intraluminal lymphocytes or by VEGFR3 or D2-40 immunoreactivity; for RHE: retiform arborizing vascular channels lined by hobnail endothelial cells, with no atypia or mitotic activity; variable sclerotic stroma; expression of a lymphatic phenotype marker (PROX1 or D2-40).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Both PILA and RHE may locally recur if incompletely excised {21679834}. PILA and RHE are not reported to metastasize or cause death from disease.
