5.2.2.4 Plantar/palmar fibromatoses

Definition

Palmar/plantar fibromatoses are benign fibroblastic and myofibroblastic proliferations that involve the volar aspects of the hands or the plantar aponeuroses.

ICD-O coding

8813/1 Palmar/plantar fibromatoses

ICD-11 coding

FB51.Y & XH75J5 Other specified fibroblastic disorders & Palmar/plantar-type fibromatosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: for palmar fibromatosis: Dupuytren contracture; for plantar fibromatosis: Ledderhose disease.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In the paediatric population, palmar fibromatosis arises in the volar aspect of the hand and finger, whereas plantar fibromatosis typically involves the medial aspect of the arch or anteromedial portion of the fat pad of the heel {16006806; 8989694}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation of palmar fibromatosis includes contractures, swelling, or a painless nodular mass {24163199}. Patients with plantar fibromatosis usually present with asymptomatic nodules {16006806; 8989694}. A subset of patients have synchronous or metachronous lesions {16006806; 1946077}.

Epidemiology

Although palmar fibromatosis usually affects adults, with incidence increasing with age, rare cases have been reported in children {16006806; 24163199}. Plantar fibromatosis more frequently arises in patients aged < 30 years, including children as young as 9 months {6507097; 16006806; 8989694}. There is no definitive sex predilection in paediatric patients.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Although superficial fibromatosis lacks somatic CTNNB1 or APC mutations, the WNT/β-catenin pathway is probably still involved in the pathogenesis of these tumours {11455002}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gross examination reveals small (< 20 mm), poorly defined, rubbery, cord-like nodules with a tan-white cut surface.

Histopathology

Microscopically these tumours are composed of a nodular, sometimes plexiform, arrangement of spindle cells {16006806}. Three phases of growth (proliferative, involutional, and late-stage) have been described, with progression from cellular lesions composed of parallel fascicles of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts to tumours with increased collagen deposition and low cellularity. Mitotic activity can be prominent, but atypical mitoses are absent. Perivascular chronic inflammation, microhaemorrhage, and multinucleated giant cells may be appreciated. Chondroid foci and areas of calcification characteristic of calcifying aponeurotic fibroma are absent.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: long fascicles of bland fibroblasts and myofibroblasts; variably collagenous stroma.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

When conservative measures fail, surgical excision is the treatment of choice {29310463}. Recurrence rates are typically high and dependent on the extent of surgical resection.
