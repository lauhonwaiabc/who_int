5.4.2.2 Enchondroma and enchondromatosis

Definition

Enchondroma is an intramedullary, benign hyaline cartilaginous neoplasm. Enchondromatosis is a heterogeneous skeletal disorder in which patients have multiple symptomatic, intramedullary cartilaginous neoplasms that cause bone expansion.

9220/1 Enchondromatosis

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

Not recommended: chondroma.

Subtype(s)

Enchondromatosis

Ollier disease; Maffucci syndrome

Localization

Enchondromas most commonly affect the short tubular hand bones [[Picci P, Manfrini M, Fabbri N, et al., editors. Atlas of musculoskeletal tumors and tumorlike lesions. The Rizzoli Case Archive. Cham (Switzerland): Springer International Publishing; 2014.]]. The long tubular bones are less frequently affected, and location in flat bones is uncommon.

The enchondromas in Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome affect the short and long tubular bones of the extremities, but flat bones such as the pelvis and ribs may be involved. The craniofacial bones and vertebrae are usually spared, and one side of the body is often more severely affected than the other.

Clinical features

Enchondromas of the small bones may cause swellings, pain, and fracture, whereas long bone tumours are often asymptomatic.

Ollier disease is non-hereditary and usually manifests in early childhood. Maffucci syndrome is also non-hereditary and manifests with cavernous and spindle cell haemangiomas at birth or in early childhood {7560353; 7661276}.

Imaging

Radiographs show that enchondromas of short tubular bones are usually well defined, < 50 mm, round to oval, lucent, and located centrally in the diametaphysis {15995123}. Superficial endosteal scalloping is common, expansile remodelling is frequent, and mineralization is variable but often completely absent {11061692}. Enchondromas of long tubular bones are less well defined and their size is difficult to appreciate on radiographs. Bone expansion and deep cortical scalloping, features acceptable in enchondromas of short tubular bones, suggest a more aggressive lesion in long bones {22707094}. Extraosseous tumour and cortical destruction are concerning for malignancy, irrespective of location.

In enchondromatosis involving the hands, lesions may arise in the medulla, cortex, and juxtacortical sites {1604350}. Deformity results from bone expansion, growth disturbance, and previous fracture {27603196}. In long and short bones, elongated lucencies frequently extend from the physis into the metaphysis. Tumours often undergo progressive mineralization {16995932}.

Epidemiology

Enchondromas are relatively common, accounting for 10–25% of all surgically removed benign bone tumours, but the exact incidence is unknown because many are discovered incidentally {25994192}. Enchondromas that come to clinical attention are usually found in the third and fourth decades of life (age range: 1–86 years, median: 36 years) [[Picci P, Manfrini M, Fabbri N, et al., editors. Atlas of musculoskeletal tumors and tumorlike lesions. The Rizzoli Case Archive. Cham (Switzerland): Springer International Publishing; 2014.]]. On average, patients with an enchondroma are younger than those with a chondrosarcoma. The sexes are equally affected.

Enchondromatosis is rare: the incidence of Ollier disease is 1 case per 100 000 person-years {16995932}. About 75% of cases of Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome are diagnosed before the age of 20 years {22147000}, and there is no significant ethnic or sex bias.

Etiology

Enchondromas may be caused by heterozygous somatic mutations in the isocitrate dehydrogenase genes IDH1 and IDH2 (52% of sporadic enchondromas; 90% of enchondromatosis cases) {21598255; 22057234; 22057236}. Patients with enchondromatosis are somatic mosaic for IDH1 or IDH2.

Pathogenesis

The gain-of-function mutations in IDH1 and IDH2 result in a mutant enzyme that produces the oncometabolite D-2-HG, causing epigenetic changes (i.e. altered histone methylation) {22057234; 23863747}. D-2-HG has been shown to promote chondrogenic and inhibit osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells, the presumed precursor cells of enchondroma {26046462; 26161668}.

Macroscopic appearance

The curettage specimen consists of fragments of bluish-white cartilage tissue admixed with medullary bone, with yellow foci of calcification. Resection specimens are uncommon and demonstrate a multinodular, whitish-blue glistening tumour in the medulla, with intact cortex.

Histopathology

Enchondromas are hypocellular tumours with abundant hyaline matrix and a multinodular or confluent architecture. The cells are embedded within evenly dispersed lacunae. The cytoplasm is barely visible and the nuclei are small and round with condensed chromatin. Cytological atypia and mitoses are absent. In the phalangeal bones, in children and in patients with enchondromatosis, enchondromas can be more cellular, and cytological features such as open chromatin, small nucleoli, binucleated cells, and spindling can be seen. The matrix can be myxoid. However, permeation of host bone and soft tissue invasion is never seen {10547545; 28797501}. Often a diagnosis cannot be established without proper radiological correlation.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular pathology is rarely used for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an abundance of cartilaginous matrix; an absence of cytological atypia, mitoses, cortical invasion, and soft tissue extension; tumours arising in small bones and/or in patients with enchondromatosis may show more cellularity and atypia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Enchondromas can be followed clinically or curetted, depending on symptoms. Malignant transformation is rare (< 1%), especially in children. Pain without the presence of a fracture and without radiological changes should raise the possibility of progression.

Ollier disease is associated with an increased risk of developing secondary chondrosarcomas (frequency: 5–50%) {3805090; 10637885; 3815310; 22147000}. The risk is higher in Maffucci syndrome (as high as 53%) {22147000}.
