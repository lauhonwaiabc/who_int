5.2.9.5B PEComa

Definition

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumours (PEComas) are mesenchymal tumours composed of perivascular epithelioid cells (PECs), which are characterized by the coexpression of muscle and melanogenetic markers.

ICD-O coding

8714/3 Malignant PEComa

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

Acceptable: angiomyolipoma; epithelioid angiomyolipoma; lymphangioleiomyomatosis.

Not recommended: clear cell myomelanocytic tumour; sugar tumour of the lung.

Subtype(s)

Malignant PEComa; angiomyolipoma; angiomyolipoma, epithelioid

Localization

PEComas have a wide anatomical distribution, with the genital areas, falciform ligament / ligamentum teres, kidney, and intestinal wall being frequent sites (90% of all cases reported in the literature). Rare sites are the orbit/eye, breast, pancreas, and bone.

Clinical features

Clinical presentation depends on the site. Intra-abdominal tumours may cause acute abdominal symptoms. A few cases of paediatric PEComas, especially in the prepubertal age group, show associations with other malignancies such as neuroblastoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.

Epidemiology

Although most PEComas are described in adults, an increasing number of cases have been reported in the paediatric age (range: 2–18 years), half of which are in children aged < 10 years. Paediatric PEComas have a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:2), but this predominance becomes particularly evident only in adolescence, suggesting a potential hormonal role in their pathogenesis.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Loss of heterozygosity involving the TSC2 or TSC1 (9q34) locus has been found in PEComa in the adult population {18085521}. Deletion of 16p (the location of the TSC2 gene) indicates that PEComas and angiomyolipomas are oncogenetically related as TSC2-linked neoplasms. TSC1 and TSC2 encode hamartin and tuberin, respectively. In the normal cell, the hamartin/tuberin complex acts by inhibiting the activity of mTORC1, whereas in PEComa its alteration due to the TSC1 or TSC2 mutation causes activation of the mTOR pathway.

Occasional TFE3-rearranged epithelioid but non-epithelial neoplasms contain melanin pigment; these were initially described in the kidney as melanotic Xp11 translocation renal cancer, but they have also been described in soft tissue {19065101; 26975036} and most closely fit into the category of TFE3-rearranged PEComa. Unlike typical PEComas, TFE3-rearranged PEComas retain immunolabelling for tuberin and do not harbour TSC2 gene alterations. In paediatric patients, the occurrence of PEComa in tuberous sclerosis is rarely reported, whereas TFE3-rearranged PEComas represent one third of the cases in the literature. The most frequent fusion partner gene is SFPQ.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, paediatric PEComa is a mass with a fleshy appearance, varying in diameter from 12 to 270 mm (depending on the site) but usually large with foci of haemorrhage and necrosis. A few cases occurred as abdominal multiple nodules.

Histopathology

Histologically, these tumours consist of epithelioid cells with a clear to granular cytoplasm, a round to oval centrally located nucleus, an inconspicuous nucleolus, and a typical perivascular location. In some areas, spindle cells with clear, elongated cytoplasm and distinct cell borders arranged in fascicles are present; in a few tumours (20%), these areas can represent the predominant component, especially in younger patients. Neither adipocytes nor thick-walled blood vessels are observed. Melanin pigment has been identified in the epithelioid and spindle cells in 25% of the tumours. Mitoses may be present, and foci of necrosis can be seen in 20% of these tumours.

Immunohistochemistry

A variable percentage of neoplastic cells consistently stain for melanogenetic markers (e.g. HMB45 and melan-A [MART1]), cathepsin K (a lysosomal papain-like cysteine protease), and muscle markers (especially actin and calponin, and less commonly desmin). Immunoreactivity for vimentin is usually inconspicuous.

Differential diagnosis

In childhood, the diagnosis of PEComa may be extremely challenging because of a relatively large differential diagnosis, which differs depending on the tumour site and can include clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue (also known as melanoma of soft parts), alveolar soft part sarcoma, smooth muscle tumours, MiT-family translocation renal cell carcinoma, clear cell sarcoma of the kidney, gastrointestinal stromal tumour, and metastatic melanoma {25804448; 22703822}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a variable proportion of spindle and/or epithelioid cells with abundant clear to eosinophilic cytoplasm, round nucleoli, and pinpoint nucleoli in intimate association with thin-walled capillary vessels; coexpression of melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

Desirable: TFE3 expression (if smooth muscle markers are negative).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Clinically malignant PEComas are generally large (diameter > 50 mm) and show infiltrative growth with high cellularity, remarkable cytological atypia, conspicuous mitoses, and necrosis {16327428; 30001237; 22619565}. However, the prognosis of paediatric PEComas (apart from rare frankly sarcomatous neoplasms) remains not easily predictable, and most of them – including those with unfavourable histopathological features – display an indolent clinical course. In association with reports of cases with lymph node involvement or with synchronous multiple nodules, this raises the question of whether such cases may represent the expression of a multicentric disease rather than metastases, analogously to angiomyolipomas arising in lymph nodes in the context of tuberous sclerosis {16327428}. Although surgical resection remains the gold standard of therapy for PEComas where possible, genetic findings demonstrating two different pathogenetic mechanisms have suggested the use of mTOR inhibitors to treat clinically aggressive PEComas with TSC2 alterations, but not those with TFE3 rearrangement {20871214; 22456611; 25651471}.
