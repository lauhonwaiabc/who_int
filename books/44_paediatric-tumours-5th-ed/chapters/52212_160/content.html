5.2.2.12 Infantile fibrosarcoma

Definition

Infantile fibrosarcoma (IFS) is a fibroblastic tumour, most frequently occurring in infancy, that is often locally aggressive but infrequently metastasizes. Genetically, IFS is characterized by an ETV6::NTRK3 fusion or other fusions and/or alterations leading to oncogenic activation of tyrosine kinase signalling.

ICD-O coding

8814/3 Infantile fibrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH7BC6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Infantile fibrosarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: congenital fibrosarcoma; cellular congenital mesoblastic nephroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

IFS most commonly involves the soft tissues of the extremities, followed by the trunk and the head/neck. Tumours in intra-abdominal, retroperitoneal, or visceral sites are less frequent but do occur {974993; 21843073; 25150723; 32669612}. Analogous tumours in the kidney are designated as cellular (or occasionally mixed-type) congenital mesoblastic nephroma {9823307; 29915264}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a painless mass or exophytic nodule; rapid growth may occur {974993}. Approximately one third of cases are congenital {974993}. Imaging, typically ultrasound in young children, demonstrates a heterogeneous enhancing mass with relatively nonspecific characteristics. Dilated vessels and haemorrhage may be present, resembling a vascular tumour or malformation; in these cases the patients may (rarely) present with Kasabach–Merritt phenomenon {16918626; 24706181}.

Epidemiology

Studies of paediatric patients show that the median age is 3–4 months {974993; 19917847; 30585824}, with the majority (75%) of cases occurring in the first year of life. Fewer than 10% of cases occur in older children and adults {28877062; 30585824; 30520818}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Oncogenesis of IFS is through the activation of kinase signalling, most commonly as a result of in-frame gene fusions or (less frequently) through activating mutations {29915264}. The most frequent genetic alteration is the classic ETV6::NTRK3 gene fusion resulting from the t(12;15)(p13;q25) chromosomal translocation; other NTRK3 fusion partners have been reported, including EML4::NTRK3 fusions {29099503; 30585824}. Additionally, alternative fusions involving other tyrosine kinase genes (including NTRK1, NTRK2, RET, MET, and RAF1) have been described, with a variety of fusion partners {30585824; 28877062; 29915264; 28510278; 31994201}. BRAF fusions, complex deletions, and point mutations have been described, all probably resulting in the constitutive activation of BRAF {28877062; 29915264}, a downstream effector molecule in the MAPK pathway. Similar genetic aberrations are present in cellular congenital mesoblastic nephroma, which also has a substantial morphological overlap with IFS {9823307; 29099503; 29915264; 31994201}. IFS / congenital mesoblastic nephromas with and without ETV6::NTRK3 fusions show the same pattern of receptor tyrosine kinase activation, and they cluster together by unsupervised hierarchical gene expression cluster analysis, which suggests a common pathogenesis {22374738}. Preliminary studies of RAF1 tumours with CD34 and/or S100 expression also cluster with BRAF-altered IFS-like tumours by RNA unsupervised hierarchical cluster analysis, again suggestive of related entities and/or a common pathogenesis (see provisional entity section Paediatric NTRK-rearranged spindle cell neoplasm) {30276917}.

Macroscopic appearance

The size of tumours varies, although they can be quite large relative to the patient’s size; the median size is about 50–60 mm, but tumours can range from 10 to > 150 mm. The gross appearance is typically of a poorly circumscribed tumour, infiltrative into the surrounding soft tissues. A subset of tumours may have a thin, fibrous pseudocapsule {561651; 30585824}.

Histopathology

IFS may demonstrate a spectrum of histological patterns across cases and within a given case. Most frequently, IFS is a cellular, monomorphic neoplasm composed of primitive, ovoid/round to spindled cells, arranged either in compact, haphazard sheets or in long fascicles. Often tumours demonstrate a prominent ectatic or haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern. The background stroma may range from myxoid to heavily collagenous. A subset of cases have decreased cellularity with a collagenized matrix; these tumours may resemble myofibromatosis and/or fibromatosis {22420727; 30585824}. Prominent chronic inflammation may also be present {974993}. Mitotic counts range widely and are without prognostic significance; no atypical mitotic forms are present {974993; 8159611; 19917847; 30585824}. Necrosis also may be present, without prognostic implications.

Immunohistochemistry

The IFS immunoprofile is nonspecific, with variable expression of SMA, S100, CD34, and (rarely) desmin {22420727; 30585824}. Those tumours harbouring NTRK gene rearrangements typically show expression for pan-TRK antibodies {29863809; 29683818}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Gene fusions or activating alterations involving receptor tyrosine kinase (e.g. ETV6::NTRK3) or the downstream effector molecule (BRAF) fusions or activating alterations may be demonstrated by various molecular modalities {10895816; 11684968; 30585824}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primitive ovoid/round cell or spindle cell tumour, often arranged in sheets or fascicles; haemangiopericytoma-like vessels and/or mixed chronic inflammation.

Desirable: ETV6::NTRK3 or other reported fusions or activating alterations of receptor tyrosine kinase pathway genes (e.g. NTRK1, NTRK2, NTRK3, RET, MET, BRAF).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

IFS is a locally aggressive, infrequently metastatic tumour with an overall favourable prognosis. With standard treatment regimens, including combinations of surgery and/or cytotoxic chemotherapy, the 10-year overall survival rate is approximately 90% {974993; 561651; 19917847}. Spontaneous tumour regression is rare. Local recurrence rates range from 25% to 40%, with incomplete resection associated with an increased risk of recurrence {19917847; 30585824}. However, given the size and location of many tumours, complete surgical excision can entail significant morbidity. The proportion of patients developing metastasis is approximately 8–15% {30585824; 19917847; 561651}. Recent advances in targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapies are likely to change the clinical course of IFS and the treatment regimens employed.
