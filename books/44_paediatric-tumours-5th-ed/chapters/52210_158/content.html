5.2.2.10 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour (IMT) is a rarely metastasizing neoplasm composed of myofibroblastic and fibroblastic spindle cells with an infiltrate of plasma cells, lymphocytes, and/or eosinophils.

ICD-O coding

8825/1 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

8825/1 Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH66Z0 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Myofibroblastic tumour, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: plasma cell granuloma; inflammatory pseudotumour; inflammatory myofibrohistiocytic proliferation; omental-mesenteric myxoid hamartoma; inflammatory fibrosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma

Localization

IMT most frequently involves abdominal soft tissues (mesentery, omentum, retroperitoneum, pelvis), followed by lung, mediastinum, head and neck, gastrointestinal tract, and genitourinary tract {7611533; 17938159; 11381424; 15577680; 31690781}. Unusual locations include limbs, pancreas, liver, and CNS {7611533; 17938159; 8847061}. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma (EIMS) predominates intra-abdominally {21164297; 31933992}.

Clinical features

Abdominal tumours may cause vague abdominal pain, gastrointestinal obstruction, or bleeding. Pulmonary IMTs are associated with cough, chest pain, and dyspnoea {2239820}. Some patients develop a constitutional inflammatory syndrome of fever, malaise, weight loss, anaemia, thrombocytosis, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia, and elevated ESR and C-reactive protein {17938159; 15602716; 7611533; 2311072}.

Epidemiology

IMT primarily affects children and young adults {17414097; 7611533; 17938159; 31690781}. It may occur in infants. The EIMS subtype is rare in children {21164297; 27460384; 28415158; 27874193; 28787324}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The majority of IMTs are characterized by recurrent gene rearrangements (involving chromosome band 2p23) that fuse the 3′ kinase region of the ALK gene with various partner genes. The fusion genes lead to oncogenic kinase gene activation with ALK kinase overexpression {11485898; 11406658; 11684952; 10383129; 10934142}. More than 15 different genes providing a strong promoter and oligomerization domain have been identified as ALK fusion partners, including TPM3, TPM4, CLTC, CARS1 (CARS), ATIC, SEC31A (SEC31L1), PPFIBP1, DCTN1, EML4, PRKAR1A, LMNA, TFG, FN1, HNRNPA1, RANBP2, and RRBP1 (the last two of which associate with EIMS) {11485898; 18701132; 13679433; 10383129; 10934142; 21430068; 16984614; 28504207; 24875859; 21164297; 27874193}.

One third of IMTs harbour ROS1 or NTRK3 gene fusions, which are enriched in infants and children {24875859; 25612511; 27259007; 25723109; 26647767; 30550870; 31690781}. Rare cases have RET or PDGFRB gene rearrangements {25723109; 24875859}.

Macroscopic appearance

IMT is a nodular or multinodular, circumscribed mass of variable size, from 10 mm to > 200 mm. The cut surface is tan, whorled, fleshy, or myxoid with variable haemorrhage, necrosis, and calcification {7611533; 17414097; 17938159}.

Histopathology

IMT is characterized by a proliferation of spindled and elongated fibroblastic-myofibroblastic cells, with variation in cellularity, stroma, and inflammatory infiltrate, featuring three basic patterns that are often combined in a single tumour (#18108Table 6.05) {17414097; 7611533; 17938159}. Scattered ganglion-like cells with abundant cytoplasm, vesicular nuclei, and prominent nucleoli are frequent. Dystrophic calcification and osseous metaplasia may be present. Necrosis is uncommon and mitotic activity is variable. EIMS shows epithelioid/histiocytoid cells with prominent nucleoli and amphophilic/eosinophilic cytoplasm, often admixed with neutrophils in an abundant myxoid stroma {18701132; 21164297}.

IMT displays variable staining for SMA, MSA, calponin, and desmin. Focal keratin immunoreactivity is seen (in ~30% of cases). ALK immunohistochemistry is positive (membranous, cytoplasmic, perinuclear, or dot-like) in 50–60% of cases and correlates with the presence of ALK gene rearrangement {12218210; 11395553; 11406658; 11684952; 17938159; 21164297; 27874193}. Highly sensitive ALK antibody clones (e.g. 5A4, D5F3) may improve detection {21430068; 26647767}. ROS1-rearranged IMT shows cytoplasmic expression of ROS1 {26647767; 25612511}.

Cytology

Cytology consists of bland spindle cells with oval nuclei and small distinct nucleoli in a background of lymphocytes and plasma cells. Ganglion-like polygonal cells can be seen {20730898}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular assays are recommended for diagnosis {11406658}. FISH for ALK gene rearrangements is widely used; however, occasional false negative results occur in the setting of abundant inflammation or in the presence of intrachromosomal inversions, such as EML4::ALK fusion {28490045}. RNA-based fusion assays have shown increased sensitivity. In ALK-negative cases, FISH or fusion assays are recommended for detecting ROS1, NTRK3, or other gene fusions {26647767; 24875859; 27259007; 25723109}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: loose or compact fascicles of spindle cells with a prominent inflammatory infiltrate and a variable fibrous or myxoid stroma; expression of ALK (in as many as 60% of cases)

Desirable: demonstration of rearrangements of ALK, ROS1, NTRK3, or other genes.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for IMT in the paediatric population is favourable, even in cases that are unresectable and ALK-negative by immunohistochemistry {32007712; 32584482}. Distant metastases are rare. It is uncertain whether the aggressive behaviour of EIMS reported in adults is also seen in children {18701132; 21164297}. IMTs with kinase fusions involving ALK, ROS1, and NTRK3 are targets for kinase inhibitor therapy, with promising results reported in children {24875859; 28787259; 29286567; 30550870; 30697903}.
