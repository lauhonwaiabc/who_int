8.4.1.1 Haemangioma of placenta

Definition

Haemangioma of placenta is a benign nodular lesion composed of numerous capillaries.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F31.2 & XH5AW4 Benign non-mesenchymal neoplasms of uterus, other parts & Haemangioma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: chorangioma; chorioangioma.

Not recommended: chorangioendothelioma; angiomyxoma; fibroangiomyxoma.

Subtype(s)

Some authors have classified lesions as angiomatous, cellular, and degenerative [[Marchetti AA. A consideration of certain types of benign tumors of the placenta. Surg Gynecol Obstet. 1939;68:733–74.]], or as capillary, cavernous, endotheliomatous, fibrosing, and fibromatous [[Heerema-McKenney A, Popek EJ, De Paepe ME, et al. Diagnostic pathology: placenta. 1st ed. Philadelphia (PA): Elsevier; 2015. Section II-8, pp. 6–7.]], but the behaviour is the same and relates to lesion size.

Localization

These haemangiomas occur in the placenta. They are usually subchorionic but may be peripheral.

Clinical features

Placental haemangiomas occur more frequently in patients with multiple gestations; maternal age ≥ 30 years; and with maternal hypertension, pre-eclampsia, or diabetes mellitus {10987255; 12747233}.

Epidemiology

Haemangioma of the placenta is the most common placental tumour. It is often found incidentally, occurring in as many as 1% of placentas {25906437}.

Etiology

Placental haemangioma may be a hamartoma rather than a true neoplasm, but this is unresolved {25906437}.

An association with hypoxic placental findings, increased frequency at high altitudes, and location in relatively hypoxic areas of the placenta, have been cited as evidence of hypoxia as the underlying stimulus {26992689}.

Pathogenesis

The molecular genetic basis of placental haemangiomas is unknown and there are currently no molecular diagnostic markers for use in clinical practice. A recent study of large chorangiomas failed to show any large-scale genetic aberrations such as duplications or deletions {30428272}. Occasional case reports have shown microdeletions at 2q13 and 7p21.1 {17203486}, and H19 hypermethylation in a case of Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome {21955307} in the placental tumours.

Macroscopic appearance

Placental haemangiomas are usually present in a subchorionic or marginal location. They may bulge above the fetal surface {20594143}. They may not be appreciated grossly if small. They may be single or multiple, and they are well circumscribed and expansile. On cut section, they are red-tan and firm, and they may appear haemorrhagic. Some may resemble fibromas or have myxomatous change {20594143}. If infarction has occurred, the lesion may be white.

Histopathology

Placental haemangiomas arise from stem villi, and usually a layer of trophoblast is seen surrounding the nodule. The angiomatous pattern is the most common, composed of numerous capillaries embedded in a fibroconnective stroma composed of fibroblasts, macrophages, and scant collagenized tissue {26992689}, resembling cutaneous capillary malformation. Cellular and degenerative subtypes have been described. Degenerative changes, such as necrosis, hyalinization, haemosiderin, and calcification, may be seen {20594143}. The lesion is distinct from adjacent chorionic villi. The endothelial lining stains for usual vascular markers such as CD31, CD34, GLUT1, and von Willebrand factor (factor VIII–related antigen). Pericytes around each capillary will stain for MSA {20594143}. It is not uncommon for peripheral trophoblast proliferation to be present in this benign lesion, leading to the misnomer “chorangiocarcinoma” in the literature. “Chorangioma with trophoblast proliferation” is more reflective of the biology of this pattern. Rare cases of chorangioma showing atypical features such as increased cellularity and mitotic activity have been described, termed “atypical cellular chorangioma” in the literature, but these have behaved in a benign manner {25998317}.

Cytology

Cytology is not used in clinical practice for this lesion.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: discrete lesion(s); well circumscribed; subchorionic or peripheral location; bulging above the fetal surface; numerous capillaries in a stroma surrounded by trophoblastic cells.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lesions < 40 mm are essentially asymptomatic {25906437}. Intermediate-sized lesions have been associated with intrauterine growth restriction {2247373}. Large lesions (≥ 90 mm) have been associated with fetal heart failure, hydrops fetalis, prematurity, and intrauterine fetal demise {8894632; 8476479; 11694744}. They do not have malignant potential.
