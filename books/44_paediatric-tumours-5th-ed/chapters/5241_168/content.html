5.2.4.1 Myofibroma and myopericytoma

Definition

Myofibroma (MF) and myopericytoma (MP) are spindle cell neoplasms with perivascular myoid features.

ICD-O coding

8824/0 Solitary myofibroma

8824/1 Multicentric myofibromatosis

8824/1 Generalized myofibromatosis (with visceral involvement)

8824/0 Myopericytoma

8824/0 Myopericytomatosis

ICD-11 coding

2E84.Y & XH0953 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of other specified sites & Myofibroma

2E84.Y & XH2HE9 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of other specified sites & Myopericytoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: infantile myofibroma; infantile myofibromatosis; infantile haemangiopericytoma.

Subtype(s)

Solitary myofibroma; multicentric myofibromatosis; generalized myofibromatosis (with visceral involvement); myopericytomatosis

Localization

The head and neck region is the most common site for MF, followed by the trunk, limbs, viscera, and bone {22486319; 7284977}.

For MP, the most common site is the lower extremities, followed by the upper extremities, head and neck, and trunk {16330949; 28505006}.

Clinical features

Patients with MF or MP present with one or more painless, superficial or deep, often reddish nodule(s) {22420727; 16330949; 24894456}.

Epidemiology

MF is the most frequent fibroblastic tumour in children and adolescents. MF is diagnosed in patients of all ages, from birth to adulthood, but most frequently occurs within the first 2 years of life {1946077; 22486319}.

MP is a rare neoplasm, occurring at any age {16330949; 28505006; 31350225}.

Etiology

In familial forms of MF, germline mutations of PDGFRB are inherited in an autosomal dominant manner with incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity {23217896; 15054839; 23731542; 28286173}.

There are reports that MP can be associated with EBV infection in immunocompromised children {19675451; 22175892}.

Pathogenesis

Familial MF and sporadic MF are associated with germline and/or somatic PDGFRB gain-of-function mutations {27776010; 28334876; 31017643; 23731537}. These mutations lead to the activation of the PDGF pathway, providing a possible therapeutic target for tyrosine kinase inhibitors {31017643; 28183292}. Mutations in NOTCH3 and in NDRG4 are also reported. A PTPRG mutation could be a modifier contributing to variable expressivity in patients with inherited PDGFRB mutations {23731542; 25241110; 25158255}. The activation of PDGFRB may also be secondary to a chromosomal rearrangement {31645346}.

A subset of MFs harbour rearrangements of SRF, combined most often with RELA but also with NCOA2, CITED1, CITED2, and NFKBIE {28248815; 33021523}.

Mutations of PDGFRB and rearrangements of NOTCH genes are also reported in MP {28505006; 32604167}.

Macroscopic appearance

MF and MP are nodular lesions ranging from < 10 mm to 100 mm in size. Most are well circumscribed, but some show infiltrative borders {7284977; 22486319; 1946077; 16330949}.

Histopathology

MF has a nodular growth pattern and is characterized by a biphasic zonation appearance associated with a biphasic cell population {22420727; 1946077}. Some areas consist of fascicles and whorls of spindle-shaped cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and elongated nuclei with fine chromatin; these areas alternate with areas showing a haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern composed of more primitive, round to polygonal cells, with small amounts of amphophilic cytoplasm, hyperchromatic nuclei, and indistinct cell borders. These cells often form nodules bulging into vascular lumina. There is no marked atypia, and the rate of mitosis is variable. Areas of infarction and calcification can be present {16394283; 7284977; 22486319; 24921644}. In some cases, a prominent/predominant haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern is observed {19006426; 8067513}.

MP is characterized by a proliferation of bland round to ovoid cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm arranged in a concentric perivascular pattern. Many of the lesions have a haemangiopericytoma-like pattern. Mitoses are infrequent and necrosis is absent. Morphological overlap with angioleiomyoma and MF is reported {22420724; 16330949; 16394283; 28505006}. Malignant MP displays nuclear atypia, a high rate of mitosis, and areas of necrosis {12405913}.

MF and MP cells express SMA and may stain for desmin and h-caldesmon.

The main differential diagnoses of MF are infantile fibrosarcoma, infantile spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma, vascular tumours, solitary fibrous tumour, desmoid tumour, and nodular fasciitis.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of SRF rearrangement is helpful in the diagnosis of cellular MF {28248815}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for MF: a biphasic pattern with myoid cells forming fascicles and more primitive cells associated with vascular structures; no atypia; for MP: condensation of tumour cells around numerous vessels.

Desirable: for MF: identification of PDGFRB mutation in challenging cases, and SRF rearrangement in cellular cases; for MP: identification of PDGFRB mutation in challenging cases.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Solitary and multicentric forms of MF have a good prognosis, whereas generalized forms have a poor prognosis. Solitary forms show a low rate of recurrence. Spontaneous regression is observed in multicentric and generalized forms {24894456; 22486319; 22038698; 8597844}.

MP has a good prognosis, with a low rate of recurrence after excision. Rare cases of malignant MP have been reported {12405913; 16330949}.
