16.0.0.3 Ganglioneuroblastoma, intermixed

Definition

Intermixed ganglioneuroblastoma (GNBi) is a form of peripheral neuroblastic tumour transitional between neuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma, composed of Schwannian stroma containing scattered well-defined foci of neuroblastic cells at various stages of maturation.

ICD-O coding

9490/3 Ganglioneuroblastoma, intermixed

ICD-11 coding

2A00.21 & XH0RB7 Mixed neuronal-glial tumours & Ganglioneuroblastoma, intermixed

Related terminology

Acceptable: Schwannian stroma–rich intermixed tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GNBi occurs most frequently in the abdominal cavity / retroperitoneum (usually adrenal) but also in the mediastinum, cervical region, and pelvis {28465480}.

Clinical features

Patients with GNBi often present with an asymptomatic mass {27465021; 29369484; 19530234} and generally lack the elevated urine catecholamines seen in patients with neuroblastoma.

Imaging

Cross-sectional imaging shows an encapsulated and well-circumscribed mass. MRI diffusion-weighting characteristics may help distinguish ganglioneuroma/GNBi from neuroblastoma {20462716; 27340927}. Tumours are less likely to be MIBG-avid than are neuroblastomas {27465021; 29369484}.

Epidemiology

Ganglioneuroma and GNBi together account for approximately 25% of peripheral neuroblastic tumours {27465021; 29369484; 19530234}. Compared with neuroblastomas, GNBi tumours occur in older children (median patient age: 4–7 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Peripheral neuroblastic tumours originate from primordial neural crest cells that normally give rise to the adrenal medulla and sympathetic ganglia.

Histologically and genetically low-risk neuroblastomas (see section Neuroblastoma) may spontaneously mature into GNBi {10421272; 8618605}. MYCN oncogene amplification is rare (< 1%) {19530234; 22744966}. Segmental chromosomal aberrations are also infrequent, but the neuroblastic cells may harbour numerical chromosomal aberrations. The DNA content is frequently diploid {19530234} because of the abundance of stromal cells.

Macroscopic appearance

GNBi is a solid, well-defined mass displaying a homogeneous pale-grey cut surface.

Histopathology

Well-circumscribed lacunar areas of neuroblastic cells embedded in varying amounts of neuropil are randomly distributed in ganglioneuromatous tissue composed of Schwannian cells and maturing/mature ganglionic cells. The neuroblastic cells span various stages of differentiation and are dominated by differentiating neuroblasts. No pushing borders are encountered between the areas of neuroblastic cells and the ganglioneuromatous component {10421272}.

Cytology

Smears and touch imprints show a heterogeneous population of ganglionic cells, small neuroblasts, and spindly Schwannian cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a Schwannian stroma–rich tumour; well-demarcated microscopic foci of neuropil and mainly differentiating neuroblastic cells; a ganglioneuromatous component constituting > 50% of the microscopically evaluable tumour volume.

Desirable: immunohistochemistry for synaptophysin and S100 aids in distinguishing neuropil and Schwann cells (in selected cases).

Staging

All neuroblastic tumours are staged according to the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group (INRG) Staging System (INRGSS) {19047290}.

Prognosis and prediction

According to the INRG risk-stratification system (see #22105Table 4.03, in the section Neuroblastoma section), GNBi is usually locoregional and classified as low-risk, with extremely rare metastatic spread {27465021; 29369484; 20462716}. The tumours are generally considered not sensitive to chemotherapy {27465021}.
