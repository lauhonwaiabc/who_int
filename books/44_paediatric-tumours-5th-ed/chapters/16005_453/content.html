16.0.0.5 Ganglioneuroma

Definition

Ganglioneuroma is a benign sympathetic nervous system tumour composed mainly of Schwann cells, with a lesser component of ganglion cells, representing the differentiated mature tumour in the peripheral neuroblastic tumour continuum.

ICD-O coding

9490/0 Ganglioneuroma

9490/0 Maturing ganglioneuroma

9490/0 Mature ganglioneuroma

ICD-11 coding

2A00.21 & XH03L9 Mixed neuronal-glial tumours & Ganglioneuroma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Maturing ganglioneuroma; mature ganglioneuroma

Localization

Unlike neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroma occurs more commonly in the thorax than in the adrenal gland {11346873; 27465021; 29369484}. Tumours at unusual sites may represent matured metastases of a previous neuroblastoma.

Clinical features

The majority of ganglioneuromas are incidentally discovered during imaging for unrelated reasons {25501841}. Most are non-functioning {11346873; 27465021}.

Epidemiology

The exact incidence rate of this rare tumour is unknown. It mainly affects older children and young adults (median age: 6 years) {18375900; 19530234; 29369484}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The Schwann cell origin is controversial. It is postulated that Schwann cells are recruited into the tumour by biologically favourable neuroblasts that produce factors critical for their proliferation and development {10421272; 15743800; 9788610}. In turn, Schwann cells can secrete antiproliferative and differentiation factors crucial for neuronal maturation. This process is very unlikely to occur in tumours with unfavourable histology that cannot differentiate {8618605}.

Macroscopic appearance

Ganglioneuroma is a firm, solid, unencapsulated white mass, usually with a whorled cut surface and no haemorrhagic or necrotic foci.

Histopathology

Considered different from its soft tissue counterpart, ganglioneuroma contains abundant bland Schwann cells (> 50% of tumour volume) arranged in sheets or fascicles with individually dispersed or clustered maturing and/or mature ganglion cells. Maturing ganglion cells vary in size and shape and may be multinucleated. Rarely, differentiating neuroblasts are present, but they are not nested and are without neuropil. Mature ganglion cells are covered by satellite cells. A lymphocytic infiltrate is common.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a dominant Schwann cell component; maturing and/or mature ganglion cells; no mitoses.

Desirable: no nests of neuroblasts; no neuropil (naked neurites); no nodules of neuroblasts.

Staging

The International Neuroblastoma Risk Group Staging System (INRGSS) is used for all neuroblastic tumours {19047290}, with clinical staging based on image-defined risk factors. Ganglioneuromas are locoregional and low-stage {19530234; 27465021; 29369484}.

Prognosis and prediction

Ganglioneuroma is categorized as having a favourable histology {10421272}. The prognosis is excellent for both subtypes, even with incomplete excision {11745303; 25501841; 27465021; 29369484}.
