12.2.1.2 Sinonasal angiofibroma

Definition

Sinonasal angiofibroma is a locally aggressive, variably cellular fibrovascular neoplasm arising in the nasopharynx of boys and young men.

ICD-O coding

9160/0 Sinonasal angiofibroma

ICD-11 coding

2E90.6 & XH1JJ2 Benign neoplasm of nasopharynx & Angiofibroma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiofibroma; juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours arise in the nasopharynx or posterolateral wall of the roof of the nasal cavity {22588045; 23483486; 4355257}.

Clinical features

The classic triad of nasal obstruction, epistaxis, and nasopharyngeal mass is seen in most patients, often over a prolonged symptom duration {23483486; 24381014}. Biopsy is contraindicated because of potentially profuse bleeding. With tumour progression, facial deformity, deafness, diplopia, and proptosis may be seen {22588045; 4355257}. Imaging studies, used for anatomical staging {6087710; 20566910; 2538688; 8630204; 16550949; 24381014}, reveal a soft tissue–density vascularized mass, with anterior bowing of the posterior wall of the maxillary antrum (Holman–Miller sign, considered pathognomonic) {4355257; 22588045}. Bone destruction and intracranial extension may be seen {4355257; 23483486; 21572079; 23553370}. Angiography is used for presurgical embolization {21819885}.

Epidemiology

Sinonasal angiofibroma is rare, with an incidence of 3.7 cases per 1 million person-years in the at-risk population {22588045; 23483486; 17364367}. The tumour develops exclusively in adolescent boys and young men {6312826; 4355257; 23483486; 28508272}, and if identified in female patients, the patients should be evaluated for testicular feminization.

Etiology

Hormone dependency is correlated with tumour growth at puberty onset and strong AR expression {4355257; 6312826; 9831211; 28508272}.

Pathogenesis

Somatic mutations in the gene encoding β-catenin (CTNNB1) are seen in 75% of tumours, although immunohistochemical nuclear localization of β-catenin is seen in most tumours {11238055}. These findings and the higher incidence in patients with familial adenomatous polyposis support the importance of aberrant WNT signalling as a common event in these tumours {26572152}. Gain of the X chromosome and loss of the Y chromosome have been documented {12883689; 14647927}.

Macroscopic appearance

Polypoid to lobulated tumours can be as large as 220 mm (average: 40 mm).

Histopathology

There are numerous blood vessels of various sizes, from slit-like capillaries to irregularly dilated and branching vessels. The vascular walls may be thin to focally or continuously thickened and muscular. No elastic tissue is identified in the vessels except in the feeding arteries. The stroma is composed of bipolar or stellate fibroblastic cells with plump, vesicular, spindled nuclei and indistinct nucleoli. Isolated multinucleated stellate stromal giant cells may be seen. Mitoses are inconspicuous. The stromal cellularity varies from loose and oedematous to densely collagenized. Mast cells are common. Embolized tumours show areas of necrosis and intravascular foreign material. AR blocker–treated tumours are hypocellular, with increased stromal collagen {1318484}. Vascular markers highlight the vessels and SMA in the smooth muscle vascular wall, while the stromal cells only show strong nuclear expression of AR and β-catenin {9831211; 11238055; 28508272}. EBV and KSHV/HHV8 are absent {20614308}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Aberrant nuclear β-catenin expression is seen in most tumours {11238055}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: male patient; nasopharynx/posterior nasal cavity location; numerous vessel types and sizes, some with muscular walls; stromal stellate fibroblasts with variably collagenized stroma.

Desirable: stromal cell nuclear β-catenin and AR immunoreactivity.

Staging

Radiographic and clinical staging systems are used to guide treatment and assess recurrence risk {6087710; 20566910; 2538688; 8630204; 16550949; 24381014}.

Prognosis and prediction

The course is characterized by recurrence in 5–25% of cases; sometimes multiple recurrences occur {23483486; 24381014; 23553370}. Prognosis depends on the size and extent of the tumour and the completeness of surgical resection {20566910; 21572079}. Malignant sarcomatous transformation has been reported in association with radiotherapy, as has metastasis {6312826}. Spontaneous regression after puberty may rarely occur {6312826}.
