2.3.3.4 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTL) is an aggressive type of extranodal cytotoxic T-cell lymphoma characterized by hepatosplenic and bone marrow involvement without lymphadenopathy.

ICD-O coding

9716/3 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A90.8 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: erythrophagocytic Tγ lymphoma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The spleen, liver, and bone marrow are involved, whereas lymph nodes are usually not involved {12907441; 24128129}.

Clinical features

Patients with HSTL typically present with hepatosplenomegaly, systemic symptoms, and marked thrombocytopenia, often with anaemia and leukopenia. Peripheral blood involvement is uncommon at presentation but may occur later {12907441; 24128129}. Haemophagocytic syndrome may occur {15167351}.

Epidemiology

HSTL is a rare form of lymphoma, reported in both the Western Hemisphere and Asian countries {18626005}. It accounts for < 5% of peripheral T-cell lymphomas in the adult population. Peak incidence occurs in adolescents and young adults (in a review of 143 cases of paediatric peripheral T-cell lymphomas, HSTL accounted for 14%) {27270301}, with a male predominance {12907441; 8944363; 24128129; 25395120; 29921021; 32452165}.

Etiology

HSTL may arise in the setting of chronic immunosuppression (most commonly long-term immunosuppressive therapy for solid organ transplantation {10761449; 12907441; 17509984}) or in children affected by Crohn disease, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, or autoimmune hepatitis treated by azathioprine or mercaptopurine, often in association with TNF inhibitors {17255842; 17480018; 24621284; 23032984; 28038706; 27516526; 29984520; 24374875}.

Pathogenesis

HSTL derives from cytotoxic T cells of the innate immune system, usually of the γδ phenotype {8943863}. Isochromosome 7q is common {11807981}. The commonly gained region mapped at 7q21.12 associates with increased expression of the multidrug resistance gene ABCB1 {25057852}. Trisomy 8 may also be present. Gene expression profiling reveals a distinctive signature unifying γδ and αβ HSTL {22510872}. Mutations in SETD2 and in STAT5B are common, whereas those in STAT3 are uncommon {24947020; 28122867}. EBV is generally absent.

Macroscopic appearance

The liver and spleen show diffuse enlargement without an identifiable mass lesion.

Histopathology

HSTL cells are typically monotonous, with medium-sized nuclei, small inconspicuous nucleoli, and a rim of pale cytoplasm. They involve the cords and sinuses of the splenic red pulp and the sinusoids of the liver, and they show an almost exclusively intrasinusoidal distribution in the bone marrow. Their identification often requires immunohistochemistry or flow cytometry. Pleomorphism, large cell changes, or blastic changes may be seen, especially with disease progression {12907441; 24128129}.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells have a mature, non-activated cytotoxic T-cell phenotype, with aberrations. They are CD3+, CD5−, CD4−, CD8−/+, CD56+/−, and usually TCRγδ+ {8943863; 12907441}. A minority of cases are TCRαβ+ {11224598; 24128129}. TIA1 is positive, with granzyme B and perforin expressed in 30–40% of cases.

Flow cytometry shows a CD2+, CD3+, CD7+/−, CD4−, CD5−, and CD8− profile. NK cell–associated markers (CD11b, CD16, CD56) are frequently expressed {29921021}.

Differential diagnosis

HSTL differs from T cell–type large granular lymphocytic leukaemia. The latter is a rare indolent neoplasm in children with a more mature lymphocytic appearance and a CD8+, CD57+, granzyme B+ phenotype. Moreover, T cell–type large granular lymphocytic leukaemia exhibits a diffuse interstitial infiltrative pattern in bone marrow, without prominent infiltration of sinuses {17509984; 24128129; 29921021}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a characteristic pattern of extranodal disease, with sinusoidal localization of lymphoma cells in bone marrow, liver, or spleen; uniform small to medium-sized lymphoma cells; cytotoxic T-cell phenotype, with TIA1 expression.

Desirable: expression of TCRγδ (usually); expression of TCRαβ (sometimes); EBV negativity; isochromosome 7q; trisomy 8.

Staging

Lymphoma staging follows the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

The course is aggressive and often fatal {12907441; 29984520; 32452165}. Among children with peripheral T-cell lymphomas, those with HSTL have the poorest outcome {25395120}. Patients may initially respond to chemotherapy, but relapse is almost certain. Long-term disease-free survival has been reported in children treated with multiagent chemotherapy followed by allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation {23107915; 25234166; 16282893; 29921021}.
