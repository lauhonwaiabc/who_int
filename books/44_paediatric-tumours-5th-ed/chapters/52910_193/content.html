5.2.9.10 Liposarcoma

Definition

Liposarcomas are a heterogeneous group of mesenchymal neoplasms with adipocytic differentiation and varying biological behaviour ranging from locally aggressive to metastasizing.

Related terminology

See the fifth-edition WHO classification of soft tissue and bone tumours.

Subtype(s)

Atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma (ALT/WDLPS); dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS); myxoid liposarcoma (MLPS); pleomorphic liposarcoma (PLPS); myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma (MPLPS)

Localization

The most common site of involvement of ALT/WDLPS is the extremities, followed by the head and neck, trunk, mediastinum, and retroperitoneum {20028212; 28705709; 30850231}. MLPS usually involves the extremities, trunk, head and neck, and abdominal regions {19194281; 30850231}. MPLPS has a predilection for the mediastinum; other reported locations include the thigh, head and neck, perineum, abdomen, and back {19194281; 24970901; 26647907; 28160093}. PLPS most often involves the extremities, trunk, and retroperitoneum {15371941}.

Clinical features

Liposarcomas of all types generally manifest as large (> 50 mm), deep-seated, painless soft tissue masses {19194281; 24970901; 30850231; 30853315; 31501988}.

Epidemiology

Liposarcomas represent < 2% of all paediatric soft tissue malignancies. Nearly 90% occur in the second decade of life, with a female predominance; > 70% are of myxoid type {21394894; 30850231}. High-grade MLPS (round cell myxoid liposarcoma) is extremely rare in children {30852045}. Paediatric ALT/WDLPS is very uncommon, and DDLPS even more so {19515501; 30887579}. MLPS, although exceedingly rare, is overrepresented in children and young adults, with a female predominance {19194281; 24970901; 26647907; 28160093}. Conventional PLPS is also vanishingly rare in children {19194281}.

Etiology

ALT/WDLPS and MPLPS may be associated with Li–Fraumeni syndrome {28160093; 30850231; 31129458; 31559875}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of paediatric liposarcomas is identical to that of their adult counterparts [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.fr/588.]].

Histopathology

The morphological features of paediatric ALT/WDLPS, DDLPS, and conventional PLPS are identical to those seen in older patients [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.fr/588.]]. MDM2 and/or CDK4 nuclear immunopositivity is present in most cases of ALT/WDLPS and DDLPS {16160477; 17895748; 19737942}, with the exception of Li–Fraumeni-associated cases, which overexpress p53 {30850231}.

The morphological features of MLPS in children and adults are identical. However, unlike in adults, progression to high-grade MLPS (round cell) is not clearly associated with a worse prognosis (probably owing to the rarity of such tumours) in children. MPLPS consists of areas resembling conventional MLPS, juxtaposed to highly cellular and mitotically active areas containing bizarre pleomorphic lipoblasts, as seen in PLPS {19194281; 30850231; 30852046; 30853315; 31501988}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Amplification of MDM2 and CDK4 distinguishes ALT/WDLPS from benign mimics. DDIT3 rearrangement and the absence of PLAG1 rearrangement help in the differential diagnosis of MLPS from extensively myxoid lipoblastomas {30857767; 26319758}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

ALT/WDLPS

Essential: mature fat transected by irregular fibrous septa containing enlarged hyperchromatic cells and (less commonly) lipoblasts.

Desirable: nuclear expression of MDM2 and/or CDK4, or evidence of MDM2 and/or CDK4 gene amplification {30857767}.

DDLPS

Essential: ALT/WDLPS with abrupt transition to (usually) high-grade pleomorphic sarcoma.

Desirable: nuclear expression of MDM2 and/or CDK4, or evidence of MDM2 and/or CDK4 gene amplification {30857767}.

MLPS

Essential: a mixture of non-lipogenic spindle cells, lipoblasts, myxoid stroma, and branched thin-walled vasculature; hypercellularity, diminished myxoid matrix, and elevated nuclear grade (high-grade).

Desirable: DDIT3 gene rearrangement, or detection of specific FUS::DDIT3 or EWSR1::DDIT3 gene fusion {30857767}.

PLPS

Essential: presence of multivacuolated pleomorphic lipoblasts in a high-grade sarcoma.

MPLPS

Essential: variable proportions of conventional MLPS-like areas and more cellular, solid and sheet-like, high-grade, PLPS-like areas.

Staging

Liposarcomas are staged using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

ALT/WDLPS in children demonstrates an overall indolent course {8221578; 28705709; 30850231}; the significance of dedifferentiation in paediatric ALT/WDLPS is unknown, owing to the rarity of this event. Conventional MLPS in paediatric patients has an excellent prognosis {19194281; 22420730; 30850231}. The significance of round cell features in paediatric MLPS is unclear. MPLPS is extremely aggressive, with a high recurrence rate and metastases to lung, bone, and soft tissue, and overall survival is poor {19194281; 22420730; 30850231; 30853315; 31501988}. The natural history of paediatric conventional PLPS is unknown.
