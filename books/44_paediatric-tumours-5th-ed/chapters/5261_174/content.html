5.2.6.1 Rhabdomyoma

Definition

Extracardiac rhabdomyomas are benign mesenchymal tumours with skeletal muscle differentiation. Fetal rhabdomyoma accounts for the majority of paediatric cases.

Related terminology

Acceptable: extracardiac rhabdomyoma.

Subtype(s)

Fetal rhabdomyoma; adult rhabdomyoma; genital rhabdomyoma

Localization

The vast majority of cases arise in the head and the neck, particularly the posterior auricular region. Other sites include the chest, mediastinum, abdomen, pelvis, extremities, epididymis {22520028; 11035587}, vulva {26352088}, tonsil {26246927}, and scrotum {23768526}.

Clinical features

A growing mass may cause proptosis, decreased vision, airway obstruction, or hoarseness {8319954}. See #18078Table 6.06 for a description of the subtypes listed above.

Epidemiology

Fetal rhabdomyomas occur at all ages, with two peaks (< 3 years and 60 years) {26214666}. About 25% of cases are congenital, and these account for 2% of all striated muscle tumours {8319954}.

Etiology

Fetal rhabdomyoma may be associated with naevoid basal cell carcinoma (Gorlin) syndrome, caused by loss-of-function mutations in the tumour suppressor gene PTCH1 {23780909}. Several cases have been reported with Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome {29744825}, caused by FLCN mutation.

Pathogenesis

Dysregulation of hedgehog signalling is an important genetic underpinning in the pathogenesis of both syndromic and sporadic rhabdomyomas {28700439; 16294371}. In syndromic forms, hedgehog signalling is activated by the loss of the inhibitory function of PTCH1. In the other forms, the mechanisms are unknown.

Macroscopic appearance

Fetal rhabdomyomas are well-circumscribed masses with glistening cut surfaces. Multicentric tumours have been reported in association with naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome {24517962}. Sessile or pedunculated polyps occur on mucosal surfaces. Tumour sizes range from 10 to 130 mm (median: 30 mm) {8319954}.

Histopathology

The classic fetal (myxoid) type contains primitive spindle cells with indistinct cytoplasm arranged in irregular bundles within a myxoid background, interspersed with immature skeletal muscle fibres that resemble fetal myotubes. The intermediate fetal type (juvenile or cellular) shows greater cellularity, less myxoid stroma, and a wide spectrum of skeletal muscle differentiation including differentiated rhabdomyoblasts or ganglion-like rhabdomyoblasts. Mitoses can be relatively frequent (as many as 5 mitoses per 50 mm2), but tumours lack substantial nuclear atypia, infiltrative margins, atypical mitoses, and necrosis {8319954; 19165887}.

Tumour cells are positive for desmin, MSA, focal SMA, and occasional S100. Myogenin and MYOD1 are positive in primitive spindle cells and rhabdomyoblasts.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Fetal rhabdomyoma occurs in association with inherited or sporadic inactivating germline mutations in PTCH1 {24517962}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed mass with mature rhabdomyoblasts lacking atypia or necrosis; mitotic activity may be present.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision of fetal rhabdomyoma is curative, with exceptional recurrences {19165887; 16406074}. Rare cases of possible malignant transformation have been reported.
