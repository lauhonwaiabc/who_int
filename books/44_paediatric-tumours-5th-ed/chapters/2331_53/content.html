2.3.3.1 Aggressive NK-cell leukaemia

Definition

Aggressive NK-cell leukaemia (ANKL) is a systemic proliferation of neoplastic NK cells with an acute presentation and usually an aggressive clinical course.

ICD-O coding

9948/3 Aggressive NK-cell leukaemia

ICD-11 coding

2A90.3 Aggressive NK-cell leukaemia

Related terminology

Acceptable: aggressive NK-cell leukaemia/lymphoma.

Not recommended: aggressive large granular lymphocytic leukaemia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

All organs can be involved; the sites most commonly involved are marrow, blood, liver, and spleen {29263371}.

Clinical features

Constitutional symptoms such as fever, leukaemic spread of variable extent, anaemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia are common. LDH levels are often markedly elevated {9886336; 17123604; 8612231}. Hepatosplenomegaly and lymphadenopathy may occur. Patients are usually very ill; haemophagocytic syndrome may occur. Multiorgan failure is thought to be mediated by the shedding of FASL from the tumour cells {11012744; 12495907; 14961041; 24524877; 29263371}.

Epidemiology

ANKL is rare, and its prevalence is highest in Asian populations {15621755}. It affects mainly adolescents and young adults, but also older individuals. There is no sex predilection {12495907; 24524877; 24438142}.

Etiology

The frequent association with EBV argues for a pathogenetic role of the virus {1314113}.

Rare cases are thought to evolve from chronic active EBV infection of the T- or NK-cell type, extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, or NK cell–type large granular lymphocytic leukaemia (the last of these being characteristically EBV-negative) {17509983; 2731121; 8043452; 25031771; 9045900; 9092681; 24438142}.

Pathogenesis

EBV is present in clonal episomal form in approximately 90% of cases {9192774; 1314113; 2544630}. Recurrent cytogenetic aberrations are del(6q) and del(11q) {17261497}. Array hybridization often shows deletions in 7p and 17p, as well as gains in 1q {16049916}. Recurrent mutations in STAT3 and STAT5 have been identified in EBV-negative ANKL {27631517}. Whole-exome sequencing and targeted sequencing revealed mutations in the JAK/STAT and RAS/MAPK pathways, such as in STAT3 (17–21%). About 50% of cases harbour mutations in epigenetic regulatory molecules or histone-modifying molecules such as TET2 (28%), CREBBP (21%) and KMT2 (MLL2) (21%), or DDX3X (29%). TP53 shows alterations in one third of cases (34%) {29148541; 27631517; 32590457}. Drug sensitivity profiling has confirmed the role of JAK/STAT signalling in the pathogenesis of ANKL and other NK-cell malignancies, identifying the neoplastic NK cells as highly sensitive to JAK and BCL2 inhibition compared to other haematopoietic cells. Elevated expression of the extracellular STAT3 stimulator IL-10, as well as the expression of protein products of other mutated genes, has been observed. NK-cell leukaemia cells show prominent STAT3 phosphorylation, MYC expression, and transcriptional activation in multiple metabolic pathways {26192917; 29674644}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Bone marrow and extramedullary sites show focal or extensive and destructive infiltration by medium-sized monomorphic cells with scant to moderate, often pale, cytoplasm and round to irregular, sometimes pleomorphic, nuclei. Angioinvasive and angiodestructive growth inducing zonal necrosis is frequently noted. Interspersed apoptotic bodies are seen. In the bone marrow, haemophagocytosis is often present.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells are typically CD2+, sCD3−, CD3ε+, CD5−, CD56+, and positive for cytotoxic markers. In contrast with extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, CD16 is expressed in about 75% of cases {14961041}. Aberrant immunophenotypes are frequent {24524877}. FASL is expressed by the neoplastic cells.

Cytology

Variable numbers of circulating leukaemic cells (5–80%) may resemble large granular lymphocytes or be more immature and pleomorphic, with pale or slightly basophilic cytoplasm and irregular nuclei with distinct nucleoli.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

TR genes are in germline (wildtype) configuration, supporting an NK-cell lineage.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: acute presentation, with fever and systemic symptoms; systemic (multiorgan) proliferation of neoplastic lymphoid cells; NK-cell immunophenotype; absence of T-cell receptor protein expression and/or absence of clonal rearrangement of TR genes; exclusion of other entities (i.e. T-cell lymphomas, extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, and NK cell–type large granular lymphocytic leukaemia).

Desirable: EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) positivity; haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases have a highly aggressive clinical course often complicated by multiorgan failure, coagulopathy, and/or haemophagocytic syndrome. The median survival time is < 2 months {24524877; 12495907; 14961041; 25031771}.
