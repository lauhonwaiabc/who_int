4.2.1.3 Phakomatous choristoma

Definition

Phakomatous choristoma is a benign congenital developmental lesion of the lens placode, composed of extraocular crystalline lens tissue and its basement membrane.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.0Y Other specified structural developmental anomalies of eyelids

Related terminology

Not recommended: Zimmerman tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The lesion generally occurs deep in the medial lower eyelid, but cases with exclusive involvement of the inferior anterior orbit have been reported {30407999; 10406159}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a smooth, firm, slowly growing subcutaneous mass in the medial lower eyelid, already present at birth.

Epidemiology

Phakomatous choristoma is extremely rare, with < 30 cases reported worldwide.

Etiology

This is a developmental abnormality.

Pathogenesis

Two hypotheses have been proposed as to the origin of the lesion: the formation of an additional lens vesicle locus {5542122}, and the displacement or migration of cells from the lens placode {973823; 16102745}.

Macroscopic appearance

They are composed of pale, firm, round tissue and are as large as 20 mm.

Histopathology

The histological appearance is typical and consists of desmoplastic-like stroma with islands of cuboid to polygonal cells surrounded by a thick layer of eosinophilic, amorphic, PAS-positive material analogous to lens capsule. Other features are the presence of bladder/Wedl-like cells and psammomatous calcification {5542122}. The epithelioid cells stain positively for vimentin, S100, and lens crystalline proteins and are negative for cytokeratins. Electron microscopy reveals 10 nm whorled cytoplasmic microfilaments within degenerating epithelial cells.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: orbital/eyelid localization; extraocular crystalline lens tissue and dense collagen bundles surrounded by a PAS-positive membrane.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Phakomatous choristoma is benign; malignant transformation has not been described. Simple resection is sufficient; recurrences have not been reported. The ipsilateral eye is typically normal. However, a case of a myopic eye with a colobomatous hypoplastic optic disc and staphyloma has been reported {16424545}.
