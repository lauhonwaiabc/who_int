5.2.10.3 Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour

Definition

Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour is a highly malignant soft tissue tumour that mainly affects infants and children. Morphologically and genetically identical tumours also arise in the kidney and brain. The majority of tumours are characterized by biallelic alterations of the SMARCB1 gene.

ICD-O coding

8963/3 Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH3RF3 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Malignant rhabdoid tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: rhabdoid tumour of soft tissue; malignant rhabdoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This rare tumour arises most often in deep axial locations such as the neck, paraspinal region, perineal region, abdominal cavity or retroperitoneum, and pelvic cavity {1712749; 8545590; 9930572; 16704491}. Lesions in the extremities (especially the thigh) and cutaneous lesions are also well documented. This tumour also often affects visceral organs such as the liver, thymus, genitourinary tract, and gastrointestinal system. The liver appears to be the single most common visceral location (73% of all cases) {23816299; 21744471}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a rapidly enlarging soft tissue mass; the associated clinical symptoms depend on the primary organ involvement. Occasional patients present with multiple cutaneous nodules {24284868}. Some cases, particularly in infants, manifest as disseminated disease without an obvious primary tumour – such cases are often associated with rhabdoid tumour predisposition {21108436}.

Epidemiology

Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour is exceedingly rare, and it is largely confined to infants and children. Among fetal and neonatal rhabdoid tumours, extrarenal tumours are more common than tumours in the kidney or brain. Conversely, patients with extrarenal extracranial rhabdoid tumours tend to be older than patients with renal rhabdoid tumours {19798737}.

Etiology

Familial cases are typically associated with germline mutations in SMARCB1 (also known as INI1, SNF5, or BAF47) at 22q11.23 {9892189; 9671307; 21108436}. Germline mutations or deletions in SMARCB1 (rhabdoid tumour predisposition syndrome 1) are present in approximately 13% of patients with extrarenal rhabdoid tumours {29706634}. Patients with germline alterations in SMARCB1 typically present in early childhood, but they are at increased risk for other soft tissue tumours, notably schwannomas, in later decades. A very small percentage of rhabdoid tumours are associated with SMARCA4 mutations, which may be germline in the predisposition syndrome (rhabdoid tumour predisposition syndrome 2).

Pathogenesis

Extrarenal rhabdoid tumours arise as a consequence of homozygous inactivation of the SMARCB1 gene in chromosome band 22q11.2. Extrarenal rhabdoid tumours have a high incidence of homozygous deletions of the SMARCB1 gene. This is often a consequence of a chromosomal translocation between chromosome band 22q11.2 and a variety of different partner chromosomes. The translocation is unbalanced at the molecular level, resulting in the deletion of SMARCB1. The second allele is usually lost as a result of an interstitial 22q11.2 deletion. Consistent with the function of SMARCB1 as a tumour suppressor gene, initiating germline mutations or deletions in SMARCB1 function as the first hit in patients who have a genetic predisposition to the development of rhabdoid tumours. In rare rhabdoid tumours with retained SMARCB1 expression, mutation and/or loss of the SMARCA4 gene at 19p13.2 has been reported {20137775}. Inactivation of the SWI/SNF complex by targeting either SMARCB1 or SMARCA4 gives rise to both intracranial and extracranial rhabdoid tumours. The loss of this epigenetic remodeller leads to the silencing of a variety of tumour suppressor and differentiation genes and pathways that are considered hallmarks in the pathogenesis of these tumours {30595504; 30397315; 30753823}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours are unencapsulated and measure > 50 mm in maximum diameter. The tumours are usually soft and grey to tan in colour on cut surface and are frequently accompanied by foci of coagulative and haemorrhagic necrosis.

Histopathology

The tumour is characterized by rhabdoid cells with large vesicular rounded to bean-shaped nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant cytoplasm, arranged in sheets or in a solid trabecular pattern. Many tumour cells have juxtanuclear eosinophilic, PAS-positive, diastase-resistant hyaline inclusions or globules. At the periphery, tumour cells infiltrate the surrounding tissue. Nuclear pleomorphism is not evident, whereas mitoses are frequent. The tumour often shows a loss of cellular cohesion. Some cases demonstrate predominant proliferation of undifferentiated small round cells, with only a small number of typical rhabdoid cells {27338635; 27356182; 31835848}. Immunohistochemically, most of these tumours show expression of epithelial markers such as keratins and EMA {9930572; 16704491}. The expression of neural or neuroectodermal markers such as CD99 and synaptophysin is also frequently observed in soft tissue tumours. Less commonly, the cells express MSA and focal S100 {9930572; 27338635}. SALL4 and glypican-3 (GPC3) immunoexpression is frequently observed {24827994; 25479928; 23084579}. Characteristically, rhabdoid tumours show a loss of SMARCB1 (INI1, BAF47) expression {15489652; 21934399} or, alternatively, a loss of SMARCA4 (BRG1) {31403913}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primitive undifferentiated or rhabdoid cell morphology; loss of expression of SMARCB1 (INI1).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Regardless of tumour location, patient outcome is dismal. Because of the entity’s rarity, there are no large studies of survival analysis in uniformly treated patients with extrarenal tumours. The median age is 28 months, and the overall 5-year survival rate is ≤ 25% {18506766; 17828773; 30914466}. Survival is worse in patients with rhabdoid tumours of the liver than in patients with rhabdoid tumours at other extracranial and extrarenal sites {21744471; 27082136}.
