5.2.6.5 Ectomesenchymoma

Definition

Ectomesenchymoma is an exceedingly rare multiphenotypic sarcoma composed of both mesenchymal and neuroectodermal components, and it mostly exhibits intermingled cells recapitulating rhabdomyosarcomatous and ganglionic/neuroblastic differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8921/3 Ectomesenchymoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH0S12 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Ectomesenchymoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: gangliorhabdomyosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ectomesenchymoma appears most frequently in the pelvis, urogenital organs, and intra-abdominal or retroperitoneal soft tissue, but the head and neck, extremities, and mediastinum may be affected {10742419; 22535600; 26872011}.

Clinical features

Patients with ectomesenchymoma present with a deep or superficial soft tissue mass.

Epidemiology

Ectomesenchymoma has a predilection for infants or children aged < 5 years (range: birth to 15 years) and an M:F ratio of 1.38:1 {2950992; 10742419; 26872011}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic and array comparative genomic hybridization findings of ectomesenchymoma show overlap with those of embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, with common trisomies of chromosomes 2, 8, and 11 {21803398}. Frequent HRAS mutations and upregulated myogenesis-related genes (e.g. MYOG, CHRND, and MRLN) have been demonstrated, further underscoring the shared alterations seen in embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma {26872011}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is usually multilobulated with a thin capsule, tan cut surface, and variable necrosis and haemorrhage. The size ranges from 30 to 180 mm, with most tumours being > 50 mm in diameter {10742419}.

Histopathology

The prototypical morphology is a combined tumour with areas resembling embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma and neuroectodermal elements. The latter cover the entire spectrum of neuroblastic phenotype, ranging from scattered ganglion cells to mature ganglioneuroma, intermediate ganglioneuroblastoma, and primitive neuroblastoma. The appearances of embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma can be botryoid {21803398}, classic fascicular spindle cell with alternating myxoid/cellular areas {26872011}, and the so-called dense pattern of primitive round cells {10742419; 26872011}. Rare cases originally diagnosed as rhabdomyosarcoma revealed a ganglioneuromatous component in residual tumour after chemotherapy. Retrospective analysis demonstrated a minimal or absent neural component in the original biopsy {32769567; 12469232}. There are correspondent immunophenotypic patterns of MSA, desmin, MYOD1, and myogenin expression in the rhabdomyosarcomatous area, and synaptophysin and chromogranin A staining in the neuroblastic components, with or without S100-positive Schwannian or satellite cells.

Cytology

No data available

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: composite growth of histologically recognizable rhabdomyosarcomatous and neuronal/neuroblastic elements, which are evident by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Ectomesenchymoma is staged using the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for rhabdomyosarcoma or the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with ectomesenchymoma treated with multimodality rhabdomyosarcoma-based protocols have a comparable outcome to those with embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma {10742419; 22535600}. Favourable prognostic factors include age ≤ 3 years, size < 100 mm, and a superficial location. Localized stage and the presence of primitive neuroblastic elements do not appear to have a significant impact on outcome {10742419}.
