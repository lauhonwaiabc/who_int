2.3.2.4 Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma

Definition

Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma (PTFL) is a rare nodal mature B-cell lymphoma occurring predominantly in the paediatric and adolescent age groups. PTFL is characterized by a clonal proliferation of germinal-centre B cells with a pure follicular growth pattern and a high proliferation fraction. Translocations of IRF4, BCL6, and BCL2 are absent.

ICD-O coding

9690/3 Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A80.4 Paediatric-type follicular lymphoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: paediatric follicular lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most cases of PTFL manifest within the lymph nodes of the head and neck region (i.e. cervical, submandibular, and periclavicular lymph nodes) and manifest as stage I or (eventually) stage II disease {19123728; 19679882; 32452165; 9792312; 23665980; 21462303}. Infradiaphragmatic lymph nodes are rarely the primary site of involvement. Extranodal presentation is excluded by definition, although rare clonal follicular proliferations with features of PTFL have been described in the conjunctiva {31868792}.

Clinical features

Most patients with PTFL present with single painless lymph node enlargement. The disease is usually associated with male sex; adolescent age (most tumours arise in patients aged 2–25 years, and almost all occur before the age of 40 years); low serum LDH levels; and limited stage I/II disease, mostly involving peripheral lymph nodes of the head and neck region {26980727; 32452165; 23665980}. Nevertheless, because previous studies have also identified stage III/IV patients, the initial diagnostic workup should follow the revised International Pediatric Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma Staging System (IPNHLSS) {26980727; 32452165; 23665980; 25940716}.

Imaging studies including sonography and CT or MRI usually confirm localized disease and can inform the surgeon on the possibility of a complete resection without excessive risk to the patient.

Epidemiology

According to the available literature, PTFL accounts for < 2% of childhood non-Hodgkin lymphoma cases {19123728; 9739436; 23108024; 22855608; 19679882; 26980727}.

Etiology

Reasons for the occurrence of PTFL in children and adolescents remain obscure. There are no pre-existing disorders such as immunodeficiencies or autoimmune disorders reported for PTFL {32452165}.

Pathogenesis

PTFL is a monoclonal proliferation of germinal-centre–derived B cells preserving the follicular architecture. PTFL shows low genomic complexity and usually lacks translocations found in other germinal-centre–derived lymphomas (i.e. translocations involving BCL2, BCL6, IGH, or MYC) {22855608; 19679882; 31686194; 23445872; 27257180}. It has to be differentiated from large B-cell lymphoma with IRF4 rearrangement (see #16944Table 2.09) {21487109; 23073988}. Recurrent alterations in PTFL include deletions at 1p36 (including TNFRSF14) and mutations of TNFRSF14 and MAP2K1 {23445872; 27257180; 27325104; 28533310}. A hotspot mutation affecting IRF8 has also been reported {28533310; 27338637}.

Macroscopic appearance

PTFL appears as enlarged and encapsulated lymph nodes without sclerosis or necrosis.

Histopathology

The normal lymph node architecture is at least partly effaced by atypical, enlarged, and sometimes floral, serpiginous, or confluent follicles. Mantle zones are thinned or missing. Germinal centres are expansile and lack zonation but might contain tingible-body macrophages. Some marginal zone differentiation might be discernible. A rim of reactive lymph node tissue might be observed (also described as a “node-in-node” phenomenon) {26416032}. PTFL usually consists of blastoid cells (resembling cells intermediate between centrocytes and centroblasts) with small nucleoli. Although PTFL is not graded, most cases would be consistent with a grade 3 morphology rich in large cells. Any diffuse area of large cells consistent with a diffuse large B-cell lymphoma will exclude the diagnosis of PTFL.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells always stain positive for the B-cell markers CD20, CD79a, and PAX5, as well as the germinal-centre marker BCL6. Most cases also express CD10. The latter two markers will highlight the pure follicular architecture and usually spare the interfollicular areas. BCL2 is negative in most cases, although weak or partial BCL2 expression might be seen. The proliferation index is usually high (> 30%) {19679882; 11877266; 31686194; 26416032; 28277421; 15690646}.

Differential diagnosis

Paediatric nodal marginal zone lymphoma typically contains large germinal centres, often with changes similar to those seen in the progressive transformation of germinal centres. Helpful differential diagnostic features in PTFL are the absence of progressively transformed germinal centres, no interfollicular expansion of marginal zone B cells, and a diminished number of PD1-positive T cells within the neoplastic germinal centres.

Cytology

The cytology resembles a reactive lymph node, rich in large cells; it is insufficient for PTFL diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A monoclonal IGH and/or IGK gene rearrangement can be detected in nearly all cases and is helpful in the differential diagnosis from florid reactive follicular hyperplasia (see #16944Table 2.09) {31686194; 27257180; 15080297; 26416032}. Exclusion of alterations of the BCL2, IRF4, IGH, and BCL6 genes by FISH is a mandatory diagnostic criterion for PTFL {26980727}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a paediatric or young adult patient; histological features as listed in #16944Table 2.09 .

Staging

Although most patients with PTFL present with localized disease of the head and neck region and lack B symptoms, initial staging should be performed according to the IPNHLSS {32452165; 25940716}. Whether analysis of the bone marrow and cerebrospinal fluid is needed is a matter of current debate, but it may be waived in cases that are histologically and clinically typical of PTFL {32452165}.

Prognosis and prediction

PTFL does not necessarily require chemotherapy because it has an overall excellent outcome. Survival rates are > 95% {19679882; 32452165; 9792312; 23665980; 29611181; 11877266; 22211428; 21495166; 2194214}. Based on the data available, watchful waiting is possible for patients with carefully evaluated stage I disease who undergo complete resection {32452165}. Because children with unresectable PTFL have an excellent outcome with conventional multidrug chemotherapy (which is, however, associated with acute and long-term toxicity), multinational cooperations aiming for treatment reduction have yet to be established {18371107; 22940833; 15241838; 15486066}. In view of the excellent overall prognosis, local irradiation is not at all indicated in PTFL.
