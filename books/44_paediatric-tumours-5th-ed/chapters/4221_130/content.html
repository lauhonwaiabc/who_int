4.2.2.1 Conjunctival junctional, compound, and subepithelial naevi

Definition

Conjunctival junctional, compound, and subepithelial naevi are benign melanocytic neoplasms composed of melanocytic naevus cells.

ICD-O coding

8740/0 Junctional naevus

8740/0 Conjunctival junctional / predominantly junctional naevus

8760/0 Compound naevus

8760/0 Conjunctival compound naevus

8750/0 Subepithelial (stromal) naevus

8750/0 Conjunctival subepithelial (stromal) naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH8FS8 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Conjunctival naevus

Related terminology

Not recommended: naevocellular naevus.

Subtype(s)

Conjunctival junctional / predominantly junctional naevus; conjunctival compound naevus; conjunctival subepithelial (stromal) naevus

Localization

Junctional naevi are almost exclusively localized to the juxtalimbal and bulbar conjunctiva {14769591}. Compound and subepithelial naevi predominantly involve juxtalimbal and bulbar conjunctiva and, less frequently, the plica semilunaris and caruncle {2657539; 14769591; 8883545}. Naevi are extremely rare in the forniceal and palpebral (tarsal) conjunctiva (unlike blue naevi) {20683369; 14769591}. Corneal involvement is characteristically absent {21128776; 14769591}.

Clinical features

Conjunctival naevocellular naevi are well-circumscribed brown to tan-coloured or (rarely) amelanotic lesions that are freely mobile, with conjunctival movement. Junctional naevi tend to be flat; epithelial inclusion cysts and intrinsic or feeder vessels are rare {20622349}. Compound and subepithelial naevi are usually elevated, frequently harbour epithelial cysts, and can be associated with intrinsic and feeder vessels {20622349; 14769591}. Such feeder vessels in naevi tend to be minimally or mildly dilated. Growth of a naevus in childhood or adolescence is not a harbinger of malignant transformation, whereas growth during adulthood usually prompts excisional biopsy to rule out melanoma.

Epidemiology

Junctional naevi account for 3–6% of biopsied conjunctival naevi and are typically diagnosed in childhood or adolescence {20622349; 14769591; 8883545}. Compound naevi account for 70–78% of biopsied conjunctival naevi and are diagnosed at a median age of 22 years (range: 5–74 years). Subepithelial naevi account for 15–24% of biopsied conjunctival naevi and are diagnosed at a mean age of 46 years (range: 12–87 years) {20622349; 14769591; 8883545; 2657539}. Compound and subepithelial naevi occur with an equal sex distribution and are more common in White populations {20622349; 14769591; 8883545}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Conjunctival naevi are a subtype of acquired melanocytic naevus. Analogous to that of their dermal counterparts, the ontogeny of conjunctival naevi is thought to initiate with a junctional naevus, followed by the migration of intraepithelial naevus cells into the conjunctival stroma (substantia propria), resulting in a compound naevus, and terminating with the disappearance of the junctional component in a subepithelial naevus {2657539; 21128776}.

Conjunctival naevi arise from neural crest–derived melanocytes {2657539}.

Conjunctival naevi harbour BRAF p.V600E mutations (~50%) and NRAS p.Q61R (~40%) mutations {16123397; 29332123; 27009410; 31558492}. GNAQ mutations have been documented in conjunctival blue naevi {29332123}. It should be noted that the presence/absence of these BRAF or NRAS mutations does not aid in the differentiation between a naevus and a melanoma. The latter are usually associated with additional chromosomal alterations, including amplifications of 6p, deletions of 7q and 10q, and mutation-specific copy-number alterations {30672666}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically, conjunctival naevi are usually well-circumscribed, brown to tan-coloured (or, rarely, amelanotic), flat or elevated lesions, with scattered lucent foci corresponding to the epithelial cysts {2657539; 14769591; 20622349}.

Histopathology

Junctional naevi appear as intraepithelial proliferations of type A naevocytes (ovoid, plump, epithelioid) and/or type B naevocytes (ovoid to cuboidal, lymphocyte-like) arranged in single or confluent nests, and occasionally lentiginously, along the epithelial–subepithelial junction, anterior to the epithelial basement membrane. Some nests may protrude superficially into the substantia propria. In some cases, the conjunctival epithelium undergoes invagination into the substantia propria, associated with junctional naevus cells. The conjunctival epithelium overlying the naevus nests may be markedly attenuated. Pagetoid scatter is unusual. Sporadic nuclear pleomorphism and occasionally prominent nucleoli can be observed, without overt cytomorphological atypia. Mitotic figures are rare to absent. Inflammatory infiltrates can be present in the substantia propria {2657539; 21128776; 2649838}.

Compound naevi contain symmetrically arranged naevocytes both within the epithelium and in the substantia propria, where the naevocytes frequently enclose the solid and cystic rests of the conjunctival epithelium (termed “compound cystic naevus”). Maturation towards the base of the lesion can be observed in some naevi as the naevus cells progressively transition from type A naevocytes in the superficial layers, to type B naevocytes, and ultimately to type C (neuronal-like) naevocytes at the base of the lesion, but this feature is not uniformly present in all conjunctival naevi. Multinucleated cells and sporadic nuclear pleomorphism can be seen, without overt nuclear atypia. Mitotic activity, if present, is confined to the junctional component {2657539; 21128776; 20142539}. Compound naevi in children and adolescents can be associated with prominent inflammation (inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevi) and can feature significant cytomorphological and architectural atypia {18300811}. Analogous to their dermal counterparts, unusual patterns of conjunctival compound naevi include balloon cell naevus, granular cell naevus, and clonal / deep penetrating naevus {19667333; 31469587; 31558492}. The term “combined naevus” is used to describe a collision lesion that contains two naevus components, including a common acquired naevus and another naevus-type component (most frequently a blue naevus) {10703123; 20683369; 31558492}.

Subepithelial naevi share morphological similarity with the substantia propria component of the compound naevus {2657539; 21128776; 20142539}.

Conjunctival naevi stain positively for S100, SOX10, MITF, and melan-A. HMB45 is positive in some naevi, typically weakly and spottily in the junctional component {20142539; 28161440; 11683945}. Ki-67 staining is generally limited to the junctional component, where the proliferation index is low (~2%) {20142539; 28161440}.

Cytology

Cytology is generally not performed.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular diagnosis is rarely required. FISH with probes used for cutaneous melanoma (RREB1 [6p24.3], MYB [6q23], MYC [8q24.21], and CCND1 [11q13]) has been shown to be useful in classifying conjunctival melanocytic lesions of uncertain malignant potential {23137666; 32707054}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nested intraepithelial melanocytic proliferation and/or a subepithelial melanocytic proliferation.

Desirable: architectural symmetry; presence of regular inclusion cysts lined by conjunctival epithelium; absence of an extensive intraepithelial component peripheral to the subepithelial component; absence of pagetoid scatter and intraepithelial migration; absence of significant cytomorphological atypia; absence of mitotic figures in the subepithelial component; absence of chromosomal and mutation-specific copy-number alterations.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The junctional component of the conjunctival naevus can infrequently give rise to conjunctival melanoma {14769591; 2657539}.
