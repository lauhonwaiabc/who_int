6.2.3.1 Embryonal carcinoma

Definition

Embryonal carcinoma is composed of cells resembling embryonic stem cells, with ovoid to columnar profiles, clear to granular or amphophilic cytoplasm, and markedly pleomorphic nuclei, with diverse morphological patterns.

ICD-O coding

9070/3 Embryonal carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

Not recommended: undifferentiated malignant teratoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Embryonal carcinoma arises in the gonads, mediastinum, and CNS (in which most cases occur in the pineal region or suprasellar compartment, and fewer occur in the basal ganglia / thalamus and other locations) {31966414}.

Clinical features

After seminoma, embryonal carcinoma is the most prevalent testicular germ cell tumour (pure embryonal carcinomas account for 2.3–16% of testicular germ cell tumours) {24503753; 2854295; 6291228}); it is present in 87% of non-seminomatous tumours {6093440; 2854295}. It is rare in childhood, except in patients with disorders of sex development {3022907; 6093440}. Most testicular examples come to clinical attention as a mass, with 10% showing metastatic disease {3016346}. Retroperitoneal lymph node involvement relates to the proportion of embryonal carcinoma in mixed germ cell tumour (MGCT) {3016346}. Serum β-hCG is generally not elevated {29603174}, although LDH and PLAP can be {6164464; 9058515}. Imaging does not distinguish embryonal carcinoma from MGCT. Embryonal carcinoma in the CNS manifests with symptoms depending on location, β-hCG level, and associated hydrocephalus {31966414}. Spinal leptomeningeal metastases rarely occur {2991485; 111147}. Levels of AFP and β-hCG can be elevated {31420671}.

Epidemiology

See section Intratubular germ cell neoplasia (male gonadal) . CNS embryonal carcinoma accounts for 3.3–5% of all germ cell tumours {9046301; 2991485} and in 5.6% of non-germinomatous tumours {9046301}, with a male predominance and a peak in incidence at 16–18 years (mean age: 11.8 ± 7.1 years) {9046301; 2991485}.

Etiology

See section Intratubular germ cell neoplasia (male gonadal) {16607373; 24249158; 24452629; 24896186; 27391150}. Fewer CNS germ cell tumours exhibit gains of 12p than do testicular and mediastinal examples {12071636; 19801160; 24249158; 24452629}. Gains of CCND2 and PRDM14 (primordial germ cell specification regulator) and losses of RB1 imply involvement of the cyclin/CDK/RB1/E2F pathway {24249158}. Activating mutations involving the MAPK pathway (KIT, RAS family members) and (less frequently) the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway have been identified in germ cell tumours beyond infancy / early childhood {24452629; 24896186; 26956871; 27391150}. More CNS germ cell tumours appear to occur in Down syndrome {28919233} and Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY) {14670679; 30291793; 31916730} than in the general population.

Pathogenesis

See section Intratubular germ cell neoplasia (male gonadal) . Clinical and epigenetic studies of CNS germ cell tumours arising after infancy / early childhood {26956871; 27391150; 28078450} suggest a primordial germ cell origin, although alternative origins have also been suggested {17705807; 22926997}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut section has a variegated, granular, solid, grey-white to tan appearance and may exhibit haemorrhage, necrosis, and cysts.

Histopathology

Embryonal carcinoma cells are epithelioid, with vesicular nuclei, dense amphophilic cytoplasm, and poorly defined cytoplasmic membranes, and they show prominent mitoses (atypical) and apoptosis. Nuclear crowding, overlapping, and marked pleomorphism can be characteristic; necrotic foci are common. The most frequent patterns are solid, glandular, papillary, and combined {24503753}. Embryonal carcinoma cells can mimic germinoma-like cells {24503753} and may show syncytiotrophoblast cells and lymphocytic infiltrates, although a granulomatous reaction is rare {24503753}. Immunohistochemically, embryonal carcinomas express CD30 (possibly lost after chemotherapy) {11683938}, OCT3/4 and SOX2 {17527070}, LIN28A {21057460}, cytokeratin AE1/AE3 {18045648}, and SALL4.

Cytology

Squash and smear preparations show cohesive clusters of large epithelioid cells with prominent nucleoli and abundant cytoplasm.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Isochromosome 12p and increased gains of 12p in most embryonal carcinomas are helpful diagnostic findings to differentiate from non–germ cell, poorly differentiated malignancies {10357096; 8527395}. Embryonal carcinoma has a lower DNA index than seminoma {1318004; 2536126}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large epithelioid cells with a defined immunohistochemical pattern (OCT3/4, SOX2, LIN28A, AE1/AE3, and SALL4).

Staging

See section Intratubular germ cell neoplasia (male gonadal) . CNS embryonal carcinomas rarely show leptomeningeal metastasis at diagnosis {2991485}; if present, treatment follows the regimen for poor prognostic groups with metastasis {11767296}. Staging is according to the Delphi consensus {26370356}.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis correlates with stage and the proportion of embryonal carcinoma in testicular MGCT {8558664; 8275469; 10637250; 2480680}. Pure embryonal carcinoma behaves more aggressively than MGCT {2162186; 2480680}, with frequent lymphovascular invasion and an increased risk of metastasis. Haematogenous spread to the lungs and other sites may occur. Post-orchiectomy combination chemotherapy plus retroperitoneal lymph node dissection is necessary in many cases {12697874; 11000572; 2838876}. For CNS embryonal carcinoma, pure or mixed, outcomes are dismal {9046301; 9624246; 11767296}, but combined irradiation and chemotherapy increase 5- and 10-year survival rates to 75–80% {29048505; 31420671}. Logarithmic decreases of AFP and hCG augur favourably {21359564}, whereas AFP levels of > 1000 ng/mL and residual disease after treatment have negative implications {29048505}. Long-term survivors are at risk of premature death due to subsequent treatment-associated malignant neoplasms, stroke, and other iatrogenic effects {25422317}.
