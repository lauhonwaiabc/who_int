5.2.9.3A Perineurioma

Definition

Perineurioma is a benign tumour composed of neoplastic perineurial cells.

ICD-O coding

9571/0 Perineurioma

ICD-11 coding

2A02.3 & XH0XF7 Benign neoplasm of cranial nerves & Perineurioma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: for intraneural perineurioma: localized hypertrophic neuropathy.

Subtype(s)

Some rare subtypes such as reticular and sclerosing perineuriomas are described in the 2020 WHO classification of soft tissue tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.fr/588.]]. The very rare malignant perineurioma is usually considered a malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour with perineurial differentiation {9808129; 22762889} and is discussed in a separate section (see section Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour). Hybrid nerve sheath tumours may include a perineurioma component.

Localization

Intraneural perineuriomas primarily affect peripheral nerves of the extremities; cranial nerve lesions are rare {16703891; 17881938; 22420725}. Soft tissue perineuriomas are located in the deep soft tissue and are not grossly associated with a nerve {22420725}.

Clinical features

In intraneural perineurioma, progressive muscle weakness (with or without atrophy) is more frequent than sensory disturbances. Patients with soft tissue perineurioma present with nonspecific mass effects. Both intraneural and soft tissue perineuriomas have been described in patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 and type 2; however, these are rare cases and it is unclear if there is a true association {24294391; 28812461; 32271143; 32529330}. One case has been reported in association with Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome {15750909}.

Epidemiology

Intraneural perineurioma typically affects young adults or adolescents {30637060}, although rarely it occurs in children {21039177; 22420725}. There is no sex predilection. The incidence of soft tissue perineurioma peaks in middle-aged adults, with an M:F ratio of 1:2 {15958848; 22420725}. Both intraneural and soft tissue perineuriomas are rare, accounting for approximately 1% of nerve sheath and soft tissue neoplasms, respectively.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The majority of intraneural perineuriomas harbour missense mutations in TRAF7 affecting the WD40 domain of the protein, similarly to meningiomas. Some TRAF7-wildtype intraneural perineuriomas were shown to carry large genomic deletions/duplications, including deletions of chromosome 22q {28019650}.

Soft tissue perineuriomas do not carry TRAF7 mutations but commonly show chromosomal alterations. The most frequently observed alterations are deletions of 22q (including the NF2 tumour suppressor gene) and deletions of 17q11 (encompassing the NF1 tumour suppressor gene), as well as 2p deletions {30303818}. The sclerosing subtype of soft tissue perineurioma shows recurrent rearrangements or deletions of chromosome 10q {16096405}.

Macroscopic appearance

Intraneural perineurioma produces a segmental several-fold enlargement of the affected nerve. Individual nerve fascicles appear coarse and pale. Most lesions are < 100 mm long, but one sciatic nerve example 400 mm long has been reported {7785653}. Although multiple fascicles are often involved, a bag-of-worms plexiform growth is not seen. Involvement of two neighbouring spinal nerves has been reported {7785653}.

Soft tissue perineurioma is solitary, generally small (< 70 mm), and well circumscribed but unencapsulated. The cut surface is firm, greyish-white, and occasionally myxoid.

Histopathology

Intraneural perineurioma consists of concentric layers of perineurial cells around axons, forming characteristic pseudo–onion bulbs. This distinctive architectural feature is best seen on cross-section, wherein fascicles vary in cellularity. Perineurial cells accumulate in the endoneurium, but the perineurium is often affected as well. Particularly large whorls can envelop numerous nerve fibres. In early lesions, axonal density and myelination may be almost normal, whereas in fully developed lesions, when most fibres are surrounded by perineurial cells and therefore widely separated, myelin is often scant or absent. At late stages, only Schwann cells without accompanying axons may remain at the centre of the perineurial whorls. Hyalinization may be prominent.

Soft tissue perineurioma is composed of spindle cells with wavy or tapering nuclei, indistinct nucleoli, and bipolar cytoplasmic processes. They typically exhibit a storiform or whorled growth pattern. The stroma is usually collagenous. Mitoses are very rare to absent. Necrosis is typically absent. Degenerative features may include nuclear pleomorphism, multinucleated cells, and a myxoid matrix {15958848}.

Immunohistochemically, tumour cells of intraneural and soft tissue perineurioma are consistently positive for EMA, which ranges from weak and focal to strong and diffuse {15958848}, and claudin-1 and GLUT1 are also often positive {12459629; 12836021}. CD34 is expressed in about 60% of soft tissue perineuriomas {15958848}. Staining for S100, SOX10, and GFAP is negative.

Cytology

There is little experience with cytological diagnosis of these lesions, but FNAB specimens are said to be paucicellular, with smears containing fragments of myxoid stroma {15060885}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analyses do not have an established role in the diagnosis of perineurioma. Because of the rareness of perineurioma, a reference group for its DNA methylation–based classification is not currently available.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Intraneural perineurioma

Essential: a pseudo–onion bulb pattern on cross-section, with axons in the centre; tumour cells are positive for at least one perineurial antigen (EMA, claudin-1, GLUT1) and negative for S100.

Soft tissue perineurioma

Essential: slender spindle cells with bipolar cytoplasmic processes; storiform and/or whorled architecture; tumour cells are positive for at least one perineurial antigen (EMA, claudin-1, GLUT1) and negative for S100.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Long-term follow-up of intraneural perineuriomas indicates that they do not have a tendency to recur or metastasize. Radiological follow-up studies have shown that intraneural perineuriomas only rarely grow in length and do not grow to involve new nerves or nerve divisions, and that their growth does not correlate with clinical progression {30481319}.

Soft tissue perineuriomas are usually amenable to gross total removal. Recurrences are very infrequent, even in cases with histological atypia/degeneration, and none have been reported to metastasize {9414186; 11257623}. Malignant progression of soft tissue perineurioma has not been documented. Malignant perineurial tumours with high mitotic activity and a poor prognosis including metastases correspond to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours {9808129}.
