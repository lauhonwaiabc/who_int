5.2.3.2B Tufted angioma and kaposiform haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Tufted angioma (TA) and kaposiform haemangioendothelioma (KHE) are related (probably biologically synonymous) vascular tumours, usually paediatric in presentation. They are characterized by nodular capillary proliferations intertwined with a variably prominent component of spindled endothelial cells with ambiguous lymphatic/blood vascular differentiation, often associated with lymphatic vessels, and widely regarded as variations within a single disease spectrum. Together they cause most instances of Kasabach–Merritt phenomenon (KMP).

Related terminology

Not recommended: Kaposi-like infantile haemangioendothelioma; haemangioma with Kaposi-like features; angioblastoma of Nakagawa.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

TA is a superficial lesion affecting the skin and subcutis {11167978; 20644037}. Bulky, more proliferative examples of this genre, traditionally described as KHE, also affect the skin and subcutis, crossing tissue planes to extend more deeply into the soft tissues of the extremities, head and neck, trunk, axilla, and groin. Less common sites of KHE involvement include the retroperitoneum, mediastinum, and bone, with rarely reported direct extension into the viscera {23796341; 8494101; 15105642; 9420544}. Multifocal examples occur, primarily in bone {23373432; 27820128; 24931465}.

Clinical features

Patients with TA present with a pink or red-purple cutaneous macule or infiltrated plaque, whereas those with KHE present with a poorly defined, large, bulging, indurated purple-red tumour. Both may be painful and/or associated with hypertrichosis or hyperhidrosis {11167978; 20644037; 23796341}. KMP usually occurs in the setting of large congenital or early infantile TA/KHE lesions and is characterized by profound thrombocytopenia due to intratumoural platelet trapping, frequently accompanied by petechiae, ecchymoses, and a sudden increase in tumour size {15105642}. Some lesions may partially regress or wax and wane {10642677}.

Epidemiology

Most cases of TA/KHE are congenital or acquired in childhood; there is a slight male predilection {11167978; 20644037; 27607323; 28727248; 28579853}). Approximately 50% are detected at birth, and 90% are detected within the first year of life {29603128}. The estimated prevalence of KHE in the USA is about 0.91 cases per 100 000 children {22871490}. Rare cases arise in adolescence and adulthood (without KMP) {15105642; 17186794}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Genetic alterations reported in KHE and TA are heterogeneous and include a somatic activating GNA14 c.614A>T (p.Q205L) mutation in some cases {27476652}. Methylation profiling studies have revealed a distinctive shared epigenetic pattern in KHE and TA {31887709}, bolstering the consensus that TA and KHE fall within the same biological spectrum. Hypothetically, podoplanin expressed by spindled endothelial cells elicits platelet activation by binding its natural ligand CLC1B (a lectin receptor highly expressed on platelets, also called CLEC2) and stimulates the platelet-trapping phenomenon responsible for KMP {28992650; 19630798}.

Macroscopic appearance

TA and KHE appear as multiple grey to red infiltrative nodules within fibrous tissue.

Histopathology

Classic TA histology shows multiple small, well-circumscribed vascular tufts dispersed in a cannonball-like fashion within the dermis, sometimes extending into the subcutis. The tufts are composed of closely packed capillaries with compressed lumina, occasionally accompanied by subtle fascicles of delicate spindled endothelial cells. Dilated thin-walled lymphatic vessels focally form crescentic clefts at the tuft periphery.

Classic KHE histology includes the presence of vascular tufts equivalent to those of TA but accompanied by larger, less-defined, coalescing nodules composed of fascicles of spindled endothelial cells forming elongate slit-like lumina containing erythrocytes and curving around epithelioid, pericyte-rich nodules with central microthrombi. Other areas display more typical capillary formation. Dilated lymphatic vessels reminiscent of lymphatic malformation are frequently seen within adjacent desmoplastic stroma {29217054; 24965196}. Mitoses are rare and normally configured. Residual lesions are fibrotic versions of the original {10642677}.

TA/KHE endothelial cells are strongly positive for the panendothelial markers CD31, CD34, and ERG, and the spindled forms are also positive for lymphatic markers including podoplanin, LYVE1, and PROX1. Lymphatic markers are less-extensively positive in superficial TA lesions than in KHE, reflecting their lesser complement of spindled endothelial cells {20975337}. TA/KHE endothelial cells are GLUT1-negative {15105642} and WT1-positive {21697701}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for TA: a cannonball-like distribution of multiple small and discrete intradermal lobules of tightly packed small capillaries; for KHE: poorly defined coalescing vascular nodules containing fascicles of plump spindled endothelial cells.

Desirable: for TA and KHE: a background of variably dilated lymphatic vessels; dual positivity of spindled endothelial cells for panendothelial and lymphatic markers; presence of platelet-rich microthrombi.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Bulky, deep KHE results in KMP more frequently than TA {20644037}. A minority of untreated superficial examples (TA) may partially regress, leaving minor fibrous cutaneous changes, but they generally recur {16785378; 15942226}. Large, deep KHE is associated with higher morbidity and risk of mortality due to tumour infiltration/compression and KMP. KHE does not fully regress even with successful medical therapy; biopsies of fibrotic residual tissue demonstrate foci of viable tumour, and recurrence is common {10642677; 28727248}. Wide surgical excision, although rarely feasible, can be curative.

TA is classified as a benign tumour, whereas KHE is deemed borderline because of its locally aggressive behaviour and a report of 2 cases with multifocal lymph node involvement along a lymphatic chain {26055853}. No distant metastases have been reported {15105642}.
