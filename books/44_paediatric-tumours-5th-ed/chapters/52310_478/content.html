5.2.3.10 Angiosarcoma

Definition

Angiosarcoma is an aggressive malignant vascular tumour showing morphological and immunohistochemical endothelial differentiation.

ICD-O coding

9120/3 Angiosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B56.Y & XH6264 Angiosarcoma, other specified primary site & Haemangiosarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: haemangiosarcoma; lymphangiosarcoma; malignant haemangioendothelioma; malignant angioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Unlike in adults, most angiosarcomas in children arise from deep soft tissues (head and neck, extremities, mediastinum, and peritoneum) and viscera (mainly liver and heart) {18987547; 31814291}. Cutaneous cases mainly affect the lower extremities and the head and neck {21164289; 31814291}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation is variable, depending on location. Cutaneous paediatric angiosarcomas tend to be unifocal, rapidly enlarging masses that can ulcerate, although some are multifocal {21164289}.

Epidemiology

Paediatric angiosarcoma is extremely rare. It can arise at any age from early infancy to late adolescence, and it has no sex predilection {8416140; 18987547}.

Etiology

The etiology is mostly unknown. Angiosarcoma has been reported in congenital hemihypertrophy of the contralateral limb, Aicardi syndrome, congenital lymphoedema, radiated congenital haemangioma, xeroderma pigmentosum, Maffucci syndrome, and neurofibromatosis {21164289; 4065351; 10460655; 29138703}. Radiation-induced angiosarcoma is very rare but may occur later in life.

Pathogenesis

Angiosarcomas are genetically complex {20217954; 25231439}. Genes related to angiogenesis and vascular-specific tyrosine kinases, including TIE1, TEK, KDR (VEGFR2), and FLT4 (VEGFR3), are often upregulated {19723655; 18041747}. CIC abnormalities are present in a subset of highly aggressive angiosarcomas in young adults {26735859}. Mutations in angiogenic signalling pathways (e.g. KDR, PTPRB, and PLCG1) are relatively common {19723655; 24633157; 22648906}. MYC amplification is seen in almost all radiation- or lymphoedema-associated angiosarcomas {20008140; 20949568; 22121953; 21909081}. Coamplification of FLT4 is present in 25% of secondary angiosarcomas {20949568}.

Macroscopic appearance

Angiosarcoma has a haemorrhagic, spongy to solid, fleshy appearance. It may be infiltrative or relatively well demarcated.

Histopathology

Angiosarcomas are infiltrative lesions, variably composed of anastomosing vessels or sheets of epithelioid to spindled cells. Vasoformative channels are usually scarce in paediatric cases and often exhibit multilayering of endothelial cells and papillary projections. Most have high-grade nuclear features; well-differentiated tumours may have small hyperchromatic nuclei. Marked pleomorphism is rare. Epithelioid angiosarcoma often has a sheet-like growth pattern, with atypical cells exhibiting vesicular nuclei and abundant cytoplasm. Most are immunoreactive for CD31 and ERG and variably positive for CD34 {7512718; 21317715} and GLUT1 (50%) {28552538}. SMA-positive pericytes are absent. Epithelioid angiosarcoma may be positive for EMA and cytokeratin {17284115; 1718176}. Radiation- and lymphoedema-associated angiosarcomas usually have nuclear immunoreactivity for MYC {22121953; 21909081}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MYC amplification in lymphoedema- and radiation-associated angiosarcomas may help for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a vasoformative or sheet-like growth pattern; nuclear atypia; mitotic activity; immunoreactivity for CD31 and ERG.

Staging

Staging is not recommended; all angiosarcomas behave in a highly aggressive fashion.

Prognosis and prediction

Angiosarcoma is highly aggressive, with a mortality rate of > 50% {18987547}. Cutaneous cases may be slightly less aggressive {21164289}.
