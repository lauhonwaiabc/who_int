5.2.2.2.5 Calcifying aponeurotic fibroma

Definition

Calcifying aponeurotic fibroma (CAF) is a benign tumour of children and adolescents, with a predilection for the distal extremities, characterized by bland spindle cells and zones of calcification with plump epithelioid fibroblasts.

ICD-O coding

8816/0 Calcifying aponeurotic fibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH8ZE3 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Calcifying aponeurotic fibroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: juvenile aponeurotic fibroma; aponeurotic fibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CAF commonly involves the palmar and plantar surfaces, with a predilection for the hands. Wrists, ankles, lower arms, and lower legs are less frequently affected. Uncommon sites are the proximal extremities, trunk, and head and neck region {9865839; 5506609}.

Clinical features

Patients with CAF typically present with a slowly growing, superficially located painless mass {9865839}.

Epidemiology

The age range is wide, with a peak in incidence at 5–15 years. Patients are more commonly male than female {9865839}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

An FN1::EGF fusion gene appears to be the main driver. The FN1 gene is highly expressed in CAF and its promoter activity drives the inappropriate expression of the biologically active portion of EGF {26691015}, allowing EGF to activate growth via its receptor, EGFR (HER1). However, other pathogenetic mechanisms may be involved and the fusion may enter into the cellular secretory pathway, promoting transformation via autocrine/paracrine mechanisms.

Macroscopic appearance

CAF is an irregular, firm, fibrous tumour with variable calcifications {9865839}.

Histopathology

Lesions are infiltrative into the surrounding fat, muscle, fascia, tendons, and neurovascular bundles. They consist of fascicular, fibromatosis-like spindle cells and less cellular chondroid zones, often with calcification, bordered by rounded epithelioid cells. Both components are variably present, and early lesions show only a fibromatosis-like pattern. Mitotic activity is usually low. Osteoclast-type giant cells may be present. By immunohistochemistry, lesional cells variably express SMA, MSA, and CD99, while S100 is positive in the chondroid areas {5506609; 30310176; 9865839}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of an FN1::EGF fusion may be helpful {26691015}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an infiltrative fibromatosis-like fibroblastic lesion with variable chondroid foci with possible calcification.

Desirable (in selected cases): demonstration of FN1::EGF fusion.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence, which may occur years after initial excision, is observed in about 50% of cases. Children aged < 5 years are at higher risk for local recurrence {9865839}.
