2.3.3.10 Peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS

Definition

Peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS (PTCL-NOS) is a heterogeneous category of mature T-cell lymphomas that do not correspond to any of the specifically defined entities of mature T-cell lymphomas.

ICD-O coding

9702/3 Peripheral T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A90.C Peripheral T-cell lymphoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: T-cell lymphoma NOS; peripheral T-cell lymphoma, pleomorphic small cell; peripheral T-cell lymphoma, pleomorphic medium and large cell; peripheral T-cell lymphoma, large cell; Lennert lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PTCL-NOS has a predominantly nodal presentation. The liver and spleen are involved in a small subgroup, whereas other sites such as the skin, nasal cavity, CNS, and bone marrow are usually not involved. Most patients present with advanced-stage disease {25395120; 27270301}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are related to tumour site and extension. Even at advanced stages, B symptoms are usually not detected {27270301; 25395120; 19856396; 18300314; 23042011}.

Epidemiology

PTCL-NOS is the second most frequent mature T-cell lymphoma in children in central Europe after ALK-positive anaplastic large cell lymphoma, but there is wide geographical variability {25395120; 27270301; 31352772; 18022899}.

Etiology

As many as 25% of cases of PTCL-NOS arise in patients with pre-existing conditions such as congenital immunodeficiencies, autoimmune disorders, or a previous malignancy of a different type {27270301; 27515251}.

Pathogenesis

Besides the mutations and translocations identified in single paediatric and adolescent patients, PTCL-NOS in young patients also differs from its adult counterpart by having a lower frequency of the mutations found in T follicular helper (TFH) cell lymphomas (e.g. ID2, RHOA, and TET2) and a lower frequency of TP53 alterations {34390161; 34258755}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The tissue architecture is effaced. The cytological picture is variable, but tumours are usually composed of medium-sized polymorphic cells with irregular nuclei. A variable number of inflammatory cells are admixed. However, the pattern of overlap with angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma seen in adults seems exceedingly rare in children {25395120}.

The cytological picture of lymphoblastic lymphoma (small monomorphic blasts, finely dispersed chromatin, narrow rim of cytoplasm) is not expected in PTCL-NOS and should prompt further immunohistochemical analysis even if TdT expression is undetectable (see below).

Immunophenotype

CD3 is usually positive, and there is variable expression of other pan–T-cell markers. TdT should be negative, and any expression of TdT, even in a small number of neoplastic cells, is suggestive of lymphoblastic lymphoma. Lymphomas with a morphological picture of lymphoblastic lymphoma (see above) that lack expression of TdT should be carefully checked for features such as expression of CD1a, expression of the haematopoietic precursor cell marker CD34, and a multilineage phenotype (e.g. myeloperoxidase and CD79a) {21451365; 32452165}.

Differential diagnosis

Expression of CD30 is absent or only weakly detectable in a subpopulation of neoplastic cells. Any strong and abundant expression of CD30 should raise the suspicion of anaplastic large cell lymphoma. By definition, ALK is not expressed. EBV association should be excluded by EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) in situ hybridization. Whenever EBV is detected, EBV-associated lymphoproliferations of different types should be considered. Autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS) and other immunodeficiency-associated lymphoproliferations may mimic PTCL-NOS and need to be carefully excluded {9811346; 25728094; 27555459; 32452165}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Because the clinical presentation of PTCL-NOS may mimic that of inflammatory conditions, and because the histological picture is very heterogeneous, demonstration of clonal TR gene rearrangement is useful to establish the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mass-forming clonal T-cell proliferation with a variable histological appearance; exclusion of immunodeficiency-associated lymphoproliferative disorders other than lymphoma; exclusion of specific T-cell lymphomas (i.e. lymphoblastic lymphoma, anaplastic large cell lymphoma, EBV-associated lymphoma/lymphoproliferation, and cutaneous T-cell lymphomas).

Desirable: involvement of more than one site; aberrant T-cell phenotype confirming neoplastic nature.

Staging

Staging is performed according to the revised International Pediatric Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma Staging System (IPNHLSS) {25940716}.

Prognosis and prediction

In a retrospective analysis of patients who underwent various treatments, the 5-year overall survival rate was 56%.
