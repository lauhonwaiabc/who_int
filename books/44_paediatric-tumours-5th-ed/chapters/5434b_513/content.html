5.4.3.4B Adamantinoma

Definition

Adamantinoma is a biphasic, locally aggressive or malignant tumour characterized by an epithelial neoplastic component within a bland osteofibrous background.

ICD-O coding

9261/3 Adamantinoma of long bones

9261/3 Classic adamantinoma (malignant)

9261/1 Osteofibrous dysplasia–like adamantinoma

9261/3 Dedifferentiated adamantinoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5J & XH1SV4 Malignant miscellaneous tumours of bone or articular cartilage of other or unspecified sites & Ameloblastoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: well-differentiated adamantinoma.

Subtype(s)

Classic adamantinoma (malignant); osteofibrous dysplasia–like adamantinoma; dedifferentiated adamantinoma

Localization

Adamantinoma affects the proximal anterior tibia and/or fibula. Multifocal and bilateral lesions may be present in some cases. Other bones may exceptionally be involved {28249983; 2743266}.

Clinical features

Adamantinoma causes pain, swelling, or bowing deformity. At imaging, adamantinoma appears as an intracortical, well-circumscribed, multilobulated lytic lesion, with a soap-bubble appearance. Multifocality, involvement of the medullary cavity, and cortical destruction with infiltration into the soft tissues can occur.

Epidemiology

Adamantinoma is an extremely rare bone tumour. Osteofibrous dysplasia (OFD)-like adamantinoma affects patients in the first two decades of life (rarely those aged > 15 years), whereas adamantinoma affects adults aged > 20 years. Progression into dedifferentiated adamantinoma may occur in middle/advanced age {14657712; 20717000; 32769809}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There have been reports of rare OFD-like adamantinomas arising from pre-existing OFD, but this is matter of debate {28249983}. OFD-like adamantinoma may rarely progress into classic adamantinoma. Evidence of recurrent numerical abnormalities (extra copies of chromosomes 7, 8, 12, and/or 21), similar to those found in the spindle cell component of adamantinoma and OFD-like adamantinoma, suggests a relationship between these two lesions {18300815; 11227067; 8156503}. Extra copies of chromosome 19 and other chromosomal structural abnormalities have been identified in adamantinoma {9242211; 11227067; 18300815}, supporting the notion of a multistep evolutive process.

Recently, whole-exome sequencing and RNA sequencing in a small series of cases demonstrated genetic and transcriptomic differences between adamantinoma and OFD-like adamantinomas. Moreover, recurrent copy-number gains and losses in BRAF, JAK2, KRAS, TP53, or EGFR were found only in adamantinomas, whereas OFD-like adamantinomas showed only non-recurrent copy-number losses in JAK2 and KRAS. A fusion, EPHB4::MARCHF10 (MARCH10), was identified in one adamantinoma {31021853}.

DNA index studies have demonstrated that aneuploidy in adamantinomas is restricted to the epithelial component {7495301}. Similarly, TP53 aberrations are also restricted to the epithelial component.

Macroscopic appearance

Adamantinoma is a cortical, lobulated, grey/white mass, with or without involvement of the medullary cavity or adjacent soft tissues.

Histopathology

OFD-like adamantinoma morphologically overlaps with OFD but contains clusters of epithelial cells readily identifiable by H&E. Classic adamantinoma is dominated by epithelial elements, with only a minor OFD component. Epithelial cells in classic adamantinoma may be tubular, squamous, basaloid, or spindle-shaped. Dedifferentiated adamantinoma shows areas of classic adamantinoma merging with a high-grade sarcoma. Epithelial cells are positive in both subtypes for cytokeratin (typically CK14, CK19, CK5, and CK17) {7694513}, as well as for p63 {21674157}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for OFD-like adamantinoma: histology similar to that of OFD but with inconspicuous clusters of epithelial cells embedded within fibrous stroma; almost exclusively in the tibia and/or fibula, always involving cortex; for classic adamantinoma: epithelial malignancy with variable morphology arising within a fibro-osseous stromal background; almost exclusively in the tibia and/or fibula, always involving cortex.

Desirable: for OFD-like adamantinoma: may extend into the medullary cavity or adjacent soft tissues; for classic adamantinoma: often extends into the medullary cavity or adjacent soft tissues; variable morphology of epithelial component.

Staging

Staging according to bone sarcoma protocols.

Prognosis and prediction

OFD-like adamantinoma has a recurrence rate of approximately 20% {28249983}, almost never metastasizing {32769809}. In contrast, classic adamantinoma metastasizes in 12–29% of patients. The two subtypes have similar mortality rates {2743266}. Surgical resection with negative margins is the treatment of choice. Pathological fracture may predispose to recurrence {32769809}. Dedifferentiated adamantinoma has a more aggressive clinical course {14657712}.
