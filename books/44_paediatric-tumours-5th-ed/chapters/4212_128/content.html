4.2.1.2 Epibulbar osseous choristoma

Definition

Epibulbar osseous choristoma is a choristoma composed of mature bone.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.06 Epibulbar choristoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: episcleral osseous choristoma; osteoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Epibulbar osseous choristomas are typically unilateral and located in the superotemporal conjunctiva {22496017; 29589231; 22516954; 13871021}. They can also occur temporally, as well as (exceptionally) in the inferotemporal conjunctiva {13871021}.

Clinical features

Patients with epibulbar osseous choristoma present with a yellow, orange, pink, or white subconjunctival mass that may be multinodular and may be freely mobile or adherent to the sclera or muscles. The mass usually extends from the equator to the superotemporal fornix. Epibulbar osseous choristomas are firm, and calcification can be seen using CT {22496017}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of epibulbar osseous choristoma is unknown. Fewer than 80 cases have been reported. They are present from birth and frequently occur in patients younger than 20 years {22496017; 12358295}, but they can also be discovered later in life {18569837}. There appears to be a slight female predilection {13871021; 12358295}. Most reported cases have been in White patients. However, a few cases in African-American patients have been reported {22516954; 13871021}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Epibulbar osseous choristoma manifests as a firm whitish-yellowish mass.

Histopathology

Epibulbar osseous choristomas are composed of compact lamellar bone with normal osteocytes. Haversian canals can be found. Haematopoietic bone marrow is usually absent but has been reported (rarely) {12358295}. The bone is surrounded by dense fibrous tissue (periosteum) and an internal osteogenic layer {13871021}. Other types of tissue than bone, such as lacrimal acini, cartilage, nerves, or adipose tissue, may be present in the context of complex choristoma {22496017; 7733194; 3681614; 12358295}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: conjunctival localization; compact lamellar bone with normal osteocytes.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Epibulbar osseous choristomas are benign, and malignant transformation has not been described. It is believed that these lesions do not grow, although slight enlargement has been reported {22496017; 12358295}.
