5.2.3.5 Lymphatic anomalies

Definition

Lymphatic anomalies are localized or generalized malformations of the lymphatic system.

ICD-O coding

9170/0 Lymphangioma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

LA90.1Z Lymphatic malformations, unspecified

Related terminology

Not recommended: lymphangioma; cystic hygroma; lymphangioma circumscriptum; lymphangiomatosis.

Subtype(s)

Lymphangioma, NOS; common (cystic) lymphatic malformation (CLM); generalized lymphatic anomaly (GLA); central conduction lymphatic anomaly (CCLA), also known as channel-type lymphatic malformation; kaposiform lymphangiomatosis (KLA); primary lymphoedema (PL)

Localization

In CLM, the soft tissues of the axilla, chest, and cervicofacial region are most frequently involved, followed by the retroperitoneum, mediastinum, buttock, and anogenital area. Adjacent skin and mucosal surfaces may be affected. In GLA, soft tissue is most commonly involved multifocally, with bone and viscera (particularly lung and spleen) also often involved. In CCLA, distention of central conduction channels is present with reflux into neighbouring structures or organs. In KLA, the mediastinal soft tissue, lungs, bones, and spleen are the sites of predilection. In PL, the lower limbs are most commonly affected {26055853; 25241096; 31864531}.

Clinical features

CLM usually manifests as a localized or regional swelling, sometimes with cutaneous vesicles, or as one component of a combined vascular anomaly or syndrome. GLA often manifests as serous cavity effusions; thoracic duct abnormalities may be present. Patients with CCLA may present with serous cavity effusions, enteric protein loss, and/or organ dysfunction, and dysmotility or obstruction of major lymphatic channels including the thoracic duct may be seen. In KLA, serous cavity effusions, coagulopathy, and respiratory and multiorgan failure are dominant, and imaging findings incorporate features of GLA and CCLA. In PL, lymphoedema in one or both lower limbs is the most common presentation, with imaging demonstrating findings such as deficient tracer uptake, lymphatic hypoplasia, and reflux into dilated lymphatic channels {26055853; 31030813; 30542204; 32409509; 182058}. Increased lesional growth disproportionate to somatic growth occurs in some lesions.

Epidemiology

Children, adolescents, and young adults are most commonly affected.

Etiology

Somatic mutations in PIK3CA are usually encountered in both CLM and GLA {25681199; 29217067}, and somatic mutations in BRAF are occasionally found in CLM {35373151}. An EPHB4 mutation has been reported in CCLA {29905864}. KLAs have a somatic mutation in NRAS in most cases {30542204}, and rarely in HRAS {35202617}. Many genetic alterations and syndromes are associated with PL {32409509}.

Pathogenesis

RAS/MAPK signalling, acting in concert with PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling, is essential for vascular development. Loss of EPHB4 or RASA1 function results in constitutive activation of RAS/MAPK and AKT/mTOR signalling, causing abnormal endothelial differentiation and disordered vascular development {28687708}.

Macroscopic appearance

CLM may be macrocystic (sometimes defined as > 10 mm) or microcystic, or combined. The macrocystic form may be unilocular or multilocular with thick walls, and the microcystic form is spongiform with thin walls. The cystic content may be clear, haemorrhagic, and occasionally chylous. Spongiform and macrocystic forms can also be observed in GLA and KLA.

Histopathology

CLM, GLA, CCLA, and KLA display malformed lymphatic channels (often excessive in number) with irregularly muscularized walls. Large channels or cysts usually have predominantly fibrous walls with occasional strands of smooth muscle. The lymphatic endothelium is flat without atypia and is immunopositive for D2-40, PROX1, CD31, ERG, and FLI1. Endothelial cells often display only focal immunopositivity for CD34 and faint or focal immunopositivity for D2-40 in larger channels or cysts {26055853}. KLA virtually always has tiny foci of variably canalized kaposiform clusters of lymphatic endothelial cells with a similar immunophenotype. In PL, biopsies may show diminished or focally dilated lymphatic vessels, oedema, and fibrosis.

Cytology

Aspiration of cysts usually reveals abundant lymphocytes and some macrophages.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of mutations, most commonly in PIK3CA or NRAS, may help to assess the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: malformed and usually excessive lymphatic channels; correlation with clinical features and imaging.

Desirable: immunopositivity for D2-40 and PROX1; molecular evidence of various gene alterations associated with lymphatic malformations, particularly in KLA if biopsy samples are not diagnostic.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Sclerotherapy or operative excision are therapeutic options for CLM. GLA, CCLA, and KLA require a multimodal approach for control, with drugs that inhibit the PIK3CA/AKT/mTOR and RAS/MAPK/MEK pathways. KLA currently has a high mortality rate {31030813}.
