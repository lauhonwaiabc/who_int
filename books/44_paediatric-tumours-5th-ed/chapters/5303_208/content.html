5.3.0.3 CIC-rearranged sarcoma

Definition

CIC-rearranged sarcomas are high-grade sarcomas composed of primitive round cells harbouring rearrangements of the CIC gene (19q13.2).

ICD-O coding

9367/3 CIC-rearranged sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, primary site

Related terminology

Acceptable: CIC::DUX4 sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CIC-rearranged sarcomas usually occur in the deep soft tissues of the limbs or trunk, with smaller numbers occurring in the head and neck, retroperitoneum, and pelvis {28346326; 21813156}. Some arise in superficial soft tissues {32026485; 31870501}, and 10% arise in viscera, including the kidney, gastrointestinal tract, and brain {29901569; 28346326; 26685084; 26919435}. Fewer than 5% are primary to bone {28346326; 27079694}. CIC::NUTM1 sarcomas have a predilection for paravertebral soft tissue, skull, and the CNS of paediatric patients {30407212}.

Clinical features

CIC-rearranged sarcomas usually manifest as mass lesions with or without pain; some patients present with symptoms due to visceral involvement, and some present with symptoms of metastatic disease {27079694; 28346326; 29901569}.

Epidemiology

CIC-rearranged sarcomas occur over a wide age range, with as many as 22% of cases occurring in the paediatric age group {21813156; 28346326}. The peak incidence is between 25 and 35 years of age, and there is a slight male predominance {28346326; 27079694; 26685084}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

A CIC::DUX4 fusion is present in 95% of cases, resulting from either a t(4;19)(q35;q13) translocation or a t(10;19)(q26;q13) translocation {16717057; 21813156; 22072439; 19837261}. Less common partner genes include FOXO4, LEUTX, NUTM1, and NUTM2A {30407212; 28188754; 25007147; 26735859}. CIC encodes an HMG-box transcriptional repressor, and most breakpoints are located within CIC exon 20 {28233365}. The DUX4 gene encodes a double homeobox transcription factor located within the D4Z4 repeat region in the subtelomeric regions of chromosomes 4 and 10 (4q35 or 10q26.3). The predicted CIC::DUX4 chimeric protein retains the CIC HMG-box, with a large part of the N-terminal DUX4 being lost. In a subset of cases, the CIC::DUX4 fusion leads to a stop codon right after the breakpoint, suggesting that a truncated CIC protein may be sufficient to trigger oncogenesis {28233365; 28493604}. Trisomy 8 and MYC amplification are some of the other common genetic changes {24947144}. Concurrent CIC mutations have been described in the context of tumours with CIC::LEUTX fusion {26735859}. The CIC::DUX4 fusion enhances CIC transcriptional activity, which upregulates its targets including CCND2, MUC5AC, and PEA3-family genes {24723486; 16717057; 28404587}. Array-based DNA methylation profiling is helpful to differentiate CIC-rearranged tumours from other small round cell malignant soft tissue tumours {32155762; 29572501}.

Macroscopic appearance

CIC-rearranged sarcomas are well-circumscribed white to grey fleshy masses ranging in size from 30 to 140 mm. Areas of haemorrhage and necrosis are common {26685084; 29901569}.

Histopathology

CIC-rearranged sarcomas have similar morphological features regardless of the fusion partner. The tumour exhibits a diffuse or vaguely lobular or nodular architecture. Tumour cells are round to oval, small to intermediate in size, and exhibit focal spindling. There can be a moderate degree of nuclear pleomorphism and intratumoural heterogeneity. The cytoplasm is pale eosinophilic to clear, and epithelioid and rhabdoid cytomorphology has also been described. Nuclei are predominantly vesicular with prominent nucleoli, and mitoses are common. The stroma can be sclerotic or desmoplastic, and varying degrees of myxoid degeneration can sometimes be seen, resulting in a reticular or pseudoalveolar architecture {21813156; 22072439; 26685084; 28346326; 29901569; 27079694; 28197724}. Immunohistochemistry shows positivity for CD99 (diffuse or patchy, membranous and/or cytoplasmic), WT1 (nuclear with or without cytoplasmic in as many as 95% of cases) and ETV4 {27443513; 27562494; 27079694}. In the paediatric age group, WT1 has been reported to be less avid {29901569}. Tumours with the CIC::NUTM1 fusion show positivity for NUT {30407212}. BCOR, NKX2-2, keratin, S100, and myogenic markers are generally negative.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CIC gene rearrangements can be detected by a variety of molecular methods {19837261; 21813156; 28346326}, with next-generation sequencing assays being the most sensitive {28493604}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: predominant round cell phenotype; mild nuclear pleomorphism; epithelioid and/or spindle cell components; variably myxoid stroma; immunoprofile with variable CD99 staining and frequent WT1 and ETV4 positivity.

Desirable: demonstration of CIC gene rearrangements.

Staging

CIC-rearranged sarcomas can be staged using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM principles.

Prognosis and prediction

CIC-rearranged sarcomas are aggressive tumours, with frequent metastases and a poor outcome. Five-year survival rates range from 17% to 43%, and the response to standard Ewing sarcoma chemotherapy regimens is generally poor {28346326; 26685084; 30407212; 36791667}.
