2.3.6.1 Primary immunodeficiency-associated lymphoproliferative disorders

Definition

Lymphoproliferative disorders (LPDs) associated with primary immunodeficiencies (PIDs) are B- and T-lymphoid cell proliferations that arise in the setting of immunodeficiency due to a primary immunodeficiency or immunoregulatory disorder.

Subtype(s)

Localization

PID-associated LPDs may involve lymph nodes, spleen, or (frequently) extranodal sites such as the gastrointestinal tract and lungs, often with widespread disease.

Clinical features

Clinical features depend upon the type of LPD and underlying PID and include mass effects, organ-specific pathology, and EBV-related symptoms. LPD may be the first presentation of a PID. Patients, especially those with DNA repair disorders, may develop multiple genetically unrelated LPDs {18788073}.

Epidemiology

The frequency and epidemiological spectrum of LPD vary widely depending on the PID. The incidence is highest in DNA repair disorders, combined immunodeficiencies, antibody deficiencies, and diseases of immune dysregulation (see #19854Table 2.14) {31953710}. Overall, approximately 2.5% of patients with a PID develop a lymphoid neoplasm, representing an 8- to 10-fold increase in risk compared with the general population {20466855; 25451158; 28606585; 31275905}.

Etiology

The cause of the LPD is related to the underlying PID.

Pathogenesis

Ineffective immunological control of EBV, EBV-mediated oncogenesis, defective DNA damage repair, somatic genomic alterations, intrinsic lymphocyte defects secondary to PID-causing mutations, and chronic antigenic stimulation are implicated in the pathogenesis of different PID-associated LPDs {28669558}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Hyperplasias and polymorphic LPDs

PID-associated LPDs that do not meet the diagnostic criteria for lymphoma vary considerably in appearance between different PIDs and between individuals with the same PID.

Hyperplastic lymphoproliferations are expansile or infiltrative processes in which tissue architecture is preserved. The lymph nodes and spleen in patients with PIDs may have a nonspecific reactive appearance, but immunological dysfunction may result in architectural distortion, with the expansion, near absence, or poor definition of lymphoid compartments such as germinal centres, mantle zones, paracortex, or plasma cells. Sometimes immunostaining or clonality testing is required to exclude lymphoma. Features of lymph nodes and spleen in common variable immunodeficiency include small or irregular hyperplastic germinal centres, reduced numbers of class-switched plasma cells, and granulomas {1334378; 24789743; 29935219}. Lymphoid tissue in activated PI3Kδ syndrome types 1 and 2 typically shows follicular hyperplasia with poorly defined germinal centres, attenuated mantle zones, intrafollicular and interfollicular expansion of PD1+/CD57+ T cells, monocytoid B-cell hyperplasia, and reduced numbers of class-switched plasma cells {24165795; 27221134; 27555459}. Autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS) lymph nodes are characterized by paracortical expansion by medium-sized proliferative CD4−/CD8− (double-negative) CD45RO− T cells accompanied by hyperplastic, atrophic, or progressively transformed follicles {9811346}.

EBV-associated infectious mononucleosis–like LPDs are non-destructive but sometimes florid, polymorphic, immunoblast-rich infiltrates, similar to those found in infectious mononucleosis in immunocompetent patients; they may be associated with haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, such as that seen in X-linked lymphoproliferative syndrome 1 (XLP1) {3579509}. By contrast, lymphoid tissue or bone marrow from patients with chronic EBV viraemia typically shows a moderate number of small EBV-positive cells without atypia. Extranodal lymphoid hyperplasia is characteristic of several PIDs (see #19854Table 2.14). These may be T-cell infiltrates or mixed infiltrates with follicle formation, such as mucosal nodular lymphoid hyperplasia. Extranodal infiltrates of expanded minor physiological T-cell subsets (such as double-negative, γδ, and/or cytotoxic T cells) may be particularly difficult to distinguish from lymphoma/leukaemia {28395105}. EBV-positive mucocutaneous ulcers are circumscribed shallow ulcers containing EBV+/CD30+ immunoblasts and/or HRS-like B cells in a reactive immune background, which occur rarely in patients with PID but should be specifically diagnosed {20154586; 28395107}.

Polymorphic B-cell LPDs are characterized by the effacement of tissue architecture by a mixed infiltrate of small and medium-sized lymphocytes, immunoblasts, and plasma cells, representing the spectrum of B-cell maturation. In most cases, numerous EBV+ B cells are present. T cells may be prominent, CD30+ HRS-like B cells may be present, and light chain–restricted plasma cells may be detected. Criteria for distinguishing large cell–rich polymorphic LPD from diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) are not well defined {28395107}.

Lymphomas and leukaemias

PID-associated lymphomas and leukaemias can generally be classified according to the criteria established for diagnosis in immunocompetent individuals [[Swerdlow SH, Campo E, Harris NL, et al., editors. WHO classification of tumours of haematopoietic and lymphoid tissues. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2017. (WHO classification of tumours series, 4th rev. ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.fr/556.]]. PID-associated aggressive B-cell lymphomas are most often DLBCL-NOS, Burkitt lymphoma, or high-grade B-cell lymphoma NOS, and they are often EBV-positive. Patients with DLBCL may present with a dense monomorphic infiltrate of large B cells or (particularly in EBV-positive cases) with large, atypical, often HRS-like B cells dispersed in a background rich in T cells and histiocytes {28395108}. The neoplastic cells strongly express B-cell antigens and are often CD30-positive. EBV-positive DLBCL-NOS cases have a non–germinal-centre immunophenotype, whereas EBV-negative DLBCL-NOS cases may have either a germinal-centre or a non–germinal-centre phenotype.

PID-associated classic Hodgkin lymphoma (CHL) should fulfil the standard diagnostic criteria. Cases that resemble CHL but that show strong expression of CD20 by the HRS-like cells, EBV positivity in small B cells, light chain–restricted plasma cells, and/or a lytic or type III EBV latency pattern are better classified according to their overall features as DLBCL, polymorphic LPD, or EBV-positive mucocutaneous ulcer; the term “Hodgkin-like lymphoma” should be avoided.

Some PIDs in children are associated with an increased incidence of EBV-negative marginal zone lymphomas (usually extranodal), and EBV-positive extranodal marginal zone lymphomas with monocytoid and/or plasma cell differentiation have also been reported {25692616; 25749928; 27163767; 31942637}. PID-associated T-cell neoplasms, most often identified in patients with DNA repair disorders, are mostly peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS or T-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, but ALK-negative systemic or cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphomas are also increased in incidence {10573576; 27270301; 27515251; 31057537}. Occasional plasmacytomas are encountered {32371070}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

EBV positivity is best demonstrated, across all latency types, using in situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER). Lymphomas and leukaemias are expected to show clonal IG or TR gene rearrangements, and most polymorphic B-cell LPDs also show clonal IG rearrangements. However, clonal IG or TR gene rearrangements may also be detected in hyperplastic proliferations and alone are not sufficient for a diagnosis of neoplasia {14616793; 28395105}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Hyperplasias and polymorphic LPDs

Essential: a history of PID; variable alterations of germinal centres, mantle zones, and paracortex according to the type of immunodeficiency; EBV-associated LPDs can show a mixture of B-cell lymphoblasts, histiocytes, T cells, and/or plasma cells that can show light chain restriction; demonstration of EBV infection, preferably by EBER in situ hybridization.

Lymphomas and leukaemias

Essential: effaced architecture of lymph nodes or other organs; features of lymphoma (see the essential diagnostic criteria in sections on specific lymphomas).

Staging

The St. Jude staging system is used for non-Hodgkin lymphomas and the Lugano classification is used for paediatric Hodgkin lymphoma associated with HIV [[Amin MB, Edge S, Greene F, et al., editors. AJCC cancer staging manual. 8th ed. New York (NY): Springer; 2017.]].

Prognosis and prediction

Polymorphic LPDs and aggressive lymphomas are associated with the greatest morbidity and mortality, but extensive or symptomatic lymphoid hyperplasias and systemic EBV-driven proliferations often require treatment. Children with PID and a clinically significant LPD/lymphoma generally have worse outcomes than children without PID, with survival rates of approximately 50% in retrospective series, but allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation may achieve survival rates as high as 80% {10573576; 21923652; 26011068; 27515251; 32371070}.
