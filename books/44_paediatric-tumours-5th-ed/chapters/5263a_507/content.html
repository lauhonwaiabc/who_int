5.2.6.3A Intramuscular/juxta-articular myxoma

Definition

Intramuscular/juxta-articular myxomas are rare paediatric myxomatous lesions occurring in the muscles of the head and neck and adjacent to large joints, with morphological and immunohistochemical features similar to those of their counterparts in adults.

ICD-O coding

8840/0 Intramuscular/juxta-articular myxoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH6Q84 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Myxoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: myxoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intramuscular myxomas have been reported within the muscles of the head/neck region and the hand {11819080; 15368634; 17438256}; juxta-articular myxomas occur adjacent to large joints, most commonly the knee and occasionally the wrist and hip {7570164; 15680575; 20157818; 31875202}.

Clinical features

Overall these tumours occur sporadically, as slow-growing, solitary, painless or painful masses {17438256; 11819080}. On MRI, myxoma is bright in T2-weighted images and shows low signal intensity compared with skeletal muscle in T1-weighted images {9172250}. Intramuscular myxomas are mainly sporadic but may occur in combination with fibrous dysplasia, a condition called Mazabraud syndrome {4709710}.

Epidemiology

Muscular and juxta-articular myxomas are rarely reported in children {11819080; 15368634; 17438256; 7570164; 15680575; 20157818; 31875202}.

Etiology

In adults, activating point mutations (exons 8 and 9) in GNAS have been reported in intramuscular myxomas {30111377}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours appear as circumscribed nodular masses; the cut surface is grey or whitish, gelatinous, glistening, and sometimes cystic.

Histopathology

Intramuscular and juxta-articular myxomas share a common morphological theme: uniform, bland-looking, spindled to stellate cells with round/ovoid nuclei and palely eosinophilic cytoplasm, haphazardly embedded in an abundant myxoid stroma with sparse capillary-like blood vessels. Stromal cystic and frothy changes, sometimes mimicking univacuolated lipoblasts, may be seen. Both lesions may infiltrate, at least focally, into the surrounding tissues. Hypercellularity, sometimes associated with a more collagenous stroma and/or increased vascularity, internal fibrous septa, haemorrhage, chronic inflammation, and/or haemosiderin/fibrin deposition are focally observed. Immunohistochemically, the neoplastic cells are variably stained with CD34 and SMA {17438256; 20157818}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland-looking spindled to stellate cells set in a myxoid stroma.

Desirable: sparse capillary-like blood vessels.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

No local recurrence has been reported in children.
