5.2.3.6A Lobular capillary haemangioma (pyogenic granuloma)

Definition

Lobular capillary haemangioma (LCH, also known as pyogenic granuloma) is a common acquired vascular neoplasm of the skin and mucosae composed of lobules of capillary blood vessels.

ICD-O coding

9131/0 Lobular capillary haemangioma / pyogenic granuloma

9131/0 Disseminated lobular capillary haemangioma

9131/0 Subcutaneous lobular capillary haemangioma

9131/0 Intravascular lobular capillary haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2F26 Lobular capillary haemangioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: pyogenic granuloma.

Not recommended: granuloma telangiectaticum; granuloma gravidarum; pseudobotryomycosis.

Subtype(s)

Disseminated lobular capillary haemangioma; subcutaneous lobular capillary haemangioma; intravascular lobular capillary haemangioma

Localization

LCH mostly involves the skin and mucous membranes of the oral cavity and conjunctivae. Visceral lesions are very rare. Typical locations are the head and neck and the distal extremities {1792196; 14871318}.

Clinical features

Most patients with LCH present with rapidly growing, solitary, erythematous, dome-shaped or pedunculated papules that bleed easily {14871318}. Lesions can arise in port-wine stains and other vascular malformations {20940617; 22066576}. A congenital form, manifesting as multiple cutaneous and visceral lesions, has been reported {32622671}.

Epidemiology

LCH is one of the most common vascular tumours in children (mean age: 6.7 years) and the M:F ratio is 3:2 {1792196}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Trauma, infection, hormonal influences, drugs, and viral infections may have a role. Most children with LCH have no history of trauma or predisposing conditions {1792196}.

Pathogenesis

Pathological angiogenesis in LCH could be driven by FLT4 and the nitric oxide pathway {22955520}. COX-2 and IL-10 may be involved in the pathogenesis of oral cases {22153609}. BRAF and RAS mutations have been identified, suggesting a true neoplastic process {26802240}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions are polypoid, usually ulcerated with prominent crusting, and vary in size from a few millimetres to as large as 20 mm {1792196}.

Histopathology

LCH is an exophytic lesion with frequent ulceration, crusting, and neutrophilic inflammation that is usually restricted to the ulcerated area {7435775}. An epidermal collarette is present in most cases. LCH is composed of lobules of capillaries in an oedematous stroma. Vessels are lined by bland endothelial cells that are positive for CD31, CD34, and ERG. Focal epithelioid change may be seen. Capillaries are surrounded by a layer of SMA-positive pericytes. Mitotic activity is variable and can be prominent in early lesions. A feeding vessel is usually identified. Deep dermal and even subcutaneous lobules are sometimes seen. Evolving/late lesions develop perilobular fibrosis. Intravascular and purely deep dermal lesions rarely occur {575269; 17667179}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobules of capillary vessels; oedematous stroma.

Prognosis and prediction

Regression is not seen, but lesions may become smaller. Local recurrence is possible, and in children, recurrence may occur with multiple satellite papules {5647967}.
