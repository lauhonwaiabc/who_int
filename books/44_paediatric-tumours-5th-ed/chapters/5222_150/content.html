5.2.2.2 Gardner fibroma

Definition

Gardner fibroma (GF) is a benign fibroblastic/myofibroblastic, plaque-like lesion composed of dense collagen bundles with sparse fibroblasts, usually associated with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP).

ICD-O coding

8810/0 Gardner fibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH7GT0 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Gardner fibroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: Gardner-associated fibroma.

Not recommended: desmoid precursor lesion; nuchal fibroma; collagenosis nuchae.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GF arises in the superficial and deep soft tissues of the torso (most commonly in the back or paraspinal region), head/neck, and rarely other non-visceral sites (e.g. the mesentery) {11342777; 17325483}.

Clinical features

GF is a painless plaque/mass {11342777; 17325483}.

Epidemiology

GF is usually diagnosed in young children (aged < 10 years), including neonates, but it can be seen in older children {11342777; 17325483; 28018803}. In contrast, nuchal-type fibroma, which has many overlapping features with GF, occurs mainly in middle-aged men, and as many as 50% of nuchal-type fibroma patients have diabetes mellitus {7872429}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

GFs are associated with APC germline mutations and are often the first evidence of FAP {17325483; 26840078; 25074465}. Biallelic inactivation of APC has been reported in GFs, with documented germline mutations accompanied by somatic mutations of APC in tumour tissue {25074465}.

Macroscopic appearance

GF is poorly circumscribed, rubbery, and tan-white; it varies widely in size {11342777; 17325483}.

Histopathology

GF exhibits haphazardly arranged coarse collagen bundles interspersed with bland spindled fibroblasts and prominent clefts (cracking artefact). A helpful diagnostic feature is the entrapment of fat and neurovascular bundles at the edge of the lesion. These tumours are CD34-positive, SMA-negative, and often show nuclear β-catenin staining, reflective of their association with FAP {11342777; 17325483; 26840078}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

GF is associated with germline and somatic APC mutations {25074465}. An aggressive phenotype has been described with a specific c.4666dupA (p.T1556NfsX3) APC variant {30696621}. The molecular abnormalities in nuchal-type fibroma have no clinical relevance.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a poorly defined, bland and paucicellular, densely collagenized, CD34-positive tumour occurring in a child; often associated with FAP and APC mutations.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

GF is benign, but its occasional transformation into desmoid fibromatosis (in 20% of cases), as well as its association with FAP, should prompt further screening of the patient and their relatives {17325483; 25064609}.
