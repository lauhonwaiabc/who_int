5.2.2.2.3 Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis (hyaline fibromatosis syndrome)

Definition

Hyaline fibromatosis syndrome (HFS) is a rare autosomal recessive disease characterized by abnormal deposits of amorphous substance in the dermis, subcutaneous soft tissues, gingiva, and visceral sites, with clinical manifestations varying from very severe (infantile systemic hyalinosis) to milder (juvenile hyaline fibromatosis).

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: infantile systemic hyalinosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

HFS is a multisystem disease and involves the skin, subcutaneous tissue, viscera, and bone.

Clinical features

HFS manifests in infancy or later in childhood as slow-growing papular and nodular skin lesions {17284973; 30176098; 22383261}. Patients affected by juvenile hyaline fibromatosis show joint contractures (in 85–95% of cases), gingival hyperplasia, osteopenia, and osteolytic lesions. Infantile systemic hyalinosis frequently manifests with extreme pain at minimal handling. Visceral involvement with intractable diarrhoea (in 50%) and recurrent infections (in 30%) lead to failure to thrive and death {30176098; 22383261; 19344977}. A grading system for HFS has been proposed: grade 1, mild (skin and/or gingiva); grade 2, moderate (joint and/or bone); grade 3, severe (internal organ); and grade 4, life-limiting {19344977; 22771664}.

Epidemiology

HFS is rare. The incidence is unknown.

Etiology

HFS is caused by loss-of-function mutations including inactivating homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the ANTXR2 (CMG2) gene {29801470; 30176098; 17284973}.

Pathogenesis

HFS is caused by sequence variants in the ANTXR2 gene, which encodes a protein involved in basement membrane matrix assembly (in particular collagen VI homeostasis) and endothelial cell morphogenesis {17284973; 22215446; 28604699}. Phenotype–genotype correlations have demonstrated that ANTXR2 missense mutations in exons 1–12 and mutations leading to a premature stop codon more commonly lead to severe disease, whereas missense mutations in exons 13–17 lead to a mild form of disease {30176098; 29801470; 22215446; 20331448}.

Macroscopic appearance

The nodules are solid and homogeneous.

Histopathology

HFS lesions are characterized by plump to spindled uniform fibroblasts, often arranged in cords simulating vascular spaces, admixed with abundant extracellular eosinophilic, non-fibrillar hyaline material, obliterating normal structures {22383261; 15078358}. The hyaline-like extracellular matrix deposits stain strongly with PAS and are diastase-resistant {22383261; 15078358}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing is necessary to demonstrate a germline mutation of ANTXR2.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: subcutaneous nodules and gingival hypertrophy with prominent hyaline-like material.

Desirable: germline sequencing of ANTXR2.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Earlier presentation is associated with more severe disease, systemic involvement, and poor prognosis (median age at death: 15 months) {30176098; 19344977; 22383261}. In milder forms, wheelchair use may become necessary. Treatment is largely supportive. Surgical excision of subcutaneous nodules and/or gingivectomy for gingival hypertrophy is often performed; however, recurrences are common {30176098; 22383261}.
