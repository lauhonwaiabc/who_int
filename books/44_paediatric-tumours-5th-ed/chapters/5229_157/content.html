5.2.2.9 Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

Definition

Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma (LGMS) is a rarely metastasizing tumour of cells with myofibroblastic differentiation, typically with mild to moderate atypia, that has a predilection for head and neck sites in childhood.

ICD-O coding

8825/3 Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH2668 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: myofibrosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumours have a predilection for the extremities (40%) and the head and neck (30%) (especially the oral cavity, tongue, and face / facial bones). Occurrence in the trunk, abdomen/pelvis, or viscera is rarer {9777985; 11176071; 17513510; 27377169}. Subcutaneous or deep soft tissue sites are most frequent; dermal presentation is rare {11510507}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with an enlarging mass. Pain and associated symptoms are rare. Radiologically, the tumours show destructive growth.

Epidemiology

A proportion of tumours arise in paediatric patients (particularly in the head and neck), but most occur during the fourth or fifth decades of life, with a slight male predominance {9777985; 11176071; 8597827; 15173943; 31950474}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically firm, infiltrative lesions with pale, fibrous cut surfaces {9777985}, or (more rarely) circumscribed with pushing borders {11176071}.

Histopathology

Tumours are diffusely infiltrative, often growing between skeletal muscle fibres, and typically comprise cellular, loosely fascicular, or storiform proliferations of spindle cells with elongated vesicular nuclei, small pinpoint nucleoli, and poorly defined and palely eosinophilic cytoplasm, in a collagenous stroma. Rarely, neoplasms are hypocellular, with extensive collagenous/hyalinized matrix. LGMS can mimic benign or intermediate myofibroblastic neoplasms (e.g. nodular fasciitis or fibromatosis), and although the cells of LGMS can be bland in areas, there is at least focal atypia (typically mild to moderate) with enlarged, hyperchromatic, irregular nuclei. Mitotic activity is typically low, and necrosis is infrequent {9777985; 18400254; 11176071; 17513510; 15220822; 15173943; 22420727}. Some tumours progress to morphologically higher-grade myofibroblastic sarcomas {9777985; 11176071}. Tumours express SMA at least focally (frequently with subplasmalemmal accentuation) and calponin. Some express desmin; most lack h-caldesmon. About 30% express nuclear β-catenin {17711447}.

Ultrastructural studies show features of myofibroblasts (rough endoplasmic reticulum, peripheral contractile filaments, and the fibronexus cell–matrix junction) {15173943; 9777985; 18930983}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No recurrent molecular alterations have been described {10362810}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infiltrative cellular fascicles of myofibroblastic spindle cells; at least focal nuclear atypia; variable SMA and/or desmin expression.

Staging

The eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system is used.

Prognosis and prediction

LGMSs are prone to local recurrence; this is relatively uncommon after complete wide excision. Complete excision can be challenging in head and neck sites and patients may die from unresectable local disease {8597827}. Metastases are rare, frequently arising after prolonged intervals {9777985; 31950474; 22156834}.
