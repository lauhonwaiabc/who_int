5.2.10.5 Myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue

Definition

Myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue are rare in children. These neoplasms share morphological, immunophenotypic, and genetic features with their counterparts in the salivary gland and skin, and they are classified as benign (myoepithelioma and mixed tumour) or malignant (myoepithelial carcinoma).

ICD-O coding

8982/0 Myoepithelioma

8940/0 Mixed tumour

8982/3 Myoepithelial carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH3CQ8 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Myoepithelioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: parachordoma.

Subtype(s)

Myoepithelioma; mixed tumour; myoepithelial carcinoma

Localization

Most myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue (75%) arise on the extremities, more commonly the lower limbs. Tumours may also arise on the trunk or the head and neck {12960802; 18043035; 20815032}, where they usually involve subcutaneous tissue, although deep-seated and primary intradermal tumours occur {12960802; 14745720; 18043035; 23588365}. Rarely, tumours arise in bone or visceral organs {20815032; 24585572; 23681076}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a painless, palpable mass {8990137; 12960802; 18043035}. Patients with visceral or deep-seated tumours may have fever, cough, chest pain, respiratory distress, and paraesthesias or weakness due to spinal cord or nerve compression {18043035}.

Epidemiology

Overall, 20% of myoepithelial tumours arise in children, and they are more likely to be malignant in this age group {18043035}. In the paediatric population, myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue show equal distribution between the sexes and come to clinical attention any time from birth to 18 years of age (median: 9 years) {18043035; 24136896}. Rare congenital malignant tumours have been reported {18043035; 28675958}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

EWSR1 gene rearrangements are present in approximately half of all myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue {20815032} and have also been identified in primary skin and bone tumours {21725291; 23588365; 25231231; 31135487}. POU5F1 is the most common fusion partner (identified in as many as 28% of cases), especially in children, followed by PBX3 and PBX1 (in ~19% and ~11% of cases, respectively) {20815032; 31994243}. Other (rare) fusion partners include ZNF444, KLF15, KLF17, and ATF1, and occasional cases show alternate FUS gene rearrangements with similar partner genes {20815032; 25706482; 25231231; 22154050; 31994243}. Some genotype–phenotype associations have been suggested. Most tumours with EWSR1::POU5F1 fusions are malignant, arise in deep soft tissues of the extremities in young patients, and show nested and sheet-like growth of epithelioid cells, frequently with clear cytoplasm {20815032; 31994243}. Tumours with EWSR1::PBX1 or EWSR1::PBX3 fusions tend to be benign and composed of fascicles of spindle cells and stromal sclerosis {20815032; 31994243}; furthermore, EWSR1::PBX1 or EWSR1::PBX3 fusions are common in primary bone tumours {31994243}, and EWSR1::PBX3 is consistently present in cutaneous syncytial myoepithelioma {31135487}, which is very rare in children. A parachordoma-like morphology has been observed among myoepithelial tumours with FUS::KLF17 fusions {31994243}. A subset of myoepithelial carcinomas harbour homozygous deletions of SMARCB1 {24585572}.

Mixed tumours with ductal differentiation have PLAG1 gene rearrangements (occasionally with LIFR as the fusion partner) {22038920; 23630011}.

The presence of rearrangements of PLAG1 {15920557} and EWSR1 {25581728} in salivary gland pleomorphic adenoma and myoepithelial carcinoma, respectively, emphasizes the genetic relationship of soft tissue myoepithelial neoplasms to their salivary gland counterparts.

Macroscopic appearance

Benign myoepithelial tumours appear well circumscribed and nodular, whereas malignant tumours typically show infiltrative margins and a larger size. The cut surface is variably gelatinous and glistening to firm or fleshy. Tumour size ranges from 10 to 200 mm in greatest dimension (mean: 40–60 mm) {18043035; 12960802; 10389622}.

Histopathology

Myoepithelial tumours show marked architectural and cytological heterogeneity, both within a given tumour and between tumours. Most appear multinodular at low power, with variably myxoid, collagenous, or hyalinized stroma. Growth patterns are commonly reticular and trabecular, but they may be nested, fascicular, and solid {12960802; 18043035; 20815032}. Tumour cells are typically spindled or epithelioid, with uniform round or ovoid nuclei and palely eosinophilic, occasionally clear, cytoplasm. Associations between genotype and phenotype have been observed (see Pathogenesis, above). Some examples may appear more uniform, with a single predominant growth pattern and/or cytomorphological appearance, including myoepithelial tumours composed of large epithelioid cells with prominent cytoplasmic vacuolation (classified in the past as parachordoma {907973; 10478665}) and tumours with predominant spindled morphology, including the cutaneous syncytial form {23588365}. Occasional cases may show plasmacytoid cells with hyaline cytoplasmic inclusions {7541966; 8990137; 12960802}. Osseous or cartilaginous metaplasia may be present (in as many as 15% of cases), and squamous or adipocytic differentiation is seen (more rarely) {12960802}. Mixed tumours have a ductal component, appearing indistinguishable from salivary pleomorphic adenoma, and constitute approximately 10% of all soft tissue myoepithelial tumours.

Myoepitheliomas are benign, and cytological atypia is the sole criterion for malignancy. Myoepithelial carcinomas show appreciable nuclear atypia, often together with a high mitotic count and necrosis. Myoepithelial carcinoma may show an undifferentiated round cell component, notably in as many as one third of cases in paediatric patients {12960802; 18043035; 31994243}.

Immunohistochemistry

Confirmation of myoepithelial differentiation is necessary for diagnosis, although immunophenotypes can be variable. The vast majority (90%) of cases express both epithelial markers (broad-spectrum keratins and/or EMA) and S100 {18043035; 12960802; 10389622}. GFAP is positive in about 50% {18043035; 12960802}. SOX10 is positive in 80%, although in malignant tumours is it less frequent (30%) {25724000}. p63 is positive in a subset of cases (as many as 40%) {12960802; 22031315}. Expression of myogenic markers is more variable (and thus less helpful diagnostically); most frequently positive are calponin (90%), SMA (60%), and desmin (20%) {8990137; 12960802}. PLAG1 is positive in mixed tumours, correlating with PLAG1 gene rearrangement {22038920}. Loss of expression of SMARCB1 (INI1) occurs in a small subset of myoepithelial carcinomas (< 10%) {18043035; 19033866; 24585572}.

Cytology

Aspirate smears of myoepithelial tumours yield fragments of fibrillary myxoid stroma in which spindle, epithelioid, and plasmacytoid cells are embedded {26644362; 25693574}, like in their salivary counterparts. Rhabdoid morphology may be seen, as well as appreciable cytological atypia and undifferentiated round cells in myoepithelial carcinoma {25693574}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FISH can be used to detect rearrangements of EWSR1 in myoepithelioma/myoepithelial carcinoma or PLAG1 in mixed tumours. However, RNA or DNA sequencing panels that identify the fusion partner may be needed, because EWSR1 FISH alone may not be sufficient to distinguish myoepithelial neoplasms from other tumours with EWSR1 fusions. Specific fusion genes can be detected by targeted RNA or DNA sequencing panels.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: myoepithelial tumours show trabecular, reticular, nested, and/or solid growth, with a frequent myxoid or hyalinized stroma; mixed tumours show ductal differentiation (which may be focal); myoepithelial carcinomas show increased cytological atypia and mitotic activity; an immunophenotype supporting myoepithelial differentiation is also essential (positivity for EMA and/or keratin and for S100, SOX10, or GFAP).

Desirable (in selected cases): demonstration of EWSR1 rearrangements (these occur in ~50% of myoepithelial neoplasms, with a wide range of fusion partners).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Overall, most myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue show indolent behaviour. The single known reliable criterion for malignancy is the presence of moderate to severe nuclear atypia with discernible nucleoli {12960802}. Histologically benign tumours have a 20% risk of recurrence and only rarely metastasize. Myoepithelial carcinomas recur and metastasize in 40–50% of cases; sites of metastasis include lung/pleura, lymph nodes, bone, soft tissue, liver, bone marrow, and brain {12960802; 18043035}. In one series of paediatric tumours, 10% of patients died of disease {18043035}.
