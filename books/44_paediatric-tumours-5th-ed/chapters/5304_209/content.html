5.3.0.4 Sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations

Definition

Sarcomas with BCOR genetic alterations are clinically distinct sarcomas arising in soft tissues and bone, characterized by oncogenic BCOR fusion genes (BCOR fusion sarcomas) or internal tandem duplications (ITDs) in BCOR (BCOR ITD sarcomas). These sarcomas share a morphological spectrum and have similar immunoprofiles and gene expression. Other childhood solid tumours with BCOR ITD (and rarely fusions) include clear cell sarcoma of the kidney and high-grade neuroepithelial tumour.

ICD-O coding

9368/3 Sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations

ICD-11 coding

2B5F Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, primary site

Related terminology

Acceptable: BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma; BCOR-rearranged sarcoma; infantile undifferentiated round cell sarcoma; primitive myxoid mesenchymal tumour of infancy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma occurs slightly more often in bone than in soft tissue (ratio: 1.5:1), with a predilection for the pelvis, lower extremities, and paraspinal region {22387997; 24805859}. Rare locations include the head and neck region, lung, and kidney {29300189; 28817404; 28877060}. Infantile undifferentiated sarcomas and primitive myxoid mesenchymal tumours of infancy (PMMTIs) with BCOR ITD occur mainly in the soft tissues of the trunk, retroperitoneum, and head and neck, typically sparing the extremities {26945340}.

Clinical features

Pain (in 65% of cases) and swelling (in 40%) are the most common symptoms of BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma {25176412}. PMMTIs and infantile undifferentiated sarcomas manifest as soft tissue masses of variable size {16538060; 26945340}.

Epidemiology

Sarcomas with BCOR alterations are uncommon. BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma has a much lower incidence than Ewing sarcoma and a striking predilection for boys (> 90% of patients aged < 20 years are male; M:F ratio: 4.5:1) {29300189; 25176412; 28877060; 28420319; 24805859; 28197724; 25360585; 27228320; 26037154}. Sarcomas with BCOR ITD and PMMTIs occur within the first year of life and may be present at birth {16538060; 26945340}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

BCOR ITD and BCOR fusion sarcomas show a similar gene expression signature, including overexpression of BCOR and HOX genes {26573325; 26752546; 29431183; 27428733; 29300189}. Rare cases of clear cell sarcoma of the kidney and infantile undifferentiated round cell sarcomas with YWHAE::NUTM2B fusions also upregulate BCOR {26945340}. BCOR::CCNB3 sarcomas show upregulation of cyclin B3 (CCNB3). BCOR-altered sarcomas display a specific methylation profile, separate from that of brain tumours with BCOR ITD {36853789; 35174661}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically large (50–100 mm), tan-grey, soft to fleshy lesions, often with areas of necrosis {16538060; 25176412}. Osseous lesions often show cortical destruction and extension into the soft tissues {22387997}.

Histopathology

BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma is variably cellular and composed of primitive, small, round to slightly elongated cells with poorly defined eosinophilic cytoplasm, arranged in solid sheets or a vague nesting pattern, surrounded by a rich capillary network {28877060; 29300189; 24805859}. Less cellular areas can be seen, with plump or spindled discohesive cells in a prominent myxoid matrix rich in thin-walled branching vessels, and with microcystic areas. Cells arranged in short fascicles reminiscent of poorly differentiated synovial sarcoma (in short, cord-like structures and in clusters) have also been described {27228320; 25360585}. The nuclei have finely dispersed chromatin, and nucleoli are generally inconspicuous. Mitotic activity is variable {29300189; 25176412}. Metastatic/recurrent lesions occasionally display pleomorphic nuclei and osteoid deposition {29300189; 24805859}. Rare tumours reported in the kidney show similar histology to that of clear cell sarcoma of the kidney {28817404}.

BCOR ITD soft tissue tumours (infantile undifferentiated sarcoma and PMMTI) show variable cellularity ranging from solid sheets of small primitive ovoid cells to hypocellular areas of dispersed stellate cells within a myxoid matrix with delicate vasculature {16538060; 29430000; 26945340}. Tumours with predominant spindle cell morphology may mimic an infantile fibrosarcoma. Tumours infiltrate into adjacent structures and mitoses are frequent.

Immunohistochemically, all sarcomas with BCOR alterations display strong and diffuse nuclear BCOR positivity and in most cases also express SATB2, TLE1, and cyclin D1. CD99 is positive in approximately 50% of cases {29300189}. However, BCOR expression is not specific, being observed in half of all synovial sarcomas {27428733; 28877060}. BCOR::CCNB3 sarcomas also show reactivity for cyclin B3 (CCNB3), which is not seen in other BCOR-family tumours {24805859; 28877060; 26037154}. BCOR ITD soft tissue tumours are strongly and diffusely EGFR-positive {32790583}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BCOR gene rearrangements can be detected by FISH break-apart assays; RT-PCR for BCOR::CCNB3 transcripts can also be performed. BCOR ITD can be detected by targeted Sanger sequencing or fragment length analysis. All BCOR abnormalities can also be detected by next-generation sequencing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primitive round to spindle cells arranged in nests or sheets or with a fascicular growth pattern; variably myxoid stroma with delicate vasculature; immunohistochemical positivity for BCOR, SATB2, and cyclin D1.

Desirable: molecular confirmation of a BCOR genetic abnormality (fusion or ITD).

Staging

Sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations is staged under Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM principles.

Prognosis and prediction

Emerging data suggest that patients with BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma have similar 5-year survival rates to patients with Ewing sarcoma (72–80% {36791667}) and show histological response to Ewing sarcoma–based treatment regimens {25176412; 29300189; 24805859}. A significant proportion of patients present with metastatic disease; the most common metastatic site is the lung, followed by bone, soft tissue, and visceral locations {25176412; 29300189; 24805859}. In a recent study, the overall survival of patients with BCOR ITD PMMTIs / undifferentiated sarcomas was 34% at 5 years (median survival time: 26 months) {32372022}.
