5.2.3.1 Congenital haemangioma

Definition

Congenital haemangiomas are benign vascular tumours that are fully grown at birth, with the exception of a subgroup having undergone involution during fetal life.

ICD-O coding

9131/0 Congenital haemangioma

9131/0 Rapidly involuting congenital haemangioma with fetal involution

9131/0 Rapidly involuting congenital haemangioma

9131/0 Partially involuting congenital haemangioma

9131/0 Non-involuting congenital haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.2Y & XH27G6 Other specified benign vascular lesion of infancy and childhood & Congenital haemangioma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: congenital haemangioma.

Not recommended: capillary haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

Rapidly involuting congenital haemangioma with fetal involution; rapidly involuting congenital haemangioma (RICH); partially involuting congenital haemangioma (PICH); non-involuting congenital haemangioma (NICH)

Localization

All congenital haemangiomas have a predilection for the head and neck region and the lower extremities.

Clinical features

RICH with fetal involution is virtually regressed by birth, leaving only a cutaneous stain {25492638}. RICH, PICH, and NICH are well-circumscribed lesions usually measuring several centimetres in diameter. They are raised and pink, blue-red, or purple, with superficial telangiectasia and sometimes a pale halo. Some RICHs and PICHs also have central depression, ulceration, or scarring. Neonatal thrombocytopenia develops with some RICHs and PICHs. Arteriovenous shunting occurs rarely in RICH {11735711; 11391180; 15018449; 32281140}.

Epidemiology

Congenital haemangiomas are rare. There is no sex predominance.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

GNAQ and GNA11 somatic missense variants identified in RICH and NICH have been shown to constitutively activate MAPK and/or YAP1 signalling {27259057}.

Macroscopic appearance

RICH and NICH have a red homogeneous surface on sectioning. Occasional RICHs have foci of fibrosis or cysts.

Histopathology

All congenital haemangiomas involve skin and hypodermis. In RICH with fetal involution, a few small clusters of capillaries, enlarged dysmorphic veins, arteries with intimal fibrosis, and dilated lymphatic channels are present within a fibrotic dermis and hypodermis. At an early age, RICH, PICH, and NICH are usually similar, with lobules of capillaries with round or stellate lumina, and bland endothelial cells and pericytes. In RICH, regression is accompanied by smaller lobules; apparent loss of lobules; dysmorphic veins; extensive fibrous stroma; and occasionally haemosiderin, calcifications, and cystic spaces. Some RICHs display these changes at birth, indicating a degree of prenatal involution. NICHs in older children tend to have variably sized and often large lobules and prominent interlobular arteries and dysmorphic veins that at times incorporate lobules. The endothelium is hobnailed and pink; PAS-positive, diastase-resistant cytoplasmic globules often appear. All congenital haemangiomas have GLUT1-immunonegative endothelium {11735711; 11391180; 15018449; 32281140}. Transient and focal intralobular endothelial immunopositivity for D2-40 has been reported in RICH and PICH in association with neonatal thrombocytopenia {32281140; 25492638}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Most RICHs and NICHs have GNAQ or GNA11 mutations {27259057}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a lobular capillary lesion in RICH, PICH, and NICH, supported by clinical and imaging features.

Desirable: detection of GNAQ or GNA11 mutations.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most RICHs involute in < 1 year. PICHs partially involute and then stabilize. NICHs do not involute: their growth is proportional to that of the child, and a few show extension and increased blood flow over time {11735711; 11391180; 15018449; 32281140}.
