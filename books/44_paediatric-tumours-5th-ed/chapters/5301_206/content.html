5.3.0.1 Ewing sarcoma

Definition

Ewing sarcoma is a small round cell sarcoma with gene fusions involving a FET-family gene (usually EWSR1) and a member of the ETS family of transcription factors.

ICD-O coding

9364/3 Ewing sarcoma

9364/3 Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B52.3 Ewing sarcoma of soft tissue

2B52.Y Ewing sarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of other specified sites

Related terminology

Not recommended: diffuse endothelioma of bone; Askin tumour (Ewing sarcoma arising in the chest wall); primitive neuroectodermal tumour.

Note: some small round cell sarcomas previously considered subtypes of Ewing sarcoma (Ewing-like sarcomas) are genetically and clinically distinct entities and include CIC-rearranged sarcoma and sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations, described in separate sections.

Subtype(s)

Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma

Localization

Ewing sarcoma arises in the diaphysis and diaphyseal-metaphyseal portions of the long bones, pelvis, and ribs, although any bone can be affected. Extraskeletal Ewing sarcoma occurs in about 12% of patients {29977059} and has a wide anatomical distribution.

Clinical features

Ewing sarcoma usually manifests with localized pain followed by a palpable mass {10819277}. Bony tumours may be associated with pathological fracture. B symptoms (fever, night sweats, loss of appetite) are rare except in advanced or metastatic disease. Plain radiographs show a poorly defined, permeative, destructive or moth-eaten, osteolytic lesion with a prominent multilayered (onion-skin) periosteal reaction.

Epidemiology

Ewing sarcoma constitutes 6–8% of all primary malignant bone tumours; in the paediatric age group, it is the second most common after osteosarcoma {8000997}. The peak incidence occurs during the second decade of life. There is an overall male predilection (M:F ratio: 3:2). The incidence of Ewing sarcoma varies with ethnicity – the incidence in individuals of African or Asian descent is lower than in those of European descent (0.2, 0.8, and 1.5 cases per 1 million children annually, respectively) {19548262; 21212061}.

Etiology

Most Ewing sarcomas are sporadic. Germline predisposition is a possibility in rare cases {26580448; 6704922}.

Pathogenesis

In addition to FET::ETS fusion genes (see Definition, above), uncommon additional mutations may occur in STAG2 (in 15–22% of cases), CDKN2A (in 12%), and TP53 (in 7%) {25010205; 25186949; 25223734; 26580448}. FET::ETS fusion genes encode chimeric transcription factors that activate and repress thousands of genes. Expression of these aberrant transcription factors, in the correct cellular and developmental context (largely unknown), is required for the development of Ewing sarcoma. Proteins bind both GGAA microsatellites and canonical ETS binding sites in the genome and recruit chromatin regulators. Binding of the fusion proteins to GGAA microsatellites results in the transition from a closed chromatin state to an open state that establishes de novo enhancers, which activate genes. Conversely, binding to canonical ETS binding sites displaces wildtype ETS factors and represses gene expression. Together, these events establish an oncogenic gene expression programme that underlies transformation and subsequent tumour initiation.

Macroscopic appearance

Ewing sarcoma is a grey-white fleshy tumour with a soft consistency. Necrosis and haemorrhage may be present.

Histopathology

Ewing sarcoma features solid sheets of small round tumour cells with round nuclei containing finely granular chromatin; inconspicuous nucleoli; and scant, indistinct, clear to palely eosinophilic cytoplasm {19841938}. The tumour cells may be highly uniform or show some degree of cytological atypia, in which tumour cells are larger with nuclear contour irregularities and greater nucleolar prominence. Some cases may display neuroectodermal differentiation with well-formed pseudorosettes that feature cells radially oriented towards a central fibrillar core. Necrosis is a frequent finding and may be focal or extensive.

CD99 immunohistochemistry typically shows strong diffuse membranous reactivity, but this is not specific for Ewing sarcoma {8150461}. NKX2-2, likewise, is a highly sensitive but only moderately specific marker for Ewing sarcoma {30376220; 26847175}. FLI1 and ERG are often expressed in cases with the corresponding gene fusions {11117787; 21084965; 22766791}. Cytokeratins are expressed in a minority of Ewing sarcomas {16006796}. Other markers that may be positive include neuroendocrine antigens, S100, and TRK proteins.

Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcomas are a subset of lesions with identical fusions, characterized by nests of small cells with peripheral palisades of basaloid cells and immunohistochemical expression of high-molecular-weight cytokeratins {32932018; 26034869}.

Cytology

Cytological smears are highly cellular and show dispersed small round tumour cells. Characteristic features include finely granular chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli, and scant cytoplasmic rims. FNAB cytology with immunocytochemistry may be a reasonable diagnostic approach in certain settings {11835234}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The certainty of a diagnosis of Ewing sarcoma is significantly strengthened by molecular confirmation of a gene fusion involving a FET gene (FUS, EWSR1, or TAF15) and an ETS gene. The most common gene fusion is EWSR1::FLI1, resulting from a t(11;22)(q24;q12) translocation, present in about 85% of cases {1522903}. The exon breakpoints of the EWSR1 and FLI1 genes are variable, with the two most common breakpoint combinations being exons 7 and 6 of EWSR1 and exons 7 and 5 of FLI1. The second most common gene fusion is EWSR1::ERG, resulting from a t(21;22)(q22;q12) translocation, seen in about 10% of Ewing sarcomas {8162068}. Other reported FET::ETS gene fusions include EWSR1::ETV1 {22432475}, EWSR1::ETV4 {9858836}, EWSR1::FEV {21458383}, FUS::ERG {12907633}, and FUS::FEV {17620387}.

A FISH-based approach using break-apart probes for EWSR1 and/or FUS will uncover the majority of cases of Ewing sarcoma. However, a minority of cases with complex inversion/insertion structural rearrangements (as may occur with ERG gene rearrangements) or with cryptic insertions (described in certain EWSR1::FLI1 fusions) may be negative by FISH and require RNA-based techniques for molecular diagnosis {26690869; 20513536}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffuse infiltrates of small round tumour cells; diffuse and strong membranous CD99 immunoreactivity; exclusion of morphological mimics.

Desirable: identification of a FET::ETS gene fusion.

Staging

Ewing sarcoma is staged using the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

The presence of metastases appears to be the main prognostic factor. Other negative prognostic factors include the anatomical location of the tumour, such as the pelvis. Complete pathological response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a favourable prognostic factor {27482030}. The prognostic role of STAG2, CDKN2A, and TP53 mutations, as well as that of copy-number variations, is in the process of prospective validation {29977059}.
