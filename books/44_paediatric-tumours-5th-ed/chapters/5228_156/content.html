5.2.2.8 Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma / sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma

Definition

Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma (LGFMS) and sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma (SEF) are malignant fibroblastic neoplasms showing morphological and molecular overlap. LGFMS displays alternating collagenized and myxoid areas, with whorls of bland spindled cells. SEF comprises cords and nests of epithelioid fibroblasts in a sclerotic stroma.

ICD-O coding

8840/3 Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma

8840/3 Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH4V76 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma

2B5F.2 & XH4BT2 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: hyalinizing spindle cell tumour with giant rosettes.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours are usually deep-seated and subfascial, involving the proximal extremities or trunk {3673943; 17721195; 21550274; 11395547; 7661286; 20614275}. Rarer locations include the head and neck, intra-abdominal sites, retroperitoneum, mediastinum, viscera, or bone. Superficial LGFMSs are more common in children than in adults. SEFs in children occur with a similar anatomical distribution to those in adults, including in the extremities and in intra-abdominal/retroperitoneal and visceral sites {18275982; 21677541; 29385413; 15644777; 15322876; 24921641; 25353281; 32769431; 31592798; 31913156}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a painless mass. In patients aged < 21 years, most LGFMSs are < 50 mm at presentation {31736251} and typically localized, without evidence of nodal or distant metastases {31736251}.

Epidemiology

LGFMS typically occurs in young adults, with a wide age range. As many as 20% occur in patients aged < 18 years {3673943; 17721195; 15644777; 11023096; 7797200}; in a large study of LGFMS in children and adolescents, the median age at diagnosis was 10.9 years (range: first month of life to 17.1 years) {31736251}. There is a male predominance {31736251; 26204295}. Although LGFMS is rare, its incidence has probably been underestimated previously, because histologically it mimics other neoplasms (particularly benign ones). SEF usually arises in older patients, with a slight male predilection, but a small proportion of cases of SEF or mixed SEF/LGFMS can also occur in childhood {32769431; 31592798; 31913156}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Two thirds of LGFMSs show a t(7:16)(q33;p11) translocation; 25% harbour a supernumerary ring chromosome {12960807; 15139001; 15640831}. Both aberrations result in the fusion of the 5′ end of FUS (16p11) with the 3′ end of CREB3L2 (7q33), with a chimeric FUS::CREB3L2 transcript in > 90% {17721195; 15640831; 16931951; 21536545}. Rare cases show FUS::CREB3L1 or EWSR1::CREB3L1 {15640831; 23588368; 24896634}. The FUS::CREB3L2 protein results in deregulated expression of the CREB3L2 target genes CD24 and MUC4 {21536545}. SEF shows a similar gene expression profile {21536545; 28939748}.

The most common fusion (occurring in > 60% of cases) in SEF is EWSR1::CREB3L1 {24441665; 25123073; 25231134; 24025527}. Less often, EWSR1 is exchanged for FUS (the most common variant fusion partner) or PAX5, and/or CREB3L1 for CREB3L2, CREB3L3, or CREM {28939748; 28569045; 17721195; 25231134; 22388756}. Most hybrid SEF/LGFMSs harbour FUS::CREB3L2 fusions, like in LGFMS. In contrast, pure and hybrid SEFs typically have complex genomic profiles, with deletions or copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity at chromosome 11p, loss of chromosome 22, and intragenic DMD deletions {28939748}.

A small subset of SEFs (and hybrid SEF/LGFMS-like tumours) lack MUC4 expression or the above fusions; a proportion of these, including those occurring in childhood, harbour rearrangements of KMT2A and YAP1 genes (predominantly recurrent YAP1::KMT2A gene fusions, rarely KMT2A::YAP1); rarer PRRX1::KMT2D and KMT2D::PRRX1 fusions have also been described {32769431; 31592798; 31913156}. Gene expression profiling has shown tumours involving rearrangements of YAP1 and KMT2A and of PRRX1 and KMT2D to be distinct from conventional LGFMS/SEF {31913156}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, LGFMS and SEF range in size from 10 to > 200 mm and are circumscribed, lobular, and firm. The cut surface is white, fibrous, and often focally mucoid, sometimes with calcifications. LGFMS in paediatric patients is typically smaller (< 50 mm) {31736251}. SEF commonly involves the fascia or periosteum with bone erosion.

Histopathology

LGFMS comprises hypocellular collagenous areas and cellular myxoid nodules, with abrupt transition. The cells are spindled or angulated, with even chromatin and indiscernible or fibrillary cytoplasm (usually minimally atypical), and arranged in short fascicles or whorls {8333558}. Mitoses are generally inconspicuous. Vessels are arteriole-sized with perivascular sclerosis, or haemangiopericytic {21415703}. Around 30% contain scattered collagen rosettes, with hyalinized cores surrounded by epithelioid tumour cells {11023096; 9414192}. Focal atypia, hypercellularity, epithelioid features, and heterotopic ossification are rare. Occasionally there are areas of SEF {3673943; 17721195; 21415703; 21550274}.

SEF comprises hyalinized sclerotic collagenous stroma containing relatively bland, monomorphic epithelioid cells in cords, nests, or larger sheets, or in pseudoalveolar or acinar patterns. Cells typically have clear cytoplasm and inconspicuous nucleoli, with few mitoses. Areas of conventional LGFMS may be present {25231134; 22982887}. Unusual features include marked pleomorphism, necrosis, and prominent mitoses {11395547; 7661286; 22982887; 9822926}. Calcifications or chondro-osseous differentiation may occur.

Immunohistochemistry

Strong diffuse cytoplasmic expression of MUC4 is seen in about 99% of LGFMSs and 90% of SEFs {21415703; 22982887} and is highly sensitive and specific for these among fibroblastic and neural neoplasms. MUC4-negative SEF can show histological features similar to those of MUC4-positive SEF and hybrid LGFMS/SEF with fusions of FUS or EWSR1 with CREB3L1 or CREB3L2, and prominent epithelioid cellular morphology. However, MUC4-negative SEF shows a spectrum of morphological features. YAP1- and KMT2A-rearranged tumours display more variable cellularity; a less prominent nested pattern; and more sparsely cellular, hyalinized collagenous areas centrally {31913156}. In particular, YAP1- and KMT2A-rearranged tumours can manifest as small, superficial, infiltrative neoplasms and can be deceptively hypocellular and fibrotic, mimicking benign lesions {31592798}. EMA is expressed in 80% of LGFMSs and 40% of SEFs {17721195; 11023096; 21415703}. There is variable expression of CD34 and claudin-1 {19540561}. Keratins, S100, and GFAP are typically negative, distinguishing SEF from carcinoma and myoepithelial neoplasms {11395547; 20614275}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

If MUC4 is negative or unavailable, identification of any of the fusion genes, or identification of FUS or EWSR1 rearrangement, supports the diagnosis. LGFMS typically harbours FUS::CREB3L2 or FUS::CREB3L1 fusions; these also occur in SEF, but EWSR1::CREB3L1 is more common.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

LGFMS

Essential: alternating fibrous/myxoid areas; bland spindle cells in whorls / short fascicles; diffuse strong MUC4 (in nearly all cases).

Desirable: demonstration of FUS gene rearrangement (in selected cases).

SEF

Essential: epithelioid cells in cords, nests, or trabeculae, in a dense sclerotic stroma; diffuse strong MUC4; keratin-negative.

Desirable: demonstration of EWSR1::CREB3L1 or other rearrangements (in selected cases).

Staging

The eighth-edition American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) system is used.

Prognosis and prediction

Surgery remains the therapeutic mainstay {26204295}. Although LGFMS shows low rates of recurrence and metastasis at 5 years (10% and 5%, respectively), these rates are much higher with long-term follow-up {3673943; 17721195; 15644777; 11023096; 7797200; 9822926; 8333558}. A large study showed recurrences, metastases, and death from disease in 64%, 45%, and 42% of cases, respectively {3673943}. Metastases (most commonly to lung and pleura) occurred as late as 45 years after primary excision (median: 5 years), with a median interval to tumour-related death of 15 years. Cases of LGFMS with areas of SEF or round cell morphology are more aggressive (similarly aggressive to SEF) {3673943}. Studies of LGFMS in children and adolescents show a better prognosis {26204295; 31736251}, with a 5-year event-free survival of 71%, local recurrence rate of 19%, and distant metastasis rate of 6% at first relapse {31736251}. The development of metastatic disease is very low in this population, although follow-up has been short (2.7 and 3.2 years) in these specific paediatric and adolescent population studies {26204295; 31736251}; patients with R1 resection and T2 neoplasms have a higher risk of local and metastatic disease {31736251}. SEF is aggressive, with recurrences in about 50% and metastases (usually lung, pleura, bone, and brain) in 40–50% of cases. Large size and proximal location are prognostically adverse {11395547; 7661286}.
