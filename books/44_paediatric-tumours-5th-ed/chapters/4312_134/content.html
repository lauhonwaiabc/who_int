4.3.1.2 Lisch nodule (iris hamartoma)

Definition

A Lisch nodule is a melanocytic hamartoma of the iris, comprising benign, predominantly spindled melanocytes and occurring in neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1).

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: iris hamartoma; melanocytic hamartoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lisch nodules occur on the iris, mainly the inferior half {14560838}.

Clinical features

The presence of ≥ 2 Lisch nodules is one of the seven clinical diagnostic criteria for NF1 established by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH) {3128965}. They are small, bilateral, minimally elevated iris nodules, predominantly in the inferior iris {14560838}, that usually appear in early childhood (~2 years). Their colour can vary from tan to cream {27493794}. They can be mistaken for iris mammillations {8763309}.

Epidemiology

In NF1, the prevalence of Lisch nodules rises with age {1901624}. They occur in 63% of NF1 patients of all ages and in > 92% of postpubertal adults with NF1 {14560838}.

Etiology

The main etiology is mutations in the NF1 gene. A unilateral case without NF1 has been reported, although this may be a forme fruste {12654389}. Other rare associations described are Cushing disease {8470969}, familial angiolipomatosis {10506451}, and neurofibromatosis type 2 {2510706}. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation may play a role {19516012}.

Pathogenesis

Mutations in NF1 lead to the dysregulation of RAS signalling {3107130; 20027112}. UV radiation–induced DNA damage may play a possible role {19516012}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features (above).

Histopathology

On the anterior face of the iris, there is usually a dome-shaped plaque of mostly spindle-shaped, cytologically bland melanocytes, arranged haphazardly. Nucleoli are not prominent and mitotic figures are not identified. Ovoid to rounded melanocytes and melanophages can also be present. The underlying iris stroma often contains similar melanocytes arranged with a poorly defined margin {6814406; 1908964}. The melanocytes are positive for S100. Transmission electron microscopy shows cells containing immature and mature melanosomes. No pre-melanosomes are seen {6814406}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

To date, no molecular pathology studies have been performed on Lisch nodules.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour localized to the iris; the melanocytes must be cytologically benign.

Desirable: immunohistochemical confirmation of melanocytic lineage (e.g. S100, melan-A, SOX10).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lisch nodules are neither sight-threatening nor do they undergo malignant transformation.
