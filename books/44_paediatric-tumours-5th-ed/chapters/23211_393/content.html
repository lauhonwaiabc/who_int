2.3.2.11 EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (EBV+ DLBCL) is a large B-cell neoplasm in which the majority of the neoplastic cells are infected by EBV. Patients with EBV+ DLBCL do not have a history of previous lymphoma or congenital (primary) or acquired immunodeficiency and the neoplasm does not fulfil the criteria for other specific EBV+ lymphoproliferative disorders or lymphomas.

ICD-O coding

9680/3 EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A81.ZY & XH1QK0 Other specified diffuse large B-cell lymphomas & EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In younger patients, EBV+ DLBCL appears to be primarily nodal, with a minor subset of patients also having extranodal disease {25999451; 24789501}.

Clinical features

The clinical features at presentation are variable. Patients may present with low- or high-stage disease. EBV serology and/or serum EBV viral load are recommended as part of the clinical workup and for disease monitoring. Cases occurring in immunodeficiency settings are excluded, and primary immunodeficiency, HIV, posttransplant status, or (less commonly in this age group) iatrogenic immunosuppression should be carefully evaluated. Lymphomas in those patients are classified under immunodeficiency-associated lymphoproliferative disorders.

Epidemiology

These are uncommon lymphomas, and most occur in older adults where immunosenescence may be a factor. However, they can occur at any age and in the absence of any known immunodeficiency. Although uncommon in Europe and North America, the incidence appears to be higher in Asian, African, and Latin American countries, where EBV infection tends to occur at a younger age {25704629; 24789501; 20591151; 9308726}. There appears to be no sex predominance {22987474}.

Etiology

Although the etiology is largely unclear, it is speculated that immune system immaturity might play a role in the development of the disease, similarly to immunosenescence in elderly patients with defective surveillance of EBV-infected cells.

Pathogenesis

Primary immunodeficiencies in T and NK cells play a pivotal role in some cases with genetic defects that affect B- and T-cell interactions {27931829; 31942615}, and these cases should be considered under the category of immunodeficiency-associated lymphoproliferative disorders. Although the pathogenesis remains uncertain, LMP1-mediated promotion of NF-κB signalling is believed to contribute to clonal B-cell proliferation {14523386; 15510157}. A permissive tumour microenvironment may also play a role {25999451}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Most cases demonstrate scattered large B cells in a background of numerous small lymphocytes and histiocytes, in a pattern that has been described as T-cell histiocyte large B-cell lymphoma–like {25999451}. The large cells can resemble HRS cells or immunoblasts. Other cases appear monomorphic and similar to DLBCL-NOS. Importantly in paediatric patients, infectious mononucleosis can exhibit similar morphological features. However, there is at least partial preservation of the lymph node architecture, with parafollicular hyperplasia consisting of a mixture of various-sized B and T cells. Consideration of the clinical presentation and serological findings is essential to avoid a misdiagnosis of EBV+ DLBCL in such cases.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells demonstrate a B-cell immunophenotype with expression of CD20, PAX5, CD79, OCT2, and BOB1. Most cases show a non–germinal-centre immunophenotype with expression of IRF4 (MUM1) but not CD10. BCL6 is variably positive. CD30 is positive in most cases, with expression ranging from focal and weak to strong and uniform, but CD15 is usually negative. EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) and LMP1 are uniformly positive in the large cells, while EBNA2 is negative in most cases, consistent with type II EBV latency {25999451; 25704629; 22987474}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Monoclonal IG rearrangements are detectable in about half of the cases {25999451}. In one small study, MYC, BCL2, and BCL6 translocations were not detected {25704629}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: architectural effacement by large neoplastic cells with a B-cell immunophenotype; evidence of EBV infection of neoplastic cells by positivity for EBER (preferred) or LMP1 immunohistochemistry; exclusion of history of previous lymphoma or primary or acquired immunodeficiency.

Staging

Staging is based on the St. Jude classification {7414342}, which has been recently updated {25940716}.

Prognosis and prediction

Unlike older adults, in whom EBV+ DLBCL is associated with worse outcomes, younger patients typically have a good prognosis {25999451; 25475080} unless the disease is refractory to primary therapy {30151967}.
