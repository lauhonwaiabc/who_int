2.2.4.3 Myeloid neoplasms associated with germline predisposition

Definition

Myeloid neoplasms associated with germline predisposition arise in individuals with specific genetic pathogenic gene variants and include acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), and juvenile myelomonocytic leukaemia (JMML).

Localization

Myeloid neoplasms associated with germline predisposition manifest in the blood and bone marrow. Depending on the syndrome, benign and/or malignant manifestations may affect other organs.

Clinical features

This heterogeneous group of diseases is caused by germline pathogenic variants in genes (or by related mechanisms) that affect critical biological pathways leading to varying abnormalities of benign, malignant, haematopoietic, and/or non-haematopoietic disorders.

For each condition and its clinical features, please see #20123Table 2.06 ; for myeloid proliferations associated with Down syndrome, please see the next section.

The term “aberration of indeterminate potential in patients with Fanconi anaemia” has been proposed for classifying Fanconi anaemia with clonal aberrations in cases not fulfilling the diagnosis of myeloid neoplasia {34196171}.

Following the classification of MDS with excess blasts, the classification for myeloid neoplasia has been adopted for patients with Fanconi anaemia. This may serve as a model for other myeloid neoplasms with a germline predisposition.

Epidemiology

With the exception of Down syndrome (incidence: 100–313 cases per 100 000 live births) and neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) (incidence: 33 cases per 100 000 live births), individual syndromes predisposing to myeloid neoplasms are rare but in aggregate contribute to a growing subset of patients with myeloid neoplasms. Among children with AML, Down syndrome occurs in 5–10% of cases, and all other syndromes are rare. Among children with MDS, genetic conditions are common and increasingly recognized. About 25% of children with JMML have CBL syndrome or NF1.

Etiology

CEBPA-associated familial AML: Disease gene CEBPA; haematopoietic transcription factor {15575056; 28637622}.

Familial platelet disorder with associated myeloid malignancy: Disease gene RUNX1; haematopoietic transcription factor {10508512}.

GATA2-deficiency: Disease gene GATA2; haematopoietic transcription factor {21670465; 21892158; 26702063}.

Severe congenital neutropenia: Disease genes ELANE, HAX1, others; misfolded protein response, other functions {11001877}.

Shwachman–Diamond syndrome: Disease genes SBDS (EFL1, DNAJC21, and SRP54); ribosome biogenesis and function {28942353; 12496757}.

Fanconi anaemia: Disease genes FANC family; homologous recombination, DNA crosslink repair {28575658}.

Dyskeratosis congenita and other telomere biology disorders: Disease genes DKC1, TINF2, TERC, TERT, NHP2, NOP10, NAF1, PARN, WRAP53, ACD, STN1, CTC1, RTEL1, TINF2, POT1, others; telomere maintenance {9590285; 31478401; 33035329}.

RASopathies (NF1 and CBL syndrome): Disease genes NF1 and CBL; RAS signalling {9180088; 20694012}.

MIRAGE syndrome (myelodysplasia, infection, restriction of growth, adrenal hypoplasia, genital phenotypes, and enteropathy): Disease gene SAMD9; endosome fusion {27182967}.

SAMD9L-related ataxia-pancytopenia (ATXPC) syndrome: Disease gene SAMD9L; endosome fusion {27259050}.

ANKRD26-related thrombocytopenia: Disease gene ANKRD26; cilia gating {21211618}.

Down syndrome: See next section.

In addition, myeloid neoplasms may arise in individuals with Li–Fraumeni syndrome, constitutional mismatch repair deficiency, constitutional trisomy 8 mosaicism, MECOM-related syndrome, and other conditions.

Pathogenesis

In general, individuals with myeloid neoplasms with a germline predisposition acquire additional somatic genetic events in the haematopoietic system, which eventually lead to neoplastic transformation. Notably, not all acquired anomalies are associated with transformation. For each AML type, the targets of frequently observed secondary somatic events are as follows:

CEBPA-associated familial AML: CEBPA (C-terminal).

Familial platelet disorder with associated myeloid malignancy: RUNX1, GATA2, monosomy 7.

GATA2 deficiency: RUNX1, SETBP1, IKZF1, CRLF2, monosomy 7, trisomy 8.

Severe congenital neutropenia: Monosomy 7, RUNX1, CSF3R.

Shwachman–Diamond syndrome: TP53, monosomy 7 / del(7q). TP53 mutations in this syndrome are not diagnostic of MDS, nor do they portend impending malignant transformation; they can persist at a low variant allele frequency (VAF) for many years without progression. Common cytogenetic abnormalities that are not associated with a high risk of progression to MDS/AML include del(20q) and isochromosome 7q.

Fanconi anaemia: Monosomy 7, 3q amplification (MECOM [EVI1]), RUNX1.

Telomere biology disorders: Monosomy 7.

NF1 and CBL syndrome: NF1, CBL.

MIRAGE syndrome: Partial loss of chromosome 7 carrying the SAMD9 variant.

SAMD9L-related ATXPC syndrome: Partial loss of chromosome 7 carrying the SAMD9L variant.

ANKRD26-related thrombocytopenia: Not specified.

Histopathology

The histopathology of myeloid neoplasms associated with germline predisposition is not pathognomonic for a specific subentity {30573048}. The combination of the clinicopathological presentation and the molecular findings provides the ultimate diagnosis. The somatic mutation spectrum and cytogenetic clonal abnormalities may hint at the presence of an underlying syndrome (e.g. isochromosome 7 may suggest Shwachman–Diamond syndrome).

Whereas histopathology is mostly nonspecific, with many patients presenting with aplastic anaemia, MDS/AML, or normal bone marrow findings, certain subentities do have characteristic morphological findings that can narrow the differential. Familial platelet disorder with associated myeloid malignancy shows megakaryodyspoiesis, characterized by > 10% of the megakaryocytes being small in size and showing hypolobation {32238878; 30600763}; severe congenital neutropenia caused by pathogenic variants of ELANE shows increased peritrabecular myeloid precursor expansion, promyelocyte arrest with decreased to absent mature neutrophils, and increased eosinophils {20727024}. Shwachman–Diamond syndrome shows hypocellularity with incomplete nuclear segmentation and variable hypogranulation of neutrophils {31879230}, as well as variable low-level dysplasia of the megakaryocytic and (less commonly) erythroid lineages. Fanconi anaemia shows hypocellularity with low-level baseline dysplasias. RASopathies with leukocytosis (as seen in some cases of Noonan syndrome) shows hypercellular marrow with myeloid hyperplasia.

Notably, the bone marrow findings in patients with inherited bone marrow failure such as that of Fanconi anaemia can resemble those of refractory cytopenia of childhood. Therefore, these conditions need to be ruled out before the diagnosis. As a consequence, refractory cytopenia of childhood cannot be diagnosed in a child with an inherited bone marrow failure disorder. The diagnosis of MDS requires the presence of leukaemic blasts or other signs of transformation (e.g. monosomy 7 in a patient with Fanconi anaemia).

Diagnostic molecular pathology

To diagnose these myeloid neoplasms, standard procedures are required that do not differ from the procedures for diagnosing acquired myeloid neoplasms. To diagnose the underlying condition, a range of additional tests are required.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for all conditions, clinical examination, haematological evaluation, and mutation analysis lead to the diagnosis; in individuals with a germline predisposition, it may be necessary to analyse non-haematopoietic tissue such as fibroblasts.

Desirable: because germline predisposition may not always have a recognizable phenotype, a high degree of suspicion is warranted, and screening for underlying germline variants in myeloid malignancies (especially MDS) should be considered.

Ancillary tests include: for Shwachman–Diamond syndrome: pancreatic isoamylase; for MIRAGE syndrome and SAMD9L-related ATXPC syndrome: immunoglobulin levels, lymphocyte subsets; for Fanconi anaemia: hypersensitivity of Fanconi anaemia cells to diepoxybutane (DEB) or mitomycin C (MMC) (DEB or MMC chromosomal breakage); for telomere biology disorders: immunoglobulin levels, lymphocyte subsets, telomere length.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis largely depends on early diagnosis and appropriate medical care. Although myeloid neoplasms associated with germline predisposition often have a worse prognosis than their sporadic counterparts, this is not true for all conditions (e.g. JMML in patients with CBL syndrome, MDS with GATA2 deficiency, and AML with CEBPA mutation).
