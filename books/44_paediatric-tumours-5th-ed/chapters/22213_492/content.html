2.2.2.13 Desmoid fibromatosis

Definition

Desmoid fibromatosis is a locally aggressive, non-metastasizing (myo)fibroblastic neoplasm with a high propensity for local recurrence.

ICD-O coding

8821/1 Extra-abdominal desmoid

8822/1 Abdominal fibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH13Z3 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Desmoid-type fibromatosis (aggressive fibromatosis)

2F7C & XH6116 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Abdominal (mesenteric) fibromatosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: aggressive fibromatosis; desmoid tumour.

Not recommended: musculoaponeurotic fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

Extra-abdominal desmoid; abdominal fibromatosis

Localization

In childhood, desmoid fibromatosis predominantly occurs in the extremities, head/neck, and trunk {1946077; 19950127; 24206198}.

Clinical features

Patients with desmoid fibromatosis present with a slow-growing deep-seated mass.

Epidemiology

Desmoid fibromatosis is rare, affecting < 4 patients per 1 million person-years and accounting for approximately 2–3% of soft tissue tumours in children {7091046; 1946077; 19950127; 22924016; 28209418}. The peak incidence in childhood is between the ages of 6 and 15 years {19950127}. There is no sex predominance in paediatric patients.

Etiology

Genetic, physical, and hormonal factors are involved in the etiology of desmoid tumours {28961825}.

Pathogenesis

The majority (90–95%) of sporadic desmoid tumours result from mutations in CTNNB1, the gene that encodes β-catenin {17721184; 18832571; 23913621; 20197769; 26171757}. Less commonly, desmoid tumours arise in the setting of familial adenomatous polyposis (germline mutations in the APC tumour suppressor gene) {10094547; 11241320; 8150351; 18722078; 15095846}. Aberrations in CTNNB1 or APC lead to nuclear β-catenin accumulation, resulting in increased activation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway and tumorigenesis {19436199; 28575679; 17952864}.

Macroscopic appearance

Desmoid fibromatosis is firm and poorly circumscribed, with a grey/tan whorled cut surface.

Histopathology

Histological examination reveals bland (myo)fibroblasts arranged in long, sweeping fascicles with an infiltrative growth pattern. Approximately 20% have an associated Gardner fibroma {17325483; 25064609}. The lesional cells typically show tram-track staining for SMA, and the majority (80%) show nuclear β-catenin expression.

Cytology

The cytological findings are nonspecific {8498134}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

β-catenin mutation analysis may be helpful in small biopsies and/or when β-catenin immunostaining is difficult to interpret {21884214; 22766794}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infiltrative long, sweeping fascicles of bland fibroblasts and myofibroblasts.

Desirable: nuclear β-catenin expression; CTNNB1 mutation (in challenging cases or small biopsies).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The behaviour of desmoid fibromatosis is variable with unpredictable growth, stabilization, and regression {19950127}. Local recurrence does not consistently correlate with surgical margin status, and a watchful waiting approach is suggested for asymptomatic patients {21844500; 22419028; 28961825}.
