2.3.2.8 Plasmablastic lymphoma

Definition

Plasmablastic lymphoma (PBL) is an aggressive lymphoma composed of large atypical B cells with plasmablastic and immunoblastic morphology and a terminal B-cell differentiation phenotype that includes downregulation of mature B-cell markers and expression of plasma cell–associated antigens.

ICD-O coding

9735/3 Plasmablastic lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A81.2 Plasmablastic lymphoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PBL in children involves extranodal locations at diagnosis, such as the nasal/oral cavity (~52% of cases), skin and soft tissue (~16%), digestive system (~15%), and CNS (~3%), but it can also involve nodal locations (~16%) {23036261; 24771257}.

Clinical features

In children, PBL arises de novo and transformation from a previous lymphoma/leukaemia has not been reported {23282972; 23791008}.

Epidemiology

PBL accounts for approximately 1% of all large B-cell lymphomas and 2% of all HIV-related lymphomas in adults {29019447; 25636338}. PBL is an uncommon disease in children, with 27 cases reported so far in the English literature. There is a male predilection (M:F ratio: 1.6:1). The mean age (in children) is 10 years (range: 1–18 years) {23036261; 24771257; 20582963; 29456531; 28631667}.

Etiology

Immunodeficiency is a predisposing factor in almost every reported paediatric patient. Most cases occur in the contexts of HIV infection (74%), a history of immunosuppressive therapy associated with bone marrow / solid organ transplantation (14%), or primary immunodeficiencies such as adenosine deaminase–deficient severe combined immunodeficiency. In some cases there is no evidence of immunodeficiency {23036261; 24771257; 20582963; 29456531; 28631667}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

PBL is characterized by a diffuse and cohesive infiltration of large immunoblasts and plasmablasts. A prominent starry-sky pattern is frequently found. Mitoses are common. Few cases may show smaller lymphoplasmacytic cells intermingled with the large cells. Areas of necrosis may be seen {15166665}.

Immunophenotype

CD20, PAX5, and CD45 are either negative or weakly positive in a minority of cells. CD79a is expressed in approximately 40% of cases. The IRF4 (MUM1) and plasma cell–related markers CD138, CD38, VS38c, Blimp1 (PRDM1), and XBP1s are found in the majority of cases. CD10, CD56, and CD30 are positive in 20–40% of cases. BCL6 positivity is rarely found. The Ki-67 index is usually > 90% {15166665; 23036261; 25636338; 27687004; 20418245}. Light chain restriction may usually be demonstrated. MYC protein expression is consistently found, with higher expression in cases with MYC rearrangements. Phosphorylated STAT3 (Y705) overexpression is seen in cases with STAT3 mutations {32273478}. Overexpression of PDL1 {27175027} and loss or aberrant expression of MHC class II molecules have been demonstrated in some adult cases {22689685}, coupled with an enrichment in tumour-associated macrophages and PD1-reactive T lymphocytes in the microenvironment {27175027; 32273478}. In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is positive in virtually all paediatric cases. LMP1 is usually negative. ALK protein expression and KSHV/HHV8 are negative {25636338}. The differential diagnosis in the paediatric population includes ALK-positive large B-cell lymphoma and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular features have been described mainly in adults. Clonal IG gene rearrangements can be found even in cases without detectable immunoglobulin expression. MYC translocations, usually with IG genes, are more frequent in HIV-positive patients (80%) than in HIV-negative patients (40–60%). MYC gain/amplifications are uncommon {20962620; 32273478; 24832164}. STAT3 SH2 domain mutations are found in 13% of cases {32273478}. Other uncommonly mutated genes include BRAF, EP300, CD79A, and CD79B {32273478}. PRDM1 (BLIMP1) variants have been found in 45% of cases but their oncogenic role needs to be confirmed {27687004}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lymphoma with immunoblastic or plasmablastic morphology; terminal B-cell differentiation phenotype with the expression of plasma cell–associated antigens (e.g. CD138, IRF4 [MUM1]); negativity for CD20, ALK, and KSHV/HHV8.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of PBL in children remains poor, but it seems slightly better than in adults. With a median follow-up of 36 months (range: 6–156 months), the survival rate is 40%. Survival was significantly longer in posttransplant cases than in HIV-related cases, although treatment modalities were heterogeneous and historic cases were not treated with combined chemotherapy and antiretroviral therapy {24771257; 23036261; 20582963; 29456531; 28631667; 15166665; 24832164}.
