5.2.2.14A Fibroma of tendon sheath

Definition

Fibroma of tendon sheath (FOTS) is a benign fibroblastic/myofibroblastic, nodular, bland proliferation that attaches to tendon.

ICD-O coding

8813/0 Fibroma of tendon sheath

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH0WB3 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Fibroma of tendon sheath

Related terminology

Not recommended: tenosynovial fibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

FOTS usually occurs on the finger tendons (most commonly in the thumb, index finger, and middle finger) and rarely in the knee, elbow, or wrist {2299175; 32349463; 24366807; 30783711}.

Clinical features

FOTS is a slow-growing, painless nodule, measuring < 30 mm. FOTS may occasionally be multiple {22028571}.

Epidemiology

FOTS usually occurs in young or middle-aged adults, but cases in infants and children have been reported {2299175; 32349463; 24366807; 30783711; 91424; 1946077; 2729499}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Chromosome 11q rearrangements have been identified in 2 cases of FOTS {9639118; 25202108}. There are increasing reports of FOTS-like tumours that harbour genetic alterations of USP6, and these are probably better classified as nodular fasciitis {27125357; 32583473; 16434899}.

Macroscopic appearance

FOTS has a lobular, tan-white cut surface.

Histopathology

Histological examination of FOTS reveals a circumscribed arrangement of bland fibroblasts and myofibroblasts in a densely collagenous or hyalinized stroma. The cellularity is typically low. Characteristic slit-like blood vessels are often prominent at the periphery of the tumour {91424; 2729499}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a firm nodule consisting of a paucicellular proliferation of fibroblasts/myofibroblasts in a collagenous background with slit-like blood vessels, attached to tendon.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

FOTS is benign, but recurrences may occur in as many as 24% of patients {91424}.
