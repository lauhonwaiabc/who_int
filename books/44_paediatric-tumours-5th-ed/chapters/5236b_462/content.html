5.2.3.6B Epithelioid haemangioma

Definition

Epithelioid haemangioma is a benign tumour of well-formed blood vessels lined by epithelioid endothelial cells with abundant cytoplasm and a variable eosinophilic infiltrate.

ICD-O coding

9125/0 Epithelioid haemangioma

9125/0 Cellular epithelioid haemangioma

9125/0 Atypical epithelioid haemangioma

9125/0 Intravascular epithelioid haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.0Y & XH10T4 Other specified neoplastic haemangioma & Epithelioid haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiolymphoid hyperplasia with eosinophilia; histiocytoid haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

Cellular epithelioid haemangioma; atypical epithelioid haemangioma; intravascular epithelioid haemangioma

Localization

Cutaneous tumours usually involve the head and neck {4008683}. Soft tissue tumours usually involve the extremities {26135557}. Osseous lesions usually involve tubular bones {18852673}. Penile cases are rare {31693501; 15087672; 26135557}.

Clinical features

Patients with cutaneous tumours present with one or sometimes multiple erythematous to violaceous papule(s) or nodule(s) {4008683; 29266025}. In children, the lower extremities are commonly involved and may be multifocal {30207085}.

Epidemiology

Most cases occur in adults. In children, the mean patient age is 14 years (range: 2–18 years) {30207085}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. A subset may be due to a reactive process secondary to an underlying arteriovenous malformation or traumatic pseudoaneurysm {1924276}.

Pathogenesis

Rearrangements of FOS or FOSB are seen in approximately half of all cases {26135557}. Fusion-positive tumours are usually soft tissue and osseous tumours {26135557; 26173738}. Fusion partners for FOS include LMNA, MBNL1, VIM, and lincRNA. For FOSB the fusion partner is typically ZFP36 and rarely WWTR1 or ACTB {25043949; 26135557; 26173738}. The FOS family of transcription factors are leucine zipper proteins that dimerize with JUN-family proteins. In FOS fusions, there is loss of the C-terminal part, preventing rapid degradation of FOS and prolonged activation {29150442}. FOSB fusions result in activating promoter-swap, leading to the upregulation of FOSB {28797505}. Cutaneous tumours lack fusions {26135557}.

Macroscopic appearance

Epithelioid haemangioma is usually small (most are < 20 mm, range: 5–70 mm) {4008683; 18852673}. Cutaneous/soft tissue tumours are usually unremarkable. Osseous tumours are haemorrhagic and may expand the bone {18852673}.

Histopathology

Epithelioid haemangioma is composed of a lobular proliferation of well-formed blood vessels lined by endothelial cells with enlarged vesicular nuclei, small nucleoli, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Intracytoplasmic vacuoles may be present. There is no multilayering, and vessels have a pericyte layer. The stroma is variably oedematous and may have a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with eosinophils. A subset have more solid growth and atypia (atypical epithelioid haemangioma) {25043949}. Intravascular epithelioid haemangioma arises within veins {32094426}. They express CD31, CD34, ERG, and frequently FOSB (in ~50% of cases) {28009608}. EMA and keratin immunoreactivity is common {8333560; 21904630; 21317715}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of FOS or FOSB rearrangement is useful in a subset {26135557; 26173738}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-formed vessels lined by epithelioid endothelial cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.

Desirable: variable eosinophilic infiltrate; immunohistochemical expression of panendothelial markers and FOSB; FOS or FOSB rearrangement in selected cases.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Epithelioid haemangiomas are benign, but local recurrence is common.
