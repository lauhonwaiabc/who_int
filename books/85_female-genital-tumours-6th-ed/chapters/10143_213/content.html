10.1.4.3 Liposarcoma

Definition

Liposarcoma is a heterogeneous group of mesenchymal neoplasms with adipocytic differentiation and varying biological behaviour, ranging from locally aggressive to metastasizing.

Myxoid liposarcoma

Not recommended: round cell liposarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Atypical lipomatous tumour / Well-differentiated liposarcoma; myxoid liposarcoma; dedifferentiated liposarcoma; pleomorphic liposarcoma.

Localization

Liposarcomas may arise in the vulva, vagina, and uterus in the female genital tract.

Clinical features

Liposarcoma of the lower genital tract is rare. Atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma (ALT/WDLPS) and myxoid liposarcoma are the more frequently reported types at this site {9475187; 25851712; 29019868}, whereas dedifferentiated liposarcoma and pleomorphic liposarcoma are exceptionally uncommon {8926618; 33422117}.

Epidemiology

Liposarcoma of the female genital tract is very rare and only several cases have been published to date. Information on the occurrence is therefore limited to the case reports. Patients with ALT/WDLPS are in the fifth to sixth decades of life at diagnosis. Dedifferentiated liposarcoma and pleomorphic liposarcoma are exceptionally uncommon {8926618; 33422117}. Patients with myxoid liposarcoma are comparatively younger at diagnosis, ranging from the second to fourth decade of life {8926618; 8475187; 25851712}.

Macroscopic appearance

ALT/WDLPS is composed of lobulated adipocytic tissue with a yellow to tan cut surface that may contain grossly visible strands of fibrous tissue {17197914; 9475187}. Dedifferentiated areas are red-tan-grey and firm or fleshy {10872666}. Myxoid liposarcoma is well circumscribed and multinodular, with a gelatinous tan-white-grey cut surface {25851712; 17510918}. Round cell regions in myxoid liposarcoma are firm or fleshy {17510918}. Pleomorphic liposarcoma has variable gross features, ranging from well-defined to infiltrative, with a tan-yellow cut surface and with or without necrosis {11979090}.

Histopathology

Liposarcomas described in the female genital tract include ALT/WDLPS, dedifferentiated liposarcoma, myxoid liposarcoma, and pleomorphic liposarcoma.

The histopathological findings are identical to those of liposarcomas elsewhere (see the sixth-edition Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of the WHO Classification series).

ALT/WDLPS has sheets and lobules of relatively well-differentiated adipocytes that vary in size and shape and at least focal nuclear enlargement and hyperchromasia. Fibrous bands separate adipocytes into lobules and contain scattered enlarged, hyperchromatic stromal cells. Univacuolated or multivacuolated lipoblasts can be present but are not required for diagnosis. In dedifferentiated liposarcoma, zones of dedifferentiation usually consist of higher-grade sarcoma that can take on a variety of homologous and heterologous morphological manifestations (cellular, pleomorphic, myxoid, etc.) {10872666; 11979090}. Mitotic rate is used to distinguish dedifferentiated liposarcoma from ALT/WDLPS with non-lipogenic areas (also known as cellular ALT/WDLPS), although HPF diameter was not indicated for most of these studies to accurately count the number of mitosis/mm2; only cellular ALT/WDLPS with ≥ 5 mitoses/10 HPFs should be considered dedifferentiated liposarcoma {17197914; 25581729; 18704991; 37057834}.

Myxoid liposarcoma has a lobulated or multinodular mixture of non-lipogenic low-grade–appearing spindle cells, variable quantities of univacuolated or multivacuolated lipoblasts, ample myxoid stroma, and prominent arborizing thin-walled vasculature. The periphery of tumour lobules/nodules tends to have increased cellularity {30853315}. In contrast, high-grade myxoid liposarcoma is defined by ≥ 5% of the tumour volume containing distinctive hypercellular areas with relatively higher-grade cytological atypia and more mitotic activity. The presence and percentage of hypercellular areas should be recorded. Some cases show transitional areas with modestly increased cellularity without elevated nuclear grade and mitotic activity {8554106; 30853315}.

Pleomorphic liposarcoma is a morphologically heterologous high-grade sarcoma with lipoblastic differentiation in the form of large, hyperchromatic multivacuolated lipoblasts {11979090; 36006773}. Homologous lipoblastic differentiation in a dedifferentiated liposarcoma is distinguished from pleomorphic liposarcoma by the presence of a conventional component of ALT/WDLPS and an underlying MDM2 gene amplification {20588177; 20431480}.

Cytology

Smears of ALT/WDLPS display cohesive tissue fragments of adipocytes showing variable sizes and nuclear atypia, from slight hyperchromasia to pronounced pleomorphism and multinucleation. Floret cells and lipoblasts may be present.

Myxoid liposarcoma shows an abundant extracellular myxoid stroma and tissue fragments, containing small anastomosing capillary vessels in a plexiform or arcade pattern, along with small, uniform spindle to ovoid cells with slightly irregular nuclear membranes and small prominent nucleoli and a variable numbers of small and large lipoblasts. High-grade myxoid liposarcomas contain small round cells with increased N:C ratios.

Pleomorphic liposarcoma displays loosely cohesive tissue fragments and scattered single cells displaying marked pleomorphism, as well as stripped nuclei, along with variable numbers of lipoblasts {15108227; 17973999; 25132684; 31042519}.

However, the diagnostic value of cytology is limited.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

ALT/WDLPS and dedifferentiated liposarcoma are associated with amplification of MDM2 and usually CDK4 {1568170; 17895748; 25862836; 21117066}. The majority (> 90%) of myxoid liposarcomas have recurrent t(12;22)(q13;p11), producing FUS::DDIT3 gene fusion; the remaining tumours have recurrent t(12;22)(q13;q12), generating EWSR1::DDIT3 gene fusion {7805034; 17647282}. Pleomorphic liposarcomas have non-recurrent complex karyotypic abnormalities and frequent TP53 and NF1 mutations {1568170; 20601955; 9829856; 18784837; 21598240}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

ALT/WDLPS: mature and atypical adipocytes and thickened fibrous septa with cells containing atypical nuclei – lipoblasts may or may not be present.

Dedifferentiated liposarcoma: non-lipogenic sarcoma that is higher-grade and more mitotically active (≥ 5 mitoses/10 HPFs) than the coexisting ALT/WDLPS.

Myxoid liposarcoma: a mixture of non-lipogenic spindle cells, lipoblasts, myxoid stroma, and thin-walled branched vasculature.

Pleomorphic liposarcoma: multivacuolated, enlarged lipoblasts in a high-grade sarcoma.

Desirable:

ALT/WDLPS and dedifferentiated liposarcoma: MDM2 amplification or protein overexpression; myxoid liposarcoma: DDIT3 rearrangement.

Staging

This entity is staged using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Most lower genital liposarcomas are associated with infrequent local and distant recurrences {9475187; 25851712}. Myxoid liposarcomas that have ≥ 5% round cell or hypercellular areas are considered higher-grade, and patient outcome is generally unfavourable {17510918}.
