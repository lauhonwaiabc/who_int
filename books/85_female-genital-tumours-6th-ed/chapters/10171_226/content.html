10.1.7.1 Rhabdomyoma

Definition

Rhabdomyoma is a benign mesenchymal tumour demonstrating skeletal muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: genital rhabdomyoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most tumours arise in the vagina. Less frequently, they develop in the cervix or vulva.

Clinical features

Genital rhabdomyomas are often asymptomatic and discovered during clinical examination for other reasons {22325314}. When symptomatic, these lesions can cause dyspareunia, bleeding, or urinary tract compression.

Epidemiology

Genital rhabdomyoma is the least common subtype of extracardiac rhabdomyoma. Because of the rarity of these tumours, with < 30 reported cases in the vagina and 3 in the cervix, epidemiological data remain limited {22325314; 28700439; 32769342}. It generally occurs in young to middle-aged female individuals (median age: 60 years; range 30–65 years).

Etiology

In contrast to fetal rhabdomyomas, genital rhabdomyomas have not been reported in association with basal cell naevus syndrome.

Pathogenesis

One cervical tumour revealed activation of the AKT/mTOR signalling pathway by immunohistochemical analysis {26247533}. A genome-wide microarray study did not detect somatic copy-number alterations or allelic loss events in 2 cases of genital rhabdomyoma {28700439}. There was no evidence of somatic changes involving hedgehog pathway genes.

Macroscopic appearance

Rhabdomyoma is often a solitary polypoid or nodular lesion < 20 mm, with a grey, rubbery to glistening cut surface.

Histopathology

Genital rhabdomyomas are characterized by a subepithelial proliferation of polygonal, spindled, and/or elongated rhabdomyoblasts set in fibrous stroma. Some rhabdomyoblasts exhibit distinct cross striations. A cambium layer and mitotic activity are absent, but scattered cells with cytological atypia may be observed {28700439}. Tumour cells are positive for desmin and markers of skeletal muscle differentiation, including myogenin and MYOD1.

Cytology

Smears show tissue fragments and scattered cells containing abundant eosinophilic, granular cytoplasm, peripherally placed round nuclei, and prominent nucleoli {19217050; 21053538}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed subepithelial proliferation of mature rhabdomyoblasts without atypia and mitotic activity; lack of cambium layer.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Genital rhabdomyoma is benign and cured by total excision {22325314; 28700439}.
