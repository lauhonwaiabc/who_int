4.5.1 Neuroendocrine tumours

Definition

Neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) are cytologically low-grade primary epithelial neoplasms with morphological and immunohistochemical features of neuroendocrine differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: typical carcinoid; atypical carcinoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

Well-differentiated NET; mixed neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine neoplasm (MiNEN) (81540/3)

Localization

These tumours are localized to the cervix.

Clinical features

Patients with cervical NET typically present with symptoms similar to those of other cervical tumours including abnormal vaginal bleeding, abnormal cervical cytology screening results, and detection of a cervical mass {28878659}. Hormone-related symptoms are uncommon, although occasional cases have been associated with elevated urinary 5-HIAA levels {10566674}. Carcinoid syndrome can occur if metastatic disease involves the liver {10566674}.

Epidemiology

NETs of the cervix are extremely rare tumours, and precise incidence figures are lacking. However, available literature suggests that most cases are diagnosed in the third to fifth decades of life {28878659; 35075048; 37567596}. [[Mittal, P, Nagargoje, A, Chopra, S, et al. Clinical Outcomes of Neuroendocrine Carcinoma of the Cervix: Institutional Audit. Indian J Gynecol Oncolog. 2025;23(96). https://doi.org/10.1007/s40944-025-01026-9]].

Etiology

From the limited literature available on these rare tumours, some NETs are HPV associated and some HPV independent, while mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNENs) are more likely to be HPV associated, particularly types 16 and 18 {35075048; 29555602}.

Pathogenesis

NETs of the cervix are thought to arise from neuroendocrine cells within the cervix, especially within the endocervical glands, while MiNENs may develop through a divergent differentiation process. The histological overlap and potential regression of neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) to NET following chemotherapy, which has been reported in occasional cases {35075048}, suggest that tumour plasticity and differentiation shifts could play a role in pathogenesis of occasional cases. Molecular studies have identified alterations in pathways such as PI3K/AKT/mTOR and TP53 {35075048}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically, there are no characteristic features, but these tumours typically present as solid, well-circumscribed or polypoid masses {35075048}.

Histopathology

NETs exhibit a trabecular, pseudoglandular, rosette-like, nested or insular growth pattern. The neoplastic cells are monotonous and cuboidal/columnar/polygonal, with a variable amount of cytoplasm. The nuclei can be either regular with uniform chromatin and small nucleoli (grade 1 tumours) or variable in size and shape with irregularly distributed chromatin (grade 2 tumours). High-grade nuclear atypia should not be present. Grade 1 tumours show rare mitotic figures, whereas grade 2 tumours can show 5–10 mitotic figures /2 mm2 (5–10 mitotic figures /10 HPFs of 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area), as well as foci of necrosis. Immunohistochemical staining shows positivity, which is usually, but not always, diffuse for neuroendocrine markers such as chromogranin, synaptophysin, INSM1, and CD56, with variable expression of cytokeratins {35075048; 35406607}. Neoplasms that are HPV-associated exhibit block-type staining with p16, while HPV-independent neoplasms exhibit negative/non–block-type staining with this marker.

MiNENs comprise a combination of neuroendocrine neoplasm (including NET) and a non-neuroendocrine neoplasm such as adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or adenosquamous carcinoma {35406607}. MiNENs frequently exhibit shared mutations between the neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine components, suggesting a common clonal origin. When reporting such mixed neoplasms, it is recommended to document the individual tumour types and their approximate percentages.

Cytology

Cytologically, NETs typically consist of small to medium-sized monomorphic cells with scant cytoplasm and finely granular chromatin, often described as having a salt-and-pepper appearance because of the dispersed nature of the chromatin {35406607}. Nucleoli are usually inconspicuous, and nuclear pleomorphism is minimal.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: low-grade epithelial neoplasm with limited mitotic activity (≤ 10 mitotic figures / 2 mm2) and morphological and immunohistochemical features of neuroendocrine differentiation.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

NETs generally follow an indolent course but can metastasize to distant sites including the liver, lymph nodes, and lung. Unlike in NETs of other organs, the value of mitotic activity assessment and Ki-67 proliferation index in prognostication is not established. Also, unlike the case with NETs in other organs, a category of grade 3 NET is not yet included because of insufficient evidence, although occasional examples have been reported {34570015}.
