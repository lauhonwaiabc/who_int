10.1.9.11 Undifferentiated uterine sarcoma not classified further

Definition

Undifferentiated uterine sarcoma not classified further is a malignant mesenchymal tumour lacking evidence of specific lines of differentiation; therefore, this is a diagnosis of exclusion.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours arise in the endometrium and/or myometrium, and rarely in the cervix of the uterus.

Clinical features

Patients are typically postmenopausal {25341584; 30789359; 28622181; 18580489; 39921027; 37193821} and present with abnormal uterine bleeding, pelvic pain/pressure, and symptoms related to extrauterine disease {30772825}.

Epidemiology

Sarcomas of the female genital tract are very rare {25341584; 21826194; 30541504}, although their incidence has been steadily increasing. Reported age-standardized incidence rates for uterine sarcoma include 10.55 cases per million in Israel (2021), 7.4 cases per million in the UK, and 5.8 cases per million in Germany {25853381; 34736574}. In case series, undifferentiated uterine sarcomas have been reported at a median age of 63 years, with a wide range spanning 14 to 85 years {37193821}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

This category appears to be a highly biologically heterogeneous group of uterine sarcomas, as tumours show varied gene expression patterns related to genital tract development, extracellular matrix, muscle function, and proliferation, as well as highly variable copy-number alterations, with some showing extensive chromosomal gains and losses while others are near diploid {30617134}.

Macroscopic appearance

Undifferentiated uterine sarcomas not classified further form large intracavitary and polypoid or intramural fleshy masses frequently with haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

Like its soft tissue counterpart, undifferentiated uterine sarcoma not classified further shows no identifiable line of differentiation. At present, this entity is considered likely to be a heterogeneous group of tumours and a diagnosis of exclusion. Therefore, adequate sampling is important to exclude the presence of a better-differentiated mesenchymal (i.e. morphologically recognizable) or epithelial component that would impact diagnostic classification.

Undifferentiated uterine sarcomas not classified further typically display a destructive pattern of myometrial invasion. In the past, these tumours have been classified as uniform or pleomorphic types {18580489}. Currently, tumours with uniform nuclear features frequently represent high-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma, SMARCA4-deficient uterine sarcoma, or undifferentiated endometrial carcinoma, and thus these entities should be excluded prior to making the diagnosis {39921027}. More commonly, tumours presently within the category of undifferentiated uterine sarcoma not classified further typically consist of sheets of variably pleomorphic epithelioid and/or spindled cells associated with brisk mitotic activity. Necrosis and lymphovascular invasion are commonly seen {19898427}. Unlike fusion-driven uterine sarcomas, which are often monomorphic, most pleomorphic undifferentiated uterine sarcomas show abnormal/mutant-type p53 staining {33118176}. Tumours may show focal nonspecific staining for smooth muscle actin but should be negative for desmin or caldesmon {28622181}. Some may show positivity for ER and/or PR, as well as variable, nonspecific positivity for CD10 {15863163}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Although undifferentiated uterine sarcomas frequently exhibit high copy-number alterations {30617134; 28570276}, there are no pathognomonic/diagnostic molecular alterations, and the role of diagnostic molecular pathology is primarily to exclude the presence of molecular alterations associated with other recognized entities and/or provisional entities.

Pathogenic molecular alterations involving SMARCA4, SMARCB1, and TSC1/2 or genetic fusions involving YWHAE, BCOR (including internal tandem duplication), BCORL1, PHF1, JAZF1, KDM2, ALK, RET, ROS1, NTRK, COL1A1, TFE3, RAD51B, KAT6B/A and KANSL1 underpin sarcomas that should not be classified in this category {29700418; 28621321; 11371647; 28664932; 29553955; 31917155; 34322931; 38141829; 30877273; 39392508; 30789359; 39921027}.

YWHAE, JAZF1, and NTRK rearrangements may underpin rare pleomorphic sarcomas that should not be classified in this category {28621321; 11371647; 29553955; 30877273}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: undifferentiated mesenchymal cells; exclusion of other tumour types by extensive sampling and immunohistochemistry showing no clear line of differentiation.

Desirable: molecular analyses to exclude alterations associated with fusion-driven sarcomas, particularly in cases displaying monomorphic nuclear features.

Staging

This entity is staged as uterine sarcoma using the FIGO, Union for International Cancer Control (UICC), or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are associated with a poor prognosis. The 5-year overall survival is between 15% and 30% {37193821; 33210235}. FIGO stage is an important prognostic factor {37193821}. Mitotic count and hormone receptor expression appear to define a group of cases in which long-term survival can be achieved {28622181; 30617134}; ER and/or PR expression is associated with improved overall survival {25130488; 30617134}. The expression of genes related to extracellular matrix and an elevated mitotic count, respectively, may correlate with decreased overall and 5-year survival {25130488; 30617134; 28622181}.
