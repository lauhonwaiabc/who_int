9.2.2.4 Sex cord tumour with annular tubules

Definition

Sex cord tumour with annular tubules (SCTAT) is a sex cord tumour consisting of sharply circumscribed nests composed of bland cells arranged in ring-like tubules that encircle basement membrane–like material.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized to the ovary.

Clinical features

Tumours may occur in patients with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome or may be non-syndromic; the latter patients usually present at a later age. Syndromic cases are typically bilateral and an incidental finding in a patient undergoing oophorectomy for Peutz–Jeghers syndrome. Non-syndromic cases are usually unilateral and present with symptoms and signs related to the presence of an ovarian mass or occasionally symptoms and signs related to hyperestrinism or androgen production such as precocious puberty or virilization {7104978; 39971421}.

Epidemiology

This is a rare neoplasm (accounting for < 1% of all ovarian sex cord tumours) but is commonly seen in patients with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome {7104978; 39971421}. SCTAT is the most common ovarian tumour in patients with this syndrome, seen in approximately one third of cases {7104978; 39971421}. Rarely SCTATs occur in patients with hermaphroditism {5429475; 7104978; 36575516}. SCTAT may be diagnosed at any age, with a mean age of 22.5 years {36331303}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Syndromic cases have germline STK11 gene mutations on chromosome 19p13.3 {28900777; 10623683}. The underlying molecular events in non-syndromic cases are currently unknown. The underlying molecular events in non-syndromic cases are currently largely unknown, although one study reported TERT promoter mutations and monosomy 22 in a proportion of tumours {40946742}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range from small, microscopic, bilateral, and multifocal lesions (usually syndromic) to sizable unilateral masses (non-syndromic). The latter are typically solid and tan to yellow, although cystic areas may be seen and occasionally predominate. In syndromic cases especially, a gritty texture may be present due to calcification {5429475; 7104978}.

Histopathology

In both syndromic and non-syndromic cases, the lesions are characterized by variably sized, usually rounded nests composed of simple or complex tubules that encircle hyaline basement membrane–like material, which may also be present around the tubules. In some cases, the nests coalesce and there may be extensive sclerosis {37184091}. In Peutz–Jeghers-associated neoplasms especially, extensive calcification within the basement membrane–like material may be present {5429475}. Cells are tall, with pale cytoplasm and basally located round to ovoid nuclei that often display an antipodal distribution within the tubules {5429475}. There is usually little in the way of nuclear atypia or mitotic activity. In rare cases, a solid proliferation of indifferent sex cord cells is seen in both syndromic and non-syndromic cases. Occasional cases are associated with very small areas resembling adult-type granulosa cell tumour, Sertoli cell tumour, or unclassifiable sex cord tumour (sex cord tumour NOS); these should be diagnosed as SCTAT {36710570}. A degree of cytological atypia and mitotic activity may be seen in SCTATs {5429475; 7104978 }.

The tumour cells of SCTAT are typically positive for sex cord markers calretinin, WT1, inhibin, SF1, CD56, and also with SOX9 {12966351; 17581419; 12808064; 33566167}. Cytokeratins may be positive {12808064}, but they are almost always negative for EMA {12808064} and CD10 {17885484}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The presence of an STK11 mutation should raise the possibility of Peutz–Jeghers syndrome.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic tubular pattern with antipodal distribution of nuclei and basement membrane–like material.

Desirable: immunohistochemical positivity for sex cord markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Peutz–Jeghers syndrome–associated tumours are typically benign, whereas non-syndromic cases exhibit extraovarian spread in as many as 20% of patients {7104978; 5429475 }. The most common sites of metastasis are lymph nodes {10786816} and omentum {33566167}. Overall, reported mortality is 12.5% {36331303}.
