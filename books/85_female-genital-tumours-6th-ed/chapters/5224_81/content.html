5.2.2.4 Clear cell carcinoma

Definition

Clear cell carcinoma of the endometrium is a malignant epithelial neoplasm demonstrating papillary, tubulocystic, and/or solid architectural patterns and variably pleomorphic polygonal, cuboidal, flat, or hobnail cells with clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterus

Clinical features

Postmenopausal bleeding is the most frequent presenting symptom, but cervical cytology screening may be abnormal, especially in advanced-stage disease {27173475}. About 50–60% of the tumours are at an early stage at presentation {16495918; 28945214; 26854651}. Patients may have an increased risk of venous thromboembolic events {21464728; 19370896; 32147891}.

Epidemiology

This is a rare tumour, accounting for < 10% of all endometrial carcinomas {16495918; 26854651}. The mean patient age is in the mid to late seventh decade of life {23359866; 28945214}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There is no known histotype-specific molecular profile; tumour subsets demonstrate molecular heterogeneity and can overlap with serous carcinoma, endometrioid carcinoma, neither, or both {25308272; 28485815; 28718916; 31269413; 37353806}. Recurrent somatic mutations include mutations in TP53 (36–59%), PPP2R1A (16–37%), PIK3CA (11–50%), PIK3R1 (16–21%), KRAS (0–14%), ARID1A (15.9–41%), PTEN (0–21%), and SPOP (14–18%) {28718916; 28485815; 25308272; 31269413; 30585826; 14976538; 37081760; 36857998}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most cases produce a friable mass without features distinct from those of other endometrial carcinomas {28945214}.

Histopathology

Strict adherence to diagnostic criteria, both architectural and cytological, is required to distinguish clear cell carcinoma from potential histological mimics and to maximize diagnostic reproducibility {22790851; 25851704}. The major architectural patterns (tubulocystic, papillary, and solid) are frequently admixed {943228; 23359866; 30550483}. Papillae are typically short and rounded and often have hyalinized stroma. Tubulocystic areas display variably confluent glands and cysts. Constituent tumour cells show cuboidal, polygonal, hobnail, or flat appearances, with clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm. However, neither cytoplasmic clearing nor hobnail cells are required for the diagnosis. Tall columnar cells, abundant stratification, intraglandular tufting, and detached budding are either absent or only present focally; squamous differentiation is absent {30550483; 23359866}. Nuclear pleomorphism is variable, but at least focal moderate to severe atypia is typically present {30550483; 23359866}. There may be striking intratumoural and intertumoural variability in mitotic activity, but most tumours display < 5 mitoses/2 mm2 (equating to < 6 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area) {943228; 23359866; 30550483}. Immunohistochemically, tumours are positive for napsin A and AMACR (P504S) in 56–93% and 75–88% of cases, respectively, usually in the majority of cells {25308272; 28796747; 24145649; 17442376; 22495362; 24103020; 24119561; 31269413; 23807777}. ER and PR are usually negative or are expressed in only a minority of tumour cells {24103020; 23807777}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an admixture of tubulocystic, papillary, and/or solid patterns; clear to eosinophilic cuboidal, polygonal, hobnail, or flat cells.

Desirable: ER negative; napsin A and/or AMACR(P504S) positive.

Staging

This entity is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of tumours of the uterus – endometrium [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

The overall 5-year survival rate is 55–79% {23359866; 28945214; 31500893; 36973753}. Most relapses occur outside the pelvis {8859187; 27668398}. Most analyses have found advanced patient age and/or tumour stage to be poor prognostic factors {28945214; 8859187; 23359866; 16495918; 35075084; 36973753}. All four endometrial carcinoma molecular subtypes are represented in clear cell carcinoma, with the following reported frequencies: POLEmut (range: 0–7%, median: 2%); dMMR (range: 0–23%, median: 11%); no specific molecular profile (NSMP) (range: 38–56%, median: 46%); and p53abn (range: 34.3–59%, median: 38%) {28718916; 32331700; 37081760; 36857998; 30585826; 37353806}. The overall survival rate for patients with dMMR tumours is substantially higher than those with tumours of p53abn molecular subtype {34266691; 36857998}, and there are no known disease-specific deaths for the rare POLEmut cases. Other possibly prognostic factors that reportedly may affect progression-free survival and/or overall survival, but for which there are presently only preliminary or conflicting data, include high expression of L1CAM {28941294}, IMP3 {23465280}, and cyclin E {26050345}; loss of expression of ARID1A (BAF250a) {29451900; 23524907}; positive peritoneal cytology in otherwise early-stage disease {28541637; 36973753}; race {29129389; 24552897}; p16 overexpression {37353806}, surgical and adjuvant therapeutic modalities {35402252; 29129389; 25019570;27668398; 40184713}; tumour size {29129389; 40184713}; tumour architectural patterns {8859187}; high expression of immune response–associated genes {37353806}; and lymphovascular invasion {28945214; 2013441; 26854651; 36973753}.
