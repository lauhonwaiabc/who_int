2.2.1.6 Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV status undetermined

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV status undetermined, is a malignant squamous tumour with stromal invasion, where p16 or HPV testing are either unavailable or uninterpretable, and subclassification into HPV-associated or HPV-independent types cannot be performed.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Epidemiology

Although there is currently no difference in treatment between HPV-associated and HPV-independent tumours, it is recommended that the type of vulvar SCC (HPV-associated or HPV-independent) be documented in the pathology report. Using p16 (+/- p53) immunohistochemistry, approximately 97% of vulvar SCC can be readily stratified into HPV-associated and HPV-independent types {36828360}, which provides superior accuracy than using morphological assessment alone {26171917; 28815579; 26630231}. However, in cases where p16 (+/- p53) immunohistochemistry or HPV testing are not available or not interpretable, a morphological diagnosis of SCC HPV status undetermined is an acceptable alternative. The percentages of HPV-associated and HPV-independent squamous cell carcinomas (SCCs) in the vulva are highly variable, depending on geographical area {23886586; 28590952; 23190170; 32410226}; overall, approximately two thirds of vulvar SCCs arise via an HPV-independent pathway {23886586}, but the percentage of HPV-associated SCC may be as high as 95% in sub-Saharan African countries and lower than 20% in several European countries {36214794}.
