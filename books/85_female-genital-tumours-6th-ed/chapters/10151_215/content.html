10.1.5.1 Prepubertal fibroma

Definition

Prepubertal fibroma is a benign spindle cell proliferation, probably of fibroblastic differentiation, generally presenting with unilateral enlargement without a discrete mass and composed of infiltrative spindle cells in the vulvar region of (mostly) prepubertal girls.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: childhood asymmetrical labium majus enlargement (CALME).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This lesion arises in the vulvar region, as either a poorly defined submucosal or a subcutaneous diffuse enlargement. The labia majora is the most common site {15577679; 16006794}.

Clinical features

Patients with prepubertal fibroma most commonly present with a unilateral painless swelling or enlargement in the vulvar region of mostly prepubertal girls, appearing as an asymmetrical enlargement of one labium majus {15577679; 16006794}; however, rarely bilateral involvement has been reported {16006794}. Lesions are slow growing, often enlarging over a period of months to 3 years {15577679}. Rare examples have been reported to spontaneously regress {16006794}.

Epidemiology

Prepubertal vulvar fibroma is uncommon. Most patients are affected between the ages of 3 and 13 years {15577679; 16006794; 30029490; 40012929}. Rarely, patients may develop lesions identical to prepubertal fibroma in adulthood, including after menopause {17405983}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There are no known predisposing genetic or environmental risk factors. Normal karyotypes have been reported for 6 cases that have undergone cytogenetic analysis {16006794; 17601903}. Some authors have suggested that lesions develop as asymmetrical physiological enlargement secondary to hormonal changes during prepuberty and early puberty, analogous to asymmetric breast swelling during early puberty {16006794; 17601903; 30029490}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions are poorly defined without discernible borders and range in size from 20 to 80 mm and have a pinkish-grey to tan cut surface without discrete masses {15577679; 16006794; 17601903}. The stroma may be myxoid. Necrosis and haemorrhage are absent.

Histopathology

Prepubertal vulvar fibroma is poorly defined, showing diffuse infiltrative growth through pre-existing vulvar connective tissue of the submucosa and dermis, and may encircle adnexal structures {15577679; 16006794; 17601903}. Prepubertal vulvar fibroma shows a hypocellular and patternless growth of bland fibroblastic spindled cells set within a variably collagenous, oedematous, or occasionally myxoid stroma. Collagen fibres, varying in appearance from thick to finely wispy, are common. The cells of interest are predominantly spindled or occasionally stellate and have ovoid nuclei with fine chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. The cytoplasm is poorly defined and typically amphophilic in quality. Nuclear atypia, pleomorphism, and necrosis are absent. Mitotic activity is sparse or absent. CD34 staining is typically multifocal to strong and diffuse suggesting their fibroblastic lineage {15577679}. Immunohistochemical stains for S100, SMA, and desmin are consistently negative {15577679; 16006794}. Staining for ER and PR is commonly positive but may be weak/patchy {15577679; 16006794; 17601903}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: hypocellular, patternless, and diffusely infiltrative growth of bland spindle cells; collagenous, oedematous, or occasionally myxoid stroma.

Desirable: positivity for CD34.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These lesions are clinically benign, although at least 30% of patients may have local recurrence, particularly after incomplete excision {15577679; 16006794; 17601903}. Rare examples have been reported to spontaneously regress {16006794}.
