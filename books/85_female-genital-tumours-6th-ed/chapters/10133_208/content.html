10.1.3.3 Atypical polypoid adenomyoma

Definition

Atypical polypoid adenomyoma is a polypoid lesion composed of architecturally complex endometrioid-type glands set within a benign myomatous or fibromyomatous stroma.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: atypical polypoid adenomyofibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Atypical polypoid adenomyoma most commonly involves the lower uterine segment but may also arise in the uterine corpus.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with abnormal vaginal bleeding, or the lesion may be found during investigations for infertility.

Epidemiology

Atypical polypoid adenomyoma is rare, and patient age varies widely (mean: 39 years in one large study) {8540600}. Most patients are of reproductive age. Occasional cases have been associated with Turner syndrome {3692666}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Some cases arise in association with prolonged estrogenic stimulation {3692666}. No specific molecular abnormality has been identified; however, atypical polypoid adenomyoma exhibits molecular features similar to those of atypical hyperplasia and low-grade endometrioid carcinoma (e.g. abnormalities in PTEN, KRAS and CTNNB1) {24182564; 25828387}.

Macroscopic appearance

Atypical polypoid adenomyoma is a polypoid, firm lesion. It is typically exophytic, but an endophytic growth pattern may also occur. The average size is 20 mm, but lesions as large as 60 mm have been reported. Most atypical polypoid adenomyomas are single, but multiple polypoid lesions are occasionally present.

Histopathology

Atypical polypoid adenomyoma consists of irregular, often architecturally complex (crowded) endometrioid-type glands set within a myomatous or fibromyomatous stroma {3739968; 8540600}. Squamous morular metaplasia is almost always present, sometimes with central necrosis. The glands may exhibit minor tubal and/or mucinous metaplasia. The glandular component often exhibits mild to moderate atypia with nuclear rounding, and the glands may exhibit a lobular arrangement, focally or diffusely. The surrounding stroma is composed of bland cellular spindled myomatous or fibromyomatous elements, which may contain occasional mitoses. In curettage specimens, fragments of atypical polypoid adenomyoma are often admixed with fragments of normal endometrium {8540600}. Rarely, there are areas of significant glandular crowding associated with limited intervening stroma in keeping with a low-grade endometrioid carcinoma arising within the atypical polypoid adenomyoma.

In most cases, the stromal cells are positive for desmin, SATB2, and p16 (moderate to strong diffuse expression); however, these markers may also be positive in the stroma of adenomyomas and thus are not specific to the diagnosis {38833724}. The stromal cells of atypical polypoid adenomyoma are usually negative for h-caldesmon and CD10 {18619643; 30339971; 30085940; 33459390}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a polypoid lesion composed of architecturally complex endometrioid-type glands, often with squamous morular metaplasia, set in a benign myomatous or fibromyomatous stroma.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Hysterectomy is curative. Most patients can be treated with curettage or hysteroscopic polypectomy and close follow-up if fertility preservation is desired or if a hysterectomy is contraindicated for another reason {8540600}. Women may have successful pregnancies despite persistent disease {8540600}. Patients with atypical polypoid adenomyoma may have concurrent atypical hyperplasia in as many as 40% of cases and endometrial carcinoma in approximately 15–20% of cases {38863630; 40125358; 36836047}. There is an approximately 30% risk of recurrence for atypical polypoid adenomyoma, but the risk is higher for lesions containing more architecturally complex glands {8540600}. The risk of progression to atypical endometrial hyperplasia is approximately 20%, and an aggregated review of published series has found that the risk of progression to endometrial carcinoma is approximately 8.8% {16623790}. Almost all these cancers are low-grade and minimally myoinvasive and may involve the atypical polypoid adenomyoma and/or the surrounding myometrium.
