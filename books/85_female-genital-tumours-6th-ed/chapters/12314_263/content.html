12.3.1.4 Mixed trophoblastic tumour

Definition

Mixed trophoblastic tumour is a gestational trophoblastic tumour composed of a mixture of two or three histological types of gestational trophoblastic neoplasia, including choriocarcinoma, placental site trophoblastic tumour (PSTT), and epithelioid trophoblastic tumour (ETT).

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries.

Clinical features

Abnormal vaginal bleeding is the most common presenting symptom {31803628}. The preceding gestational event is a term pregnancy in approximately half of cases, abortion in 40%, and hydatidiform mole in about 10% of cases {31803628; 9808132; 38959396; 12823059}. The serum hCG level is usually slightly elevated with mixed ETT and PSTT and may be markedly elevated with tumours that have a choriocarcinoma component {38959396}.

The interval between presentation and the antecedent pregnancy ranges from 14 weeks to 38 years, with 50% of patients presenting within < 12 months {31803628}.

Epidemiology

These tumours are very rare; > 50 cases have been reported to date {12060455; 12823059; 29897391; 18227827; 26597227; 31206335; 31803628; 38959396}. With such a low incidence, no population-based studies will be available to describe the epidemiology, but data from case series report age distribution across included cases: patients are aged 15–60 years (median: 34 years) {38959396}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The development of mixed trophoblastic tumour has been proposed to recapitulate the differentiation of trophoblast in early placenta. Cytotrophoblast, the presumed trophoblastic stem cell, undergoes neoplastic transformation along different lineages: into PSTT when it differentiates towards implantation-site intermediate trophoblast and into ETT when it differentiates towards chorionic-type intermediate trophoblast {18059230}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour typically forms a mass lesion ranging between 10 and 120 mm in size {29897391}.

Histopathology

Histopathology shows the presence of discrete areas of choriocarcinoma, PSTT, and/or ETT, with characteristic histomorphology of each subtype as described above. The most common histological combination of mixed trophoblastic tumour is choriocarcinoma and ETT; less often the tumour consists of choriocarcinoma and PSTT, or ETT and PSTT {26261623; 29897391; 34392267; 38959396}. Rare cases with three tumour components – choriocarcinoma, ETT, and PSTT – have also been reported {9808132; 18227827}.

Each tumour component demonstrates their characteristic immunohistochemical profile as described in other sections.

Cytology

Malignant trophoblastic cells may be seen in cervical cytology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Short tandem repeat (STR) genotyping can establish the gestational origin of these tumours and rule out tumours of non-gestational (germ cell or somatic) origins {38959396}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: distinctive morphological features of each component of gestational trophoblastic neoplasia, further supported by expression of trophoblastic marker(s).

Desirable: STR genotyping.

Staging

The FIGO and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classifications are used for staging.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis and clinical course depend on the tumour histological components and stage. Mixed ETT and PSTT has the most indolent course {29897391}, while metastatic disease has been observed most commonly in cases with a choriocarcinoma component {9808132; 18227827}. In the largest series of mixed trophoblastic tumour, one third of patients experienced relapse {31803628}. Immunotherapy has been shown to be effective in patients with chemotherapy-resistant tumours {37703867}.
