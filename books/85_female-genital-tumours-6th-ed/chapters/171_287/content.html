17.1 Genetic tumour syndromes of the female genital tract: Introduction

Tumour susceptibility syndromes are usually caused by germline mutations in tumour suppressor genes and account for a minority of many cancer types. Throughout this edition, the term “germline” should be considered to be synonymous with “constitutional”.

Within patients with these syndromes, cancers occur in susceptible cells when the function of the second copy of the gene is lost, through mutation, deletion, or epigenetic events. Although some such cancers are histologically and/or clinically distinct, others are identical to sporadic cancers of same histotype. The pathologies associated with genetic tumour syndromes are discussed in detail in the Genetic tumour syndromes volume in this series. Syndromes that have well-established associations with gynaecological cancers are listed in #54792Table 17.XXX; these are sorted by the biological function of the causative mutated genes. The table includes syndromes represented by their own sections in the past edition including Carney complex, PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (Cowden syndrome), ataxia-telangiectasia syndrome, ovarian dysgenesis, and von Hippel–Lindau syndromes.
