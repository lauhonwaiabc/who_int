10.1.1.6 Leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata

Definition

Leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata is a condition characterized by the presence of multiple benign smooth muscle nodules on peritoneal surfaces of the pelvic and abdominal cavities.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: disseminated peritoneal leiomyomatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pelvic and abdominal peritoneum; uterine, adnexal, and intestinal surfaces; omentum

Clinical features

Leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata is often an incidental finding at the time of caesarean section or postpartum tubal ligation. In some cases, patients may have symptoms related to uterine leiomyomas. This should be distinguished from implantation of leiomyomas secondary to morcellation {20068330; 20842731; 23189178; 18946223}.

Epidemiology

Leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata is a rare smooth muscle tumour that occurs in patients of reproductive age. More than 70% of patients are pregnant or using oral contraceptives, and exceptionally there is an underlying estrogen-producing ovarian neoplasm {8604458; 11426907; 1326849; 24047039}.

Etiology

Pregnancy and oral contraceptive use have been clinically associated with leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata. In addition, lesional smooth muscle cells express estrogen and progesterone receptors. These observations suggest that this pathological disorder may be initiated or promoted by estrogen and progesterone {12090595; 8332577; 7184891}.

Pathogenesis

Nodules of leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata share a common pattern of loss of heterozygosity or identical MED12 mutations, indicative of metastasis from a uniclonal neoplastic origin {9176406; 18946223; 23635816}. Cytogenetic or abnormalities are relatively simple and bear resemblances to those found in uterine leiomyomas {20842731; 25925998; 32359372}. Presence of MED12 mutations in both uterine leiomyoma and peritoneal leiomyomatosis also supports a common origin and related pathogenesis {23635816}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesional nodules are multiple if not miliary, firm or rubbery, white or tan, and usually < 10 mm. They stud the peritoneal, omental, or visceral surfaces without invasion.

Histopathology

The nodules are well circumscribed and resemble typical or cellular leiomyomas. Their smooth muscle cells are arranged in fascicles with absent to mild nuclear atypia and low mitotic activity {7184891; 7360455}. Immunohistochemical staining confirms smooth muscle differentiation. Rarely, sex cord–like or adipocytic differentiation is encountered {1398545; 1326849}. In pregnant patients, decidual cells also may be present as a coincidental finding. Coexisting endometriosis or endosalpingiosis may be found in 10% of cases {12090595; 15875729}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not generally required for pathological diagnosis. MED12 mutations, however, may be identified by sequencing if performed. Complex cytogenetic or genomic alterations should prompt consideration of other malignancies.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multiple or miliary nodules of benign smooth muscle superficially involving peritoneal surfaces.

Desirable: smooth muscle marker positivity.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are generally clinically benign, although some cases may become symptomatic due to mass effect, or recur in a subsequent pregnancy if incompletely excised {7184891}. Successful treatment with GnRH agonists or aromatase inhibitors has been achieved {34307570; 11576235; 18669776}.
