15.2.0.4 Atypical melanocytic naevus of genital type

Definition

Atypical melanocytic naevus of genital type (AMNGT) is a melanocytic neoplasm occurring in the genital area, with histological atypical features and a benign clinical course.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: atypical genital naevus; atypical melanocytic naevus of special site.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

AMNGT is most commonly located on the labia majora or mons pubis. AMNGT affecting the labia minora is less frequent.

Clinical features

AMNGT presents as a slow-growing, irregularly pigmented (tan, brown) papule, often > 6 mm. It is usually an incidental finding. Patients with AMNGT often have a personal or family history of dysplastic naevus {18162770; 21366454} [[Prieto VG, Pfeifer JD, Wick MR. Genital Naevus. Epidemiology

AMNGT is mainly found in young women in the third decade of life.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

AMNGTs of genital type frequently harbour BRAF NM_004333.4:c.1799T>A p.(Val600Glu) (BRAF V600E) mutations {24842760}; they do not display the anomalies found in mucosal melanomas {27220476}.

Macroscopic appearance

There is a papular lesion, usually pigmented, < 10 mm.

Histopathology

AMNGT is typically a compound, rather symmetrical melanocytic proliferation with architectural and at least focal cytological atypia of the junctional and (to a lesser degree) dermal components. The junctional nests are usually large, irregularly shaped, and confluent and often show a loss of cohesion among the melanocytes. The melanocytes tend to be larger than in a common naevus and may show mild cytological atypia such as visible nucleoli and abundant heavily pigmented or foamy cytoplasm. Pagetoid growth is usually absent; when present, it is confined to the centre of the lesion. The periphery of the lesion sometimes displays a single-cell lentiginous growth pattern. The dermal/subepithelial component usually consists of dispersed nests, horizontal rows, or sheets of smaller melanocytes. There may be prominent stromal fibroplasia. Cytological atypia is less prominent in the subepithelial/dermal component than in the junctional component. Mitotic activity is low but can be found superficially.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: at least focally prominent architectural atypia in the junctional component; mostly symmetrical lesion with maturation with depth.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

AMNGT behaves in a benign fashion without recurrence when completely removed.
