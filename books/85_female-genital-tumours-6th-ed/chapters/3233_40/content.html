3.2.3.3 Adenocarcinoma, intestinal type

Definition

Adenocarcinoma of intestinal type of the distal female genital tract is a primary vulvar or vaginal adenocarcinoma exhibiting intestinal differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: cloacogenic adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Vaginal primaries usually occur in the lower third, especially on the posterior wall, close to the vulvar junction. Vulvar primaries especially occur in the vestibule close to the vagina.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a polypoid mass or abnormal bleeding.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare and have been reported across a wide age range from 31 to 86 years {24722061; 39522642; 39868745}. No other epidemiological data are available.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Primary vulvar and vaginal adenocarcinomas of intestinal type are very rare tumours displaying significant morphological, immunohistochemical, and molecular overlap with colorectal adenocarcinoma. The few cases that have been tested for HPV have been negative, in keeping with an HPV-independent tumour. Some cases arise in association with an intestinal-type tubulovillous/villous adenoma and/or benign-appearing intestinal-type epithelium, suggesting an adenoma–carcinoma sequence {24722061}. The most frequent molecular alterations are TP53 and KRAS variants in 71.4% and 42.9%, respectively {39522642; 39868745}. At a molecular level, vaginal intestinal type adenocarcinomas are closely related to their vulvar counterpart and represent identical lesions {39522642}. On RNA sequencing analysis, vaginal and vulvar intestinal-type adenocarcinomas cluster together close to colorectal adenocarcinomas and separate from HPV-associated intestinal-type and HPV-independent gastric-type cervical adenocarcinomas {39522642}. In two vulvar intestinal-type adenocarcinomas, amplification of CD274/PDL1 was reported, suggesting potential therapeutics with immune-checkpoint inhibitors {39868745}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, these neoplasms form a polypoid and/or ulcerated mass {24722061; 39522642}.

Histopathology

These tumours are adenocarcinomas with varying amounts of tubular/glandular, tubulovillous, and solid architecture. The tumours contain columnar cells with intracytoplasmic mucin, typically with goblet cells {24722061; 39522642}. Signet-ring cells, mucin extravasation, and dirty necrosis may be present {39522642}. Occasional tumours have prominent neuroendocrine cells {24722061}. As discussed, in some cases there is an intestinal-type tubulovillous/villous adenoma and/or benign-appearing intestinal-type epithelium. Before making the diagnosis of a primary vulvar or vaginal adenocarcinoma of intestinal type, a tumour involving the vulva or vagina from the gastrointestinal tract (direct extension or metastasis) should be excluded. These tumours typically express CDX2, CK20, SATB2, and CEA and are negative for ER, PR, PAX8, and SOX17, with variable CK7 staining. p53 immunohistochemistry may be wild type or mutation type {24722061; 39522642}.

Cytology

Similar to colorectal adenocarcinoma, vaginal intestinal-type adenocarcinoma is characterized by crowded glandular tissue fragments of moderate to large epithelial cells with enlarged oval to pleomorphic vesicular nuclei, prominent nucleoli with frequent perinucleolar chromatin clearing (in Pap-stained slides), and eccentric delicate cytoplasm producing in most cases a columnar orientation. The presence of multiple or single large intracytoplasmic vacuoles containing mucin and that of goblet cells supports intestinal differentiation but is not always seen.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: adenocarcinoma with an intestinal-type morphology and immunophenotype; exclusion of direct spread/metastasis from a primary colorectal adenocarcinoma.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most tumours are stage I at diagnosis. There are few reported cases with long-term follow-up. Occasional recurrences after many years and deaths are reported, and long-term follow-up is needed {24722061; 36291953; 39522642}.
