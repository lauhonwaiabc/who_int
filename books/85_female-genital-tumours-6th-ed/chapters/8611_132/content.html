8.6.1.1 Seromucinous cystadenoma and seromucinous adenofibroma

Definition

Seromucinous cystadenoma is a benign ovarian neoplasm composed of glands and cysts lined by an admixture of bland Müllerian-type epithelia, typically a combination of Müllerian mucinous epithelium with ciliated and/or endometrioid epithelium. Seromucinous adenofibroma differs from cystadenoma in that it has a prominent fibromatous stromal component.

Related terminology

Not recommended: Müllerian-type mucinous cystadenoma/adenofibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary, and bilateral involvement is common (as many as 40% of cases) {33577227}. A left-sided predominance, similar to endometriosis, has been documented {32810322}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with symptoms related to a pelvic mass, or the mass may be an incidental finding on imaging or at surgery performed for other reasons.

Epidemiology

Seromucinous cystadenomas and adenofibromas are considered relatively rare and present over a wide age range (26 to 85 years; mean 55.7 years) {32810322; 33577227}. They are associated with endometriosis in 27–36% of cases, in case series {32810322; 33577227}.

Etiology

At least a subset of seromucinous cystadenomas and adenofibromas are thought to arise from endometriosis, with some benign seromucinous tumours arising in continuity with endometriotic cysts {32810322; 33577227}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Cystadenomas are most commonly multilocular and predominantly cystic {32810322; 33577227}. The cyst walls are generally smooth and thin within no to minimal gross nodularity. Adenofibromas are predominantly solid, with a dense fibrous cut surface; they may arise as a nodule in the wall of an endometriotic cyst. Tumours range in size from 7 to 300 mm (mean: 90 mm) {32810322; 33577227}.

Histopathology

Seromucinous cystadenomas are composed of cysts lined by a mixture of benign Müllerian-type epithelia, including mucinous-type, serous (ciliated), and endometrioid, in varying proportions. The endometrioid component may have hobnail morphology {32810322}. Typically, the mucinous-type epithelium is endocervical in appearance (columnar cells with abundant, pale-pink cytoplasm and small basally located nuclei). A subset of seromucinous cystadenomas may demonstrate foci of gastrointestinal-type epithelium, namely intestinal-type goblet cells or foveolar-type epithelium, in a minority of glands {37125505; 33275953}. The epithelium is predominantly single layered with occasional mild epithelial stratification {32810322}. Papillary architecture comprising broad papillae, with no or minimal hierarchical branching, may be seen {33577227}. If epithelial stratification or hierarchical branching exceeds 10% of the epithelial lining, a borderline designation is appropriate.

Seromucinous adenofibromas are characterized by a prominent fibromatous hypocellular stroma containing widely spaced glands lined by an admixture of cytologically bland Müllerian-type epithelia as described above. An admixture of cystic and adenofibromatous components may occur (cystadenofibroma).

On the basis of the common association with endometriosis, noting the shift in classification of the malignant seromucinous counterpart, it has been suggested that some benign seromucinous tumours may represent endometrioid neoplasms with foci of serous and/or mucinous metaplasia {32810322}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: glands within a fibromatous stroma or cysts lined by an admixture of Müllerian-type epithelia with bland cytological features.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are benign.
