9.2.1.7 Leydig cell tumour

Definition

Leydig cell tumour is a benign steroid cell tumour confined to or predominantly located in the ovarian hilum.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: hilus cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Leydig cell tumours are centred in and predominantly confined to the ovarian hilum, sometimes with extension into the ovarian parenchyma {2553628}.

Clinical features

The usual presentation is with endocrine manifestations, most commonly androgenic and rarely estrogenic {2553628}. In cases where a hormone-producing ovarian neoplasm is suspected clinically but imaging has not been contributory, the location of a Leydig cell tumour may be pinpointed preoperatively by detecting high levels of androgens after selective ovarian vein sampling {33665140; 38045790}.

Epidemiology

The average patient age in one study was 58 years (range: 32–82 years) {2553628}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are usually small (average size: 20 mm) and are rarely bilateral {34137345; 33370966}. The lesions are usually well-circumscribed at the ovarian hilum, and the cut surface is typically solid, lobulated, and soft with a brown, red, or pink appearance {2553628}.

Histopathology

These tumours typically comprise a well-circumscribed nodular growth of large polyhedral or rounded epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic, but occasionally pale, cytoplasm. Cytoplasmic lipochrome pigment is commonly seen. Clustering of nuclei, creating intervening eosinophilic nuclear-free zones, is a characteristic feature. Cytoplasmic Reinke crystals (rod-shaped elongated eosinophilic crystals) are sometimes seen but may be rare or absent. Nuclei are typically round, with a single prominent nucleolus; nuclear pseudoinclusions may be present, and bizarre nuclear atypia is sometimes seen. Mitoses are rare or absent. In approximately one third of cases, fibrinoid material is present within blood vessel walls. Occasional tumours have a conspicuous fibrous stroma. Hyperplasia of non-neoplastic hilus/Leydig cells is commonly present in the uninvolved ovarian hilum {2553628}. The diagnosis of Leydig cell tumour is appropriate for tumours that lack identifiable Reinke crystals but show otherwise typical features and are located exclusively or predominantly in the ovarian hilum.

Immunohistochemically, Leydig cell tumours are usually positive for sex cord stromal markers, such as inhibin {9688187}, calretinin {11603218}, SF1, and melan-A {20567142}. Cytoplasmic SATB2 immunoreactivity may also occur in Leydig cell tumours {37668387}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed tumour centred in the ovarian hilum, consisting of bland steroid cells.

Desirable: cytoplasmic Reinke crystals.

Staging

These are benign neoplasms and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

If strict diagnostic criteria are used, Leydig cell tumours are almost always benign.
