8.8.0.2 Dedifferentiated and undifferentiated carcinomas

Definition

Undifferentiated carcinoma is a malignant epithelial tumour lacking overt evidence of a specific line of differentiation. Dedifferentiated carcinoma is composed of an undifferentiated carcinoma and a differentiated component.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with high-stage tumours, with 70% showing extraovarian disease {37925057}.

Epidemiology

These are uncommon tumours, representing approximately 0.5% of ovarian carcinomas in case series {20407318}. Median age of diagnosis is 52 years (range: 21–82 years) {16306785; 20305618; 37925057}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Association with low-grade endometrioid carcinoma suggests progression from endometrioid carcinoma in a subset of tumours. Inactivation of multiple core components of the SWI/SNF chromatin remodelling complex, such as the loss of SMARCA4 expression and the loss of both ARID1A and ARID1B expression, appears to be important in the transition to an undifferentiated state (i.e. dedifferentiation). Inactivating mutations in SWI/SNF chromatin remodelling genes (ARID1A/ARID1B coinactivation or SMARCA4/SMARCB1 inactivation) are common {27020216; 27562491; 36843546; 37925057}. Mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR) / microsatellite instability (MSI) is found in about one third of cases {20305618; 37925057}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are typically large solid masses ranging from 55 to 450 mm (average: 180 mm) with extensive necrosis {37925057}.

Histopathology

Monomorphic undifferentiated carcinoma

The morphological characteristics are similar to those observed in undifferentiated/dedifferentiated endometrial carcinomas (see section Dedifferentiated and undifferentiated carcinomas). Undifferentiated carcinomas usually display sheet-like growth, which is frequently associated with geographical necrosis. The cells are usually monotonous, discohesive, and round with minimal cytoplasm. Rhabdoid features can be seen in a subset of cases, and focal spindling of tumour cells may rarely be present. Myxoid stroma is sometimes present. Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are often prominent. The mitotic activity is typically high {16306785; 20305618; 37925057}. Undifferentiated carcinoma can show significant histological overlap with small cell carcinoma of the ovary of hypercalcaemic type (see section Small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcaemic type).

Dedifferentiated carcinoma

In dedifferentiated carcinomas, the differentiated component usually comprises a FIGO grade 1 or 2 endometrioid carcinoma or rarely other types of ovarian carcinoma {37925057}. The differentiated and the undifferentiated components can vary in proportion, and the interface between the two components can be abrupt, imparting a biphasic appearance.

Undifferentiated areas show only focal EMA, pankeratin, and CK18 staining, typically as scattered single or small clusters of cells. PAX8 is either focally positive or completely negative {20305618; 26598976}. Tumour cells are usually negative for ER, PR, and E-cadherin. Loss of SMARCA4 (BRG1), co-loss of ARID1A and ARID1B, and (in some cases) loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) are common in the undifferentiated tumours as in their endometrial counterparts {27562491; 26598976; 33432679; 37925057}. It is important to note that loss of ARID1A is common in ovarian endometrioid carcinoma and ovarian clear cell carcinoma, and loss of ARID1A expression in isolation is not associated with dedifferentiation. dMMR is also frequently seen in the undifferentiated tumour (and the differentiated tumour if present) {20305618; 37925057}, and it can be useful in distinguishing between undifferentiated carcinoma and small cell carcinoma of the ovary of hypercalcaemic type (MMR proficient). p53 usually exhibits wildtype immunoreactivity unless the differentiated component is a high-grade serous carcinoma. Caution is advised when using CD138 as part of a panel to investigate plasmacytoid differentiation, because this marker may be expressed in undifferentiated carcinoma {20305618}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of SWI/SNF pathway alterations, including concurrent inactivating mutations of ARID1A and ARID1B or SMARCA4 and SMARCB1, may be helpful in diagnostically challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: undifferentiated carcinoma: a malignant epithelial tumour of monotonous and discohesive cells, with exclusion of mimics; dedifferentiated carcinoma: a biphasic neoplasm with a differentiated, typically low-grade endometrioid component, and an undifferentiated component.

Desirable: loss of epithelial markers, PAX8, ER, and PR.

Staging

These tumours are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system {34669199}.

Prognosis and prediction

These are highly aggressive neoplasms. Patients typically present with advanced-stage disease, usually with bulky pelvic and para-aortic lymph node metastases as well as distant metastases {20305618; 37925057}. The overall median survival time was 9 months in one series {20305618} and 3 months for tumours showing extraovarian spread in another series {37925057}.
