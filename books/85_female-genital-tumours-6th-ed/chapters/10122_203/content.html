10.1.2.2 Low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma

Definition

Low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma is a malignant stromal tumour with cells resembling proliferative-phase endometrial stroma and displaying infiltrative (permeative) growth and/or lymphovascular invasion.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: (for extrauterine tumours) low-grade endometrioid stromal sarcoma; extrauterine low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma.

Not recommended: endometrial stromal tumour with limited infiltration.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours arise more frequently in the uterine corpus than in the cervix {2327549}. Extrauterine primary tumours are uncommon and may arise in association with endometriosis; sites of origin include the adnexa, abdomen/peritoneum, bowel wall, pelvis, vagina, and retroperitoneum {8253545; 24121169; 35569210}.

Clinical features

Patients present with abnormal uterine bleeding, pelvic pain, a uterine mass, and/or lung metastases or adnexal and nodal metastasis {21276609}.

Epidemiology

Rare endometrial stromal sarcoma is the second most common type of uterine sarcoma. Uterine and extrauterine tumours occur over a wide age range (uterine median patient age: 52 years, range: 17–96 years; extrauterine median patient age: 51 years, range: 20–87 years) {18813312; 35569210}. Risk factors include prolonged estrogen or tamoxifen exposure and pelvic radiation {12039943; 3756818; 21531011}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Primary uterine tumours frequently harbour genetic fusions involving polycomb family genes, with JAZF1::SUZ12 being most common and occurring in 55–66% of tumours {37867306; 21836477}. Rare fusions include JAZF1::PHF1, EPC1::PHF1, MEAF6::PHF1, MBTD1::EZHIP (CXorf67), BRD8::PHF1, BRD8::C5orf24, CREZBF::PHF1, EPC2::PHF1, HCFC1::PHF1, ING3::PHF1, MBTD1::PHF1, PHF1::NUTM2B, PHF1::TFE3, EPC1::EED, EPC1::EZH2, EPC1::SUZ12, EP400::SUZ12, MEAF6::SUZ12, MEAF6::PTGR2, JAZF1::AQP1, JAZF1::GLI3, and SVIL::EPC1 {39921027; 37867306; 31101969; 32237188; 23959973; 34651371; 36445224; 29721194}. YWHAE::NUTM2 fusion has been detected in rare tumours that are histologically purely low-grade {37032555}. Primary extrauterine tumours arise from endometriosis {6198065; 24121169; 24525500; 28968994}, and JAZF1::SUZ12, EPC1::PHF1, and PHF1 rearrangements have been documented in extrauterine tumours {21836477}. JAZF1::SUZ12 inhibits apoptosis and induces cell proliferation that is accelerated upon transcriptionally silencing the wildtype/unrearranged SUZ12 allele {18077430}. ESR1 or TSC2 somatic mutations may occur in primary or recurrent low-grade endometrial stromal sarcomas {34961764; 34561551}. TSC2-mutant tumours share epigenetic similarity with conventional low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma and exhibit upregulation of PI3K/AKT, calcium, and RAP1 signalling pathways {34561551}.

Macroscopic appearance

In all primary sites, tumours may consist of a single nodule or multiple masses with soft, solid or solid/cystic, and yellow to tan cut surfaces, sometimes associated with haemorrhage and/or necrosis. Uterine tumours often form intracavitary or intramural, poorly defined, coalescent nodules associated with overt myometrial infiltration and/or intravascular tumour plugs, the latter sometimes prominent when involving parametrial veins {18813312; 2327549}. Ovarian tumours are typically unilateral with a mean size of 100 mm, solid or solid/cystic, and sometimes multinodular; rare tumours are predominantly cystic with wall thickening {28968994; 24121169; 24525500}. Peritoneal tumours may involve one or multiple locations and range widely in size (as much as 250 mm), typically infiltrating underlying organs/tissues {8253545; 24121169}. Cystic degeneration may be seen in association with endometriosis {8253545}.

Histopathology

In all primary sites, tumours are characterized by irregular, densely cellular islands of spindle cells associated with a delicate arteriolar network. Uterine tumours frequently exhibit tongue-like myometrial permeation and/or lymphovascular invasion, while extrauterine tumours may be multinodular and associated with a fibromatous background and endometriosis. Occasionally, predominantly circumscribed tumours exhibit > 3 finger-like projections measuring > 3 mm; these tumours with limited infiltration are considered low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma. The spindle cells are typically small and have uniform, oval to fusiform nuclei with no or minimal atypia, inconspicuous nucleoli, and scant cytoplasm. The mitotic activity is usually low (< 2.1 mitoses/mm2, equating to < 5 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 area) but can occasionally be brisk (> 4.21 mitoses/mm2, equating to > 10 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area) {2327549}. Vasculature may be hyalinized and/or curvilinear and frequently associated with perivascular whorls of tumour cells. Variant features include smooth muscle, fibromyxoid/fibrous, sex cord–like, epithelioid, skeletal muscle, and fatty differentiation, as well as clear cell change, presence of bizarre nuclei, and hyaline plaques {21836477; 37867306; 40434060}. Although most variant histological features are not associated with specific genetic fusions, nodules of epithelioid and sex cord–like morphology admixed with conventional low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma are associated with somatic TSC2 mutations {34561551}.

The typical immunoprofile includes strong, diffuse expression of CD10, IFITM1, WT1, ER, and PR with no or limited cyclin D1 positivity (staining in < 50% of tumour nuclei) {11914617; 10737385; 22982899; 39836188; 15213600}. Wide-spectrum keratins may be positive {1706303; 29406332}. Desmin, SMA, h-caldesmon, calponin, HDAC8, and transgelin often highlight areas of smooth muscle differentiation {16538051; 39836188}. Foci of sex cord–like differentiation may express inhibin and calretinin {10374776; 16118629; 11914617}. HMB45, melan-A, and cathepsin K expression may be observed in epithelioid areas resembling perivascular epithelioid cell tumour in TSC2-mutant low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma {34561511}. ER expression may be decreased in intensity and/or extent in ESR1-mutant tumours {34961764}.

Cytology

Smears are hypercellular, composed of cohesive tissue fragments of rather monomorphic small, oval cells with bland chromatin tightly woven in a metachromatic, extracellular matrix {17536556}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular confirmation of low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma–associated gene fusions may be helpful in tumours demonstrating extensive unusual histology mimicking other tumour types.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: all primary sites – diffuse, infiltrative growth of small uniform cells with minimal cytological atypia, resembling proliferative-type endometrial stroma; uterine tumours – permeative myometrial infiltration and/or lymphovascular invasion; extrauterine tumours – exclusion of a uterine tumour.

Desirable: extrauterine tumours – presence of associated endometriosis; all sites – CD10, ER, and PR expression; characteristic gene fusions in diagnostically challenging cases.

Staging

Uterine tumours are staged as uterine sarcomas using the FIGO and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging systems. Ovarian tumours are staged according to the UICC TNM and FIGO staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Regardless of primary site, tumours are indolent with a protracted clinical course even in patients with recurrent disease. Stage is an important predictor of overall improved survival, with 5-year progression-free and disease-specific survival rates of 89.5% and 92.0%, respectively, for stage I primary uterine tumours {33375988; 18813312; 35641004}. Lymphadenectomy without obvious adenopathy has no overall survival or disease-specific survival benefit {33375988; 18978112}. Endometrial stromal tumours with limited infiltration should be diagnosed as low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma because of the risk of recurrence {30807369}. High-grade histological transformation and/or altered ER expression may be associated with ESR1 hotspot mutation and endocrine resistance; molecular confirmation of ESR1 hotspot mutation is recommended {34961764}. Hormone suppression and mTOR inhibition have clinical benefit in TSC2-mutant tumours; molecular confirmation of TSC2 mutation and gene fusion is recommended in mesenchymal tumours with epithelioid histology and nuclear uniformity {34561551}.
