9.4.0.3 Solid pseudopapillary neoplasm

Definition

Solid pseudopapillary neoplasm is a low-grade malignant tumour identical to its pancreatic counterpart morphologically and immunohistochemically.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: solid and pseudopapillary neoplasm; solid pseudopapillary tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Symptoms are typically related to the presence of an adnexal mass, although some cases are incidentally detected during radiological examination {21778097; 20871224; 21979589; 28463908}. Hormonal manifestations are absent.

Epidemiology

This is a rare tumour, with limited epidemiological data available. Less than 20 cases have been reported {20871224; 21778097; 21979589; 28463908; 22534259; 25186079; 26339451; 27510674; 28889196; 33298496; 32566338; 37664705; 38792331; 40115014}. Although the age at presentation varies, relatively young women are typically affected, with a mean age of 35 years (range: 17–57 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

CTNNB1 mutations in exon 3 are reported in tumours that have been sequenced {28463908; 25186079; 38792331}. These neoplasms exhibit significant morphological, immunophenotypic, and molecular overlap with microcystic stromal tumour; it has been speculated that these represent related entities {35020947}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours range in size, but most are > 50 mm (to as much as 250 mm), with an intact external surface. On sectioning, they are characteristically solid and cystic; the cystic areas are friable and yellow to tan. They may show areas of haemorrhage {20871224; 21979589; 28463908}.

Histopathology

These tumours show three main histological patterns that are often present in combination: solid (sometimes punctated by cysts), nests (separated by stroma, which may be conspicuously hyalinized), and pseudopapillae (due to loss of cohesion; sometimes with myxoid to hyalinized cores). Cells are polygonal, with cytoplasm that varies from eosinophilic to pale and foamy with frequent paranuclear vacuoles. Intracellular and extracellular hyaline globules may be seen. Nuclei are round (sometimes with grooves), uniform in size, and cytologically bland. There is typically minimal or no mitotic activity. There may be an antipodal distribution of the nuclei at the base of the papillae. The stroma may contain thin arborizing blood vessels. Tumour cells show aberrant nuclear and cytoplasmic positivity for β-catenin. They are usually positive for vimentin, CD10, CD56, CD99 (sometimes dot-like cytoplasmic), and α1-antitrypsin. They may stain for CAM5.2 (sometimes dot-like perinuclear), WT1, cyclin D1, PR, synaptophysin, and KIT (CD117). Chromogranin, calretinin, ER, and inhibin are typically negative. There is loss of membranous E-cadherin expression {20871224; 21979589; 28463908}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of a CTNNB1 alteration may assist in diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: admixture of solid and pseudopapillary patterns; positive nuclear and cytoplasmic staining for β-catenin.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

The majority of the tumours are confined to the ovary and show benign behaviour {20871224; 21979589; 28463908}. However, extraovarian spread, including omental involvement and lymph node metastases, have been reported {21778097; 27510674; 38792331;41744282}.
