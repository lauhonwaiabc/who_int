8.8.0.3 Carcinosarcoma

Definition

Carcinosarcoma is a biphasic malignant neoplasm composed of high-grade carcinomatous and sarcomatous elements.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant mixed Müllerian tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This tumour occurs in the ovary.

Clinical features

These tumours are often diagnosed at advanced stage {38854725; 22588065}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of carcinosarcomas was 0.6 cases per million in the United States from 2000 to 2016 {36769835}, and 0.064 cases per 100 000 women in the Republic of Korea from 1999 to 2018 {38072401}. These tumours account for approximately 2% of all ovarian malignancies in clinicopathological studies. These tumours typically occur in postmenopausal women aged > 60 years {38854725; 22588065}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The tumours are of epithelial cell origin, and molecular studies support a monoclonal origin by revealing concordant TP53 abnormalities within the carcinoma and sarcoma components in the majority of tumours {9496259; 10646862; 38097740}. Further indirect evidence supporting the epithelial origin is that metastases most often consist of carcinoma {38733380}. Origin in the fallopian tube is likely for the majority of carcinosarcomas in which the epithelial component consists of high-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC) {38733380; 30913944}.

Ovarian carcinosarcomas commonly harbour mutations in TP53 {38733380; 38097740, 27791010; 39220645}. Other genes mutated in these tumours include PIK3CA, PPP2R1A, FBXW7, KRAS, PTEN, RB1, BRCA1, and BRCA2 {38733380; 38097740; 27791010; 39220645}. Amplification of PIK3CA, CCNE1, TERT, and MYC were additionally found {38097740; 27791010}, as well as copy-number gain in regions encoding SRC and GNAS in the sarcomatous component {38097740}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is characteristically large (mean size: 140 mm) and predominantly solid, with frequent cystic degeneration and extensive haemorrhage and necrosis {22588065}.

Histopathology

These tumours are composed of a mix of high-grade carcinoma and sarcoma. One or the other may predominate. The two components are distinct but typically intermingled with one another. The carcinomatous component is most often HGSC, but other histological types may be seen, particularly endometrioid carcinoma {22588065; 35715633; 39220645}. The sarcomatous elements are classified as homologous when the stromal component has a nonspecific appearance or as heterologous when rhabdomyosarcoma (most commonly), chondrosarcoma, or osteosarcoma (or rarely liposarcoma or angiosarcoma) is present.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a biphasic pattern with high-grade carcinomatous and sarcomatous elements.

Staging

Carcinosarcoma is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Ovarian carcinosarcomas are highly aggressive tumours with a median survival time of < 24 months and a 5-year survival rate of 15–30%, both of which compare unfavourably with those of all other tubo-ovarian carcinoma histotypes {15139057; 21420726; 38854725; 35715633}. The majority of patients are diagnosed with advanced-stage disease (FIGO stage III-IV), and optimal tumour debulking is the most important prognostic factor {15139057}, as response to chemotherapy is less favourable than that in HGSC {17395252; 21420726; 35715633}. The presence of extraovarian sarcomatous elements has been reported to be an adverse prognostic factor {22588065}, whereas tumours carrying TP53 mutations were reported to be associated with longer survival compared with those with wildtype TP53 {39220645}. Tumours with an epithelial component of HGSC may harbour BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutations, with potential therapeutic impact {39220645}.
