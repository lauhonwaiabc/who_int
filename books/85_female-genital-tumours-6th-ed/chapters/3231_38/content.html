3.2.3.1 Mixed tumour of the vagina

Definition

Mixed tumour of the vagina is a benign neoplasm composed of an admixture of spindled and epithelial (glandular and/or squamous) elements.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: spindle cell epithelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours are located at the posterior and distal vaginal wall close to the hymen.

Clinical features

Patients are asymptomatic or report nonspecific symptoms related to a mass.

Epidemiology

These are rare vaginal neoplasms. Patients present over a wide age range (mean: 40 years) {15154010; 25998318; 7682380}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Mixed tumours typically range in size from 15 to 50 mm and are solid, soft, rubbery, and mostly well circumscribed.

Histopathology

Mixed tumours are typically well circumscribed and variably cellular and composed of spindled and epithelial elements in varying proportions and arrangements. A cellular spindle cell component, with fascicular, corded, nested, and/or reticular growths, typically predominates and is sharply demarcated from the epithelial elements, which may be glandular (typically mucinous) or comprise mature squamous epithelium; sometimes there is an admixture of glandular and squamous epithelium {15154010; 7682380}. Eosinophilic hyaline material, sometimes in the form of globules, may be present within a myxomatous or collagenous stroma. The cells show minimal nuclear atypia, and mitotic activity varies, although it is usually low. There is often coexpression of a variety of epithelial and mesenchymal markers, including AE1/AE3, CK7, EMA, CD10, SMA, desmin, h-caldesmon, WT1, calretinin, BCL2, and CD34 {15154010; 25998318; 7682380}. S100 and SOX10 are typically negative. The spindle cell elements typically strongly express epithelial markers.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed tumour composed of morphologically benign epithelial (glandular and/or squamous) and spindle cell components.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign lesions but may recur if incompletely excised.
