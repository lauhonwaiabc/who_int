14.1.0.2 Follicular lymphoma

Definition

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is a neoplasm of germinal-centre B cells with varying proportions of centrocytes and centroblasts and at least a partial follicular growth pattern.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: FL grades 1, 2, 3A, 3B.

Subtype(s)

Classic FL (cFL); Follicular large B-cell lymphoma (FLBCL)

Localization

Classic FL frequently involves the ovaries and adnexa and (more rarely) the uterine cervix and corpus, and the vagina {28284518; 11444509; 21997689; 16224219; 36461146}.

Clinical features

Both primary and secondary involvement of FL often present as a localized mass. Nonspecific gynaecological symptoms may mimic ovarian cancer {39606044; 38637800}.

Epidemiology

FL accounts for 10–20% of all female genital tract (FGT) lymphomas and affects adults {38637800}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most primary cases of the ovary and adnexa lack a BCL2 rearrangement, whereas cases secondarily involving these organs display features of systemic FL including BCL2 translocation {21997689}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Like their nodal counterparts (detailed in fifth-edition Haematolymphoid tumours volume of this series), FL of the FGT consists of an at least partly follicular proliferation of centrocytes and centroblasts. Most cases can be subtyped as classic FL (grades 1/2 or rarely 3A) {37493666}. Primary ovarian FL frequently displays a mixed, diffuse and follicular, growth pattern and higher-grade cytology, but there are exceptions {39606044}. The immunophenotype includes expression of B-cell markers like CD20 and CD79A; and positivity for BCL6, whereas CD10 or BCL2 expression is less frequent.

The differential diagnosis includes florid reactive lymphoid hyperplasia of the FGT (detailed in fifth-edition Haematolymphoid tumours volume of this series), which may show a component of large lymphoid cells and a clonal B-cell rearrangement. An excisional biopsy is recommended in difficult cases.

Follicle centre lymphoma of the lower FGT usually displays a predominant follicular pattern, and a frequent prominent component of large centrocytes {36461146}. This diagnosis is supported by the lack of surface involvement or ulceration, a deep atypical lymphoid infiltrate with perivascular lymphoid cells and sclerosis {36461146}. Follicle centre lymphoma of the lower FGT variably expresses germinal-centre markers, is usually devoid of BCL2 expression and t(14;18), frequently contains TNFRSF14 mutations, and usually lacks CREBBP or KMT2D mutations {36461146}.

Cytology

Lymphomas of the FGT are uncommon, and the published reports rarely detail the cytopathological findings {17583814;35922773}. Please refer to the third edition of the Bethesda System for Cervical Cytopathology [[Nayar R, Wilbur DC, editors. The Bethesda System for Reporting Cervical Cytology. 3rd ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2015.]] and the WHO Reporting System for Lymph Node, Spleen and Thymus Cytopathology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A clonal B-cell rearrangement is present. The t(14;18)(q32;q21) is typically seen in secondary involvement, whereas it is often absent in primary ovarian FL cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: B-cell lymphoma composed of centrocytes and centroblasts with a dominance of centrocytes in the majority of cases and with a germinal-centre B-cell immunophenotype.

Desirable: an at least partly follicular growth pattern.

Staging

Lymphoid neoplasms are staged according to the Lugano classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

Overall, primary FL of the FGT has an excellent 5-year survival rate {28284518}. Similarly, the 5-year survival rate is close to 100% for FCL of the lower FGT, a localized disease with a low risk for systemic involvement {36461146}.
