4.2.0.4 Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-independent

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), HPV-independent, is an invasive and/or exophytic malignant epithelial tumour exhibiting squamous differentiation and arising through mechanisms not associated with HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterine cervix, usually at the transformation zone, with rare cases developing in the upper endocervical canal {34387215}.

Clinical features

Vaginal and postcoital bleeding, discharge, and abdominal pain are reported symptoms {25229645; 30911077; 40010435; 37702216}. Patients presenting at advanced stage may have ureteral obstruction and fistulas.

Epidemiology

Fewer than 2% of cervical SCCs are HPV independent in countries with cervical cancer screening programmes in different case series {25229645; 30911077; 40010435; 37702216}. Lower frequencies would be expected in other countries, but data are not available. Based on the largest reported series of 49 cases from multiple centres in high-income countries, patients with HPV-independent SCCs present at a median age of 68 years (range: 36–88 years) {37702216}.

Etiology

Longstanding inflammatory and/or ulcerating injuries to the cervix, sometimes associated with uterine prolapse, are hypothesized to predispose to the development of HPV-independent and p53-mutated SCCs {11688467; 38959413}. For HPV-independent and p53-wildtype SCCs, the etiology is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Based on the relatively small number of cases reported, TP53 mutation or mutation-type p53 immunohistochemical expression is found in approximately 50% of cases {40010435; 38959413; 34387215}. Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, HPV-independent, whose cancer risk is currently unknown, may be present adjacent to HPV-independent SCCs (see section Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, HPV-independent).

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of HPV-independent SCCs is similar to that of HPV-associated SCCs, but HPV-independent tumours are usually larger.

Histopathology

HPV-independent SCCs are morphologically indistinguishable from HPV-associated SCCs in general, but they are more frequently keratinizing and may have extensive necrosis, a dense lymphocyte-predominant inflammatory infiltrate, and highly vascularized stroma {30911077; 22226684; 40010435; 37702216}. For a complete description of the histology, see section Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-associated. There is no grading system for HPV-independent SCCs (see section Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-associated).

The absence of HPV is confirmed with a negative/non–block-type p16 immunohistochemical stain and negative HPV molecular testing, as rarely HPV-associated carcinomas show loss of p16 {40010435; 30911077}. p53 immunostaining is mutation type in approximately 50% of cases, and it is recommended that p53 immunohistochemistry or molecular testing to look for a TP53 mutation is performed in these neoplasms {40010435}.

Cytology

HPV-associated and HPV-independent SCCs are indistinguishable on the basis of cytology {30911077}. HPV-independent SCCs are rarely detected in cervical cytology specimens {30911077}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The absence of HPV can be ascertained with PCR or in situ hybridization for low- and high-risk HPV types. p53 immunohistochemical staining and/or DNA sequencing for TP53 mutations further stratifies tumours into p53 mutation type and wild type.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive carcinoma with squamous differentiation and negative/non–block-type p16 immunohistochemistry and negative HPV testing.

Desirable: none.

Staging

Tumours are staged according to the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Based on the relatively small number of cases reported, HPV-independent cervical SCCs are frequently diagnosed at an advanced stage. They have a high rate of lymph node metastasis and reduced disease-free and overall survival compared with HPV-associated cervical SCC {30911077; 25229645; 1971033; 40010435}. Although the reported numbers are small, patients with HPV-independent TP53-mutated SCC are diagnosed at higher stage, present with larger tumours, and are more likely to die of disease than patients with HPV-independent TP53-wildtype SCCs {40010435}. In one study, clinical and pathological complete response to preoperative chemoradiotherapy was significantly lower in HPV-independent cervical cancers (90% of which were SCCs) compared with HPV-associated cancers {36720505}.
