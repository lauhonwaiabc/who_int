14.1.0.1 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue

Definition

Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (EMZL) of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue is an indolent primary extranodal B-cell lymphoma with cytological and architectural features reminiscent of Peyer patch lymphoid tissue, the prototypical mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT). EMZL typically arises from marginal zone B cells of acquired MALT and is often associated with an underlying chronic inflammatory disorder.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma; extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of MALT.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common primary site for marginal zone lymphoma within the female genital tract (FGT) is the endometrium (38%), followed by the cervix (20%) {28284518}. Vagina and vulva are rare sites, while other localizations such as fallopian ducts and ovaries are only rarely reported {28284518; 35416104; 36042662; 23108066}. Secondary FGT involvement by EMZL also occurs extremely rarely {23108066; 25398916}.

Clinical features

It is typically an incidental finding, and the majority of cases are diagnosed during hysterectomy or other surgical procedures for unrelated benign or malignant disorders {27340748}. The vast majority presents with stage I or II disease {24352894}.

Epidemiology

Based on US SEER data, FGT EMZL is rare, accounting for 3–5% of all primary FGT lymphomas {28284518; 35416104}. FGT EMZL mainly affects older women, with a median age of 60–66 years {35416104; 28284518}. Because of its rarity, (reliable) data on racial associations and geographical variation are lacking.

Etiology

EMZL usually arises in acquired MALT at extranodal sites, which results from a chronic inflammatory process caused by infection, autoimmunity, or unknown factors. A relationship with specific infectious factors has not been identified for FGT EMZL.

Pathogenesis

The genesis of EMZL is the result of oncogenic cooperation among dysregulated immune responses and somatic genetic changes {28288707}. The mutational and translocation profiles differ according to primary extranodal sites {34494161; 39542179}. Data on FGT EMZL are very sparse and have not shown any characteristic alterations in the few cases reported {24352894; 27340748; 26598978; 26045815; 28521466}.

Macroscopic appearance

FGT EMZL can sometimes present as an endometrial polyp {27340748}.

Histopathology

EMZL is a proliferation of atypical small to medium-sized lymphoid cells that efface the architecture of the affected sites, with or without site-specific lymphoepithelial lesions. Neoplastic cells include variable proportions of small lymphocytes; marginal zone cells with oval to slightly irregular nuclei, smooth chromatin, and pale cytoplasm; monocytoid cells with abundant pale cytoplasm; and scattered large cells. Plasma cells are often admixed, sometimes forming large aggregates; they are monotypic in one third of cases {39624879}. Reactive follicles, sometimes with follicular colonization (infiltration and partial to complete replacement by neoplastic marginal zone cells) are found in some cases. Scattered centroblast- or immunoblast-like cells are common. In the endometrium, EMZL typically takes the form of aggregates composed of small, monotonous lymphoid cells with pale cytoplasm, sometimes surrounding endometrial glands; lymphoepithelial lesions and reactive follicles are generally lacking {27340748; 26045815}.

Similarly to EMZL at other sites, large-cell transformation should be diagnosed when there are sheets of large cells unassociated with follicular dendritic cell meshworks {27460179; 31912792}.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic cells express CD20, CD79a, and PAX5; usually IgM; occasionally IgG or IgA; and exceptionally IgD. EMZLs are typically negative for CD5, CD10, BCL6, CD23, cyclin D1, and SOX11 {31912792}. Specific data on expression in female genital tract EMZL of IRTA1 (FCRL4, CD307d) and MNDA, markers associated with marginal zone cell differentiation and frequently expressed in EMZL at other more frequent sites, are not available {30346478}. Immunoglobulin light chain restriction can aid in the distinction from reactive lymphoid proliferations. CD21 highlights expanded follicular dendritic cell meshworks in the background of the lymphoma, encompassing both reactive follicles and colonized follicles. Cytokeratin can highlight subtle lymphoepithelial lesions.

Differential diagnosis

In all FGT sites, other mature B-cell lymphomas (e.g. follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma) can be excluded by a lack of CD10, BCL6, CD5, cyclin D1, and LEF1 protein expression.

In the lower FGT, florid lymphoid hyperplasia, also called lymphoid-like lesion, is the main differential diagnostic consideration. Most cases present in the cervix (79%) followed by the endometrium (14%), while vulvar cases are rare. The growth pattern of these lesions can be nodular, diffuse, or mixed. A component of overt reactive lymphoid follicles is present in most cases, especially those associated with chlamydia infection. A plasma cell component is often prominent, but very rarely immunoglobulin light chain restricted. However, monoclonal immunoglobulin gene rearrangements have been reported in a sizable proportion of the few studied cases {20087162; 34723846}. A prominent of chronic inflammatory background (chronic cervicitis, endometritis) is helpful in raising suspicion of a reactive nature. Since a large cell component is almost always found in these lesions, a diagnosis of aggressive B-cell lymphoma should be approached with caution.

Cytology

Cytopathology can be helpful in the evaluation of lesions at selected FGT sites, but distinguishing EMZL from reactive lymphoid infiltrates may be difficult. Please refer to the third edition of the Bethesda System for Reporting Cervical Cytopathology [[Nayar R, Wilbur DC, editors. The Bethesda System for Reporting Cervical Cytology. 3rd ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2015.]] and the WHO Reporting System for Lymph Node, Spleen and Thymus Cytopathology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of monoclonal IG gene rearrangements may help establish a diagnosis when the histopathological and immunophenotypic findings are suspicious but not diagnostic; however, results must be interpreted with caution as lymphoma-like lesions may harbour clonal IG gene rearrangements.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primary lymphoma arising at an FGT site; atypical small/medium-sized lymphoid cell proliferation mimicking reactive MALT and showing architectural distortion; expression of B-lineage markers; exclusion of other small B-cell neoplasms, e.g. follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, small lymphocytic lymphoma, lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma, and plasmacytoma.

Desirable: demonstration of light chain restriction or clonal IG gene rearrangement; lymphoepithelial lesions; remnants of underlying inflammatory background (e.g. reactive lymphoid follicles).

Staging

Lymphoid neoplasms are staged according to the Lugano classification as adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}. The Ann Arbor classification has often been used for non-gastric EMZL {31912792; 26773441}.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with endometrial MZL generally present with limited stage disease and are almost always free of disease at follow-up after hysterectomy with or without bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy alone {27340748; 31008989}. Clinical information on EMZL at other FGT sites and/or higher-stage disease is limited to single case reports only.
