9.4.0.4 Small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcaemic type

Definition

Small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcaemic type, (SCCOHT) is a high-grade undifferentiated malignancy composed of small cells, with or without a large cell component, and is characterized in almost all cases by SMARCA4 inactivation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Most patients present with symptoms related to pelvic or abdominal disease. Two thirds have paraneoplastic hypercalcaemia, although only a minority present with symptoms related to this. Tumours may arise in the context of germline (constitutional) SMARCA4 variants, which cause rhabdoid tumour predisposition syndrome 2 and are associated with an increased risk for atypical teratoid / rhabdoid tumours and other extracranial malignant rhabdoid tumours {7943531; 26975901}. These tumours are almost always unilateral, but bilateral neoplasms rarely occur in patients with germline SMARCA4 variants.

Epidemiology

SCCOHT is rare, accounting for < 1% of ovarian malignancies {7943531; 26975901}. Fewer than 500 cases have been reported, and familial cases occur at an earlier age {26975901; 32156746}. Most patients are in their second or third decades of life (median patient age: 25 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Mutations in SMARCA4 are detected in almost all tumours {24375037; 24658001; 24658002; 24658004; 26942101; 32156746}. Constitutional SMARCA4 variants have been reported in 20–43% of tumours {26942101; 26975901}. Possible alterations include biallelic structural variations, a structural variation combined with a mutation, or compound heterozygous mutations. Mutations in other genes are rare and copy-number changes infrequent {26942101; 24658002}. SMARCB1 mutations have been identified in occasional cases and are mutually exclusive with SMARCA4 mutations {26942101; 35200537}. SCCOHTs harbour distinct DNA methylation profiles, which distinguish them from other undifferentiated malignancies with SMARCA4 or SMARCB1 inactivating mutations {35218556}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are usually large, with a mean size of 150 mm (range: 60–260 mm). They are solid, fleshy, and tan to white to grey, often with haemorrhage, necrosis, and cystic degeneration {7943531}.

Histopathology

The tumours are composed of dyscohesive cells typically growing predominantly in diffuse sheets with occasional nests, cords, and trabeculae, usually associated with minimal intervening stroma. Scattered follicle-like spaces with eosinophilic or basophilic secretions are typically seen. Some regions may show loosely arranged tumour cells and clusters within a myxoid stroma. Cells have monomorphic round, ovoid, or occasionally spindled nuclei with coarse chromatin, small nucleoli, scant cytoplasm, and brisk mitotic activity. Areas of necrosis and vascular invasion are common. Large cells are present in approximately half of these tumours, which are designated small cell carcinoma, large cell pattern if the large cells are predominant (> 50% of the neoplasm). The large cells display eccentric nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, sometimes imparting a rhabdoid appearance. Approximately 15% of tumours have a very minor mucinous component, which usually consists of benign glands or cysts or rarely signet-ring cells {7943531; 24375037; 28130795}. A few cases have been associated with teratomatous elements {28130795; 24375037}.

Immunohistochemistry reveals an absence of SMARCA4 (and SMARCA2) staining in almost all tumours {27100627; 26645725; 26564006; 26135561; 26356327}. In rare cases, SMARCA4 mutations may be associated with retained SMARCA4 expression, but SMARCA2 is usually not expressed in these cases {37345679}. In tumours with retained SMARCA4 expression, loss of SMARCB1 has rarely been reported {26942101; 35200537}. There is no report of an association with abnormal mismatch repair protein expression, and p53 exhibits wildtype immunoreactivity {37067676}. WT1 is often, but not always, diffusely positive, while claudin-4, SALL4, cytokeratins, EMA (especially in the large cell component), CD10, calretinin, CD99, TLE1, neuroendocrine markers, and PTHrP have variable expression. Inhibin and TTF1 are usually negative {28130795; 15381902; 37345679}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

SMARCA4 mutation analysis may be useful for diagnosis, including in cases with retained SMARCA4 expression.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: undifferentiated malignant tumour; predominantly dyscohesive monomorphic cells with or without follicle-like spaces.

Desirable: loss of SMARCA4 immunohistochemical staining or molecular demonstration of SMARCA4 mutation.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis is poor despite combined surgery and aggressive chemoradiation {32156746}. Stage is the most important prognostic factor, with only 30% long-term survival in early-stage cases {32156746}. Favourable prognostic factors include age > 30 years, normal serum calcium levels, tumour size < 10 cm, and absence of large cells {7943531; 26975901}. All patients with a diagnosis of SCCOHT should be referred to clinical genetics for germline testing and counselling.
