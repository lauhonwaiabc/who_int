8.8.0.1 Mesonephric-like carcinoma

Definition

Mesonephric-like carcinoma is an adenocarcinoma displaying mesonephric differentiation but lacking an association with mesonephric remnants.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This tumour occurs in the ovary.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation is most commonly pelvic or abdominal pain followed by bloating. Most patients present with stage I disease, with approximately a third of patients presenting at advanced stage (FIGO III or IV) {33165093; 37026792}.

Epidemiology

No epidemiological data are available on this very rare tumour. One recent case series found that 0.7% of endometrial carcinomas were mesonephric-like carcinomas {35195579}. Most of these tumours occur in postmenopausal women {33165093; 37026792}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis remains unclear. These are hypothesized to represent Müllerian carcinomas with secondary mesonephric transdifferentiation. This hypothesis is supported by a lack of associated mesonephric remnants; the association of mesonephric-like carcinomas with endometriosis, cystadenomas, adenofibromas, borderline tumours, low-grade serous carcinomas, and other neoplasms {37026792; 30575604; 33772212; 34183523}; as well as shared molecular alterations more commonly seen in Müllerian carcinomas such as PIK3CA, PTEN, and ARID1A {37026792; 34183523; 33772212; 28984674}. Of note, ovarian mesonephric-like carcinomas with a coexisting Müllerian component typically show common molecular alterations (KRAS or NRAS mutations) in both components {30575604; 34183523; 33772212}. KRAS mutations, loss of 1p, and gain of 1q are the most common molecular alterations {28984674; 35384888}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are usually unilateral, with variable size (4–32 cm). The cut surface is solid or mixed solid and cystic, with a greyish-white or yellowish-tan appearance {26484981}.

Histopathology

The histological features are identical to those of mesonephric carcinomas occurring at other sites in the female genital tract and include tubular, glandular (pseudoendometrioid), ductal (with slit-like or angulated glands), papillary, and solid architectural patterns. An admixture of architectural patterns within the same tumour is seen in most but not all cases. Eosinophilic colloid-like material can be present within the lumen. The nuclei are often angulated with open, vesicular chromatin. Nuclear overlap and grooves are frequent findings. No squamous or mucinous elements are seen {37026792}; however, other Müllerian components, such as endometrioid carcinoma, may be present, in which case the tumour should be designated as a mixed carcinoma. By immunohistochemistry, most cases are positive for GATA3 and/or TTF1. CD10 luminal staining and PAX8 positivity may also be present. WT1 is negative. Expression of ER and PR are significantly reduced or negative {30148742; 37026792}. These tumours are typically p53 wild type, and all reported cases are mismatch repair proficient {37026792; 33772212}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of a canonical activating KRAS mutation in codon 12 (most common) or 13 may be helpful in diagnostically challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: histopathological features prototypical for mesonephric carcinoma.

Desirable: expression of GATA3 or TTF1; absent or reduced expression of ER and PR.

Staging

Mesonephric-like carcinoma is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Because these tumours are rare, it is difficult to predict their behaviour. However, mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma behaves similarly to other high-grade ovarian cancer histotypes {37026792}. Small studies demonstrate that 42–55% recur {33165093; 37026792}, most commonly to distant sites {33165093; 37026792}.
