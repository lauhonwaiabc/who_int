3.2.3.2 Adenocarcinoma of Skene gland origin

Definition

Adenocarcinoma of Skene gland origin is an adenocarcinoma arising from the Skene (periurethral) glands, morphologically and immunohistochemically similar to prostatic adenocarcinoma.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: Skene’s gland adenocarcinoma, periurethral gland adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Skene glands occur in the anterior vagina and distal one third of the female urethra; thus, these tumours, which arise from these minor vestibular glands, are typically localized to periurethral/paraurethral, urethral, anterior vaginal wall, and bladder neck locations {14668555; 9584326; 7525428; 3114949; 28872768; 7507278; 20870432; 29901570; 29439715; 31883942; 32504685; 32795117; 34706525; 35192863}.

Clinical features

The usual presentation is with a mass and (less frequently) haematuria or incontinence. Serum PSA may be elevated {14668555; 20870432; 7525428; 29901570; 29439715; 32504685}.

Epidemiology

This extremely rare tumour has been reported in patients ranging in age from 46 to 88 years (median: 71 years) {14668555; 9584326; 7525428; 3114949; 28872768; 7507278; 20870432; 29901570; 29439715; 31883942; 32504685; 32795117; 34706525; 35192863}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These tumours arise from the periurethral Skene glands, the female equivalent of the prostate gland in male individuals. A frameshift variant in the PTEN gene with loss of heterozygosity was demonstrated in one case {32795117}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours may present as a polypoid nodule or flat tumour, 0.3–3.5 cm in size {29901570; 31883942; 34706525; 35192863}.

Histopathology

These neoplasms are usually morphologically similar to prostatic acinar adenocarcinoma. Typically, there is a population of well-formed to poorly formed glands lined by cuboidal or columnar epithelium, with pale or eosinophilic cytoplasm, round nuclei, and prominent nucleoli. A cribriform pattern is common, and individual glands, solid areas, or a single-file pattern can be seen. The degree of nuclear atypia is variable. Goblet cells are rarely seen. Residual non-neoplastic Skene glands or foci resembling high-grade prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia may be present. Immunohistochemically, Skene gland adenocarcinoma is generally positive for prostatic markers such as PSA, PAP, NKX3-1, and P501S {14668555; 9584326; 7525428; 3114949; 28872768; 7507278; 20870432; 29901570}. AMACR expression has been present in some of the reported cases {29901570; 31883942; 32795117}. CK7 is generally negative {20870432; 28872768; 31883942; 32504685; 32795117; 34706525}. CK20 is positive in some cases {28872768; 29901570; 31883942}. AR may be positive.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: adenocarcinoma morphologically resembling prostatic adenocarcinoma; positivity for prostatic markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Experience with this neoplasm is limited. Although several cases with inguinal or pelvic lymph node metastases have been documented {9584326; 32504685; 35192863}, no recurrence or disease-related mortality was observed in the few cases with clinical follow-up after treatment {35192863}.
