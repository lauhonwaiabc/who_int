9.3.1.1 Mature teratoma (including somatic neoplasms arising from teratoma)

Definition

Mature teratoma is a tumour composed exclusively of mature tissues derived from one or more germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm).

Benign or malignant tumours can arise from any of the elements and are termed “somatic neoplasms arising from a teratoma”.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: mature cystic teratoma; mature solid teratoma.

Not recommended: dermoid cyst.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary and rarely in the fallopian tube, uterine corpus, cervix, and vagina.

Clinical features

Abdominal pain or other signs and symptoms related to an adnexal mass are a common presentation; some cases are detected incidentally. Rarely, young women present with anti-N-methyl-D-aspartic acid receptor (NMDAR) encephalitis {22833082; 25272299; 31021857}. About 10% of cases are bilateral {13238472; 16990705}. Somatic neoplasms arising from teratomas generally also present with signs and symptoms related to a mass {17240432; 2712191}. Although not specific, size > 100 mm and elevated CEA in patients aged ≥ 45 years are suggestive of malignancy {18971155; 17240432}; sarcomas arising in teratomas tend to occur in younger patients than carcinomas. Uterine corpus, cervix, and vaginal tumours usually present with abnormal vaginal bleeding.

Epidemiology

Mature teratomas account for 20% of all ovarian neoplasms in pathological studies {13238472; 16990705; 40070286}. Teratomas of the fallopian tube, uterine corpus, cervix, and vagina are extremely rare tumours. Most cases of mature teratoma occur in women of reproductive age.

A somatic neoplasm arising within an ovarian mature teratoma is an uncommon event, with reported incidence rates ranging from 0.17% to 5.5% {40070286} and averaging approximately 2%. Any of the elements within a mature teratoma can potentially undergo neoplastic transformation. Of the malignant tumours, squamous cell carcinoma is the predominant histological type, accounting for roughly 80% of cases. Adenocarcinoma represents the second most frequent carcinoma, comprising approximately 7% of cases {13493921}, while sarcomas constitute about 8% {13493921; 9421078}. Somatic neoplasms arising from teratomas occur on average at an older age (average: 55 years), typically two decades later than uncomplicated teratomas {18971155; 17240432; 19038764; 13493921}.

Other less frequently observed neoplasms include melanomas, melanocytic naevi, basal cell carcinomas, and sebaceous neoplasms {13493921; 13752348; 16990706; 1774106; 8886884; 15166669; 11844060; 6893919; 4757597}. Additionally, a diverse array of both benign and malignant neoplasms has been documented, including choroid plexus papilloma, Paget disease, glomus tumour, benign soft tissue tumours, and various lymphomas {1774106; 20034987; 8503739; 1718175; 15028031; 10975942; 28700440; 28864175}. Some ovarian Wilms tumours also arise in teratomas {38084641} (see section Wilms tumour).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The favoured pathogenetic explanation for mature teratomas is the parthenogenetic theory, which suggests an origin from the primordial germ cell {162806; 5476662; 5921072; 6363699; 28664933}. Most studies support a derivation from postmeiotic I or premeiotic II germ cells {10233836; 5259759; 162806}.

The pathogenesis of somatic neoplasms arising within a mature teratoma is incompletely understood. Squamous cell carcinoma arising in a mature teratoma has a high mutational burden, with TP53 mutations being the most common abnormality {28954785}. KRAS and TP53 mutations are frequently observed in teratoma-associated mucinous tumours {31695154}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most ovarian mature teratomas are cystic (mature cystic teratoma), but some are solid. They are usually 50–100 mm but are typically smaller (average: 19 mm) in women with anti-NMDAR encephalitis {13238472; 31021857}. The cysts contain sebaceous material, hair, and sometimes teeth or cartilage. A solid nodule lined by hair-bearing skin (Rokitansky protuberance) is sometimes present along the cyst lining. Mature solid teratoma is solid, sometimes with interspersed cysts {4722922; 13362421}. Those resembling a malformed human fetus have been termed “fetiform teratoma” {6724790}. Solid areas should be liberally sampled (at least one section per centimetre). Most reported teratomas of the fallopian tube have been small and predominantly solid but some have been large and cystic {23445130}.

Somatic neoplasms arising in a mature teratoma are typically large and solid, or solid and cystic, with or without a recognizable typical benign component. They may protrude into a cyst or cause thickening of the wall {18971155; 17240432}. Extraovarian spread is common.

Histopathology

Most mature teratomas contain tissues from at least two germ cell (embryonic) layers. However, occasional examples contain tissues from only one germ cell layer. Ectodermal derivatives include squamous epithelium and cutaneous adnexal structures, as well as neuroectoderm (glia, ependyma, choroid plexus, and cerebellum). Mesodermal derivatives include adipose tissue, bone, cartilage, and smooth muscle. Endodermal derivatives include gastrointestinal and respiratory/bronchial epithelium, thyroid, and salivary glands. Rarely, prostate {12068172}, pituitary, adrenal, parathyroid, and retinal tissue {5774665} can be seen as well as a wide range of other elements. Bronchus-like structures with a well-formed bronchus surrounded by glandular structures have also been reported {33770061}. Fat necrosis and a foreign body reaction to keratin are common. Florid benign vascular proliferations may be seen in occasional tumours, appearing as tightly packed small vessels, gaping vessels, or glomeruloid formations, which may be mistaken for a vascular tumour {40070286}. In patients with anti-NMDAR encephalitis, neuroglial tissues are typically infiltrated by lymphoid aggregates with germinal centres {31021857; 22833082}.

Peritoneal lesions uncommonly associated with mature teratomas include gliomatosis peritonei, granulomatous reactions secondary to the leakage of cystic contents, and melanosis peritonei.

The somatic neoplasms arising in mature teratomas exhibit the typical morphological appearances of the particular neoplasm. Usually, some residual mature teratoma is present, but occasionally this is overgrown or may only be identified with extensive sampling. Squamous cell carcinoma shows a varied morphology, ranging from well-differentiated and keratinizing to poorly differentiated to anaplastic (including sarcomatoid). Adenocarcinomas most commonly arise from gastrointestinal-type epithelium {7821857} and less frequently from respiratory-type epithelium {23026196; 15656343; 13493921; 1653182}.

Mucinous neoplasms arising within ovarian teratomas are predominantly of gastrointestinal type and span a spectrum ranging from benign to borderline and overtly malignant lesions {40300267}. Most of these tumours exhibit an intestinal immunophenotype and can closely resemble primary gastrointestinal tract neoplasms. Some resemble typical primary ovarian mucinous neoplasms not associated with teratomas (upper gastrointestinal morphology and immunophenotype), while others are more akin morphologically and immunohistochemically to those of lower gastrointestinal origin, particularly appendix. Morphologically these latter neoplasms typically exhibit a hypermucinous appearance with scalloped borders and subepithelial stromal clefts. Immunohistochemically, they typically express CK20, CDX2, and SATB2 diffusely, while demonstrating only focal or absent CK7 expression, and are consistently negative for PAX8 and ER. As such, these mucinous neoplasms originating in teratomas closely mimic appendiceal / lower gastrointestinal tumours. Pseudomyxoma ovarii and pseudomyxoma peritonei can be seen in association with these lower gastrointestinal-type mucinous tumours derived from teratomas. In contrast, the occurrence of pseudomyxoma peritonei in mucinous ovarian neoplasms not associated with teratomas is usually indicative of metastasis from a gastrointestinal primary, especially the appendix. Accurate diagnosis requires correlation with clinical findings, careful evaluation of the appendix, and thorough sampling of the ovarian neoplasm to identify teratomatous elements; however, these may occasionally be totally overgrown. An alternative terminology, akin to that used for appendiceal mucinous neoplasms, is recommended for large intestinal-type mucinous neoplasms arising in teratomas {33963606} (#56054Table 9. XXX).

Many different sarcomas rarely arise in teratomas {22833084; 21623194; 19696615; 12759630}. Malignant melanomas may show usual (epithelioid and/or spindled) or unusual appearances, including pseudopapillary architecture, follicle-like spaces, and a myxoid background {16990706}. Sebaceous lesions include sebaceous hyperplasia, adenoma, and carcinoma {1774106}. Rarely, sebaceous carcinoma arising in a mature teratoma is associated with mismatch repair abnormalities and Lynch syndrome {35077082}.

Malignant thyroid-type neoplasms that arise in a struma ovarii and ovarian carcinoids are discussed in separate chapters (see section Struma ovarii, and section Ovarian carcinoid).

Cytology

Teratomas display anucleate squames and squamous cells against a background of amorphous debris, giving a dirty background with inflammatory cells. Other features include epithelial cells and rarely hair shafts {19500123}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

Mature teratoma: neoplasm composed of mature tissues from at least one germ cell layer

Somatic neoplasms: the essential and desirable diagnostic criteria are based on the histological type of the somatic neoplasm

Staging

Mature teratomas are benign tumours and are not staged.

Malignant somatic neoplasms arising from mature teratomas are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Mature teratomas are benign except in rare instances of concurrent malignant transformation and rare cases of development of immature teratoma in the residual ovary after partial excision of a mature teratoma {3429105; 10202679}. Gliomatosis peritonei can rarely occur in women with mature teratoma but does not adversely affect prognosis {5521737} and is not staged in this context.

The presence of occasional microscopic foci of immature neural tissue in an otherwise mature teratoma remains controversial regarding its clinical significance and impact on tumour classification. Some studies indicate that occasional immature elements do not worsen prognosis and do not justify reclassifying the tumour as an immature teratoma {3429105}. An alternative perspective highlights the subjectivity inherent in histopathological assessment and notes that both mature teratomas with occasional immature foci and low-grade immature teratomas exhibit similarly favourable outcomes, including low rates of recurrence and excellent long-term survival; moreover, there is no definition of what is meant by occasional {32763784; 38544795}.

Because of this overlap in clinical behaviour, strict classification based on minimal immature tissue may have limited clinical relevance, but it is recommended to classify these neoplasms as immature teratomas. The presence of occasional microscopic foci of immature neural tissue should prompt thorough sampling to exclude the possibility of more extensive immature elements.

The prognosis for somatic malignant neoplasms is highly dependent on tumour type and stage, and most data are derived from squamous cell carcinoma. Overall, patients with tumours confined to the ovary have a favourable outcome. The 5-year overall survival rate is 15–52% for all stages combined {2712191} and 75.7% for stage I tumours {18971155; 21430455; 17240432; 19038764}. The prognosis for advanced-stage squamous cell carcinoma is poor and worse than that of more common ovarian cancers {18971155; 17240432; 19038764; 17877681; 31092447}.
