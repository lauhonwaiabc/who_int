10.1.4.1 Lipoma

Definition

Lipoma is a benign tumour of mature adipocytes.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: conventional or classic lipoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These most commonly occur in the vulva but may occur at any location.

Clinical features

Lipoma presents as a superficial, solitary, soft, mobile, and painless mass {24946809; 24629715; 19853747}.

Epidemiology

Lipomas are rare entities in the female genital tract, with an estimated incidence of just 0.03% of all gynaecological tumours {36782265}. Lipomas occur in adults of reproductive age, as well as in children {24946809; 24629715; 19853747}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

An abnormal karyotype is present in the majority of conventional lipomas. The most common alteration involves 12q13-q15, resulting in fusion of HMGA2 with a variety of gene partners {9530339}. Other reported chromosomal abnormalities include 6p and 13q rearrangements {9530339}. MDM2 amplification is not a feature.

Macroscopic appearance

Lipomas are lobulated, well circumscribed, and sometimes covered by a thin membrane. The cut surface is homogeneously yellow, with thin fibrous septa dividing the tumour into lobules. Focal haemorrhage can be present {19853747}. Tumours have been reported to exceed 150 mm in greatest dimension {24629715}.

Histopathology

Tumours consist of a relatively uniform population of mature adipocytes arranged in sheets and/or separated into lobules by thin fibrous septa {24946809; 24629715; 19853747}. Adipocytes and septal stromal cells lack cytological atypia; lipoblasts are not present. Degenerative changes, including myxoid stromal change and fat necrosis, can occur, particularly in larger tumours. Tumours lack MDM2 and CDK4 expression by immunohistochemistry.

Cytology

Smears are variably cellular, composed of tissue fragments of mature adipocytes. There are uniform adipocytes containing a single large cytoplasmic clear vacuole and small nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli against a background that is bloody with fat globules and small round open spaces {12920746}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: superficial location; uniformly sized mature adipocytes; lack of atypical stromal cells in fibrous septa.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign tumours.
