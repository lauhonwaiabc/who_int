5.2.2.3 Mesonephric-like carcinoma

Definition

Mesonephric-like carcinoma of the endometrium is a malignant epithelial neoplasm displaying mesonephric differentiation but lacking an association with mesonephric remnants.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma.

 

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Endometrium

Clinical features

The clinical presentation is most commonly abnormal vaginal bleeding. Over half of the cases are FIGO stage II–IV at the time of diagnosis {31725471; 33165093}.

Epidemiology

No epidemiological data are available on this very rare tumour. Most of these tumours occur in postmenopausal women {31725471; 33165093}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis remains unclear. These tumours are most likely Müllerian-derived, with an acquired phenotype of mesonephric-type differentiation. This hypothesis is supported by the rare association of mesonephric-like carcinoma with more common forms of endometrioid neoplasia (atypical endometrial hyperplasia, endometrioid adenocarcinoma) as well as the association of molecular alterations commonly encountered in Müllerian-type adenocarcinomas such as PIK3CA, PTEN, ARID1A, and CTNNB1 {29189288; 33165093; 33772212; 36860193}. KRAS mutations, loss of 1p, and gain of 1q are the most common molecular alterations observed in this tumour, but these are not unique to mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma {28984674; 33772212}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are single, exophytic, endometrial-based lesions of variable size (13–130 mm). The cut surface is tan to grey, and usually solid. Deep myometrial invasion with a poorly defined tumour to myometrial interface is a common feature {29189288; 31725471}.

Histopathology

The histological features are identical to mesonephric and mesonephric-like carcinomas occurring at other sites in the female genital tract. Common architectural patterns include tubular, ductal/glandular, papillary, glomeruloid, and solid patterns. Spindled growth may also be present, but nuclear features tend to be similar to that seen in the non-spindled components. An admixture of different patterns within the same tumour is seen in most but not all cases. Characteristic cytological features include angulated nuclei with open or vesicular chromatin, overlapping nuclei, and nuclear grooves. Eosinophilic colloid-like material can be present within luminal spaces. No squamous or mucinous differentiation is seen, and mesonephric remnants should not be present {31725471}. By immunohistochemistry, most cases are positive for GATA3, TTF1, CD10 (apical staining), and PAX8, although the extent of positivity is variable. Nuclear expression of estrogen and progesterone receptors is usually absent or minimal, and p53 shows a wildtype expression pattern. These tumours are mismatch repair proficient by immunohistochemistry {31725471; 33670088; 36974792}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of a canonical activating KRAS mutation in codon 12 (most common) or codon 13 may be helpful in diagnostically challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: carcinoma displaying mesonephric differentiation; lacking associated mesonephric remnants.

Desirable: nuclear expression of GATA3 and/or TTF1; negativity or minimal expression of estrogen and progesterone receptors; expression of wildtype p53; intact mismatch repair protein expression.

Staging

Mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Compared with other, more common types of endometrial carcinoma, mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma has a stronger association with adverse uterine factors, more often presents at advanced stage, and has an association with distant recurrences particularly in the lung {31725471}. For this reason, these tumours are not graded and are considered as an “aggressive” histological type in endometrial carcinoma.
