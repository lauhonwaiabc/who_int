9.2.3.1 Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour

Definition

Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour (SLCT) is a mixed sex cord and stromal tumour composed of varying proportions of Sertoli and Leydig cells.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour, well-differentiated; Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour, moderately-differentiated/ poorly-differentiated/ retiform.

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

About 97% of these tumours are unilateral. Occasional bilateral tumours occur, usually in patients with DICER1 syndrome {32333409}. Presentation may be with hormonal manifestations or symptoms related to the presence of an ovarian mass; 40–60% of patients have androgenic manifestations, and occasional patients have estrogenic manifestations {3911780; 22850410; 6731664}. Patients may present with abdominal pain, ascites, or tumour rupture.

Epidemiology

These tumours account for < 0.5% of ovarian neoplasms in clinicopathological studies. Moderately and poorly differentiated forms are more common than well-differentiated neoplasms {31106943}. Patients present over a wide age range, with reports describing an age range of 1–84 years (mean: 25 years) {3911780; 22850410; 6731664}. Retiform tumours occur at a younger age as do tumours in patients with a pathogenic germline DICER1 mutation {40267387}.

Etiology

Tumours can occur either sporadically or in association with the DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome (DICER1 syndrome) {19556464; 21205968; 21501861; 21266384; 21882293}. For more comprehensive information on DICER1 syndrome, refer to section DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome (DICER1).

Pathogenesis

Molecular profiles, including DNA methylation patterns {36966138; 38114269}, are associated with distinct morphological and clinical features in SLCT (see below).

More than half of all SLCTs (range: 15–97%) harbour somatic missense variants in the RNase IIIb domain of DICER1, affecting one of five codons (p.Glu1705, p.Asp1709, p.Gly1809, p.Asp1810, or p.Glu1813) {22187960; 28654427; 26428316; 26033501; 23763383; 29037807; 25469243; 24136150}. These somatic missense mutations are typically accompanied by a second, loss-of-function mutation in the other DICER1 allele that truncates the coding sequence (compound DICER1 mutations) {22187960}. In tumours associated with DICER1 syndrome, somatic missense mutations are accompanied by germline (constitutional) loss-of-function variants that are mostly nonsense or frameshift mutations. Rarely, they involve intronic variants that are predicted to cause incorrect splicing {22187960; 28654427; 26289771; 37883719}. In some studies, germline variants have been found in more than half of SLCTs {28654427; 29037807; 38657450}, a figure that may be artificially high as a result of referral bias and more-intensive study of hereditary cases in younger patients. In non-syndromic tumours, the inactivation of the second DICER1 allele typically occurs through somatic mutations {22187960}. Somatic missense variants in the RNase IIIb domain impair the endoribonuclease function of DICER1, leading to reduced expression of 5p strand miRNAs. This disruption can alter global gene expression, differentiate an ovarian cell into a sertoliform phenotype, and induce androgenic symptoms {26408257; 23132766}.

SLCTs with DICER1 mutations are typically moderately or poorly differentiated or retiform tumours, with or without heterologous elements, and are typically seen in younger patients. Tumours are often diploid and gain of chromosome 8 has been observed in the presence of heterologous elements {36966138}.

In contrast, SLCTs that are wild type for DICER1 include essentially all well-differentiated tumours, and these neoplasms do not contain heterologous elements {30986800; 36583307; 39592485}. These tumours may be aneuploid {36966138} and can occur across a wide age range. The available morphological and molecular evidence currently indicates that well-differentiated SLCTs represent a distinct tumour type, separate from moderately/poorly differentiated and retiform SLCTs.

FOXL2 NM_023067.4 c.402C>G p.(Cys134Trp) mutation has been reported in some moderately to poorly differentiated SLCTs in older (generally postmenopausal) patients and is mutually exclusive with DICER1 mutations {28654427; 26033501; 30986800; 28700438; 36583307}. Moderately to poorly differentiated tumours with FOXL2 mutations do not contain retiform or heterologous elements. The clinical and some pathological features of these neoplasms (estrogenic manifestations, older patients, absence of retiform and heterologous elements) are more characteristic of adult granulosa cell tumours than of SLCTs with DICER1 mutations, and the exact classification of these neoplasms is controversial. This group of tumours could represent adult granulosa cell tumours with morphological mimicry of SLCT, and a differential diagnosis of adult granulosa cell tumour should be strongly considered in those tumours that harbour a FOXL2 hotspot mutation.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range in size with a mean size of 120–140 mm. They may be solid, solid and cystic, or rarely predominantly cystic. Solid areas are fleshy and pale yellow, pink, or grey. Haemorrhage and necrosis may be present.

Histopathology

SLCTs are divided into well-differentiated, moderately differentiated, and poorly differentiated forms on the basis of the degree of tubular differentiation of the Sertoli cell component (decreasing with increasing grade) and the quantity of primitive gonadal stroma (increasing with increasing grade). Leydig cells also typically decrease with increasing grade. Well-differentiated tumours almost always occur in pure form, whereas moderately and poorly differentiated tumours form a spectrum, often with elements of both in an individual neoplasm.

Well-differentiated SLCTs are composed of Sertoli cells in open/hollow or solid/closed tubules, generally without significant nuclear atypia or mitotic activity. Nested and corded and more uncommonly diffuse architecture may also be present. Delicate fibrous stroma contains Leydig cells in clusters, in cords, and singly; the Leydig cells may be eosinophilic or vacuolated/clear and may contain lipofuscin. Moderately differentiated SLCTs typically have a lobular architecture and contain Sertoli cells growing as diffuse sheets, nests, open or solid tubules, or cords, with mild, moderate, or (rarely) bizarre degenerative cytological atypia and modest mitotic activity. Leydig cells are usually most prominent at the periphery of the lobules. Poorly differentiated SLCTs consist of diffuse arrangements of cells resembling primitive gonadal stroma, typically with a minor component of moderately differentiated SLCT. Mitotic figures are conspicuous. Leydig cells are typically sparse but can usually be identified. Moderately and poorly differentiated SLCTs may contain follicular elements closely mimicking juvenile granulosa cell tumour. This is part of the morphological spectrum of SLCT, and these neoplasms should not be diagnosed as gynandroblastoma (mixed SLCT and juvenile granulosa cell tumour) {29660837; 32815828}.

Retiform SLCTs are composed predominantly of anastomosing, slit-like spaces or papillae lined by cuboidal or columnar epithelium, or they may have a multicystic pattern with sieve-like spaces lined by flattened cells {3911780; 22850410; 6731664; 7139538; 7139537; 6660351}. Small retiform areas may also be seen in otherwise typical moderately and poorly differentiated SLCTs, and these neoplasms are best termed “moderately or poorly differentiated SLCTs with retiform elements”.

Moderately and poorly differentiated SLCTs and retiform tumours may contain heterologous elements, either admixed with the sex cord areas or present as discrete areas {3911780; 22850410; 6731664; 7139538; 7139537; 6660351; 35245609}. The heterologous elements may be epithelial or less commonly mesenchymal and are seen in about 20% of tumours. Benign enteric/intestinal mucinous epithelium is most common; however, architectural complexity within the mucinous elements morphologically in keeping with mucinous borderline tumour or mucinous carcinoma may be seen. Rarely, carcinoid tumours occur. Heterologous mesenchymal elements usually consist of fetal-type cartilage and/or skeletal muscle, and areas of rhabdomyosarcoma, usually of embryonal type, may be present. Neuroectodermal elements may be rarely present {35245609}. In pregnancy, these neoplasms may exhibit prominent oedema.

The Sertoli cells are typically positive for pancytokeratins, as well as (to varying degrees) the sex cord markers inhibin, calretinin, SF1, CD56, WT1, and FOXL2 {21378549; 15754297; 12808064; 9416696; 9688187; 12409724; 19033865}. Retiform and poorly differentiated tumours are more likely to be negative. Leydig cells typically show either negative or only minimal staining for FOXL2 and WT1, but they usually express inhibin, calretinin, SF1, CD56, melan-A, and SATB2 (cytoplasmic) {37668387}. EMA is typically negative. Heterologous elements exhibit the immunoprofile of their constituent tissues.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Well-differentiated tumours are consistently negative for DICER1 mutations, and molecular analysis is typically not required for diagnosis since no recurrent molecular events are, as yet, reported {36583307}. However, in cases with a differential diagnosis of well-differentiated and moderately differentiated SLCT, DICER1 mutation analysis is recommended if available. Patients with moderately differentiated, poorly differentiated, or retiform tumours should be referred to clinical genetics for germline testing and counselling.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sex cord stromal tumour consisting of an admixture of Sertoli cell and Leydig cell components.

Desirable: positivity for sex cord immunohistochemical markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with well-differentiated tumours have a survival rate of almost 100%. Moderately and poorly differentiated tumours are clinically malignant in about 10% and 60% of cases, respectively; recurrence usually occurs in the peritoneal cavity within 2 years. Poor prognostic features include advanced stage (most cases are stage I at diagnosis), higher grade, retiform pattern, and heterologous mesenchymal elements, especially rhabdomyosarcoma {3911780; 6660351; 22446621; 38657450}.
