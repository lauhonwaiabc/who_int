2.2.1.7 Basal cell carcinoma

Definition

Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is a carcinoma that arises from epithelial cells of either the epidermis or hair follicle stem cells.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: basal cell epithelioma; basalioma; trichoblastic carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours are most frequently located on the labium majus.

Clinical features

The commonest symptoms are vulvar lump and/or pruritus.

Epidemiology

Vulvar BCCs constitute < 5% of vulvar malignancies and < 1% of all BCCs in different case series. Age at diagnosis ranges from 44 to 99 years {24625418; 33770062}.

Etiology

Not known

Pathogenesis

BCCs at sun-protected sites are characterized by recurrent PTCH1 alterations and upstream hedgehog pathway alterations. They do not show UV mutation signatures seen in BCCs of sun-exposed sites {39807820}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most commonly presents as papule or nodule. It may show ulceration and pigmentation.

Histopathology

BCC is characterized by nests of basaloid tumour cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and scant cytoplasm showing peripheral palisading at the tumour-stromal interface. Stromal alterations include fibromyxoid change, retraction of tumour nests from stroma, calcification, keloidal-type collagenous stroma, and amyloid deposition. Desmoplasia is not seen. Tumour nests may be colonized by melanocytes and contain melanin pigment. Keratinizing BCC tumour nests show central mature keratinization. Nodulocystic or cystic BCC shows cystic degeneration within tumour islands.

BCCs show strong expression of p40/p63, CK5/6, BCL2, BerEP4, and CD10 and are negative for EMA, CEA, and CK20 {29072946}. p16 usually shows patchy staining {36731045}. The diffuse BerEP4 expression and lack of block p16 expression are useful in distinguishing BCC from basaloid squamous cell carcinomas, especially those occurring in the perianal region {24625418; 23599161}. Neuroendocrine markers may be positive {28548901}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nests of basaloid cells with hyperchromatic nuclei, minimal cytoplasm with peripheral palisading.

Staging

There is no specific staging for vulvar BCC.

Prognosis and prediction

Simple wide excision is curative in the majority of cases. Tumour size of > 20 mm, thickness of > 3 mm, and close surgical margins are risk factors for recurrence {33770062}. Distant spread has not been described.
