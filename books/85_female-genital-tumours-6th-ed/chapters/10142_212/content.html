10.1.4.2 Lipoblastoma-like tumour

Definition

Lipoblastoma-like tumour (LLT) is an adipocytic neoplasm that shares some morphological features with lipoblastoma, spindle cell lipoma, and myxoid liposarcoma, but lacks the characteristic genetic alterations seen in those neoplasms.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LLT can occur at essentially any anatomical site {37307874; 37355153}. Within the female genital tract, LLT commonly arises in the vulva and inguinogenital region {37307874}. Rare examples have been reported in the pelvis and retroperitoneum {37355153}.

Clinical features

LLT generally presents as soft tissue masses, swellings, or enlarging nodules that are occasionally associated with pain or tenderness {12047760; 25929353; 29976943; 37307874; 37355153}.

Epidemiology

LLT is an exceptionally rare tumour with only a limited number of cases published in medical literature. It has been described to occur in both sexes {37307874; 37355153} and most commonly arises in adults in their fourth to fifth decades of life {12047760; 25929353; 29976943; 37307874; 37355153}. Only rare examples in children or older adults have been reported {37307874; 37355153}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Recurrent gene rearrangements, fusion events, and mutations have not been observed in LLT. By microarray, LLT has a flat copy-number profile with occasional focal gains and losses; the latter specifically do not involve RB1 {37307874; 37355153}. These tumours do not exhibit DDIT3 or PLAG1 gene rearrangements {25929353; 29976943; 37307874; 37355153}. Neither MDM2 and/or CDK4 gene amplifications nor RB1 regional gains or losses are present, and there is a normal diploid profile in the majority of tumours {29976943; 37307874; 37355153}.

Macroscopic appearance

LLT is a grossly well-defined, multinodular/septated mass with a tan-yellow fatty to variably myxogelatinous cut surface without necrosis {25929353; 37307874; 37355153}.

Histopathology

LLT has a lobulated architecture delineated by variably sized intervening fibrous septa and composed of a mixture of mature adipocytes, univacuolated and bivacuolated lipoblasts, and cytologically bland, normochromatic spindle cells that have blunt-ended to tapered nuclei, indistinct nucleoli, and ample fibrillary cytoplasm. A subset of the spindle cells show lipoblastic differentiation with individual optically clear vacuoles at one or both ends of the nucleus, imparting an ice-cream–cone or hourglass appearance. Lipoblasts of these types vary widely in number from very limited in some tumours (discovered only after careful search) to easily discernible in others. The lipoblasts are often concentrated towards the periphery of the tumour lobules. The background stromal matrix ranges from extensively myxoid and occasionally with pulmonary oedema-like microcystic changes to collagenized with either dense aggregates or wispy fibres. The vascular tree is consistently delicate, branching and thin-walled, resembling the vasculature found in myxoid liposarcoma and lipoblastoma. Some cases contain stromal erythrocyte extravasation and chronic inflammation (mast cells, plasma cells, lymphocytes, eosinophils), which may be so extensive to obscure the basic architecture. Mitotic activity is infrequent and necrosis is absent {12047760; 25929353; 29976943, 37307874; 37355153}.

LLT may have complete loss or a mosaic pattern of weak nuclear expression of RB1. The spindle cells may be positive for CD34. Although rare cases have been reported to have weak expression of PLAG1, DDIT3, MDM2, and CDK4 by immunohistochemistry {37355153}, all tumours are negative for genetic rearrangements involving PLAG1 and DDIT3 and lack gene amplification of MDM2 and CDK4 and lack regional gains or losses involving RB1 {29976943; 37307874; 37355153}.

Cytology

There are no reports on FNAB of lipoblastoma-like tumour.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobulated growth delineated by variably sized septa; admixture of mature adipocytes, optically clear univacuolated and bivacuolated lipoblasts, and bland spindle cells; variably myxoid to collagenous matrix and thin-walled branching vasculature.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours may infrequently locally recur, linked to resection margin involvement {25929353; 37307874; 37355153}. One myxoid tumour reported as lipoblastoma-like tumour metastasized {37355153}. Apart from this one exceptional case, metastatic disease has not been reported in LLT.
