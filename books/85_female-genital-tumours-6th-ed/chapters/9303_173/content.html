9.3.0.3 Embryonal carcinoma

Definition

Embryonal carcinoma is a malignant germ cell tumour composed of primitive large and pleomorphic epithelioid cells recapitulating the early stages of embryonic development.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized to the ovary.

Clinical features

Embryonal carcinoma usually presents with symptoms related to an ovarian mass, including abdominal pain. Endocrine alterations are common, including precocious puberty and menstrual abnormalities {63319}. Serum β-hCG and/or AFP may be elevated {63319}.

Epidemiology

Pure embryonal carcinoma is rare, and an embryonal carcinoma component is much more likely to part of a mixed germ cell tumour {63319}. The pure form occurs in children and young women (average age: 12–15 years) {63319} and rarely in older adults {8898183}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Chromosome 12 abnormalities are common (isochromosome 12p or 12p amplification) {20096442}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are usually unilateral, 160 mm on average, solid, haemorrhagic, and necrotic {63319}.

Histopathology

Embryonal carcinoma is composed of monomorphic to pleomorphic cells growing in solid, nested, glandular, and papillary patterns, which are often admixed. The tumour cells are polygonal, contain abundant amphophilic or clear cytoplasm, and are mitotically active, with hyperchromatic and severely atypical nuclei. Syncytiotrophoblastic giant cells may be present {63319}. Embryonal carcinoma is mostly admixed with other malignant germ cell components as part of a mixed germ cell tumour. Immunohistochemically, embryonal carcinoma is positive for CD30, OCT4 {20096442}, SALL4 {19295406}, SOX2, and LIN28 {22034885}.

Cytology

Smears display markedly atypical and pleomorphic large cells in crowded tissue fragments with high N:C ratio, pleomorphic nuclei, and hyperchromatic chromatin resembling poorly differentiated carcinoma {9034742; 19500123}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: high-grade primitive malignant germ cells arranged in glandular, papillary, and/or solid patterns.

Desirable: positivity for OCT4, CD30, or SOX2.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Limited outcome data are available for pure embryonal carcinoma, because it is an exceedingly rare tumour. A study of 15 cases predating contemporary immunohistochemistry reported a 39% survival rate {63319}.
