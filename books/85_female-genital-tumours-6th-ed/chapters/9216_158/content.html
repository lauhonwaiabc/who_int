9.2.1.6 Signet-ring stromal tumour

Definition

Signet ring stromal tumour is a benign pure stromal tumour composed of cells with signet-ring morphology in a fibromatous stroma.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with symptoms attributable to an ovarian mass, or the tumour may be incidentally discovered on imaging. Hormonal manifestations are usually absent.

Epidemiology

Signet ring stromal tumour is exceedingly rare, with cases reported to occur at a mean age of 49 years (range: 21–83 years) {7483017; 14668550; 20664453; 30676431; 36040033}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Signet ring stromal tumour is usually unilateral, but occasional bilateral tumours have been reported {30676431; 20664453}. They have a mean size of 130 mm (range: 30–200 mm), with a solid tan-white cut surface {36040033}.

Histopathology

Variable quantities of signet-ring cells are set in a fibromatous stroma, which resembles fibroma or cellular fibroma {7483017; 14668550}. Signet-ring cells have a single large cytoplasmic vacuole that does not contain mucin, glycogen, or lipid. Nuclei are eccentrically located, small, uniform, and bland. Small nucleoli and occasional nuclear grooves may be present. Mitoses are rare or absent. Cells with distinctive eosinophilic hyaline globules may be seen; ultrastructural studies of these globules suggest a lysosomal or erythrocytic origin {18807143; 7483017}.

It has been suggested that most tumours with signet-ring cells with β-catenin and cyclin D1 nuclear positivity are better classified as microcystic stromal tumour; thus, the immunohistochemical profile of signet ring stromal tumour is not well established {36040033}. Based on limited data, most are positive for SF1, CD10, and WT1, and variably express inhibin, calretinin, and cytokeratins. EMA is consistently negative {36040033}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: signet-ring cells in a fibromatous stroma EMA negative.

Desirable: positivity with sex cord markers; negative staining with cyclin D1 and no nuclear staining with β-catenin; EMA negative; absent intracytoplasmic mucin.

Staging

These are benign neoplasms and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Signet ring stromal tumours are benign {36040033}.
