2.2.2.3 Mixed tumour

Definition

Mixed tumour is a benign sweat gland tumour with an admixture of epithelial, myoepithelial, and stromal elements.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: pleomorphic adenoma; mixed tumour of the vulva

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumour can occur on the vulva, but it is rare in this location {16681787}.

Clinical features

Mixed tumours are solitary (rarely multiple), well-circumscribed, slow-growing nodules with a smooth surface. They are typically 5–30 mm but can be as large as 120 mm. Tumours with an osteocartilaginous component may be firm {17707152; 21697702}.

Epidemiology

These infrequently occur on the vulva. Mixed tumours are most common in middle-aged men (median: 53.5 years); the reported age range is 10–96 years {17707152; 22532914}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

PLAG1 and HMGA2 fusions are found in apocrine-mixed tumours {22038920; 32345665}. PLAG1 activation through gene fusion typically occurs due to promoter swapping from various fusion partner genes that are ubiquitously expressed, such as CTNNB1, LIFR, and CHCHD7 {38266920}. The eccrine pattern lacks the PLAG1 gene rearrangement {30839344}; internal tandem duplications (ITDs) of SOX10 (SOX10-ITD) have been reported in this pattern {38266920}.

Macroscopic appearance

Mixed tumour is typically a well-defined, round to ovoid mass < 30 mm in diameter, with a solid or solid and cystic, white, glistening cut surface.

Histopathology

Apocrine-mixed tumours demonstrate considerable variations in their epithelial, myoepithelial, and stromal components and are usually characterized by branching tubular structures, often manifesting apocrine secretion, embedded in a chondromyxoid cartilaginous or osseous stroma. Additional follicular and/or sebaceous differentiation can be seen. Prominent myoepithelial differentiation results in hyaline cell–rich tumours.

In contrast to its apocrine counterpart, eccrine-mixed tumours do not display signs of decapitation secretion, sebaceous, or follicular differentiation. Their epithelial component is composed of simple monolayered tubular elements set in a myxohyaline and cartilaginous stroma. Small epithelial nests, strands, and cords or even single-cell units can be seen. Unusual findings include cribriform areas, clear cell change, hyaline cells, pseudorosette-like structures, prominent osseous metaplasia, and striking intracytoplasmic vacuolization of the neoplastic cells resulting in physaliphorous-like cells {17592277; 21697702}.

The panel of antibodies to highlight its multiple cell populations includes SOX10, p63, S100, calponin, SMA, EMA, KIT, CEA, and various cytokeratins {10408348}. A more specific marker is PLAG1 (clone 3B7), which is overexpressed in the majority of the apocrine pattern, while rare cases show HMGA2 expression {30839344; 32345665}. TRPS1 immunoreactivity was also observed {37649299}.

Cytology

The cytopathology is similar to that of the corresponding salivary gland tumours {26619021}.

FNAB smears show fibrillary chondromyxoid material, with a prominent cellular component composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells {20391975; 26800260; 28772003}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed; admixture of epithelial, myoepithelial, and stromal elements.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign neoplasms that grow slowly, but over the course of years they can reach several centimetres in size. The tumour may recur if incompletely excised. Rare cases of adenocarcinoma arising in mixed tumour of the vulva have been reported {14501823}.
