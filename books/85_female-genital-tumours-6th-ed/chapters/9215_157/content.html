9.2.1.5 Microcystic stromal tumour

Definition

Microcystic stromal tumour is an ovarian tumour of stromal origin characterized by distinctive solid and microcystic morphology.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Some patients present with mass-related symptoms, while others are asymptomatic and diagnosed incidentally on imaging studies. Hormonal manifestations are usually absent {18971779}.

Epidemiology

Age at presentation of this rare tumour varies (mean age: 45 years, range: 23–71 years) {25820106; 29076875; 27015438}.

Etiology

These tumours may rarely occur as an extracolonic manifestation of familial adenomatous polyposis {25820106; 29076875; 27015438}.

Pathogenesis

These are presumed to arise from ovarian stromal cells, although the histogenesis is not firmly established. Almost all of these neoplasms exhibit CTNNB1 or APC mutations, which are mutually exclusive. Mutations in CTNNB1 and APC result in aberrant nuclear immunoreactivity for β-catenin and diffuse nuclear cyclin D1 staining through activation of the WNT/β-catenin signalling pathway. Most tumours exhibit either a point mutation in CTNNB1 (encoding β-catenin) or, less frequently, a mutation in APC {29076875; 26200099; 21881488; 25913412}. Microcystic stromal tumour may be a sentinel event in the setting of familial adenomatous polyposis; thus, detection of APC mutations in the tumour may be useful in identifying patients with this syndrome, and referral for germline testing is advised {35020947}. Mutations in FOXL2 and DICER1 are absent {27830327}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are typically unilateral, with a mean size of about 90 mm. Bilateral ovarian involvement is rare but occasionally occurs in patients with familial adenomatous polyposis {34282105}. The ovarian surface is smooth and on cut section usually predominantly solid, firm, and tan or tan-white, sometimes with cystic or haemorrhagic foci {18971779}.

Histopathology

These are well-circumscribed but unencapsulated tumours. Most tumours exhibit a classic triad of solid cellular zones, microcystic spaces, and fibrous septa, which vary in proportion {18971779}. Microcysts are composed of small rounded to oval cystic spaces, sometimes in areas coalescing to larger irregular channels. The microcystic areas and solid cellular areas are well demarcated and intersected by fibrocollagenous stromal septa, sometimes with hyaline plaques. Infrequently, tumours are focally, predominantly, or almost exclusively composed of diffuse, corded, and nested arrangements, with minimal or absent microcysts {30325056}. The cells are round to epithelioid, containing a moderate amount of finely granular, lightly eosinophilic cytoplasm, with generally bland, round to oval or short spindle-shaped nuclei with small indistinct nucleoli. Intracytoplasmic vacuoles are frequently seen, sometimes imparting a signet-ring morphology. Bizarre nuclei with a symplastic appearance are sometimes present. Mitotic activity is low. The tumour cells are usually diffusely positive for β-catenin (nuclear and cytoplasmic), CD10, WT1, FOXL2, cyclin D1, and SF1, while inhibin, calretinin, CD56, and EMA are typically negative {18971779; 26200099; 24684527}. Tumours may be positive for AR but are usually negative for ER and PR {30325056}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of a CTNNB1 or APC mutation may be diagnostically useful in challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ovarian neoplasm with variable solid and microcystic architecture with fibrous septa and a uniform ovoid cell population.

Desirable: diffuse SF1, nuclear β-catenin, CD10, and cyclin D1 immunoreactivity; lack of expression of inhibin and calretinin.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most reported tumours have been benign on follow-up. However, occasional cases have recurred or metastasized within the pelvis or abdomen {29458068; 35699785; 36044304}. The features predicting malignant behaviour are so far not defined.
