2.2.2.6 Adenocarcinoma of mammary gland type

Definition

Adenocarcinoma of mammary-gland type is a primary vulvar tumour showing histopathological features identical to those of breast carcinomas with glandular differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: adenocarcinoma of anogenital mammary-like glands [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Skin tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 12). https://publications.iarc.who.int/650.]].

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common location is the labia majora.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a solitary vulvar mass. Reports of synchronous carcinomas involving the vulva and breast have prompted consideration of an underlying cancer predisposition syndrome; however, no definitive syndromic association has been confirmed to date {187318; 16515639}.

Epidemiology

These are rare vulvar neoplasms, typically occurring in multiparous postmenopausal women (median age: 62 years) {16625074; 33489145}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Adenocarcinoma of mammary-gland type is thought to originate from anogenital mammary-like glands {27759697; 16625074}. However, its uncommon association with these glands, along with reports of concurrent Paget disease, raises the possibility that, in some cases, the carcinoma may instead arise from Paget disease {34570018}. They show molecular similarity to breast carcinomas and can be separated into breast molecular subtypes: luminal A, luminal B, ERBB2 (HER2)-enriched, and basal-like, using immunohistochemistry or gene expression {21132401; 28418164; 28877932}. Mutations in the genes of the PI3K/AKT cascade have been found in a few studied cases {28291131}. Secretory carcinoma harbours ETV6::NTRK3 fusion {29672325; 33290355}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is that of a solitary nodule with a fibrous cut surface, similar to breast carcinomas, with a mean size of 3.2 cm (range: 1–20 cm) {32097114}.

Histopathology

Most tumours are identical to mammary invasive ductal carcinoma, but in situ component or adjacent anogenital mammary-like glands are uncommonly identifiable. Rare types include lobular, tubulolobular, mixed ductal and lobular, secretory carcinomas {16625074; 16931966; 20505503; 33489145}. Occasional examples of mucinous and adenoid cystic-like carcinoma have been reported {12296762; 15897743, 21169735}. The immunohistochemical profile typically aligns with what is expected from the analogous breast carcinoma, including a similar frequency of expression for ER, PR, ERBB2 (HER2), EGFR, and CK5 {28418164}. The lesion must be dermally centred, and there should not be extensive overlying epidermal involvement – which would instead be classified as invasive Paget disease. The presence of associated in situ component or proximity to anogenital mammary-like glands is helpful but not necessary for diagnosis.

Cytology

The cytopathology features for adenocarcinoma of mammary-gland type have not yet been documented in the cytopathology literature.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dermally centred invasive adenocarcinoma with no or focal epidermal involvement.

The histopathological features and biomarker expression profiles are identical to those of invasive breast carcinoma.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Adenocarcinomas of mammary-gland type are locally aggressive tumours that often metastasize to regional lymph nodes. Distant metastases are relatively uncommon.
