7.2.1.1 Serous adenofibroma and serous papilloma

Definition

Serous adenofibroma is a benign biphasic tumour composed of fibromatous stroma with papillae or cysts lined by tubal-type epithelium. Serous papilloma is a benign papillary tumour lined by the same epithelium.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fallopian tube

Clinical features

Most women with these tumours are asymptomatic, and the majority of cases are an incidental finding at the time of surgery for another gynaecological disorder. Adenofibromas are mainly located in the fimbria {9421082}. Papillomas may cause tubal obstruction or occur without symptoms {3176920; 3721698} and are loosely attached to the tubal mucosa in the lumen.

Epidemiology

These are rare entities occurring in women of reproductive age (24–45 years) {39877487}. The reported frequency of fallopian tube adenofibromas in salpingectomies ranges from about 2% to 30% {31972564; 18580316; 26630221; 24820399; 28319576}. Tubal papillomas are exceedingly rare tumours primarily described in case reports {39877487}.

Etiology

There are conflicting data as to whether these lesions are more or less common in individuals with BRCA1/2 mutations or a strong family history of breast/ovarian carcinoma than in women who are not at high risk {26630221; 18580316}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis for tubal adenofibromas and papillomas is unknown. A BRAF (c.1799T>A) mutation (Val600Glu) has been identified in one papilloma case {29851866}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most adenofibromas are < 10 mm and many are microscopic; uncommon features include multicentricity and bilaterality {18580316}. Papillomas usually appear as an intraluminal friable tissue < 30 mm in diameter {24442176}.

Histopathology

Adenofibromas have admixed epithelial and mesenchymal components and may be cystic. The dense fibromatous stromal component can be nodular or have papillary growth. Tubal-type epithelium (mixture of secretory and ciliated cells) covers the surface of the papillae or lines cystic spaces. Papillomas are composed of delicate, branching fibrovascular stalks lined by bland cuboidal to columnar epithelial cells. These two tumours can show overlapping morphological features that may result in interobserver variation in separating the entities. However, the distinction is of no clinical significance.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

Adenofibroma: benign, biphasic tumour composed of bland tubal-type epithelium and fibromatous stroma.

Papilloma: benign, delicate papillary proliferation attached to the tubal mucosa.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Both adenofibroma and papilloma have a benign clinical course.
