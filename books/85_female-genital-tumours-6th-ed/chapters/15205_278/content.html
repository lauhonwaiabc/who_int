15.2.0.5 Dysplastic melanocytic naevus

Definition

Dysplastic melanocytic naevus is an acquired naevus in the genital area, with architectural and cytological atypia.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: naevus with architectural and cytological atypia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Dysplastic melanocytic naevi are usually on the cutaneous labia majora and less commonly on the perineum. Mucosa is not involved {1923241; 18976416}.

Clinical features

Dysplastic melanocytic naevi share many clinical and epidemiological features with atypical genital naevi. They are often irregularly bordered macules or papules > 6 mm with central pink tones in a background of tan to brown at the periphery {20868400}. Asymmetry is often seen. In patients with dysplastic naevus syndrome, numerous dysplastic naevi can be observed in sun-protected sites, such as the anogenital skin, as well as in sun-exposed sites {1923241; 20868400} [[Moran JMT, Hartsough EM, Hoang MP. Vulvovaginal Melanocytic Lesions: A Review. Diagnostic Histopathology 2024;30:15–24.]].

Epidemiology

Dysplastic melanocytic naevi are uncommon. They occur most frequently in women of reproductive age (average age: 32 years) {28778635; 18976416}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown, except for uncommon cases associated with dysplastic naevus syndrome.

Pathogenesis

Dysplastic melanocytic naevi, like other acquired melanocytic naevi, are associated with activating mutations, most often in BRAF and NRAS. Genital dysplastic naevi are less studied.

Macroscopic appearance

Dysplastic melanocytic naevus is a macular or papular lesion with variable pigmentation.

Histopathology

Dysplastic melanocytic naevi are characterized histologically by the presence of architectural disorder and cytological atypia. Architectural disorder manifests as bridging of junctional nests between adjacent rete ridges and variation in the size, shape, and position of junctional nests. A lentiginous junctional component, of variable density, is frequently present at the periphery of the lesion and extends beyond the dermal component (the so-called shoulder phenomenon). There is usually associated concentric eosinophilic fibroplasia in the papillary dermis near the dermo-epidermal junction. Significant pagetoid scatter is not a feature, particularly in the periphery. Randomly distributed mild to severe cytological atypia can be seen in the melanocytes {18976416}. The dermal component, when present, is composed of bland melanocytes in the underlying dermis. A lymphohistiocytic inflammatory reaction is often present in the superficial dermis accompanying the lesion. There is often histological overlap between dysplastic melanocytic naevus and atypical genital melanocytic naevus. However, elongation of the rete ridges, a significant lentiginous component predominating over the nested pattern, a well-developed shoulder, randomly distributed melanocytic atypia instead of uniform atypia, marked inflammatory reaction, and concentric, eosinophilic fibroplasia rather than the broad zones of superficial dermal fibrosis are features that may help distinguish dysplastic melanocytic naevi from atypical genital melanocytic naevi. Atypical melanocytic naevus is more common in children and younger women. Mucosal melanomas are more common in older adult women, are larger, may be multifocal, and are characterized by confluent proliferation of junctional melanocytes with irregular and prominent pagetoid distribution, severe cytological atypia, and frequent ulceration. The dermal melanocytes of melanoma lack maturation with descent and are mitotically active with atypical and deep mitotic figures.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a benign melanocytic neoplasm with a significant lentiginous component; a well-developed shoulder; randomly distributed melanocytic atypia; a marked inflammatory reaction.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Dysplastic melanocytic naevus is a benign lesion that may regress with time. Dysplastic melanocytic naevus with severe atypia may be a precursor of melanoma, and some authors recommend excision of dysplastic melanocytic naevus with narrow but histologically clear margins {28778635}.
