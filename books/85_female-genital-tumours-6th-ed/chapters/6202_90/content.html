6.2.0.2 STK11 adnexal tumour

Definition

STK11 adnexal tumour is a neoplasm of uncertain histogenesis usually arising in the paratubal soft tissue, with multiple growth patterns and STK11 genomic alterations.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most arise in the paratubal/para-ovarian soft tissues {33534223; 39169716}. Rare examples originate in the ovary, fallopian tube, uterine serosa, or abdominopelvic peritoneum.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with symptoms related to a pelvic mass; 10–15% are incidental {33534223; 39169716}.

Epidemiology

These are rare neoplasms. Patients present over a wide age range (16–74 years; median: 40 years) {33534223; 39169716}.

Etiology

Approximately 30% occur in association with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome {33534223; 38919071; 39169716; 39801168}. For more comprehensive information on Peutz–Jeghers syndrome, refer to section Peutz–Jeghers syndrome (STK11).

Pathogenesis

The cell of origin of STK11 adnexal tumour is unknown. These tumours are defined by STK11 loss-of-function genomic alterations, including point mutations, indels, structural rearrangements, and deletions {33534223; 39169716}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range from 40 to 225 mm, with a median size of 110 mm {33534223; 39169716}. They are usually solid or solid and cystic, with frequent haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

STK11 adnexal tumours exhibit a spectrum of histological patterns, with the most common being interanastomosing cords and trabeculae {33534223; 39169716}. Other patterns include cribriform, tubular, cystic, acinar, papillary, nested, and solid. The stroma is often myxoid, but it may also be oedematous, hyalinized, or loosely collagenous. Cells are usually columnar to cuboidal and uniformly atypical with round to ovoid nuclei, vesicular chromatin, prominent nucleoli, and eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm; overt pleomorphism is absent. Spindled cells can be observed but are rarely dominant. Signet ring–like cells or whorling of tumour cells is occasionally seen. Mitoses are variable and necrosis is uncommon.

STK11 shows complete loss of cytoplasmic staining {39169716}. The immunohistochemical profile is otherwise nonspecific, with variable expression of keratins, sex cord markers, mesothelial markers, hormone receptors, CD10, and desmin {33534223; 38919071; 39169716}. PAX8, EMA, GATA3, and SF1 may show limited expression. Claudin-4, FOXL2, and TTF1 are negative. p53 is wild type.

Although STK11 adnexal tumour and Wolffian tumour may exhibit overlapping morphological and immunohistochemical features, they should be distinguished from each other due to their contrasting prognosis and hereditary syndrome risk.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing with an assay covering the entire coding region of STK11 and assessing for copy-number alterations is helpful for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multiple histological patterns including interanastomosing cords and trabeculae; myxoid, oedematous, or hyalinized stroma; STK11 immunohistochemical loss and/or STK11 genomic alterations if not Peutz–Jeghers related.

Staging

A staging system has not been established.

Prognosis and prediction

Most tumours are aggressive, with metastatic disease at diagnosis in 50–60% and recurrences in 80% {33534223; 39169716}. Disease-related death occurs in approximately 50%, but follow-up is limited {40262437}. Prognostic features have not been identified.
