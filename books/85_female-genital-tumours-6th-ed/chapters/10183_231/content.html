10.1.8.3 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a malignant spindle cell tumour with the histological and immunohistochemical features suggesting Schwannian differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant schwannoma; neurofibrosarcoma; neurogenic sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Primary MPNST in the female genital tract is extremely rare. According to previously reported cases, the uterine cervix is the most commonly affected location {39848787}, followed by the vulvovaginal region {37844578}, and uterine corpus {26881581; 39508369}. A few cases have been reported in the round ligament {38143968} or ovary {37151162}.

Clinical features

MPNSTs in the female genital tract are typically seen in patients aged 20–70 years; rarely do they arise in children. Although MPNST in soft tissue is often associated with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1), patients with tumours in the female genital tract usually show no history of NF1. Patients typically present with an enlarging mass or abnormal uterine bleeding {26881581}. The size of MPNSTs in uterine corpus (40–190 mm) {26881581; 38143968; 39508369} is larger than that of uterine cervix (10–90 mm) {39848787} or vulvovaginal region (25–60 mm) {37844578}. There are no specific imaging characteristics that distinguish MPNST from other sarcomas.

Epidemiology

MPNST is a rare sarcoma in the female genital tract, accounting for approximately 3% of cervical sarcomas {16981999}. Currently, < 30 cases of cervical MPNSTs have been reported in the literature and only little is known about the epidemiology of these tumours. Reports describe MPNSTs as more common in middle-aged women, and the reported age ranges from 21 to 73 years, with a median age of 47 years and a mean age of 45.3 years. It has also been reported that 40–50% of patients with MPNSTs overall have NF1 {38192675; 38881559}.

Etiology

MPNST may be associated with NF1 or irradiation.

Pathogenesis

Conventional MPNSTs have complex karyotypes. Despite different clinical presentations (e.g. NF1-associated, sporadic de novo, or radiotherapy-associated), recent genomic studies of MPNSTs demonstrated highly frequent and concurrent inactivating mutations in three pathways: NF1, CDKN2A/CDKN2B, and PRC2 core components (EED or SUZ12), resulting in complete loss of function {25240281; 25119042; 25305755; 30722027}. About 80% of all high-grade MPNSTs exhibit complete loss of PRC2 activity through loss of the PRC2 core component (EED or SUZ12) and complete loss of H3K27me3 expression {26645727; 25240281; 26585554}.

Macroscopic appearance

MPNSTs are usually > 50 mm at the time of diagnosis and have a tan-white, fleshy cut surface, often with areas of haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

Tumours are typically composed of fascicles of spindle-shaped cells, often with a haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern, and alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas (tapestry appearance). There are often areas of geographical necrosis and conspicuous mitotic figures. The cells are spindle or serpentine in shape, with hyperchromatic nuclei and pale wavy cytoplasm. MPNSTs may occasionally show extensive pleomorphism, simulating an undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. Perivascular accentuation of tumour cells may be seen; in those areas, the cells frequently become plumper. Heterologous differentiation, such as skeletal muscle, bone, cartilage, and blood vessels, is seen in about 15% of tumours. A malignant triton tumour is an MPNST with skeletal muscle differentiation.

Immunohistochemically, MPNSTs may be positive for S100 (< 50% of tumours), SOX10 (< 70%), and GFAP (20–30%) {23929265}. Importantly, staining for S100 and SOX10 is patchy or focal. Diffuse staining for S100 or SOX10 is not usually compatible with the diagnosis of conventional MPNST. Complete loss of staining for H3K27me3 may be helpful in the diagnosis of MPNST, with high-grade tumours (and radiation-induced MPNST) showing more-frequent loss than low-grade tumours {28776579; 26645727; 26585554; 29482987}. The heterologous components (e.g. skeletal muscle differentiation or angiosarcomatous areas) stain for appropriate markers.

Cytology

Hypercellular smears show large syncytial tissue fragments with vague fascicular growth, as well as single intact cells, which are mostly of uniform size and shape, with ovoid or buckled, hyperchromatic nuclei with evenly dispersed chromatin and small or inconspicuous nucleoli, and sparse to more abundant elongated cytoplasm with tapering ends. Necrosis and stripped nuclei are frequently seen in the background. Other variable cytopathological features are a fibrillary myxoid stroma, marked pleomorphism in high-grade tumours, and heterologous rhabdomyoblastic cells. Epithelioid MPNSTs show large epithelioid cells, often in tissue fragments with myxoid or fibrillary extracellular matrix {22434579}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cell sarcoma with Schwann cell differentiation (S100/SOX10 focal positivity) and/or loss of H3K27me3 expression.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

MPNST is an aggressive tumour with a poor prognosis. Tumour size > 50 mm, local recurrence, and high-grade morphology are all adverse prognostic factors {27158754}. Malignant triton tumours are particularly aggressive {23084090}.
