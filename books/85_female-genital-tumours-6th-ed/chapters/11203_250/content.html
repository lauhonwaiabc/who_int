11.2.0.3 Mesothelioma

Definition

Mesothelioma is a malignant tumour arising from the mesothelial lining of the visceral organs, peritoneal cavity, and other body cavities.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: malignant mesothelioma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid mesothelioma (90520/3); sarcomatoid mesothelioma (90510/3); biphasic mesothelioma(90530/3); mesothelioma in situ (90500/2)

Localization

Mesotheliomas can arise throughout the peritoneal cavity and are almost always multifocal or disseminated at presentation. Approximately 1% are unifocal on clinical, radiographic, and pathological examination (including negative peritoneal fluid cytology) and are diagnosed as localized mesothelioma {31485011; 35713639}. Synchronous or metachronous coinvolvement of pleural and peritoneal cavities can occur {31065962}.

Clinical features

Peritoneal mesothelioma can present with nonspecific pain, distension, or gastrointestinal complaints, or as an incidental finding. Ascites is present in most patients. It can present with an ovarian mass or lymphadenopathy (mimicking carcinoma), acute cholecystitis, acute appendicitis, incarcerated hernia, or in association with endometriosis, Crohn disease, or longstanding ventriculoperitoneal shunt {27484913; 32826527; 33577225; 12548173; 8764743; 2389813}.

Epidemiology

Peritoneal mesothelioma represents 15–20% of all mesotheliomas, with an annual incidence of 0.07–0.16 cases per 100 000 person-years in European cohorts {29080447; 30953970; 35149582; 31876584}. It occurs roughly equally in men and women and is diagnosed from childhood to old age, with a peak in the sixth and seventh decades of life {33049597; 32277799; 33060816; 32769428}. Tumours with ALK fusions tend to occur in childhood or young adulthood {28910456; 33399341; 39059063}.

Etiology

Asbestos is a recognized cause of mesothelioma, including peritoneal mesothelioma {31190990; 19635740; 23189751}.

Pathogenesis

Ten to fifteen percent of peritoneal mesotheliomas are associated with BAP1 germline mutation {21874000; 30113886; 36773602}. Biallelic somatic mutation or homozygous deletion of BAP1 occurs in 40–80%, with less frequent recurrent alterations including CDKN2A homozygous deletion, mutations and deletions of NF2, and mutations of SETD2, PBRM1, DDX3X, and TP53 {25889843; 26493618; 27813512; 28034829; 35704798; 34648949; 36138075; 36773602}. A minor subset (~3–8%) shows genomic near-haploidization, characterized by loss of one copy of most chromosomes; low rates of BAP1, SETD2, and PBRM1 mutation; and frequent SETDB1, NF2, and TP53 or VHL inactivation {30322867; 35704798; 36515470}. Rare tumours harbour ALK fusions, which appear to be mutually exclusive with BAP1, CDKN2A, NF2, and TP53 alterations {28910456; 33399341; 39059063; 35704798; 36138075}. Fusions between a FET RNA-binding protein family gene (EWSR1 or FUS) and CREB family transcription factors (ATF1, CREB1, or CREM), and also rarely EWSR1::YY1 fusion, have been reported in peritoneal tumours, a subset of which have morphology and immunophenotype compatible with mesothelioma, although these EWSR1 fusion-driven tumours as a group show variable expression of mesothelial immunomarkers, and their proper classification is not conclusively resolved {39059063; 34049318; 32770123; 35665561}. Peritoneal tumours with EWSR1::NR4A3 or other NR4A3 fusions and pure adenomatoid-microcystic morphology may represent a low-grade mesothelioma subtype {36524687; 39965984}.

Macroscopic appearance

Nodules and plaques variably involve visceral and parietal peritoneum {8764743}.

Histopathology

Mesothelioma is diagnosed on the basis of invasive or tumefactive growth, following confirmation of mesothelial lineage and exclusion of reactive or benign mesothelial proliferations. Mesothelioma is classified morphologically into epithelioid, biphasic, and sarcomatoid subtypes.

Epithelioid mesothelioma

Eighty-five to ninety percent of peritoneal mesotheliomas are epithelioid, defined by polygonal, cuboidal, or low columnar cells with generally moderate cytoplasm {23846294; 25393250; 33060816}. Mitotic counts are usually low (median: 1; range: 0–11 per mm2) {32769428}. The most common architectural patterns are tubulopapillary, solid, trabecular, adenomatoid, and micropapillary. Multiple patterns are often admixed {15981812}. Tubules and papillae are usually lined by one to two cell layers, with minimal stratification or budding. Surface papillary change may mimic well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour. To guide management, documentation of each tumour’s architectural pattern(s) is recommended, along with the percent contribution of each pattern to the nearest 10%.

Rare cytomorphological patterns include deciduoid (polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and sharp cell borders), rhabdoid (peripheral displacement of the nucleus by an eosinophilic inclusion), signet-ring (indentation and peripheral displacement of the nucleus by a clear to palely basophilic cytoplasmic vacuole), and clear cell (optically clear or foamy cytoplasm; sometimes associated with VHL mutations and genomic near-haploidization) {16400322; 22684220; 23041828; 15948112; 36515470}. Pleomorphic mesothelioma (defined by anaplastic nuclei in > 10% of tumour cells) and lymphohistiocytoid mesothelioma (defined by syncytial tumour cells resembling histiocytes, in a dense inflammatory background) are rare in the peritoneum and can be classified as epithelioid or sarcomatoid, depending on tumour cytomorphology {22388762; 23846294}. Small cell features, as described in pleural mesothelioma, are not well attested in the peritoneum {22222641}.

Mesotheliomas with ALK fusions show morphology indistinguishable from epithelioid meosthelioma of more conventional pathogenesis {28910456; 33399341}. Peritoneal tumours with EWSR1::NR4A3 and other NR4A3 fusions are mostly well circumscribed and non-invasive, with pure adenomatoid-microcystic architecture and bland cytomorphology {36524687; 39965984}.

Biphasic and sarcomatoid mesothelioma

Five to ten percent of peritoneal mesotheliomas are biphasic, defined by admixed epithelioid and sarcomatoid morphology, each accounting for ≥ 10% of the tumour. Just 1–4% show pure sarcomatoid morphology, defined by spindled growth {33060816; 26371785; 31620941). Desmoplastic morphology and heterologous differentiation are rare {26371785}. The transitional pattern of sarcomatoid mesothelioma is not yet characterized in the peritoneum {32165206}.

Mesothelioma in situ (MIS)

MIS is defined by a flat to papillary monolayer of bland or atypical mesothelial cells, harbouring a pathogenic alteration in BAP1 or (more rarely) CDKN2A, detected by immunohistochemical or molecular methods {31375770; 32932212; 38084880; 36876759}. Stromal invasion and clinical or radiographic mass formation are absent. Careful correlation with surgical and radiological findings is required. Immunohistochemical or molecular studies to exclude mesothelioma in situ are recommended on tissue from patients with an increased risk of mesothelioma, recurrent unexplained ascites, mesothelial atypia, or diffuse papillary abnormalities of the peritoneum {36876759; 38084880}.

Localized mesothelioma

Localized mesothelioma is microscopically indistinguishable from diffuse mesothelioma, including a spectrum of epithelioid architectural patterns and instances of biphasic or sarcomatoid morphology {31485011; 35713639}.

Ancillary testing

Mesothelioma is positive for broad-spectrum cytokeratins. Calretinin, WT1, CK5/6, and D2-40 are each positive in > 80% of epithelioid mesotheliomas, although the individual markers’ sensitivities fall to 30–60% for biphasic and sarcomatoid mesothelioma {29048219; 23196794}. Mesothelioma is negative for claudin-4. Patchy staining for MOC31 and BerEP4 is seen in 5–15% {23596113; 16056246; 11895489; 17667535; 35512220}. PAX8 and SOX17 are positive in approximately 15% {28877056; 29241740; 39479966; 40329501}. ER and PR are positive in 0–2% and 0–7%, respectively {29048219; 17667535; 16260268}. Loss of nuclear BAP1 is seen in 60–70% {27813512; 29076876; 28034829; 35512220; 34648949}. Loss of cytoplasmic MTAP (an immunohistochemical surrogate for CDKN2A deletion) is seen in 20% {38812360; 35512220}. Both BAP1 loss and MTAP loss are specific to mesothelioma in the differential with benign mesothelial proliferations, but only BAP1 loss is specific to mesothelioma in the differential with serous carcinoma {30171198; 27067777; 35512220}.

Cytology

The cytopathological features of mesothelioma are characterized by 3D fragments of medium to large cells showing loss of polarity, absent fibrovascular cores, cell-in-cell arrangements, and nuclear atypia with high N:C ratio, macronucleoli, and irregular nuclear membranes {17709315; 26681974}. Individual malignant cells show similar features. Scattered orangeophilic cells are often present. Diagnostic immunohistochemical and molecular studies, if properly validated, can be performed on cell blocks to confirm the diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Pathogenic alterations of BAP1, CDKN2A, NF2 and TP53 are specific to mesothelioma in the differential with reactive or benign mesothelial proliferations. BAP1 alterations are additionally specific to mesothelioma in the differential with serous carcinoma {27067777; 35512220}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mesothelial proliferation showing invasion, mass formation with complex architecture, or extensive florid involvement of peritoneal surfaces; immunohistochemical confirmation of mesothelial differentiation; exclusion of non-malignant mass-forming mesothelial proliferations.

Desirable: characteristic molecular alterations by molecular or immunohistochemical techniques.

Staging

No TNM staging system has been adopted in major guidelines, and the peritoneal cancer index (PCI) is usually used to quantify disease extent {21509762; 29423664}.

Prognosis and prediction

Peritoneal mesothelioma has a more favourable prognosis than pleural mesothelioma. In a modern treatment context, median survival is 39 months for epithelioid and 14 months for biphasic tumours {33060816}. Favourable prognostic factors include young age, complete cytoreduction, epithelioid subtype, lower histological grade, and BAP1 germline mutation, whereas extraperitoneal spread, lymph node metastasis, predominantly solid growth (in epithelioid tumours), pleomorphic and rhabdoid cytomorphology, sarcomatoid morphology, and CDKN2A homozygous deletion are prognostically adverse {17039392; 17076527; 25393250; 31190990; 23489943; 25380601; 26202904; 26272336; 26493618; 33060816}.

Peritoneal epithelioid mesothelioma is graded on the basis of nuclear atypia and mitotic counts (see table) {21983936; 33060816}. Histological grade is prognostic and may influence management, and routine reporting is recommended. Unlike in pleural mesothelioma, necrosis has not been shown to be an independent prognosticator in peritoneal mesothelioma, limiting application of the two-tiered pleural mesothelioma grading system in the peritoneum {37872123}. Biphasic and sarcomatoid mesotheliomas are not graded.

Mesothelioma in situ has an approximately 30–70% rate of progression to mesothelioma, often years after diagnosis {31375770; 38084880; 36876759}. Approximately half of localized mesotheliomas recur following resection {35713639}.
