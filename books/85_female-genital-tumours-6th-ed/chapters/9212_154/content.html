9.2.1.2 Thecoma

Definition

Thecoma is a pure ovarian stromal tumour composed predominantly of cells resembling theca cells; sometimes a minor population of fibromatous cells is present.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: fibrothecoma.

 

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Almost all cases are unilateral, with only about 3% being bilateral. Patients may present with hormonal manifestations (estrogenic or less commonly androgenic) or with symptoms related to an adnexal mass. A significant percentage are associated with endometrial proliferative lesions {25025365}.

Epidemiology

Thecomas are uncommon, accounting for a small proportion of all sex cord stromal tumours. Population-based estimates are not available, but case series report that they occur over a wide age range (16–81 with an average of 50 years in the largest series) {25025365}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

No recurrent molecular abnormalities have been reported, although there has been limited study of this {39492459}. FOXL2 mutations have been demonstrated in occasional cases, but these may represent misclassified adult granulosa cell tumours {19516027; 23348906}.

Macroscopic appearance

Thecomas are usually 50–100 mm in diameter. In the largest reported series, only 7% measured > 100 mm. The cut surface is typically solid, occasionally lobulated, and yellowish-tan, but it may be focally white. Some cases contain cystic, haemorrhagic, or necrotic areas {25025365}.

Histopathology

Thecomas typically exhibit a diffuse (or less often lobulated/nested) growth of uniform cells with appreciable pale-grey cytoplasm and indistinct cell membranes, resulting in a syncytial appearance. Less commonly, the tumour cells have lipid-rich clear cytoplasm. The tumour cell nuclei are predominantly ovoid to round, sometimes with small nucleoli. Nuclear grooves can be seen but are only rarely frequent. Mitotic activity is either absent or minimal. Usually there is little or no nuclear atypia, but uncommonly, thecomas have small numbers of pleomorphic bizarre nuclei, which are degenerative in nature and unassociated with increased mitotic activity {6309684; 25025365}. The cells are sometimes intersected by a stromal component, most often in the form of hyaline plaques, sometimes forming larger confluent zones of keloid-like sclerosis. A significant portion of thecomas exhibit a fibromatous component, which is generally minor {25025365}; these should not be termed “fibrothecomas”. Focal calcification and more uncommonly adipose metaplasia may be seen {25025365}. Some thecomas contain small clusters of steroid-type cells with eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm; in the past these were referred to as “luteinized thecomas”, but this term is not recommended in this scenario. Rarely, a minor component of sex cord elements is present {6315609}. Reticulin stain surrounds individual cells {29708950}. Immunohistochemically, thecomas are typically positive for inhibin, calretinin, SF1, and other sex cord stromal markers but staining is sometimes focal {12808064; 9688187}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FOXL2 and DICER1 mutations are generally absent, and this can be useful in diagnostically challenging cases. In the presence of a FOXL2 mutation, an adult granulosa cell tumour with a thecoma-like morphology should be strongly considered {29708950; 32782216}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: predominant population of stromal cells with bland ovoid to round nuclei with appreciable pale-grey or clear, lipid-rich cytoplasm.

Desirable: reticulin stain highlighting pericellular reticulin network; positivity for sex cord stromal markers.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Thecomas are almost always benign. Rare malignant thecomas have been reported, but these may be misclassified cases of other neoplasms {228839}.
