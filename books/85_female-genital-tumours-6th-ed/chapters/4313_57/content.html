4.3.1.3 Adenocarcinoma in situ, HPV-independent

Definition

Adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), HPV-independent, is an intraepithelial glandular neoplasm unassociated with HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: gastric-type adenocarcinoma in situ; atypical lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Gastric-type adenocarcinoma in situ (gAIS) is typically located at or just proximal to the transformation zone {28394803; 27334141} and can uncommonly extend into the lower uterine segment and endometrium {28394803}. Atypical lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia (LEGH) is typically located in the upper endocervix {23752085}. Insufficient data exist regarding precursors of other HPV-independent adenocarcinomas.

Clinical features

Most patients with gAIS and atypical LEGH are asymptomatic, the lesions being found incidentally in hysterectomy or cervical excision specimens performed for other indications {28394803}. Patients may present with abnormal cervical cytology, watery discharge, or abnormal bleeding {27334141; 28394803; 38419118; 22878090}. LEGH (and atypical LEGH) may be identified as a multicystic lesion on imaging {23752085}.

Epidemiology

There are no established epidemiological data for AIS, HPV-independent {40258714}. Cervical AIS is most often HPV-associated, and by comparison, only rare HPV-independent cases have been reported {23552379; 28394803; 38419118; 27334141; 22878090}. Consequently, with the transition to HPV-based cervical screening, epidemiological data on HPV-independent AIS may remain largely unknown. Based on case series, the mean age at presentation is in the sixth decade of life {28394803; 36499345}.

Etiology

The etiology of gAIS and atypical LEGH is unknown in most cases. An association with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome has been documented in a few cases of atypical LEGH {22878090, 38419118}.

Pathogenesis

A pathogenic variant of KRAS has been demonstrated in a case of atypical LEGH {24965111}. Pathogenic variants of GNAS, STK11, and KRAS have been demonstrated in a subset of LEGH lesions without cytological atypia, suggesting that at least some cases of non-atypical LEGH are neoplastic in nature {24145653; 39230420}. There are currently limited data regarding the pathogenesis of gAIS occurring in the absence of concurrent LEGH.

Macroscopic appearance

gAIS does not form a visible mass. LEGH (and atypical LEGH) may form a multicystic mass visible macroscopically, typically in the upper endocervix {10435557}.

Histopathology

In gAIS, the pre-existing endocervical glandular architecture is largely retained, with varying degrees of intraglandular architectural complexity in the form of papillary infoldings and cribriform formations, without haphazard gland crowding or confluence {28394803; 27334141}. The epithelium is predominantly gastric in type, characterized by columnar cells with eosinophilic, pale pink, clear or foamy cytoplasm. Nuclei are basally oriented with limited stratification. Nuclear atypia, characterized by nuclear enlargement, irregular nuclear contour, vesicular appearance with coarse chromatin texture, and distinct nucleoli, is often mild but in occasional cases may be severe, with conspicuous large nucleoli {28394803; 23752085; 27334141}. Mitoses and apoptosis are present but are typically not brisk and may only be identified at high magnification. Varying degrees of intestinal (goblet cell) differentiation is often observed {28394803}. Eosinophilic cytoplasmic granules resembling Paneth cells may occasionally be present.

Atypical LEGH exhibits the architecture of LEGH, typically comprising aggregates of small glands surrounding a dilated duct, together with cytological atypia as described for gAIS and/or epithelial infoldings or papillae {15143335}. By definition, destructive stromal invasion is absent.

The immunohistochemical profile of gAIS (including atypical LEGH) is variable. ER and PR are usually negative or at the most focally and weakly positive. CK7 is usually positive, while PAX8, CK20, and CDX2 may be positive or negative {28394803}. p16 expression is usually negative/non–block-type, but diffuse positivity is rarely seen (in such cases, the distinction from HPV-associated AIS may require molecular HPV studies) {24965111; 28394803}. Various immunohistochemical markers such as MUC6, HIK1083, TFF2, CAIX, and claudin-18 may be used to help demonstrate gastric differentiation, but these are not essential for the diagnosis of gAIS; moreover, the sensitivity and specificity of these markers vary, and gAIS has not been widely evaluated for in situ lesions {15143335; 27334141; 28394803; 30324235; 39652450}. Mutation-type p53 immunohistochemical expression, suggestive of an underlying TP53 mutation, is seen in some cases, which supports the diagnosis if present {19723155; 28394803}. Neuroendocrine markers often highlight the presence of significant numbers of neuroendocrine cells.

Some cases of gAIS (including atypical LEGH) are accompanied by synchronous gastric-type mucinous lesions, both metaplastic and neoplastic, elsewhere in the female genital tract (vagina, endometrium, fallopian tubes, ovaries) {19207943; 29670794}. This phenomenon, labelled synchronous mucinous metaplasia and neoplasia of the female genital tract, refers to multifocal gastric-type mucinous lesions occurring in at least two sites and can occur in Peutz–Jeghers syndrome. It may be difficult to distinguish multifocal synchronous lesions from metastasis of one lesion to another site {23752085}.

In situ equivalents for other HPV-independent cervical adenocarcinomas are not well characterized. Limited data propose tubo-endometrial metaplasia with atypia as a possible precursor to clear cell carcinoma {33770055}. An atypical form of mesonephric hyperplasia harbouring identical KRAS mutations or gain of chromosome 1q as concurrent mesonephric adenocarcinoma has been proposed as a tumour precursor {33099482}. A potential precursor lesion has also been proposed for the extremely rare cervical endometrioid adenocarcinoma, but there are currently insufficient data to determine its validity {38199133}.

Cytology

LEGH is characterized on cervical cytology preparations by glandular cells forming monolayered sheets with uniform small nuclei, whereas atypical LEGH and gAIS show more complex glandular structures, mucin localization to the surface of cell groups, vesicular nuclear chromatin, and prominent nucleoli {32814324; 27334141; 30417947}. Scattered goblet cells may be identified {30417947}. gAIS can appear morphologically similar to HPV-associated AIS with stratified epithelial strips or acinar structures and enlarged, hyperchromatic nuclei with palisading mimicking feathering {27334141; 30417947}. Cytological features suggesting gastric differentiation include the presence of abundant cytoplasm, which may contain golden yellow mucin, best seen on conventional cervical cytology preparations {29982289}. Because mucin is paler on liquid-based preparations, it can be difficult to distinguish yellow gastric-type mucin from normal endocervical mucin. HIK1083-labelled latex agglutinin test is an alternative to cytology as a preoperative screening tool, which can detect atypical LEGH {18794052}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV testing, if performed, should be negative.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a non-invasive cytologically atypical glandular proliferation exhibiting gastric/ gastrointestinal differentiation; almost always negative/non–block-type p16 immunohistochemical staining.

Desirable: negative HPV molecular testing in the rare cases exhibiting block-type p16 staining; immunohistochemical evidence of gastrointestinal differentiation.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

gAIS and atypical LEGH are proposed precursors of cervical gastric-type adenocarcinoma, although data are limited {15143335; 19723155}. Shared gene mutations in concurrent LEGH or atypical LEGH and gastric-type adenocarcinoma have been reported in several cases {39230420; 24965111}, but the precise biological behaviour and malignant potential of each pathology is currently unknown. Complete excision with negative margins is recommended; as atypical LEGH is typically situated within the upper endocervix, hysterectomy may be required. Insufficient data exist regarding precursors of other HPV-independent adenocarcinomas.
