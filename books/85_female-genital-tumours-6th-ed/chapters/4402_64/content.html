4.4.0.2 Adenosquamous carcinoma, HPV-associated

Definition

Adenosquamous carcinoma, HPV-associated is an invasive malignant epithelial tumour exhibiting both squamous and glandular differentiation, caused by persistent HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: glassy cell carcinoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized to the cervix and vagina. Most cervical cases are located at the squamocolumnar junction. The most common vaginal location is the upper third of the vagina, especially the anterior wall {25594143; 11812092}.

Clinical features

Like other cervical and vaginal tumours, most patients present with abnormal vaginal bleeding or a mass.

Epidemiology

Adenosquamous carcinomas are uncommon, accounting for about 5–7% of all cervical cancers, although studies do not distinguish between HPV-associated and HPV-independent neoplasms {34367953}. In a large series, they accounted for one fifth of all cervical carcinomas with glandular differentiation {26096203}. Cervical tumours affect predominantly patients younger than 60 years, with a mean age of 45–50 years {25312397; 34367953; 26096203}. The mean patient age at presentation of vaginal tumours is 55 years in limited case series, but these neoplasms may also occur in younger patients {25594143; 4720400; 14155003; 3345952}; studies do not distinguish between HPV-associated and HPV-independent neoplasms.

Etiology

These tumours are associated with high-risk HPV; the most prevalent types are HPV18, 16 and 45 representing 48%, 35%, and 9% of adenosquamous carcinomas of the cervix associated with a single HPV type {19727809}. Multiple HPV types are detected in 6% of the cases {26096203}.

Pathogenesis

HPV is the driver agent. Occasional studies have identified KRAS mutations (3.1%) in cervical adenosquamous carcinoma {35871081}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour appears as an ulcerated, nodular, or polypoid firm mass.

Histopathology

Adenosquamous carcinomas are characterized by a mixture of malignant glandular and squamous components. The pathological diagnosis is based on H&E morphological features, and the two components should be recognizable without special stains. The exact amount of each component necessary to make this diagnosis is not defined, and a diagnosis of adenosquamous carcinoma can be made in the presence of recognizable squamous and glandular components of any amount. Necrosis, inflammatory infiltrates, and lymphovascular invasion are frequently identified {36044310}.

The tumours typically exhibit block-type staining for p16 within both components. Other immunohistochemical stains are not specific, but the glandular elements are typically positive for CK7 and PAX8 and the squamous elements for p63 and p40. ER and PR are typically negative {36044310}. In situ hybridization or PCR techniques are used to confirm the presence of high-risk HPV.

Glassy cell carcinoma {37037083} and mucoepidermoid carcinoma {31963763} are considered as part of the morphological spectrum of adenosquamous carcinoma. Although these terms are still used in occasional recent reports, their use is not recommended. Invasive stratified mucin-producing carcinoma is considered a variant of HPV-associated cervical adenocarcinoma and thus should be excluded {36044310}.

Cytology

Although the concomitant finding of malignant glandular and squamous cells suggests the possibility of adenosquamous carcinoma, the definitive classification should be deferred to a histological specimen {39988765}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Routine HPV testing is not required, but detection of high-risk HPV may be helpful to confirm HPV association.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive carcinoma exhibiting glandular and squamous differentiation; block-type staining with p16 and/or positive HPV testing.

Staging

Tumours are staged according to the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with cervical adenosquamous carcinoma exhibit a clinical outcome similar to that of cervical adenocarcinoma when cases are stratified into risk groups based on clinicopathological features {27646608; 32678039; 28028992; 25312397} but have shorter overall survival and progression-free survival than patients with cervical squamous cell carcinoma {27646608; 28028992; 35299841}. Outcome when stratified by HPV association is unknown but is likely similar as the majority (~85%) of adenosquamous carcinomas are HPV associated. Vaginal adenosquamous carcinoma is clinically aggressive and may develop distant metastases (especially to the lungs); the overall prognosis is poor {25594143; 4720400; 3345952}.
