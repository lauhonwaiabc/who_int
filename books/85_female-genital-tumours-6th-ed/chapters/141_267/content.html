14.1 Haematolymphoid proliferations and neoplasia: Introduction

The female genital tract (FGT) is an uncommon primary site for haematolymphoid tumours. Certain lymphomas may arise primarily in this organ system, and the most common of these are included in this chapter. Many other haematolymphoid neoplasms may arise in the FGT or involve it secondarily in patients with locally extensive or systemic disease. The reader is referred to the fifth-edition Haematolymphoid tumours volume of this series for additional information on neoplasms not listed in this volume.

In a study based on the SEER Program database, the overall incidence of primary lymphoma of the FGT is 0.437 cases per 1 million person-years (adjusted to the US standard population in 2000) and appears to have increased from 1975 to 2017. {35416104} Patients may present with a broad range of signs and symptoms depending on the anatomic location involved, and the most common histological types include diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (59.8%) and follicular lymphoma (11.9%) {28284518}.

Primary FGT lymphomas are uncommon; they account for approximately 1% of extranodal lymphomas {5007387}. FGT myeloid sarcoma is even less common than lymphoma. Haematolymphoid neoplasms occur in women and girls over a broad age range, mostly affecting middle-aged and older adults {28284518; 29984481}. The symptoms are nonspecific. Neoplasms usually present as mass lesions or with abnormal vaginal bleeding {29984481}. They are infrequently incidental findings {27340748; 11706069}. FGT lymphoma involves the ovaries most often, followed by the uterine cervix, uterine corpus, vagina, and vulva {28284518}. Primary fallopian tube lymphoma is very rare {15581994}, but ovarian lymphoma often shows tubal involvement {11073334}. FGT involvement is common in patients who develop lymphoma (especially Burkitt lymphoma) during pregnancy {23725710}.

Almost all primary FGT lymphomas are B-lineage non-Hodgkin lymphomas. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is most common, followed by follicular lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma, and extranodal marginal zone lymphoma {28284518; 29984481; 11444509}. Other types, including B-lymphoblastic and T-lymphoblastic lymphomas {21193918; 16224219; 11706069; 20509008}, anaplastic large cell lymphoma and other peripheral T-cell lymphomas {11706069; 28284518}, and NK-cell lymphoma {11444509} are rare. Almost all lymphomas in children and adolescents are diffuse, high-grade B-cell lymphomas involving the adnexa {16224219}. Lymphomas involving the FGT secondarily are of more-varied types (see #10294Table 14.XXX) {16224219; 10658906; 10907172}.
