4.3.0.1 Endocervical polyp

Definition

Endocervical polyp is a benign exophytic lesion with a stromal core lined by bland mucinous endocervical epithelium and/or metaplastic squamous epithelium.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Endocervix

Clinical features

Endocervical polyps are usually asymptomatic and sometimes discovered during cervical cytology sampling, but they may be associated with vaginal bleeding (especially postcoital) and/or discharge.

Epidemiology

These are common lesions that may occur at any age. Case series report mean patient ages as 40–50 years, suggesting they are more common among women aged > 40 years {18001188; 35072226}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These are most likely inflammatory-associated lesions {22468800}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most are solitary and < 1 cm {18001188}.

Histopathology

Endocervical polyps are composed of stromal tissue lined by bland mucinous endocervical-type epithelium, with glands invaginating into the central stroma. The stroma is vascular and often inflamed with a mixture of acute and chronic inflammatory cells. There is commonly squamous metaplasia (often immature), tubo-endometrial metaplasia, or microglandular hyperplasia involving the surface glands and/or the glands within the stroma. Rarely, Arias-Stella reaction or stromal decidual change may occur, especially when patients are pregnant. The surface of polyps may be inflamed or eroded and associated with reactive/reparative changes in the form of hobnail cells. Squamous intraepithelial lesions or adenocarcinoma in situ may uncommonly involve a pre-existing benign polyp {18753960; 22885648; 27465834; 23569547}. In occasional endocervical polyps, there is a focal club-like (phyllodes-like) architecture with intraglandular stromal protrusions and increased cellularity around glands; while some of these features may overlap with adenosarcoma, these lesions are almost always benign {34675347; 25118811}.

Cytology

Reactive or reparative changes can be seen in cytology preparations in the setting of an endocervical polyp. Repair in squamous cells is characterized by monolayered sheets with round to oval nuclei and smooth nuclear contours. Nucleoli may be present, and streaming can be seen but is less apparent in liquid-based preparations. Reactive endocervical cells are notable for mild nuclear enlargement and slight crowding but have round to oval nuclei with smooth nuclear contour and even chromatin. Marked reactive changes in a polyp can lead to a cytological diagnosis of atypical squamous cells or atypical glandular cells {23221274}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: stromal core lined by bland endocervical-type mucinous epithelium, often with squamous metaplasia.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Endocervical polyps should be excised for pathological examination. They are benign lesions but can recur and can rarely be involved by premalignant or malignant squamous or glandular lesions, either solely or as part of a more widespread lesion {18001188;18753960; 22885648; 27465834}.
