7.2.2.3 Endometrioid carcinoma

Definition

Endometrioid carcinoma of the fallopian tube is a malignant epithelial neoplasm showing endometrioid differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: endometrioid adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fallopian tube

Clinical features

The patients may present with a pelvic mass, or the tumours may be discovered incidentally, mostly occurring in the distal two thirds of the tube.

Epidemiology

The tumour is rare. Despite endometrioid carcinoma being the second most common type of tubal carcinoma, the largest reported series comprises 26 cases with a mean age of 57 years, ranging between 37 and 85 years {8946874}.

Etiology

Endometriosis, which can occur in 23% of cases and sometimes takes the form of an endometrioid polyp, is a possible risk factor {8946874; 24901398; 15104291}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is undetermined but may be similar to that of endometrioid carcinoma of the endometrium and ovary (see <<5.2.21>>Endometrioid carcinoma and section Endometrioid carcinoma, ).

Macroscopic appearance

Endometrioid carcinoma may occur as a fimbrial mass or as diffuse tubal dilatation, ranging from 4 mm to 60 mm. The majority of cases are confined to the tube or extend to the ovary {8946874}.

Histopathology

The morphology is similar to that of endometrial and ovarian endometrioid carcinomas (see <<5.2.21>>Endometrioid carcinomaand section Endometrioid carcinoma). On rare occasions, they may have a prominent spindle cell appearance with squamous-like differentiation or osseous metaplasia {24901398}. In contrast to endometrial and ovarian endometrioid carcinomas, neither the criteria nor the prognostic role for assigning a grade such as FIGO or the Silverberg system to primary fallopian tube endometrioid carcinoma have been studied {36305537}. Secondary involvement of the tube by a synchronous or metastatic endometrioid carcinoma of the endometrium and/or ovary needs to be excluded.

Endometrioid carcinomas are usually WT1-negative and p53 wild type {31097111}.

Cytology

Malignant cells may be identified in pelvic washings in advanced cases {24368120; 31734259}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tubal mural proliferation of closely packed endometrioid glands that meets the criteria used to classify endometrioid carcinoma in the endometrium/ovary.

Desirable: lack of diffuse strong WT1 staining and usually wildtype p53.

Staging

This entity is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025.]]) even in instances when the carcinoma involves the ovary and the peritoneum. Most cases present at a low stage and do not invade the fimbria {31097111}.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis depends on stage. As most cases present at a low stage, the prognosis is favourable {31097111}. Synchronous tubal and endometrial carcinomas are rare, tumours are low-grade and low-stage, which may translate into a good prognosis {18802716}.
