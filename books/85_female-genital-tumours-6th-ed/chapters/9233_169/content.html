9.2.3.3 Gynandroblastoma

Definition

Gynandroblastoma is a mixed tumour composed of elements of two or more sex cord stromal tumours.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: mixed sex cord stromal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Patients can present with symptoms related to the presence of an ovarian mass or hormonal manifestations that may be estrogenic or androgenic {29660837; 27751908; 2067122}. Occasional cases, including rare examples of bilateral tumours, have been reported in patients with DICER1 syndrome {29037807; 37493465}.

Epidemiology

Gynandroblastoma is a rare tumour with information provided by only small case series. Patients present over a wide age range from prepubertal to postmenopausal (median: 24.5 years) {29660837; 29037807}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

In the largest study to date, heterozygous somatic hotspot mutations in the RNase IIIb domain of DICER1 were demonstrated in both tumour components in 3 of 16 cases, all of which showed an admixture of juvenile granulosa cell tumour (JGCT) and moderately to poorly differentiated Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour (SLCT); such tumours should be categorized as SLCT with areas mimicking JGCT (see below). All tumours were FOXL2 wild type, including 7 cases with an adult granulosa cell tumour (AGCT) component; none showed a mutation within the pleckstrin-homology domain of AKT1 {29660837}. Several additional DICER1-mutant (but only very few FOXL2-mutant) gynandroblastomas have been reported {26428316; 22296244; 29037807}. Gynandroblastomas containing components of moderately to poorly differentiated SLCT and JGCT usually exhibit DICER1 mutations and represent morphological variants of pure SLCT with close mimicry of JGCT in areas {32815828; 25844550}, since moderately to poorly differentiated SLCT is characterized by high frequency of mutations in DICER1 {22187960; 28654427; 36583307; 28654427; 26428316; 26033501; 29037807; 24136150; 28654427}. Gynandroblastoma containing a component of AGCT seems to be different from pure AGCT, since almost all of the latter harbour hotspot mutations in FOXL2 (NM_023067.4 c.402C>G p.Cys134Trp) {19516027; 27297428}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are usually unilateral. The average tumour size is 110 mm (range: 55–200 mm). The cut surface is typically predominantly solid with cystic areas, but totally solid or cystic tumours also occur. The cut surface is typically pale yellow or white {14479145}.

Histopathology

These tumours comprise a mixture of elements, most commonly AGCT, JGCT, Sertoli cell tumour, and SLCT. The diagnosis of gynandroblastoma should be reserved for tumours with definitive, geographically distinct areas of two or more sex cord stromal tumours. Pure sex cord tumours, such as AGCT, with fibromatous areas, should not be diagnosed as gynandroblastoma, nor should an AGCT with tubular areas, which out of context could be interpreted as a Sertoli cell tumour {40326456}.

The most commonly reported combination is a predominant moderately to poorly differentiated SLCT component and a smaller JGCT component {29660837}; as discussed, most of these represent SLCT with areas closely mimicking JGCT, and they should be diagnosed as such, especially if a DICER1 mutation is present {29660837; 32815828; 39492459}.

Immunohistochemically the tumour components are usually positive for sex cord markers, such as inhibin, SF1, calretinin, CD56, and FOXL2 {9688187; 29660837}. Cytokeratins may be positive while EMA is almost always negative.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing for pathogenic variants in DICER1 and FOXL2 may be useful to help diagnose a tumour as gynandroblastoma versus SLCT or AGCT, depending on the clinicopathological context. To date, no differences have been identified in the molecular profile between the different elements in gynandroblastoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour showing an admixture of two or more distinct sex cord stromal tumours; areas of morphological mimicry in a pure tumour should be excluded.

Desirable: positivity for sex cord immunohistochemical markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most tumours are benign. Only rare recurrences have been reported {17197894}.
