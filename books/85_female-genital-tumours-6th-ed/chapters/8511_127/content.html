8.5.1.1 Clear cell adenofibroma / clear cell borderline tumour

Definition

Clear cell borderline tumour (CCBT) is an adenofibromatous clear cell tumour with variable nuclear atypia, but no papillary architecture or stromal invasion.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: clear cell cystadenofibroma.

Not recommended: atypical proliferative clear cell tumour; clear cell tumour of low malignant potential.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized to the ovary.

Clinical features

Patients may present with symptoms of a pelvic mass, including increased abdominal girth and pelvic pain, or the tumour may be an incidental finding. Presentation with extraovarian disease has not been reported.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare. The incidence of CCBT lacking atypia, which may be classified as clear cell adenofibroma, is unknown because of its rarity; only small case series exist in the literature {514560; 6692303; 4052962; 21479128}.

CCBTs account for < 1% of all ovarian borderline tumours in case series {22622950}. They are rarely found as the exclusive element in a tumour, but rather as a component of clear cell carcinoma. Most patients with CCBT are postmenopausal {22622950; 21479128; 4052962; 6692303}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

CCBT is almost always associated with clear cell carcinoma, and sometimes associated with endometriosis {6692303; 4052962; 21479128}.

CCBTs that accompany clear cell carcinoma show genetic changes similar to those of the carcinomatous component, including PIK3CA and ARID1A abnormalities {22157930; 22120431}.

Macroscopic appearance

CCBTs are almost always unilateral and form solid or predominantly solid fibromatous tumours, often with small to large cysts. CCBTs range from 35 to 260 mm, with a mean size of 60 mm {514560; 6692303; 4052962; 21479128; 22622950}.

Histopathology

CCBTs contain variably sized glands embedded in a fibromatous stroma. The cells are low cuboidal with clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm. Nuclei are enlarged, sometimes with nucleoli, and mitotic activity is typically absent. Minimal nuclear stratification is permitted, but densely aggregated glands, papillary or tubulocystic architecture, and a stromal response to invasion are absent by definition. In tumours previously considered clear cell adenofibroma, nuclei are small, without chromatin abnormalities or nucleoli; mitotic activity is typically absent. Immunohistochemically, CCBT may be negative or weakly positive for estrogen and progesterone receptors {36580137}.

Extensive sampling is required to exclude carcinoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: adenofibromatous clear cell tumour without stromal invasion or papillary architecture.

Staging

CCBT is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

There have been no reported recurrences for CCBT {22622950; 4052962; 6692303}.
