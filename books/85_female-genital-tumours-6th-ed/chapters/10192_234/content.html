10.1.9.2 Deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma

Definition

Deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma is a locally infiltrative, benign, myxoid, bland spindle cell neoplasm that occurs in the deep soft tissue of the vulvovaginal and pelviperineal regions.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This tumour most commonly arises in the deep soft tissues of the vulvovaginal region, perineum, and pelvis {8646730; 22288973}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a large and painless mass, or poorly defined swelling; rarely, the tumour is pedunculated. Rapid growth may occur during pregnancy. The initial clinical impression is often that of a Bartholin gland cyst or hernia {29692596}. The tumours are often much larger and extend more deeply than is clinically appreciable.

Epidemiology

Deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma of the vulva is a rare tumour, with only about 350 cases documented in the scientific literature so far. This tumour is typically described as most common in women of reproductive age, but it may arise in male individuals, too {10716303}. Reported cases show a wide age distribution (range: 18–63 years), with the peak incidence at 31–35 years and the median age in the fourth decade of life {3997139; 8646730; 6684403; 34441295}. An M:F ratio of 1:6.6 has been described in one of the larger case series {10716303}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

HMGA2 encodes a DNA architectural factor important in transcriptional regulation. Rearrangements of the HMGA2 (12q14.3) locus have been identified in approximately one third of tumours and may be intragenic or extragenic {11550285; 17654722}. Rearrangement partners include chromosomes 1, 5, 7, 8, 11, 12, and 21, with the identification of YAP1 at 11q22.1 and C12orf42 at 12q23 as novel fusion partners {11550285; 18295664; 31142482; 35985684}. RT-PCR studies have confirmed the presence of aberrant HMGA2 transcript expression in deep (aggressive) angiomyxomas exhibiting HMGA2 locus rearrangements {11550285; 17654722; 16568309}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is typically unencapsulated, and poorly or vaguely circumscribed. Tumour size is variable, ranging from 15 to 200 mm, with a glistening, gelatinous to fibrogelatinous cut surface. Areas of congested blood vessels, haemorrhage, or fibrosis may be present {3997139; 8646730; 6684403; 25925211; 22288973; 18637148}.

Histopathology

Deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma is a uniformly paucicellular tumour composed of bland, small oval, spindle, and stellate cells interspersed in abundant loose myxomatous stroma with delicate collagen fibrils; mitoses and nuclear atypia are absent. Tumours lack a capsule, and soft tissue infiltration is a frequent finding, characterized by entrapment of muscle, nerve, and adipose tissue. The stroma often contains medium-sized blood vessels, some of which have thick, muscular walls or are hyalinized. Perivascular cuffs of delicate fibrillary collagen and small bundles of smooth muscle, sometimes with a perivascular arrangement, are common {25925211; 9023551; 18637148}. Recurrent tumours often show fibro-sclerotic stroma with a well-represented vascular component, often with perivascular hyalinization {34441295}.

The neoplastic cells are often positive for desmin and variably positive for SMA, ER, PR, and CD34 {11002763; 21804399; 25925211; 22288973; 15626915; 15735978}. HMGA2 nuclear immunoreactivity is present in most cases {20551826; 20834238; 35204448}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: poorly marginated; uniformly paucicellular proliferation of bland spindle, oval, or stellate cells in myxoedematous stroma; capillary-like and medium/large-sized vessels.

Desirable: perivascular hyalinization; stromal delicate fibrillary collagen and bundles of smooth muscle cells, often with perivascular arrangement.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence occurs in as many as 30–40% of cases, sometimes many years (often decades) after the initial excision {3997139; 8646730; 6684403; 25925211}, usually if the tumour is incompletely excised initially. Adequate local excision may be difficult because tumour extension into adjacent tissues may be greater than appreciated on clinical examination. Wide local excision (with 10 mm margins) is therefore recommended. GnRH agonist therapy has been successfully used to treat unresectable primary or recurrent tumours {16246403}.
