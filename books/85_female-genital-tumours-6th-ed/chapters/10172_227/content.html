10.1.7.2 Rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

Rhabdomyosarcomas are a family of malignant mesenchymal tumours exhibiting skeletal muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: sarcoma botryoides; mixed embryonal and alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The frequency at different sites within the female genital tract varies by histological type. Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma (PRMS) is typically seen in the uterine corpus. Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS) is most common in the vagina in children and in the uterine cervix and corpus in adolescents and adults, with rare examples involving the fallopian tube / ovary {31990691}. Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS) is the least common type and is most frequent in the vulva {28634755; 29320751}.

Clinical features

Patients with rhabdomyosarcomas often present with bleeding and are found to have a mass on pelvic examination. Cervical and vaginal ERMSs often present as a polyp {22157934; 35941719} and may protrude through the introitus, sometimes with a grape-like or botryoid appearance {35941719}. PRMS typically occurs in postmenopausal patients, who often present with abnormal vaginal bleeding {20173498; 9421077}.

Epidemiology

ERMS is an uncommon tumour that represents > 75% of lower female genital tract rhabdomyosarcomas {28634755}. Most ERMSs arise in children. One large case series reported a mean age of 16 years (range 13–30 years), and other studies describe a peak in the incidence during the second decade of life. In children, ERMS tends to occur in the vagina, can occur in the uterine corpus, and rarely occurs in the uterine cervix, whereas ARMS and PRMS occur at an older age without this predisposition {18427824; 29781567; 27116984}.

Etiology

Individuals with DICER1 syndrome (germline DICER1 mutations) are at risk for ERMS, most often arising in the uterine cervix {22157934} (see section DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome (DICER1)).

Pathogenesis

Most uterine and tubo-ovarian ERMS harbour DICER1 alterations, in contrast to ERMS of other sites including the vagina {22180160; 26461232, 33846547; 31990691}. This includes somatic missense variants in the RNase IIIb domain of DICER1, affecting one of five codons (E1705, D1709, G1809, D1810, or E1813). These mutations are typically accompanied by a second, loss-of-function mutation in the other allele of DICER1 that truncates the coding sequence, resulting in compound DICER1 mutations. Such mutations may have a germline origin in cases associated with DICER1 syndrome. In one study, germline DICER1 variants have been identified in more than half of uterine ERMS studied {31900434}. Somatic missense variants in the RNase IIIb domain disrupt the endoribonuclease activity of DICER1, resulting in reduced expression of 5p strand miRNAs, which can alter global gene expression {23132766}. Other alterations identified in DICER1-associated tumours include gain of chromosome 8, mutations in the RAS signalling pathway, and TP53 mutations, the latter of which may be associated with anaplasia {33846547; 36966138}. Uterine and tubo-ovarian ERMS share histomorphological features (see Histopathology, below), as well as DNA methylation and mutational signatures with other DICER1-associated mesenchymal neoplasms, including pleuropulmonary blastoma, anaplastic sarcoma of the kidney and primary intracranial sarcoma, DICER1-mutant {36966138}. As a result, some authors proposed the unifying term “DICER1-associated sarcoma” for such neoplasms {33825038; 36966138}.

Non-DICER1–associated ERMS typically harbour RAS signalling pathway mutations, which activate the MAPK effector pathway to repress myogenin expression and block myogenesis {29973406;26138366; 33846547}.

ARMS is characterized by t(2;13) or t(1;13) translocations producing PAX3::FOXO1 or PAX7::FOXO1 fusion genes encoding transcription factors that activate PAX3/PAX7 target genes involved in the regulation of proliferation, survival, differentiation, and motility {23526739; 17471488}.

PRMS is characterized by recurrent TP53 and RB1 alterations {38722224; 29320751}.

Macroscopic appearance

Rhabdomyosarcoma usually has a fleshy greyish-tan appearance. ERMS often manifests as clusters of submucosal, multiple, polypoid, grape-like masses or as a solitary polyp {3278956; 35941719}.

Histopathology

ERMS is usually polypoid and partially covered by squamous or glandular epithelium {3278956; 35941719}. It is characterized by clusters of primitive, darkly staining ovoid to spindled cells with minimal cytoplasm arranged within an oedematous, or less often collagenous, stroma. Primitive cells may also be arranged in a band beneath the surface epithelium or around entrapped glands (cambium layer). Tumour cells may be infrequent or subtle, which may result in misdiagnosis as a benign polyp {35941719}. Overt rhabdomyoblasts are variably seen but are often not prominent and show eosinophilic cytoplasm arranged in an elongated band (strap cell), with or without cross striations, or in a rounded globule displacing the nucleus to the periphery. Spider cells with a central nucleus, vacuolated cytoplasm, and radiating eosinophilic bands may be rarely seen. Occasional tumours have densely cellular areas resembling the solid pattern of ARMS; many of these have likely been called mixed rhabdomyosarcomas in the past, but clinical and genomic features are most consistent with those of ERMS {23765537}. Anaplasia (3× variance in nuclear size) occurs in approximately 15% and is typically focal {35941719}. However, it may be more prominent and resemble that in PRMS, particularly in biopsy specimens. Mitoses and apoptoses are frequent. Approximately 40% contain fetal-type cartilage {23348207; 3278956}, a finding mostly restricted to tumours with DICER1 alterations {33846547}. Of note, some DICER1-associated ERMS have shown primitive-appearing glandular epithelium {31990691} or neuroectodermal elements {22157934}.

ARMS is composed of small round blue cells with variable numbers of overt rhabdomyoblasts growing in loose cohesive nests or alveoli, separated by collagenous stroma {21615615}. The cells show dyscohesion centrally and adhere to septa at the periphery. Multinucleate cells may be present. A solid pattern without fibrous septa may be seen.

PRMS shows an admixture of round, polygonal, bizarre, or spindle cells, with marked atypia and with or without giant cells {29320751; 20173498}.

Rhabdomyosarcomas are immunohistochemically positive for myogenin and MYOD1, both of which are specific. Staining is often diffuse in ARMS and PRMS but may be only focal in ERMS. MSA, desmin, myoglobin, and myosin may be positive, but SMA is negative.

The diagnosis of rhabdomyosarcoma should only be made after exclusion of other tumours with a component of rhabdomyoblastic differentiation (e.g. adenosarcoma or carcinosarcoma). Rhabdomyosarcoma arising in the context of other neoplasms should not be classified by type.

Cytology

Smears are mostly hypercellular, composed of single cells, small loosely cohesive tissue fragments, and large tissue fragments with tightly packed small round, stellate, and short spindle cells with scant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic nuclei with granular chromatin. In addition, variable numbers of cells with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation, binucleate and multinucleate cells, along with variably prominent loose myxoid matrix, are observed {3076110}. However, the diagnostic value of cytology is limited.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In uterine and tubo-ovarian ERMS, DICER1 mutation analysis is recommended if available. Demonstration of fusion genes may be helpful in selected settings.

In ARMSs, PAX3::FOXO1 and PAX7::FOXO1 account for about 70–90% and 10–30% of the fusions, respectively {28521080; 22447499}. Immunohistochemically, using antibodies that target the FOXO1 C-terminus or the junctional region of the fusion protein, the presence of the PAX3::FOXO1 or PAX7::FOXO1 fusion oncoprotein can be evaluated {33299109; 36860202}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ERMS: clusters of primitive cells with subepithelial condensation (cambium layer), with or without overt skeletal muscle differentiation; expression of skeletal muscle markers, including desmin with variable nuclear positivity for myogenin and MYOD1. ARMS: rhabdomyoblasts arranged in classic alveolar or solid pattern; with expression of desmin and nuclear myogenin and MYOD1. PRMS: epithelioid and spindled rhabdomyoblasts with marked atypia and pleomorphism; with variable expression of desmin and variable nuclear expression of myogenin and MYOD1.

Desirable: molecular confirmation in selected cases.

Staging

Pathological staging is based on tumour, nodal, and metastatic status, and clinical group is based on extent of disease {16921036}. In the Intergroup Rhabdomyosarcoma Study Group (IRSG) clinical staging system, the female genital tract is a favourable site, and non-metastatic tumours are considered stage 1.

Prognosis and prediction

Rhabdomyosarcoma of the lower female genital tract is associated with an overall 5-year survival rate of about 70% {28634755}. However, survival is higher for younger patients with ERMS {28634755; 29159540}, and DICER1-associated ERMS appears to form a distinct subgroup with improved prognosis {33846547}. Patients with tumours harbouring pathogenic DICER1 variants should be referred to clinical genetics for germline testing and counselling. Tumours with ARMS histology but without gene fusions are associated with an outcome similar to that associated with ERMS histology {20351326; 23526739}. Although anaplasia has been associated with poor prognosis in aggregated studies of ERMS of all sites {18985676}, there is no clear association with outcome in uterine tumours, which may be related to DICER1 mutational status {35941719}.
