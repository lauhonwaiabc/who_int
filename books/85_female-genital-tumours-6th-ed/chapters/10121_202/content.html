10.1.2.1 Endometrial stromal nodule

Definition

Endometrial stromal nodule is a well-circumscribed endometrial stromal tumour resembling proliferative-phase endometrial stroma and lacking lymphovascular invasion.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign endometrial stromal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized within the uterus; they may be submucosal, intramural, or (rarely) subserosal.

Clinical features

Patients may present with abnormal uterine bleeding, abdominal pain, enlarged uterus, or a pelvic mass {2327549; 7793473; 11979087; 7216172}.

Epidemiology

ESN is a rare tumour {2327549; 7793473; 11979087; 7216172}. A study from Scandinavia reported an estimated incidence of 0.3 cases per 100 000 person-years {22377186; 26221551}. It occurs more often in perimenopausal women (mean patient age: 53 years; range: 23–86 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Approximately 70% of ESNs with classic histology, as well as some tumours with smooth muscle or sex cord differentiation, harbour t(7;17)(p21;q15) resulting in JAZF1::SUZ12 fusion {11371647; 17667554; 15043312; 21836477}. Alternative JAZF1 fusion partners have also been reported as well as fusions involving other genes such as PHF1 {21836477; 32820570}. Rare cases with fusions more typical of high-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma have been reported, including JAZF1::BCORL1 and YWHAE::NUTM2 {36811828; 21836477; 37032555}; however, making a diagnosis of ESN with such molecular alterations should be approached with caution until more data become available regarding their behaviour.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are well circumscribed, solid, and yellow to tan, with a mean size of 70 mm (range: 10–220 mm). They may be polypoid or may show cystic change {2327549; 7793473; 11979087; 7216172}.

Histopathology

ESNs are typically well-circumscribed nodules. As many as three finger-like projections extending < 3 mm from the border with the surrounding myometrium have been permitted in the diagnosis of ESN. Although this cut-off value is somewhat arbitrary and supported by limited scientific evidence, it is widely accepted as an established criterion for these lesions {30367239}. Tumours with border irregularity exceeding what is allowed for a diagnosis of an ESN but without the widespread myometrial infiltration typical of low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma are classified as endometrial stromal sarcoma with limited infiltration and may recur or metastasize {11979087; 30807369; 34166281}. Distinction from ESS cannot be made on biopsy or curetting specimens as it requires careful examination of the tumour’s interface with the adjacent myometrium. ESNs are typically densely cellular, displaying uniform small cells with scant cytoplasm (except if decidualized), round to oval nuclei, and inconspicuous nucleoli that may whorl around arterioles (which may be hyalinized). Scattered large vessels may be seen at the tumour periphery. Mitotic activity is usually low but if increased does not exclude this diagnosis. Collagen bands, foamy histiocytes, and less commonly cholesterol clefts may be present. Other morphology include smooth muscle differentiation {17667554; 15043312}, fibromyxoid change (hypocellular appearance), sex cord–like differentiation, endometrioid glands, and rhabdoid or epithelioid morphology {2327549; 11979087; 7216172; 10542938; 11781523; 19236513; 9689655}. The immunoprofile mirrors that reported for low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma, and staining pattern may be helpful to exclude other diagnoses such as cellular leiomyoma {7793473; 34556086}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular studies may be helpful in excluding other neoplasms with overlapping features; however, the presence of JAZF1, PHF1, or related fusions do not distinguish ESN from endometrial stromal sarcoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-demarcated nodule; tumour cells reminiscent of proliferative-type stroma; no more than 3 mm myometrial invasion; no lymphovascular invasion.

Desirable: CD10, ER, and PR expression.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Patients have excellent outcomes, providing the tumour interface with myometrium is well sampled to exclude sarcoma {11979087; 2327549; 7793473; 7216172}. Endometrial stromal tumours with limited infiltration have potential for malignant behaviour, emphasizing the importance of extensive sampling of the lesion’s interface with adjacent myometrium {30807369}.
