10.1.9.6 Perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa)

Definition

PEComa is a rare mesenchymal neoplasm composed of perivascular epithelioid cells (PECs) that typically coexpress melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

TFE3 translocation–associated PEComa

Localization

PEComas typically arise in the uterine corpus but may occur in the cervix, vagina, vulva, ovary, and broad ligament {16327428; 24418852; 30001237}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with a pelvic mass, abnormal bleeding, or abdominopelvic pain, but smaller tumours may be incidental findings {16327428; 24418852; 30001237}.

Epidemiology

Gynaecological PEComas are uncommon and have been reported in case series to occur in adults (median: 56 years; range: 27–86 years) {16327428; 24418852; 30001237; 36856023; 38905754}.

Etiology

Most PEComas are sporadic, but approximately 10% are associated with tuberous sclerosis complex {18156981; 21293289; 24418852; 30001237}.

Pathogenesis

Many gynaecological PEComas harbour inactivating mutations, deletions, or rearrangements involving TSC1 or TSC2, resulting in constitutive activation of the mTOR signalling pathway {25651471; 34131293; 36856023}. TFE3 translocation–associated PEComas are defined by TFE3 rearrangements, which are mutually exclusive with TSC1 and TSC2 alterations {20871214; 22456611; 25517951; 25651471}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours have a median size of 55 mm (range: 2 mm to > 200 mm) and are typically well circumscribed and firm, but may exhibit infiltrative borders, haemorrhage, necrosis, or softening {16327428; 24418852; 30001237}.

Histopathology

Conventional PEComas are often well circumscribed but may show permeative (tongue-like) or infiltrative growth {11756764; 16327428; 24418852; 30001237}. They are composed of epithelioid and/or spindled cells with clear to eosinophilic granular cytoplasm. Epithelioid cells are arranged in nests surrounded by delicate thin-walled vessels, sheets, or cords, while spindled cells form fascicles resembling smooth muscle. Other features include a radial/perivascular distribution of tumour cells, multinucleated cells, cells with lipid-rich or rhabdoid cytoplasm, melanin pigment, and stromal hyalinization (if extensive, the tumour may be descriptively designated as sclerosing PEComa). Cytological atypia, mitotic activity, and necrosis may be seen. Rare tumours form a mass with a lymphangioleiomyomatosis-like morphology {30001237}, demonstrate biphasic growth with spatially distinct components of PEComa and leiomyosarcoma {34131293; 38497360}, or exhibit hybrid features between PEComa and low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma {34561551}. PEComatosis is characterized by multiple, usually microscopic PEComas in uterine and extrauterine tissues {15494070; 18156981; 34593703}.

Conventional PEComas express melanocytic and smooth muscle markers with variable intensity and extent. Among melanocytic markers, HMB45 and PNL2 are the most sensitive, being positive in most tumours {24418852; 30001237; 34131293; 31851063; 36856023}. Melan-A is specific but may be focal or negative in many tumours. Cathepsin K is typically strong and diffuse, but not specific as it may also be expressed in smooth muscle tumours. Smooth muscle markers, including desmin, h-caldesmon, and SMA are similarly expressed in PEComas. Rare tumours are mismatch repair protein deficient {30765880; 34131293; 36856023}.

TFE3 translocation–associated PEComas are composed of uniform epithelioid cells with abundant clear or clear to eosinophilic cytoplasm, arranged in nested, alveolar, or solid patterns {25517951; 25651471;40462401}. Melanin pigment may be seen. They are diffusely positive for TFE3, HMB45, and cathepsin K, with rare to absent expression for melan-A and smooth muscle markers.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genomic profiling may be useful in suspected PEComas with ambiguous features or limited expression of melanocytic markers. Although detection of inactivating TSC1 or TSC2 genomic alterations is helpful in confirmation, their absence does not exclude the diagnosis {32889887; 36856023}. In TFE3 translocation–associated PEComas, molecular confirmation of TFE3 rearrangement can be useful in tumours with equivocal TFE3 staining.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid or spindled cells with clear to eosinophilic granular cytoplasm; myomelanocytic differentiation except in the TFE3 translocation–associated subtype where diffuse TFE3 expression and/or TFE3 rearrangement is necessary if smooth muscle markers are negative.

Staging

Malignant PEComa is staged as uterine sarcoma using the FIGO, Union for International Cancer Control (UICC), and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most gynaecological PEComas are benign, but multiple algorithms based on clinicopathological features have been proposed to predict outcome (#53563Table 10.XXX) {16327428; 24418852; 25750268; 30001237; 34157139; 38905754}. Although data are limited, most malignant PEComas also harbour genomic alterations involving TP53, RB1, ATRX, BRCA2, CDKN2A, or RAD51B {25651471; 30001237; 32889887; 34131293; 36856023; 38141829}. Targeted therapy with mTOR inhibitors can be considered for patients with malignant PEComas even in the absence of mTOR pathway alterations, including those with TFE3 rearrangements {34637337; 38427923}.
