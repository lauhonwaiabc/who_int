10.1.9.7 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour (IMT) is a distinctive mesenchymal neoplasm comprising myofibroblastic and fibroblastic spindle cells accompanied by an inflammatory infiltrate.

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflammatory pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma

Localization

IMT usually arises in the uterine corpus {25321329; 28664932; 36344483}, less commonly in the cervix {25321329; 36344483}, and rarely in the vulva {35180768}. Pregnancy-associated IMT may involve the placental disc / extraplacental membranes or is expelled shortly following delivery {32271187; 32971128}. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma (EIMS) is usually of peritoneal origin {21164297} but rarely occurs in the uterus {36344483} or ovary {28787324}.

Clinical features

Large tumours may cause abnormal uterine bleeding or pelvic pain {25321329; 28664932; 36344483} but can be an incidental finding at the time of hysterectomy or caesarean section {32271187; 32971128; 39833069}. Tumours in the peritoneum may cause abdominal pain or ascites {21164297}.

Epidemiology

Gynaecological IMTs are uncommon. They typically occur in adult, premenopausal women (median: 42 years; range: 3–78 years) {36344483}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Most (> 95%) uterine IMTs are ALK-positive by immunohistochemistry, which typically correlates with ALK gene rearrangement. ALK rearrangements (2p23.2-p23.1) are detected by FISH or RNA-/DNA-based fusion analysis. Common fusion partners include IGFBP5, FN1, THBS1, and TIMP3 {30015720; 36344483; 38630911}; the latter two are enriched in pregnancy-associated IMTs {31917155; 32271187; 32971128}. Tumours negative by FISH often have complex genetic rearrangements, such as intrachromosomal inversions, which can be detected by fusion analysis {28490045; 29794871; 30741845}. ALK-negative uterine IMTs are rare and probably underrecognized, with only rare examples reported involving ROS1, RET, PDGFRB, and INSR {31917155; 32247788; 35426837; 36624188; 36730016; 37156213} [[Smith J, Sharma A, Momeni-Boroujeni A, et al.1079 ROS1-Associated Uterine Inflammatory Myofibroblastic Tumours: Rare Neoplasms Occurring over a Wide Age Range and with Potentially Aggressive Behaviour. Laboratory Investigation. 2025 Mar,105(3):103313.]]. EIMS harbours fusions between ALK and either RANBP2 or RRBP1 {21164297; 27874193}.

Macroscopic appearance

Uterine IMTs have a median size of 63 mm (range: 10–220 mm) {36344483; 38630911}. Most tumours are intramural, but polypoid intracavitary growth can occur {39833069}. They display a soft and gelatinous or firm and whorled cut surface, with haemorrhage or necrosis in about one third {16160478; 25321329; 28664932}. EIMS is usually large (median: 150 mm), with a fleshy and mucoid cut surface {21164297}.

Histopathology

At low magnification, IMTs may be well circumscribed or infiltrative. Three histological patterns can be seen, which may be admixed {25321329; 28664932; 36344483}: (1) a loose myxoid pattern, characterized by hypocellular areas of individual cells in an abundant myxoid matrix, imparting a fasciitis-like or tissue culture–like appearance; (2) a fascicular/compact pattern (leiomyoma-like), characterized by cellular areas arranged in intersecting fascicles or less commonly storiform architecture {35426837; 36806735}; and (3) a hyalinized pattern, associated with dense collagenous matrix and inconspicuous tumour cells. Tumour cells are usually spindled, containing wispy eosinophilic cytoplasm and elongated nuclei with evenly dispersed to open chromatin. Ganglion-like, epithelioid, and multinucleated cells may be observed. Nuclear atypia is usually minimal and mitoses infrequent, but severe atypia and brisk mitoses may occur. The inflammatory component is usually lymphoplasmacytic but may be mixed and varies in intensity and distribution. Decidualization of tumour cells can be seen in pregnancy {32271187; 32971128}.

ALK granular cytoplasmic positivity is highly sensitive and specific to the diagnosis of uterine IMT; of note, the degree of positivity varies and is often dependent on the antibody clone {33290353}. IMTs frequently express smooth muscle markers, particularly in the compact/fascicular areas, as well as CD10, ER, and PR {25321329; 28664932; 29794871; 39833069}. In general, BCOR is negative/weakly positive, p53 wild type, and p16 patchy/heterogeneous {28130839; 32568825}.

EIMS is composed of sheets of epithelioid cells with large nucleoli and amphophilic to eosinophilic cytoplasm, as well as a minor spindle cell component {21164297; 28787324; 34138797}. Prominent myxoid stroma and inflammation (often neutrophilic) are common and mitoses are infrequent. ALK is localized to either the nuclear membrane (RANBP2::ALK fusions) or cytoplasm (RRBP1::ALK fusions), the latter demonstrating perinuclear accentuation {21164297; 27874193}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Immunohistochemistry for ALK or ROS1 is usually sufficient for diagnosis, although demonstration of fusions involving other tyrosine kinase receptor genes may be useful in negative cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cells with myxoid stroma and/or fascicular growth.

Desirable: lymphoplasmacytic inflammation; ALK or ROS1 immunohistochemical expression; molecular confirmation of ALK, ROS1, or other tyrosine kinase receptor gene fusions if immunohistochemistry is equivocal or negative.

Staging

Aggressive IMTs, termed “malignant IMTs”, are staged as uterine sarcomas using the FIGO, Union for International Cancer Control (UICC), and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most uterine IMTs are benign; however, a minority exhibit extrauterine spread and/or recur {34138797; 36344483; 38630911}. Several features have been associated with aggressive behaviour, including older age, infiltrative borders, moderate to severe atypia, increased mitoses, and predominant epithelioid morphology. A risk-stratification model based on several of these clinicopathological features has been proposed (see <<52656>>Table 10.XXX) {36344483}. Other features potentially predictive of outcome include abnormal p16 (positivity in < 1% or > 90% of tumour cells) {38630911}, aberrant p53 {36344483}, and concurrent genomic alterations (CDKN2A deletions, TP53 or TERT promoter mutations, and genomic instability) {36344483; 36624188; 38630911}. IMTs that harbour rearrangements in tyrosine kinase receptor genes may potentially respond to targeted therapy using tyrosine kinase inhibitors {34522753; 38630911}. EIMS is reported to have a highly aggressive clinical course, characterized by rapid local recurrence and a significant rate of tumour-related mortality {21164297; 28787324; 34138797}.
