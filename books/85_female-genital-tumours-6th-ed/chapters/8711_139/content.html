8.7.1.1 Brenner tumour

Definition

Brenner tumour is composed of nests of bland transitional/urothelial epithelium set within a dense fibromatous stroma.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur predominantly in the ovary. Extraovarian Brenner tumours have also been reported {1565384; 2071061, 32164751}.

Clinical features

These are usually unilateral ovarian tumours but are occasionally bilateral {5043433; 903146; 36810485}. Most patients are asymptomatic, and the tumours are typically found incidentally in ovaries removed for other reasons {21897739}. Larger tumours may result in abdominal enlargement or pain {445369}. Occasionally, Brenner tumours with functioning stroma are associated with endocrine symptoms {2649834; 4673027}.

Epidemiology

Brenner tumours account for approximately 5% of benign ovarian epithelial tumours in clinical studies {5100396; 2685680}. The majority of these tumours arise in adults in the fifth to seventh decades of life, although they may occur in patients aged < 30 years or > 80 years {5043433; 450368;35000896; 36810485; 38539441}.

Etiology

The etiology of these tumours is not well understood {195239; 18976011; 7448700; 445369}. Some may be derived from Walthard rests, which are nests of urothelial-type epithelium in paratubal tissue {24012099}. The rare cases associated with a teratoma may originate from germ cells {4366206; 4367229}.

Pathogenesis

Walthard nests have been described in association with ovarian mucinous tumours and Brenner tumours {18976011}. Non-recurrent mutations in a variety of genes {28639280} and MYC amplification {26797085} have been reported at low frequency in these tumours. In combined mucinous and Brenner tumours, there is a shared clonal relationship between the two different tumour components {26095692}. However, mucinous tumours frequently carry KRAS mutations that are not present in Brenner tumours {26797085; 9506354}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most Brenner tumours are small (< 20 mm), and only rare examples are > 100 mm in dimension {21594602}. Brenner tumours are solid, well-circumscribed nodules with a firm rubbery consistency. The cut surface is greyish white or yellow and can be calcified. Small cysts are common, and in rare cases the tumour can be predominantly cystic {12649675}. One quarter of Brenner tumours are associated with other tumour types, with mucinous being the most common {11781528; 15482570}.

Histopathology

Brenner tumours are typically composed of irregular nests of bland urothelial-type epithelium in dense fibromatous stroma. The nests often show microcystic spaces containing eosinophilic or mucinous material. These lumina may be lined by transitional, mucinous, ciliated, or cuboidal cells. The cells are uniform, with elongated oval nuclei, occasional nuclear grooves, and fine chromatin. Nucleoli can be present. There should not be significant epithelial atypia, and mitotic activity is low. Mucinous metaplasia within the lesion is not uncommon {4005815}; and when Brenner tumours are associated with mucinous neoplasms, the mucinous component is usually a cystadenoma. Focal or extensive calcification is frequently seen, and the stroma can be hyalinized.

Immunohistochemistry

Brenner tumours express GATA3, CK7, p63, S100P, AR, uroplakin, and thrombomodulin, but they do not express (or only focally express) CK20 {19092634; 11253121; 2744751; 25281026; 22198845}. PAX8, ER, and PR are typically negative {26095692; 25596159; 25281026}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nests of bland urothelial-type epithelium set within fibromatous stroma.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign tumours with no risk of recurrence or progression.
