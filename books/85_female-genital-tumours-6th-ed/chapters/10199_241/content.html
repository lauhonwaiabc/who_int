10.1.9.9 CNS-type tumours

Definition

CNS-type tumours are malignant tumours with central neuroectodermal differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: embryonic-type neuroectodermal tumour.

Not recommended: primitive neuroectodermal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CNS-type tumours generally occur in the ovary or uterus in which the corpus is more commonly involved than the cervix {28296680; 18223324; 8393302; 30074494; 40189027; 18360284; 6204919; 26990854}. Rare tumours arising in the fallopian tube, broad ligament, omentum, and peritoneum have been reported {30074494; 18360284; 26990854}.

Clinical features

The median age of patients with ovarian CNS-type tumours is 24 years (range: 6–73 years) {30074494; 8393302; 28296680; 18360284; 6204919; 26990854}. The median age of patients with uterine CNS-type tumours is 58 years (range: 31–81 years) {30074494; 18223324; 28296680}. Patients with ovarian tumours frequently present with a pelvic/abdominal mass, pelvic/abdominal/back pain, and/or abdominal distension/ascites; abnormal vaginal bleeding and virilism are rare {28296680; 30074494; 8393302; 40189027; 6204919}. Patients with uterine tumours frequently present with abnormal vaginal bleeding; a pelvic mass and pelvic pain or pressure are less common {30074494; 18223324; 28296680}.

Epidemiology

These are rare tumours.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The frequent association of ovarian tumours with teratoma and/or yolk sac tumour suggests germ cell derivation {30074494; 8393302; 28296680; 40189027; 26990854}. The association of uterine tumours with another tumour type (endometrial hyperplasia, carcinoma, carcinosarcoma, or sarcoma) suggests somatic transformation {30074494; 18223324; 28296680}.

Macroscopic appearance

Ovarian tumours are typically large and solid or solid and cystic {30074494; 8393302; 28296680; 26990854; 40189027; 26990854}. The solid component is white, tan, or pink. Haemorrhage and necrosis can be prominent. Teratomatous elements, such as waxy sebaceous or gelatinous material, hair, bone, or teeth may be present {28296680; 8393302}. Uterine tumours often form an exophytic, polypoid, fleshy, white, tan, or pink mass with haemorrhage and can be associated with myometrial invasion {28296680; 30074494}.

Histopathology

CNS-type tumours are characterized by a variable degree of neuronal or glial differentiation. Tumours with glial or neuronal differentiation may look like adult-type diffuse gliomas (astrocytoma, oligodendroglioma, or glioblastoma), circumscribed astrocytic gliomas (pilocytic astrocytoma), glioneuronal and neuronal neoplasms (glioneuronal tumour, neurocytoma); or ependymal lesions (ependymoma) {28296680; 30074494; 8393302; 40189027; 26990854; 6204919; 18360284}. Tumours with embryonal differentiation may have an appearance reminiscent of medulloblastoma or embryonal tumour with multilayered rosettes (ependymoblastoma, medulloepithelioma) {28296680; 30074494; 8393302; 40189027}. In the ovary, approximately 50% of tumours are associated with germ cell neoplasia that is most frequently teratoma and rarely teratoma with a component of yolk sac tumour or pure yolk sac tumour {28296680; 30074494; 8393302; 40189027; 26990854}. Some uterine tumours are associated with another tumour type, such as endometrial hyperplasia, carcinoma, carcinosarcoma; adenosarcoma; and high-grade sarcomas, including rhabdomyosarcoma {18223324; 28296680}. Tumours demonstrate variable expression for synaptophysin, GFAP, NeuN, neurofilament, S100, and chromogranin {28296680; 18223324; 30074494; 40189027; 26990854}. Patchy, nonspecific membranous CD99 and nuclear FLI1 expression can be seen {26990854; 40189027;18223324; 28296680}; Ewing sarcoma should be excluded if the former is diffuse and when there lacks an underlying neoplasm (e.g. germ cell tumour, carcinoma, carcinosarcoma, or sarcoma). These tumours are rarely positive for broad-spectrum cytokeratin or EMA, and they are negative for desmin; however, increased epithelial marker expression may be present in areas of immature neuroectoderm {18223324; 40189027; 26990854; 28296680; 30074494}. Rare tumours may be positive for ER, PR, and/or PAX8 {30074494; 26990854}. SOX2 is a sensitive but nonspecific biomarker that will stain immature and mature neural elements {18162777, 39259293}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a malignant neoplasm with any degree of neuroectodermal differentiation as seen in brain tumours.

Desirable: immunoreactivity for neuronal/glial and/or neuroendocrine markers.

Staging

Uterine tumours are staged as uterine sarcoma. Ovarian tumours are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Clinical behaviour is difficult to ascertain because of tumour rarity. Limited data demonstrate highly variable clinical behaviour and suggest that stage is an important prognostic factor {30074494; 8393302; 28296680; 40189027; 26990854; 30074494; 18223324; 28296680}.
