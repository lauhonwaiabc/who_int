9.2.2.1 Adult granulosa cell tumour

Definition

Adult granulosa cell tumour (AGCT) is a pure sex cord tumour consisting of granulosa cells growing in a variety of patterns, admixed with a variable amount of fibromatous stroma.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

AGCT usually presents with abdominal pain or other symptoms related to an ovarian mass or with estrogenic manifestations, typically abnormal uterine bleeding {21998762; 30500284}. Concurrent endometrial hyperplasia (seen in about one third of cases) and endometrial endometrioid carcinoma (seen in ~10% of cases) are common {25527364}. Androgenic manifestations (more common in cystic tumours) occur in about 10% of patients, and haemoperitoneum due to tumour rupture can also be rarely seen {6548120; 30500284}. Patients may have elevated serum levels of inhibin {17306349}.

Epidemiology

AGCT is a rare ovarian tumour that can occur at any age but is most common in perimenopausal women aged 50–55 years {34027996}. It is the most common malignant sex cord stromal tumour and accounts for about 1–4% of all ovarian cancers {39740818}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Nearly all AGCTs harbour a recurrent somatic FOXL2 missense mutation NM_023067.4:c.402C>G p.(Cys134Trp) {19516027; 20693978; 26033501; 39615884}; however, the mechanism of tumour formation remains unknown {14736745; 21763750}. TERT promoter mutation may occur and is reported to be more frequent in recurrent/metastatic tumours {39740818}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours are unilateral, with an average size of 100 mm. They are typically solid and cystic but may be solid or rarely entirely cystic. The solid areas are usually soft and tan to yellow. Haemorrhage is common {6548120}.

Histopathology

AGCT commonly exhibits a variety of architectural patterns, often more than one admixed together. The most common is a diffuse pattern. Other common patterns include corded, trabecular, nested (insular), and microfollicular. In some tumours, Call–Exner bodies are present, consisting of tumour cells arranged around a small space containing eosinophilic (or rarely basophilic) material. Less common patterns include gyriform and watered silk (undulating rows and cords), macrofollicular, macrocystic, tubular, and pseudopapillary growth {29132723; 40326456}.

A variable amount of fibromatous stroma is present {29708950}. In AGCT, the granulosa cell component is usually dominant and makes up > 50% of the tumour. Cases of AGCT in which the granulosa cell component represents < 10% of the neoplasm are classified as fibroma with minor sex cord elements {6315609}. The classification of tumours in which the stromal component ranges from 10% to 50% is somewhat controversial, and they are sometimes classified as granulosa-theca cell tumours; but this is not a generally accepted term, and these are recommended to be classified as AGCT with a prominent fibromatous component rather than as true mixed tumours. FOXL2 mutation is present in some of these cases, but the evidence for classifying these tumours on the basis of FOXL2 mutation status is limited {28319575}. Some AGCTs are predominantly spindled, closely mimicking a cellular fibroma {17717430}; others are predominantly cystic, typically lined by multilayered granulosa cells with various architectural patterns and often with nests of tumour cells within the stroma surrounding the cyst wall {6548120}.

Tumour cells usually have uniform, pale, round to oval nuclei with an irregular nuclear membrane and variable numbers of nuclear grooves, and scant cytoplasm. Mitotic activity is usually low. However, rare AGCTs exhibit foci of marked cytological atypia and high mitotic count sometimes with atypical mitoses (referred to as high-grade transformation); in some cases these foci exhibit mutation-type p53 immunohistochemical expression {31162286}. A component resembling juvenile granulosa cell tumour may be present in some cases of high-grade transformation, and these should not be reported as mixed AGCT and juvenile granulosa cell tumour.

Some AGCTs contain tumour cells with moderate to abundant pale eosinophilic cytoplasm, referred to as luteinized AGCT {21804396; 7814191} or thecoma-like AGCT {29708950}. This overall morphological appearance with abundant cytoplasm and the relative lack of nuclear grooves may resemble thecoma if no areas of classic AGCT are present. However, extensive tissue sampling may reveal focal areas of cords or other architectural patterns and less luteinized granulosa cells, especially at the tumour periphery. Rare findings in AGCT include bizarre nuclei (not associated with mitotic activity) and heterologous elements such as mucinous epithelium, adipocytes, and hepatocyes {6309684; 10478669; 7680545; 15968196; 21623197; 36563290}.

Reticulin stain highlights reticulin fibres in the stroma around the nests of tumour cells, but the individual tumour cells do not exhibit complete pericellular reticulin fibres. The reticulin stain is either completely absent or markedly reduced around individual tumour cells. In contrast, complete pericellular reticulin fibres are expected in fibroma and thecoma {28700438}. Therefore, reticulin staining patterns that do not show unequivocally intact pericellular staining or unequivocal absence of pericellular staining should trigger concern for AGCT and consideration for FOXL2 mutation testing.

Tumours are typically positive for sex cord markers FOXL2, inhibin, SF1, and calretinin {21378549; 19033865; 23240671; 38904760; 19033865; 38904760}, although staining may vary in distribution. EMA is typically negative {12808064}.

Cytology

Smears are cellular and composed of cells with monomorphic small round nuclei with intranuclear grooves and microfollicle formation. Stripped similar nuclei are seen in the background. Call–Exner bodies can be seen in some cases and constitute an additional diagnostic clue {18418853}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A FOXL2 p.(Cys134Trp) point mutation is present in > 95% of cases {31846522; 39492459}. Demonstration of this mutation may be helpful in selected cases with equivocal morphological features and equivocal reticulin stain results. However, rare cases with classical morphology of AGCT do not harbour a FOXL2 mutation. Predominantly cystic variants of AGCT and AGCTs with a prominent fibromatous stromal component may exhibit false negative FOXL2 mutation testing perhaps due to low cellularity {35353158}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour predominantly composed of granulosa cells with pale nuclei, often with nuclear grooves, growing in a variety of patterns, with a variable fibromatous component.

Desirable: complete or partial absence of pericellular reticulin fibres around individual tumour cells; immunohistochemical positivity for sex cord markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most patients present with stage I disease, which is associated with a 10-year survival rate of about 90–95% and a recurrence rate of about 10–15%; the overall recurrence rate for all stages combined is 20–30%. Tumours have a propensity for late recurrence, typically > 5 years later, but in some cases after 20 years or more {26003143; 27297428}. Peritoneum, omentum, liver, and lung are common sites of extraovarian spread. Tumour stage is the most important prognostic factor, and tumour rupture in stage I tumours is particularly important {18663055}. Tumour morphology, mitotic activity, and nuclear atypia are not independent prognostic factors of survival {22019525; 25347095; 27297428; 27729108}. For tumours with extraovarian spread, the presence of residual tumour after surgery appears to be significantly associated with recurrence {22019525}. Patients may be followed for recurrence by monitoring of serum inhibin levels {17306349}. High-grade transformation may be associated with aggressive behaviour {31162286}.
