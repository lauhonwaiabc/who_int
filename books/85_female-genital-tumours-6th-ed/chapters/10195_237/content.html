10.1.9.5 Uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumour

Definition

Uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumour is a uterine neoplasm with morphological patterns that resemble those seen in ovarian sex cord tumours, without a component of endometrial stromal neoplasia.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are typically located in the uterine corpus or, rarely, cervix.

Clinical features

These tumours typically occur in middle-aged women, who usually present with abnormal vaginal bleeding or pelvic pain, but the tumours can also be an incidental finding {28656712; 961630; 19542872; 25150955; 36306239}.

Epidemiology

These are rare neoplasms, accounting for < 1% of uterine mesenchymal tumours. The tumours affect both premenopausal and postmenopausal women, and case series report a broad age distribution with a mean age of 48 years (range: 12–86 years) {38276058; 30273195; 28656712}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is uncertain. Rearrangements involving either ESR1 or GREB1 have been reported, with a variety of partners, most commonly NCOA1, NCOA2, and NCOA3, although less common fusion partners with CTNNB1, NR4A3, CITED2, and SS18 have been described {30350331; 30273195; 31094921; 31464709; 39869077; 38000680; 39265954}. ESR1-rearranged tumours tend to occur in younger women than GREB1-rearranged tumours {40150134; 31094921}.

Uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumours do not harbour the molecular alterations seen in low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma (JAZF1::SUZ12), adult granulosa cell tumour (FOXL2 alterations), or Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour (DICER1 alterations) {19542872; 26598979; 25581731; 31464709}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically intramural and/or submucosal, forming a polypoid mass with an average size of 60 mm (range: 4–200 mm) {36306239}. They commonly display well-circumscribed margins and have a predominantly solid, variably soft to firm, and yellow or tan cut surface.

Histopathology

These tumours are typically well circumscribed, but some have an irregular edge with entrapment of surrounding smooth muscle. They grow in sheets, cords, nests, insulae, trabeculae, or tubules, sometimes with a retiform appearance {31464709}, although the proportion of the tumour showing histologically apparent sex cord–like differentiation can vary between tumours. Cells have scant to abundant cytoplasm and may be pale (including foamy) or eosinophilic, sometimes resulting in a rhabdoid appearance. In the majority of cases, nuclei are ovoid and show minimal cytological atypia, and mitotic activity is low. A subset of the tumours can display prominent cytological atypia, brisk mitotic activity, necrosis, and vascular invasion, which are histological features that have been associated with malignant behaviour {36306239; 25150955}. Tumours have a polyphenotypic immunohistochemical profile, with variable positivity for sex cord markers (inhibin, calretinin, WT1, CD56, CD99, SF1, FOXL2, and melan-A), epithelial markers, ER, PR, smooth muscle markers (actin, desmin, and h-caldesmon), and CD10 {17182656; 21084963; 26598979; 16118629; 25581731; 27311867}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of ESR1 or GREB1 rearrangements may be helpful in some cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sex cord patterns without a component of endometrial stromal tumour.

Desirable: immunoreactivity for sex cord stromal markers; detection of GREB1 or ESR1 fusions and absence of recurrent low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma rearrangements.

Staging

This entity is staged as a uterine sarcoma using the FIGO, Union for International Cancer Control (UICC), and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Uterine tumours resembling ovarian sex cord tumours are benign in most cases but should be considered to have a low malignant potential because they may recur in 6–23% of cases {28656712; 25150955; 36306239}. In one series, tumours associated with malignant behaviour had more than three of the following features: moderate cytological atypia, necrosis, infiltrative borders, size > 50 mm, or > 1.27 mitoses/mm2 {36306239}. Extensive rhabdoid morphology may also be associated with aggressive disease {32675660}. Tumours with GREB1 fusions are larger and have increased mitotic activity, more frequent lymphovascular space invasion, and shorter disease-free survival compared with tumours with ESR1 fusions {37132508; 40150134}.
