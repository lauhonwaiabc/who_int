8.6.2.1 Seromucinous borderline tumour

Definition

Seromucinous borderline tumour (SMBT) is an architecturally complex papillary neoplasm composed of an admixture of Müllerian-type epithelia, lacking gastrointestinal-type differentiation and without confluent or destructive invasion.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: endocervical-type mucinous borderline tumour; Müllerian mucinous borderline tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary and are bilateral in as many as 40% of cases {3334969; 12459620}. Peritoneal implants occur in a small subset of cases {3334969}, and rare lymph node involvement has been reported {3334969}.

Clinical features

Patients with these tumours typically present with symptoms related to a pelvic mass, or the tumour may be an incidental finding on imaging or during surgery for other indications.

Epidemiology

SMBTs are uncommon ovarian neoplasms {3338022; 3334969}. Patients with these tumours present over a wide age range (mean age: 34–39 years) {3338022; 3334969; 12459620; 15371946}. A significant number of patients have associated endometriosis, most commonly an ipsilateral or contralateral ovarian endometriotic cyst or endometriosis at other sites (31–71% of cases) {12459620; 15371946; 3338022; 20955384; 32023964}.

Etiology

Many SMBTs arise from endometriotic cysts, with transition from endometriotic to neoplastic epithelium documented {15371946; 20955384}.

Pathogenesis

SMBT shows frequent somatic mutations in KRAS (69 – 100%), PIK3CA (60.7%), and ARID1A (14.3%) {32616873; 20955384}. Loss of ARID1A expression (via immunohistochemistry, a surrogate for ARID1A mutation) has been reported in one third of SMBTs, a frequency similar to that seen in endometrioid neoplasms, supporting their close relationship {22653341}. The DNA mismatch repair pathway is proficient {32616873}. In cases of bilateral SMBT, shared somatic mutations suggest a clonal relationship {32616873}.

Macroscopic appearance

SMBTs are usually confined to the ovary, typically with a smooth external surface {15371946}, although capsular involvement may occur {20955384}. As many as 40% are bilateral {3334969; 12459620}. The mean tumour size ranges from 70 to 100 mm, and the tumours typically comprise a unilocular or paucilocular cyst with friable papillary excrescences involving the inner cyst lining and mucoid cyst contents {15371946;12459620; 32023964}. Solid areas are occasionally seen.

Histopathology

SMBTs are composed of papillae exhibiting hierarchical branching, with variably oedematous or fibrous stromal cores {3338022; 3334969; 12459620; 15371946}. Larger luminal papillae tend to have an oedematous stroma containing conspicuous neutrophils that also infiltrate the epithelium; mixed chronic inflammatory cells may also be present. The epithelium lining the papillae is cytologically low-grade, stratified, and often tufted. It represents an admixture of Müllerian cell types in varying proportions, including endocervical-type mucinous, ciliated/serous, endometrioid, hobnail, clear, squamous, and indifferent cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Sometimes, mucinous differentiation greatly predominates. Mitotic activity is variable and necrosis may be present {12459620}. Microscopic foci of invasion have been described {12459620; 15371946}. Malignant transformation may occur and usually has an endometrioid phenotype; rarely clear cell carcinoma may arise in association with SMBT {33741765}. A portion of the cyst lining may exhibit features of an endometriotic cyst, and a component of benign serous or mucinous cystadenoma is often present {15371946; 20955384}. The epithelial cells are generally positive with PAX8, ER, PR, CK7 and negative with WT1 and CK20. p53 exhibits wildtype staining {26598986; 20955384; 33963606}.

The previously recognized malignant counterpart of this tumour, seromucinous carcinoma, has been removed from the fifth and sixth editions of the WHO classification because of a lack of evidence supporting its existence as a distinct entity. Integrating pathological and molecular findings, seromucinous carcinoma can be assigned to one of the other major tumour categories; most cases are reclassified as endometrioid carcinoma and less frequently as low-grade serous carcinoma or rarely mucinous carcinoma {28125452}. Clinicopathological and molecular overlap also exists between borderline seromucinous and endometrioid tumours.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: papillae exhibiting hierarchical branching, with oedematous and fibrous stromal cores lined by an admixture of Müllerian cell types exhibiting proliferation; absence of confluent or destructive invasion.

Desirable: evidence of endometriosis.

Staging

SMBT is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is excellent, with most cases presenting as FIGO stage 1. Local tumour recurrence may occur, particularly if treated by cystectomy alone {20955384}. Aggressive behaviour has not been reported in well-documented cases {12459620; 15371946}.
