9.2.1.8 Steroid cell tumour

Definition

Steroid cell tumour is a pure ovarian stromal tumour composed of steroid cells.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: lipid cell tumour; lipoid cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized to the ovary.

Clinical features

Tumours are usually unilateral but 2–5% are bilateral {2823622; 38512100}. Clinical presentations include symptoms related to an ovarian mass and/or endocrine manifestations, although a significant subset of patients exhibit no symptoms related to their tumour and are incidentally detected {37796179; 2823622}. Patients with endocrine manifestations typically show symptoms related to androgenic excess, although estrogenic symptoms or symptoms related to cortisol excess are present in smaller subsets {2823622; 38512100}. Occasional steroid cell tumours have been reported in patients with von Hippel–Lindau syndrome {37421226; 31433374; 36106071; 20664446; 30700659}.

Epidemiology

The median patient age in the largest series of cases reported for this rare tumour was 55 years (range: 9–84 years), and 77% of patients were ≥ 35 years old {38512100; 37796179}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There are no known pathognomonic and recurrent molecular alterations {38352891}. Genomic alterations are relatively infrequent and may be present in clinically benign and clinically malignant cases {37796179}. Next-generation sequencing has identified pathogenic variants in hypoxia-related genes including HIF1A, VHL, SDHB, SRC, IDH2, and FOXO4 in steroid cell tumours, but none are known to be recurrent {37655726}. Other non-recurring molecular aberrations that have been reported in individual cases involve the LHCGR, HOXA13, CTNNB1, CASP10, BAP1, MDM2, ATRX, FH, and TP53 genes {37655726; 37796179}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumour size varies substantially, but a steroid cell tumour should form a distinct nodule or mass. Mean tumour diameters in the three largest series ranged from 48 to 84 mm {37655726; 38512100; 2823622}, and median diameters ranged from 18 to 30 mm {37655726; 38512100}. 62% of cases are ≤ 40 mm {37655726; 38512100}. The cut surface is solid or solid to cystic and may show areas of haemorrhage and necrosis; solid areas are typically yellow, orange, red, brown, or black {2823622; 37655726; 38512100}.

Histopathology

These tumours are located within the ovarian parenchyma, are typically well circumscribed, and display an expansile and diffuse growth of large polygonal cells; however, nests, cords, and pseudoglandular and follicle-like arrangements of tumour cells may also be seen. Stroma ranges from scant to prominent, with fibrous bands, and (rarely) areas morphologically in keeping with fibroma {29369920}. The tumour cells have abundant cytoplasm that ranges from eosinophilic (lipid-poor) to pale and vacuolated (lipid rich). Hypercellular areas comprised of cells with less cytoplasm are occasionally present {38512100; 29369920}. The nuclei are typically round, with a variably prominent central nucleolus; substantial nuclear atypia and necrosis are sometimes present, usually accompanied by increased mitotic activity. Variable amounts of intracytoplasmic lipochrome pigment can be seen. Uncommon findings include stromal calcification, hyalinization, adipocytic metaplasia, cytoplasmic granules, and hyaline globules {37655726}. In some steroid cell tumours, in particular small tumours, stromal hyperthecosis may be seen in the adjacent ovarian stroma and contralateral ovary {2823622}. Immunohistochemically, steroid cell tumours are variably positive for sex cord stromal markers such as inhibin {9688187}, calretinin {11603218}, SF1 {19033865}, and melan-A {20567142}, but they are usually negative for FOXL2 {21378549} and WT1 {19033865}. Cytoplasmic or membranous expression of less specific markers may be seen, including CD10 {17885484}, SATB2 {37668387}, CD56 {17581419), and CD99 {19033865}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour consisting entirely of cells with steroid-secreting morphology involving the ovarian parenchyma.

Desirable: positive immunohistochemical staining for sex cord markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

The recurrence rates after excision in two recent series were 6.1% and 15%, and the rates of disease-specific deaths were 3.5% and 6.1% {37655726; 38512100}. Approximately 95% of tumours are FIGO stage I and 90% are FIGO stage IA at diagnosis {37655726; 38512100}. Most recurrences and deaths occur in patients whose tumours are originally FIGO stage I {37655726; 38512100}, and recurrences may occur many years after primary resection {2823622; 38512100}. Prediction of malignant behaviour on the basis of clinical and pathological features is difficult. Factors that potentially predict outcomes are nonspecific since they may be seen in both clinically benign and clinically malignant cases, although the presence of several such factors in a single tumour is more likely to be encountered in malignant cases {37655726; 38512100}. Such factors include size > 70 mm, mitotic activity greater than 1 mitoses /mm2 (equating to > 2 mitoses / 10 HPF 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area), necrosis, haemorrhage, significant nuclear atypia, older age, and FIGO stage > IA {2823622; 37655726; 38512100}.
