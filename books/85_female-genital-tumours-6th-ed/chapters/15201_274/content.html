15.2.0.1 Junctional, compound, and dermal naevi (formerly: acquired melanocytic naevus)

Definition

Melanocytic naevi are acquired benign clonal proliferations of melanocytes with features similar to those found in extragenital skin or mucosa.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: common acquired naevi; banal naevi.

Not recommended: naevocellular naevus.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Melanocytic naevi usually affect the labia majora, labia minora, and clitoris, and less frequently the perineum or mons {1606765}. Melanocytic naevi arising in a background of lichen sclerosus have been reported mainly in mucosal areas such as the labia minora and clitoris {11790170; 15097951; 24018819}.

Clinical features

Naevi usually appear during infancy or in individuals of childbearing age, occurring as well-defined flat or papular pigmented lesions. Accelerated growth may be observed during pregnancy. Melanocytic naevi usually present as single, tan or brown lesions that are uniform in colour, symmetrical, and well-demarcated with regular borders, measuring as large as 5–6 mm in diameter {2298946}. Vulvar mucosal melanocytic naevi are usually flat-topped or macular, whereas naevi in hair-bearing skin tend to be dome-shaped, flat-topped, or (less commonly) macular.

Epidemiology

Melanocytic neoplasms in the genitalia account for 10–12% of vulvar pigmented lesions, with melanocytic naevi representing 2.3% of these lesions {2298946; 1606765}. Dermal naevi predominate {3597916}. Among children and adolescents, the incidence is 3.5% {24373784}, with a median patient age at onset of 5.5 years. In adults, vulvar melanocytic naevi are generally found in women in the reproductive age group {28778635; 25267379}. They are frequently an incidental finding during self-examination or a medical examination for another reason (pregnancy, etc.).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

BRAF NM_004333.4:c.1799T>A p.(Val600Glu) (BRAF V600E) mutations have been detected {24842760}.

Macroscopic appearance

Acquired melanocytic naevi are homogeneously pigmented lesions that are typically solitary, well-circumscribed and symmetrical, and can be flat, papular, or plaque-like. They are typically < 10 mm in size [[10.1016/j.mpdhp.2023.10.003]]}.

Histopathology

Similar to naevi occurring elsewhere, genital naevi are composed of variable proportions of junctional and/or subepithelial/dermal components. Regularly sized and distributed melanocytic nests and single cells in the epidermis characterize junctional melanocytic naevus. In dermal melanocytic naevus, dermal melanocytes are seen displaying zonal maturation as the melanocytes become smaller and more spindled and lose pigment with depth. Lesions displaying both described junctional and subepithelial/dermal components are classified as compound melanocytic naevi. Immunohistochemical stains are often not needed for diagnosis; however, melanocytic markers such as S100, SOX10, and melan-A (MART1) are positive. HMB45 demonstrates a top-heavy staining profile in most cases. Immunohistochemistry for PRAME is typically negative or only a minority of melanocytes show immunoreactivity in naevi including those arising in the genital area {38085951}.

Melanocytic naevi superimposed on lichen sclerosus can share some architectural features (i.e. confluent growth pattern and impression of pagetoid growth pattern in atrophic epithelium or epidermis) with recurrent naevi and melanoma. However, they are sharply demarcated and symmetrical, and pagetoid spread (if present) is rather focal. The trizonal pattern (confluent growth of predominantly single cells in the epidermis with dermal sclerosis and an underlying dermal naevus) and dermal melanocytic maturation can be seen. No mitotic figures are expected, and the Ki-67 proliferation index is < 5%. HMB45 in the dermal component is seen in the nests entrapped in the sclerosis. {11790170}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed, typically small, symmetrical lesion composed of naevus cells in nests, strands, or single-cell patterns with evidence of maturation (compound and intradermal/stromal).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most naevi have a completely indolent course, and recurrence after excision is rare. Tumour progression of genital nevi to malignant melanoma is rare.
