6.2.0.1 Wolffian tumour of the broad ligament and other uterine ligaments

Definition

Wolffian tumour is a tumour of presumed mesonephric (Wolffian) origin.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: female adnexal tumour of probable Wolffian origin.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most arise in the broad ligament {4693596; 10414506; 31591497; 38919071}, with rare tumours developing in the ovary {6859387}, fallopian tube {30134342}, or non-adnexal sites {6624720; 8135182; 15512055; 30967956}.

Clinical features

Patients range in age from 19 to 83 years (mean: 45 years) and may present with abdominopelvic pain or abnormal bleeding; however, many cases are incidental findings {4693596; 10414506}.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare, with < 200 cases reported in the English literature, mainly in middle-aged women {39949601}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

A Wolffian origin is favoured because these tumours arise along the path of the regressing Wolffian system.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are often well circumscribed and solid or solid and cystic, ranging in size from 8 to 300 mm (mean: 60–100 mm) {4693596; 10414506}. Haemorrhage, necrosis, or focal calcification may be seen.

Histopathology

The tumours are composed of an admixture of hollow to solid tubules, sieve-like cysts, and diffuse growth {4693596; 10414506; 31591497; 38919071}. Hyalinized bands may be prominent (imparting a nodular appearance) or delicate and interspersed within the stroma. Cells are cuboidal to ovoid to occasionally spindled, with scant cytoplasm, small nuclei, and inconspicuous to small nucleoli. PAS-positive eosinophilic luminal secretions may be present; cytological atypia and mitoses are usually minimal.

Tumours show variable expression of sex cord stromal, epithelial, and mesothelial markers, hormone receptors, and CD10; but they are usually negative for EMA, claudin-4, PAX8, GATA3, and TTF1 {10414506; 26135559; 26352548; 30134342; 38919071}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Wolffian tumours lack recurring genomic alterations {30134342; 31591497}. In challenging cases, molecular analysis may be helpful to exclude morphological mimickers including mesonephric/mesonephric-like carcinoma (KRAS/NRAS), STK11 adnexal tumour (STK11), sex cord stromal tumours (FOXL2, DICER1), endometrioid carcinoma (PTEN, PIK3CA, ARID1A), and peritoneal mesothelioma (BAP1, CDKN2A, NF2).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: typically broad ligament origin; tubular, sieve-like, and/or solid growth.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Wolffian tumours are usually benign. Rare recurrences have been reported {6859387; 8135638; 38919071}, some of which were associated with incomplete excision {33787642} or iatrogenic dissemination {40062785}. Many tumours previously classified as malignant probably represent morphological mimickers {35395118}.
