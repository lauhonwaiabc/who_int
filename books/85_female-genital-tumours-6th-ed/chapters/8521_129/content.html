8.5.2.1 Clear cell carcinoma

Definition

Clear cell carcinoma (CCC) is composed of clear, eosinophilic, and hobnail cells, with tubulocystic, papillary, and solid architecture.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovary

Clinical features

Patients typically present with symptoms related to a pelvic mass, or the mass may be an incidental finding. CCC is the ovarian carcinoma most commonly associated with paraneoplastic hypercalcaemia {19745599} and venous thromboembolism {17014897}.

Epidemiology

CCC has a higher prevalence in Asia (25–30% in Japan) {30905436} than in North America, where it accounts for 10–12% of ovarian carcinomas {20407318; 29718305}. The mean patient age is 56 years {30062862}. Endometriosis is an important risk factor (odds ratio: 3.05; 95% CI: 2.43–3.84) {22361336}. Lynch syndrome is a predisposition, but not BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline mutations {18469706; 25025451; 30967419}. For more comprehensive information on Lynch syndrome, refer to section Lynch syndrome (MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, MSH6). Menarche after the age of 15 years, menopause before the age of 40 years, tubal ligation, parity, hysterectomy, and oral contraceptive use lower the risk {27325851}. The genetic susceptibility locus, HNF1B, when expressed, has been proposed to lower risk through epigenetic mechanisms {23535649}.

Etiology

Endometriosis is found in 55–74% of CCCs {26813747; 31335355; 36857998}. Age-related (40%) and APOBEC (26%) mutation signatures predominate {28436987; 39543535}.

Pathogenesis

About 50% of cases harbour loss-of-function mutations in ARID1A, which is often concurrent with loss of ARID1A expression by immunohistochemistry {20942669; 20826764; 29659191; 35816189; 36857998; 39815187}. PIK3CA mutations are common and frequently occur with ARID1A mutations {19349352; 22157930; 26937756; 35816189; 36857998; 39815187}. TERT promoter mutations (16–21%) {24338723; 35816189}, TP53 mutations (14–18%) {35816189; 36857998; 39815187}, KRAS mutations (8–14%) {35816189, 36857998}, ERBB2 amplification (1–6%) {36857998; 38029652}, mismatch repair deficiency (0–6%) {26799366; 31335355; 30967419; 26813747}, and POLE mutations (0–3%) {36857998; 39815187} are uncommon. Genomic, transcriptomic, and epigenetic studies suggest that there are two major molecular subgroups of CCC that may correlate with disease features and outcome {31761620; 34697060; 35816189}.

Macroscopic appearance

CCCs are typically unilateral, with a mean size of 130 mm (wide range). The cut surface varies from solid to solid and cystic to mainly cystic, with fleshy, pale-yellow nodules protruding into an endometriotic cyst.

Histopathology

CCC displays tubulocystic, papillary, and solid architecture, frequently admixed {21164285; 22885379; 26813747}. The tubulocystic pattern exhibits tubules and cysts, sometimes with dense eosinophilic secretions, lined by a single layer of hobnail, cuboidal, or attenuated and sometimes deceptively benign-appearing cells. The papillary pattern comprises simple, non-branching papillae with round stromal cores seen in cross-section; some hierarchical branching is permitted. Cells are cuboidal and/or hobnail (but not columnar), arranged in a single layer with only minimal stratification. Stromal hyalinization and myxoid stroma are frequent {18223330; 21164285}. The solid pattern exhibits sheets of polyhedral cells separated by delicate septa. The cytoplasm ranges from clear to (less commonly) eosinophilic (the oxyphilic pattern). The glycogen-rich clear cytoplasm is PAS-positive and diastase-sensitive, with only rare diastase-resistant mucin along apical cell membranes {22885379} or within the cytoplasm. This may impart a signet-ring appearance (so-called targetoid cells). Nuclei are large and monomorphic, with rounded to angulated contours, hyperchromasia, prominent nucleoli, and (rarely) nuclear pseudoinclusions. Nuclear pleomorphism may be present focally or in scattered cells but is typically not diffuse. Mitotic activity is variable but usually low. Hyaline and psammoma bodies are infrequent. A peritumoural and/or diffuse lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate may be present. CCC may be mixed with other carcinomas, particularly endometrioid carcinoma (see section Mixed carcinoma), and (rarely) a component of somatically derived yolk sac tumour or choriocarcinoma may be present {21804392; 20881859; 32404953} (see section Yolk sac tumour and section Non-gestational choriocarcinoma).

CCCs are typically positive for PAX8, napsin A, HNF1β, and AMACR and negative for WT1, ER, and PR {18830127; 25551297; 26974996; 29901523; 25871622; 34534137; 36857998}.

Cytology

There are cells with abundant, pale-staining, finely granular, and vacuolated cytoplasm and eosinophilic, globular, and hyaline extracellular substance with formation of so-called raspberry bodies {11127728; 26932246;29956857}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tubulocystic, papillary, and solid architectural patterns with flat or cuboidal cells (clear, eosinophilic, hobnail) that have uniform but malignant nuclear features.

Staging

CCC is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Stage is most important prognostically {25065903}. Most CCCs are limited to the ovary (FIGO stage I) {29587661; 29718305; 31273067; 35816189}, with 69–79% confined to the pelvis (stage I–II). The cause-specific 5-year survival rates are 87%, 70%, and 24%, respectively, for stage IA/IB, IC/II, and III/IV ovarian cancers {29718305}; with rigorous pathology review, the 5-year survival rate is 92% for FIGO stage I disease {25065903}. TP53 mutations are associated with a worse overall survival {35816189; 36857998; 39815187}. Grading is not applicable. With regard to predictive biomarkers, the PDL1, the PDL1 combined positive score (CPS) is > 1 in approximately 50% of tumours {36857998}. FOLR1 is positive in 0–8% {38914019; 40150910}.
