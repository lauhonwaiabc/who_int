10.1.3.2 Adenomyoma

Definition

Adenomyoma is a tumour composed of an admixture of morphologically benign endometrioid-type glands, endometrioid-type stroma, and smooth muscle. Adenomyomas of endocervical type are composed of endocervical-type glands, rather than endometrioid glands and stroma, surrounded by smooth muscle.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: endometrioid-type adenomyoma; polypoid adenomyoma; adenomyoma of endocervical type; endocervical-type adenomyoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized predominantly in the uterine corpus and occasionally in the uterine cervix, broad ligament, or ovary {37688388}. They may be polypoid, intramural, or serosal-based lesions {8685218; 16990713; 19047912; 30339971; 10907166}.

Clinical features

Adenomyomas occur over a wide age range. Most patients present with abnormal vaginal bleeding.

Epidemiology

This benign tumour affects as many as 20.9% of women of reproductive age attending gynaecology clinics, and a cohort study reported an overall incidence of adenomyosis of 1% in a population of women aged 16–60 years. The incidence peaked in women aged 40–45 years {31954156; 23001775}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Some cases are associated with underlying adenomyosis and represent a localized form of adenomyosis; in one study, adenomyosis was present in 30.8% of cases {16990713}. One study found that these tumours lacked mutations in exon 2 of MED12 (characteristic of uterine leiomyomas) {30339971}.

Macroscopic appearance

In one large series, the tumours measured 3–170 mm in maximum dimension {10907166}. They are nodular or polypoid tumours with a predominantly solid white whorled cut surface, sometimes with small cysts. They can be submucosal or subserosal but are more commonly intramural.

Histopathology

Adenomyomas of the uterine corpus, broad ligament, or ovary are nodules or polyps composed of glands and cysts lined predominantly by bland endometrioid-type epithelium surrounded by endometrioid-type stroma and smooth muscle, with smooth muscle typically predominating {10907166; 16990713}. Various metaplasias may occur within the epithelial component, including tubal, mucinous, and squamous. The endometrioid-type stroma surrounds the glands but may be inconspicuous throughout much of the lesion. The smooth muscle component is typically morphologically bland without increased cellularity, often with a component of thick-walled blood vessels, but occasionally it is cellular, has an epithelioid appearance, or contains occasional bizarre nuclei without mitotic activity {10907166; 16990713; 23926192}. It is not uncommon for a minor component of smooth muscle to be present in the stroma of endometrial polyps, and these cases should not be termed “adenomyomas”. Immunohistochemically, the endometrioid-type stromal component is typically positive with CD10, and the smooth muscle component with desmin, SMA, and h-caldesmon. SATB2 may be positive in the stromal component of these lesions {38833724}. The less common adenomyomas arising in the cervix (endocervical-type adenomyomas) are predominantly or exclusively composed of endocervical-type mucinous glandular epithelium, consisting of cells with intracellular mucin and basal nuclei, without associated endometrioid stroma; these glands are surrounded by a stromal component containing bland smooth muscle {8685218; 25219586 }. There may be a minor component of tubal and endometrioid-type glands.

Cytology

There is no substantial literature on cytopathological features of an adenomyoma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mass composed of endometrioid-type glands, endometrioid-type stroma, and smooth muscle or, less commonly, endocervical-type glands and smooth muscle.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign, and patients have uneventful outcomes after hysterectomy, myomectomy, polypectomy, or surgical removal {10907166}.
