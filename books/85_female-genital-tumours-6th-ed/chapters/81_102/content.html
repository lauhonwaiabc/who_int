8.1 Epithelial tumours of the ovary: Introduction

This introduction summarizes changes made to the classification of ovarian epithelial tumours since the publication of the fifth edition of Female genital tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series.

Tumour-like lesions have been removed from this chapter. Most tumour-like lesions of the ovary derive from non-epithelial structures and are therefore listed in <<54969>>Table 9.XXX in section Non-epithelial tumours of the ovary: Introduction. Endometriosis is included where relevant in individual sections of this chapter, for example in the discussion of endometrioid and clear cell carcinomas.

Serous surface papilloma has been deleted from the benign serous tumours section. Where a papillary serous lesion is present on the ovarian surface, the possibility of a serous borderline tumour should be considered.

The classification of small foci of invasion in the context of serous borderline ovarian tumours has been modified, with the recommendation that the terms “microinvasion” and “microinvasive” are not used. The terminology for extraovarian disease associated with serous borderline tumours is also clarified, with “low-grade serous carcinoma” preferred to “invasive implant” for invasive disease. This preference was expressed in both the fourth and fifth editions of the WHO Classification of Tumours series and is underscored in this edition, with the term “invasive implant” discouraged.

Although it is recognized that the large majority of high-grade serous carcinomas (HGSCs) arise in the fallopian tube {26827965}, HGSC often presents as an ovarian/adnexal mass. HGSC is therefore included in the chapters for both fallopian tube tumours (Chapter 7: Tumours of the fallopian tube) and ovarian epithelial tumours (this chapter), but with the content harmonized for consistency. Criteria for the assignment of primary site in extrauterine HGSCs are summarized in <<56078>>Table 8.XXX {24660659; 26126051; 26089092; 26827965; 26977579}. The impact of the reclassification of many HGSCs as primary fallopian tube carcinomas on disease epidemiology remains to be established.

The classification of small foci of invasion in the context of mucinous borderline ovarian tumours has also been modified, again with the recommendation that the terms “microinvasion” and “microinvasive” are not used. This is supported by the recommendation that a pattern-based grading system is used for mucinous carcinoma, as unfavourable outcome is associated with a high proportion of infiltrative/destructive invasion (> 10% of total carcinomatous areas) {31782197; 35363158; 38155475}.

The classification of mural nodules in the context of a mucinous ovarian tumour has been clarified, with clearer separation of reactive-type nodules and malignant nodules, typically anaplastic carcinoma or sarcoma, which should be classified according to their malignant component. Teratoma-associated mucinous carcinomas should be considered somatic tumours arising from teratomas and classified as such see Chapter 9: Non-epithelial tumours of the ovary {33963606}.

Endometrioid cystadenoma has been deleted, as it overlaps with, and cannot be reliably distinguished from, an endometriotic cyst. Benign and borderline clear cell tumours have been combined into a single section in view of the overlap between these two entities. Following the deletion of seromucinous carcinoma from the classification in the fifth edition, the text has now been removed, with reference to this previous entity included in the section on endometrioid carcinoma.
