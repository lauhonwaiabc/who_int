9.4.0.1 Ovarian myxoma

Definition

Ovarian myxoma is a benign tumour with an abundant myxoid matrix that histologically resembles intramuscular myxoma of soft tissue.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovary

Clinical features

Patients typically present with nonspecific symptoms and signs related to a pelvic/abdominal mass, although some cases are discovered incidentally on imaging or following oophorectomy {2032767}.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare. They typically occur in women of reproductive age {23722509; 2032767}; however, no population-based data have been published so far, and the epidemiology of the tumour remains largely unstudied.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is uncertain. It has been postulated that ovarian myxomas may be related to the fibroma / thecoma / sclerosing stromal tumour categories, because some of these ovarian stromal tumours exhibit small areas of myxomatous stroma resembling myxomas {7688213; 25083960}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range in size but are typically < 100 mm and show a mucoid/gelatinous cut surface, sometimes punctated by cysts {2032767}.

Histopathology

Myxomas are intraparenchymal and are typically well circumscribed, although not encapsulated. They have abundant background myxoid matrix that displaces follicles and their derivative structures. Cellularity varies from area to area, with ovarian myxomas often being more cellular than their intramuscular counterparts. Spindled to stellate cells are haphazardly arranged and display long, tapering cytoplasmic processes and oval, sometimes indented, bland nuclei with occasional nuclear pseudoinclusions and absent to minimal mitotic activity. Small capillary-type vessels, sometimes with a plexiform arrangement, are typically seen throughout the tumour. Small cysts/pseudocysts and areas of haemorrhage with or without fibrin deposition may be seen.

Ovarian myxomas typically show positive staining with Alcian blue because of the abundant hyaluronic acid in their myxomatous stroma {28840847}. On immunohistochemistry, the tumour cells are typically positive for vimentin and SMA, but they are negative for inhibin, desmin, S100, cytokeratins, and EMA {1996543; 7688213; 2032767}. By electron microscopy, the tumour cells show fibroblastic/myofibroblastic differentiation {1502739}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: paucicellular tumour with extensive myxoid matrix; oval to spindle cells with bland cytological features; abundant capillaries with or without plexiform arrangement.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are benign {2032767}.
