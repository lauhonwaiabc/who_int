4.2.0.2 Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, HPV-independent

Definition

Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN), HPV-independent, is an abnormal intraepithelial squamous proliferation that arises through mechanisms not associated with HPV infection and is a possible precursor lesion of HPV-independent squamous cell carcinoma of the uterine cervix.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: carcinoma in situ; low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (LSIL)-like CIN.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

HPV-independent CIN arises in mature squamous epithelium of the ectocervix or in mature and immature squamous epithelium of the transformation zone, both on the surface and within the crypts.

Clinical features

Cervical HPV-independent CIN is an asymptomatic lesion, identified through cytological screening, or colposcopy by visual inspection, with 3–5% acetic acid.

Epidemiology

This is a newly described entity, and reliable data are not available. Only a few reports comprised of a limited number of patients with HPV-independent CIN have been published. Patients with isolated HPV-independent CIN (without squamous cell carcinoma) were aged 36–42 years and younger than those with HPV-independent squamous cell carcinomas. The majority of lesions, however, have been identified adjacent to HPV-independent invasive squamous cell carcinomas {32354471; 34387215; 37283469; 38959413; 37702216; 40010435; 40347270}.

Etiology

Presently, the etiology is unknown. No risk factors for HPV-independent squamous cell carcinoma and CIN are recognized so far. Peritumoural HPV-independent CIN of the differentiated pattern (see below) was identified in HPV-independent keratinizing squamous cell carcinomas that were diagnosed in association with uterine prolapse {38959413; 37702216}; one patient had a dense lichenoid infiltrate with associated scarring and vaginal stenosis {37283469}.

Pathogenesis

Preliminary data highlight a potential role for mutations in TP53 in the development of the differentiated pattern of HPV-independent CIN. Verrucous/verruciform patterns are typically p53 wild type and may harbour mutations in PIK3CA, EGFR, and SMARCB1. TP53 mutated and TP53 wildtype subtypes have been suggested but are not well established. 

Macroscopic appearance

Some lesions are exophytic and hyperkeratotic proliferations. Other papillary proliferations lack keratinization. Both may resemble condyloma acuminatum macroscopically {40010435; 37283469}. These lesions are visible to the naked eye and at colposcopic examination. Other lesions occur on the surface but also deep inside the crypts and may be difficult to recognize with the naked eye and may need acetic acid for visualization; they may be indistinguishable from high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL) at colposcopic examination. {34387215; 37283469; 38959413}.

Histopathology

Based on a limited number of published cases, the morphology of HPV-independent CIN is heterogeneous, with three patterns described (differentiated, verrucous/verruciform, and basaloid), but the reproducibility of their distinction and their clinical significance is unknown. Koilocytosis, however, is absent in all patterns, although pseudokoilocytosis can occur.

The differentiated pattern is characterized by the presence of thickened squamous epithelium with near normal superficial maturation, sometimes with parakeratosis. The elongated and occasionally anastomosing rete ridges show nuclear atypia and increased mitotic activity restricted to the basal and occasionally suprabasal cell layers. Abrupt premature maturation with dyskeratoses and keratin pearls are typical features. Immunohistochemistry for p16 in the differentiated CIN adjacent to HPV-independent keratinizing squamous cell carcinomas is negative/non–block-type. The atypical basal cells show mutation-type staining with p53 {37283469;38959413;37702216; 40010435; 40347270}.

The verrucous/verruciform pattern of HPV-independent CIN is characterized by an exophytic keratinizing proliferation without significant basal cell nuclear atypia and with minimal or no mitotic activity. Stacks of hyperkeratosis and parakeratosis with loss of the granular cell layer can also be features. Immunohistochemistry for p53 shows a wildtype pattern and p16 is negative/non–block-type {37283469}.

Occasional examples of HPV-independent CIN with a basaloid morphology have been described, particularly when developing in endocervical crypts {34387215; 37702216; 40010435}. This pattern resembles HSIL. However, it usually exhibits negative/non–block-type staining for p16, but occasional block-positive examples have been reported despite them lacking HPV DNA by molecular testing {34387215}. Since HPV molecular testing is not available in all laboratories, more study of the basaloid pattern with unexpected block-type positive p16 staining is needed to establish criteria / ancillary testing by which it can be accurately diagnosed.

Cytology

There are only rare descriptions of cytological findings for HPV-independent CIN. Cervical cytology preparations reveal atypical basal cells, hyperkeratotic squamous cells, or keratin pearls {34387215}. Koilocytes are absent. Notably, this lesion will remain undetectable by cervical cancer prevention programmes based on HPV testing alone. This may also be the case even in programmes based on cervical cytology microscopy as the accuracy is unknown, as is the accuracy of colposcopic examination in identifying a lesion for a targeted biopsy.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV testing, if performed, should be negative.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: one of the following histological patterns: (1) differentiated CIN – nuclear atypia restricted to the basal or suprabasal layers with abrupt premature keratinization; (2) verrucous/verruciform CIN – exophytic keratinized or non-keratinized papillary proliferation with absence of significant atypia; (3) basaloid CIN – undifferentiated basaloid histology. Negative/non–block-type staining for p16.

Desirable: negative HPV testing; in cases of differentiated CIN, mutation-type p53 staining {37283469; 38959413}.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The biological behaviour of HPV-independent CIN and risk of progression of the different patterns are unknown, as the majority of lesions have been detected in association with invasive HPV-independent squamous cell carcinoma.
