3.3.0.2 Fibroepithelial stromal polyp

Definition

Fibroepithelial stromal polyp is a benign polypoid mesenchymal proliferation of the lower female genital tract.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: fibroepithelial polyp; cellular pseudosarcomatous fibroepithelial stromal polyp; pseudosarcoma botryoides; mesodermal stromal polyp; vaginal polyp.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fibroepithelial stromal polyp most commonly occurs in the vagina, less often in the vulva, and rarely in the cervix {3229895; 2294061; 32217091; 10680891; 23136578}.

Clinical features

Fibroepithelial stromal polyps of the vagina can present with bleeding, discomfort, or a prolapsing mass. Approximately half are discovered incidentally {5905466; 2294061; 7260876}. Vulvar lesions present as a slow-growing mass {3229895}. Cervical examples may present with bleeding or be discovered incidentally. Fibroepithelial stromal polyps are usually solitary, but multiple polyps can occur, particularly in pregnancy {10680891}.

Epidemiology

Fibroepithelial stromal polyps most often occur in reproductive-aged women, but they can also arise in postmenopausal women, prepubertal patients, and rarely in infants {5905466; 7260876; 6681596; 2294061; 40230502}. Approximately 15% occur in pregnancy {10680891; 38562389}.

Etiology

The association with pregnancy and occasional association with tamoxifen therapy suggest a hormonal influence {10680891}.

Pathogenesis

Fibroepithelial stromal polyp is thought to arise from hormone-responsive subepithelial stromal cells of the lower female genital tract {4683156; 2294061}. RB1 inactivation has been proposed as a pathogenetic mechanism, although data are conflicting {22790852; 37922949}.

Macroscopic appearance

Fibroepithelial stromal polyps are usually < 50 mm, but they can be larger, particularly in pregnancy {10680891; 23842282}. They range from sessile to pedunculated to villiform and are typically soft to rubbery and greyish pink.

Histopathology

Fibroepithelial stromal polyps are polypoid or pedunculated stromal proliferations lined by squamous epithelium. The stroma is most often hypocellular and poorly marginated, directly abutting the overlying squamous epithelium, which can be thickened or ulcerated. The stromal cells are spindled to stellate with delicate cytoplasmic processes, set in fine collagenous to oedematous stroma. Stromal cell nuclei are typically bland, but (in some cases) they can be enlarged, angulated, and hyperchromatic. Multinucleated stromal cells are a hallmark and are generally most evident in the subepithelial stroma or around larger central vessels. They typically show a rosette-like nuclear arrangement and moderate to abundant, eosinophilic to amphophilic cytoplasm {6681596; 3229895; 2185283; 2294061}. Mitoses are rare to absent. The stroma typically contains dilated capillaries and medium-sized arterioles, which can be hyalinized. A central core of larger vessels is present in some cases. Particularly in pregnancy, fibroepithelial stromal polyps can exhibit stromal hypercellularity, nuclear pleomorphism, and significant mitotic activity, including atypical mitoses, which can result in misdiagnosis as a malignant lesion {10680891; 3229895; 6681596}.

The stromal cells are consistently positive for desmin, ER, and PR; sometimes positive for CD34; and rarely positive for SMA {10680891; 32217091}. Myogenin or MYOD1 can be positive in occasional nuclei, but coexpression of these markers has not been reported {23944986; 32217091}. In a subset of cases, especially in large fibroepithelial stromal polyps, there is HMGA2 nuclear positivity {32897956}. Immunohistochemical loss of RB1 has been reported {37922949}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: polypoid lesion with fibrovascular stromal core, directly abutting overlying squamous epithelium.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Fibroepithelial stromal polyps are benign but can recur locally, particularly with continued hormonal stimulation {23136578; 10680891}. Complete local excision is the treatment of choice for primary and recurrent lesions. The association between incomplete excision and recurrence risk is uncertain. Spontaneous postpartum regression can occur {10680891}.
