10.1.9.10 Ewing sarcoma

Definition

Ewing sarcoma is a small round cell sarcoma showing gene fusions involving a member of the FET family of genes (usually EWSR1) and a member of the ETS family of transcription factors (usually FLI1).

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: peripheral-type primitive neuroectodermal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours involve the vulva {19820381; 28296680; 10782405; 11468565; 17557870; 31108450; 38708674}, vagina {22007080; 10782405; 17557870; 31108450; 38708674}, uterine cervix {31108450; 33538423; 38708674}, uterine corpus {28296680; 38708674}, and pelvis / broad ligament {38708674}.

Clinical features

Patients with uterine tumours present with abnormal bleeding, while vaginal and vulvar tumours typically manifest as a painless mass {10782405; 17557870; 11468565; 33538423; 33552288; 38708674}.

Epidemiology

These are extremely rare tumours. They tend to affect premenopausal women, with a median age of 29 years (range: 6–65 years) {28296680; 38708674}.

Etiology

A statistically significant enrichment of rare pathogenic germline FANCC variants has been identified in patients with Ewing sarcoma compared with population controls; however, these variants appear to account for only a small subset of cases {26580448}.

Pathogenesis

Most tumours harbour t(11;22)(q24;q12) {6713356; 6713357}, resulting in an EWSR1::FLI1 fusion {38708674}. Less commonly, there is a translocation that involves FUS or other members of the ETS family (e.g. ERG or FEV) {17620387}.

Macroscopic appearance

There is a soft, circumscribed, unencapsulated mass with a uniform and/or nodular lobulated cut surface.

Histopathology

Tumours consist of a monotonous population of small round cells arranged in sheets or solid aggregates, separated by fibrous septa. Focal rosettes are sometimes present. Cells have round nuclei, coarse chromatin, small nucleoli, and scant to abundant eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm. Other morphological features seen in some tumours includes clear, vacuolated cytoplasm and ovoid nuclei {38708674}. The permeative myometrial invasion of uterine tumours may show a low-power appearance reminiscent of endometrial stromal sarcoma. Mitotic activity is brisk and necrosis common. Immunohistochemically, there is diffuse, strong, membranous CD99 expression, and nuclear NKX2-2 and FLI1 expression. Focal positivity with broad-spectrum cytokeratins and synaptophysin is common {33538423; 38708674}. Myogenin and MYOD1 are negative.

Tumours previously considered Ewing-like sarcoma (terminology no longer accepted) are genetically and clinically distinct entities, which are described in detail in the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. XXX). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]].

Cytology

Aspirates are cellular, composed of a monotonous population of small cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and scant cytoplasm {17973999; 22180234}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic confirmation can be helpful in some cases. An FET::ETS fusion (most commonly EWSR1::FLI1) is detected in all tumours.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: small round cell morphology; diffuse membranous CD99 staining.

Desirable: NKX2-2 expression (in most cases); demonstration of an FET::ETS fusion.

Staging

Staging is the same as for other soft tissue neoplasms at the corresponding site.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic factors include stage at presentation, tumour location, tumour size, patient age, and response to therapy {30449479}. Primary Ewing sarcoma of the vulvar skin may have a better prognosis {22098102; 17557870}.
