4.4.0.5 Carcinoma, unclassifiable

Definition

Carcinoma of the uterine cervix, unclassifiable, is an invasive malignant epithelial tumour of the cervix that cannot be further subclassified.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: poorly differentiated carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized to the cervix.

Clinical features

The clinical features are similar to those of cervical carcinoma in general.

Epidemiology

Unclassifiable carcinomas of the cervix are exceedingly rare, accounting for a very small percentage of cervical carcinomas, and should only be diagnosed after extensive histopathological evaluation excludes other tumour types. Little reliable epidemiological data are available.

Etiology

Data are limited, but most cases are HPV associated and/or block-type p16-positive {25884453}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is similar to that of cervical carcinoma generally.

Histopathology

The tumours demonstrate an epithelial phenotype but lack histological or immunohistochemical features of other specific types of cervical carcinoma (e.g. squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and neuroendocrine carcinoma). Gynaecological and non-gynaecological malignancies that secondarily involve the cervix should be excluded {23748795; 32910021; 22353422}, including sarcomas and other non-epithelial malignancies with cytokeratin and other epithelial marker positivity. Diffuse expression of p63, p40, and/or CK5/6 should elicit consideration of a squamous cell carcinoma {32843061; 25884453}. Sometimes when this diagnosis is made on a biopsy specimen, areas diagnostic of other neoplasms may be present in a subsequent resection specimen.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV testing may be useful in determining an HPV association.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primary invasive malignant epithelial neoplasm lacking specific features of differentiation; molecular analysis and/or p16 immunohistochemistry may help in determining HPV-associated etiology.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

In a SEER analysis, the 10-year cause-specific survival rate was 84.9%, not statistically different from that of cervical squamous cell carcinoma, and 70% of cases were American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) stage I {17718897}.
