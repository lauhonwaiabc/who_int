4.3.1.4 Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, gastric type

Definition

Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, gastric-type, is an invasive adenocarcinoma exhibiting gastric/gastrointestinal differentiation and arising through mechanisms not associated with HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: minimal deviation adenocarcinoma; adenoma malignum; mucinous adenocarcinoma of endocervix (HPV-negative).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This tumour occurs in the cervix, most commonly the upper endocervix, with frequent spread to the corpus { 18631191; 25188888}. These tumours may also occur as primary vaginal neoplasms { 38477341; 29664741}; before making a diagnosis of a primary vaginal gastric-type adenocarcinoma, spread from a primary cervical neoplasm should be excluded.

Clinical features

Clinical features include bleeding and watery vaginal discharge.

Epidemiology

Gastric-type adenocarcinoma accounts for approximately 10–15% of all cervical adenocarcinomas in large case series worldwide {29135516; 30679193}, and for approximately 20–25% in Japan {17460448; 20829441; 28296678}. The mean patient age is 50–55 years (range: 37–84 years), which is significantly older than that for HPV-associated adenocarcinomas {17460448; 26457350; 29135516}.

Gastric-type adenocarcinomas of the vagina are rare with few cases reported, although this is a relatively recently described entity {29664741}.

Etiology

These tumours are HPV independent. They can be associated with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome (germline STK11 mutation) and with synchronous gastric-type lesions elsewhere in the female genital tract (endometrium, fallopian tube, ovary) {19207943}; in such cases, it may be difficult to distinguish between synchronous independent and metastatic lesions. Referral for genetic testing should be considered if there is any clinical suspicion of Peutz–Jeghers syndrome.

Pathogenesis

These tumours may be associated with and arise from lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia (complex gastric metaplasia). Atypical lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia and gastric-type adenocarcinoma in situ appear to be precursor lesions {15143335; 17540439; 18779726; 24145653}. These tumours are negative for HPV DNA {20727021; 20829441; 21490443; 32474915}. TP53 is the most frequently mutated gene, followed by CDKN2A/B, KRAS, SLX4, ARID1A, PIK3CA, BRCA2, MSH2, MSH6, ATM, NTRK, and MSH2 {31308508; 33318584; 31855952; 33817764; 36253302; 32641744}. STK11 mutations can be found {12533684; 33318584; 33817764; 39025403}, and in some cases the mutations are germline in patients with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome {12533684; 31308508}. ERBB2 (HER2) or MDM2 gene amplification are detected in some tumours {31308508; 36253302}. Tumours have a low mutational burden {36253302}. Deficiencies in cell cycle, PI3K/AKT and MAPK signalling pathways, NOTCH signalling pathway, Fanconi anaemia pathway, mismatch repair pathway, or homologous recombination are implicated in a minority of cases, and they are potential therapeutic targets {31308508}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically large, and they can be polypoid or ulcerated. Circumferential involvement of the cervix imparting a barrel shape is common {23346318}.

Histopathology

The morphology is of an adenocarcinoma composed of cells with abundant clear or pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and distinct cell borders {17460448; 20459563}. Apical mitoses and basal apoptoses may be present but are typically inconspicuous compared with HPV-associated adenocarcinomas, although there is overlap {29135516}. Cytoplasm contains neutral mucins, which stain pale pinkish-red on Alcian blue / PAS staining (in contrast to the dark purple of acid mucins of normal endocervix) {10757403; 17543073}. Morphology ranges from extremely well-differentiated adenocarcinoma (previously termed “adenoma malignum” or “minimal deviation adenocarcinoma”), with deep haphazardly arranged hairpin or claw-like glands with a limited desmoplastic stromal reaction, to poorly differentiated glands, clusters, and single mucin-containing cells, sometimes including signet-ring cells; a majority of cases are architecturally well differentiated {17460448}. Poorly differentiated glands are lined by cells with variable nuclear morphologies, including large vesicular nuclei with visible nucleoli. Other histological features include endometrioid-like glands {28296678; 30234500}, foamy cytoplasm {30234500; 26517626}, cuboidal cells with depleted cytoplasm {30234500}, and goblet cells {29135516}. Lymphovascular space invasion is frequent {26457350}, and there may be mucin extravasation. These tumours are not graded since there is no correlation between morphology and outcomes {17460448; 33570864}. Occasionally there is coexistence of lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia (including atypical lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia) or gastric-type HPV-independent adenocarcinoma in situ {15143335; 19167836}. Primary vaginal gastric-type adenocarcinoma may be associated with vaginal adenosis exhibiting gastric differentiation; the adenosis may be cytologically bland or exhibit nuclear atypia and may contain goblet cells {29664741}.

By immunohistochemistry, gastric (pyloric) markers, including HIK1083 {15143335}, claudin-18 {34864774}, TFF2 {30324235; 32897966}, CAIX, and MUC6 {15143335} are frequently positive, although MUC6 and CAIX are less specific {15143335; 30324235; 31021421} and these markers are not necessary for diagnosis. These markers are typically more focally expressed in gastric-type adenocarcinoma, in contrast to the typically diffuse expression seen in lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia {15143335; 28296678}. CEA and CK7 are usually positive, and PAX8 may be positive or negative {23391777; 31279344; 26685087}. CK20 and CDX2 may be focally or diffusely positive {31279344; 26685087}. HNF1β is often positive { 26685087 }, and napsin A may be positive {29901522}. PAX2 expression is typically lost { 26685087 }. ER and PR are usually negative. p16 is usually negative/non–block-type; however, diffuse block-type expression is rarely seen {17460448; 21490443; 26685087; 28296678}. Mutation-type p53 staining is seen in as many as 70% of cases {17460448; 28296678; 26685087; 31279344}.

Cytology

Identification of two-toned mucin (yellow gastric-type and pink normal endocervical) on conventional cervical cytology specimens has been proposed as a diagnostic clue for lesions with gastric differentiation {29982289; 36128862; 33176300}, although this finding is less apparent on liquid-based preparations {33152741}. Other features of gastric-type adenocarcinoma include monolayered honeycomb-like sheets of glandular cells with foamy vacuolated cytoplasm, distinct cell borders, intracytoplasmic neutrophils, and vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli {26173165; 29750702; 33152741}. Because of the difficulty of diagnosing gastric-type adenocarcinomas, particularly in very well differentiated cases, the cytological diagnosis often falls short of a definitive diagnosis of malignancy and is instead categorized as atypical glandular cells { 29982289 }.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV testing, if performed, should be negative.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primary cervical adenocarcinoma exhibiting gastric/gastrointestinal differentiation.

Desirable: immunohistochemical evidence of gastrointestinal differentiation; negative/non–block-type p16 staining and/or negative HPV testing.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Gastric-type adenocarcinomas are clinically aggressive neoplasms. Compared with HPV-associated cervical adenocarcinomas, they have a higher prevalence of deep stromal invasion, extracervical and extrauterine spread, and advanced stage at presentation, with frequent ovarian metastasis (which can mimic a benign or borderline mucinous tumour) and/or intra-abdominal spread. Spread to other distant sites is also more common. Disease-free and overall survivals are significantly worse than those for HPV-associated adenocarcinomas {17460448; 17460448; 30679193; 33570864}. Prognosis is not related to the degree of differentiation {26457350; 30709650}. In addition to advanced stage, positive peritoneal cytology, positive surgical margins, and perineural invasion are indicative of high-risk disease {39981977}. Primary vaginal gastric-type adenocarcinomas are also clinically aggressive, but there are few reported cases with long-term follow-up { 29664741 }.
