11.2.0.1 Adenomatoid tumour

Definition

Adenomatoid tumour is a benign mesothelial neoplasm of the genital tract and peritoneum.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adenomatoid tumours occur in the uterus, fallopian tube, ovary, and rarely in the peritoneum {11781521; 19543245; 21337036; 156063; 19133002}.

Clinical features

These tumours are usually asymptomatic and incidentally discovered during surgeries for other conditions; however, they can present as a mass or be detected in endometrial curettings {11781521; 21337036; 36572896}. Uterine tumours can be associated with immunosuppression, which is seen more commonly with large or multifocal/diffuse tumours in this site {19396001; 29148537; 30099776}.

Epidemiology

Adenomatoid tumour is an uncommon neoplasm {11781521; 21337036}, and its occurrence in the peritoneum is even rarer {19133002; 156063}. The median ages of patients with uterine and fallopian tube tumours, respectively, are 44 years and 56 years {21337036}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Adenomatoid tumours are genetically defined by somatic missense mutations in the TRAF7 gene {29148537; 33667423}, similar to well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumours {30171198}. Adenomatoid tumours demonstrate intact/retained nuclear expression of BAP1 and uniformly lack BAP1, CDKN2A, and NF2 mutations or deletions that characterize the majority of mesotheliomas {29148537; 27813512}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tubal tumours may measure as much as 10–20 mm and are subserosal. Most uterine tumours are located in the outer myometrium; they are usually solitary, < 40 mm, and solid but rarely can be diffuse, multicentric, multifocal, large (as much as 110 mm), or cystic {12959144; 11781521; 21337036}. In the peritoneum, the tumours are usually unicentric. Regardless of site of origin, most tumours have relatively poorly defined borders and a nodular, greyish-white, firm cut surface {11781521}.

Histopathology

These tumours show a variable combination of slit-like, tubular, or cystic branching spaces and solid areas intermixed with smooth muscle bundles. The neoplastic cells are eosinophilic and cuboidal or flattened, with mild to moderate atypia. Short papillae are seen in grossly cystic tumours. Usually, there are thread-like bridging strands within the luminal spaces. Signet-ring cells, vacuolated lipoblast-like cells, and a lymphoid infiltrate may be seen. Cytological atypia can be present in infarcted adenomatoid tumour {14707867}. Immunohistochemistry is not necessary for the diagnosis, but tumour cells express calretinin, WT1, D2-40, cytokeratin AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, and L1CAM {11781521; 19543245; 21337036; 29148537; 33847416}. Ki-67 labelling index is usually very low. Adenomatoid tumours of the genital tract uniformly have intact/retained nuclear expression of BAP1, in contrast to mesothelioma, which frequently demonstrates loss of nuclear BAP1 expression {29148537; 27813512; 27067777}. These tumours should be differentiated from leiomyoma, adenocarcinoma, well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour, peritoneal inclusion cysts, and mesothelioma with adenomatoid-like areas {21337036}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Usually not necessary for diagnosis, but molecular testing demonstrating TRAF7 mutation and an absence of BAP1 mutation and CDKN2A and NF2 alterations can help confirm the diagnosis in challenging cases {29148537; 27813512; 33667423}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: variably sized slit-like, tubular, and cystic spaces lined by mesothelial cells; tumours arising in the peritoneum have no invasion into the adjacent tissue.

Desirable: immunohistochemical expression of mesothelial markers, if needed; demonstration of intact BAP1 expression and presence of TRAF7 mutation can be useful in diagnostically challenging cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

This is a benign tumour.
