14.1.0.3 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) NOS is a diffuse neoplasm of large B lymphocytes lacking features of other defined types of large B-cell lymphomas .

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

DLBCL of the female genital tract (FGT) most commonly is a manifestation of systemic disease but can also arise primarily in the FGT {10658906; 10907172; 16224219}. DLBCL can involve one or (less often) both ovaries, uterine cervix or corpus, vagina, or vulva (in decreasing order of frequency) {20509008; 28284518; 34622835}. Isolated fallopian tube DLBCL is very rare, but > 25% of cases of ovarian DLBCL can involve the fallopian tubes {33546005}.

Clinical features

Patients most commonly present with symptoms related to the presence of a mass or abnormal vaginal bleeding. Fewer patients have constitutional symptoms. Ovarian lymphomas may be associated with ascites {10658906; 10907172; 20509008}. Most patients with ovarian DLBCL present with extraovarian spread, involving the lymph nodes, peritoneum, other genital organs, and/or distant sites {9062148; 25403088}. Most uterine DLBCLs are localized {16150291}.

Epidemiology

Lymphomas very rarely arise in the FGT. In one review, the authors estimate the risk of primary FGT lymphoma to be 0.115 cases per 100 000 person-years {16306779}. DLBCL is most common and occurs in adults over a broad age range {10658906; 20509008; 28803429; 28284518; 40310407}, with few cases in adolescents {16224219; 28803429; 29519565} and children {29519565}.

Etiology

The etiology of DLBCL arising in the FGT is unknown. Rare cases occur in immunocompromised patients {29086996; 26813900}.

Pathogenesis

Using gene expression profiling, DLBCL NOS can be divided into germinal-centre B-cell (GCB) and activated B-cell cell-of-origin subtypes. The mutational profiles of these subtypes overlap, but some mutations are preferentially associated with each subtype {28985567; 29666115}. More granular molecular subgroups with different biological and prognostic significance have been defined {29641966; 29713087; 32187361; 32289277} but are not currently in use for therapeutic decision-making. Simultaneous MYC and BCL2 rearrangements in DLBCL are detected more often in patients with systemic DLBCL {25403088; 29519565}. Cases with dual MYC and BCL6 (without BCL2) rearrangements are considered a genetic subgroup of DLBCL NOS or high-grade B-cell lymphoma NOS (based on morphology).

The mutational landscape of DLBCL NOS is fairly well characterized, but few data exist for primary FGT DLBCL. Immunoglobulin genes are clonally rearranged {10658906}. 50% of cases have MYD88 NM_002468.5(MYD88):c.755T>C p.(Leu265Pro) mutation and 25% have CD79B p.Tyr196 mutation (with or without MYD88 p.(Leu265Pro)) {28803429; 29519565}. Most MYD88-mutated cases have a non-GCB immunophenotype {28803429}. In the few cases of ovarian DLBCL studied, genomic analysis has revealed GCB-type and activated B-cell–type mutation profiles {29519565}.

Macroscopic appearance

Ovarian lymphomas usually form a mass (median size: 80–120 mm) {20509008; 25403088}. Endometrial lymphomas can be polypoid or infiltrative. Cervical lymphomas produce bulky lesions, with circumferential enlargement (barrel-shaped) or submucosal {16150291}, polypoid {15790461}, or fungating masses. Vaginal lymphomas cause poorly defined mural thickening or induration. Cervical and vaginal DLBCLs often invade adjacent structures {10800991}. Vulvar lymphomas form masses or indurated areas {10907172}.

Histopathology

Ovarian DLBCL invades diffusely, often sparing the ovarian surface and normal structures. Cervical and vaginal lymphomas show deep invasion, with a band of uninvolved tissue beneath the surface with usually intact overlying epithelium {1594503; 23645066}. DLBCL NOS is composed of centroblasts, immunoblasts, and/or multilobated cells. Neoplastic cells may grow in cords and nests associated with sclerosis, simulating carcinoma {10658906; 11706069; 15790461; 16306779}. DLBCL can exhibit spindle-shaped cells or a sarcomatoid pattern mimicking sarcoma {12819398;16400323; 27167673}. Rarely, specific types of large B-cell lymphomas can be localized to the FGT, including intravascular large B-cell lymphoma {29954821}, plasmablastic lymphoma {28228433}, ALK-positive DLBCL {23896707}, and fibrin-associated DLBCL involving a teratoma or leiomyoma {39608291}.

The neoplastic cells express pan–B-cell antigens including CD20 {10658906; 11706069} and can have a GCB (CD10+ or CD10−, BCL6+, IRF4 [MUM1]−) or non-GCB immunophenotype (CD10−, BCL6+ or BCL6-, IRF4 [MUM1]+) {11706069; 28803429}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is usually > 50% {11706069; 25996637; 25403088}. EBV is typically absent {25403088}.

Cytology

Pap smears in patients with uterine, cervical, and vaginal DLBCL may be abnormal {16150291}, but more often they are negative because surface epithelium often remains intact {1594503}. When positive, large single lymphoid cells are present, with many cells showing varying degrees of degeneration. DLBCL can present with centroblastic, immunoblastic, or anaplastic cytomorphology. Please refer to the third edition of the Bethesda System for Cervical Cytopathology and the WHO Reporting System for Lymph Node, Spleen, and Thymus Cytopathology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Immunoglobulin genes are clonally rearranged {10658906}. FISH analysis or colorimetric in situ hybridization for EBV-encoded RNA (EBER) are useful for excluding specific entities, such as DLBCL with MYC and BCL2 rearrangement or EBV-positive DLBCL.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffuse proliferation of large atypical lymphoid cells; B-cell lineage; exclusion of other specific types of large B-cell lymphoma.

Desirable: cell-of-origin subtyping; reporting of MYC, BCL2, or BCL6 rearrangements and in particular dual MYC and BCL2 or dual MYC and BCL6 rearrangements.

Staging

The Lugano classification for staging has been adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for ovarian DLBCL is similar to that of nodal lymphomas of comparable stage {9062148; 11706069}. Ovarian DLBCL with bilateral involvement, widespread disease, high International Prognostic Index (IPI), and a large mass are associated with poorer outcome {20509008}. Localized FGT lymphomas have a favourable prognosis {10800991; 16150291;39105590}. Surgical excision of FGT DLBCL does not appear to impact prognosis {28284518}.
