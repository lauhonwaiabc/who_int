17.1.0.5 Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma syndrome (FH)

Definition

Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma (HLRCC) syndrome is an autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndrome with early-onset development of cutaneous and uterine leiomyomas, as well as renal cell carcinoma (RCC), associated with germline (constitutional) FH variants.

MIM numbering

150800 Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer; HLRCC

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: hereditary multiple cutaneous leiomyomas; multiple cutaneous and uterine leiomyomas; Reed syndrome {11549574}.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Leiomyomas occur in the skin and uterus, and renal cell carcinomas predominantly arise in the renal cortex (see #13464Table 17.XXX).

Clinical features

Tumours usually present with an earlier onset (see #13464 Table 17.XXX).

Epidemiology

HLRCC syndrome is rare, with uncertain prevalence; > 300 families have been reported to date {20301430}, and 0.24% of unselected women with uterine leiomyoma are affected {30741757}.

Etiology

Autosomal dominant HLRCC syndrome is caused by heterozygous germline (constitutional) loss-of-function mutation of the FH gene (chromosome 1q43) {11283798; 21398687; 23211287}. More than 80 different FH pathogenic variants are known {15937070; 21398687}, and founder variants have been reported in multiple populations {20301430}. There is incomplete penetrance {15724016; 21398687} and no established genotype–phenotype correlation {15937070}. The vast majority of fumarate hydratase deficiency in uterine leiomyoma is driven by sporadic genetic alterations in the FH gene, unrelated to HLRCC syndrome {28664937; 26574848}.

Pathogenesis

Biallelic inactivation of FH occurs in the syndromic setting due to a germline pathogenic variant with the addition of a somatic second hit. FH encodes fumarate hydratase, a Krebs cycle enzyme that converts fumarate to L-maleate. Absence of fumarate hydratase enzymatic activity impairs oxidative phosphorylation, shifting to aerobic glycolysis. The subsequent accumulation of fumarate and other oncometabolites leads to neoplasia through a variety of downstream effects including stabilization of HIFα and suppression of homologous recombination double-strand break repair of DNA {32494005; 21398687; 27580029}.

Macroscopic appearance

See #13464Table 17.XXX.

Histopathology

Uterine leiomyomas may show a staghorn vascular pattern, alveolar-pattern oedema, and/or ovoid smooth muscle cells arranged in chains, rather than in intersecting fascicles. The smooth muscle cells exhibit prominent eosinophilic nucleoli surrounded by perinucleolar haloes and eosinophilic cytoplasmic globules. Bizarre nuclei are common (see #13464Table 17.XXX) {27454940; 26574848; 24309325}. These fumarate hydratase (FH)-deficient morphological features may be seen in conventional leiomyomas, cellular leiomyomas, and leiomyomas with bizarre nuclei. Rare FH-deficient uterine smooth muscle tumours of uncertain malignant potential and leiomyosarcomas have also been described, but none with confirmed germline (constitutional) FH variants {36729957; 34108400}.

Immunohistochemistry

Loss of immunohistochemical staining for FH is specific to FH deficiency. However, the sensitivity is limited as some FH-deficient leiomyomas may display retained protein expression due to FH missense mutation {31162287}. Positive immunostaining for 2SC is highly specific and sensitive, even when FH protein expression appears to be retained, and is therefore more accurate than FH immunohistochemistry in detecting FH gene deficiency {24441663; 21630274; 34678832}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Although FH and 2SC immunohistochemistry identify FH-deficient neoplasms, they do not replace genetic testing because biallelic FH inactivation can occur as a purely somatic phenomenon. The identification of an FH germline (constitutional) variant or deletion is necessary for definitive diagnosis {15937070}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

HLRCC syndrome can be suspected or confirmed if the criteria in #10888Box 17.XXX are met.

Staging

RCC should be staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

RCCs arising in this syndrome are aggressive {17895761; 12772087}. Although the smooth muscle tumours may show atypical cytological features, there are only rare reports of biologically malignant behaviour {27454940; 26574848; 16477632}.
