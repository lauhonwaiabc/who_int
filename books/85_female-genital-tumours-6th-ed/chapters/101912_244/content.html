10.1.9.12 Other mesenchymal tumours

Synovial sarcoma

Synovial sarcoma is a rare malignant mesenchymal neoplasm of uncertain differentiation that can occur in the female genital tract, most often in the vulva and more rarely in the vagina {21131827}, endometrium {21491161; 35233814}, fallopian tube, and ovary. Typically, young or middle-aged patients are affected {32675659}, and tumours may mimic a benign lesion both clinically and radiologically. Macroscopically, tumours are often well circumscribed and may appear polypoid {21491161}. Microscopically, synovial sarcoma can be monophasic, biphasic, or poorly differentiated. Biphasic tumours feature glandular epithelial elements admixed with uniform spindle cells, while monophasic forms consist of fascicles of monomorphic spindle cells with scant cytoplasm. Poorly differentiated areas may be composed of small to medium-sized round or oval cells with high N:C ratios arranged in diffuse sheets {21131827; 32675659}. Immunohistochemically, tumours often express EMA, keratins, CD99, S100, and TLE1, and fusion-specific antibodies show a high sensitivity and specificity {21131827; 32675659; 32141887}. Molecular confirmation of t(X;18)(p11;q11) or demonstration of the SS18::SSX1/2/4 gene fusion (formerly known as SYT::SSX) by FISH, RT-PCR, or next-generation sequencing is diagnostic. Prognosis varies. Although long-term disease-free survival may be observed, local recurrence and distant metastases have also been reported {35233814; 32675659; 21131827; 21491161}.

Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma

Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma (LGFMS) is a rare malignant mesenchymal neoplasm that most commonly arises in the deep soft tissue of the extremities and trunk, and it has also rarely been reported in the vulvovaginal region {35617488; 15644777}. Macroscopically, tumours are usually well circumscribed with a firm to gelatinous cut surface {35617488}. Microscopically, LGFMS exhibits an admixture of fibrous and myxoid areas, often arranged in a lobulated pattern. The neoplastic cells are spindled to stellate, with bland, slender to ovoid nuclei, embedded in a collagenous to myxoid stroma. The architecture varies, and although a patternless arrangement has been described, storiform and occasionally fascicular patterns may be seen. Mitoses are rare, and necrosis is typically absent. Capillary arcades may be observed, especially in myxoid zones {35617488}. Immunohistochemically, MUC4 is a highly sensitive and specific marker for LGFMS, and EMA may be variably expressed {35617488}. Most tumours harbour a characteristic t(7;16)(q33;p11) involving the FUS gene, which can be identified by FISH {35617488}. Long-term follow-up is strongly recommended, as lesions may recur or even metastasize, sometimes decades after initial diagnosis {35617488; 17413985; 29385413}.

Evolving mesenchymal neoplasms of the female genital tract

There are a number of mesenchymal tumours in the female genital tract that defy classification within currently established diagnostic categories but show recurrent genomic alterations. Further studies are necessary to increase published case numbers and to effectively classify such lesions. It is expected that in the near future, a molecular-based consensus classification of these tumours will be possible. To facilitate this process, specialized pathology review of these unclassified lesions is recommended.
