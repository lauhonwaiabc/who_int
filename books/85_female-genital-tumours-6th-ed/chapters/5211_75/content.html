5.2.1.1 Endometrial hyperplasia without atypia

Definition

Endometrial hyperplasia without atypia is a disorganized proliferation of endometrial glands showing irregular size and shape, with an increased gland-to-stroma ratio and without associated cytological atypia.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign endometrial hyperplasia; simple endometrial hyperplasia without atypia; complex endometrial hyperplasia without atypia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Endometrium

Clinical features

Patients present with symptoms of abnormal, non-cyclical vaginal bleeding. Younger patients with an underlying hyperestrogenic state (such as polycystic ovarian syndrome and obesity) are also at an increased risk. The endometrium may show increased thickness on ultrasound.

Epidemiology

Endometrial hyperplasia without atypia affected 142–213 per 100 000 women in a large case series, mostly in women in the fifth decade of life {19393600}. It is most commonly diagnosed in perimenopausal women. Other risk factors include obesity, polycystic ovarian syndrome, and diabetes {18682485}. Estrogen-secreting sex cord stromal tumours of the ovary, particularly adult granulosa cell tumours, are another less common risk factor {24257556}.

Etiology

Prolonged exposure to unopposed estrogen is the major risk factor, most commonly in the setting of obesity and/or chronic anovulation. Early menarche and late menopause are also associated with increased risk.

Pathogenesis

Hyperplasia without atypia is the result of prolonged estrogen exposure unopposed by progesterone or progestational agents acting on the entire endometrial field {22290745}.

Macroscopic appearance

The endometrium varies from having the uniform, tan appearance of the late proliferative phase to appearing highly thickened and sometimes polypoid or spongy with cysts.

Histopathology

Architecturally abnormal glands with branching and outpouchings and/or cystically dilated glands, often irregularly distributed, result in a variably increased gland-to-stroma ratio. The epithelium is simple, but staggered nuclei can confer a pseudostratified appearance. Mitotic activity tends to be increased, although waning estrogen levels may result in mitotically quiescent round or elongated nuclei. These findings are generally present in the entirety of the submitted tissue. Changes of breakdown, including stromal condensation and fibrin thrombi, may be present.

Associated metaplastic changes, most commonly tubal (ciliated) metaplasia, are often seen in endometrial hyperplasia without atypia, and should not be interpreted as evidence of cytological atypia when also identified in background normal endometrium. However, when these changes are confined to the glands demonstrating abnormal architecture and/or glandular crowding and are absent in the background benign endometrium, they could be evidence of a clonal process demonstrating altered differentiation {18637968}. Strict adherence to the diagnostic criteria of endometrial hyperplasia with atypia/endometrioid intraepithelial neoplasia is required to properly characterize the significance of this finding in this particular setting.

Foci of crowded (namely, gland-to-stroma ratio > 1) and/or cystically dilated glands can be present in endometrial polyps, and do not warrant a diagnosis of endometrial hyperplasia without atypia as long as the crowded glands are not cytologically distinct from background non-crowded polyp glands. Altered epithelial appearance when compared with the background non-crowded polyp glands should raise the possibility of endometrial hyperplasia with atypia/endometrioid intraepithelial neoplasia within the polyp. Suspicious yet subdiagnostic foci of crowding warrant clinical correlation and consideration for repeated sampling, as a subsequent diagnosis of endometrioid neoplasia is documented in a small minority of such cases {18637968; 38062647}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: increased endometrial gland-to-stroma ratio; tubular, branching, and/or cystically dilated glands resembling proliferative endometrium, often with pseudostratified nuclei; uniform distribution of nuclear features across the submitted tissue.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Women exposed to unopposed estrogen have a 3- to 4-fold increased risk of endometrial carcinoma, rising to 10-fold after a decade {4005805}. The cumulative 20-year progression risk of endometrial hyperplasia without atypia is < 5% {20065186}.
