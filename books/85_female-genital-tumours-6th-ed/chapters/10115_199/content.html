10.1.1.5 Metastasizing leiomyoma

Definition

Metastasizing leiomyoma is an extrauterine, well-demarcated, often nodular proliferation of benign-appearing smooth muscle of gynaecological origin.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: benign metastasizing leiomyoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lung is the most common site. Metastasis to other sites, such as abdominopelvic and/or mediastinal lymph nodes, bone, retroperitoneum, skin, heart, and brain, have been reported {34926773; 22384439; 34211791; 38172926}. Involvement of multiple extrauterine sites may be found in as many as 53% of cases {38172926}.

Clinical features

Lesions are incidentally discovered on radiological studies performed for other indications, but patients may present with respiratory symptoms (e.g. dyspnoea, cough) {27514603; 11373202}. Pulmonary lesions can be multiple and bilateral. They can occur in patients with a history of prior hysterectomy or myomectomy, ranging from months to decades earlier {26321441}.

Epidemiology

Metastasizing leiomyoma is rare, and only approximately 100 cases have been reported in the literature. The condition typically affects women of reproductive age, and presentation is often at young to middle age {27659239; 37457601}.

Etiology

These lesions are considered to represent spread from a histologically benign uterine smooth muscle tumour. Although a relationship between this entity and intravenous leiomyomatosis has been proposed {24487468}, definitive proof for the majority of cases remains elusive.

Pathogenesis

It has been shown that metastasizing leiomyoma is clonally derived from uterine leiomyoma in at least a subset of cases {16357844}. The hypothesis of uniclonal origin also is supported by detection of gene mutations as well as RAB2A::PLAG1 gene fusion {39230504; 36948524; 29484624; 28533481} shared between uterine and/or extrauterine sites. Further, molecular analysis reveals broad similarities between uterine and metastasizing leiomyomas based on a common pattern of MED12 mutations and expression abnormalities {29484624; 18382364; 30989050}. Loss of fumarate hydratase (FH) also has been observed in 2 cases {38642139}. In 1 case, rearrangement of HMGA1 on 6p21 and loss of chromosome 7, findings similar to those found in a subset of uterine leiomyomas, has been found in synchronous benign smooth muscle masses involving deep soft tissue and lung {22682624}. Cytogenetic analyses by different groups, however, have highlighted chromosomal aberrations not typically found in uterine leiomyomas, including 19q and 22q terminal deletion {17460458; 30597752; 28533481}. The latter also has been commonly observed in intravenous leiomyomatosis {25033729}.

Macroscopic appearance

Metastasizing leiomyomas are well-circumscribed, white to tan nodules with a bulging cut surface. The size ranges from a few mm to 45 mm (mean: 18 mm) {11037349}.

Histopathology

There is usually a well-demarcated proliferation of intersecting fascicles composed of spindle cells with moderate eosinophilic cytoplasm and blunt-ended nuclei; the cells are cytologically bland, with minimal to absent mitoses. Metastasizing leiomyoma may be cellular or admixed with adipose tissue {12649679}. In the lung, it tends to have a peribronchiolar pattern and may entrap alveolar spaces peripherally {17460458}. ER and PR are positive {10506455; 12649679} by immunohistochemistry.

Cytology

Smear will show features similar to those of a leiomyoma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Comparison of genomic profiles of the presumed uterine primary tumour and pulmonary metastatic lesion(s) may help identify shared alterations and demonstrate a relatively simple genomic profile.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: smooth muscle tumour without atypia or necrosis and minimal to absent mitosis, in lungs, lymph nodes, or other extrauterine sites; absence of any history of gynaecological or non-gynaecological leiomyosarcoma or smooth muscle tumour # of uncertain malignant potential.

Desirable: a history uterine or gynaecological type leiomyoma(s).

Staging

Metastasizing leiomyoma is not usually staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases have an indolent course, but extensive disease may lead to respiratory failure and death {11037349; 26321441; 30989050}. As lesions are hormonally sensitive, they may respond to progestin, aromatase inhibitors, and/or GnRH agonists {15240591; 16343217}.
