2.2.2.2 Papillary hidradenoma

Definition

Papillary hidradenoma is a benign glandular tumour associated with anogenital mammary-like glands.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: hidradenoma papilliferum

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most cases affect the interlabial sulcus or labia majora of the vulva; less frequently, the perianal and perineal skin is involved {26944596}.

Clinical features

The usual presentation is a solitary asymptomatic nodule or cyst-like lesion measuring 2–20 mm, sometimes with overlying ulceration and bleeding {26863059; 38435116}.

Epidemiology

This is the most common benign glandular neoplasm of the vulva in a case series, occurring almost exclusively in women aged 20–89 years {10411423}. It is rare in children and Black population {10986678}.

Etiology

Papillary hidradenoma is thought to arise from anogenital mammary-like glands {8005737}.

Pathogenesis

Tumorigenic alterations in the PI3K/AKT and MAPK pathway have been identified as the most prevalent genetic aberrations. KRAS and SMO mutations have rarely been reported {26493284; 27184479; 28291131; 28648934; 31010589; 38055985}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is typically a tan or red nodule with a solid to variably cystic cut surface, measuring on average 15 mm.

Histopathology

The tumour is a well-circumscribed intradermal solid-cystic nodule composed of complex branching and interconnecting tubules and papillae consisting of central delicate fibrovascular cores lined by uniform, cuboidal to columnar secretory cells and underlying myoepithelial cells. Cystic change, sclerosing adenosis-like, and necrosis can be encountered. Oxyphilic metaplasia is common, especially in areas with solid growth with nuclear polymorphism, and may simulate adenocarcinoma. Remnants of anogenital mammary-like glands adjacent to the lesion can be seen. Mitotic activity is often present and is occasionally high {16871035; 26863059}. Immunohistochemical profile is CK7/GATA3/ER/PR/AR positive, with focal mammaglobin and GCDFP-15 expression, and CK20/PAX8 negative {38055985; 38435116}. Tumours also show TRPS1 positivity {38959400}.

Cytology

There are few case reports. Smears show small tissue fragments of benign epithelial cells, including cuboidal and columnar forms {8005045; 21053567}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed complex growth of branching and interconnecting epithelial tubules and papillae, with subepithelial myoepithelial cells.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

This is a benign tumour, but it may recur locally if incompletely excised. Cases of carcinoma in situ resembling ductal carcinoma in situ of the breast and low-grade phyllodes tumour arising in papillary hidradenoma have been reported {1662944; 18580327; 18645314; 33908057; 34926767; 36731045}.
