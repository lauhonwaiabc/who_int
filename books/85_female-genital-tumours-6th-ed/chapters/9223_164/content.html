9.2.2.3 Sertoli cell tumour

Definition

Sertoli cell tumour is a sex cord neoplasm composed of Sertoli cells arranged in a variety of patterns.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovary

Clinical features

Sertoli cell tumours are almost always unilateral. They may be discovered incidentally, or patients may present with symptoms related to a pelvic mass or with estrogenic or, less commonly, androgenic manifestations. Prepubertal patients may develop isosexual pseudoprecocity. Rarely, renin, progesterone, or aldosterone production by the tumour may be responsible for symptoms, including hypertension. A subset of patients has Peutz–Jeghers syndrome {7427869; 15644771}.

Epidemiology

Sertoli cell tumours are exceedingly rare, representing < 5% of ovarian sex cord stromal tumours {15644771}. They can occur at any age (mean: 30 years, range: 2–76 years) {15644771; 20349797}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

A subset of Sertoli cell tumours has been shown to harbour DICER1 mutations, which may be somatic or germline {26428316}. STK11 may also contribute to pathogenesis, particularly in tumours arising in the setting of Peutz–Jeghers syndrome {15644771}. Other molecular events have not been widely investigated in Sertoli cell tumours.

Macroscopic appearance

These are typically unilateral neoplasms with a mean size of 80 mm (range: 8 to 300 mm in the largest published series) {15644771}. They are typically solid with a tan to yellow cut surface, but they may be solid and cystic or rarely cystic. Haemorrhage and necrosis are seen in a minority of tumours {7427869; 15644771}.

Histopathology

Sertoli cell tumours may show a broad array of patterns, with tubules (either hollow or solid) present at least focally in most neoplasms and often being the predominant pattern. Other patterns include trabecular, diffuse, alveolar, pseudopapillary, retiform, pseudoendometrioid, and spindled. The stroma ranges from scant to abundant and hyalinized. Cuboidal or columnar Sertoli cells usually have pale, sometimes foamy lipid-rich (folliculome lipidique) to deeply eosinophilic (oxyphilic) cytoplasm and bland oval to round nuclei, typically with a small nucleolus. Rarely, bizarre nuclei, marked cytological atypia, brisk mitotic activity, or necrosis are seen. Occasional small microscopic foci resembling an adult granulosa cell tumour may be seen and should not result in a diagnosis of gynandroblastoma {7427869; 15644771}. Rare tumours have areas in keeping with Sertoli cell tumour and areas in keeping with sex cord tumour with annular tubules; especially in patients with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome, these are probably best regarded as sex cord tumour with annular tubules, with areas resembling Sertoli cell tumour (see section Sex cord tumour with annular tubules). Lipid-rich or oxyphilic patterns of Sertoli cell tumour may also occur in patients with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome {6724788; 7523322; 26621753}.

Most tumours are positive for SF1 and WT1. Inhibin, calretinin, CD99, and broad-spectrum cytokeratins show variable positivity, while EMA, CK7, PAX8, and chromogranin are typically negative {17255771; 17721194; 18753972; 19033865}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tumour composed of sertoliform tubules with low-cuboidal to columnar cells with oval to round nuclei.

Desirable: positivity for sex cord stromal immunohistochemical markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Sertoli cell tumours are typically benign. In the largest series with outcome data, adverse events, which are extremely rare in this already uncommon entity, were enriched in tumours showing increased nuclear atypia and a mitotic index of ≥ 5 per 10 HPF (equivalent to ≥ 2 mitoses/mm² in a 0.237 mm² HPF area); these features were present in 5 of the 7 cases with aggressive clinical behaviour {15644771}.
