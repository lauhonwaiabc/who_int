12.3.1.1 Epithelioid trophoblastic tumour

Definition

Epithelioid trophoblastic tumour (ETT) is a malignant neoplasm of chorionic-type intermediate trophoblast.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterine corpus, lower uterine segment, and cervix are the most common sites of involvement. Rare sites include fallopian tube, ovary, vagina, broad ligament, caesarean scar, small bowel, gallbladder, kidney, and peritoneum {9808132; 31047719; 26980014; 30480644}. Rare isolated pulmonary lesions without uterine involvement have been reported {38391331; 39128338}.

Clinical features

Vaginal bleeding is the most common symptom. Other clinical presentations include amenorrhoea, abdominal pain, abdominal bloating, or metastasis-related symptoms {31047719}. Incidental detection in asymptomatic patients has been reported {9808132; 16258513; 30480644}. A third of the patients are clinically diagnosed with ectopic pregnancy {23159816}. Majority of antecedent gestations include term pregnancy, while a smaller percentage are represented by spontaneous abortions and hydatidiform moles, with latency ranging from few months to 33 years {9808132; 25773203; 31047719}. The antecedent pregnancy may not always be the causative pregnancy {27246306}. Mildly to moderately elevated serum hCG (< 2500 mIU/mL) is detectable in 80% of cases {16258513; 18720920; 23970158}. Extrauterine or metastatic disease at presentation is reported in 25–42% of the cases {35677151; 31047719; 30662520; 25773203; 30473257}.

Epidemiology

ETT is the rarest form of gestational trophoblastic tumour, representing 1–2.2% of gestational trophoblastic neoplasia {20087692; 30662520; 35677151}. It develops in patients aged 15–68 years (mean: 37 years) {9808132; 23970158; 30480644}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ETT arises from trophoblast with differentiation towards chorionic-type intermediate trophoblast {9808132}. Atypical placental site nodule has been proposed as the precursor lesion {23688193; 18983471; 25675185; 36788063}. An absence of the Y chromosome complement is seen in the majority of tumours {20182630; 27246306}. Molecular studies show low tumour mutational burden and an undisturbed genome {18820674; 38301309}. LPCAT1::TERT fusion transcripts as well as alterations in RTK/RAS and PI3K signalling pathways have been reported {34033669; 36788063; 38301309; 31954539}. The tumour is microsatellite stable {38301309}.

Macroscopic appearance

Within the uterus, nearly 50% of ETT arise in the cervix or lower uterine segment {9808132; 30480644; 16258513}. The tumour size ranges from 5 to 148 mm {9808132; 30480644; 35677151; 31047719}. ETT characteristically forms deeply invasive, discrete nodules with solid, tan-white to brown, or cystic cut surface with varying amounts of haemorrhage and necrosis. Ulceration and fistula formation are common {30480644}.

Histopathology

ETT characteristically shows a well-circumscribed, nodular, expansile proliferation of medium-sized trophoblastic cells. Areas of viable tumour cells in a perivascular arrangement are typically separated by prominent geographical necrosis. Tumour cells form nests or cords that coalesce to form large masses. The cells are relatively uniform, with a moderate amount of finely granular eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm, distinct cell membranes, and round nuclei with distinct small nucleoli. Moderate nuclear atypia is generally present, and the mitotic count generally ranges from 0 to 4 mitoses/mm2 (equivalent to 0 to 12 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.62 mm in diameter and 0.30 mm2 in area, with a mean of 2 mitoses/10 HPF) {16258513; 31047719}. Eosinophilic hyaline-like material, resembling keratin, is characteristically present in the centre of tumour nests or between tumour cells {16258513; 9808132; 11192071}. Calcifications and haemosiderin deposits can be seen. Decidualized stromal cells may be found at the tumour periphery. Peritumoural lymphocytic infiltrate is common {9808132}. When involving the cervix, the tumour cells may colonize the mucosal epithelium, mimicking a high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion {16258513}.

The tumour cells express p63, α-inhibin, HSD3B1, and GATA3. Scattered cells express MCAM and hPL. SALL4 is negative in ETT {27068524; 36656227; 39876093}. Ki-67 proliferation index is 10–25% in most cases, and double immunostaining with CK-Ki67 can be useful for accurate assessment of the proliferation index {9808132; 17498600}.

The differential diagnoses include cervical squamous cell carcinoma, atypical placental site nodule, choriocarcinoma, placental site trophoblastic tumour, and epithelioid leiomyosarcoma {9808132; 26630228; 16258513}. ETT may be part of a mixed trophoblastic tumour {29897391; 38959396}.

Cytology

Cytological smears may show clusters of slightly discohesive mononuclear tumour cells with irregularly enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei, one or two inconspicuous nucleoli, and abundant vacuolated cytoplasm {11940211; 24525845}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

DNA genotyping, particularly short tandem repeat (STR) polymorphism analysis, is useful for detecting unique paternal alleles from the index pregnancy, thereby supporting the diagnosis of gestational ETT over somatic carcinoma {18820674; 26980014; 37743102} or non-gestational ETT of germ cell origin {31688005}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nodular lesion with destructive growth composed of epithelioid intermediate trophoblastic cells.

Desirable: whenever available, immunohistochemical positivity for trophoblastic marker(s) and p63 is important for diagnosis. In some cases, a Ki-67 proliferation index > 10%. When necessary and if available, DNA genotyping confirms the gestational origin of the tumour.

Staging

The FIGO and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classifications are used for staging.

Prognosis and prediction

ETTs are generally slow-growing, chemoresistant tumours, and simple hysterectomy is curative in most cases. However, metastasis occurs in 25–50% of cases, most commonly to the lungs, and patients die of disease in nearly 20% of cases {30473257; 30662520}. Advanced-stage disease and an interval of > 4 years since antecedent pregnancy are poor prognostic factors {25773203; 30473257; 30662520}. PDL1 is highly expressed in ETT {27362903; 31013360; 30339966}, and checkpoint immunotherapy may be an effective salvage therapeutic option {29185430; 35681761}. The FIGO risk scoring system is not appropriate for guiding treatment {27789086}.
