10.1.5.5 Solitary fibrous tumour

Definition

Solitary fibrous tumour (SFT) is an intermediate, rarely metastasizing fibroblastic tumour characterized by bland spindle cell morphology, a patternless growth pattern, prominent thin-walled branching (staghorn) vasculature, and NAB2::STAT6 gene rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: haemangiopericytoma, benign solitary fibrous tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SFT uncommonly involves the female genital tract, typically the vulva or vagina, but rarely the uterus, fallopian tubes, or ovary {29106748; 34739418}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with a slow-growing mass {29106748; 34739418}.

Epidemiology

SFT of the female genital tract is a rare tumour that occurs over a wide age range (22–78 years), with peak incidence in the fifth decade of life {29106748; 34739418}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The genetic hallmark of SFT is a paracentric inversion involving chromosome 12q, resulting in the fusion of NAB2 and STAT6 {23313954; 23761323; 23313952}. Tumours with more aggressive behaviour or showing histological evidence of dedifferentiation may gain subsequent mutations, including TERT promoter mutations and deletions or mutations of TP53 {25582503}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours vary in size from 10 mm to > 100 mm {29106748; 34739418} and are generally well circumscribed, with a firm, homogeneous, yellowish-tan cut surface.

Histopathology

SFT is composed of haphazardly arranged spindled to ovoid cells with indistinct, pale eosinophilic cytoplasm within a variably collagenous stroma, admixed with branching, staghorn-shaped (pericytoma-like) blood vessels. There is a wide histological spectrum, ranging from paucicellular lesions with abundant stromal keloidal-type collagen to highly cellular tumours consisting of closely spaced cells with little or no intervening stroma. Generally, cellularity is variable within individual tumours, with alternating cellular and hypocellular zones. Histological patterns that have been described in the female genital tract include myxoid SFT, where abundant myxoid stroma may obscure typical histological features, adipocytic differentiation (fat-forming or lipomatous SFT), and multinucleated giant cells. Rarely, dedifferentiated tumours, which show abrupt transition to an anaplastic component, occur {34739418}. SFT is typically positive for CD34 and nuclear STAT6 {23575898; 24030747; 25873501} although rare tumours are CD34-negative {34152108}. STAT6 immunohistochemistry is a sensitive and specific surrogate for STAT6 fusions {24030747; 24702701; 23575898}. Protein expression may rarely be lost in dedifferentiated tumours {26022454}.

Cytology

Spindle to elongate cells with moderate cytoplasm and elongated nuclei are present in a metachromatic, amorphous to fibrillary, collagenous matrix {34049841; 35778919}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

NAB2::STAT6 gene fusions are pathognomonic for SFT {23313952; 23313954}. Although detection of this fusion is not required for diagnosis, it may be considered in diagnostically challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindled to ovoid-shaped cells in a patternless architecture arranged around a branching and hyalinized vasculature; variable stromal collagen deposition.

Desirable: STAT6 expression; NAB2::STAT6 fusions (although not required for diagnosis, may support it in diagnostically challenging cases).

Staging

Risk-stratification models are preferred over anatomical staging.

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence (distant or local) occurs in 10–30% of SFT {22575866; 29039030; 28838212; 30206022; 29106748; 34739418}. SFTs are not graded, and historical definitions of benign and malignant SFTs are not recommended for prognostication. The most widely used risk-stratification model for extrameningeal SFT incorporates mitotic count (> 2 mitoses/mm2, equating to > 4 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area), patient age (≥ 55 years), and tumour size stratified by 50 mm tiers to classify tumours into low-risk, intermediate-risk, and high-risk groups {22575866}. Including necrosis as a fourth variable results in higher numbers of cases being classified as low-risk {28731041; 29935303}. This model has been validated to predict recurrence of SFT arising in the female genital tract {34739418}. Dedifferentiated SFTs are always considered high risk and should be managed as high-grade sarcomas.
