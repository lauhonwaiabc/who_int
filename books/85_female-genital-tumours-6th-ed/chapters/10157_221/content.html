10.1.5.7 NTRK-rearranged spindle cell sarcoma

Definition

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell sarcoma is a spindle cell tumour harbouring NTRK1–3 gene rearrangements.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: fibrosarcoma; neurofibrosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours have been mostly documented in the uterine cervix and (less frequently) uterine corpus {35713627}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with vaginal bleeding or a cervical mass.

Epidemiology

The incidence of the NTRK-rearranged spindle cell sarcoma is extremely low, and information on occurrence is provided by a limited number of case reports and series, especially for uncommon localizations. Although this tumour typically arises in the uterine cervix of young women, reported cases range in age from 13 to 69 years (mean: 31 years) {21317712; 29553955; 34430691; 35713627} [[Moh M, Johnson CM, Geurts J, et al. Uterine Sarcoma with a Novel WWOX::NTRK2 Fusion in a Postmenopausal Woman with Li-Fraumeni–like Syndrome: A Case That Expands the Spectrum of NTRK-Rearranged Uterine Tumours. AJSP Rev Rep. 2021;26:304–306. DOI: 10.1097/PCR.0000000000000476]].

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Other than an association with NTRK1–3 rearrangements, the pathogenesis of these neoplasms is unknown. NTRK1 fusion partners include LMNA, NUMA1, TPM3, TPR, HAPSTR1 (C16orf72), and IRF2BP2, while NTRK3 partners include RBPMS, TFG, STRN3, SPECC1L, and EML4 {35713627; 38151535; 29553955; 31971278}.

Macroscopic appearance

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell sarcomas are typically firm, slightly fleshy lesions centred in the cervical stroma and less frequently the myometrium. They range in size from 13 to 230 mm {34723848; 31107720; 35713627}.

Histopathology

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell sarcoma consists of a proliferation of predominantly uniform spindle cells with mild to moderate and occasionally severe atypia and inconspicuous nucleoli. Scattered atypical symplastic-like cells may be present. The mitotic count is variable (< 1 to 20 mitoses/mm2). A staghorn vascular pattern, hyalinized vessels, nuclear pseudoinclusions, and necrosis may be present. Vascular invasion is uncommon. The spindle cells variably express S100, CD34, SMA, and cyclin D1. Pan-TRK expression is a sensitive, but not specific, marker for an underlying NTRK1–3 rearrangement {37988282; 33290352}. Importantly, there is no expression of CD10, desmin, BCOR, ER, or PR {30877273; 21317712; 35713627}.

Cytology

Smears show a mixed pattern of sparse and dense tissue fragments of oval/spindle cells with eosinophilic amorphous material in the background {39670651}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of NTRK1–3 rearrangement is diagnostic.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cell sarcoma with NTRK1–3 rearrangement.

Desirable: absence of CD10, desmin, ER, and PR expression.

Staging

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell sarcoma is staged as uterine sarcoma using FIGO, Union for International Cancer Control (UICC), and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical course of NTRK-rearranged spindle cell sarcoma is variable, but at least some tumours are associated with metastasis and an aggressive clinical course. Data are limited, but tumours with lymphovascular invasion, necrosis, > 3.3 mitoses/mm2, or NTRK3 fusion appear to be at higher risk of adverse outcomes {35713627}.
