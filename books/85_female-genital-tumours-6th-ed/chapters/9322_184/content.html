9.3.2.2 Mixed germ cell–sex cord stromal tumour, unclassified

Definition

Mixed germ cell–sex cord stromal tumour, unclassified, is a neoplasm composed of germ cells and sex cord cells that do not have the appearance of a gonadoblastoma and that occur in phenotypically and genetically female patients.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Presentation is usually with symptoms related to an ovarian mass. Occasionally, tumours are associated with hormonal manifestations such as isosexual pseudoprecocity {196747; 17581418}.

Epidemiology

These are rare tumours and their actual incidence is unknown. Most tumours occur in girls aged < 10 years although they can also occur in women {32850222}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are typically large, unilateral, solid masses with a grey-pink or yellow to pale-brown cut surface.

Histopathology

Microscopically, these tumours show a variable and haphazard admixture of germ cells and sex cord cells {27458149; 33002849}. The germ cells are found singly or in small clusters and are large, with pleomorphic nuclei and ample, often clear, and PAS-positive cytoplasm; they resemble dysgerminoma tumour cells. Sex cord cells may form cords or trabeculae, hollow or solid tubules (sometimes resembling sex cord tumour with annular tubules), or cysts, or they may grow diffusely. Immunohistochemically, the sex cord elements are typically positive for sex cord markers such as inhibin, calretinin, and SF1. The germ cells are typically immunoreactive for germ cell markers such as SALL4, PLAP, OCT4, and KIT (CD117) {16538443}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an ovarian tumour consisting of an irregular admixture of dysgerminoma-like germ cells and sex cord cells not exhibiting the distinctive appearance of a gonadoblastoma.

Desirable: positive staining of the germ cells with germ cell markers and the sex cord cells with sex cord markers.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most, but not all, of the lesions are clinically benign. Occasionally (~10% of patients), a malignant germ cell tumour, most commonly dysgerminoma, develops, usually in postpubertal patients {17581418}.
