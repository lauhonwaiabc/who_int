7.1 Tumours of the fallopian tube: Introduction

Few types of malignancies arise from the fallopian tube, the chief one being high-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC), followed by endometrioid carcinoma, which is rare. Serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (STIC) is the precursor of fallopian tube HGSC and often coexists with ovarian/peritoneal HGSC even in the absence of fallopian tube HGSC. Tumour cell dissemination of STIC or fallopian tube HGSC is hypothesized to be the origin of many ovarian/peritoneal HGSCs. Alternatively, some ovarian HGSCs are hypothesized to arise from benign fallopian tube epithelium displaced to the ovary where it forms benign ovarian inclusion glands that subsequently acquire a TP53 mutation, leading to malignant transformation to ovarian HGSC. Risk-reducing salpingectomy in patients with hereditary risk for ovarian HGSC has been shown to be effective. Opportunistic salpingectomy in the general population may be associated with a reduced risk as well {35138400}. Pathological detection of STIC and early-stage fallopian tube HGSC is maximized by enhanced tissue processing and microscopic examination (the SEE-FIM [sectioning and extensively examining the fimbriated end] protocol). Multiorgan involvement by extrauterine HGSC poses a challenge for staging purposes. By consensus, the involvement of the fallopian tube by STIC or HGSC is evidence for assigning the primary origin to the fallopian regardless of the relative size of tumour in different organs. Secondary involvement of the fallopian tube by metastatic endometrioid carcinoma or serous carcinoma of the endometrium, by primary endocervical adenocarcinoma, or by non-gynaecological neoplasms may simulate STIC or fallopian tube HGSC.

A variety of benign neoplasms may occur in or adjacent to the fallopian tube, including serous adenofibroma/papilloma, adenomatoid tumour, and leiomyoma.

The following non-neoplastic entities in the fifth edition of the classification of the female genital tumours have been removed in the current edition: paratubal cysts, tubal hyperplasia, mucinous metaplasia, endosalpingiosis, tubo-ovarian abscess, salpingitis isthmica nodosa, metaplastic papillary lesion, and placental site nodule of the fallopian tube.
