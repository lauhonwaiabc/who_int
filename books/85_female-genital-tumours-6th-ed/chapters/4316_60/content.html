4.3.1.6 Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type

Definition

Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type, is an invasive adenocarcinoma of the cervix or vagina exhibiting mesonephric (Wolffian) differentiation and arising through mechanisms not associated with HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

During early development of the gynaecological tract, and in the absence of Müllerian inhibiting substance, atresia of the mesonephric duct can result in mesonephric remnants persisting laterally in the vagina, cervix, uterine corpus, and adnexa. Accordingly, mesonephric adenocarcinomas, which typically arise from mesonephric remnants or hyperplasia, occur most frequently deep in the lateral cervical wall; however, with larger tumours there may be involvement of the whole cervix {2252101; 15105647; 22456609}. These tumours may also occur in the vagina, again arising in the lateral walls where mesonephric remnants reside; however, before diagnosing a primary vaginal mesonephric adenocarcinoma, spread from a primary cervical neoplasm should be excluded.

Clinical features

Symptoms include abnormal vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain, and dyspareunia {11224609; 15105647; 7573674}. Patients can also present with abnormal cervical cytology specimens or symptoms related to metastatic disease to other organs {11224609; 7573674; 18580312; 27172762}.

Epidemiology

These neoplasms are rare, accounting for < 1% of cervical adenocarcinomas {29135516; 39052435}. Primary vaginal neoplasms are even more uncommon {15105647}. The average patient age at presentation is around 50–53 years {7573674; 11224609; 15105647; 26336887}.

Etiology

While the etiology is unknown, there is no association with high-risk HPV {22456609; 11021808; 31846527; 31209635; 26645463}.

Pathogenesis

Mesonephric adenocarcinomas are thought to arise from vestiges of the embryological male reproductive system (Wolffian/mesonephric duct). Most harbour KRAS mutations and gains in chromosome 1q (with a subset showing concurrent loss of 1p). Occasionally, tumours have NRAS mutations. Two thirds have mutations in chromatin remodelling genes (ARID1A/B or SMARCA4) and one third in BCOR/BCORL1 {26336887}. A minority have mutations in TP53 or CTNNB1 {31266530; 26336887}. They have a low mutational burden and absence of microsatellite instability {31266530}. Although many occur in association with and are thought to arise from mesonephric remnants or hyperplasia, the latter is not required for diagnosis nor is it associated with a KRAS mutation {28703285}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour epicentre is usually within the lateral cervical or vaginal wall, where mesonephric remnants reside, often with extension to the overlying cervical mucosa {11224609}. Full-thickness stromal invasion, circumferential involvement, ulceration, and extension into the lower uterine segment are not uncommon {11224609; 15105647; 7573674}. Some tumours can form an exophytic or polypoid mass {15105647; 7573674}. On cut section, tumours are firm and white or grey, and they can be diffusely haemorrhagic {15105647; 7573674}.

Histopathology

Mesonephric adenocarcinomas are characterized by a variety of different architectural patterns, and heterogeneous growth patterns can be a clue to the diagnosis. These varied growth patterns can all be present within an individual neoplasm, or one or more patterns may predominate. The classic pattern is individual or back-to-back tubules lined by cuboidal cells; lumina are often filled with dense eosinophilic PAS-positive secretions {7573674}. The ductal (pseudoendometrioid) pattern consists of angulated glands lined by columnar cells. Additional growth patterns include papillary, cribriform, retiform, sex cord–like, hobnail, slit-like, glomeruloid, sieve-like, spindled, and solid {30148742; 7573674; 18580312; 2252101}. Nuclei are typically relatively uniform, without overt pleomorphism, and with coarse or vesicular chromatin, sometimes with nuclear grooves, and inconspicuous nucleoli {11224609; 15105647}. Mitotic activity is variable, and intraluminal necrotic debris may be present. Importantly, squamous differentiation and cytoplasmic mucin are absent.

A population of relatively bland spindle cells is a feature of some mesonephric adenocarcinomas, and this should not result in a diagnosis of mesonephric carcinosarcoma unless there are overt nuclear atypia and a lack of epithelial biomarker expression within the spindle cell component. Heterologous elements, including chondrosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and osteosarcoma, support a diagnosis of mesonephric carcinosarcoma {7573674; 30148742; 11224609; 15105647}. There is no grading system applicable to mesonephric adenocarcinoma. Distinction from florid mesonephric hyperplasia is based on the presence of a macroscopic tumour mass, expansile growth, the presence of different architectural patterns, crowding, haphazard infiltrative growth, elevated mitotic activity, and intraluminal necrotic debris {11224609}. The demonstration of a KRAS mutation may also be of value {28703285}.

Tumours are typically positive for GATA3, PAX8, and CD10 (luminal staining pattern), negative for ER and PR, and usually negative for napsin A and AMACR {25851711; 25272301; 26135559; 30148742; 12548163; 31361605}. A subset can be positive for TTF1 and calretinin {11224609; 15105647; 30148742; 22456609}. p53 staining shows wildtype pattern {25272303}. p16 is negative/non–block-type, and HPV is not detected {25272301; 22456609; 11021808; 31846527; 31209635; 26645463}. The majority of cervical mesonephric adenocarcinomas are negative for SOX17 {39245863}.

Cytology

Loosely cohesive clusters of relatively uniform cuboidal cells with hyperchromatic nuclei but smooth nuclear contours can suggest mesonephric differentiation on a cytology sample. Additionally, mesonephric adenocarcinomas with more atypical nuclei can result in a diagnosis of malignancy based on the atypical cytological features, but classification as mesonephric adenocarcinoma would depend on histological and immunohistochemical findings {16181310}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV testing, if performed, should be negative. Demonstration of pathogenic KRAS or (less commonly) NRAS mutations may be helpful in diagnostically challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primary cervical or vaginal adenocarcinoma exhibiting mesonephric (Wolffian) differentiation.

Desirable: association with adjacent mesonephric remnants and/or mesonephric hyperplasia; positivity for GATA3, CD10 and/or TTF1; ER/PR negative; negative/non–block-type p16 staining and/or negative HPV testing.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence/metastasis can occur over long periods (mean: 4 years; range: 1–11 years) and can include both local (pelvic and abdominal) and distant sites (lung, bone, liver, mediastinum) {26336887; 11224609; 15105647; 7573674; 33165093}. In one of the largest studies of cervical mesonephric adenocarcinomas, 60% presented at stage II–IV, 50% developed recurrence (most commonly distant), and the 5-year disease-specific survival was 74% {33165093}. As primary vaginal mesonephric adenocarcinomas are rare, there is little information on their specific behaviour although recurrences appear to be less common {37510961}.
