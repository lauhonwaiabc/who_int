10.1.9.3 SMARC-deficient sarcoma

Definition

SMARC-deficient uterine sarcomas are undifferentiated neoplasms of uncertain histogenesis, typically demonstrating epithelioid cytomorphology and harbouring SWI/SNF complex alterations that inactivate SMARCA4 or rarely SMARCB1.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant rhabdoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These sarcomas usually arise in the uterine corpus; occasional cases have been reported in the uterine cervix.

Clinical features

Patients present with signs or symptoms resulting from a uterine mass, such as vaginal bleeding, or complications of pelvic or distant spread. Many patients are at an advanced stage at the time of diagnosis {29700418; 31567195}.

Epidemiology

SMARC-deficient uterine sarcomas are extremely rare (< 1% of uterine mesenchymal tumours) and tend to occur in premenopausal women (median age: 42 years, range: 24–60 years) {31190001; 31567195}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Somatic and/or germline inactivating alterations in SMARCA4 underlie these tumours {29700418; 31190001; 31567195; 31954538}. A single case with SMARCB1 inactivation has been reported {31567195}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are large (median: 106 mm, range: 55–255 mm) uterine masses with a tan-pink to white, soft, cut surface that may have haemorrhage and necrosis {31190001; 31567195}.

Histopathology

The tumours grow as sheets, nests, and cords of monomorphic, medium to large, frequently rhabdoid, epithelioid cells with prominent nucleoli. A subset consists of smaller epithelioid cells or contains a spindled component. Extracellular matrix may be hyalinized, myxohyaline, or occasionally myxoid. Mitotic activity is brisk (mean: 8 mitoses/mm2). Lymphovascular invasion, necrosis, and haemorrhage are frequently seen. In some tumours, entrapped and distorted benign endometrial or endocervical glands impart a phyllodes-like appearance.

SMARC-deficient uterine sarcomas demonstrate immunohistochemical loss of SMARCA4 (BRG1) or SMARCB1 (INI1) expression. They are negative for epithelial markers or exhibit staining of only rare cells; expression of melanocytic and myogenic markers is absent.

The main differential diagnosis is undifferentiated endometrial carcinoma, which tends to occur in older women, is often microsatellite unstable, harbours mutations in TP53, PIK3CA, PTEN, and CTNNB1, and has a distinct methylation profile {31567195; 35218556}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

These tumours harbour biallelic inactivating alterations in SMARCA4 or SMARCB1 in the absence of other molecular alterations and exhibit relatively few copy-number alterations {31567195; 35218556}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: undifferentiated epithelioid to rhabdoid neoplasm with loss of SMARCA4 (BRG1) or SMARCB1 (INI1) expression; intact mismatch repair immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: SMARCA4 or SMARCB1 inactivation; exclusion of molecular alterations associated with undifferentiated carcinoma (mutations in TP53, PIK3CA, PTEN, or CTNNB1).

Staging

SMARC-deficient uterine sarcomas are staged as uterine sarcomas using the FIGO, the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC), and the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours appear to be uniformly fatal with a median survival of 9 months {31567195}. Prognostic features have not been identified.
