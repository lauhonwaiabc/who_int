10.1.9.4 Alveolar soft part sarcoma

Definition

Alveolar soft part sarcoma (ASPS) is a rare tumour of uncertain lineage, featuring variably discohesive epithelioid cells arranged in nests, resulting in a distinctive alveolar appearance and characterized by an ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common sites in the female genital tract include the uterine corpus and cervix, vagina, and vulva.

Clinical features

Patients present with nonspecific signs and symptoms related to a mass involving the uterus, vagina, or vulva {28009610}.

Epidemiology

ASPS is a rare malignant mesenchymal tumour that uncommonly involves the female genital tract. Although ASPS primarily affects adolescents and young adults, some series report a broader demographic with a mean age of 34 years (median: 32 years; range 15–68 years) {35626258}, and others indicate a peak incidence between the second and fourth decades of life {28009610}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ASPS is characterized by an unbalanced der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25) resulting in the fusion of ASPSCR1 and TFE3 {11244503}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours have a yellow to tan or grey cut surface with occasional haemorrhage and/or necrosis.

Histopathology

ASPS typically has lobules with nested architecture in which tumour cells fill or occupy the centre of nests, and/or alveolar architecture in which there is loss of central tumour cell cohesion resulting in unoccupied spaces. Some tumours show pure sheet-like growth. In lobulated tumours, a prominent sinusoidal vasculature surrounds individual nests. Extracellular hyalinization can be present, usually focally. Classically, the cells are discohesive and uniformly polygonal, with ample granular eosinophilic cytoplasm and central or eccentrically located round nuclei with prominent nucleoli. A small subset of cells have extensive clear cytoplasm. Mitotic activity is infrequent {28009610; 8598329}. Some tumours have intracytoplasmic rectangular or rhomboid crystals that are highlighted by PASD. Immunohistochemically, TFE3 is diffusely and strongly positive in all tumours. Rare cases express muscle markers or focally express HMB45 {28009610; 8598329}.

Cytology

Cellular smears show cohesive tissue fragments and scattered cells with stripped nuclei in a clean background. Individual tumour cells are polygonal with central to eccentric round nuclei, perinucleolar chromatin clearing (vesicular in Pap), and prominent nucleoli, and abundant, well-defined to poorly defined, granular to finely vacuolated, concentric or eccentric cytoplasm, along with a variable number of binucleate to multinucleate giant cells. However, the diagnostic value of cytology is limited.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of TFE3 rearrangement {23715164} or ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion transcripts {30139549; 30459475} may be diagnostically helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nested, pseudoalveolar, or sheet-like growth; discohesive tumour cells with clear to eosinophilic granular cytoplasm; infrequent mitotic activity.

Desirable: TFE3 nuclear expression by immunohistochemistry (strong and diffuse); PAS-positive intracytoplasmic rectangular or rhomboid crystals; identification of der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25) or ASPSCR1::TFE3, in selected settings.

Staging

ASPS is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system for sarcoma.

Prognosis and prediction

ASPS is associated with late recurrence, sometimes decades, after diagnosis. Although data are limited, tumours arising in the female genital tract may have a better prognosis than tumours at other sites, and the prognosis within the female genital tract may vary, with tumours of the corpus having a superior prognosis to those of the cervix / lower uterine segment, and vulvovaginal/perineal tumours having the worst prognosis {28009610; 8598329, 37679012}. To date, only very few reported cases arising in the uterus had metastases {22977670; 4054722}.
