10.1.1.2 Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential

Definition

Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential (STUMP) is a neoplasm that shows morphological features that exceed the criteria for leiomyoma yet are insufficient for a diagnosis of leiomyosarcoma; it should be a diagnosis of exclusion.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: atypical smooth muscle neoplasm; atypical leiomyoma.

Subtype(s)

Spindle smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential; epithelioid smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential; myxoid smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential

Localization

These tumours arise in the uterus (myometrium), cervix, vagina, vulva, broad ligament, fallopian tube, or ovary.

Clinical features

Patients’ symptomatology is similar to that of patients with typical leiomyomas of the uterus. For other Müllerian sites, patients may present with a mass lesion.

Epidemiology

These smooth muscle tumours are rare. They typically occur in individuals of reproductive age or who are postmenopausal, with a mean age of approximately 43 years, which is a decade younger than the mean age of patients with leiomyosarcoma {29537683}. Patients diagnosed with a STUMP who develop recurrences are also up to 10 years younger than those with an uneventful follow-up {19342083; 19417585; 21865091}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

STUMPs are genomically heterogeneous, with features ranging from very simple profiles characterized by a few chromosomal alterations to high chromosomal instability and complexity {25932961; 29327710}. These features are similar to those of uterine leiomyosarcomas, but chromosomal gains are not as frequent {33557274; 39691991}. These findings have been reported in the spindle cell and myxoid subtypes, in which copy-number variation is important in tumour pathogenesis {31189997; 25932961; 26637855}. Unlike in leiomyoma, MED12 alterations occur only in as many as 10% of STUMPs {22768200; 26037152; 24986214}. HMGA2 alterations occur in 17.8% of cases {29258902}. FH alterations may be seen in a subset of STUMPs {39869100; 34108400}.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross features of these smooth muscle tumours often overlap with those seen in typical leiomyomas. The size of tumours associated with recurrence is similar to that of tumours that do not recur, but some neoplasms associated with recurrence have been noted to have irregular borders {29537683}.

Histopathology

This category of smooth muscle tumours is histologically heterogeneous. It has been stated that the inclusion criteria of STUMP require the presence of only one of the key features used in the diagnosis of leiomyosarcoma, which include diffuse moderate to severe atypia, tumour cell necrosis, or an elevated mitotic count (with cut-off values varying by subtype). Atypical mitoses, vascular intrusion, and infiltrative/irregular margins are acceptable as features of STUMP, provided that features diagnostic of leiomyosarcoma are absent {29537683; 33445214; 35976464}.

When an unusual smooth muscle tumour is encountered, every effort should be made to establish a diagnosis of either leiomyoma (usually one of its patterns) or leiomyosarcoma by integrating gross and microscopic features with generous sampling {26715170}.

This is a challenging diagnostic area, and the following (1–3) are general guidelines for spindle cell smooth muscle tumours to be placed in this group, but these should not be taken as strict diagnostic criteria:

(1) Nuclear atypia with 2–4 mitoses/mm2 (equating to 5–9 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area); tumours with focal, multifocal, or diffuse nuclear atypia, and lacking tumour cell necrosis, including tumours with bizarre nuclei and tumours with fumarate hydratase deficiency. Approximately 44% of STUMPs with these features fall into this category {39711162}, and 15% of these have recurred. In tumours with diffuse nuclear atypia and uncertain mitotic counts due to prominent karyorrhexis, PHH3 immunohistochemistry has been proposed for the evaluation of mitotic activity {27864989; 19483635; 26191133}.

(2) Tumours with tumour cell necrosis: In the absence of other worrisome features, a smooth muscle tumour with this feature recurred in 32% of cases {39711162}. Because of the difficulty of assessment of tumour cell necrosis and its confusion with early infarct-type necrosis {17667546; 23552382}, this diagnosis is appropriate for any bland-appearing smooth muscle tumour containing unequivocal tumour cell necrosis or necrosis of uncertain type. Pregnancy and progesterone agents {3182168; 4073354; 26685083}, gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonists {9100066; 14615835; 9563785}, anti-fibrinolytic agents such as tranexamic acid {17667546; 27801754}, or recent uterine artery embolization {12548162; 20407327; 15958862} may produce incipient necrosis, mimic tumour cell necrosis, and should be excluded.

(3) Tumours with a high mitotic count: Smooth muscle tumours that lack cytological atypia and tumour cell necrosis but demonstrate a mitotic count ≥ 8.4 mitoses/mm² (equivalent to ≥ 20 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area) have been associated with a recurrence rate of 6% (see #52654Table 10.XXX) {39711162}. In contrast, no recurrences were reported in a cohort of 42 tumours with similar morphology but a lower mitotic count of ≥ 6.3 mitoses/mm² (equating to ≥ 15 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area) and < 8.4 mitoses/mm² (equating to < 20 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area) {8179071; 19417585; 29537683}. These observations support a threshold of ≥ 8.4 mitoses/mm² and may offer a more reliable criterion for stratifying recurrence risk in this subset of smooth muscle tumours.

(4) Epithelioid and myxoid tumours: Epithelioid and myxoid STUMPs are exceedingly rare, and a diagnosis should only be made when differential diagnoses have been excluded (e.g. inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, PEComa). Tumours that exceed the criteria for leiomyoma (#53457Box 10.XXX) but fall below the threshold of leiomyosarcoma (#55619Box 10.XXX) fall into the STUMP category {9130984; 56982; 26866354; 28787279}.

Cytology

Smears show features, especially regarding the degree of atypia, that are intermediate between those of leiomyomas and leiomyosarcomas.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Currently, no well-established molecular tests can be used for the diagnosis. However, genomic profiling of uterine smooth muscle tumours from biopsies and resection specimens is emerging as a potential tool, although more research is needed.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: smooth muscle tumour in which histological features do not meet the diagnostic criteria for a leiomyoma or a leiomyosarcoma.

Staging

These tumours are not usually staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence of STUMPs occurs on average in 14% of cases (range: 0–50% across 18 outcome-based studies {39711162; 39330016}), depending on microscopic criteria (see #52654Table 10.XXX). Atypical mitoses, vascular intrusion, and infiltrative/irregular margins are not independent risk factors for recurrence {29537683; 33445214; 35976464}. Epithelioid and myxoid subtypes demonstrate higher recurrence rates {35976464; 18162776; 29537683}. The average time to recurrence (47 months) exceeds that of leiomyosarcomas, with histological features in recurrent tumours resembling those in either the primary tumour or a leiomyosarcoma. Patients with recurrences have a median survival of 5.5 years {21865091}. Several studies have attempted to assess the risk of recurrence in smooth muscle tumours using risk-stratification algorithms based on immunohistochemical markers (such as Ki-67, PR, and p16) and molecular-based models {35976464; 39711162}. Recently, a Cox regression analysis of 171 STUMPs showed that abnormal p53 and/or diffuse p16 positivity correlated with a 54–56% recurrence risk versus 7% in tumours with wildtype p53 and/or focal/negative for p16 {34481212}. Loss of ATRX or DAXX expression predicts adverse outcomes {27499896; 30423196; 26891131}. Although some of these methods are promising, especially in identifying high-risk cases, they remain limited by a lack of standardized protocols and require validation in larger, independent cohorts before they can be integrated into routine diagnostic practice.

Gains of chromosomes 5p and 17p and loss of chromosome 13q indicate a poor prognosis {25932961; 29327710}. A genomic index < 10 is associated with benign behaviour {38481055}. Preliminary data suggest that the use of a genomic index of copy-number alterations may predict tumour biology {36287348}. Genomic profiling and artificial intelligence–driven approaches have shown promise in capturing tumour complexity and refining prognostic assessment {36675952; 35954449; 40602459}. However, the absence of standardized cut-offs across different technologies and platforms, along with limited experience in rare histological subtypes such as the myxoid and epithelioid subtypes, currently limits their routine diagnostic application {33099852; 25932961; 29327710}.
