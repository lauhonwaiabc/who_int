10.1.1.3 Leiomyosarcoma

Definition

Leiomyosarcoma is a malignant smooth muscle neoplasm.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Spindle cell (conventional) leiomyosarcoma; epithelioid leiomyosarcoma; myxoid leiomyosarcoma

Localization

The uterus is the most common primary site. Most arise in the corpus and are intramural (most common), subserosal, or submucosal. One percent originate in the cervix {35121810; 20940535; 21751199; 23456762}. Extrauterine primary sites are less common, and include vagina, vulva (principally labia majora), uteropelvic ligaments, and ovary {8669526; 28786880; 7617359; 1879812; 30702465; 15489647}.

Clinical features

Patients with uterine, vaginal, or adnexal tumours present with abnormal vaginal bleeding, a pelvic mass, and/or abdominopelvic pain, or as an incidental finding {12798712; 30702465; 15489647}. Gastrointestinal or urinary tract symptoms may occur as a result of regional spread. Patients with vulvar tumours present with a vulvar mass {28786880}.

Epidemiology

Leiomyosarcoma is the most common sarcoma of the uterus, vulva, vagina, and uteropelvic ligaments in adults {7617359; 28786880; 30702465}. Leiomyosarcoma accounts for 50–60% of all uterine sarcomas and 1–2% of all uterine malignancies {15047237; 19236512; 27149024; 27177280; 39725428}. Its incidence is approximately 1.0–1.5 cases per 100 000 person-years [[Bartwal D, Gupta S, Mishra R, et al. Leiomyosarcoma: Epidemiology and Treatment Approaches. Oncology and Radiotherapy. 2024,18(8):001–006.]] {15047237; 27177280}. Gynaecological leiomyosarcoma occurs over a wide age range with a median age in perimenopause {12798712; 18189292; 21751199; 23456762; 28786880; 30702465; 15489647}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Leiomyosarcoma frequently displays extensive genomic instability and is characterized by recurrent SNVs and complex numerical and structural chromosomal alterations, including chromothripsis and whole genome duplication {29100075; 29321523; 33876771}. Most leiomyosarcomas arise de novo. Rare uterine tumours are associated with a leiomyoma (conventional or subtype) or smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential {39691991; 36704944; 19633649}. Clonal evolution has been demonstrated in leiomyosarcoma associated with leiomyoma with bizarre nuclei {39691991}.

Genotyping studies of gynaecological leiomyosarcomas are mainly limited to uterine tumours, which are characterized by altered function of p53, RB1, and PTEN and disruption of alternative telomere lengthening and DNA damage response pathways. The most commonly altered genes include TP53, RB1, ATRX, and PTEN, and these mutations frequently co-occur; some of these alterations can be detected by immunohistochemistry {36788080}. Aberrations involving CDKN2A, DAXX, MEN1, and MDM2 are significantly less common {29100075; 29321523; 35705560; 32299819; 33876771; 36788080}. Recurrent gain of chromosome 17p12 (MYOCD and MAP2K4) and amplification of chromosome 8p24 (MYC) and chromosome 5p15 (TERT) have been reported {33876771; 36788080}. DNA damage response gene alterations frequently involve the homologous recombination pathway, including alterations in BRCA1/2, RAD51, ATM, CHEK1/2, XRCC1/3, ERCC5, PTEN, and FANCA1/2 {29321523; 33283135}. Rare uterine tumours are characterized by CDKN2C deletion, enriched for alterations in CIC1, CDKN2A, and/or RAD51B and 1p/19q codeletion, with low rates of TP53, ATRX, and RB1 mutation {33015533}. Rare uterine, vaginal, and vulvar leiomyosarcomas associated with high-risk HPV51 infection are enriched for ATRX and TSC1 mutations and lack TP53 and RB1 mutations {35034043}.

Transcriptomic and proteomic studies indicate multiple subgroups of leiomyosarcoma, including muscle-enriched and dedifferentiated clusters {29321523; 34301934; 25896974; 37386008}.

A subset of gynaecological leiomyosarcomas with heterogeneous morphology, including a proportion of epithelioid and myxoid leiomyosarcoma subtypes, demonstrate recurrent gene fusions, likely resulting in promoter hijacking. NR4A3 rearrangement with fusion partners PGR, CARMN, ACTB, and SLCO5A1 and PLAG1 rearrangement with fusion partners TRPS1, PUM1, CHCHD7, TRIM13, KATNBL1 (C15orf29), CD33, MYOCD, FRMD6, PTK2, YWHAZ, and RAD51B are detected in a subset of epithelioid and/or myxoid leiomyosarcomas {30829727; 38508521; 40072944; 30489320; 38942115}.

Macroscopic appearance

Leiomyosarcoma typically shows a soft, bulging, fleshy cut surface, with frequent necrosis and/or haemorrhage. Myxoid tumours may be gelatinous or mucoid and friable {7180961; 26866354}. Tumour size varies by primary site, with average diameters of 100 mm, 60 mm, and 150 mm in uterine, vulvovaginal, and uteropelvic ligament/ovarian tumours, respectively {35121810; 20940535; 8600076; 33333592; 15489647; 30702465; 28786880; 1250563}.

Histopathology

Three main subtypes are recognized: spindle cell (conventional), epithelioid, and myxoid. One or more morphologies can occur in a single tumour. Histological criteria for malignancy are based on the presence of tumour cell necrosis, extent and degree of nuclear atypia, and mitotic index, defined below. Thresholds for these criteria differ between tumour subtypes. These criteria are applicable to tumours throughout the gynaecological tract, although experience for non-uterine tumours is limited.

Tumour cell necrosis is characterized by an abrupt transition from viable to necrotic tumour, without intervening hyalinization or granulation tissue. Viable perivascular tumour cells may be observed adjacent to blood vessels within otherwise necrotic areas. This feature, which can be seen in leiomyosarcoma, may simply reflect regional differences in oxygenation rather than a specific pattern of tumour cell necrosis {8179071}. Distinction between tumour cell necrosis and infarct-type necrosis is subject to interobserver variability {23552382}.

Moderate atypia is defined by intermediate-size nuclei with irregular nuclear contours, coarse or dense chromatin, and occasionally prominent nucleoli. Marked atypia is characterized by striking nuclear pleomorphism, enlargement, hyperchromasia, prominent nucleoli, and multinucleated tumour cells {8179071}.

Mitotic counts are obtained using the highest-count method in the area(s) of greatest mitotic activity {8600076; 8179071; 9130984; 939541}.

Uterine leiomyosarcoma diagnosed by these criteria are considered intrinsically high-grade. Well-established criteria for prospective diagnosis of low-grade uterine leiomyosarcoma are currently lacking.

Spindle cell (conventional) leiomyosarcoma typically comprises long, intersecting and/or compact, disorganized, cellular fascicles of spindle cells with eosinophilic fibrillar cytoplasm. Nuclear atypia is often moderate to marked. Multinucleated tumour cells and/or osteoclast-like giant cells may be present, but some tumours are monomorphic and appear deceptively bland at low magnification. The mitotic count is usually high (mean: 6–8 mitoses/mm2, equating to 15–20 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm diameter), and atypical mitoses are common {21751199; 23456762; 35121810}. Tumour cell necrosis, infiltrative growth, and vascular invasion are common {8179071; 35121810}. At least two of the following are required for diagnosis: (1) tumour cell necrosis; (2) moderate to marked nuclear atypia; and (3) ≥ 4 mitoses/mm2 (equating to ≥ 10 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm diameter).

Epithelioid leiomyosarcoma is defined by ≥ 50% epithelioid cytomorphology, characterized by round or polygonal, often dyscohesive cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm, with a nested, corded, nodular, or diffuse arrangement {8600076; 34419987}. Epithelioid areas frequently blend with conventional spindle cell cytomorphology. Rhabdoid or signet ring–like cells, cytoplasmic clearing, pseudoglandular spaces, and stromal hyalinization may be seen. Myometrial and vascular invasion can be present. At least two of the following are required for diagnosis: (1) tumour cell necrosis; (2) moderate to marked nuclear atypia; and (3) ≥ 1.7 mitoses/mm2 (equating to ≥ 4 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm diameter) {56982; 9130984; 8600076; 34419987}.

Myxoid leiomyosarcoma is defined by myxoid stroma. Most studies have not quantified the extent of myxoid matrix required for designation as a myxoid smooth muscle tumour; however, two studies suggest that myxoid stroma should occupy ≥ 50% of the tumour {26866354; 31189997}. The tumour cells are spindled and/or epithelioid with scant to moderate eosinophilic cytoplasm. Tumours are variably cellular, with tumour cells arranged individually, in bundles or thin fascicles, or in broader intersecting fascicles. Mitoses range from 0 to 13 mitoses (mean: 5.5) / mm2 (equating to 0–30 mitoses [mean: 13] / 10 HPF of 0.55 mm diameter). Tumours frequently show coagulative necrosis and bulbous nested, tongue-like, or irregular destructive infiltration of the myometrium, but some are circumscribed {22175894; 26866354; 7180961; 3988243; 8600076; 27717885; 30489320}. At least two of the following are required for diagnosis: (1) tumour cell necrosis; (2) at least focal moderate nuclear atypia; (3) ≥ 0.8 mitoses/mm2 (equating to ≥ 2 mitoses /10 HPF of 0.55 mm diameter); and (4) infiltrative growth.

In rare cases, a conventional smooth muscle tumour (conventional or leiomyoma with different patterns, smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential, or spindle cell or epithelioid leiomyosarcoma) is associated with pleomorphic tumour foci showing at most rare cells positive for smooth muscle markers {36704944; 38248037}. Heterologous osteosarcomatous, rhabdomyosarcomatous, chondrosarcomatous, and liposarcomatous differentiation may be seen {32687944; 36704944; 38248037; 35687345}. A diagnosis of dedifferentiated leiomyosarcoma may be considered in this setting.

Gynaecological leiomyosarcomas with recurrent NR4A3 fusions comprise sheet-like or lobulated growth of epithelioid and/or spindled cells with uniform moderate nuclear atypia, myxoid and haemorrhagic stroma, and prominent microcyst to macrocyst formation {30829727; 38508521}.

PLAG1-rearranged uterine sarcoma is frequently indistinguishable from myxoid leiomyosarcoma without gene rearrangement {30489320}. Some may show morphology resembling myxoid liposarcoma, osteosarcoma, and pleomorphic sarcoma {38942115; 34184333}.

Leiomyosarcomas express desmin, h-caldesmon, and smooth muscle actin, but expression can be weak and/or patchy if the tumour is poorly differentiated, myxoid, or epithelioid {11257619; 11914617}. Positivity for CD10, epithelial membrane antigen, and broad-spectrum cytokeratins is common, with EMA and cytokeratin positivity being frequent in epithelioid tumours {11914617; 10692172}. Estrogen and progesterone receptors are frequently positive {27149024; 36270831}. Rare tumours demonstrate FH deficiency {36729957}. Diffuse nuclear NR4A3 (NOR1) is expressed in tumours harbouring NR4A3 fusion {38508521}. Diffuse nuclear PLAG1 expression frequently correlates with underlying PLAG1 fusion but may be seen in a subset of myxoid leiomyosarcomas without detectable PLAG1 rearrangement {31189997; 30489320}.

Cytology

Smears are mostly hypercellular, comprising tissue fragments and scattered cells. There are parallelly arranged fascicles of spindle cells with cigar-shaped nuclei that are blunt-ended, and occasionally indented or segmented, exhibiting significant atypia. Besides spindle cells, there is a varying admixture of epithelioid and pleomorphic cells, including binucleated giant cells with intracytoplasmic granules, along with mitotic figures and tumour necrosis {12619091; 16900474}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular confirmation of NR4A3 or PLAG1 fusions may be helpful in uterine mesenchymal tumours with epithelioid and/or myxoid histology and cytological uniformity.

Staging

Uterine leiomyosarcoma is staged using FIGO and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems. Leiomyosarcomas of the vulva, vagina, and ovary are staged according to the appropriate AJCC/UICC TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

Gynaecological leiomyosarcoma is associated with poor prognosis. Stratified by stage, uterine leiomyosarcoma has 5-year survival of 48–77%, 60%, 45%, and 29% for stage I, II, III, and IV, respectively {18189292; 20940535}. Various nomograms incorporating clinicopathological parameters (patient age, tumour size, local extension, distant metastases, mitotic index, malignant peritoneal cytology) or genetic parameters (genomic index, 5p and 17p gains including MYOCD, 13p loss including RB1, TP53 mutation, 20q amplification, ATRX mutations) and transcriptomic signature complexity index in sarcomas (CINSARC) have been developed {35121810; 39711162; 21751199; 23456762; 29327710; 38488807; 31796515}. However, no grading or risk-stratification system has achieved widespread adoption. RAD51B fusions and PDGFRB mutations may portend worse prognosis, but experience is limited {38141829}.

Frequent homologous recombination deficiency provides rationale for poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibition, with efficacy demonstrated in the setting of somatic BRCA2 deletions {40117531}.
