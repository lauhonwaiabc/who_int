10.1.6.1 Kaposi sarcoma

Definition

Kaposi sarcoma (KS) is a locally aggressive KSHV/HHV8 induced endothelial proliferation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: idiopathic multiple pigmented sarcoma of skin; angiosarcoma multiplex; granuloma multiplex haemorrhagicum.

Subtype(s)

Classic indolent KS; endemic African KS; AIDS-associated KS; iatrogenic KS

Localization

KS can be limited to the vulva or disseminated {16363765}. Although the vulva is the most common female genital tract location, KS can rarely involve the vagina and cervix {24052876; 41607084}.

Clinical features

Vulvar KS manifests as single or multiple bluish-red masses {7675418} or exophytic indurated nodules, and can mimic a papilloma or abscess {15898528}. Lesions can be asymptomatic or associated with pain or pruritus.

Epidemiology

KS is an extremely rare tumour in most regions of the world, with global incidence rates of 0.3–0.5 cases per 100 000 population per year. Also, the female genital tract is an uncommon localization for KS lesions; particularly rare is the occurrence of a vulvar lesion, with only a few cases reported in the literature {34830846}. AIDS-associated KS {16105346; 15881707; 11054682; 15898528; 33909288} and classic (associated with Mediterranean, eastern European, or Ashkenazi Jewish descent) KS {26082598; 41607084} are the most frequent epidemiological types.

Cases typically occur in the second to fourth decades of life, or rarely postmenopausally {26082598}.

Etiology

KS is consistently associated with KSHV/HHV8, which has been established as a key risk factor.

Pathogenesis

Disease expression results from complex interactions among genetic, immunological, and environmental factors {11423257; 11536246}. KSHV/HHV8, which is a mainly sexually transmitted DNA virus, can be detected in all forms of KS (> 95% of AIDS-related and non-related cases) {7723505; 12244338; 10749966}, with genetic variants linked to specific populations {20079510}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients with KS presents with variably sized haemorrhagic nodules or, less often, necrotic poorly defined masses.

Histopathology

In the patch stage of KS, numerous small slit-like vascular spaces lined by flat to oval endothelial cells dissect surrounding collagen fibres. Ovoid and spindled endothelial cells with minimal or no atypia are admixed with lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates, extravasated erythrocytes, and haemosiderin deposits, sometimes with intracellular/extracellular hyaline globules. In the plaque and nodular stages, all features are exaggerated. A rare pattern with angiosarcoma-like histopathology features has been reported to occur in the skin {35948518}. Lesional cells express endothelial markers (e.g. CD31, CD34, ERG) and lymphatic markers (e.g. D2-40), with almost invariable nuclear KSHV/HHV8 positivity.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: proliferation of slit-like vascular spaces lined by minimally to mildly atypical cells, dissection of collagen fibres, extravasated erythrocytes.

Desirable: nuclear KSHV/HHV8 expression by immunohistochemistry (in selected settings).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Disease evolution depends on the epidemiological-clinical type and disease extent. The degree of immunosuppression at the time of diagnosis is the most important determinant of survival in KS overall. Metastatic disease is rare {15898528}.
