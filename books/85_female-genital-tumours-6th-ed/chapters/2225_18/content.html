2.2.2.5 Phyllodes tumour

Definition

Phyllodes tumour is a biphasic neoplasm that displays prominent intracanalicular architectural pattern with leaf-like stromal fronds, stromal hypercellularity, and is lined by mammary-type epithelium.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The labium majus is the most common site of occurrence {25357118}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a vulvar mass that may be rapidly growing {20035149; 25357118; 23354802; 21293278}.

Epidemiology

These are very uncommon vulvar neoplasms. The average patient age at presentation is 39 years (range: 17–69 years) {20035149; 25357118; 23354802; 21293278}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These are thought to arise from anogenital mammary-like glands. Proliferative lesions of the anogenital mammary-like glands have been reported in the setting of germline PTEN mutation (Cowden syndrome) {27090873; 33850997}. The only reported genomic study analysed 2 low-grade vulvar phyllodes tumours, one of which showed mutations involving ABL1 and TP53 genes {28648934}.

Macroscopic appearance

These are variably circumscribed masses with a solid or solid and cystic cut surface. Leaf-like structures may be evident grossly. The average size is 33 mm (range: 7–66 mm) {20035149; 25357118; 23354802; 21293278}.

Histopathology

Phyllodes tumours contain bland glandular elements lined by mammary-like epithelium, with a prominent leaf-like stromal growth protruding into the glandular lumina. They typically display regional variability in stromal cellularity, with band-like areas of hypercellularity and increased mitotic activity around the epithelial components (periglandular cuffing). Phyllodes tumours are classified as benign, borderline, or malignant types; it is recommended that similar criteria be used as are used in the breast {26768026}. See the WHO classification of breast tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]].

Cytology

The cytopathology features for vulvar phyllodes tumour have not yet been documented in the cytopathology literature.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: biphasic histology; mammary-like epithelium with a prominent leaf-like architecture; hypercellular stroma.

Staging

Because the stromal component of phyllodes tumours represents the neoplastic component, malignant phyllodes tumours may be staged as sarcomas using the ninth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025.]].

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 20% of reported cases have recurred locally, including some histologically benign tumours. There have been no reported cases of distant metastases {20035149; 25357118}. It is recommended that similar criteria be used to predict malignant behaviour as are used in the breast; however, because these are uncommon vulvar neoplasms, the criteria predicting malignant behaviour are not well established {26768026}.
