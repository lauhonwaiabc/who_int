2.2.1.5 Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-independent

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), HPV-independent (HPV-I), is a malignant squamous tumour with stromal invasion, arising through mechanisms not associated with HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Aberrant p53 (p53abn) and p53 wild-type (p53wt)

Localization

External female genitalia, including labia majora, labia minora, vestibule, clitoris, vaginal introitus, perineum, and urethral meatus

Clinical features

HPV-independent vulvar SCC often occur in association with underlying inflammatory lesions (lichen sclerosus or lichen planus) {35285455}. Tumours typically present as asymptomatic masses or ulcers, occasionally accompanied by pruritus, bleeding, or pain {39857968}. Multiple primary tumours and/or high FIGO stage are common at presentation.

Epidemiology

General epidemiology of vulvar SCC (including HPV-associated and HPV-independent tumours) is discussed in section Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-associated. Marked differences in the proportion of vulvar SCCs arising through HPV-independent mechanisms have been reported, ranging from < 5% in several sub-Saharan African countries to > 80% in European countries, reported in different case series {23886586; 36214794}. The peak incidence of HPV-independent vulvar SCC is in the eighth decade of life, but it can occur in women < 60 years of age {28815579}. Risk factors for HPV-independent SCC include advanced age and presence of longstanding inflammatory lesions involving the vulvar skin {35285455}.

Etiology

The precise etiology remains incompletely understood. While these tumours demonstrate a well-established association with chronic inflammatory dermatoses, the role of persistent inflammation in malignant transformation continues to be investigated {39787746}.

Pathogenesis

The favoured mechanism underlying vulvar squamous cell carcinogenesis, in most cases, is thought to be persistent epithelial destruction and regeneration with progressive dermal sclerosis, leading to oxidative stress and (epi)genetic events.

Two clinically distinct subtypes of HPV-I SCC have been recently delineated: those associated with a pathogenic mutation in TP53, HPV-I (p53 aberrant) SCC (~80% of all HPV-independent SCCs) and those with wildtype TP53, HPV-I (p53 wildtype) SCC {32972785; 37099408}. These subtypes can be identified on the basis of tumour protein 53 (p53) immunohistochemistry staining pattern, with the former showing mutation-type p53 expression while the latter showing wildtype expression {32203095}. Both types of tumours frequently show mutations in TERT, CDKN2A, amplifications of EGFR, and PDL1 expression {39089654; 34650187; 34209172; 39481051; 32923875}. The alterations typical of HPV-I (p53wt) SCC are not well defined, but studies suggest enrichment in PIK3CA and HRAS mutations {34650187; 37131274; 28899974}.

HPV-I (p53abn) VIN, which typically shows morphological features of differentiated VIN, is considered to be the precursor lesion of p53abn SCC {20062014}. HPV-I (p53wt) VIN, most commonly showing morphological features of verruciform acanthotic VIN (vaVIN), is considered the precursor of p53wt SCC {35437330; 39868730}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are frequently exophytic masses, which may be ulcerated.

Histopathology

HPV-independent SCCs are typically keratinizing tumours with infiltrative growth, characterized by small tumour nests forming frequent keratin pearls. Single-cell budding at the invasive front (spray-like pattern), frequently accompanied by marked desmoplasia, is suggestive of epithelial–mesenchymal transition and correlates with aggressive clinical behaviour {27327194}. Other patterns such as non-keratinizing, basaloid, or warty {28815579}, as well as acantholytic (pseudoglandular or adenoid), spindle cell (pseudosarcomatous) areas with fibromyxoid stroma may also occur. Verrucous carcinomas are part of the spectrum of the HPV-independent SCC, showing a special verruciform growth pattern with bulbous, broad-front invasion, minimal atypia, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. This latter pattern shows usually wildtype p53 immunohistochemical expression with scattered positive cells {27834349}. Independently from the histological pattern, the adjacent epithelium frequently shows precursor lesion: either dVIN, indicative of p53 aberrant subtype, or vaVIN, indicative of p53 wildtype HPV-I SCC subtypes, as well as inflammatory lesions, such as lichen sclerosus, or lichen planus, which can be seen in both subtypes of HPV-independent SCC.

Diagnosing vulvar SCC requires unequivocal evidence of invasion, which may be difficult to confirm in superficially invasive lesions or in small biopsy samples. Irregular, angulated nests, abrupt keratinization, loss of polarity, cytoplasmic eosinophilia of the invading cells, and desmoplastic or inflammatory stroma are histological features indicative of invasiveness. Some cases might show button-hole type of invasion.

Traditionally, depth of invasion is measured from the most superficial dermal papilla adjacent to the tumour to the deepest invasion point (conventional method). An alternative approach {25189641}, currently endorsed by the FIGO 2021, measures from the basement membrane of the deepest adjacent non-tumour rete ridge to the deepest invasion point {34520062}. The alternative depth of invasion measurement method downstages some vulval SCCs from IB to IA, reducing the requirement lymphadenectomy in 19% of patients, thereby decreasing overtreatment morbidity {32366757}.

Definitive distinction from HPV-associated SCC is not reliable when based solely on H&E morphology {28815579}. To confirm HPV-independent status, p16 immunohistochemistry serves as a first-line test {28815579}, where negative or non–block-type staining reliably predicts absence of high-risk HPV infection {26630231}. However, since p16 overexpression can occur independently of HPV infection, particularly in tumours with concurrent TP53 mutations {36828360}, direct molecular testing for high-risk HPV (via DNA PCR, mRNA in situ hybridization, or other nucleic acid–based methods) may be necessary to definitively exclude HPV involvement. Notably, rare HPV-associated tumours may also exhibit complete p16 negativity; this should be suspected in tumours with basal-sparing/mid-epithelial p53 expression (discussed below), which is highly specific to HPV-associated SCC {36828360}.

p53 immunostaining is needed for subtype diagnosis of HPV-I SCC and has to be interpreted following a vulvar-specific pattern-based approach {32203095; 32236967}, in which six different patterns are recognized. Wildtype p53 expression, indicative of TP53 wildtype gene status, can be either mid-epithelial expression with basal sparing, and/or show staining of scattered basal cells, with variable intensity of staining. The four mutation-type expression patterns, indicative of TP53-mutant gene status, are basal overexpression, diffuse overexpression, null (absent) and cytoplasmic.

Grading systems, based on either nuclear pleomorphism or differentiation, do not correlate with behaviour and the International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting (ICCR) recommends not to grade these neoplasms {36305532}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Nucleic acid–based testing for neither HPV nor DNA sequencing to identify TP53 mutations {39089654} are needed in most cases, but they can be used if p16 and p53 immunostaining give indeterminate results.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive carcinoma with squamous differentiation; p16 negativity and/or negative HPV molecular testing; p53 IHC (or TP53 sequencing) for subclassification into p53abn and p53wt subtypes.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Stage and lymph node status are the strongest prognostic factors {32988675}. However, molecular subtype (p53abn or p53wt) is also of prognostic significance {32972785; 39089654}. Patients with HPV-I (p53abn) SCC have a shorter overall survival, recurrence-free period, and higher locoregional recurrence compared with those with HPV-associated SCC (HPV-A SCC) {32972785}. Growing evidence suggests that prognosis for HPV-I (p53wt) SCC has a prognosis that falls between HPV-A and HPV-I (p53abn) SCC {28899974; 32236967}. There is also evidence that recurrence-free survival of these HPV-I (p53wt) SCC may closely resemble that of HPV-I (p53abn) SCC {37099408}, while disease-specific survival might be similar to that in HPV-A SCC {39089654}.

Recurrence and cancer-related mortality are also associated with the distance of pathological margins {34365709}. A minimum pathological margin of 8 mm (clinical clearance of 10–20 mm) was suggested to significantly reduce the risk of local recurrence {17095080}; however, some recent studies put this cut-off value into question {27497345}, and further study with stratification of tumours based on HPV and p53 status is needed.

HPV-I SCC arising on precursors with undifferentiated features (HSIL-like lesions) may be more likely to recur {36593525}. SCC harbouring both TP53 mutations and CCND1 gains may show adverse outcomes, for both recurrence and disease-specific survival {39089654}. Treating residual dermatoses may help reduce the risk of SCC recurrence {32374363}.
