3.3.0.1 Mullerian papilloma

Definition

Müllerian papilloma is a benign papillary Müllerian epithelial tumour that can occur in the vagina or cervix.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: Müllerian papilloma of infancy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Müllerian papillomas are typically mucosal based but occasionally may be intramural. They occur in the vagina and cervix with relatively similar frequency {26948653}. Vaginal tumours most commonly occur in the upper vagina {26948653}.

Clinical features

Most cases occur in prepubertal girls, with a few reported in adolescents or young adults (age range: 2–24 years) {26948653; 25330952; 21073126; 11737317; 9891250; 14043062}. Most patients present with vaginal bleeding or discharge {26948653}.

Epidemiology

These are extremely rare vaginal and cervical neoplasms. The predominantly occur in children, with rare adult cases reported {7979569; 26948653; 25330952; 17703455}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is uncertain, but this neoplasm is believed to arise from Müllerian epithelium {9891250; 6170416}.

Macroscopic appearance

Mucosal-based cases are friable polypoid lesions and measure < 50 mm {26948653; 25330952; 19616458}, whereas intramural examples are solid-cystic mural masses {9891250; 6170416}.

Histopathology

Histology shows an exophytic or intramural lesion with a papillary architecture and slender fibrovascular cores lined by cuboidal or columnar epithelium, sometimes with a hobnail morphology, with little or no cytological atypia {9891250; 11737317; 19616458}. Nuclei are uniform, with fine chromatin. Mitoses are generally absent or sparse. Squamous metaplasia may be present.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: papillary architecture; cuboidal or columnar epithelium with no cytological atypia.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical outcome is favourable, with almost all cases having a benign outcome {26948653; 19616458}, but recurrence, including multiple recurrences, has been reported {30406704; 7979569; 10193336}. Malignant transformation to clear cell carcinoma after multiple recurrences was reported in one vaginal case {12713630}.
