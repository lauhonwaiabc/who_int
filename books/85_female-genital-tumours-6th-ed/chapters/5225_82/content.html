5.2.2.5 Dedifferentiated and undifferentiated carcinomas

Definition

Undifferentiated carcinoma of the endometrium is a malignant epithelial neoplasm with no overt cell lineage differentiation. Dedifferentiated carcinoma is composed of an undifferentiated carcinoma and a differentiated component.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterus

Clinical features

Most patients are perimenopausal or postmenopausal, and many report postmenopausal bleeding at presentation. A minority have abdominal pain. Patients can present with symptoms related to locally advanced disease and/or metastatic disease {16160474; 16306785; 20305618}, as about half the patients (48%) present with advanced stage (stage III-IV) disease {16160474; 16306785; 20305618; 26126041; 33125840; 36252860; 38780000; 39811899}.

Epidemiology

Dedifferentiated/undifferentiated carcinomas are uncommon, accounting for about 1–2% of endometrial cancers in case series {20305618; 35128856}. Perimenopausal and postmenopausal women are more frequently affected {20305618; 35128856}. An association with Lynch syndrome has been suggested {17365829; 20305618}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The undifferentiated component of dedifferentiated carcinomas is clonally related to the accompanying differentiated carcinoma, indicating that it arises through a process of dedifferentiation from the latter {24451280; 27491810; 26743474}. Inactivating mutations involving chromatin remodelling genes (core SWI/SNF complex components) are the best understood mechanism accounting for dedifferentiation; two thirds of dedifferentiated carcinomas and half of undifferentiated carcinomas have mutations in SMARCA4, SMARCB1, or both ARID1A and ARID1B together in the undifferentiated component. Half of undifferentiated carcinomas do as well {25920939; 26126041; 26743474; 27562491; 27656868; 28863077}.

Activating PI3K pathway mutations involving PTEN, PIK3CA, and/or PIK3R1 are seen in more than half of the tumours {24451280; 27491810; 36252860}, and these mutations are present in both the differentiated and undifferentiated components.

One half to two thirds of dedifferentiated and half of undifferentiated carcinomas are mismatch repair–deficient {20305618; 26598976; 26743474; 27491810; 28863077}. Dedifferentiated/undifferentiated carcinomas can also arise in other molecular settings, with NSMP endometrioid carcinoma being the next most common, followed less frequently by POLE and p53abn {28498284; 27491810; 39811899; 38780000; 33712277}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most undifferentiated carcinomas form large, polypoid, intraluminal masses ranging from 20 to 150 mm in size {20305618}. Necrosis is common. Most tumours involve the uterine corpus; however, many involve the lower uterine segment as well as the cervix.

Histopathology

Undifferentiated carcinoma

Undifferentiated carcinomas are one of the several tumour types that display solid architecture. They should not demonstrate epithelial differentiation on H&E slides (cell cohesion, glands, nesting, or trabecular architecture), but focal abrupt keratinization can be seen. The tumour is composed of small to intermediate-sized cells of relatively uniform size arranged in sheets, and it can therefore resemble FIGO grade 3 endometrioid carcinoma, lymphoma, plasmacytoma, high-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma, and high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma. The nuclear chromatin is usually condensed, sometimes with small nucleoli, and numerous mitotic figures are readily identified (> 25 mitoses /2 mm2, equating to > 30 mitoses /10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area). Tumours can have rhabdoid morphology and a focally myxoid matrix. Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are often numerous.

Dedifferentiated carcinoma

Nearly half of undifferentiated carcinomas contain a component of differentiated carcinoma, which is most frequently a FIGO grade 1 or 2 endometrioid carcinoma, although some can arise in association with high-grade carcinoma (e.g. FIGO grade 3 endometrioid carcinoma or serous carcinoma) {27101785; 27491810; 32557836; 37222195}. The two components can vary in proportion, and the interface between the two components can be abrupt (imparting a biphasic appearance) or admixed.

Immunohistochemistry

Undifferentiated carcinomas display limited evidence of epithelial differentiation. Diffuse, strong keratin expression is not typical of undifferentiated carcinoma. Because pancytokeratin can often be completely negative in these tumours, using more than one epithelial marker is recommended to confirm epithelial origin. Among the markers of epithelial differentiation, CK8/18 and EMA are more likely to be positive than others. Tumour cells express vimentin but not ER, PR, or E-cadherin {23743934; 30660868}. PAX8 is usually negative, but it can be focally positive in scattered single or small clusters of cells {26598976; 27101785}. Chromogranin and/or synaptophysin staining can be present in a minority of tumour cells, usually < 10% {19188820; 35394077}. Mismatch repair protein deficiency is seen in 58% of the cases (loss of both MLH1 and PMS2 staining in 80%, loss of PMS2 staining in 10%, and loss of MSH2 and/or MSH6 staining in 8%) and mutation-type p53 expression (typically the diffuse/overexpression pattern) is seen in 15% of cases {20305618; 26126041; 27491810; 33125840; 36252860; 38780000; 39811899}.

Loss of expression of SMARCA4 (BRG1), SMARCB1 (INI1) or both ARID1A and ARID1B together is seen in approximately two thirds of undifferentiated carcinomas {26126041; 27101785; 27656868; 32910019; 36252860}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The presence of inactivating mutations involving SMARCA4, SMARCB1, or both ARID1A and ARID1B together (or loss of protein expression by immunohistochemistry) can provide support for the diagnosis of dedifferentiated/undifferentiated endometrial carcinoma {27562491} in the appropriate context. Mismatch repair protein deficiency/high microsatellite instability and/or the presence of pathogenic mutations beyond SMARCA4 establish the diagnosis when SMARCA4-deficient uterine sarcoma is in the differential diagnosis {31567195; 35218556; 39921027}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

Undifferentiated: diffuse/sheet-like growth pattern; relatively uniform-sized tumour cells with condensed nuclear chromatin, occasional small nucleoli, and numerous mitotic figures; lack morphological evidence of epithelial differentiation.

Dedifferentiated: undifferentiated component in association with a differentiated (usually low-grade) carcinoma component.

Staging

This entity is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of tumours of the uterus – endometrium [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are generally highly aggressive, with recurrence or death from disease occurring in 55–95% of cases. The presence of an undifferentiated carcinoma component, regardless of the percentage, can portend a worse prognosis; therefore, distinguishing this tumour from FIGO grade 3 endometrioid carcinoma is important. Molecular classification is appropriate to guide clinical management {38780000} and determine prognosis. The presence of POLE exonuclease domain mutation is associated with a highly favourable prognosis {28498284; 39811899; 38780000; 33712277}. dMMR, while not independently prognostic, appears to have outcomes intermediate between POLE-mutated and p53abn tumours {38780000}. dMMR status may prompt additional therapeutic options (i.e. immunotherapy) in suitable patients, but efficacy data are limited. Certain subsets, those with SWI/SNF protein deficiency (i.e. loss of expression of SMARCA4 [BRG1], SMARCB1 [INI1] and both ARID1A and ARID1B together), modify outcomes and are highly aggressive {27656868; 28863077; 33125840; 32910019}.
