10.1.9.1 Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma)

Definition

Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma) is a superficial tumour composed of well-delineated lobules of bland spindle-shaped to stellate cells and thin-walled arborizing vessels with prominent myxoid stroma. The term “cutaneous myxoma” is preferred because not all tumours display prominent blood vessels.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma) arises in the dermis and/or superficial subcutis of the vulva {9421071}.

Clinical features

Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma) presents as a slow-growing, asymptomatic, and often exophytic lesion. Most are small, < 30 mm {10435560; 9421071}.

Epidemiology

Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma) is an uncommon benign neoplasm, which can occur sporadically or in association with syndromes such as Carney complex. Patients are commonly young to middle-aged {10435560; 9421071; 34968506}, with reports from a large case series showing a mean age of 55 years (range: 7–91 years) and an M:F ratio of 1:1.3 {34968506}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

If multifocal, cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma) can be a manifestation of Carney complex, an autosomal dominant disorder caused by PRKAR1A (17q24) mutations or, less commonly, by alterations of 2p16 {10973256; 8609225}. Carney complex encompasses a spectrum of skin pigmentation abnormalities, endocrine tumours or disturbances, myxomas, psammomatous melanotic schwannomas, and testicular large cell calcifying Sertoli cell tumours.

Macroscopic appearance

Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma) is usually polypoid, with a gelatinous, lobulated cut surface {10435560; 9421071}.

Histopathology

Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma) is limited to the dermis and/or subcutis or the lamina propria of mucosal sites and consists of multinodular, relatively circumscribed lobules with prominent myxoid change and composed of sparse spindled and stellate cells and thin-walled arborizing blood vessels {10435560; 9421071}. Tumour cells have ovoid to round nuclei, frequent nuclear inclusions, and delicate cytoplasmic processes; they usually lack cytological atypia and mitotic activity and are often associated with an infiltrate of neutrophils. Some tumours display entrapment of epithelial structures, predominantly hair follicles, and (more rarely) follicular induction is seen {3389450; 34968506}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Mutations of the PRKAR1A gene can be detected in cases associated with Carney complex {15371594; 26130139; 34532875}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: involvement limited to skin or subcutis; non-infiltrative growth; lobules of myxoid stroma with cytologically bland spindled to stellate cells; arborizing vasculature; stromal neutrophils.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Originally, the rate of local recurrence was reported as 30%, but it is likely that the true recurrence rate is much < 10% {10435560; 9421071; 34968506}.
