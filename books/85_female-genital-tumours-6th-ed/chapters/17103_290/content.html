17.1.0.3 Peutz–Jeghers syndrome (STK11)

Definition

Peutz–Jeghers syndrome (PJS) is an inherited cancer-prone condition caused by germline (constitutional) STK11 mutations and characterized by mucocutaneous melanin pigmentation and gastrointestinal polyposis. Gynaecological manifestations include cervical (gastric-type adenocarcinoma), ovarian (sex cord tumour with annular tubules, Sertoli cell tumour), and broad ligament (STK11 adnexal tumour) neoplasms, as well as synchronous multifocal gastric-type mucinous lesions (metaplasia and neoplasia).

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours involving the female genital tract are seen in the cervix, ovary, and broad ligament.

Clinical features

PJS is associated with a moderate or high risk of a range of malignancies, with an overall risk of any cancer by the age of 70 years of 81% (see <<#XXX>>Table 17.XXX) {16707622; 11113065; 3587280}. Tumours of the female genital tract include HPV-independent gastric-type cervical adenocarcinoma, sex cord tumour with annular tubules (SCTAT), STK11 adnexal tumour, and Sertoli cell tumour {39225118}. Patients with PJS with gastric-type cervical adenocarcinoma are younger (median age: 35 years) than non-syndromic patients {34903560}. SCTATs occur in the second and third decades of life {29373116}. Cervical tumours are associated with abdominal/pelvic pain, abnormal vaginal bleeding, and watery discharge {29233547}. Ovarian and broad ligament tumours may be an incidental finding, or patients may present with signs and symptoms related to a pelvic mass {15644771; 33534223}.

Epidemiology

The prevalence of PJS is 1 case per 50 000–200 000 births {16616343}. The cumulative risk of cervical gastric-type adenocarcinoma is 10% and that of ovarian tumours is 21% {16616343}. PJS is implicated in 10% of all cervical gastric-type adenocarcinomas, 30% of all STK11 adnexal tumours, and 36% of all SCTATs {24179492; 33534223; 39169716}. Synchronous mucinous metaplasia and neoplasia of the female genital tract is reported to occur in as many as 20% of patients with PJS {34903560}.

Etiology

A germline pathogenic mutation of the tumour suppressor gene STK11 (chromosome 19p13.3) is implicated in > 90% of cases {9428765; 24179492; 16287113}. Most germline defects are single nucleotide variants and small intragenic deletions, but larger deletions of one or more exons have also been described {17924967}.

Pathogenesis

STK11 (LKB1) localizes to chromosome 19p13.3, contains 9 coding exons, and encodes a cytoplasmic and nuclear protein (STK11 [LKB1]) of 433 amino acids. STK11 (LKB1) is a serine/threonine kinase that phosphorylates AMPK and related kinases involved in cell growth, metabolism, and polarity {19629071}. Tumorigenesis in patients with STK11 germline (constitutional) mutations usually results from loss of heterozygosity of the wildtype allele {9850045; 10623683}.

Macroscopic appearance

Cervical tumours are often locally advanced, centred on the transformation zone, and may produce an indurated, circumferentially enlarged cervix, sometimes with a friable appearance and grossly visible cysts on the cut surface; occasionally the cervix appears normal {2764221; 29233547; 18631191; 34903560}. SCTATs are usually not grossly apparent, although they occasionally form bilateral visible yellow nodules {7104978; 29373116}. STK11 adnexal tumours are often large (median size: 110 mm), with a variegated cut surface, haemorrhage, and necrosis {33534223}. Sertoli cell tumours typically form a solid mass with a yellow cut surface {15644771}.

Histopathology

Cervical gastric-type adenocarcinoma encompasses a morphological spectrum ranging from well-differentiated (previously termed “minimal deviation”) to overtly malignant tumours with the defining feature being tumour cells exhibiting a gastric (pyloric) appearance with abundant apical pale eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm and distinct cell borders {17460448}. Lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia also occurs in PJS, including atypical forms, and is considered a precursor to gastric-type adenocarcinoma {15143335; 17540439; 18779726}. Gastric-type mucinous lesions, metaplastic or neoplastic, may occur in other sites in the female genital tract (ovaries, fallopian tubes, endometrium, vagina) in patients with PJS {27550049; 38937781; 34903560}. The term “synchronous mucinous metaplasia” and “neoplasia of the female genital tract” is applied when these occur simultaneously in two or more sites {19207943}. SCTATs in syndromic patients are typically bilateral and multifocal with microcalcifications {7104978}, in contrast to non-syndromic neoplasms {29373116; 7104978}. STK11 adnexal tumours demonstrate a range of histological patterns, with the most common being interanastomosing cords and trabeculae in a myxoid matrix {33534223, 39169716}. Sertoli cell tumours may be oxyphilic or lipid rich {15644771}.

Refer also to the Histopathology subsections in section Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, gastric type; section STK11 adnexal tumour; section Sertoli cell tumour; and section Sex cord tumour with annular tubules.

Cytology

In cervical cytopathology preparations, gastric-type adenocarcinoma presents as monolayered honeycomb sheets of cells with abundant vacuolated and/or foamy cytoplasm, distinct cell borders, and vesicular, enlarged nuclei with prominent nucleoli {26173165; 29750702; 33152741}. Yellow cytoplasmic mucin is characteristic but inconsistently present {29982289; 33152741}. Refer also to the Cytology subsection in section Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, gastric type, of the uterine cervix.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A germline STK11 pathogenic variant can be demonstrated using next-generation sequencing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fulfilment of the clinical diagnostic criteria for PJS presented in #58406Box 16.XXX.

Staging

Staging is the same as for other cervical and ovarian tumours. There is no established staging system for STK11 adnexal tumour.

Prognosis and prediction

Cervical gastric-type adenocarcinoma and STK11 adnexal tumour are associated with aggressive behaviour and poor outcome {26457350; 33534223; 40262437}. In contrast, SCTATs are associated with an excellent prognosis {7104978}. Sertoli cell tumours may rarely be malignant. Tumour surveillance guidelines recommend annual pelvic examination and cervical cytology commencing at age 18–20 years; annual review for pathognomonic symptoms with a low threshold for imaging studies, cervical biopsy, and endocervical curettage has also been proposed {34903560}.
