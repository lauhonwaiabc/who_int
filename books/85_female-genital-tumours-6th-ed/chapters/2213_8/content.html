2.2.1.3 Vulvar intraepithelial neoplasia, HPV-independent

Definition

Vulvar intraepithelial neoplasia, HPV-independent (HPV-I VIN), is the non-invasive precursor of HPV-independent squamous cell carcinoma of the vulva.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: HPV-independent VIN.

Not recommended: squamous cell hyperplasia; simplex VIN; carcinoma in situ of simplex type; vulvar acanthosis with altered differentiation (VAAD), differentiated VIN (dVIN); differentiated exophytic vulvar intraepithelial lesion (DEVIL); verruciform lichen simplex chronicus.

Subtype(s)

Aberrant p53 (p53abn); p53 wild-type (p53wt)

Localization

Vulvar skin and mucosa (labia majora, labia minora, clitoris, perineum).

Clinical features

In most cases, a long history of symptomatic lichen sclerosus is reported. Pre-existing lichen sclerosus, however, is less frequent in patients with p53 wildtype (verruciform acanthotic) lesions. Pruritus, irritation, pain, and/or dysuria are the most common symptoms. However, patients may be asymptomatic and manifest only symptoms related to a concurrent carcinoma. Bleeding and mass-related symptoms can be seen in lesions associated with exophytic verruciform growth.

Epidemiology

The incidence of HPV-I VIN has increased, in one study from 0.09 to 0.69 per 100 000 woman-years between 1991–2010 and 2011–2019. HPV-I VIN tends to affect postmenopausal women in the setting of longstanding and/or poorly controlled dermatosis. It can also occur in younger patients {38518213}.

Etiology

Chronic inflammatory vulvar diseases are considered to be the main contributory factors {27113549}. Lichen sclerosus has the strongest association and is seen in a majority of cases. Rare cases have been associated with other dermatoses and scarring conditions {24999271; 34130973}.

Pathogenesis

The favoured mechanism is persistent epithelial destruction and regeneration, which, combined with progressive dermal sclerosis, leads to oxidative stress and (epi)genetic events. In the HPV-I VIN p53 aberrant subtype, TP53 mutations are present as an early driver event; TERT promoter, NOTCH1, and HRAS mutations, CCND1 amplification, and copy-number variations in chromosomes 3 and 8 have also been reported {28899974; 23190170; 30030907}. With regard to the HPV-I VIN p53 wildtype subtype, verrucous (recently also described as verruciform acanthotic) forms harbour recurrent somatic alterations in TERT promoter, PIK3CA, NOTCH1-3, HRAS, and CDKN2A {28899974; 27834349; 32427961; 39868730}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most HPV-I VINs appear as a localized pink-red erosion, macule, or plaque distinct from the surrounding lichen sclerosus. The verrucous VIN (vVIN) variant appears as a discrete elevated plaque or verruca-like growth {34265101}.

Histopathology

HPV-I VIN is morphologically heterogeneous. A morphological diagnosis of HPV-I VIN can be suggested if the clinical and morphological findings are compatible and make, in turn, an HPV-associated lesion less likely. However, the risk of misclassification on morphology alone is high {31274700; 37726173}. Therefore, confirmation of HPV status and classification into the major subtypes (p53 mutation type and p53 wild type) using p16 and p53 immunohistochemistry are recommended, at least at initial diagnosis.

HPV-I VIN p53abn has two main morphological patterns. The differentiated pattern (formerly differentiated VIN or dVIN) is keratinizing and shows retained, albeit aberrant, squamous maturation. Although its recognition can be challenging even for experienced pathologists {23370772; 28590952}, the features with the highest levels of reproducibility and vulvar cancer predictive power are (1) basal pleomorphism noticeable at low magnification (4–10× with large vesicular or hyperchromatic nuclei with macronucleoli); (2) basal cell disarray; and (3) premature maturation with abortive keratin pearls and dyskeratosis {10716158; 30640512; 36821250}. Other features include epidermal hypertrophy with rete ridge irregularity, elongation, and anastomosis, increased cell separation with prominent desmosomes and acantholysis, hyperkeratosis, and/or confluent parakeratosis. Unlike in HPV-associated high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL), extension into skin appendages is rare. The HSIL-like / basaloid pattern of HPV-I (p53abn) VIN, much less frequent, features lack of squamous differentiation highly reminiscent of an HPV-associated HSIL. Characteristics include loss of epithelial maturation with basal cell expansion, loss of nuclear organization, pleomorphic nuclei with enlargement, irregular nuclear membranes hyperchromasia, and noticeable basal and suprabasal mitosis {19730361; 32657782}. Distinction from HPV-associated lesions is difficult and unreliable without the aid of immunohistochemistry.

HPV-I p53 wildtype VIN represents a minority of VIN cases. The best-known morphological pattern is verrucous VIN (also known as verruciform acanthotic VIN), characterized by the absence of significant nuclear atypia, verruciform growth, and/or marked acanthosis (> 10 cell layers), rete ridge elongation and expansion, parakeratosis, hypogranulosis, and cytoplasmic pallor in superficial layers {27834349; 34214216; 35437330}. These lesions have been described historically as vulvar acanthosis with altered differentiation and verruciform exophytic vulvar intraepithelial lesion {15105653; 27834349; 28899974; 29470358; 31097103}, terms no longer recommended. We acknowledge that p53 wildtype lesions outside the verrucous spectrum can occur, namely HPV-I lesions with severe atypia not explained by inflammation or other confounding factors, suggesting dVIN or HSIL-like morphology, but with p53 wildtype expression. These lesions require further study.

In HPV-I VIN, p16 is either negative or shows heterogeneous (mosaic, non-block) staining in lesional epithelium, with varying intensity of labelling, which is predominantly only cytoplasmic. p53 expression is mutation-type or wild type. Mutation-type p53 expression can be (1) overexpression (defined as diffuse strong nuclear expression), which can be basal-only or suprabasal/diffuse; (2) completely absent (null) staining (basal and suprabasal); or (3) cytoplasmic staining {32203095}. Importantly, basal-only p53 overexpression can be seen in inflammatory dermatoses {16430473; 32799363} and can be difficult to interpret with risk of overdiagnosis of mutation-type p53 expression. Sequencing of TP53 can be helpful in lesions in which p53 immunostaining results are indeterminate. Wildtype p53 expression in HPV-I VIN is seen as basal and suprabasal nuclear labelling of varying intensities (heterogeneous). This contrasts with the basal-sparing wildtype p53 pattern, which is specific to HPV-associated lesions.

Diagnosis of HPV-I VIN requires a high index of suspicion and correlation with clinical context, especially if p53 is wild type. Lesions with atypia and/or verruciform acanthosis suspicious but not definite for HPV-I VIN should be interpreted descriptively with an explanatory note. The term “vulvar aberrant maturation” has been proposed for these instances, which warrant close clinical observation and repeated sampling as clinically indicated because the morphological features do not allow exclusion of VIN, but neither do they allow a definite VIN diagnosis {33105449}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Acknowledging issues with optimization and interpretation, immunohistochemistry for p53 and p16 are reliable surrogates of TP53 mutational status and high-risk HPV infection, respectively {32203095; 36828360; 39641228}. TP53 sequencing or HPV molecular testing can be performed if immunostaining results are indeterminate.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

HPV-I VIN p53 aberrant: severe basal nuclear or full-thickness atypia; non-block p16 and mutation-type p53 expression by immunohistochemistry.

HPV-I VIN p53 wild type: verruciform morphology with no atypia; non-block p16 and wild type p53 expression by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

HPV-I VIN has a more aggressive clinical course compared with HPV-associated intraepithelial lesions. For p53abn and p53wt HPV-I VIN, the absolute cancer risk (67% and 31% respectively), cumulative incidence at 10 years (67% and 28% respectively), and median time to cancer diagnosis (1.5 and 5.1 years, respectively) are higher than in HPV-associated lesions {37726173}. HPV-I (p53abn) VIN had a 4.35 times higher risk of developing vulvar squamous cell carcinoma compared with HPV-I (p53wt) VIN {38518213}.

A recent series showed that patients with HSIL-like / basaloid HPV-I (p53abn) VIN had a high absolute cancer risk and a short median time to cancer diagnosis {37726173}; these data may reflect undertreatment, as underrecognized HSIL-like HPV-I VIN may be undertreated as HPV-associated lesions.

Retrospective series have documented local lesion recurrence in as many as 44% of patients with p53wt HPV-I VIN and a 37% prevalence of subsequent cancer diagnosis with a median time of progression of 42 months {34265101}. Carcinoma in these patients is often of the verrucous variant {15105653; 32427961}.
