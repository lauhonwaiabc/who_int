8.8.0.4 Mixed carcinoma

Definition

Mixed carcinoma of the ovary is an ovarian carcinoma composed of two or more different histological types.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This tumour is localized to the ovary.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with symptoms related to an ovarian mass.

Epidemiology

Mixed ovarian carcinomas are rare (< 1% of ovarian carcinomas in pathological studies), with limited epidemiological data available {26099008; 35053578}.

Etiology

The etiology is related to the histological types present. Most mixed carcinomas are associated with endometriosis {32404953; 37125505; 30575604; 37415960}.

Pathogenesis

Several small studies and case reports demonstrate that most mixed tumours have a common clonal origin {26099008; 37125505; 30575604; 28863071; 33772212; 32693840}. Conceivably, a mixed carcinoma could develop through transdifferentiation (plasticity) of one histological type to another or through divergence of two histological types from a common precursor. Endometriosis-associated mixed endometrioid / clear cell carcinomas are the most common, followed by mixed endometrioid / low-grade serous carcinoma {26099008}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is related to the histological types present.

Histopathology

At least two histological types must be clearly recognizable on H&E-stained sections. The different histological components should be confirmed by immunohistochemistry, when available. Any percentage of a second histological type that can be confidently demonstrated is sufficient to label the tumour as mixed. The types present and their percentages should be stated in the diagnostic report.

Mixed carcinoma should be distinguished from pure histological types with ambiguous histomorphology (i.e. features intermediate between two histological types) and/or heterogeneous histomorphology (morphological mimicry) {26099008}. Mixed carcinomas should also be distinguished from independent collision tumours, which do not share molecular alterations; these are usually spatially distinct.

The most common mixed tumour is mixed endometrioid and clear cell carcinoma {26099008}. A combination of napsin A (expressed in the clear cell component) and PR (expressed in the endometrioid component) helps discriminate between the two components {26974996}. Many mixed endometrioid and clear cell carcinomas are mismatch repair deficient, a genomic category known to demonstrate morphological heterogeneity {26799366}. Mesonephric-like carcinoma may also be mixed with other carcinoma histotypes (see section Mesonephric-like carcinoma).

Dedifferentiated carcinoma and carcinosarcoma are special types of carcinomas with two components and are considered separately (see << 8.8.0.2>>Undifferentiated and dedifferentiated carcinomas of the ovary and section Carcinosarcoma). Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) are also considered separately (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasia: Introduction).

Cytology

The cytology is related to the histological types present.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the presence of two (or more) ovarian carcinoma histotypes, with the components showing distinct and unequivocal differences by histomorphology.

Desirable: the different components should show distinct immunoprofiles, characteristic of the histotypes present.

Staging

Mixed carcinoma is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

There is limited information on the clinical behaviour of these rare tumours. Small studies suggest that prognosis and prediction are dependent on the histological components {25254564} and the tumour stage {25855053}.
