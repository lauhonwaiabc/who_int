9.3.1.2 Struma ovarii

Definition

Struma ovarii is a monodermal teratoma composed either exclusively or predominantly (> 50%) of thyroid tissue.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant struma ovarii, peritoneal strumosis.

Subtype(s)

thyroid-type papillary carcinoma in struma ovarii; thyroid-type follicular carcinoma in struma ovarii; thyroid-type anaplastic carcinoma in struma ovarii

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Struma ovarii may be an incidental finding, but patients can present with signs and symptoms related to a pelvic mass {37726744; 19471561; 7802134}. A pseudo–Meigs syndrome (ascites with or without pleural effusion) is seen in some patients, and hyperthyroidism in < 10% {37391865; 19696610}. There may be an elevated serum CA125 level. Thyroid-type carcinoma may rarely arise from struma ovarii and in some cases results in an elevated serum thyoglobulin {37726744}.

Epidemiology

This tumour is extremely rare, accounting for 0.1% of all ovarian malignancies {35528006}. Struma ovarii is the most common type of monodermal teratoma, with thyroid-type carcinoma arising in about 5% of struma ovarii {37726744; 8037292}. The peak incidence is in the fifth decade of life.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of struma ovarii is unknown. Thyroid-type carcinomas arising in struma ovarii harbour more molecular alterations than struma ovarii without thyroid-type carcinoma, and these alterations are similar to those in carcinomas arising from the eutopic thyroid (see below). {37771077; 38110823}.

As in thyroid gland tumours, somatic mutations of RAS family members (NRAS, KRAS, HRAS) are not a reliable indicator of malignancy. BRAF mutations (~35% cases), specifically the p.Gly469Ala variant, and TERT promoter alterations are associated with thyroid-type carcinoma in struma ovarii, including highly differentiated follicular carcinoma of ovarian origin (HDFCO). Additionally identified alterations include RET::PTC and PAX8::PPARG rearrangements, JAK3 variants, and KIT mutation {18058267; 17721188; 26362194; 31538107; 32743766; 27258816; 37771077}.

Macroscopic appearance

Struma ovarii is typically unilateral, measures < 100 mm, and has a solid beefy (sometimes nodular), green or red to brown cut surface resembling normal thyroid or goitre. It may have cystic areas or rarely be entirely cystic {8037292}. Features of a dermoid cyst, such as hair and sebaceous material, may be seen {7802134}.

Thyroid-type carcinoma arising in struma ovarii is usually a unilateral tumour, solid and cystic, white or tan-brown, and larger than benign struma ovarii (average: 140 mm). Adhesions between the tumour and adjacent surfaces is seen in some cases {19696610; 20407319}.

Histopathology

Struma ovarii is composed of normal or hyperplastic thyroid-type tissue, sometimes with patterns seen in thyroid adenoma such as macrofollicular, microfollicular, trabecular, pseudopapillary, and solid {7802134; 8253550}. The follicles are lined by cuboidal to flat cells with round, small and bland nuclei and are filled with colloid. Less common cytological features such as cells with abundant oxyphilic or clear cytoplasm may be seen {7802134}. Predominantly cystic struma is composed of thin fibrous septa lined by attenuated epithelium and often containing sparse microfollicles lined by small cuboidal cells within the wall {8037292}. Stroma is typically scant and collagenous but can be prominent, oedematous, or hyalinized. Sometimes aggregates of stromal histiocytes are seen. Stromal luteinization may be seen peripherally {3804558}. The cells express TG, TTF1, and PAX8 {17285841; 16512597}.

Struma ovarii may be associated with mature teratoma, carcinoid tumour (typically trabecular), Brenner tumour, or mucinous cystadenoma {22558485; 30134344; 7802134}. The coexistence of struma ovarii and carcinoid is classified as strumal carcinoid (discussed in section Ovarian carcinoid).

Carcinomas arising within struma ovarii morphologically resemble those arising in eutopic thyroid, such as papillary (85% of cases) (including follicular variant), follicular, poorly differentiated, or anaplastic carcinoma {8253550; 19696610; 20407319; 22288964; 26630220}. A diagnosis of follicular carcinoma can be made when closely packed thyroid follicles (without cytology of papillary carcinoma) extend to the surface of the ovary or exhibit lymphovascular space invasion, sometimes with involvement of native ovarian structures such as corpora albicantia. HDFCO (previously termed “peritoneal strumosis”) refers to the combination of morphologically benign-appearing struma ovarii and concomitant or subsequent extraovarian involvement by benign-appearing thyroid tissue {18317221; 22288964; 36547126; 19620939}. HDFCO has no definitive features distinguishing it from struma ovarii other than extraovarian spread and/or lymphovascular space invasion. Therefore, HDFCO can only be diagnosed when extraovarian disease is present or when there is lymphovascular space invasion.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing is typically not required for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Struma ovarii

Essential: thyroid tissue alone or constituting > 50% of a mature teratoma.

Desirable : TTF1, TG expression in cases with associated thyroid-type carcinoma.

Malignant struma ovarii

Essential: the criteria for carcinoma arising in struma ovarii are the same as for carcinoma in the eutopic thyroid.

Desirable : TTF1, TG expression in cases with associated thyroid-type carcinoma.

Staging

Struma ovarii is benign and is not staged. Thyroid-type carcinomas arising in struma ovarii are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases of struma ovarii are clinically benign, but behaviour cannot always be predicted on the basis of the histological appearances {22288964}. The presence of peritoneal implants (categorized as HDFCO if the histology is benign), substantial adhesions, > 1 L of ascitic fluid, and unequivocal extension through the ovarian surface are considered adverse features {19696610; 22288964; 18317221}. Thyroid-type carcinomas arising in struma ovarii typically have a protracted clinical course, with an overall survival rate of 89% at 10 years and 84% at 25 years {19696610; 26374222; 20407319; 22288964; 19620939}. HDFCO is a low-grade malignancy that may be associated with multiple relapses over a long period of follow-up, but rarely with a fatal outcome {36547126}. Recurrences occur earlier for struma ovarii with papillary carcinoma (mean: 4 years) than for those with HDFCO (mean: 9 years) {20407319}. In one study, no patients with HDFCO died of disease while only 7% with papillary carcinoma and 14% with follicular carcinoma died of disease {18753973}. Poorly differentiated thyroid-type carcinomas arising in struma ovarii have a poor prognosis {39768652}.
