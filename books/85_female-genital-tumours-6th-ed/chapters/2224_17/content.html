2.2.2.4 Fibroadenoma

Definition

Fibroadenoma of anogenital mammary-like glands is a benign, circumscribed biphasic epithelial-stromal neoplasm resembling the corresponding tumour in the breast.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common location is the interlabial sulcus, but fibroadenomas also occasionally arise elsewhere in the vulva, as well as in the perineum and perianal region {16096415}. Rare patients also present with simultaneous mammary fibroadenoma, similar lesions in the axilla {26863057}, and bilateral anogenital tumours {197847; 13197326; 5775152}.

Clinical features

Typically, the lesion is a solitary, firm, subcutaneous nodule that is not fixed to deeper tissues. Enlargement may occur during pregnancy.

Epidemiology

These are very uncommon vulvar neoplasms, with < 100 cases reported to date. Vulvar fibroadenomas occur predominantly in women of reproductive age, but they may be seen in postmenopausal women and (rarely) in prepubertal girls {20035149}. The age at diagnosis was 20–69 years (mean: 39 years) {18190454; 20035149}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Fibroadenomas of the vulva are thought to arise from anogenital mammary-like glands {8005737; 21169735; 27655126; 27759697}, and they show features similar to those in the breast. One study identified AKT1 and MET mutations in fibroadenoma {28648934}. A single case of fibroadenoma has been reported in a patient with PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome / Cowden syndrome {33850997; 34524588}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most reported cases are < 40 mm in greatest dimension (< 10 mm to 60 mm or larger). On cut section, they are fibrous and may have small cystic spaces {18190454; 20035149}.

Histopathology

Vulvar fibroadenomas are well circumscribed and composed of glandular elements lined by a bland epithelium and of a relatively uniform, hypocellular stroma composed of spindled or stellate cells without nuclear atypia. There is rare or absent stromal mitotic activity. Morphological variations include columnar cell, oxyphilic, cystic, or lactational change; usual ductal hyperplasia; pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia; and multinucleate stromal giant cells {20035149; 23503319}. When the glandular lumina maintain round or oval configurations, the image is that of a pericanalicular growth pattern, whereas abundant stroma compressing the glandular elements into slit-like structures produces the so-called intracanalicular pattern {20035149; 21169735}.

Three glandular layers has been shown in vulvar fibroadenoma: a luminal cell population positive for CK14 and CK5/6; an intermediate luminal cell population positive for ER, PR, and focally AR, but negative for CK14 and CK5/6; and a myoepithelial cell population showing positivity for CK14 and CK5/6 and negative for ER, PR, and AR. GATA3 stains all three layers {32592993}. Stromal cells, including those in pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia areas, are positive for hormone receptors, vimentin, and CD34, with variably reaction positive for actins {16096415; 18587019; 32592993}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed biphasic histology with epithelial and stromal elements.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign lesions, usually with uneventful follow-up, but they can rarely recur, especially if incompletely excised {20035149; 30732503}.
