17.1.0.1 BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndromes (BRCA1, BRCA2)

Definition

BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndromes are inherited autosomal dominant disorders of pathogenic germline variants of BRCA1 or BRCA2 in which there is an increased risk of early-onset breast cancer, tubo-ovarian carcinoma, prostatic carcinoma, pancreatic carcinoma, and other cancer types.

MIM numbering

604370 Breast-ovarian cancer, familial, susceptibility to, 1; BROVCA1

612555 Breast-ovarian cancer, familial, susceptibility to, 2; BROVCA2

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome

Subtype(s)

BRCA1-related hereditary cancer syndrome

BRCA2-related hereditary cancer syndrome

Localization

BRCA-associated hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome is associated with carcinomas of the breast, ovary, fallopian tube, and peritoneum. Patients may also develop prostatic, pancreatic, and endometrial carcinomas {31492746}, as well as other types of cancer.

Clinical features

The clinical features are related to site-specific disease.

Epidemiology

Approximately 15% of ovarian cancer and close to 20% of high-grade serous carcinoma are associated with inherited pathogenic germline variants of the BRCA1 or BRCA2 genes {22711857;21324516;}. Germline variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 confer a high lifetime risk of breast and ovarian cancers {17416853}, as well as contralateral breast cancer {23628597; 25857409}, peritoneum, prostate, biliary tract, oesophageal, gastric, and pancreatic cancer {35420638}. The number of women who carry pathogenic BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline variants is estimated to be 1 in 300–500 {25198398}. BRCA1 pathogenic germline variants markedly increase ovarian / fallopian tube cancer risk (40–60%), with an average patient age of 50–53 years at diagnosis. The risk of ovarian/fallopian tube cancer for individuals with BRCA2 pathogenic variants is lower (11–35%), and the age of diagnosis is slightly higher (55–58 years) {17148771}. According to data from the Consortium of Investigators of Modifiers of BRCA1/2 (CIMBA), breast and ovarian cancer risks vary by type and location of BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutations {25857409}. The risk of occurrence of both cancers in the same patient is 5% (BRCA1) and 2% (BRCA2) {25857409}. Some studies suggested that, compared with the general population, women with pathogenic BRCA1 or BRCA2 variants had a greater risk of developing endometrial carcinoma {38297203; 35683509}.

The prevalence of BRCA1 and BRCA2 variants shows substantial variation in terms of mutation type and frequency by geographical region and race/ethnicity. For example, the prevalence of variants is markedly higher among Ashkenazi Jews (2.5%) than in the general population (0.1%) {9150153; 10739756}. Other geographical locations around the world, such as Norway, the Netherlands, and Iceland, also have a higher prevalence of specific harmful BRCA1 and BRCA2 variants (1.8%) {10995809}. The prevalence of specific harmful BRCA1 and BRCA2 variants may also vary among individual racial and ethnic groups in the USA, including African American, Hispanic, Asian American, and non-Hispanic White populations {19841585; 19241424}.

Etiology

Inactivation of the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes by pathogenic germline variants in combination with somatic loss of function of the other allele results in defects in the homologous recombination repair pathway responsible for the accurate repair of DNA breaks using the sister chromatid as a template.

Pathogenesis

BRCA1 is localized to chromosome 17q21.31 and contains 23 coding exons. It encodes a nuclear protein of 1863 amino acids. A RING finger domain at the N-terminus mediates interactions with other proteins. BRCA1 is often mutated in three domains: the RING finger domain, exons 11–13, and the BRCA1 C-terminal domain {22737296}. BRCA2 maps to chromosome 13q13.1 and consists of 27 coding exons. The BRCA2 protein includes 3418 amino acids. There is an association between mutations in the central part of BRCA2 (the ovarian cluster region) and an increase in the likelihood of ovarian carcinoma. The BRCA1 and BRCA2 proteins share no structural homology with any other protein {21336636}.

The BRCA1 and BRCA2 proteins play a critical role in maintaining genomic stability by promoting error-free DNA repair {16998501}. BRCA1 acts in cell-cycle checkpoint control and by binding with proteins that are involved in the repair of DNA damage, such as RAD51. BRCA2 regulates the function of RAD51-mediated recombination. Functional BRCA proteins are tumour suppressors, maintaining genome stability through repair of double-strand breaks by homologous recombination, cell division control, and cell growth regulation {15546503; 8589730}. Pathogenic germline variants in BRCA1 or BRCA2 predispose individuals to a two-hit biallelic inactivation, involving firstly the inherited pathogenic mutation of one BRCA allele and then the somatic inactivation of the second wildtype allele {1303261; 25830658; 31090900}, together with somatic mutation signatures, suggesting homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) {31090900}. Tumorigenesis in the fallopian tubes of BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers appears to require loss of heterozygosity or secondary genetic events rather than haploinsufficiency alone {40140386}. DNA double-strand breaks, when left unrepaired, can result in genomic rearrangements and cell death.

Macroscopic appearance

Not clinically relevant

Histopathology

In the context of this syndrome, the most common neoplasm of the female genital tract is high-grade serous carcinoma of the fallopian tube / ovary / peritoneum. Carcinosarcomas are also associated. Other histological tumour types have been reported in BRCA1 and BRCA2 pathogenic variant carriers (e.g. endometrioid, clear cell, and undifferentiated carcinomas), but the relation to biallelic inactivation has not been well documented {11112219; 15073127}. A localized serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (or serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma with limited invasion or spread) in the distal fallopian tube is found in 5–10% {31360879} of risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy specimens if the tube is examined using the SEE-FIM (sectioning and extensively examining the fimbriated end) protocol {16434898, 34850262}. The SET (solid, endometrial-like, transitional) architectural pattern, an increase in tumour and stromal-infiltrating lymphocytes, and necrosis have a variable correlation with HRD and BRCA status. (see section High-grade serous carcinoma) {39648143, 24577588; 26574845; 22193042}. These morphological correlates are not strong enough to substitute for molecular analysis in daily practice {39648143}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Pathogenic germline variants in BRCA1 or BRCA2 are present.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of pathogenic germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 variants.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The 10-year survival of high-grade serous carcinoma in carriers in population-based studies from the pre–poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitor era was comparable to that of non-carriers {26556769; 25398451}, and the risk of death for both BRCA1 and BRCA2 pathogenic germline variant carriers increase over time (non-proportional hazards). Survival rates for BRCA1 carriers are worse than non-carriers after approximately 5 years despite the observation that mutation carriers have a higher initial sensitivity to platinum-based chemotherapy. Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors targeting DNA damage repair significantly improve survival in patients with BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations {27908594; 29166829; 25481791}, with less beneficial effects in patients with non-BRCA HRD. To identify patients who may benefit from PARP inhibitors, in addition to the detection of mutations in BRCA1 and BRCA2, the effect of loss of these genes on homologous recombination in tumours can be detected clinically using a variety of assays that frequently measure a combination of genomic alterations such as loss of heterozygosity, telomere imbalance, and large-scale transitions {26957554}. Other assays are also available, which use variable, weighted, and often proprietary combination of multiple genomic parameters for HRD prediction {36788098}.
