10.1.9.13 SMARCB1-deficient vulvar neoplasms

Definition

SMARCB1-deficient vulvar neoplasms comprise a heterogeneous group of tumours that include epithelioid sarcoma, myoepithelial carcinoma, and myoepithelioma-like tumour.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours most frequently arise in the labium majus, followed by the mons pubis or the groin {25651469; 26171919; 36545013; 19716162}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a unilateral vulvar lesion, often clinically suspected to be a mass.

Epidemiology

SMARCB1-deficient vulvar neoplasms comprise a rare group of mesenchymal tumours. Among these tumours, epithelioid sarcoma is the most common. Epithelioid sarcomas, myoepithelial carcinomas, and myoepithelioma-like tumours of the vulva have been described in adults from 22 to 81 years of age {25651469; 10383714}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The majority of classic and proximal types of epithelioid sarcoma and a subset of myoepithelial carcinomas are associated with deletion of SMARCB1, either partial or complete, correlating with absent immunohistochemical expression of SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47). Vulvar proximal-type epithelioid sarcomas have shown both homozygous (more common) and heterozygous SMARCB1 loss {25651469}. Similarly, sequencing of the few vulvar myoepithelial carcinomas has detected both homozygous and heterozygous SMARCB1 deletions {25651469}. Myoepithelioma-like tumours have demonstrated loss of SMARCB1, as well as the identification of a fusion in two of three sequenced tumours (PPP6R3::FHDC1 and MYH9::MYH6) {40188333}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours vary in appearance, manifesting as nodular and solid or as glistening, gelatinous masses, with or without necrosis. Tumours range in size from 11 to 85 mm {25651469}.

Histopathology

Classic epithelioid sarcoma forms infiltrative nodules and/or sheets of small to medium epithelioid cells {10452506}. Some tumours have centralized necrosis, giving a low-power impression of a granulomatous process. Tumour cells are often uniform with eosinophilic cytoplasm and modest nuclear atypia. Spindling can also be observed, particularly at the periphery of nodules. Mitotic activity is usually low {10452506; 25651469}.

Although proximal-type epithelioid sarcoma also has an invasive nodular or sheet-like architecture, tumour cells are larger with more overt nuclear atypia and mitotic activity is relative to classic tumours {9042279}. The proximal type also commonly features rhabdoid cytomorphology, sometimes extensively {9042279; 25651469}.

Immunohistochemically, classic and proximal-type epithelioid sarcomas express EMA as well as low and high molecular weight keratins, although CK5/6 is negative or can be seen focally {10452506}. CD34 is positive in approximately 50% of tumours. The majority of epithelioid sarcomas demonstrate loss of SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47) expression.

Myoepithelial carcinoma has an infiltrative and diffuse growth of moderately to severely atypical epithelioid cells, often with rhabdoid features {36545013; 19716162}. Brisk mitotic activity and necrosis are usually present. Tumours can have prominent areas of spindling and myxoid matrix. Like epithelioid sarcoma, keratins and EMA are frequently positive, and a subset demonstrates immunohistochemical loss of SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47). In contrast to epithelioid sarcoma, markers of myoepithelial lineage, such as smooth muscle actin and S100, are expressed to some degree, and CD34 is negative.

Myoepithelioma-like tumour is morphologically heterogeneous with patterns combining epithelioid and/or spindled cells with myxoid and non-myxoid stroma {26171919; 40188333}. Most tumours have both epithelioid and spindled cells, and in those in which myxoid matrix is extensive, the cells form cords or loose clusters, whereas patterns in non-myxoid areas are diffuse or storiform. Rhabdoid features have been described in some tumours. Mitoses are present with a median of 2.53 mitoses/mm2 (equating to 6 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area) {26171919}. SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47) loss is seen in all tumours. Additionally, myoepithelioma-like tumour is diffusely positive for EMA, with absent or limited expression of broad-spectrum keratins. Estrogen receptor is usually diffusely positive, but occasional examples have reported only focal positivity. S100, GFAP, and CD34 are negative. Smooth muscle actin expression is variable {26171919; 40188333}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Partial or complete deletion of SMARCB1 may be diagnostically helpful {25651469}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

Epithelioid sarcoma: epithelioid to spindled cells; loss of SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47), and expression of keratins and EMA.

Myoepithelial carcinoma: epithelioid to spindled cytomorphology; nuclear atypia including prominent nucleoli; brisk mitotic activity; loss of SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47), and expression of myoepithelial markers.

Myoepithelioma-like tumour: circumscribed tumour; mixed patterns of epithelioid and/or spindled cells in a variably myxoid matrix; loss of SMARCB1 (INI1/BAF47)  and expression of estrogen receptor.

Staging

Tumours are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for sarcomas of the trunk and extremities.

Prognosis and prediction

Epithelioid sarcoma and myoepithelial carcinoma can distantly disseminate; lungs and lymph nodes are among the most frequent sites of metastasis. Myoepithelioma-like tumour has potential for local recurrence but does not metastasize {25651469; 41291778}.
