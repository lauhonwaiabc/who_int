8.3.1.1 Mucinous cystadenoma and mucinous adenofibroma

Definition

Mucinous cystadenoma and mucinous adenofibroma of ovary are benign tumours with gastrointestinal or Müllerian-type mucinous epithelium.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized in the ovary.

Clinical features

The most common symptoms are abdominal or pelvic pain and symptoms related to an abdominal or pelvic mass. Rarely, patients present with estrogenic or androgenic manifestations secondary to stromal luteinization {15626914}.

Epidemiology

Benign mucinous neoplasms account for approximately 80% of all primary ovarian mucinous tumours. Mucinous cystadenomas are far more common than adenofibromas. These tumours are seen in patients over a wide age range (median age: 50 years) {15626914; 2983869; 31283462}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The association of some of these tumours with mature teratomas suggests a germ cell origin, whereas some arise in association with Brenner tumours {18976011; 31695154}. KRAS mutations and loss of CDKN2A are found in a significant number of tumours {22891197}.

Macroscopic appearance

Mucinous cystadenomas are usually unilateral (95%) and multilocular, with a smooth outer surface. They range in size from a few centimetres to > 300 mm (mean size: 100 mm). Mucinous adenofibromas are usually smaller, predominantly solid, and punctated with small cysts {15626914; 31097115}.

Histopathology

Mucinous cystadenomas are composed of multiple cysts and glands lined by simple non-stratified mucinous epithelium resembling Müllerian, gastric foveolar-type, or intestinal-type epithelium containing goblet cells and sometimes neuroendocrine cells or Paneth cells. Rare simple papillae may be seen. Mucinous adenofibromas have small cysts and glands embedded in a prominent fibromatous stroma. Nuclei are small and basally located without cytological atypia, with absent or minimal mitotic activity and apoptotic bodies. The adjacent stroma may be cellular and show luteinization. Focal extravasated mucin or mucin granulomas may be present secondary to cyst rupture. A component of mature teratoma or Brenner tumour may be found. Rarely, mucinous cystadenomas can contain mural nodules (see section Mucinous borderline tumour) {16340150; 498013; 9886181} or be associated with clear cell carcinoma {11265180; 17867264; 19851211}, large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma {8786207}, endometrioid carcinoma {14571433}, and sex cord stromal tumour {3754727; 15968196}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tumour with cysts and glands lined by benign mucinous epithelium; no architectural complexity or cytological atypia.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are benign; however, recurrences may be seen after cystectomy or rupture and spillage of the contents {17100827; 20022314}.
