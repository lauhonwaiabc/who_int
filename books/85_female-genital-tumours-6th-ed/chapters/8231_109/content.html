8.2.3.1 Low-grade serous carcinoma

Definition

Low-grade serous carcinoma (LGSC) of ovary is an invasive low-grade epithelial neoplasm demonstrating serous differentiation.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LGSC occurs predominantly in the ovary but may also occasionally arise in the fallopian tube, peritoneum, omentum, or lymph node, often in association with an ovarian serous borderline tumour (SBT) {23714500; 26195696}.

Clinical features

Patients may be symptomatic (secondary to mass effect) or asymptomatic {23714500} (https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=1&id=1453).

Epidemiology

LGSC accounts for approximately 2–5% of ovarian epithelial carcinomas and 5–10% of ovarian serous carcinomas {29185273; 23714500; 19700937}. Patients present over a wide age range (mean: 45–57 years), approximately a decade younger than those with high-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC). A prior history of SBT is present in a subset of patients {31343420}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

LGSC has few mutations and copy-number alterations. Somatic driver mutations activating the MAPK pathway, particularly KRAS, NRAS, and BRAF hotspot mutations, are present in approximately 50% of LGSCs {32901952; 26506417; 35443055}. Advanced-stage tumours are less frequently associated with BRAF mutations {20802181}. USP9X and EIF1AX mutations and homozygous deletions of the CDKN2A/2B locus have also been reported. LGSC may arise de novo, often associated with a coexisting SBT or present as recurrence of a prior SBT {27487741; 15087669; 17320156; 31343420}.

Macroscopic appearance

LGSC is often bilateral and exhibits papillary growth, which may include surface papillary excrescences. Tumours are solid and cystic and may be gritty due to calcification.

Histopathology

LGSC exhibits a variety of patterns, including small nests, glands, solid sheets, papillae or micropapillae, and inverted macropapillae frequently free-floating within unlined clear spaces {20881864; 27487741; 37169775}. Often, several patterns of invasion are present in an individual tumour. The nuclei exhibit mild to moderate atypia, with < 3-fold variation in nuclear size, sometimes with a central nucleolus {15087669}. Mitotic figures are often present but typically do not exceed 5 mitoses /mm2, equating to 12 mitoses /10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area {15087669}. A higher mitotic count or nuclear size variation greater than 3-fold should raise the possibility of HGSC. Psammoma bodies are often present. Necrosis is rare. These tumours are frequently associated with a coexisting SBT component {27487741; 15087669}. In some cases, the primary ovarian tumour is SBT only, while LGSC is present at extraovarian sites (see Histopathology in section Serous borderline tumour for details). Furthermore, a subset of micropapillary/cribriform SBTs show areas of confluent growth, imparting an almost solid appearance, which may represent a micropapillary/cribriform SBT–like growth pattern of LGSC, although formal criteria to distinguish between these entities are lacking. However, as such cases are often associated with other areas showing definitive stromal invasion and/or extraovarian disease, a diligent search for foci of invasive LGSC is warranted {12766576; 27487741}. A small subset of ovarian serous carcinomas are morphologically ambiguous between LGSC versus HGSC {31876585; 40327333}. By immunohistochemistry, LGSC is typically diffusely positive for CK7, PAX8, ER, and WT1. PR and p16 expression are variable (negative/non–block type or focal/patchy), and p53 exhibits a wildtype expression pattern {29901523; 19700937}.

Cytology

LGSC cannot be distinguished from SBT on the basis of cytological features alone {11668465}. Fluid specimens involved by serous tumours with low-grade nuclear atypia are best characterized as involved by low-grade serous neoplasm, with definitive classification deferred to an excisional specimen.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive serous tumour; low-grade nuclear atypia (< 3-fold variation in nuclear size); low mitotic activity.

Desirable: p53 wild type (by immunohistochemistry or molecular confirmation, in select cases to rule out HGSC); immunohistochemistry to exclude mesothelial differentiation in samples from extraovarian sites.

Staging

LGSC is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

LGSC is associated with a more indolent clinical course than HGSC {16880307; 18395040}. When confined to the ovary, LGSC has an excellent prognosis, but long-term follow-up indicates that the prognosis for patients with stage III–IV disease is poor {18395040; 26195696; 24071867}. For patients with stage II–IV disease, the median progression-free and overall survival times, respectively, are 28 and 102 months {26195696}.

LGSCs harbouring MAPK pathway activating genetic alterations, most commonly KRAS, NRAS, or BRAF hotspot mutations, are associated with improved overall survival compared with MAPK–non-altered LGSCs {35606067; 35443055}. MEK inhibitors are recognized for the routine management of recurrent LGSC, and other emerging MAPK-directed therapies are on the horizon {37581616; 35123694}. Immunohistochemical loss of p16 expression or low/absent PR expression have been associated with worse overall survival {30062862; 35768582}. Rarely, LGSC can be associated with a synchronous or metachronous high-grade carcinoma (most commonly HGSC), a finding associated with rapid disease progression and poor overall survival outcomes, similar to HGSC {40327333}.
