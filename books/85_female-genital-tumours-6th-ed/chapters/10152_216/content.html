10.1.5.2 Myofibroblastoma

Definition

Myofibroblastoma of the lower genital tract is a benign tumour, analogous to its breast counterpart, composed of myofibroblastic cells and bands of hyalinized collagen, and may also exhibit an adipocytic component.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: mammary-type myofibroblastoma; superficial myofibroblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Around 45% of cases are located in the inguinal or groin region, including the vulva, vagina, and perineum; however, the tumour can arise across a broad range of anatomical sites {26523539}.

Clinical features

These tumours typically present as slow-growing, painless masses, although they may occasionally be painful, or are discovered incidentally {26523539}.

Epidemiology

Myofibroblastomas are rare tumours with < 60 reported cases in medical literature. Myofibroblastoma primarily occurs in adults during their fifth or sixth decade of life, although cases have been reported across a broad age range (4 to 96 years; median age: 54 years) {16028842; 38035683}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Myofibroblastomas are characterized by the loss of 13q14, including the RB1 gene, and share a pathogenic relationship with other benign mesenchymal tumours of the 13q/RB1 family, such as spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma and cellular angiofibroma {22575260; 26523539; 20852591; 33802620}. Based on recent literature, superficial myofibroblastoma (previously described in the fifth edition) is considered part of the myofibroblastoma spectrum sharing similar clinicopathological, immunohistochemical, and molecular features {11486170; 15693885; 21820148; 22575260}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumour size ranges from < 10 mm to 220 mm, with a median of 66 mm; however, sizes as large as 352 mm have been reported {26523539;32829069}. Tumours are generally well-circumscribed, mobile, and exhibit a rubbery to gelatinous texture. On cut surface, they frequently show a whorled or nodular pattern, with fatty components and variable coloration.

Histopathology

Tumours are unencapsulated but well-circumscribed, composed of spindle cells arranged in variably sized, generally short fascicles, interspersed with broad bands of hyalinized collagen or (less commonly) ropey collagen. The spindle cells display short, stubby nuclei with finely dispersed chromatin, small or inconspicuous nucleoli, eosinophilic to amphophilic cytoplasm with indistinct borders, and occasionally elongated cytoplasmic processes. Scattered mast cells are frequently observed. These tumours demonstrate a wide range of cellularity, from highly cellular to sparsely cellular and extensively hyalinized variants. The cellular variant is marked by a dense proliferation of myofibroblasts, showing a higher spindle cell-to-collagen ratio compared with the classic form, and may present with infiltrative margins as well as a storiform or herringbone pattern. Mature adipocytes are commonly present, varying from a minor to a predominant component. Other histological features may include schwannoma-like nuclear palisading; focal epithelioid, deciduoid, or round cell morphology; and scattered atypical mononucleated or multinucleated cells with nuclear pleomorphism, typically regarded as degenerative-type atypia {26523539}. Blood vessels are generally small and hyalinized, often accompanied by a perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate. Immunohistochemically, myofibroblastomas show diffuse coexpression of desmin and CD34 in approximately 90% of cases, although occasional tumours may be negative for one or both markers. SMA expression is seen in about one third of cases, and approximately 90% demonstrate loss of nuclear RB1 expression {26523539; 22790852}.

Cytology

Smears are moderate to hypercellular and composed of cohesive tissue fragments, including spindle cells, exhibiting mild to moderate atypia with elongated nuclei and rare nuclear grooves, against a background of small amount of metachromatic stroma {23075890}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of RB1 loss by FISH or next-generation sequencing can be helpful in diagnostically challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: circumscribed tumour; haphazardly intersecting short fascicles of bland spindle cells; bands of hyalinized collagen; a variable adipocytic component.

Desirable: expression of CD34 and desmin; loss of RB1 may be helpful in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Myofibroblastomas are benign tumours; local recurrence is rare even if the margins are positive.
