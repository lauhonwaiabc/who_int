4.4.0.3 Adenosquamous carcinoma, HPV-independent

Definition

Adenosquamous carcinoma, HPV-independent, is an invasive malignant epithelial tumour exhibiting both squamous and glandular differentiation, arising through mechanisms not associated with HPV.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: mucoepidermoid carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours are localized to the cervix. Vaginal examples of HPV-independent adenosquamous carcinoma have not been specifically reported.

Clinical features

Most patients present with abnormal vaginal discharge or bleeding or a mass.

Epidemiology

These tumours are very uncommon, accounting for approximately 15–20% of all adenosquamous carcinomas of the cervix in different case series {26096203; 30944091; 30911077}. The median age of the patients in the reported cases is 45–51 years {39477315; 36811841; 26096203}.

Etiology

The etiology of these tumours is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Pathogenesis is unknown, but in one case, pathogenic variants in KRAS and BRCA1 were present in the tumour {36811841}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour typically appears as an ulcerated or exophytic firm mass {39477315; 33404855}.

Histopathology

Histologically, adenosquamous carcinomas exhibit unequivocal areas of glandular and squamous differentiation. The proportions of the squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and adenocarcinoma components are variable from case to case. In the few studies describing the histopathological features of HPV-independent adenosquamous carcinoma, the glandular component has usually been reported to be of gastric type, whereas the squamous component may be either keratinizing or non-keratinizing {39477315; 36811841; 33404855}. The glandular component may consist of both well-formed glands and signet-ring cells {36811841}.

Occasional cases have been associated with HPV-independent glandular intraepithelial precursors (atypical lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia or adenocarcinoma in situ of gastric type) {33404855}.

HPV-independent adenosquamous carcinomas show a negative/non–block-type immunostaining pattern for p16. The squamous component is usually positive for p63 and p40 {39477315; 36811841; 33404855}. The gastric glandular component may show positivity of varying degrees for PAX8, MUC6, SATB2, and CDX2 {33404855; 36811841}. Some cases have shown mutation-type p53 immunohistochemical expression {39477315}. ER and PR are typically negative.

Some of these cases have likely been reported as mucoepidermoid carcinoma in the literature {19092631}.

Cytology

No published reports have described the cytopathological features of these tumours.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV testing, if performed, should be negative.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive carcinoma exhibiting glandular and squamous differentiation; negative/non–block-type immunohistochemical staining pattern for p16 and/or negative HPV testing.

Staging

Tumours are staged according to the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

There is limited information, and none of the published reports have compared their prognosis with that of the more frequent HPV-associated adenosquamous carcinomas {39477315}.
