9.3.1.4 Other monodermal teratomas

Definition

Monodermal teratoma is a benign tumour composed of mature tissue derived from one germ layer (ectoderm, endoderm, or mesoderm).

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with symptoms related to the presence of an adnexal mass such as abdominal distension and/or pain. Endocrine manifestations may occur in tumours with hormonal production (pituitary-type adenoma); these include amenorrhoea secondary to prolactin production and Cushing-like features in corticotroph adenomas {2304732; 2329635; 3548446}.

Epidemiology

These are very rare neoplasms, and due to their low incidence, relatively few case series have been published. There has been a wide range of patient ages, from infants to older patients, but most reported cases are in women of reproductive age {39109004}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

These are usually predominantly cystic but solid elements may be present.

Histopathology

Epidermoid cysts are lined by bland-appearing, often keratinizing, squamous epithelium and are typically surrounded by a rim of collagenous stroma containing fibroblasts. Skin appendage structures, such as sebaceous glands and hair follicles, may be present. Keratin flakes and debris often fill the cyst lumen and may result in an exuberant foreign body giant cell reaction in the adjacent stroma {943942; 8852449}. Pituitary-type adenoma (e.g. corticotrophic adenoma, prolactinoma) consists of nests of densely granulated cells resembling their pituitary counterpart, some of which may demonstrate a microfollicular pattern. Ependymal cyst is a monodermal teratoma lined by ependymal epithelium usually with a little surrounding mature glial tissue; both components are usually GFAP positive {26261871; 8088721; 2373589}. Occasional cases of monodermal teratomas composed purely of respiratory-type epithelium have been described {761172; 6866875}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lesion composed entirely of single mature tissue originating from one germ cell layer.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Monodermal teratomas are benign.
