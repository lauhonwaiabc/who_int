9.2.1.4 Sclerosing stromal tumour

Definition

Sclerosing stromal tumour is a benign stromal neoplasm characterized by cellular nodules of admixed epithelioid and spindled cells, separated by oedematous to collagenous stroma, often with prominent dilated vascular channels.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the ovary.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with abnormal uterine bleeding or symptoms related to an ovarian mass, but some tumours are incidental findings; the large majority are unilateral. Hormonal symptoms (estrogenic or androgenic) are uncommon, but virilization and precocious puberty have been reported. Rare tumours are associated with Meigs syndrome {15285324; 20925634}.

Epidemiology

These tumours are uncommon, and cases have been reported to generally occur in young women and girls, with a median age of 26 years in the largest series, and most before 30 years {4348335; 20135135; 27149006; 28982854; 34467561}. Occasional tumours may present later in life, including rare examples in postmenopausal women.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

FISH studies on a small number of tumours have revealed a subpopulation of tumour cells with trisomy 12 {9422320; 15032299; 17990931}. In one study, recurrent FHL2::GLI2 fusion genes were demonstrated in 17 of 26 sclerosing stromal tumours (65%) and other GLI2 rearrangements in an additional 15% of cases (4 of 26) {31896750}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours generally range from 10 to 230 mm (mean: 84 mm in the largest series) {4348335; 20135135; 27149006; 28982854; 34467561}. They are typically well circumscribed and usually show a solid yellow to white cut surface. Areas of oedema and cyst formation may be present.

Histopathology

Sclerosing stromal tumour typically has a pseudolobular appearance at low magnification, with cellular nodules separated by a hypocellular oedematous, collagenous, or occasionally myxoid stroma. The relative proportion of cellular and hypocellular components varies widely. Dense sclerotic areas may be present, including keloid-like fibres in rare tumours, and occasionally there is focal calcification {34467561}. Thin-walled dilated vessels, often with a haemangiopericytoma-like appearance, are typically present. The cellular nodules are composed of a jumbled admixture of bland epithelioid and spindled cells. The former are typically weakly luteinized with moderate amounts of eosinophilic or clear vacuolated cytoplasm, but occasionally show prominent luteinization, especially during pregnancy {4348335; 25851706; 34467561}. Mitotic activity is typically low, but a significant numbers of mitoses (as many as 6 mitoses/mm2, equating to as many as 12 mitoses/10 HPF 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area) are seen in a small subset of cases; this is not thought to be of clinical significance, although the number of reported cases is small {27149006}. Occasional neoplasms contain areas of infarct-type necrosis. Tumours are usually positive for sex cord markers, such as inhibin, calretinin, and SF1, although staining is sometimes focal. Cytokeratins and EMA are usually negative. TFE3 expression has been reported in a subset of tumours, but no TFE3 rearrangements have been identified {28982854; 37528481}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pseudolobules with admixed epithelioid and spindled cells; hypocellular oedematous, collagenous, or myxoid stroma; thin-walled dilated vessels, often with a haemangiopericytoma-like appearance.

Desirable: positivity for sex cord stromal markers.

Staging

These are benign neoplasms that are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Nearly all sclerosing stromal tumours are benign. Only one recurrence has been reported, in a tumour that had capsular disruption, infarct-type necrosis, and 4 mitoses/mm2 (equating to 8 mitoses/10 HPF 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area) in the most mitotically active areas {27149006}.
