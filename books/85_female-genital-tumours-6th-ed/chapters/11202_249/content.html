11.2.0.2 Well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour

Definition

Well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour (WDPMT) is a benign, papillary neoplasm of mesothelial origin.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: well-differentiated papillary mesothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority of WDPMT occur in the abdominopelvic peritoneum, whereas a subset arises in the pleura and tunica vaginalis {22024662;30419426;15087673;36624609}.

Clinical features

Peritoneal WDPMT is usually found incidentally during surgery or laparoscopy for another process. Rarely, patients present with abdominopelvic pain or increase in ascites {22024662; 30635798}.

Epidemiology

WDPMT is a very rare type of mesothelial neoplasm that predominantly occurs in the abdominal or pelvic peritoneum of women at reproductive ages {30419426}. Patients with peritoneal WDPMT range in age from 18 to 75 years (median: 47 years), showing a pronounced female predominance with a 1:6 M:F ratio {30419426}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Occasionally, the tumour coexists with adenomatoid tumour, peritoneal inclusion cyst, or endometriosis {22029681;23530588;30522362;22024662}.

Pathogenesis

Most WDPMTs demonstrate somatic missense TRAF7 mutations, localized in one of a few hotspots within the WD40 repeats at the C-terminus of the protein {30171198; 39167353}. WDPMTs without TRAF7 mutations instead harbour somatic missense mutations in CDC42 {30171198}. Genetic alterations typical of mesotheliomas, such as BAP1, NF2, and SETD2 mutations, and CDKN2A/B homozygous deletions, are absent in WDPMT {30171198;39167353;32545767}.

Macroscopic appearance

Peritoneal WDPMTs are usually solitary but may be multiple; however, they are typically not diffuse and disseminated throughout the peritoneum, as seen with mesotheliomas. WDPMTs are grey to white, firm, papillary or nodular lesions over the peritoneal surfaces, most often < 20 mm (range: 2–60 mm in maximum diameter) {22024662; 30419426}.

Histopathology

The tumour is typically composed of exophytic papillae with paucicellular brovascular to myxoid stalks covered by a single layer of bland flattened to cuboidal mesothelial cells {22024662; 30419426}. Few cases show foci of small tubules or nests within the stalks that likely represent mesothelial invaginations or tangential sections of the branching papillary projections {23530588; 24618613; 22024662; 30419426}. Foamy histiocytes and psammomatous calcifications are occasionally present {30419426; 31929500}. The main differential diagnoses include epithelioid mesothelioma, which may focally have a well-differentiated papillary pattern, papillary mesothelioma in situ (MIS), serous surface papillomas, serous borderline tumours, and low-grade serous carcinomas. Deep invasion into the stroma, areas of solid growth, complex papillae covered by multiple layers of cells, cytological atypia of the covering cells, or presence of mitoses are indicative of mesothelioma {32769428}. Loss of BAP1 expression helps separate papillary MIS from true WDPMT {36876759; 38789301}.

WDPMTs are positive for traditional mesothelial markers, including calretinin, WT1, D2-40 and CK5/6. WDPMTs uniformly have intact/retained nuclear expression of BAP1, which can be helpful in the differential diagnosis with mesothelioma and MIS {30171198;38789301}. PAX8 immunoreactivity is frequently observed in peritoneal WDPMTs; however, its expression varies depending on the antibody used {29241740; 30419426}. WDPMTs are typically negative for CEA, BerEP4, MOC31, ER, and PR, which aids in the separation from carcinomas {16482993; 16462153; 23596113}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The presence of TRAF7 or CDC42 mutations, along with retained BAP1 and/or MTAP expression, and/or absence of CDKN2A (p16INK4a) homozygous deletions may be useful in the differential diagnosis with mesothelioma and papillary MIS.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: An exophytic papillary mesothelial lesion lacking stromal invasion or coalescent papillae, cytologically bland and without diffuse involvement of the peritoneum.

Desirable: intact/retained nuclear BAP1 immunostaining.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

This is a benign tumour {22024662; 30419426; 39167353}.
