10.1.5.4 Angiomyofibroblastoma

Definition

Angiomyofibroblastoma is a benign, well-circumscribed, and superficially located stromal fibroblastic tumour, usually arising in the vulva and vagina and less frequently in the perineum and inguinal area.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Virtually all cases arise in pelviperineal subcutaneous tissue, with most arising in the vulva. About 10–15% of cases are located in the vagina. Lesions in men occur rarely {10672053; 37389007}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a painless, slowly growing subcutaneous mass/swelling misinterpreted as a cyst {1314521; 9308729}. Occasional tumours are pedunculated {22371044}. Angiomyofibroblastoma is usually smaller than 50 mm.

Epidemiology

Angiomyofibroblastoma is a rare benign soft tissue tumour that most frequently affects the lower genital tract of young to middle-aged women {16943862}. Patients are commonly of reproductive age {8685229; 9308729}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

This neoplasm is thought to derive from the subepithelial mesenchyme of the lower genital tract. Most angiomyofibroblastomas harbour recurrent MTG1::CYP2E1 fusion transcripts {34385605; 36177509}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours are well demarcated, usually unencapsulated lesions; a thin pseudocapsule can be seen. The cut surface is tan-white and solid {1314521; 7551008; 9308729}.

Histopathology

Angiomyofibroblastoma has alternating hypocellular and hypercellular areas and prominent small to medium-sized thin-walled vessels; thick-walled, often hyalinized vessels, can be seen. Stroma is variably myxoedematous to fibrous (fine fibrils or sclerotic plaques) {1314521; 7551008; 8685229; 9308729}. Tumour cells are bland-looking, spindled to ovoid and epithelioid, arranged singly or in small nests or cords, and tend to be clustered around blood vessels. Binucleated and multinucleated cells can be present. Mitoses are rare or absent. Mast cells and lymphocytes can be sparsely observed in the stroma. Intratumoural mature adipocytes can be focal or (in rare examples) nearly diffuse (lipomatous angiomyofibroblastoma) {15782077; 24880711}. Sarcomatous transformation has been suggested by a case report describing the tumour merging imperceptibly with a high-grade sarcoma {9298888}. By immunohistochemistry, the majority of cases show positive staining for desmin, whereas SMA and pan-muscle actin are variably expressed {1314521;24880711}. Desmin staining may be reduced or absent in cases in postmenopausal women. Tumour cells are consistently positive for ER and PR and occasionally positive for CD34.

Cytology

In one reported case, smears have shown scattered plump, spindle-shaped, and epithelioid cells with oval, bland, vesicular nuclei along with some multinucleated giant cells against a haemorrhagic background {19697746}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MTG1::CYP2E1 fusion can support the diagnosis {36177509; 34385605}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed tumour; prominent small to medium-sized blood vessels with clustered cytologically bland spindled to ovoid/epithelioid tumour cells; infrequent to absent mitotic figures.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

There is no potential for recurrence, although 1 case associated with sarcomatous transformation locally recurred 2 years after initial excision {9298888}.
