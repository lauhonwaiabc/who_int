9.3.0.4 Non-gestational choriocarcinoma

Definition

Non-gestational choriocarcinoma is a malignant tumour composed of syncytiotrophoblastic and mononuclear trophoblastic cells and is not of gestational origin.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most tumours arise in the ovary; however, tumours originating in extraovarian sites have been reported {28877059; 31421690}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation is often nonspecific with symptoms related to an ovarian mass (abdominal pain, palpable mass, vaginal bleeding) and may mimic an ectopic pregnancy. β-hCG is typically elevated. Isosexual precocious puberty is observed in nearly half of prepubertal patients {19809555; 25105109; 28877059; 31421690}.

Epidemiology

Non-gestational choriocarcinoma is rare, with an estimated incidence of 1 case in 389 000 000 person-years {16648414}. These tumours comprise 2.1% of all malignant ovarian germ cell neoplasms, representing much < 1% of all ovarian neoplasms, with the literature largely limited to case reports and small case series {27073524; 38701311}. This tumour typically affects children and young adults.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The vast majority of non-gestational choriocarcinomas are of germ cell origin and are genetically related to the patient. In rare cases, ovarian carcinomas may contain foci of choriocarcinoma. These foci of choriocarcinoma are somatic and may result from retrodifferentiation {28877059; 16527336; 31421690; 31010836}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are typically unilateral, large, solid, haemorrhagic, and necrotic.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of a variable admixture of mononuclear trophoblastic cells (cytotrophoblast and intermediate trophoblast) and multinucleated trophoblastic cells (syncytiotrophoblast), often accompanied by extensive haemorrhage and necrosis. In some cases, the tumour predominantly consists of mononuclear trophoblast. Although non-gestational choriocarcinoma may exist as a pure tumour, it is more frequently encountered as a component of a mixed germ cell neoplasm. Rarely, areas of choriocarcinomatous differentiation may be observed in carcinomas of the ovary of a variety of histotypes {16527336; 20707961; 20881859}.

Immunohistochemistry is useful as a supplementary tool to establish trophoblastic differentiation. All trophoblastic cells will demonstrate positivity for AE1/AE3 and CK7. Depending on the trophoblastic component, variable expression of hCG, GATA3, inhibin, SALL4, hPL, HLA-G, MCAM, MUC4, and HSD3B1 may be seen.

Cytology

Smears show atypical, large relatively uniform cells with round to pleomorphic nuclei along with multinucleate syncytiotrophoblastic tumour giant cells. Immunostaining is useful to differentiate choriocarcinoma, yolk sac tumour, and embryonal carcinoma. A correlation with tumour marker levels is useful for diagnosing a choriocarcinoma {19679980}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Short tandem repeat (STR) DNA genotyping can be used to distinguish non-gestational choriocarcinoma from gestational choriocarcinoma. In non-gestational choriocarcinoma, the tumour’s allelic pattern is identical to that of the patient’s normal tissue. Allelic imbalances may also be seen {28877059; 31421690}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tumour composed of an admixture of mononuclear trophoblastic cells (cytotrophoblast and/or intermediate trophoblast) and syncytiotrophoblastic cells of non-gestational origin.

Desirable: in cases where a diagnosis of pure non-gestational choriocarcinoma is being considered, confirmation of non-gestational origin by molecular analysis is desirable to definitively distinguish these tumours from gestational choriocarcinoma.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Non-gestational choriocarcinoma is clinically aggressive, with frequent lymphatic and intraperitoneal spread. At the time of presentation, the majority of patients exhibit metastatic disease (most commonly to the lung). Choriocarcinoma associated with ovarian carcinomas has a poor prognosis {29894083; 20881859; 28877059}.
