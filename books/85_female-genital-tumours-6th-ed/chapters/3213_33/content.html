3.2.1.3 Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-independent

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), HPV-independent, is an invasive and/or exophytic malignant, epithelial tumour exhibiting pure squamous differentiation and arising through mechanisms not associated with HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur predominantly in the upper third of the vagina and are more common on the posterior wall {18980291; 11812092}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are similar to those of HPV-associated SCC. The presence of simultaneous cervical or vulvar SCC, or a history of such cancers within the past 5 years, must be excluded before making a diagnosis of a vaginal primary.

Epidemiology

Vaginal SCC constitutes 1–2% of all female genital tract malignancies. HPV-associated SCC is more common than HPV-independent SCC; the reported proportion of HPV-independent SCC varies from 20% to 30% {19115209; 28369882; 25155250}. Most patients with HPV-independent SCC are postmenopausal; the median patient age at diagnosis is 73 years {21166704; 18500261; 2166263}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

TP53 mutations occur in some cases {22226684}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is similar to that of HPV-associated SCC.

Histopathology

In contrast to HPV-associated SCC, HPV-independent SCC is more likely to be of the keratinizing pattern {21166704; 18500261}; however, there is significant morphological overlap, and a reliable distinction cannot be made on morphology alone. p16 negativity / non–block-type staining and p53 mutation-type staining are common findings {22226684; 30561092}. Grading is currently not recommended because of the lack of evidence {35703457}.

No established precursor lesion for HPV-independent vaginal SCC has been identified to date; unlike in the cervix and vulva, a category of HPV-independent precursor lesion is not recognized.

Cytology

HPV-associated and HPV-independent SCCs are indistinguishable on the basis of cytopathology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The absence of HPV may be ascertained by PCR or in situ hybridization for low- and high-risk HPV types.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive carcinoma with squamous differentiation; no previous (≤ 5 years) or concomitant SCC of cervix or vulva; negative/non–block-type p16 staining and/or negative HPV molecular testing.

Staging

These neoplasms are staged using the FIGO and TNM staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Because of the rarity of these neoplasms, evidence for a different prognosis for HPV-independent compared with HPV-associated SCC is more limited in comparison with that for the vulva. Available evidence shows that HPV-independent SCCs of the vagina have worse overall and progression-free survival compared with HPV-associated SCC, independent of stage and patient age {21666483; 2166263; 23402906; 22226684; 34546565}. Currently, there is no evidence of a difference in survival between p53-mutated and p53-wildtype HPV-independent SCC {34546565}.
