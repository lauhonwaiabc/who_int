5.2.2.8 Carcinosarcoma

Definition

Carcinosarcoma is a biphasic tumour composed of high-grade carcinomatous and sarcomatous components.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant mixed Müllerian tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterine corpus

Clinical features

Patients usually present with vaginal bleeding, uterine enlargement, or a pelvic mass {21742560; 21309261}. Approximately 45% of cases are stage III or IV at diagnosis {27052653; 30958427}.

Epidemiology

Carcinosarcomas accounted for 5% of all uterine malignancies in the SEER database (1973–2013). Patients, who are typically postmenopausal (66–72 years), share the same predisposing risk factors as for endometrial carcinoma {29400015}.

Etiology

About 6% of women with endometrial carcinosarcoma have a history of tamoxifen use {11240687; 27931750}. Carcinosarcomas may also occur as a long-term complication of pelvic radiotherapy, with a time interval from irradiation of 5–20 years {6245780; 28761924}.

Pathogenesis

Similar genetic alterations are present in both the carcinomatous and sarcomatous components {23199529; 27499902}, supporting a clonal relationship and shared cell of origin. The sarcomatous component is derived from the carcinoma as a result of transdifferentiation (epithelial–mesenchymal transition) during tumour evolution, which may occur early or late {31324031; 21125666; 22974476; 28292439}. Most cases are characterized by TP53 mutations (90%), similar to endometrial serous carcinoma. Mutations typically associated with endometrioid endometrial carcinoma are less frequent and largely dependent upon the molecular subtype of the tumour. Accordingly, approximately 75% of carcinosarcomas are classified as p53abn, and  approximately 15% as no specific molecular profile (NSMP); 5–10% of endometrial carcinosarcomas belong to the POLE-mutated or dMMR (mismatch repair–deficient) groups {27499902; 28292439; 34536971; 31672974; 39615841}.

Macroscopic appearance

There is a large, polypoid mass, usually filling the uterine cavity and often prolapsing through the cervical os. Areas of haemorrhage, necrosis, and cystic degeneration are frequent.

Histopathology

The tumours are composed of an admixture of malignant epithelium and mesenchyme, which is typically sharply juxtaposed. Sarcoma-predominant tumours occur in 40–60% of cases {30550483}. The carcinomatous component is most often endometrioid, serous, or ambiguous, but clear cell and undifferentiated carcinoma may be encountered. The mesenchymal component most commonly consists of high-grade sarcoma NOS, but heterologous elements (including rhabdomyosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, and rarely osteosarcoma) may be seen. Deep myometrial and lymphovascular invasion, respectively, are present in 30–45% and 36–40% of the tumours {27052653; 30958427}. The morphology of metastases from carcinosarcomas is variable, but most metastases (~90%) contain the carcinomatous component {18059221}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: high-grade and distinctly biphasic malignant epithelial and mesenchymal components.

Staging

This entity is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of tumours of the uterus – endometrium [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system {37337978}. For FIGO 2023 staging system, molecular classification is strongly encouraged when feasible.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with FIGO stage I–II disease have a 5-year disease-specific survival rate of 60%, while patients with stage III and IV disease, respectively, have rates of 25% and 10%, respectively {27052653; 19896181}. In addition to advanced stage, other independent factors associated with poor prognosis are size > 5 cm, myometrial invasion > 50%, lymphovascular invasion, and sarcoma predominance. Some large recent series suggest that serous histology and heterologous rhabdomyoblasticsarcomatous differentiation are significantly associated with worse survival {27052653; 18059221; 30414738}. The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) molecular classification is also highly predictive of clinical outcomes, with p53abnormal / copy-number high and NSMP categories being associated with the most aggressive clinical behaviour {34536971}.
