3.2.2.1 Villous adenoma and tubulovillous adenoma

Definition

Villous adenoma / tubulovillous adenoma of the vagina is a primary benign glandular tumour resembling its colorectal counterpart.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: intestinal-type adenoma; adenomatous polyp.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Various locations within the vagina have been reported; however, most occur along the lower posterior wall {24722061}. Rare cases have been described in the vulvar vestibule {36307835}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a vaginal mass or abnormal vaginal bleeding {24722061}.

Epidemiology

These are extremely rare vaginal lesions. They have been reported in adults aged 19–80 years {24722061; 23202776}.

Etiology

Speculated origins include intestinal metaplasia of the vaginal surface epithelium or adenosis, congenital rests of intestinal epithelium (cloacal remnants), and extension of intestinal epithelium through a rectovaginal fistula {24722061}.

Pathogenesis

A frameshift truncating mutation in APC has been reported in one adenoma, and mutations in KRAS and TP53 have been reported in associated adenocarcinomas {30383609; 39522642}. TP53, KEL, RB1, RNF43, and PTEN mutations demonstrated high variant allele frequency in one case {36307835}.

Macroscopic appearance

These are polypoid lesions, with reported cases measuring 9–30 mm {24722061; 23202776}.

Histopathology

Histology shows villiform and/or tubular structures lined by columnar epithelium, often containing scattered goblet cells, with atypical pseudostratified elongated hyperchromatic nuclei {24722061}. No stromal invasion is present. The degree of atypia ranges from mild to severe. Cases with severe nuclear atypia and/or architectural complexity should be diagnosed as adenomas with high-grade dysplasia using the criteria applicable to their colorectal counterparts {24722061}. Immunohistochemically, they are usually positive for CK20 and CDX2; CK7 is variable, and ER is usually negative {24722061; 30383609}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tubulovillous/villous architecture; intestinal-type epithelium with nuclear pseudostratification and hyperchromasia; no stromal invasion.

Desirable: presence of goblet cells; positivity for CK20 and/or CDX2.

Staging

These are benign tumours and are not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Patient outcome may be uneventful; however, in some cases, the lesions may recur {24722061; 15661252}. One patient developed intestinal-type adenocarcinoma 2 years after the diagnosis of adenoma with high-grade dysplasia {24722061}. The coexistence of intestinal-type adenocarcinoma with villous/tubulovillous adenoma suggests that the latter is a premalignant lesion similar to those that occur in the gastrointestinal tract {11293170; 16803550; 23202776; 24371679}. Metachronous vulvar tubulovillous adenoma has been reported in one case {30383609}.
