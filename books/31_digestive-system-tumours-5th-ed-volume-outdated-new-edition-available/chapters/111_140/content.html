11.1 Oesophageal neuroendocrine neoplasms

Definition

Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) of the oesophagus are oesophageal epithelial neoplasms with neuroendocrine differentiation, including well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), and mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNENs) – an umbrella category including mixed adenoneuroendocrine carcinoma (MANEC).

Related terminology

Acceptable: carcinoid; well-differentiated endocrine tumour/carcinoma; poorly differentiated endocrine carcinoma; mixed exocrine-endocrine carcinoma; mixed adenoneuroendocrine carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

Neuroendocrine tumour, grade 1 (8240/3); neuroendocrine tumour, grade 2 (8249/3); neuroendocrine tumour, grade 3 (8249/3); large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (8013/3); small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (8041/3); combined small cell–adenocarcinoma (8045/3); combined small cell–squamous cell carcinoma (8045/3)

Localization

NENs typically occur in the lower oesophagus, often in association with metaplastic Barrett mucosa or (rarely) ectopic gastric mucosa. NECs can also occur in the middle and even upper oesophagus {18670347; 23426118}.

Clinical features

Dysphagia, pain, weight loss, asthenia, and sometimes melaena are the most common presenting symptoms {15599742; 22012036; 28656561}. Some NETs are discovered incidentally {11914632; 16437611}. Metastatic NETs may present with carcinoid syndrome {15599742}. NECs may be associated with paraneoplastic syndromes {21576835}. On endoscopy, NETs usually appear as polypoid or nodular submucosal masses, < 2 cm in diameter {11914632; 16437611}, whereas NECs are large, infiltrative, and ulcerated {28656561}.

Epidemiology

Oesophageal NENs are exceedingly rare {15599742; 22012036; 28656561}. They account for 0.04–1% of all gastroenteropancreatic NENs overall {15599742; 28656561; 28079231}, but figures as high as 5% have been reported in some countries {22012036}. The incidence is higher in males than in females (M:F ratio: 6:1), with a predominance in the sixth and seventh decades of life (mean patient age: 56 years) {15599742; 28656561; 9024720}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. The presence of endocrine cells in the submucosal glands of the lower oesophagus or in Barrett mucosa {4134908} might explain the predominance of NENs in the lower oesophagus {11914632; 8511743}. NECs might be associated with alcohol consumption and/or heavy smoking {28079231; 8511743}.

Pathogenesis

A recent study of 55 oesophageal NECs {29728688} showed a high rate of inactivating mutations in TP53 and RB1 – as are seen in most cases of small cell NEC (SCNEC) – as well as in three other genes: NOTCH1, FAT1, and FBXW7. Also reported were recurrent mutations in three genes not known to be involved in oesophageal or neuroendocrine tumorigenesis: PDE3A, PTPRM, and CBLN2.

Macroscopic appearance

NETs are usually small lesions. In a systematic review of 19 cases, the mean tumour size was 2.4 cm {30302162}. NECs are often large, bulky, deeply infiltrative tumours. They may be exophytic or ulcerated {23426118}.

Histopathology

NET: Fewer than 50 cases of oesophageal NETs have been reported in the literature. They showed the typical features of well-differentiated NENs. The growth pattern may be insular or cribriform. The tumours consist of medium-sized cells with a low N:C ratio, abundant cytoplasm, and a small ovoid nucleus with dispersed chromatin containing small nucleoli. Variable amounts of well-vascularized stroma are present. Focal necrosis may be seen. Neuroendocrine differentiation is confirmed by the expression of chromogranin A and synaptophysin. Various hormones have been detected by immunohistochemistry, including serotonin, (entero)glucagon, PP, gastrin, and calcitonin {11914632; 28944148; 9077732}. VMAT2 expression, suggestive of an enterochromaffin-like (ECL) cell–like phenotype, has been described in some cases {8511743; 17684761}. Grading information is rarely available, especially for cases reported in the older literature, but most cases can be retrospectively graded as G1–2.

NEC: These tumours account for > 90% of all oesophageal NENs {22012036}. They are subtyped as small cell NEC (SCNEC) and large cell NEC (LCNEC); the relative proportion of LCNEC varies across studies, from 12% to 62% {18670347; 23426118; 18360283}. Like other NECs, oesophageal NECs usually show solid, rosette-like or palisading patterns; necrosis is frequent and often extensive. Mitotic activity is consistently brisk. SCNECs are characterized by small to medium-sized cells with a high N:C ratio, scant basophilic cytoplasm, and elongated nuclei containing finely dispersed chromatin without well-formed nucleoli. LCNECs are characterized by medium-sized to large cells with basophilic cytoplasm and large ovoid nuclei containing large nucleoli. Neuroendocrine differentiation should be confirmed by immunohistochemistry. Synaptophysin expression is a consistent feature, whereas chromogranin A expression, present in about 60% of cases, is usually focal and faint {23426118}. TTF1 is positive in about 70% of cases {23426118}. The main differential diagnosis is basaloid squamous cell carcinoma, which usually shows immunohistochemical evidence of squamous cell differentiation, including expression of p63, p40, and CK5/6 {10759947}; however, p63 is not fully discriminant, because it may be detected in some oesophageal NECs {23426118}.

MiNEN: Oesophageal MiNENs usually consist of poorly differentiated NEC and either squamous cell carcinoma or adenocarcinoma (in Barrett mucosa or ectopic gastric mucosa) {28079231}. Rare collision tumours combining an apparently well-differentiated component and adenocarcinoma have also been reported in Barrett mucosa {11914632}.

Collision tumours: Collision tumours do not meet the definition of MiNENs.

Grading: Oesophageal NENs are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs.

Cytology

The cytological features resemble those of NECs occurring elsewhere.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: morphological criteria suggestive of neuroendocrine differentiation (must be confirmed by the immunohistochemical demonstration of specific lineage markers, e.g. chromogranin A or synaptophysin).

Staging

Unlike for NETs occurring at other gastrointestinal sites, there is no TNM staging system specifically for oesophageal NET. The staging of oesophageal NEC and MiNEN follows that of oesophageal carcinomas.

Prognosis and prediction

Despite reports to the contrary in older literature, the prognosis of oesophageal NETs appears to be good, mainly depending on tumour size and extent of dissemination. The influence of tumour grade cannot be assessed, because only very limited data are available. Metastatic dissemination is rare {16437611}. The overall survival time ranges from 1 to 23 years. In contrast, the prognosis of NECs is very poor. Metastatic dissemination is frequent at diagnosis. The median overall survival time ranges from 8 to 15 months; most patients die within 2 years of diagnosis. The presence of distant metastases seems to be the strongest adverse prognostic factor {18670347}. There is no statistically significant difference in survival between LCNEC and SCNEC {18670347}. Prognosis apparently depends on the Ki-67 proliferation index {26943788}. MiNENs might have a better prognosis than pure NECs; the median survival time is about 20 months {18670347}, and prognosis is based on the Ki-67 proliferation index of the NEC component {29592868}.

There is no consensus on the optimal treatment for oesophageal NENs {28656561}. Small NETs (< 1–1.5 cm in size) without evidence of locoregional metastasis can be successfully treated by endoscopic resection. Larger tumours require surgery; adjuvant chemotherapy has been used in metastatic cases. The treatment of NECs and MiNENs is commonly based on chemotherapy with platinum salts. Tumours with only locoregional extension can be treated by a combination of surgery, chemotherapy, and even radiotherapy {18670347}.
