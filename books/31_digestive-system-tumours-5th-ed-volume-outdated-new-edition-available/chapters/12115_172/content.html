12.1.1.5 Intestinal T-cell lymphoma NOS

Definition

Intestinal T-cell lymphoma (ITCL) NOS is an aggressive GI tract T-cell lymphoma that lacks the clinical and pathological features of enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL), monomorphic epitheliotropic ITCL (MEITL), anaplastic large cell lymphoma, or extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma.

Localization

ITCL-NOS can occur in or be confined to a particular gastrointestinal organ or involve multiple sites; dissemination to regional lymph nodes and extragastrointestinal sites is not uncommon {24128129; 20002766}. The colon and small intestine are the more commonly involved organs {24128129}. Limited data suggest that multicentric disease is less common in ITCL-NOS than in MEITL {21423155}.

Clinical features

The symptoms and signs of ITCL-NOS depend on the type and extent of organ involvement. The clinical presentation of patients with intestinal disease is similar to those with EATL and MEITL, although malabsorption is infrequent. Epigastric pain and haematemesis are common in individuals with primary gastric lymphomas {21423155; 20002766}. Approximately 50% of patients present with stage III/IV disease {21423155; 21798075}.

Epidemiology

The geographical distribution of ITCL-NOS is wide, but a higher frequency has been reported in Asia, where it is one of the more common subtypes of ITCL {24481942}. The mean patient age is similar to or slightly lower than that of MEITL, and males are more commonly affected {21423155; 24481942}.

Etiology

The etiology of ITCL-NOS is unknown, but it is probably multifactorial, given the disease heterogeneity. Occasional cases have been reported in individuals with inflammatory gastrointestinal disorders (inflammatory bowel disease and autoimmune enteropathy) {24128129; 22226659; 29446107}. If the patient is immunosuppressed or has received immunosuppressive or immunomodulatory therapy, the lymphoma is better classified as an immunosuppression-related or immunomodulatory therapy–related lymphoproliferative disorder {23541274}.

Pathogenesis

The types and frequencies of chromosome alterations in ITCL-NOS are unknown, but JAK/STAT and MAPK pathway mutations, similar to those found in EATL and MEITL, have been identified in the few cases evaluated {27389054}.

Macroscopic appearance

ITCL-NOS more commonly has an ulcerated plaque-like appearance, and protruding luminal masses can be seen in some cases {21423155}.

Histopathology

The lymphoma cells vary in size from medium to large and often show pleomorphism. Epithelial infiltration is only present in a minority of cases (~20%) {21423155; 20002766}. CD4+ and CD4−/CD8− are the more frequent phenotypes. The majority express βF1 (TCRαβ), and a subset of tumours can be T-cell receptor–silent {20002766; 21423155; 24128129}. A high proportion of tumours express the cytotoxic granule protein TIA1, whereas granzyme B and CD30 expression is variable, and a minority of tumours (10%) are EBV-positive {20002766; 21423155; 24128129}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primary T-cell lymphoma of the GI tract not conforming to EATL, MEITL, anaplastic large cell lymphoma, or extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma; infiltration by medium-sized to large lymphoma cells; expression of T-lineage markers, and often CD4+ or CD4− CD8−.

Staging

Lymphoid neoplasms are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

ITCL-NOS patients have a poor prognosis, although some studies report better median (35 months) and overall survival times than EATL and MEITL patients {21798075; 21423155}. High stage and large cell morphology have been associated with worse 5-year survival rates {20002766; 21798075}.
