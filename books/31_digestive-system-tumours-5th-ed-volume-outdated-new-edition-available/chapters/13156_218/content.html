13.1.5.6 Embryonal sarcoma of the liver

Definition

Embryonal sarcoma of the liver is a malignant hepatic neoplasm of children, composed of heterogeneous undifferentiated mesenchymal cells.

ICD-O coding

8991/3 Embryonal sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

XH42Q2 Embryonal sarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: undifferentiated embryonal sarcoma.

Not recommended: malignant mesenchymoma of the liver.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Embryonal sarcoma usually arises in the right hepatic lobe {3791152; 24210186}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with abdominal distension, pain, fever, and weight loss; hepatic rupture with haemoperitoneum is uncommon {208754}. Leukocytosis and elevated serum alkaline phosphatase are common. Invasion of the inferior vena cava with extension to the right atrium may be observed {1702267}.

Epidemiology

Embryonal sarcoma is rare, although it is the most common malignant hepatic mesenchymal neoplasm in the paediatric population. Children aged 5–15 years are typically affected, with no sex predilection {208754; 27781381}; presentation in adulthood is rare {18361435}.

Etiology

Embryonal sarcoma is sporadic. Rarely, transformation from mesenchymal hamartoma has been reported {20925497}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Embryonal sarcoma is well circumscribed but unencapsulated. Tumours are large (10–30 cm). The cut surface is heterogeneous, with alternating solid, fleshy, and mucoid areas and foci of cystic degeneration, necrosis, and haemorrhage {208754}.

Histopathology

The tumour is typically composed of variably spindled, stellate, and pleomorphic giant cells loosely arranged in a myxoid stroma; fibrotic areas with solid, fascicular, or storiform growth may be observed {208754; 1702267}. Cytoplasmic PAS-positive eosinophilic hyaline (lysosomal) globules in giant cells are a characteristic finding {208754}. Mitotic activity (including atypical mitoses) is easily detected. Peripheral entrapment of bile ducts is often seen, as is extramedullary haematopoiesis. Rare cases associated with mesenchymal hamartoma have been reported {9331300; 11779051}.

Embryonal sarcoma shows no specific immunophenotype. Variable, often limited expression of keratins, desmin, and MSA may be seen {1702267; 17378682; 16785789}; a subset of cases are positive for glypican-3 (GPC3) {21937079}. The tumour cells are negative for EMA, Hep Par-1 (CPS1), AFP, myogenin, and MYOD1 {17378682}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Embryonal sarcoma typically shows a complex karyotype {11376805}. The t(11;19)(q13;q13.4) characteristic of mesenchymal hamartoma has been detected in several cases {17311249}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindled, stellate, and pleomorphic giant cells loosely arranged in a myxoid stroma.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Although embryonal sarcoma of the liver may pursue an aggressive clinical course, with metastases to the lung and peritoneum, patients with surgically resectable tumours often have a favourable outcome {24210186; 26925712; 27427850}. Tumour size > 15 cm is an adverse prognostic indicator {27781381}.
