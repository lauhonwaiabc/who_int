14.2.1.1 Hepatic neuroendocrine neoplasms

Definition

Hepatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) are malignant epithelial neoplasms with neuroendocrine differentiation that arise in the liver.

Related terminology

Not recommended: carcinoid tumour of the liver; atypical carcinoid tumour of the liver.

Subtype(s)

Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (80130/3); small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (80410/3); mixed large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–adenocarcinoma (cholangiocarcinoma) (80132/3); mixed large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–hepatocellular carcinoma (80134/3); mixed small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–adenocarcinoma (cholangiocarcinoma) (80451/3); mixed small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–hepatocellular carcinoma (80455/3); amphicrine-like carcinoma (85743/3); amphicrine-like adenocarcinoma (cholangiocarcinoma) (85744/3); amphicrine-like hepatocellular carcinoma (85746/3)

Localization

Hepatic NENs can arise anywhere within the liver. Some neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), and mixed cholangiocarcinoma–NECs appear to arise from major bile ducts near the hilum, similarly to extrahepatic bile duct primaries {15706430}.

Clinical features

Hepatic NENs are very rare; they are much less common than hepatic metastases of histologically similar neoplasms. Therefore, the potential of a metastatic NEN must be rigorously excluded before the neoplasm can be accepted as a hepatic primary {19452590}. Metastasis should be excluded clinically including somatostatin analogue PET, and ultimate diagnosis of liver primary NEN may require clinical observation of 1 year {18727836}. Patients with NETs uncommonly experience hormonal related symptoms, such as carcinoid syndrome {26882787;11433205}, and are either asymptomatic or present with mass effect–associated symptoms {18984960}. Tumours near the hilum or bile duct origin may cause jaundice. Cases presenting with Zollinger–Ellison syndrome and Cushing syndrome have been reported {19918579;17556871}.

Epidemiology

Hepatic NENs account for < 1% of all resected hepatic primary tumours {27881473}. Reported cases of hepatic NENs have primarily occurred in adults (age range: 8–83 years; mean: 50 years), with a slight female predilection {18984960;30065788}. Few data are available, although most of the reported cases of hepatic NENs are from Asian populations.

Etiology

Some reported cases have a history of viral hepatitis, suggesting a role in the etiology of MiNEN with component of hepatocellular carcinoma and cholangiocarcinoma {30065788;19173727;29794961}.

Pathogenesis

Hepatic NETs near the hilum may have originated from the major bile ducts rather than hepatocytes. Hepatic NECs may reflect aberrant differentiation of hepatocellular carcinoma or cholangiocarcinoma {34310982}. Neuroendocrine differentiation is reported only rarely in conventional hepatic carcinomas and, despite early reports to the contrary, also appears to be uncommon in fibrolamellar hepatocellular carcinoma {20495535}.

Macroscopic appearance

Hepatic NENs are typically solitary, circumscribed parenchymal masses, which average 65 mm {30065788}. NETs have a soft consistency and show little or no necrosis, whereas NECs and mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNENs) may be grossly necrotic. MiNENs may have regions with gross features of hepatocellular carcinoma, or sclerotic foci reflecting the cholangiocarcinoma component.

Histopathology

NET

NETs display the well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Focal necrosis can occur. The eosinophilic cytoplasmic granularity of midgut serotonin-producing NETs is not typically found in hepatic primaries and would suggest a metastasis from an occult small intestine. Hepatic NETs can be G1 or G2 {12636090} with a mean Ki-67 proliferative index of 7% {27881473}; G3 NETs are yet to be reported in the liver.

NEC

NECs show the poorly differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage, either small or large cell type [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Small cell carcinomas resemble their extrahepatic counterparts {29507528;18210116;26722053} and are typically encountered as a component of a mixed hepatocellular–neuroendocrine carcinoma. Necrosis and mitoses are abundant, and the Ki-67 proliferation index is > 50%. Large cell NECs are also typically mixed with non-NEC components.

MiNEN

Hepatic MiNENs display the classic morphological features of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine neoplasia [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]] and are more common than pure NECs. Most cases include a predominant component of hepatocellular carcinoma {28647212;27895410;27384317;29794961;34310982}. The NEC and hepatocellular components can be intermingled (but with distinct morphology) or separate {27881473}. Rare cases of mixed cholangiocarcinoma–NEC have been reported {26589730;26184027;19155146}.

Amphicrine-like carcinoma

Hepatic amphicrine-like carcinoma may occur as amphicrine-like hepatocellular carcinoma {34310982} and amphicrine-like adenocarcinoma/cholangiocarcinoma {20952282}.

Immunohistochemistry

Hepatic NENs express cytokeratins, synaptophysin, chromogranin A, and INSM1 {35294740}. Chromogranin A staining is often patchy and may show dot-like perinuclear positivity in NECs {31857137;35294740}. Aberrant p53 expression and loss of RB1 expression are commonly seen in NECs. TTF1 staining may be seen, particularly in small cell NECs {33433884;35294740}.

Grading

Hepatic NENs are graded according to the system of gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

Cytology

The cytological features of hepatic NENs are indistinguishable from NENs arising elsewhere in the GI tract or pancreas. MiNENs may show evidence of the non-neuroendocrine component that, in the case of mixed hepatocellular–neuroendocrine carcinoma, would support a hepatic origin.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

NET

Essential: metastasis should be excluded clinically; well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: SSTR2 positive immunohistochemistry.

NEC

Essential: metastasis should be excluded clinically; small cell carcinoma or large cell carcinoma pattern; sheets or trabeculae of poorly differentiated cells; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: reduced/absent SSTR2 expression; loss of RB1 expression or abnormal p53 expression.

MiNEN

Essential: metastasis should be excluded clinically; a mixed tumour composed of two morphologically recognizable components; either type ≥ 30% of the neoplasm; neuroendocrine markers expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Desirable: assessment of Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count to grade the NET component or to prognostically stratify the NEC component; grading of the non-neuroendocrine component.

Amphicrine-like carcinoma

Essential: a mixed tumour with no morphologically distinct zones of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine differentiation; ≥ 30% of the neoplasm; two neuroendocrine markers expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Desirable: assessment of Ki-67 proliferation index.

Staging

Due to their rarity, hepatic NETs do not have their own staging systems. For NECs, MiNENs, and amphicrine-like carcinomas, the staging follows the criteria of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Hepatic NETs are often amenable to surgical resection, and long-term survival without recurrence has been observed {14654473;24653852;29147250;39351005;34006241}. Death from metastatic disease occurs in 18–47% of cases, with variable survival durations {19452590;18984960;30075602}, especially in G2 cases. Both NECs and MiNENs are highly aggressive and rapidly lethal {26722053;29507528}. Given the markedly different prognosis and treatment between NETs and NECs, correct classification and grading (Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count) are crucial in the interpretations of all NENs {30065788}.
