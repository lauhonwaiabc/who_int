12.1.3.1 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue

Definition

Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (EMZL) of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) is an indolent primary extranodal B-cell lymphoma with cytological and architectural features reminiscent of Peyer patch lymphoid tissue, the prototypical MALT. EMZL typically arises from marginal zone B cells of acquired MALT and is often associated with an underlying chronic inflammatory disorder.

ICD-O coding

96991/3 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue

ICD-11 coding

2A85.1 Extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue of stomach

2A85.3 Extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma, primary site excluding stomach or skin

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma (MALT lymphoma).

Not recommended: MALToma; Mediterranean lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

Immunoproliferative small intestinal disease / alpha heavy chain disease (97621/3)

Localization

EMZL may occur at any site in the digestive system, including the GI tract, liver, and pancreaticobiliary tract {26989205;37076996;39054516;28353562;28551602}. Immunoproliferative small intestinal disease (IPSID), also called alpha heavy chain disease (αHCD), involves the GI tract (primarily the small intestine) {16259867;29372346;35621691}.

Clinical features

Patients present with abdominal discomfort, gastrointestinal bleeding, or other site-related symptoms, or may be asymptomatic {36840791;37247896;39482577;39624879}. Patients with IPSID/αHCD have diarrhoea, weight loss, malabsorption, and abdominal pain {34270561;35139275;35621691}.

Epidemiology

EMZL accounts for 3–6% of gastric malignancies {36792000;36614921}, 2–10% of primary gastrointestinal lymphomas {25639480;28288721;30315345}, 35–50% of primary gastric lymphomas {35053607;36614921}, and 2–28% of primary intestinal lymphomas {25639480;29225710;35053607}, with wide geographical variation. The stomach is the most common anatomical site for EMZL, accounting for 30–35% of all EMZLs documented until 2018 {33829216;37076996;35053607}. However, widespread use of antibiotics has significantly lowered the prevalence of Helicobacter pylori, resulting in decreased incidence of gastric EMZL {33829216;36518580;36614921}. Approximately 6–8% of EMZLs arise in the intestine {33829216;37076996;35053607}. EMZL of oesophagus {28053856;28353588;39054516}, liver {12406890;28353562}, biliary tract {20679881;28551602}, and pancreas {9508528} are very rare. In the USA, the incidence in 2020 was 0.23 cases per 100 000 person-years {38893144}. Patients are almost all adults, with a median age in the sixth to seventh decades of life. Men and women are roughly equally affected {24417667;26989205;38418651;39375056;39482577;36840791}. IPSID/αHCD predominantly occurs in adolescents and young adults, mainly in the Middle East, Africa, and Asia, and only exceptionally in Europe and the American continent {35621691;34270561}.

Etiology

EMZL results from a chronic inflammatory process caused by infection, autoimmunity, or unknown factors in concert with genetic changes in marginal zone B cells. There are variations in the role of chronic inflammation and type of recurrent genetic alterations in the various anatomical sites (see #49857Table 12.02).

  • pylori–associated gastric EMZL is the prototypical model for EMZL etiology and pathogenesis. The development of gastric EMZL has been linked to chronic H. pylori infection by epidemiological {1682595} and in vivo studies. The latter show that the lymphoma cell growth critically depends on H. pylori–mediated T-cell help, involving both cognate interactions and stimulation by soluble ligands such as CD154 (CD40L) and BAFF {8102718;12814773;9137085;20428202;27873317}, which activate the non-canonical NF-κB pathway {35139275}.
  • pylori eradication by antibiotics leads to lymphoma remissions in about 75–80% of patients overall {8102719;16698756;21317175;31912792;39482577;36840791;37122607}. Since approximately 2000, with the introduction of antibiotic therapy for H. pylori–associated gastritis and the use of proton-pump inhibitors, the incidence of gastric EMZL has declined significantly {24417667;36518580;36792000;37076996}. H. pylori–negative gastric EMZL currently accounts for approximately 18–40% of cases {36840791;37076996;38418651;39482577;39375056}. A similar role has been suggested for Campylobacter jejuni infection in the development of IPSID/αHCD {14724303;15542584;35621691}, a disease that is disappearing with the worldwide availability of antibiotics {15542584}. A subset of hepatic EMZL may be related to HCV infection {36805885}. The etiology of most other digestive tract EMZLs is unknown.

    • Pathogenesis

    Clonally rearranged IG genes in EMZL show somatic hypermutations {37294969} and frequent intraclonal variations at the IG gene level, reflecting ongoing maturation of antigen-affinity during multistep, antigen-driven evolution {37294969}.

    Four mutually exclusive translocations are specifically associated with EMZL (see #49857Table 12.02): t(11;18)(q21;q21) (BIRC3::MALT1), t(1;14)(p22;q32) (IGH::BCL10), t(14;18)(q32;q21) (IGH::MALT1), and t(3;14)(p14;q32) (IGH::FOXP1). They result in the production of a chimeric protein (BIRC3::MALT1) or in transcriptional deregulation of BCL10, MALT1, and FOXP1, respectively {27452667;35139275}. Trisomies of chromosomes 3 and 18, although nonspecific, are frequent in EMZL. The frequencies of the translocations and trisomies vary markedly with the primary site of disease and also among series. The translocations all converge on the activation of the same oncogenic pathway associated with NF-κB {35139275;27452667}. The BIRC3::MALT1 translocation, which occurs in 6–51% of gastric EMZL, is significantly associated with H. pylori negativity and nuclear BCL10 expression {10760689;11984515;17894851;21890816;38843856;39482577;38418651}. With the decline of H. pylori–associated gastric EMZL, the frequency of BIRC3::MALT1 has increased. Based on limited case numbers, BIRC3::MALT1 is also found in up to 25% of primary intestinal EMZL {16556668;39624879} and is more common in EMZL secondarily involving the intestines among which the most common primary site is the stomach {16556668;39624879}. The IGH::MALT1 translocation appears common in hepatic EMZL based on small numbers of cases {12406890}. The translocation BCL10::MALT1 is infrequent in intestinal EMZL and rare in gastric EMZL {16556668}. IGH::FOXP1 is rare in the digestive tract. Extra copies of MALT1 and FOXP1, possibly representing partial or complete trisomies 18 and 3, are detected in about 25% and 17% of gastric EMZL {38418651} and in 25% and 75% of intestinal EMZL, respectively {16556668}.

    Targeted sequencing has shown mutated genes involved in epigenetic regulation, tumour suppression, and signalling pathways (e.g. B-cell receptor / NF-κB, NOTCH) {39054516;34494161}. Mutated ARID2 appears common in H. pylori–negative gastric EMZL and may contribute to lymphomagenesis {39054516}. However, the mutation spectrum of digestive tract EMZL awaits further confirmation and validation.

    Macroscopic appearance

    On endoscopy or gross examination, gastric and intestinal EMZLs take the form of superficial, ill-defined or discrete, multinodular or polypoid lesions; or deeply invasive lesions, with or without erosion or ulceration {36840791;39482577}. Lesions may be single or multiple.

    Histopathology

    Examination shows a proliferation of atypical lymphoid cells effacing tissue architecture. Neoplastic cells include variable proportions of small lymphocytes; marginal zone cells with oval to slightly irregular nuclei, smooth chromatin, and pale cytoplasm {9894477;10887100;7726367;31912792}; monocytoid cells with abundant pale cytoplasm; and scattered large cells. Plasma cells are often admixed, sometimes forming large aggregates; they are monotypic in one third of cases {39624879}. They may contain Dutcher bodies or Russell bodies. Large-cell transformation is diagnosed when there are sheets of large cells unassociated with follicular dendritic cell meshworks {26400898;27460179;31124124;31912792}. Lymphoma cells display confluent growth, and they often surround remnants of reactive follicles in a marginal zone pattern. The germinal centres can be infiltrated by the lymphoma cells (follicular colonization), potentially mimicking follicular lymphoma. Neoplastic cells infiltrate, expand, and distort epithelial structures, forming lymphoepithelial lesions in some anatomical sites. When EMZL involves regional lymph nodes, it often shows interfollicular expansion, marginal zone, or perisinusoidal growth patterns {31757436}.

    Immunohistochemistry

    Neoplastic cells express CD20, CD79a, and PAX5; usually IgM; occasionally IgG or IgA; and exceptionally IgD {31912792}. EMZLs are characteristically negative for CD10, BCL6, CD23, and cyclin D1 {31912792}. A small subset (< 4%) of EMZL expresses CD5, which may be associated with more aggressive behaviour {31912792;22406370}. MNDA is frequently positive in EMZL, but it is also expressed in a subset of other types of B-cell lymphoma {19474799;30346478}. Aberrant expression of CD43 (in 20–40%) {10191768} or TBX21 (T-bet) (in 40–89%) {23607257} and IG light chain restriction can help distinguish EMZL from reactive lymphoid infiltrates. CD21 highlights follicular dendritic cell meshworks associated with residual follicles. Colonized follicles typically have irregularly expanded follicular dendritic cell meshworks. Cytokeratin antibodies highlight lymphoepithelial lesions.

    IPSID/αHCD

    IPSID/αHCD is characterized by secretion of defective IG alpha heavy chain {15542584;2512818;29372346}, marked plasmacytic differentiation, and expression of alpha heavy chain without light chain. Deletion of most of the IGHV (VH) and all of the CH1 domains, but with intact C-terminal regions, results in expression of a truncated heavy chain that cannot bind light chains {2512818;15542584;29372346;35621691}. Early stages manifest as a dense, often extensive, lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate confined to the mucosa and/or submucosa. Small intestinal villous blunting may be seen. Transformation to DLBCL, usually with plasmacytic differentiation, occurs with advanced disease.

    Differential diagnosis

    Diffusely infiltrating CD20-positive B cells, effacement of architecture, monotypic plasma cells, and monoclonal IG gene rearrangements favour EMZL over chronic inflammatory infiltrates. Atypical marginal zone hyperplasia, a rare reactive lesion of children, presents with involvement of the appendix and few other sites {15256428}. It shows follicular hyperplasia, expanded marginal zones, and abundant B cells invading crypt epithelium or colonizing germinal centres. Lambda light chain restriction is common, but monoclonal IG gene rearrangement is not identified.

    Other mature B-cell lymphomas (e.g. follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma) can be excluded by a lack of CD10, BCL6, CD5, cyclin D1, SOX11, and LEF1 expression. EMZL with prominent follicular colonization may mimic follicular lymphoma; the presence of an appreciable extrafollicular component of B cells, plasmacytic differentiation, LELs, lack of extensive expression of follicle-centre markers within and outside follicles {33283568}, and absence of BCL2 translocation support EMZL over follicular lymphoma. Distinction between EMZL and lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma involving extramedullary, extranodal sites can be difficult. Bone marrow and lymph node involvement at presentation, high-titre IgM paraproteinaemia, and MYD88 NM_002468.5:c.794T>C p.(Leu265Pro) mutation favour the latter. EMZL with extensive plasma cell differentiation can mimic plasmacytoma, but the presence of CD20-positive B cells, as well as presentation in a typical MALT site, favour the former. Digestive tract involvement by plasma cell neoplasia is extremely rare in the absence of widespread plasma cell myeloma.

    Cytology

    Cytology may be helpful in the evaluation of hepatic or pancreaticobiliary lesions, but distinguishing EMZL from reactive lymphoid infiltrates may be difficult.

    Diagnostic molecular pathology

    Demonstration of monoclonal IG gene rearrangements or MALT1 translocation may help establish a diagnosis of lymphoma when the histopathological and immunophenotypic findings are suspicious but not diagnostic. With the exception of t(11;18)(q21;q21) (BIRC3::MALT1), commonly found in gastric EMZL, other EMZL-associated genetic changes are infrequent or of unclear diagnostic utility.

    Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

    Essential: a primary lymphoma arising in the digestive tract; proliferation of atypical small to medium-sized lymphoid cells mimicking reactive MALT and showing architectural distortion; expression of B-lineage markers; exclusion of other small B-cell neoplasms (follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, small lymphocytic lymphoma, lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma, and plasmacytoma).

    Desirable: demonstration of light chain restriction or clonal IG gene rearrangement; lymphoepithelial lesions; evidence of underlying inflammatory background.

    Staging

    Staging utilizes the Lugano classification, as adopted by the ninth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

    Prognosis and prediction

    EMZLs have an indolent clinical course; most patients have long survival {35139275;39624879}. Most available prognostic data pertain to gastric EMZL. Five-year overall survival ranging from 70% to > 95% {22893605;35139275;39375056} and 10-year disease-specific survival of > 90% {35139275;38418651} have been reported for patients with gastric EMZL. Survival rates for intestinal EMZL are slightly lower {22893605;33829216}. As noted above, H. pylori eradication results in complete remission in most patients with H. pylori–associated gastric EMZL depending on infiltration depth in the gastric wall {21890816;24363507;36840791;39482577}. Local treatment often results in prolonged disease-free intervals. BIRC3::MALT1 is associated with more widespread disease, impaired response of gastric EMZL to H. pylori eradication therapy, and with markedly decreased risk of high-grade transformation {11984515;16204012;26773441;31124124}. Extra copies of MALT1 are associated with progression or relapse and represent an adverse prognostic factor for event-free survival {38418651}. Early-stage IPSID/αHCD typically responds to antibiotics {34270561}. Regression of lymphoma has been reported in patients with HCV treated with antiviral therapy {36805885}. Large-cell transformation is associated with more aggressive behaviour {33764848}. Morphological criteria have been proposed for reporting lymphoma response after H. pylori eradication therapy and supporting clinical management decisions. Response levels are defined on the basis of the density and composition of lymphoid and plasmacytic infiltrates in the mucosa; the presence or absence of lymphoepithelial lesions; and stromal changes, including lamina propria cellularity and the extent of fibrosis {23043300}. Notably, this system does not incorporate immunohistochemical or molecular criteria (e.g. clonality assessment or translocation status). A clinical prognostic index – the MALT Lymphoma International Prognostic Index (MALT-IPI) – has been developed for stratifying patients into three prognostic groups (low, intermediate, and high risk) according to the presence of 0, 1, and 2–3 of the following parameters: age > 70 years, stage III/IV, and elevated LDH {28720586}; further studies are needed to determine its value for tailoring treatment decisions.
