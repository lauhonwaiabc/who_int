16.1.1.19 MAFA-related familial insulinomatosis (MAFA)

Definition

MAFA-related familial insulinomatosis is a syndrome with an autosomal dominant inheritance caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in MAFA characterized by adult-onset hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia.

MIM numbering

147630 Insulinomatosis and diabetes mellitus; INSDM

ICD-11 coding

2E92.9 Benign neoplasm of endocrine pancreas

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial insulinomatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The disorder is restricted to the endocrine pancreas where MAFA plays a critical role as a transcription factor regulating pancreatic beta (insulin) cell function and proliferation.

Clinical features

Reflecting the role of MAFA as a β-cell–specific transcription factor with both proliferative and functional properties, patients may present either with adult-onset hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia, which usually recurs, or maturity-onset diabetes of the young (MODY)-like diabetes mellitus, with the hypoglycaemic presentation being more common in female patients and the diabetic presentation in male patients {29339498}. While the two manifestations are generally mutually exclusive, 1 patient with diabetes went on to develop hyperinsulinism due to insulinomatosis {29339498}. Onset is in adulthood, which distinguishes it from congenital hyperinsulinism. Clues to the diagnosis in patients presenting with diabetes include a normal body mass index (BMI), no other clinical features of insulin resistance, no history of diabetic ketoacidosis, and negative islet autoantibodies {29339498}. Imaging identifies single or multicentric pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PanNETs) that measure > 10 mm {29339498;35406570;36952647}.

Epidemiology

This disorder has been described so far in three families {29339498;35406570} and is too rare to determine the actual incidence and prevalence. While the mutation is found equally in both sexes, hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia has been reported to be more frequent in females, whereas males in the same kindreds are more commonly affected by diabetes. The onset of symptoms is usually in the fourth decade, and there is no history to suggest congenital hyperinsulinism {29339498}.

Etiology

This disorder has been attributed to a specific germline variant, NM_201589.4:c.191C>T p.(Ser64Phe) in the gene encoding the transcription factor MAFA. Another report found a heterozygous missense variant, NM_201589.3:c.170C>G p.(Thr57Arg) in the same transactivation domain of MAFA, which can cause the same clinical manifestations {35406570}. The presence of a germline pathogenic variant in MAFA distinguishes this from sporadic non-familial insulinomatosis. The genetic background of sporadic cases is mostly unclear {29339498;34737724}, although germline non-pathogenic MAFA variants have been described {35041347;38264280}.

Pathogenesis

MAFA is a basic leucine zipper–containing transcription factor that regulates glucose-stimulated insulin secretion {17785922;24726898;33514698}. It also has oncogenic transformation potential in vitro. The p.Ser64Phe variant impairs phosphorylation in the MAFA transactivation domain, resulting in increased protein stability that is insensitive to glucose concentrations, thereby interrupting the regulatory action of MAFA. In addition, MAFA p.Ser64Phe exhibits enhanced transactivation compared with wildtype MAFA {29339498}.

Macroscopic appearance

The pancreas of patients with this disorder may show single or multiple tumours of > 10 mm in diameter.

Histopathology

The pancreas of patients contains normal islets intermingled with multicentric β cell proliferations ranging from dysplastic β (insulin) cell clusters and cords to typically trabecular insulin-positive microtumours (< 5 mm) and solitary macrotumours (> 5 mm) with low proliferation (Ki-67 < 2%) and often irregular outlines. While the islets contain insulin, glucagon, pancreatic polypeptide, and somatostatin cells in normal distribution and numbers, the dysplastic clusters and cords and the trabecular tumours are exclusively positive for insulin, which helps distinguish the condition from MEN1 and glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia {19011561;29339498} as well as adult non-neoplastic hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia {37279235}. The MAFA protein is expressed in both the neoplastic and normal beta cell nuclei. The insulin-positive tumours of patients with sporadic non-familial insulinomatosis are either trabecular or solid {34737724;35041347;38264280}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The missense MAFA sequence variants p.Ser64Phe and p.Thr57Arg have been shown to segregate with the manifestations of both hyperinsulinism and diabetes mellitus.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: evidence of multicentric trabecular islet cell proliferations staining exclusively for insulin; germline pathogenic variant in MAFA.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Medical therapy yields poor results with persistent hypoglycaemia {29339498}. Most patients have undergone partial pancreatectomy, but this has not led to durable cure, and several patients have undergone multiple surgical procedures. The multicentric nature of the β cell proliferations suggests that total pancreatectomy would be required for cure {33434149}. Local or distant metastases have not been documented.
