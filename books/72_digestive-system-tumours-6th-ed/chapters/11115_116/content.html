11.1.1.5 Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm of the pancreas

Definition

Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm (IOPN) is a non-invasive, grossly visible, epithelial neoplasm involving dilated pancreatic ducts and composed of exophytic papillae and nodules lined by epithelium with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.

ICD-O coding

84550/2 Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm

ICD-11 coding

2E61.Y & XA3QC5 & XH2BV2 Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm, NOS, of pancreas

Related terminology

Not recommended: oncocytic subtype of intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm of the pancreas.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately 55% of IOPNs occur in the head of the pancreas, and about one fifth diffusely involve the entire gland {8712298;25840549;27518461;38950698}.

Clinical features

IOPNs either are incidentally discovered (in one third of cases) or manifest with symptoms including abdominal pain and/or chronic pancreatitis {25840549;38950698}. Histological confirmation may be obtained through endoscopic biopsy or cytology {27518461;26415076}.

Epidemiology

IOPN is a rare tumour that represents 1–13% of all IPMNs and accounts for 4.5% of intraductal neoplasms of the pancreas. Reports of IOPNs remain rare, with < 200 cases published. IOPNs are slightly more prevalent in men (M:F ratio: 1.5:1) {38950698}. Patient age ranges from 24 to 86 years (mean: 58 years) {30986801;38950698}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

IOPNs often lack somatic genomic alterations reported in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and in intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs), such as mutations in KRAS, GNAS, and RNF43 {21547907;36209796;38950698}. Instead, IOPNs are characterized by rearrangements involving PRKACA or PRKACB, namely ATP1B1::PRKACA, DNAJB1::PRKACA, and ATP1B1::PRKACB {31676785;31678302;36152045;38378317}. These fusions, presenting in virtually all IOPNs, may play a role in the oncocytic morphology {37871652;38841868}. Genes including ARHGAP26, ASXL1, EPHA8, and ERBB4 have been reported as somatically mutated in some IOPNs {27282351}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, IOPNs are typically large (average size: 45 mm), tan-brown, friable papillary projections or solid nodules within dilated pancreatic ducts, with minimal intraductal mucin accumulation {8712298;38950698}. In some cases, a clear connection of the cysts to the ductal system may not be apparent radiologically and/or grossly {38950698}.

Histopathology

The intraductal growth may be challenging to recognize, but at least focal involvement of the ductal system can be demonstrated {8712298;30986801;38950698}. The tumours are composed of complex and arborizing papillae with delicate fibrovascular cores. The papillae are lined by stratified cuboidal to columnar epithelial cells (2–5 layers) with mitochondria-rich eosinophilic granular cytoplasm and large round nuclei with single prominent nucleoli. These cells form cribriform structures, with intracellular lumina many of which contain mucin. Fusion of adjacent papillae epithelium producing a solid growth pattern may occur. Interspersed goblet cells are common. Mitoses are frequent. Interestingly, a recent study reported that rare cases may present with simple papillae or even relatively flat epithelium, yet they still harbour the characteristic PRKACA or PRKACB fusion genes {38841868}.

Based on both the architectural complexity and the degree of nuclear atypia, essentially all IOPNs have high-grade dysplasia {8712298;38950698}.

Associated invasive carcinoma occurs in 50% of IOPNs and is typically limited in extent {8712298;25840549;30986801;38950698}. The invasive component is primarily composed of small infiltrating tubules or solid nests of oncocytic cells. In rare cases, the invasive component has abundant stromal mucin (similar to colloid carcinoma); however, the neoplastic cells floating in stromal mucin still have oncocytic cytomorphology {8712298;30986801;38950698}. It is important to note that non-invasive IOPNs can extend in the duct system to involve smaller ductules creating a pseudoinvasive appearance. Abrupt transition from a normal epithelium to an oncocytic epithelium, within the same duct, suggests pseudoinvasion. Tangential sectioning of the branching intraductal neoplasm may also mimic tissue invasion {30986801}.

Immunohistochemically, IOPNs diffusely label with antibodies to MUC1 and MUC6, whereas MUC2 and MUC5AC expression is predominantly restricted to goblet cells {20139757;27591765;38950698}. Additionally, there is consistent immunolabelling with HepPar1; however, in situ hybridization for albumin, a more specific marker of hepatocellular differentiation, is negative {27591765}. Other useful immunohistochemical markers in supporting the diagnosis of IOPNs are mesothelin {27591765} and KIT (CD117) {32806726}.

IOPNs should be distinguished from IPMNs, particularly the pancreatobiliary subtype. The histological hallmarks of IOPNs include the complex cribriform architecture of the papillae with oncocytic cells and distinctive intraepithelial lumina {8712298;38950698}. IOPNs with a predominantly solid growth pattern may resemble acinar cell carcinoma or well-differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET). Immunohistochemistry, including BCL10 and trypsin for acinar differentiation and chromogranin and synaptophysin for neuroendocrine differentiation, is helpful in these distinctions. It should be noted that when histologically typing intraductal neoplasms on the basis of the papillary components, mixed lesions (e.g. intestinal-type or pancreatobiliary-type papillae admixed with oncocytic papillae) can occur, and the background pancreatic ducts or cystic components may exhibit gastric differentiation. Therefore, the diagnosis of IOPN should be reserved for lesions that demonstrate all its histological hallmarks. The term “atypical” should be avoided, at least until the classification criteria and clinical-pathological implications are more clearly defined.

Cytology

The unique cytological features of IOPN include sheets or papillary clusters of oncocytic cells with abundant granular cytoplasm, as well as well-defined cell borders, large central nuclei, prominent eccentric nucleoli, and intercellular punched-out spaces [[International Academy of Cytology – International Agency for Research on Cancer – World Health Organization Joint Editorial Board. WHO Reporting System for Pancreaticobiliary Cytopathology. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (IAC-IARC-WHO cytopathology reporting systems series, 1st ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int/621. Bookref]]. IOPNs produce significantly less intracellular mucin and have minimal extracellular colloid-like mucin – features typically reported in IPMNs {17312458;26415076}.

IOPNs have high-grade dysplasia by virtue of their architectural complexity and/or high-grade epithelial atypia {23939829;26415076}. However, the high-grade dysplasia observed in these tumours does not necessarily indicate the presence of an associated invasive carcinoma {25840549;30986801}.

The cellularity and complex architecture of IOPNs, combined with the paucity of mucin, may result in their misdiagnosis as a pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma on cytological specimens {26415076}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

IOPNs are characterized by rearrangements involving PRKACA or PRKACB, including ATP1B1::PRKACA, DNAJB1::PRKACA, and ATP1B1::PRKACB {31676785;31678302;36152045;38378317}. Identifying these rearrangements in cyst fluid, biopsy samples, or surgical resections supports the diagnosis of IOPN and is valuable in diagnostically challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a non-invasive epithelial neoplasm with a (flat to) papillary growth pattern and oncocytic cytoplasm; located within a dilated duct.

Desirable: immunohistochemical labelling for MUC1, MUC5AC, MUC6, and HepPar1; molecular testing for PRKACA and PRKACB fusion genes may be useful in selected cases (e.g. small biopsies).

Staging

Non-invasive IOPNs are staged as Tis. Staging of an invasive carcinoma arising in association with an IOPN is based on the invasive component and follows the protocol for pancreatic carcinomas {26832882;25775066;27163957;37604731} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

Although IOPNs are associated with invasive carcinoma in about 50% of cases, the 5-year disease-specific survival rate for patients with a surgically resected IOPN approaches 100% {30986801;38950698}. Local recurrences, which occur in up to 45% of cases and may arise > 10 years after the initial pancreatectomy, can often be treated with additional resection {8712298;25840549;30986801}.
