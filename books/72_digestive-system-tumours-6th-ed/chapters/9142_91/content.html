9.1.4.2 Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, large duct

Definition

Large duct intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (LD-iCCA) is a malignant epithelial neoplasm with cholangiocytic differentiation of presumed intrahepatic large duct origin. It is typically a gland-forming adenocarcinoma with mucin production and abundant desmoplastic stroma. LD-iCCA is biologically similar to perihilar cholangiocarcinoma (pCCA) but distinct from small duct intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (SD-iCCA).

Related terminology

Acceptable: intrahepatic bile duct carcinoma, large duct type.

Not recommended: perihilar cholangiocarcinoma; cholangiocellular carcinoma; intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, bile duct type; intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, perihilar type.

Subtype(s)

Adenosquamous carcinoma (85600/3); mucinous/colloid carcinoma (84800/3); mucoepidermoid carcinoma (84300/3); enteroblastic carcinoma (85762/3)

Localization

In most cases, LD-iCCA arises in the central parts of the liver, typically around second branches of the biliary tree (segmental bile ducts) {30170977}. In cases where a tumour affects both perihilar and intrahepatic large ducts, the distinction between pCCA and LD-iCCA should be made on the basis of predominant tumour localization.

Clinical features

Nonspecific symptoms (e.g. abdominal pain, weight loss) are common. Obstructive jaundice is rare, unless the tumour is at an advanced stage, involves perihilar ducts, or is accompanied by cholangitis {24406866}.

Imaging abnormalities include an irregular mass, area of periductal soft tissue density, bile duct stricture, and upstream duct dilatation {30560357}. The early diagnosis of LD-iCCA is challenging, particularly in patients with chronic cholangiopathy.

Serum CA19-9 and CEA are often elevated, and their concentrations in cases of LD-iCCA tend to be higher than those in cases of SD-iCCA {27259014}.

Epidemiology

Epidemiological data specific for LD-iCCA are limited. Patients are typically aged > 50 years, with a slight male predominance. As liver flukes, primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), and hepatolithiasis are predisposing diseases for LD-iCCA, its relative incidence correlates with the distribution of these diseases and also with that of extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. The proportion of LD-iCCAs among cases of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma varies between geographical regions and different studies. Reported proportions are about 10–30% in the Western Hemisphere {30001234;36137398}. In contrast, LD-iCCA accounts for 45% of surgical cases of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma in eastern Asia {28338651;27259014}, and that proportion is even higher if liver flukes or hepatolithiasis are present {31735647;24406866}.

Etiology

Chronic cholangiopathies such as liver fluke infection, PSC, and hepatolithiasis increase the risk of LD-iCCA {35697170} (see #52263Box 9.06). Other potential risk factors include cholangitis, cholelithiasis, choledocholithiasis, diabetes mellitus, inflammatory bowel disease, and smoking {17689296}. Other hazards include thorium-232 and its decay products, and 1,2-dichloropropane {24553624;23493378}. However, the etiology is undefined in most cases of LD-iCCA.

Pathogenesis

LD-iCCA develops through a multistep carcinogenic process {35697170;17592273}. Gene mutations accumulate in cholangiocytes of the surface epithelium or peribiliary glands, which leads to increasing cytoarchitectural atypia of premalignant dysplastic lesions. The most common premalignant lesion is biliary intraepithelial neoplasia (BilIN), followed by intraductal papillary neoplasm (IPNB), intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm, mucinous cystic neoplasm, and intraductal tubulopapillary neoplasm. Please refer to the specific sections on those entities for descriptions of their distinct pathological and molecular features. Although some cases of LD-iCCA may arise directly from non-dysplastic cholangiocytes, the frequency and pathological features of the de novo cases are unknown.

Some relevant differences exist between LD-iCCA and SD-iCCA. LD-iCCA shares molecular abnormalities with pCCA. Commonly mutated genes include TP53 (30%), KRAS (30%), and SMAD4 (20%) {28338651;27259014;24406866}. MDM2amplification is observed in 15% of cases {29309301}. Mutations in GNAS are less common (< 5%) but are sometimes identified in cholangiocarcinoma cases associated with fluke infestation (9%) or IPNB (11%) {22561520;32100878}. Except for TP53, alterations of those genes are markedly less common in SD-iCCA. Conversely, molecular abnormalities characteristic for SD-iCCA (e.g. IDH1/2, FGFR2, BAP1) are essentially undetectable in LD-iCCA {28338651;27259014;24406866}.

Other potentially altered genes include CDKN2A, ARID1A, BRAF, MYC, and PIK3CA; however, the subtype specificity of these molecular alterations remains to be clarified {26258846}.

Macroscopic appearance

LD-iCCA forms a solid, fibrotic tumour involving both the liver parenchyma and large portal tracts with ill-defined margins and may have small foci of necrosis. The gross appearance of LD-iCCA is mass-forming (30%), periductal-infiltrating (15%), or a combination of the two (50%) {28338651}. Uncommonly (5%), LD-iCCA, particularly when arising in association with an IPNB, intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm, or intraductal tubulopapillary neoplasm, has an intraductal-growth appearance. Although the carcinoma may invade into the perihilar bile ducts, the tumour is predominantly located within the liver.

Histopathology

Histological findings

In well-differentiated to moderately differentiated cases, cancer cells are arranged in large irregular glands, fused glands, or solid nests {28338651;27259014;24406866;30001234}. The tall columnar cancer cells have variable degrees of nuclear atypia, cellular distortion, and pleomorphism. Mucus production is common; mucin can be observed within the glandular structures or the cytoplasm of cancer cells. The stroma is highly desmoplastic with activated mesenchymal cells and an inflammatory infiltrate.

In poorly differentiated cases, pleomorphic cancer cells are arranged in small nests or single cells with abundant desmoplastic stroma. Bizarre signet-ring or spindle-shaped cells can be observed. Mucus stains (e.g. mucicarmine) assist in identification of focal mucin production.

Tumours show poorly circumscribed, infiltrative growth in the liver parenchyma and portal tracts. In addition to periductal infiltration, BilIN is observed in the adjacent large ducts in 30% of cases {28338651}. Necrosis, lymphovascular infiltration, and perineural infiltration are common.

Although LD-iCCA is typically a pancreatobiliary-type adenocarcinoma, some cases, more frequently those associated with chronic cholangiopathy, show intestinal-type features such as nuclear stratification, cribriform glands, goblet cells, and comedo-type necrosis {22175890}. Those cases are often associated with IPNB of intestinal type and can show mucus accumulation (mucin lakes), resembling colloid carcinomas of the pancreas that arise in association with intestinal-type intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms {15223952;31925805}. Intestinal-type BilIN or intestinal metaplasia may be observed in the adjacent large ducts {22175890}.

Histological findings that favour a diagnosis of LD-iCCA over SD-iCCA include (1) formation of large glands, (2) tall columnar cancer cells, (3) intracellular or extracellular mucin, and (4) association with precursor lesion (e.g. BilIN, IPNB) {28338651;27259014;24406866;30001234}. In LD-iCCA, tubular components resembling SD-iCCA can be observed at the invasive front, particularly at the interface between the tumour and liver parenchyma {28338651}. Therefore, iCCA should be subtyped on the basis of histological findings of central parts. In some cases, it may be very challenging to distinguish LD-iCCA from SD-iCCA on purely morphological grounds, and immunohistochemistry and molecular analysis may be helpful.

Immunohistochemistry

CCA usually expresses CK7, CK19, EMA, and CA19-9 irrespective of the anatomical localization or subtype. Intestinal-type LD-iCCA can be positive for CK20, CDX2, and MUC2 {22175890}. There are several markers useful for iCCA subtyping (see #54172Table 9.17). HNF1β is commonly expressed in iCCA, but its subtype specificity remains to be clarified {34086854}. The immunohistochemical marker expression of LD-iCCA is similar to that of pCCA {28338651}, so immunohistochemical labelling cannot be used to distinguish between them.

Histological subtypes

Adenosquamous carcinoma: This subtype accounts for 2–3% of iCCAs {2844646}. Elements of squamous cell carcinoma are irregularly intermixed with adenocarcinoma, and the squamous component should arbitrarily account for ≥ 30% of the neoplasm for the diagnosis. Amounts and degrees of squamous differentiation vary among cases. Squamous differentiation is confirmed by keratinization or intercellular bridges. Immunoreactivity for p63 and p40 assists in identification of squamous differentiation. Necrosis and cystic degeneration are often observed.

Colloid (mucinous) carcinoma: This uncommon subtype is characterized by neoplastic cells floating in large amounts of extracellular mucus. Mucinous components should be predominant (usually ≥ 80% of the tumour) for the tumour to qualify as this subtype. Colloid carcinoma is frequently associated with IPNB of intestinal type or underlying chronic cholangiopathy {17058219;22175890}. Most cases have intestinal-type morphological features as well as immunophenotype (e.g. CDX2, MUC2) {16360234}.

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma: This rare subtype potentially originating from peribiliary glands comprises an irregular mixture of mucous, epidermoid, and intermediate cells {35697170}. The CRTC1::MAML2 gene fusion has been identified in mucoepidermoid carcinomas of many other organs, and fusion-positive cases typically show an indolent clinical course {12539049;16818685}. A single case of mucoepidermoid iCCA with a confirmed CRTC1::MAML2 fusion is available in the literature, and that patient was alive for 10 years after surgery {31351495}; however, many cases are not tested for this fusion. Fusion-negative cases, which typically show an aggressive clinical course, are probably better classified as adenosquamous carcinoma.

Enteroblastic carcinoma: This subtype is extremely rare with only two intrahepatic cases having been reported, both of which had a history of PSC {38301963}. It more frequently occurs in the extrahepatic bile duct {35472251}. Intrahepatic enteroblastic carcinoma may be mistaken for hepatocellular carcinoma clinically because of serum AFP elevation. Histologically it is a tubular or papillary adenocarcinoma characterized by clear cytoplasm, subnuclear or supranuclear vacuoles, central nuclei, and blastic coarse chromatin. Hepatoid trabecular architectures may coexist, and the fibrous stroma is relatively scanty. SALL4, HepPar1, arginase-1 (ARG1), glypican-3 (GPC3), and AFP are often expressed {38301963}.

 

Cytology

Samples are obtained by collection of bile, FNA of the hepatic mass, or brushing of the large bile duct. Aspiration of an enlarged lymph node may be considered for staging purposes. Cytological examination is not reliable for subclassification of iCCA.

Cytological abnormalities in well-differentiated cases are relatively mild. Cuboidal or columnar epithelial cells are arranged in sheets or large nests; nuclei are partly crowded or piled up and have an irregular nuclear membrane or loss of nuclear polarity {15952192}. In moderately to poorly differentiated cases, dysplastic cells are arranged in three-dimensional clusters, discohesive small nests, or single cells; nuclei are pleomorphic with coarse chromatin, irregular nuclear membrane, and potential presence of bizarre cells; and a necrotic background is common {15952192}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No molecular test is necessary for the diagnosis of LD-iCCA.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: adenocarcinoma with cholangiocytic differentiation and large duct–type histological features, lacking small duct–type characteristics.

Desirable: presence of precursor lesion (e.g. BilIN, IPNB, intraductal tubulopapillary neoplasm, intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm, mucinous cystic neoplasm) in the intrahepatic large duct; immunophenotype consistent with large duct type in cases with equivocal histomorphology.

Staging

LD-iCCA is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]]. The same staging system is currently used for both SD-iCCA and LD-iCCA.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis depends on stage and resectability of the tumour. In resectable cases, the 5-year postoperative survival in LD-iCCA is approximately 20%, which is worse than that for SD-iCCA (60%) and is similar to that for pCCA {28338651;27259014;24406866}. Unresectable cases are treated with systemic chemotherapy, and the prognosis is worse.

Histological subtypes associated with a worse prognosis are adenosquamous and enteroblastic carcinoma, while cases of colloid or intestinal-type carcinoma have a better prognosis {15942444;17058219;22175890}.

ERBB2 (HER2) amplification is observed in 15% of LD-iCCA or pCCA cases, which is somewhat higher than that in SD-iCCA (< 5%) {38709907}. Alterations in other drug target genes (e.g. NTRK genes, BRAF, ROS1, MET, and RET) are rarely identified in LD-iCCA. A global molecular analysis for these drug targets may be valuable in cases where standard treatments have been exhausted.
