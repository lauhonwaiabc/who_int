12.1.3.5 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) NOS is a diffuse neoplasm of large B lymphocytes lacking features of other defined types of large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL).

ICD-O coding

96800/3 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2A81.Z & XA7MC7 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS, of stomach

2A81.Z & XA4F42 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS, of ileocaecal junction

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

DLBCL of the GI tract commonly involves the stomach and ileocaecal region. The liver and distal colon are uncommonly involved.

Clinical features

Pain, bleeding, and diarrhoea or signs of obstruction can occur. Hepatomegaly or hepatic failure are rare manifestations of liver DLBCL. Patients may present with constitutional symptoms (fever, weight loss, or night sweats), or they may be asymptomatic, with a mass detected by screening procedures {12668970;15718146;3840404}.

Epidemiology

The GI tract is the most frequent site of involvement by extranodal lymphomas, accounting for 30–40% of all cases {2767109}. DLBCL is the most common lymphoma involving the GI tract (55–70% of all lymphomas) {11559724}. The incidence in the USA is around 7.2 cases per 100 000 person-years {38242772}. A slight male predominance is observed. It mostly affects patients aged 50–60 years {37944240;34627052}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown for many patients and is probably multifactorial. Chronic antigenic stimulation (e.g. HCV infection), immune dysregulation (e.g. autoimmune diseases), and immunodeficiency are risk factors for developing DLBCL.

Pathogenesis

The mutation landscape of DLBCL is fairly well defined, although few studies have focused specifically on gastrointestinal tumours. DLBCL can be divided into germinal-centre B-cell (GCB) and activated B-cell (ABC) cell-of-origin (COO) subtypes. Their mutation profiles overlap, but some mutations are preferentially associated with each subtype {28985567;29666115}. More granular molecular subgroups with different biological and prognostic significance have been defined {29641966;29713087;32187361;32289277} but are not yet being used for therapeutic decisions. Rearrangements of BCL6 and MYC occur in 25% and 10% of DLBCL, respectively, the former occurring more commonly in ABC DLBCL {17625604}. IGH::BCL2 rearrangement occurs almost exclusively in GCB-DLBCL {16418494}. Cases with MYC and BCL2 translocations (with or without additional BCL6 rearrangement) are classified as a separate entity. Cases with MYC and BCL6 (without BCL2 rearrangement) rearrangements now represent a genetic subgroup of DLBCL NOS or high-grade B-cell lymphoma NOS (based on morphology). Mutations of genes involved in epigenetic modification, NF-κB and B-cell receptor signalling, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, immunosurveillance, differentiation (BCL6, PRDM1), and TP53 are common {29666115;28985567}. One study from China showed that gastrointestinal DLBCL commonly carries mutations involving IGLL5, TP53, BTG, P2RY8, and PCLO, and that MYD88, EZH2, BCL2, and CD79B are uncommonly mutated {36243813}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gastrointestinal DLBCL is usually a solitary tumour with ulceration and transmural infiltration {12685544;8470758}. DLBCL of the liver may present as a large solitary mass or multiple masses {2029991;12668970;1618060;3840404;37685305}.

Histopathology

DLBCL NOS is composed of sheets of large cells resembling centroblasts (~85%), immunoblasts (up to 10%), or can be anaplastic (3–4%). DLBCL in the GI tract can be associated with a low-grade B-cell lymphoma, either follicular lymphoma or marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue.

The lymphoma cells are positive for pan B-cell markers (CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a, PAX5, BOB1 or OCT2). CD5 may be coexpressed in 5–10% of cases. Surface and/or cytoplasmic immunoglobulin is expressed in a subset of cases. GCB-DLBCLs are more often positive for CD10, BCL6, GCET1, HGAL, and LMO2. ABC DLBCL are generally strongly positive for IRF4 (MUM1) and FOXP1. BCL2 is frequently expressed in DLBCL {16418494}. Most DLBCL cases have a high proliferation (Ki-67) index, virtually always > 40% and sometimes reaching 70–80% or higher.

Cytology

Cytology is not relevant in most GI tract tumours; however, FNAB can be helpful in assessing liver tumours. Core needle or excisional biopsy is recommended whenever possible.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

COO testing is required either by mRNA (gene expression) profiling or by immunohistochemistry {24398326}. Molecular testing may be required to exclude other specific entities of large B-cell lymphoma such as DLBCL / high-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 rearrangements, large B-cell lymphoma with IRF4 rearrangement, and EBV-positive DLBCL.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffuse infiltrate of atypical large lymphoid cells; immunoreactivity for B-lineage markers; exclusion of other specific entities of large B-cell lymphoma.

Desirable: COO subtype; reporting of isolated MYC or dual MYC and BCL6 rearrangements.

Staging

Lymphoid neoplasms are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the ninth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

DLBCL of the GI tract generally tends to be localized and, hence, is prognostically more favourable than nodal DLBCL {39324011}. Lugano stage predicts prognosis {39211552}. The International Prognostic Index (IPI) is a robust prognosticator. In general, ABC DLBCL has a poorer prognosis than GCB DLBCL. More refined molecular subtyping of DLBCL {29641966;29713087;32187361;32289277} and stromal signatures have prognostic power, both within and independent of COO subtypes {19038878}. Other markers predicting survival include MYC translocation {26541272}; expression of CD5 {21199885}, CD30 {23343832}, and LMO2 {18086797}; and expression of MYC and BCL2 (double expresser) {22851565;24506200}. TP53 mutation {22955915} and CDKN2A deletion, particularly in combination with trisomy 3 {18765795}, are associated with poorer survival.
