12.1.3.4 Mantle cell lymphoma

Definition

Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) is a mature B-cell neoplasm derived from the mantle zone of lymphoid follicles and typically composed of small to medium-sized monomorphic cells positive for CD5, SOX11, and cyclin D1. It is associated with CCND-family rearrangements, usually CCND1.

Related terminology

Not recommended: centrocytic lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

Molecular subtypes

Cyclin D1–positive mantle cell lymphoma (96731/3); cyclin D1–negative mantle cell lymphoma (96732/3)

Morphological subtypes

Blastoid mantle cell lymphoma; pleomorphic mantle cell lymphoma; small cell mantle cell lymphoma; marginal zone–like mantle cell lymphoma

Localization

MCL frequently involves the GI tract; as many as 80–90% of patients have subclinical disease {12548600;17001159;32072136}. Primary GI tract MCL is rare (< 5% of patients) {33414416}. Colon is the most common site, followed by stomach and small intestine {35475204}.

Clinical features

Patients with MCL often present with widespread lymphadenopathy and involvement of spleen, liver, and bone marrow {16173960;34114641}. Patients with GI tract involvement are often asymptomatic with disease detected by (screening) endoscopy. About 10–20% of patients with GI tract disease can present with bleeding, diarrhoea, or rarely GI tract obstruction {12548600;17001159;33414416}.

Epidemiology

MCL accounts for 2–10% of all lymphoid neoplasms worldwide {25174028;24763513}. The incidence of MCL is 1.1 cases per 100 000 person-years in men and 0.26 in women in Europe {24763513}, and 0.13 cases per 100 000 person-years for both sexes in the Republic of Korea {28361523}. The median age at diagnosis is the sixth or seventh decade of life, and MCL is more common in men than in women (> 3:1) {25174028;24763513}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

t(11;14)(q13;q32) (IGH::CCND1) is present in ≥ 90% of cases {32584970;34114641}. Occasionally, IGK or IGL is the CCND1 translocation partner. CCND1 rearrangement results in overexpression of cyclin D1, which activates the cyclin D1–dependent kinase pathway {27582569;28466437}. A small subset of MCL cases lack CCND1 rearrangements and cyclin D1 expression; these cases often carry CCND2 or rarely CCND3 rearrangements {23255553}.

Secondary genomic alterations are present in > 90% of cases, and MCL displays a complex mutation landscape {32598477;32584970;34114641;39571691}. Secondary copy-number alterations and mutations affect key signalling pathways in MCL pathogenesis, including cell-cycle regulation (CCND1, RB1), DNA damage response (ATM, TP53), cell proliferation and apoptosis (BCL2, MYC), B-cell receptor / NF-κB signalling (CARD11/BIRC3/MAP3K14), and epigenetic modifiers (KMT2D, NSD2, SMARCA4). ATM is frequently mutated (40–60% of cases).

Expression of homing receptor α4β7 by MCL cells has been associated with GI tract involvement {9846960}. Specific genetic characteristics associated with GI tract involvement are unknown.

Macroscopic appearance

The GI tract may be involved by superficial ulcers, large tumours, or diffuse thickening of mucosa {17001159;33414416}. In some patients, gross lesions may not be identified {12548600}. Presentation as multiple intestinal polyps is distinctive but not specific {16241994;20206107}.

Histopathology

Morphology

Classic MCL is a monomorphic proliferation of small to medium-sized cells with scant cytoplasm, irregular nuclear contours, condensed chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli. The neoplasm may exhibit mantle zone (< 5%), nodular, or diffuse growth patterns {26926679;34114641}. Small cell subtype is characterized by small round cells that overlap with chronic lymphocytic leukaemia. Blastoid subtype is characterized by intermediate-sized cells with finely dispersed chromatin mimicking lymphoblasts. Pleomorphic subtype shows large cells with irregular nuclei and nucleoli overlapping with large B-cell lymphoma. Mitotic figures are increased in the blastoid and pleomorphic subtypes.

In situ mantle cell neoplasm is characterized by the presence of cyclin D1–positive cells restricted to mantle zones of otherwise reactive follicles. When found incidentally, progression to overt MCL is uncommon {22790016}.

Immunophenotype

MCL cells are positive for pan B-cell markers and are negative for CD3 {34114641;34767860}. Nuclear cyclin D1 is expressed in > 95% of MCL cases, and SOX11 is usually positive. MCL cells are usually CD5 positive, often CD43 positive, and usually negative for CD10, CD23, CD200, BCL6, and LEF1 {34114641}. Blastoid and pleomorphic cases can uncommonly express CD10 and/or BCL6.

Differential diagnosis

In the GI tract, the differential diagnosis includes extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma), the most prevalent small B-cell lymphoma at these sites; duodenal-type follicular lymphoma; and systemic involvement by small to medium-sized B-cell lymphomas. Pleomorphic and blastoid subtypes of MCL must be distinguished from large B-cell lymphoma (particularly cyclin D1–positive), B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, and Burkitt lymphoma {22642745;39313500;33528622}. Immunohistochemistry for SOX11 and molecular data are helpful in differential diagnosis.

Cytology

FNAB combined with immunophenotypic analysis can be helpful to detect MCL in the liver or spleen, and it is helpful in the setting of relapsed/residual disease.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In a neoplasm with appropriate morphological and immunophenotypic findings, uniform cyclin D1 expression is sufficient to confirm the diagnosis. In cases of cyclin D1–negative MCL, demonstration of SOX11 expression and presence of CCND2 rearrangement are helpful to support the diagnosis. MYC rearrangement and/or TP53 mutation are associated with disease progression {29196411;32273477;38748869}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Cyclin D1–positive MCL

Essential: lymphoma cells of B lineage (positive for CD19 or CD20 and usually CD5); monomorphic, centrocyte-like cells or (less often) variant cytology of subtypes (blastoid, pleomorphic, etc.); cyclin D1 positivity and/or CCND1 rearrangement.

Desirable: SOX11 expression.

Cyclin D1–negative MCL

Essential: lymphoma cells of B lineage (positive for CD19 or CD20 and usually CD5); monomorphic, centrocyte-like cells or (less often) variant cytology of subtypes (blastoid, pleomorphic, etc.); immunophenotype consistent with MCL with SOX11 expression; absence of cyclin D1 expression or CCND1 rearrangement.

Desirable: CCND2 rearrangement.

Staging

Lymphoid neoplasms are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the ninth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

Median overall survival time of patients with MCL is 5–10 years. Patients who present with isolated or predominantly extranodal manifestations have a more favourable outcome than patients with nodal MCL {32072136}. Prognostic biomarkers have been established for patients with conventional MCL. Cytomorphology (classic vs blastoid/pleomorphic), Ki-67 (> 30%) and p53 staining (> 50%), and/or TP53 mutation help define a high-risk MCL cohort {26926679;29196411;38104096;39284897}. The MCL International Prognostic Index (MIPI) {24687837} defines risk groups but currently does not govern treatment approaches for individual patients. Treatment can vary from watchful waiting to immunochemotherapy dependent on clinical and tumour risk factors {30132080;38104096;38748869}.
