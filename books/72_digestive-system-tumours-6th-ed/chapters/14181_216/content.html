14.1.8.1 Paragangliomas

Definition

Paragangliomas are neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) of non-epithelial (neuronal) lineage.

ICD-O coding

86930/3 Extra-adrenal paraganglioma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C1Z & XH1UN6 Extra-adrenal paraganglioma of digestive organs

Related terminology

Acceptable: extra-adrenal paraganglioma.

Not recommended: extra-adrenal phaeochromocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Gastrointestinal paragangliomas are often sympathetic paragangliomas, although parasympathetic tumours have also been reported. Paragangliomas can involve all parts of the GI tract including the oesophagus, stomach, small bowel, liver, pancreas, bile ducts, and colorectum. Pancreatic paragangliomas most commonly involve the pancreatic head {34712035}, while gastric paragangliomas most often involve the fundus {38628664}.

Clinical features

Most paragangliomas are sympathetic and functional, so patients may present with signs and symptoms of excess catecholamines. Although some patients may be asymptomatic {33479747}, the tumours are usually biochemically functional and amenable to biochemical testing {29332973;33479747}. However, many paragangliomas are identified incidentally during imaging studies or screening patients with susceptibility syndromes {33479747}. Paragangliomas with SDHB mutations tend to be non-secretory or produce dopamine only.

As most gastrointestinal paragangliomas are not suspected clinically but are incidental findings or manifest with vague abdominal pain, the diagnosis is usually not made preoperatively. In oesophageal paragangliomas, intermittent hematemesis, dysphagia, and retrosternal pain were reported {15230739;37321914}. Gastric paragangliomas often present with epigastric or abdominal pain, and (less often) hematemesis, melena, hypertension, or palpable mass {38628664}. Preoperative diagnosis of pancreatic paraganglioma is < 20%, some being functional {34712035;36387819;34621864}. Extrahepatic biliary tract paragangliomas have caused obstructive jaundice {10918729} and abdominal pain {30300790}. In a review of 21 gallbladder paragangliomas, 11 presented with pain, 3 with cholelithiasis, 1 with haematemesis, 1 with mildly elevated transaminases, 3 were incidental radiographic findings, and 2 had known multifocal paragangliomas {36994263}. None were described as secretory, but most were identified after cholecystectomy. Many hepatic paragangliomas are functional {36555899}. Colorectal paragangliomas are associated with abdominal pain, hypertension {36017518}, and lower intestinal bleeding {8756854;24307812}.

Imaging

Paragangliomas with pseudohypoxic pathogenesis express SSTR and are visualized with 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT. Tumours with kinase pathway abnormalities can be imaged with 18F-DOPA PET/CT or iobenguane {33247927;23162105}.

Epidemiology

Paragangliomas are rare tumours, with an estimated incidence of 0.18–0.5 cases per 100 000 person-years {29361475;32207276}. In the medical literature, approximately 50 pancreatic paragangliomas have been reported in patients aged 23–75 years {34712035;36387819;34621864}; around 25 gallbladder paragangliomas in individuals aged 36–79 years (with a female predominance; M:F ratio: 1:2) {36994263;34845133}, 11 cases of gastric paragangliomas in adults aged 29–85 years (5 men, 6 women) {38628664}, and approximately 20 hepatic paragangliomas in adults {36555899;36868583;35106168;35809684;26407551;23894692;21761020}. There are only a few reports of paragangliomas of the oesophagus, duodenum, extrahepatic biliary tract, colon, and rectum.

Etiology

Risk factors

There are no risk factors for paragangliomas other than genetic risk factors.

Genetics

Overall, 30–40% of paragangliomas in adults, and a higher percentage in children, occur in the context of hereditary disease, and genetic testing facilitates risk reduction {23913591;24899893}. Younger age, multiple tumours, and extra-adrenal tumours are often associated with a germline mutation {12000816}. Germline pathogenic variants were found in 78% and 59% of patients in French and Italian series, with most involving SDHB or VHL {32432211;32491270}.

Alterations of ≥ 23 genes are responsible for tumour development, of which at least 17 are hereditary susceptibility genes, including RET, NF1, VHL, TMEM127, SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD, SDHAF2, FH, MAX, EPAS1, DLST, MDH2, GOT2, SLC25A11, and DNMT3A {33059362}. NF1, RET, and VHL are associated with hereditary and sporadic tumours. SDH-related tumours are generally hereditary {33059362}.

Little data exist for gastrointestinal paragangliomas and remain limited to anecdotal descriptions. One gastric paraganglioma each carried MEN1, EPAS1, and MDH2 variants {37321914}. An SDHD mutated gallbladder paraganglioma occurred in an individual with multifocal paragangliomas {31057686}. A hepatic paraganglioma occurred in a patient with multiple head and neck paragangliomas, but genetic testing was not reported {26407551}. A duodenal paraganglioma occurred in an individual with multiple paragangliomas and germline SDHB mutation {36704041}. A primary hepatic paraganglioma occurred in a patient with multifocal gastrointestinal stromal tumours, with an incomplete Carney triad–type presentation {23894692}. A sigmoid paraganglioma showed loss of SDHB immunostaining in a patient with a gastric gastrointestinal stromal tumour {34853215}. A sigmoid paraganglioma had SDHB and ATRX genetic variants {36017518}.

Pathogenesis

Little is known about the pathogenesis of gastrointestinal paragangliomas. Most alterations in paragangliomas overall cluster either to the hypoxia signalling pathway (cluster 1, involving SDH genes, VHL, EGLN2 [PHD1], EGLN1 [PHD2], EPAS1 [HIF2A]) or to the kinase signalling pathway (cluster 2; including RET, TMEM127, HRAS, and NF1) {28162975;25385035}.

Macroscopic appearance

Paragangliomas are usually well-circumscribed tumours. Care should be taken during gross and microscopic examination to ascertain their primary location, as many clinically thought to arise in the GI tract may have originated from the adrenal or sympathetic chain.

Histopathology

Gastrointestinal paragangliomas display the classic well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of neural lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. The classic Zellballen pattern is common with nests of tumour cells separated by a delicate fibrovascular network and supported by sustentacular cells. Nuclei are round to oval and sometimes pleomorphic or hyperchromatic, and the cytoplasm is eosinophilic. Trabecular or diffuse growth with confluent nests or pseudorosettes may occur. PAS-positive hyaline globules and nuclear pseudoinclusions are often present. Nuclear atypia is sometimes conspicuous, but mitoses are rare {18549892}.

Histological features are not a reliable indicator of genotype but may be suggestive. Cytoplasmic eosinophilia, vacuoles, clearing, and tight round nests may be seen in SDH-related tumours {29239044;31789631}. Cytoplasmic clearing, thick vascular tumour capsule, myxoid and hyalinized stroma, rich vascular network, and absence of hyaline globules raise suspicion of VHL-syndrome {12114747;29239044}.

The tumour previously termed gangliocytic paraganglioma was reclassified in the fifth-edition WHO classification of endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]] as composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour (CoGNET), which arises almost exclusively in the second part of the duodenum (see section Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour).

Immunohistochemistry

Biomarkers used to confirm the neuroendocrine differentiation include chromogranin A and synaptophysin {32219703}. S100 and SOX10 can be used to highlight sustentacular cells but also commonly stain the neoplastic cells {32219703}. Importantly, in the differential diagnosis with epithelial NETs, paragangliomas are negative for keratin and positive for GATA3 {32407815;23715162}.

As with other NENs, the proliferation rate should be assessed by a formal mitotic count from areas of high mitotic density. Mitoses are often rare or absent, and the Ki-67 index is usually < 10%. Little data exist about proliferative activity in GI tract paragangliomas.

Loss of SDHB expression is a surrogate for SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD, or SDHAF2 alterations {25720320;20236688}. In addition to SDHB loss, loss of SDHA expression is found in tumours with SDHA genetic variants {25720320;21752896}. Loss of MAX expression may be a clue to MAX mutation, but its reliability has been questioned {33367756;29779206}. Loss of FH in the presence of 2-succinocysteine {29079178;29239044;35993574} can facilitate screening for FH-related tumours. Membranous CAIX supports VHL-syndrome but can be found in some SDH-related tumours as well {23257898}.

Diagnosis of metastases

Multifocal primary paragangliomas arising in the syndromic setting should not be mistaken for metastases. Metastasis is defined by the presence of tumour at sites where normal paraganglia do not occur (i.e. lymph node or bone). Involvement of liver or lung is not always a sign of metastasis, because primary paragangliomas occur, rarely, in those locations {30217041;35809684}. As metastatic neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) commonly involve the liver, keratin stains should be performed before diagnosing paraganglioma. Hepatic paragangliomas must also be distinguished from hepatocellular carcinoma {30217041}.

Cytology

FNAB is generally not advised {19958944}. Smears are cellular, with single cells and discohesive tissue fragments. The cells have eccentric, round to oval nuclei and finely granular neuroendocrine chromatin. There are few reports of FNAB of gastrointestinal paragangliomas. In a review, only 6 of 15 pancreatic paragangliomas sampled by FNAB were diagnosed as paraganglioma {34712035}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular pathology is not required. However, all patients should be offered germline screening with the extent of testing dictated by the pretest probability of hereditary disease and local resources.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a NEN with consistent morphology and negative staining for keratin.

Desirable: SDHB immunohistochemistry to guide subsequent genetic testing; routine assessment of cell proliferation (mitotic count, Ki-67/MIB-1 labelling index); molecular immunohistochemistry and biomarkers relevant to tumour pathogenesis; documentation of elevated levels of plasma free metanephrines or urine fractionated metanephrines (at least metanephrine and normetanephrine levels); functional imaging studies and routine germline testing; measurement of all 3 metabolites of parent catecholamines (including 3-methoxytyramine along with metanephrine and normetanephrine levels).

Staging

The staging of gastrointestinal paraganglioma is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) system.

Prognosis and prediction

All patients with paragangliomas are currently considered to have a lifelong risk of metastasis, and therefore conceptually all paragangliomas are considered malignant, with a metastatic rate of approximately 30% for abdominal paragangliomas {30893643}. No single histological finding or biomarker reliably predicts metastasis, and no scoring system is endorsed by WHO {32407815}. Adverse findings are high proliferative activity, necrosis, diffuse growth, cellularity, tumour size, molecular status (SDHB), resectability, somatic ATRX or SETD2 mutations, MAML3 fusions, WNT-altered pathway, and TERT activation {32407815;30769931;28162975;30301828}.
