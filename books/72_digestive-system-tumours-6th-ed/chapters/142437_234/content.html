14.2.4.3.7 Serotonin-producing neuroendocrine tumour

Definition

The serotonin-producing tumour of the pancreas is a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET), composed of serotonin-producing neuroendocrine cells sometimes associated with unregulated serotonin secretion causing carcinoid syndrome.

ICD-O coding

82410/3 Serotonin-producing tumour

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH7NM1 Serotonin-producing tumour of pancreas

Related terminology

Not recommended: pancreatic carcinoid.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is no site predilection within the pancreas; however, ductocentric examples of non-syndromic serotonin-producing neoplasms have been reported {21705949;22221702}.

Clinical features

Carcinoid syndrome is usually present only when there are liver metastases, but rare cases of carcinoid syndrome without liver metastasis have been reported {28454406}. Most patients with functioning tumours present with atypical carcinoid syndrome, abdominal pain being the most common symptom (66%), followed by diarrhoea (52%), weight loss (45%), and flushing (34%) {8758239}. Only rare cases of cardiac valve disease have been reported in literature {29040196;33442370;37720425}. The diagnosis of functioning tumours is confirmed by demonstrating high urinary 5-HIAA excretion.

Epidemiology

Pancreatic serotonin-producing tumours are extremely rare, and those responsible for carcinoid syndrome are even rarer {29912000}. Serotonin-immunoreactive tumours account for 0.58–4.1% of all PanNETs in published case series, but overall, they seem to be < 1% {21705949;25602797;22221702;28422824;10998653}. Only slightly > 50 well-documented functioning neoplasms have been reported {21705949}. They are more commonly reported in women than men. In case series, the mean ages of patients with functioning and non-functioning tumours are 41 years and 56 years, respectively {21705949}.

Etiology

There are no known etiological factors. No association with genetic syndromes has been documented.

Pathogenesis

The most frequent chromosomal abnormality is the loss of heterozygosity of the whole chromosome 1, with a prevalence of 60% in a recent study {35739266}. DNA sequencing revealed a paucity of variations in these lesions, with the lack of recurrent point mutations. KMT2C was the only gene altered, present in only 2 of 29 tumours {35739266}. Transcriptome analysis showed that these lesions are enriched in TGF-β pathway activation signatures associated with extracellular matrix remodelling {35739266}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most are small, < 20 mm, but some tumours can be larger (up to 60 mm) {21705949;16104045;35739266}. Functioning tumours are usually larger than non-functioning tumours {21705949}. Rare small non-syndromic serotonin-producing tumours centred on the main pancreatic duct, causing dilation of the upstream duct system, mimic intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm or upstream pancreatic atrophy {20713615;21862776;21431402}.

Histopathology

Serotonin-producing PanNETs display the classic well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Most serotonin-producing tumours are well differentiated, G1 or G2, with rare mitosis {21705949}. However, vascular, perineural, and adjacent organ/structure invasion is frequent, with rare tumour necrosis. Serotonin-producing PanNETs usually do not resemble their intestinal counterparts. The neoplastic cells are frequently arranged in a trabecular pattern and less frequently in solid nests, with variable amounts of stroma {21705949;22221702;33977308;35739266}. The stroma is prominent in ductocentric tumours {22221702;35739266}.

Immunohistochemistry

Phenotypically and ultrastructurally, the neoplastic cells of serotonin-producing PanNETs resemble the gastric-type and not the intestinal-type enterochromaffin cells (EC cells): unlike their intestinal counterparts, they rarely express substance P, FGF1 (aFGF), VMAT1, and CDX2, but they usually express serotonin and VMAT2 {21705949}. SSTR2 is usually detectable by immunohistochemistry {21705949}. Two immunohistochemical markers appear of help in distinguishing the pancreatic versus intestinal origin of serotonin-producing lesions: PAX6, which is always negative in intestinal tumours but positive in up to 50% of pancreatic lesions, and PDX1, which is usually negative in intestinal tumours but always positive in pancreatic neoplasms {35739266}.

Grading

Serotonin-producing PanNETs are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

Cytology

The cytology is the same as that of other PanNETs and is diagnosed as category 7 (malignant) when using the WHO Reporting System for Pancreaticobiliary Cytopathology (WHO System) {38709670}. The evaluation of the immunolabelling of serotonin is also feasible on cytological specimens {35471694}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinical evidence of carcinoid syndrome (for functioning PanNETs only); well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count; positive serotonin immunohistochemistry for non-functioning tumours.

Desirable: SSTR2 positive immunohistochemistry.

Staging

The staging of serotonin-producing PanNETs is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for neuroendocrine tumours of the pancreas.

Prognosis and prediction

The overall biological behaviour of serotonin-immunoreactive non-functioning tumours is indolent {33977308}. The functioning tumours are associated with a poorer prognosis than non-functioning ones, because they are almost always metastatic {21705949}.
