13.1.4.3 Kaposi sarcoma

Definition

Kaposi sarcoma (KS) is a KSHV/HHV8-associated vascular neoplasm characterized by disorganized endothelial cell growth with inflammation, resulting in the formation of spindle cells with erythrocyte-containing clefts or non–spindle cell proliferations.

ICD-O coding

91400/3 Kaposi sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B57.2 & XA7MC7 Kaposi sarcoma of stomach

2B57.2 & XH2WT6 Kaposi sarcoma of duodenum

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiosarcoma multiplex; granuloma multiplex haemorrhagicum

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

KS may occur in any site within the GI tract and liver.

Clinical features

KS involves the upper and lower GI tracts, usually multifocally. Concurrent cutaneous lesions are sometimes present, but isolated gastrointestinal involvement can be the first site of documented disease {39246073}. The upper GI tract (particularly stomach and duodenum {23226197}) is most commonly affected. Lesions can be asymptomatic. Symptoms, when present, depend on site and extent but usually comprise gastrointestinal haemorrhage, dysphagia, obstruction, or epigastric pain {1947766;26261737;23226197;39246073}. Hepatic KS is usually clinically silent but can cause altered hepatic chemistries; imaging shows multiple hilar and periportal nodules {30536289}.

Epidemiology

Gastrointestinal involvement occurs in two main settings: AIDS and iatrogenic. The GI tract is the most common AIDS-associated extracutaneous site {10749966;10649240;26261737;16679162}. Hepatic KS is mostly associated with AIDS, and it is asymptomatic and usually found at autopsy. Effective HIV therapy correlates with a decline in KS cases {27584730}.

Iatrogenic KS, which includes visceral involvement in half of cases, can occur after solid-organ transplantation or immunosuppressive therapy for treatment of diseases such as inflammatory bowel disease {10749966;26261737;36336687}.

Etiology

KSHV/HHV8, a DNA virus {7997879;7700310}, is the causative agent for KS {7723505;12244338;10749966}. Not all HHV8 infections lead to pathological manifestations, and most primary infections are asymptomatic {27584730}.

Pathogenesis

HHV8 infection is required but insufficient for disease induction, which is affected by genetic, immunological, and environmental factors {11423257;11536246}. KS can develop at any HIV disease stage but is more frequent in advanced immune suppression {11423260;25709361;26261737}. HHV8 impacts KS formation by both its latent and lytic viral proteins, affecting endothelial differentiation, inflammation, proliferation, and angiogenesis. It induces an aberrant spindle cell differentiation of latently infected endothelial cells via NOTCH signalling {25212381}. HHV8 can cause capillary endothelial cells to differentiate into lymphatic cells, or vice versa, in part by HHV8-induced expression of PROX1. The nuclear latency protein, latency-associated nuclear antigen (LANA), is responsible for replication and persistence of the HHV8 genome in latently infected, dividing cells, and also affects angiogenesis. Comparison of skin KS and gastrointestinal KS shows differentially expressed genes unique to gastrointestinal KS and some common to both, the latter encoding inflammatory cytokines and host factors related to angiogenesis and immune regulation {37740179}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gastrointestinal KS manifests as reddish-blue or brown flat macules, luminal polyps, ulcerating and haemorrhagic mucosal lesions, or rarely as transmural masses. Liver KS exhibits multiple dark red lesions involving the capsule and portal areas.

Histopathology

Gastrointestinal KS comprises infiltrative proliferations of small irregular vascular channels and fascicles of monomorphic, spindled endothelial cells, sometimes with mucosal ulceration. As in cutaneous lesions, erythrocyte extravasation, intracellular/extracellular hyaline globules, lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates, and haemosiderin deposits are seen at least focally {18494611}. Inconspicuous, non–spindle cell KS morphologies that are lymphangioma-like, granulation-tissue–like, haemorrhagic/telangiectatic-like, or tumours shrouded by marked inflammation, can predominate over the spindle cell presentation. The same tumour can show more than one histological manifestation {35973011}. Subtle KS spindle cells arranged around vascular spaces mimicking granulation tissue, or obscured by inflammation, are highlighted by HHV8 immunostaining. Lymphangiectatic spaces lined by HHV8-positive endothelial cells, without spindle cells, can also be a manifestation. KS can mimic gastrointestinal stromal tumour (including KIT (CD117) expression {18494611}), mucosal prolapse, and inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour. A high index of suspicion for KS in an immunocompromised patient presenting with a mass/lesion and judicious use of HHV8 immunohistochemistry aid in diagnosis of subtle KS. Rare subtypes include anaplastic KS, which is aggressive and exhibits marked nuclear pleomorphism {19638066;37080393}. Hepatic KS typically involves the portal and periportal areas as poorly vasoformative streams of relatively uniform, mildly atypical spindle cells, sometimes accompanied by cytoplasmic eosinophilic hyaline globules, associated haemorrhage, and haemosiderin deposition.

Endothelial and spindle cells express CD31, CD34, ERG, and podoplanin/D2-40. Nuclear HHV8 is immunohistochemically detectable in almost all lesions but may be focal. KS shows diffuse nuclear expression of LANA1, unlike other spindle cell tumour mimics, which are negative. In early KS, VEGFR3 is more extensively expressed than LANA1 {14990970;10200299}.

Cytology

FNA may show mildly atypical spindle cells in an inflammatory background and HHV8 positivity on cell block {31051677}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of HHV8 by immunohistochemistry, in situ hybridization, or PCR-based assays may support the diagnosis of KS in critical cases {7489408}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic histological appearance of infiltrating, small irregular vascular channels and fascicles of non-pleomorphic spindled endothelial cells.

Desirable: clinical history of severe immunosuppression or HIV infection; immunohistochemical evidence of endothelial differentiation; HHV8 expression.

Staging

This neoplasm is not formally staged according to the TNM soft tissue tumours classification system.

Prognosis and prediction

About 80% of lesions are clinically silent, and some regress spontaneously. Gastrointestinal involvement may not substantially influence survival {9311700;1947766}, which is mostly determined by the degree of immunosuppression at diagnosis {1947766}. Withdrawal of immunosuppression can sometimes cause disease resolution. Although most patients with hepatic KS are asymptomatic, the few with clinically significant disease may undergo rapid and often fatal progression to liver and multiorgan failure {28217255}. Combined highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) and systemic chemotherapy improve morbidity and mortality {15280789}.
