13.1.2.2 Desmoid fibromatosis

Definition

Desmoid fibromatosis is a locally aggressive, non-metastasizing fibroblastic/myofibroblastic neoplasm with an infiltrative growth pattern.

ICD-O coding

88210/1 Desmoid fibromatosis

88220/1 Abdominal fibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH6116 Abdominal fibromatosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: desmoid tumour; aggressive fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intra-abdominal desmoid fibromatosis arises most commonly in the mesentery of the small bowel, followed by the mesentery of the ileocolic region and the mesocolon. Less frequently involved sites include the pelvis, omentum, and retroperitoneum {28489620;26171757;32099073}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with an asymptomatic abdominal mass, whereas a minority report abdominal pain. Gastrointestinal haemorrhage and acute abdomen may rarely occur after intestinal perforation {21478276}.

Epidemiology

Desmoid fibromatosis is a rare tumour, with both sexes equally affected {28261640}. The age range is broad, with a peak in the third decade of life. Most cases are sporadic, but 10–15% are associated with germline APC gene mutations causing familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), and these tend to present at a younger age {17470870;22419028}.

Etiology

There is a considerably increased prevalence of desmoid fibromatosis in patients with FAP (Gardner syndrome). Physical factors (tissue trauma and surgery) increase the risk of tumour development {29220303}.

Pathogenesis

The WNT/β-catenin signalling pathway is dysregulated in virtually all tumours {28489620;32099073}. Somatic activating mutations in the CTNNB1 gene and germline inactivating mutations in the APC gene are mutually exclusive and are the initiating events in the vast majority of sporadic and familial tumours, respectively. The mutational consequence is interference with β-catenin proteasomal degradation resulting in its cytoplasmic and subsequent accumulation seen as nuclear β-catenin immunoreactivity {28575679}. The WNT pathway can be impaired by other mechanisms in CTNNB1 and APC wildtype tumours {26171757}. Occasionally > 1 CTNNB1 mutation can be identified in a patient’s sample, and a second non-CTNNB1 deleterious mutation can also be detected, more commonly in young patients {32099073}. However, the role of these changes is unclear.

Macroscopic appearance

Intra-abdominal desmoid fibromatosis tends to present as a large, well-defined mass, often as large as 100 mm in diameter, with a whorled cut surface.

Histopathology

Microscopically, fibromatosis exhibits a wide variety of features mimicking several other entities {27124915}. It is variably cellular and composed of monomorphic slender spindle or stellate cells with tapering or ovoid nuclei and inconspicuous or small nucleoli, arranged in long sweeping fascicles. Mitotic figures are rare and are normal in configuration. The matrix can exhibit myxoid and hyalinized keloidal scar-like zones, reminiscent of nodular fasciitis. Interspersed, delicate, elongated, compressed vessels running parallel to tumour fascicles are common, but dilated vessels can also be prominent; perivascular oedema and microhaemorrhages are also features. Although the gross appearance is circumscribed, microscopically extensive tongue-like infiltration is present {2321698}. Desmoid fibromatosis is usually positive for SMA and occasionally desmin. Nuclear expression of β-catenin is observed in ≥ 80% of tumours {15832090;17711447}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In 85–95% of sporadic cases, CTNNB1 activating mutations are identified in exon 3, encoding the N-terminus of β-catenin (codons 32–45), with the most common mutations being NM_001904.4:c.121A>G p.(Thr41Ala), NM_001904.4:c.134C>T p.(Ser45Phe), and NM_001904.4:c.133T>C p.(Ser45Pro) {26171757;28489620}. In FAP-related desmoid fibromatosis, APC germline mutations are often located beyond codon 1444 {28489620}. Absence of CTNNB1 mutations in an apparently sporadic tumour should raise concern for unrecognized FAP and prompt colonoscopy and other clinical investigations including germline testing {28489620;28961825}. Most cases have a balanced genomic profile, but a minority (~25%) show recurrent copy-number changes (loss of 6q, loss of 5q, gain of 20q, and gain of 8q); the role of these changes is unclear {25878329;32099073}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: long sweeping fascicles of myofibroblasts without substantial cytological atypia; infiltrative margins.

Desirable: nuclear β-catenin expression.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Desmoid fibromatosis is characterized by unpredictable behaviour, with periods of rapid growth and stabilization {29220303}. Spontaneous regression, most often observed in abdominal wall lesions, is rare in intra-abdominal cases. Recurrence occurs in 20–30% of patients {26171757;28961825} and is associated with young age and larger tumour size {26171757;25878329}. CTNNB1p.(Ser45Phe) mutations are a risk factor for local recurrence after surgery with curative intent; this alteration occurs more often in extra-abdominal than in intra-abdominal or abdominal cases {22419028;32099073}. Intra-abdominal tumours are at lower risk for local recurrence than tumours in the extremities but at higher risk than abdominal wall lesions {26171757;28489620}. Incomplete resection does not correlate consistently with recurrence {26171757}. Very rare tumour-related deaths have been reported {28489620;26171757}.
