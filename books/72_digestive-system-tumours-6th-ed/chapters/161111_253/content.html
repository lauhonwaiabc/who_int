16.1.1.11 Polymerase proofreading-associated polyposis and other polyposes (POLD1, POLE, AXIN2, NTHL1, MSH3)

Definition

Other adenomatous polyposes include a heterogeneous group of inherited conditions characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps. This includes polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis (PPAP), AXIN2-related polyposis, NTHL1-associated polyposis, and MSH3-associated polyposis.

Acceptable: NTHL1 tumour syndrome.

Not recommended: familial adenomatous polyposis 3; endonuclease III–like 1 (NTHL1).

Subtype(s)

Four separate syndromes are covered in this section: polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis; AXIN2-related polyposis; NTHL1-associated polyposis; MSH3-associated polyposis.

Localization

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

There is a high risk of adenomatous polyposis and cancer affecting the colorectum, and a low to moderate increased risk of cancer developing at other sites including stomach, duodenum, and pancreas {26822575}.

AXIN2-related polyposis

The polyposis primarily affects the colorectum.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

The adenomatous polyposis and main cancer susceptibility affects the colorectum.

MSH3-associated polyposis

The polyposis primarily affects the colorectum. There is an increased risk of adenomas and cancers developing in the duodenum and stomach.

Clinical features

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

PPAP is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) affecting the proofreading (exonuclease) domains of the DNA polymerase encoding genes POLD1 and POLE. The syndrome is characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps with increased risk of colorectal cancer and endometrial cancer, although other cancers may occur.

Adenomatous polyps of the large bowel typically occur by 50 years of age {29120461}. The most common tumour types are colorectal carcinomas, followed by endometrial carcinomas in POLD1 heterozygote carriers and duodenal carcinomas in POLE heterozygote carriers; POLD1 variants cause a significantly higher risk of endometrial cancer than POLE variants {33948826;25529843}. The majority of endometrial cancers occur in patients aged > 50 years. Some individuals with a POLE but none with a POLD1 pathogenic variant have developed ovarian cancer, whereas some with a POLE but very few with a POLD1 pathogenic variant develop hypermutated medulloblastoma brain tumours, sometimes at a young age {31624068;33948826}. Multiple primary tumours may also be a feature of PPAP, more so in female patients. The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in PPAP have a characteristic ultramutant somatic mutation rate. Less than 15% of PPAP-associated tumours acquire somatic mismatch repair (MMR) gene variants resulting in microsatellite instability (MSI) and immunohistochemical MMR protein abnormalities suggestive of Lynch syndrome, and thus PPAP is one rare cause of Lynch-like syndrome {26648449;25370038}. Most PPAP cases have an attenuated colorectal polyposis (10–100 polyps) that seems to be more severe in carriers of GPVs in POLE than in those with POLD1 variants (10–50 compared with < 20 colonic adenomatous polyps per patient, respectively) {26822575}.

AXIN2-related polyposis

AXIN2-related polyposis is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by germline loss-of-function pathogenic variants in AXIN2, and is characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps, increased risk of colorectal cancer, and variable extracolonic features of oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia.

The colorectal polyps in AXIN2 variant carriers are typically pancolonic, ranging from 5 to > 100 (median: 12.5), comprising predominantly adenomatous polyps, but hyperplastic or serrated polyps may also be identified {34817745;30671715}. Oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia, including sparse hair and eyebrows, occur with incomplete penetrance {15042511;21416598;26025668;23838596;34817745}. Limited data suggest there may be increased risk of other cancers (e.g. hepatocellular carcinoma) {28759178}. The paucity of published AXIN2-associated polyposis families limits genotype–phenotype correlations {30671715}.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

NTHL1-associated polyposis is an autosomal recessive syndrome caused by biallelic GPVs in NTHL1, and is characterized by multiple colorectal adenomatous polyps, increased risk of colorectal cancer and breast cancer, and variable other extracolonic malignancies (see #49002Table 16.20).

The adenomatous polyps of the large bowel arise in adulthood, generally by the age of 50 years, with increased risk of colorectal cancer as the major malignant risk {25938944;32239880;31227763}. Tumours at other sites, including endometrium and breast, are also seen, with breast cancer appearing as the most frequent cancer type in women {30753826;35292903}. Most patients with NTHL1-associated polyposis along with cancer have an extracolonic tumour as the first diagnosed tumour type {30753826;35292903}. NTHL1-associated polyposis has been suggested as a cause of Lynch-like syndrome because of the clinical overlap and the occurrence of acquired somatic MMR gene variants resulting in MSI and mismatch repair–deficient (dMMR) immunohistochemical abnormalities {26559593;27720914}. Sebaceous skin neoplasms have also been reported to occur in NTHL1-associated polyposis, and thus it is another potential cause of Muir–Torre syndrome. Additional more variable neoplastic findings include duodenal adenomas, duodenal cancer, prostate cancer, bladder urothelial cancer, psammomatous meningioma, brain tumours, basal cell carcinoma, pancreatic cancer, ovarian cancer, cervical cancer, head and neck squamous cell carcinomas, and haematological malignancies, mostly reported in small numbers of cases; the cumulative lifetime risk of developing extracolonic cancers by age 60 years is estimated at 35–78% {32239880;30753826;29105096;31227763}.

MSH3-associated polyposis

MSH3-associated polyposis has been described in only a very few familial cases, so data on this syndrome are very limited. Recessive biallelic inheritance of germline pathogenic variants in the DNA MMR gene MSH3 causes adenomatous polyposis {27476653}. The phenotypes of the few cases reported to date include variable combinations of colorectal and duodenal adenomas, colorectal cancer, gastric cancer, and early-onset astrocytoma {27476653}.

Epidemiology

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

PPAP accounts for approximately 10% of all cases of familial adenomatous polyposis {29120461}. This form of polyposis is thought to be rarer than MUTYH-associated polyposis, although the exact prevalence is unknown. Germline (constitutional) POLE and POLD1 variants are present in 0.5–2% of patients in intestinal polyposis and colorectal cancer (CRC) cohorts enriched for familial disease {26822575}. The adenomatous polyps of the large bowel occur in adulthood, generally by the age of 50 years, and the risk of developing CRC by the age of 70 years is 1–2 in 3 {29120461}. The cumulative incidence of CRC in POLE and POLD1 variant carriers is estimated at approximately 90% and 50%, respectively {33948826}.

AXIN2-related polyposis

AXIN2 variant carriers have a mean age at diagnosis of CRC/polyposis of 52.5 ± 9.2 years {34817745}. Data are limited as only a relatively small number of affected families have been described.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

NTHL1-associated polyposis is estimated to be approximately one fifth less common than MUTYH-associated polyposis, although the exact prevalence is unknown.

MSH3-associated polyposis

Insufficient data

Etiology

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

PPAP is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by GPVs, typically base substitutions and missense substitutions with relatively few frameshift mutations, affecting the proofreading (exonuclease) domains of the DNA polymerase encoding genes POLD1 and POLE.

AXIN2-related polyposis

AXIN2-related polyposis is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by germline loss-of-function pathogenic variants in AXIN2.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

NTHL1-associated polyposis is an autosomal recessive syndrome caused by biallelic GPVs, typically leading to premature stop codons, in NTHL1.

MSH3-associated polyposis

MSH3-associated polyposis is an autosomal recessive syndrome caused by germline loss-of-function biallelic pathogenic variants in MSH3.

Pathogenesis

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

Dominantly inherited germline variants in the proofreading (exonuclease) domains of POLD1 and POLE cause PPAP. These genes code for the DNA polymerases Polδ and Polε, which act in the delta and epsilon DNA replication complexes to replicate DNA on the lagging and leading strands, respectively {23263490;26822575;23447401}. Inactivation of their proofreading function, with maintenance of the polymerase and regulatory functions, causes a defect in the excision of mispaired bases inserted during DNA replication. The resulting ultramutant phenotype with exceedingly numerous single-nucleotide substitutions produces a high-penetrance predisposition to development of colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas with a variable tendency to form multiple and large neoplasms. It is not required that the normal gene allele undergo somatic mutation {23447401}, although loss of the wildtype allele has been reported and may be favoured {23263490}. PPAP is therefore an exception to the Knudson multiple-hit hypothesis; it is unique among the constitutional DNA repair disorders in that the mechanism does not require inactivation of both alleles.

A notable feature is that tumours show characteristic proofreading-deficient mutation signatures (four Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer [COSMIC] #10 signatures: SBS10a, SBS10b, SBS10c, and SBS10d), with overrepresentation of C>A transversions and C>T transitions. In some cases, these proofreading-deficient mutation signatures may co-occur with dMMR mutation signatures with associated MSI: mutation signature SBS14 is associated with concurrent proofreading-defective POLE and MSI, whereas SBS20 is associated with concurrent proofreading-defective POLD1 and MSI. In such cases, limited tumour testing for dMMR may result in a mistaken diagnosis of Lynch syndrome, but these cases are better regarded as “Lynch-like syndrome” due to acquired mutations within one of the MMR genes resulting from the very high mutation rate conferred by the defective proofreading of the inherited POLE or POLD1 pathogenic variant. In < 15% of patients with inherited POLE proofreading pathogenic variants, there is an increase in small indels in CRCs indicating MSI due to acquired defective MMR.

POLE GPVs should be distinguished from somatically acquired POLE proofreading domain mutations that occur in several tumour types, most commonly those of the endometrium (7–12%) and colorectum (1–2%), and occasionally in tumours of the breast, stomach, pancreas, and brain, where they define a distinct and ultramutated tumour subgroup. Interestingly, somatic POLD1 proofreading domain mutations appear to be very uncommon {26822575}. Mutation sites within the polymerase exonuclease domain tend to differ between PPAP and sporadically acquired polymerase mutation cases {26822575}.

AXIN2-related polyposis

Germline loss-of-function variants in AXIN2 cause AXIN2-associated polyposis with or without associated oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia {34817745}. Like GPVs in APC, which occur in most but not all families with adenomatous polyposis (either classic or attenuated phenotype), alteration of another WNT pathway component AXIN2 is associated with colorectal polyposis and an increased risk of CRC. AXIN2 is a scaffold protein that forms part of the destruction complex together with APC protein, targeting β-catenin (the signalling intermediate of the WNT pathway) for degradation. AXIN2 supports the GSK3β-dependent phosphorylation of β-catenin, which marks the protein for ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation {25236910;22745173}. AXIN2 is induced by WNT signals, and as such acts as a negative regulator of WNT signalling {11809808}. Some CRCs show evidence of a second or somatic hit to AXIN2 via loss of heterozygosity {34817745}.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

NTHL1-associated polyposis is a germline disorder of base excision repair caused by recessively inherited (homozygous or compound heterozygous) variants in NTHL1 {25938944;32239880}. In normal cells, NTHL1 encodes a DNA glycosylase that repairs a variety of DNA lesions by removing the damaged base, including 5-hydroxycytosine and 5-hydroxyuracil, two products of cytosine oxidation that are mutagenic because they mispair with adenine, resulting in high frequency C>T transitions {29105096;33087284}. The increased mutation rate caused by NTHL1 deficiency contributes to tumour development. NTHL1-associated polyposis tumours show the COSMIC single-base substitution signature SBS30 {28912133;30753826;32239880;33087284;34363023}. There may be a small increase in the risk of developing CRC or other malignancies in individuals with a heterozygous GPV in NTHL1, but this remains to be confirmed {32239880;32860789}.

MSH3-associated polyposis

MSH3-associated polyposis is an autosomal recessive syndrome caused by biallelic GPVs in MSH3 that alter the DNA MMR process in an unusual manner. The tumours do not have classic MSI at mononucleotide repeats, but instead show instability at dinucleotide, trinucleotide, tetranucleotide, and pentanucleotide repeats. This phenomenon is called elevated microsatellite alterations at selected tetranucleotides (EMAST) and leads to susceptibility to colorectal and duodenal adenomas, some of which may progress to adenocarcinomas {28367107}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is similar to that of sporadic tumours {34817745;25938944}.

Histopathology

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

Histopathological appearances are closely similar to sporadic counterparts for colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas. The predominant colorectal polyp type is adenomatous, although serrated polyps are observed.

AXIN2-related polyposis

The colorectal neoplasms show a similar morphology to sporadic colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas {23838596}. Colorectal hyperplastic/serrated lesions may also be seen.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

The colorectal polyps are mostly adenomas, although some hyperplastic polyps and serrated lesions are seen. Colorectal adenocarcinomas in NTHL1-associated polyposis show closely similar appearances to sporadic colorectal counterparts {32239880}.

MSH3-associated polyposis

Histopathological appearances are similar to sporadic counterparts, but data are very limited.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Germline POLD1 and POLE proofreading domain variants and AXIN2 variants are curated in the Leiden Open Variation Database (LOVD) (https://databases.lovd.nl/shared/genes/) and ClinVar (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/).

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

Pathogenic variants associated with PPAP involving the exonuclease domain of POLE (amino acids 268–471) or POLD1 (amino acids 304–533) generate predominantly base-substitution variants within affected tumour cells. This produces unique mutation signatures with a very marked increase in C>A transversions in the context TCT and a marked increase in C>T transitions in the context TCG. Tumour mutation signatures SBS10a and SBS10b are associated with proofreading-defective POLE, whereas SBS10c and SBS10d are associated with proofreading-defective POLD1 (mutation signature SBS14 is associated with concurrent proofreading-defective POLE and MSI, and SBS20 is associated with concurrent proofreading-defective POLD1 and MSI) {32025007;32025018;35197584}. This results in a strong bias for particular amino acid changes, with an overrepresentation of serine to tyrosine or leucine, and arginine to isoleucine or glutamine substitutions, and a substantial increase in glutamic acid to stop codon variants. These manifest as a distinctive pattern of missense and truncation variants in oncoproteins and tumour suppressors.

AXIN2-related polyposis

Due to the low incidence, AXIN2 testing for autosomal dominant loss-of-function variants is usually performed after exclusion of germline alterations in either APC or (biallelic variants in) MUTYH in patients with adenomatous polyposis (5 to > 100 polyps), with or without CRC, especially if there is evidence of oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

Following exclusion of the more common causes of polyposis (GPVs in APC, MUTYH, POLE, POLD1 or AXIN2), families with adenomatous polyposis (5 to > 100 polyps), some with CRCs, should be investigated for biallelic germline NTHL1 variants. Similarly, in individuals with multiple primary tumours and/or (bilateral) breast cancer at a young age for which TP53, BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, and ATM were excluded, NTHL1-associated polyposis should be considered. The majority of pathogenic variants in NTHL1 are truncating {32239880}, and the most common variant in northern Europe appears to be NM_002528.6:c.286C>T p.(Gln90*). DNA sequencing of multiple primary tumour types of patients with NTHL1-associated polyposis has shown the COSMIC single-base substitution signature SBS30 {28912133;30753826;32239880;33087284;34363023}. Since this mutation signature is rare in sporadic cancers, detection of SBS30 in tumour exome or genome sequencing data is a strong indication of NTHL1 deficiency and NTHL1-associated polyposis.

MSH3-associated polyposis

Tumours arising in MSH3-associated polyposis do not have classic MSI at mononucleotide repeats, but rather instability at dinucleotide, trinucleotide, tetranucleotide, and pentanucleotide repeats – a phenomenon called elevated microsatellite alterations at selected tetranucleotides (EMAST) {28367107}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

Essential: identification of a germline heterozygous missense variant affecting the proofreading exonuclease domain-encoding region of POLE or POLD1 is required to distinguish PPAP from Lynch syndrome, familial adenomatous polyposis, and MUTYH-associated polyposis.

Desirable: consistent phenotype with colonic polyposis or other neoplasms; characteristic mutation burden and spectrum in tumours.

AXIN2-related polyposis

Essential: identification of a heterozygous GPV in AXIN2.

Desirable: exclusion of GPVs in APC, MUTYH, POLE, POLD1, or NTHL1; consistent phenotype of familial adenomatous polyposis-like polyps (5 to > 100 polyps) with increased risk of CRC, with or without oligodontia and/or ectodermal dysplasia.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

Essential: identification of biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) GPVs in NTHL1.

Desirable: exclusion of GPVs in APC, MUTYH, POLE, POLD1, or AXIN2; consistent phenotype with colorectal adenomatous polyps, CRC, and/or variable extracolonic malignancies; characteristic mutation burden and spectrum in tumours.

MSH3-associated polyposis

Essential: identification of biallelic GPVs in MSH3.

Desirable: finding EMAST (instability at dinucleotide, trinucleotide, tetranucleotide, and pentanucleotide repeats) in tumours, together with a consistent phenotype of variable colonic polyposis, duodenal adenomas, or other associated neoplasms.

Prognosis and prediction

Polymerase proofreading–associated polyposis

Ultramutant cancers associated with PPAP, being rich in neoantigens, including those with MSI, appear to be good targets for PD1/PDL1 immune checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy {28404093;27310809}.

AXIN2-related polyposis

Because of the small number of published cases, there are currently insufficient data regarding prognosis and prediction of tumours arising in the setting of AXIN2-related polyposis.

NTHL1-associated polyposis

Patients with NTHL1-associated polyposis present with a wide variety of tumours, and prognosis and outcome data are currently limited. Several patients have developed multiple tumours over a longer period of time, which may suggest that tumours respond well to treatment, possibly because of the high mutation load in these tumours.

MSH3-associated polyposis

Not reported
