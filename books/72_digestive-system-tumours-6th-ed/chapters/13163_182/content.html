13.1.6.3 Calcifying nested stromal-epithelial tumour of the liver

Definition

Calcifying nested stromal-epithelial tumour (CNSET) of the liver is a low-grade hepatic neoplasm of uncertain lineage characterized by a distinctive nested architecture, a cellular myofibroblastic stroma, with psammomatous calcifications and/or bone formation.

ICD-O coding

89750/1 Calcifying nested stromal-epithelial tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F70.5 & XA5DY0 & XH8X78 Calcifying nested stromal-epithelial tumour of liver

Related terminology

Not recommended: nested stromal-epithelial tumour; ossifying stromal-epithelial tumour; desmoplastic nested spindle cell tumour; ossifying malignant mixed epithelial and stromal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CNSET more often arises in the right hepatic lobe (65–85% of cases) and rarely involves both hepatic lobes {15613851;15613852;19363442;33994539}.

Clinical features

CNSET is often discovered incidentally, sometimes with a history of a calcified hepatic nodule. Occasionally, patients present with abdominal pain, a palpable mass, nausea, or Cushing syndrome {15613852;19363442}.

Epidemiology

CNSET is rare, with approximately 50 reported cases. There is a female predominance and a predilection for children, adolescents, and young adults {30354275}.

Etiology

Most CNSETs are sporadic. Several cases have been associated with Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome {23570373;27965001}.

Pathogenesis

CTNNB1 gene alterations and TERT promoter mutations have been identified in all tested cases {22749188;33994539}. CTNNB1alterations include exon 3 deletions, as well as point mutations to both the p.Asp32-p.Ser37 ubiquitination motif and the p.Thr41 phosphorylation site.

Macroscopic appearance

CNSET is well circumscribed but unencapsulated, with a multinodular, sharp interface with adjacent liver. Tumours range from 28 to 300 mm; most are > 100 mm. The cut surface is yellow or white, homogeneous, and granular {19363442}.

Histopathology

The tumours are composed of ovoid to irregular nests of variably spindled to epithelioid cells, separated by a cellular, myofibroblastic stroma. A focally trabecular architecture is sometimes observed. Occasional nests show cystic degeneration, myxoid change, or central necrosis. The tumour cells are bland and uniform, with vesicular chromatin, indistinct nucleoli, and eosinophilic cytoplasm. Mitotic activity is variable, from low to > 20 mitoses/2 mm2 {33994539}. The nests often harbour psammomatous calcifications and sometimes ossification. The stroma may contain entrapped cords of hepatocytes and bile ducts, and there is frequently prominent bile ductular proliferation around tumour nests.

By immunohistochemistry, the tumour cells are positive for broad-spectrum keratins and express nuclear WT1 in most cases. Neoplastic cells show nuclear and cytoplasmic β-catenin expression {15613851;15613852;19363442;22749188;33994539}. Expression of CD56, EMA, PR, NSE, and KIT (CD117) is variable. Desmin, chromogranin, synaptophysin, and hepatocellular markers (HepPar1, arginase-1 (ARG), polyclonal CEA, and albumin in situ hybridization) are negative {19363442;33994539}. The stromal spindle cells express SMA and lack β-catenin expression.

Cytology

Unknown

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Somatic activating CTNNB1 deletions and point mutations have been documented in all sequenced cases {22749188;33994539}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nested architecture surrounded by cellular myofibroblastic stroma; bland and uniform epithelioid and spindle cells; psammomatous calcifications and/or bone formation.

Desirable: nuclear expression of WT1 and β-catenin; positive for cytokeratin

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most patients with CNSET are cured by surgical excision, but several reported patients had unresectable primary tumours or local recurrence necessitating liver transplantation {15613851;15613852;18338937;19363442}. Singular cases of extrahepatic involvement (lung, peritoneum, lymph nodes) have been reported {18338937;21221846;33994539}. There have been 3 reported patients who died of locally progressive or metastatic disease {21221846;29290661;33994539}. Mitotic rate, tumour size, lymphovascular invasion, and multifocality at presentation seem to correlate with worse outcomes based on limited available data {33994539}.
