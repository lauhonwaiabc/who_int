13.1.5.1 Schwannoma

Definition

Schwannoma is a benign neoplasm of nerve sheath cells showing Schwannian differentiation.

ICD-O coding

95600/0 Schwannoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E92.Z & XH98Z3 & XA7MC7 Schwannoma, NOS, of digestive organs

Related terminology

Not recommended: neurilemmoma; neurinoma.

Subtype(s)

Microcystic/reticular schwannoma (95607/0)

Localization

Gastrointestinal schwannomas form solid intramural masses centred in the submucosa and/or muscularis propria. Schwannomas are far more prevalent in the stomach than in any other gastrointestinal location; tumours of the lower oesophagus, colon, and rectum are rare {11420455}.

Clinical features

Schwannomas are homogeneously attenuating, well-defined mural masses on CT, typically lacking intratumoural haemorrhage, necrosis, and degeneration, but it can be difficult to distinguish them from gastrointestinal stromal tumours {15728600}. Depending on the tumour size, patients can be asymptomatic (with the lesions being discovered incidentally during endoscopic or other imaging workup) or can present with gastrointestinal bleeding or mass-like symptoms (larger tumours).

Epidemiology

Schwannomas of the GI tract are rare. The tumours occur in older adults, with a marked female predominance {33777207}.

Etiology

There is no known etiology; although soft tissue schwannomas may arise in the setting of neurofibromatosis, there does not appear to be any syndromic associations with gastrointestinal schwannoma.

Pathogenesis

In contrast to conventional schwannomas, which usually show loss of heterozygosity at NF2 and/or NF2-inactivating mutations, whether sporadic or associated with neurofibromatosis type 2, gastrointestinal schwannomas lack NF2 gene alterations, and instead have been found to show polysomy of chromosomes 22, 2, and 18. This suggests that they may represent a morphologically and genetically distinct group of peripheral nerve sheath tumours {13679444}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are typically located within the submucosa and/or muscularis propria and are well circumscribed. They may extend into overlying mucosa and protrude into the gastric lumen, or may bulge into the serosal aspect. The average size is 30 mm, with a range of 5–110 mm reported. On cut surface, the tumours are well circumscribed, may be vaguely lobulated, and have a yellow fleshy appearance. Cystic change is occasionally seen.

Histopathology

Gastrointestinal schwannomas differ from their soft tissue counterparts by being (1) unencapsulated, (2) uniformly cellular (i.e. lacking Antoni A and B zonation), and (3) surrounded by a prominent cuff of lymphocytes, which may contain germinal centres. It is also less common to see hyalinized vessels, nuclear palisading, and Verocay bodies in this subtype {13679444;22137423;8847066}. Tumours are composed of spindled cells with indistinct pale cytoplasm and tapering nuclei with fine chromatin and often a small nucleolus. The cells are arranged in short fascicles or whorls. Mild nuclear pleomorphism or atypia is common. Mitotic activity is low, and tumour necrosis is not seen. The stroma is usually collagenous but may be focally myxoid. Admixed lymphocytes and foamy histiocytes may be present.

The microcystic/reticular subtype occurs as a well-circumscribed, unencapsulated submucosal lesion arising along the GI tract, including in the stomach, small bowel, and colon {18520439}.

Gastrointestinal schwannomas are strongly and diffusely positive for S100 and SOX10, and often express GFAP {16623779}. The tumours are rarely positive for CD34, and are negative for HMB45, KIT, DOG1, SMA, desmin, and synaptophysin.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed tumour composed of spindled Schwann cells.

Desirable: diffuse S100 and/or SOX10 positivity.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Gastrointestinal schwannomas are benign. Long-term follow-up shows no recurrences or metastases.
