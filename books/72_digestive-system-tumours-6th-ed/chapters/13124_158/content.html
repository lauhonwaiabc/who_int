13.1.2.4 Lipoma

Definition

Lipoma is a benign tumour composed of mature adipocytes and may include other mesenchymal elements in subtypes.

ICD-O coding

88500/0 Lipoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E80.02 & XA1B13 Deep internal or visceral lipoma of large intestine

2E80.02 & XA6452 Deep internal or visceral lipoma of small intestine

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lipomas may occur anywhere in the digestive system. The large intestine is the most commonly affected site, with most lipomas arising in the caecum and the ascending colon {22298986;25368224}. Less often, lipomas can be seen in the small intestine, most commonly in the ileum, followed by the stomach and the oesophagus {28842049;28852321;27902957}. Lipomas typically arise in the submucosa; < 10% involve the subserosa. Intramucosal lipomas are rare, but they can rarely herald Cowden syndrome (PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome) {26826408;27661969;29192650}.

Clinical features

Gastrointestinal lipomas are usually asymptomatic and often incidental findings during endoscopy, surgery, or autopsy. A variety of symptoms may occur when these tumours are large, usually > 20 mm for the small intestine and > 40 mm for the stomach and colon {22298986;25368224;28852321}. These symptoms vary from mild abdominal pain and changes in bowel habits to life-threatening complications, because gastrointestinal lipomas may cause intestinal obstruction from colocolic intussusception, haemorrhage, and perforation {22298986;25368224;28852321}.

Epidemiology

GI tract lipomas are not uncommon, but prevalence and incidence have not been reported for different locations. The prevalence of lipomas in the large bowel is estimated at 0.2–4.4% of patients undergoing endoscopy {25368224}. Peak incidence is within the sixth decade of life {22298986}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

As many as 75% of lipomas harbour simple chromosomal aberrations. Abnormalities of 12q13-q14 are most common, usually with rearrangements of the HMGA2 gene, encoding a member of the high-mobility group of proteins {7670494}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gastrointestinal lipomas present as sessile or pedunculated masses, varying in size from subcentimetre lesions to lesions > 100 mm.

Histopathology

Microscopically, lipomas consist of mature adipocytes of uniform size without cytological atypia or atypical hyperchromatic stromal cells. Ulceration of the overlying mucosa may accompany large lesions. In intramucosal lipomas, the adipocytes are seen in the basal portion of the lamina propria, often surrounding the crypts. Intramucosal lipomas must be distinguished from pseudolipomatosis, which is caused by the infiltration of insufflated gas into the mucosa during endoscopy.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mature uniform adipocytes without cytological atypia.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Gastrointestinal lipomas are benign. Resection, either endoscopic or surgical, is reserved for symptomatic/complicated cases {22298986;25368224}.
