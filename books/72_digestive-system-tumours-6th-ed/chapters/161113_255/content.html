16.1.1.13 Hereditary and familial pancreatic cancer (BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, RAD51C, RAD51D, FANCC, and others)

Definition

Hereditary pancreatic cancer is caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, RAD51C, RAD51D, FANCC, and other genes, characterized by pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. Familial pancreatic cancer is defined by pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma in ≥ 2 first-degree relatives.

Localization

The localization of familial and hereditary pancreatic cancers is the same as that of sporadic pancreatic cancers {25959245}.

Clinical features

There are no differences in the clinical features of sporadic versus familial pancreatic cancers. In fact, 4–20% of patients with pancreatic cancer without a family history of cancer have a deleterious germline mutation in a familial pancreatic cancer gene when sequenced {28810144;29506128;26909576;28767289;29961768}.

Several genetic syndromes confer an increased risk of pancreatic cancer with varying clinical features and associated extrapancreatic malignancies (see #43521Table 16.23). For example, the risk of pancreatic cancer is substantially increased in patients with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome, who typically have melanocytic macules on their lips and buccal mucosa, as well as hamartomatous polyps of the GI tract and intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms of the pancreas {11113065;23240097}. Patients with cystic fibrosis (germline mutations in CFTR) have a 6-fold increased risk of pancreatic cancer, in addition to increased risk of other gastrointestinal cancers {29706374}. Hereditary pancreatitis (primarily associated with germline mutations in PRSS1 or SPINK1) confers a substantially increased risk of pancreatic cancer {9091646}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 10–12% of patients with pancreatic cancer have a family history of the disease {28726808;27899186;28767289;28246467;29922827}. Some pancreatic cancers arise in patients with recognized genetic syndromes, but in most instances the genetic basis for the familial aggregation of pancreatic carcinomas has not yet been identified. An autosomal dominant mode of inheritance has been suggested in familial pancreatic cancer {12214307}.

Having one, two, or three or more first-degree relatives with pancreatic cancer multiplies the risk of developing pancreatic cancer by 3.5, 5.4, and 10.8 times, respectively {36029239}. In addition, having a family member with young-onset pancreatic cancer increases the risk of pancreatic cancer in kindreds with multiple family members with pancreatic cancer {20068195;36029239}. Anticipation, a trend towards younger age of onset and worse prognosis, has been suggested in some kindreds {15972300}.

Familial pancreatic cancer shows a trend towards a slightly younger age of onset (58–68 years) than sporadic pancreatic cancer (61–74 years), and it is more common in certain groups, such as individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry {20068195;15516847}.

Etiology

Germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) in a number of different genes predispose individuals to pancreatic cancer. A deleterious GPV can be identified in 4–19% of patients with apparently sporadic pancreatic cancers and 10–20% of patients with familial pancreatic cancer (see #43521Table 16.23) {28767289;28810144;25719666;29506128}. Well-established pancreatic cancer susceptibility genes include BRCA2, ATM, BRCA1, PALB2, CDKN2A, STK11, PRSS1, SPINK1, and the mismatch repair genes. Less common GPVs in cancer susceptibility genes such as TP53, BARD1, CHEK2, BUB1B, CPA1, CPB1, BUB3, BRIP1, FANCA, FANCC, RAD51D, and XRCC2 have also been reported {26658419;28767289;29684357;29961768}. The penetrance of these GPVs is incomplete. For example, many patients with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma who carry a GPV in BRCA2 do not have a strong family history of breast or pancreatic carcinoma {8968085}. A number of patients with BRCA2 variants and pancreatic cancer are of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry; a founder BRCA2 variant, NM_000059.4:c.5946del, is present in about 1% of the Ashkenazi Jewish population {8968085;9140390}.

Together, the above-mentioned genetic tumour syndromes account for only a minority of familial pancreatic cancers. For most cases of familial aggregation of pancreatic cancer, the underlying genetic abnormality is currently unknown. With the advent of rapid and inexpensive whole-exome next-generation sequencing technologies, additional low-prevalence mutations responsible for familial aggregation of this disease are likely to be identified {26658419;29669919;35312039}.

Hereditary cancer syndromes related to increased risk of pancreatic cancer, mainly pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, are discussed above. There are hereditary cancer syndromes related to an increased risk of pancreatic acinar cell carcinoma (see section Pancreatic acinar cell carcinoma) and of pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (see the sections on section neuroendocrine neoplasms of the pancreas).

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of hereditary pancreatic cancer is as the same as that of its sporadic counterparts (see section Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma).

Macroscopic appearance

Hereditary pancreatic cancer resembles its sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

In general, there are no differences between invasive familial and invasive sporadic pancreatic cancers with regard to histological subtypes, mean tumour size, perineural invasion, angiolymphatic invasion, lymph node metastasis, and pathological stage {25959245}. Microsatellite-unstable tumours in patients with Lynch syndrome can show a medullary phenotype {9626054}, which is characterized by poor differentiation, a syncytial growth pattern, and pushing borders {9626054}. Microsatellite-unstable tumours also have an increased prevalence of colloid carcinomas {32350089}. Pancreatic tumours in patients with GPVs in ATM, which confer a 6.5-fold elevated risk of pancreatic cancer, are also enriched for colloid carcinomas {31285527;34529012}.

Compared with pancreatic resections from patients with sporadic pancreatic cancer, resections from patients with a strong family history of pancreatic cancer have a higher prevalence of non-invasive pancreatic cancer precursor lesions, including pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (2.75 times more) and intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm, and these lesions are of higher grade than those in patients with sporadic pancreatic cancer {16931950;19996207;25313458}.

Pancreata from patients with hereditary pancreatitis are characterized by loss of acinar parenchyma, fibrosis, and fatty replacement (lipomatous atrophy) {24525505;33097431}.

Cytology

Cytologically, hereditary pancreatic cancer is indistinguishable from its sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The germline alterations driving known causes of hereditary pancreatic cancer are summarized in #43521Table 16.23. With the exception of cancers with mismatch repair gene defects, which are more commonly KRAS-wildtype and TP53-wildtype {32350089}, the somatic mutations in driver genes in familial pancreatic cancer are similar to those in sporadic pancreatic cancer {25240578}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (pancreatic cancer) diagnosed in ≥ 2 first-degree relatives.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours

Prognosis and prediction

Screening of high-risk individuals (i.e. > 5% lifetime risk, or a 5-fold increased relative risk) for early disease has been reported, and surveillance of high-risk individuals leads to detection of lower-stage cancers with improved survival {23135763;38959011}. With adjustment for stage, there are no clear differences in the prognosis for patients with familial or hereditary pancreatic cancer versus those with sporadic pancreatic cancer {25313458}.

Patients with specific genetic defects may benefit from specific therapies, such as poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors or platinum-containing agents for BRCA-mutated tumours, immunotherapy for microsatellite-unstable tumours, and radiotherapy for ATM-mutated tumours {29069866;28596308}.
