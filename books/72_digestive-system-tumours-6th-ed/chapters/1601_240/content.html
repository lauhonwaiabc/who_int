16.0.1 Genetic tumour syndromes of the digestive system: Introduction

The recognition of genetic tumour predisposition syndromes is essential for patients and their families. Identifying germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in patients who develop precursor lesions and cancers can also provide insights into disease pathogenesis, potentially forming the basis for innovative treatments or screening and surveillance approaches. Combined clinicopathological, genetic, and mechanistic classification remains crucial even in the current era of broad genomic interrogation because the search for additional causative genes continues. The classification system follows that set out in the Genetic tumour syndromes volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series by abnormal gene and pathway involved for each syndrome, reflecting and illustrating the mechanisms contributing to tumour development.

Some of the syndromes covered in this chapter predominantly affect a single organ, such as the stomach (in hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome, and gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach [GAPPS]), the pancreas (familial and hereditary pancreatic cancer), or the large bowel (various forms of adenomatous polyposis). Others involve several organs of the digestive system (Peutz–Jeghers syndrome and Lynch syndrome), as well as other organ systems. Discussion here focuses on manifestations of these tumours in the digestive organ. While a synopsis of syndrome expression in other organ systems is briefly described in each clinical features subsection, additional details can be found in other organ-specific books of this series as well as further genetic, pathway, and mechanistic information in the Genetic tumour syndromes volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. The section on serrated polyposis has been removed from this chapter and can now be found in section Tumours of the colon and rectum, since germline pathogenic variants in RNF43 have only been reported in 2% of patients {27582512;29330307;27329244} and no other high-penetrance candidate genes have been identified. Serrated polyposis might present as part of one of the other polyposis syndromes, including PTEN hamartomatous tumour syndrome and hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome.
