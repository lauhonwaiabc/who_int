14.2.4.3.2 Gastrinoma

Definition

Gastrinoma is a functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET) composed of gastrin-producing cells associated with uncontrolled gastrin secretion.

ICD-O coding

81530/3 Gastrinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH93H8 Gastrinoma of pancreas

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Gastrinomas have no preferential localization in the pancreas {17972563;22261919;24298844}. It is worth noting that in Zollinger–Ellison syndrome, for patients without a gastrin-positive tumour in the pancreas or with only a peripancreatic lymph node metastasis, a tumour must be sought in the duodenum, which may be very small (< 10 mm).

Clinical features

The typical clinical features of Zollinger–Ellison syndrome are duodenal ulcer and/or gastro-oesophageal reflux disease {26784901}, but widespread use of proton-pump inhibitors may mask the symptoms {19693706}. These symptoms are caused by marked gastric acid hypersecretion (gastric pH ≤ 2) due to inappropriately elevated fasting serum gastrin (circulating gastrin > 150 pg/L) secreted by the tumour cells. Serum chromogranin A levels are also increased in about 90% of patients with pancreatic gastrinoma {22261919;24298844}. Gastric hypersecretion may also lead to gastric enterochromaffin-like–cell (ECL-cell) hyperplasia and, only in the setting of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) syndrome, to gastric ECL-cell neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) of type 2 {29895024}. In a small number of cases, pancreatic gastrinomas can secondarily cause Cushing syndrome due to ACTH secretion {25353285}.

Epidemiology

Gastrinomas account for 4–8% of all PanNETs {17006979;24499825}, with an estimated annual incidence of 0.5–2 cases per 1 million people, and it is the second most common functioning PanNET after insulinomas {24121170;24499825}. Sporadic pancreatic gastrinomas usually occur at a younger age than non-functioning PanNETs, often in the fifth or sixth decade of life, with no sex predilection {22261919;22525418}. Patients with MEN1-associated gastrinomas are usually a decade younger {14747767;25905301;22261919}. Gastrinomas have also been rarely reported in the context of other inherited syndromes including the von Hippel–Lindau disease, tuberous sclerosis, and neurofibromatosis type 1 and type 2 {25905301}.

Etiology

There are no known etiological factors specific to sporadic pancreatic gastrinomas.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of pancreatic gastrinoma is unknown. In patients with MEN1, multicentric preneoplastic gastrin cell proliferations have only been observed in duodenal mucosa of MEN1-related gastrinomas {15887103}. Somatic MEN1 mutations and loss of heterozygosity at the MEN1 locus (11q13) have been described in 33% and 44% of pancreatic sporadic gastrinomas, respectively {9354421;25905301}. These findings strongly suggest that alterations in menin are also important in the pathogenesis of sporadic gastrinomas through interactions with key genes involved in DNA damage repair (MLH1, PMS1 [MLH2], MLH3, PMS2 [MLH4], MSH5, etc.), chromatin modification (SETD2, MLL3, etc.), altered telomere length (DAXX, ATRX, etc.), mTOR signalling (PTEN, TSC1, TSC2, etc.), homologous recombination and double break repair (CHEK2, BRCA1, TP53, etc.), and cell-cycle regulation (CDKN2C, MAPK8 [JNK], etc.) {31072588}. In addition, MEN1 mutations lead to abnormal menin that interacts with FOXN3 (CHES1). This may lead to the aggressive behaviour of the tumours noted in patients with MEN1 {25210877}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pancreatic gastrinomas are generally large (mean: 38 mm; only 6% are < 10 mm), but their macroscopic appearance does not otherwise differ from that of the other PanNETs [[Schmitt AM, Blank A, Perren A. Gastrinoma. In: La Rosa S, Sessa F, editors. Pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms: practical approach to diagnosis, classification and therapy. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2015. pp. 105–8. Bookref]].

Histopathology

Gastrinomas display the classic well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Gastrinomas often display solid and glandular structures. The margin of the tumour can be expansive or focally infiltrative. The stroma is normally delicate but may be prominent in large tumours.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells are positive for gastrin (often focal), keratins (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, CK8/18, CK19), neuroendocrine markers (synaptophysin, chromogranin A, and INSM1), pancreas lineage–related transcription factors (ISL1 and PDX1) {19065104;19240184;21739268;23503646}, and SSTR2. Other hormones that may be expressed include insulin, glucagon, PP, and somatostatin. Rarely, a switch in hormone hypersecretion from gastrin to another hormone, with consequent change in the associated clinical syndrome, has been reported {6878103;25353285}.

Grading

Gastrinomas are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

Cytology

The gastrinoma cytology is the same as that of other PanNETs and is diagnosed as category 7 (malignant) when using the WHO Reporting System for Pancreaticobiliary Cytopathology (WHO System) {38709670}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinical evidence of gastrin hyperproduction; well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count; positive immunohistochemistry for gastrin.

Desirable: SSTR2 positive immunohistochemistry; DAXX and ATRX for prognostic assessment (selected cases).

Staging

The staging of pancreatic gastrinomas is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for neuroendocrine tumours of the pancreas.

Prognosis and prediction

Tumour stage and grade are important prognostic factors. About 60% of pancreatic gastrinomas metastasize to regional lymph nodes {3421754;22864882}, but this seems to have little effect on their prognosis {9683758;17939142;22864882;28483790}. On the contrary, liver metastases have a negative impact on survival {9683758;17939142;22864882;28483790}. The 10-year survival rate for sporadic pancreatic gastrinoma is 57% {37042078}. Recently, initial fasting serum gastrin levels (20× upper limit of normal), tumour size (≥ 20 mm), and multiple concurrent NETs have been also reported to be associated with overall survival in patients with MEN1 with gastrinomas {31401809}.
