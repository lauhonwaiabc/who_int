7.1.1.1 Colorectal hyperplastic polyps

Definition

Colorectal hyperplastic polyps (HPs) are characterized by an epithelial proliferation with serrated (saw-tooth or stellate) crypts that retain the normal crypt architecture.

ICD-O coding

82134/0 Colorectal hyperplastic polyp

ICD-11 coding

DB35.0 & XA4ZR7 Colorectal hyperplastic polyp

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Hyperplastic polyp, microvesicular type (82135/0); hyperplastic polyp, goblet cell–rich type (82136/0)

Localization

The majority (75–90%) of HPs are found in the distal colon and rectum.

Clinical features

HPs are asymptomatic and thus detected only during screening or surveillance colonoscopy.

Epidemiology

HPs are very common, and the described frequency of HPs varies in average-risk populations, but they probably account for as many as 30% of all colorectal polyps {20717107;17101316;27852795}. HPs share risk factors with other serrated and adenomatous polyps; these risk factors include smoking, alcohol consumption, high body mass index, and age {27852795}. In most cases diagnosis occurs after the age of 50 years, with men more likely to develop polyps than women {25050119;38004066}.

Etiology

See section Colorectal carcinoma.

Pathogenesis

Microvesicular hyperplastic polyps (MVHPs) share molecular alterations with sessile serrated lesions. They frequently harbour BRAF mutations with only rare KRAS mutations {17101316;23069257}. They do not show substantial CpG island methylation, and they are mismatch repair–proficient {34818448}.

Goblet cell–rich hyperplastic polyps (GCHPs) frequently have KRAS mutations; BRAF mutations are uncommon {34818448;32915145}. These lesions are mismatch repair–proficient and lack CpG island methylation.

Macroscopic appearance

HPs are usually small (< 5 mm) and appear as discrete sessile polyps. Similar to sessile serrated lesions, MVHPs tend to have a mucus cap with endoscopically visible expanded crypt openings, and varicose microvascular vessels {39524198}. These features are significantly more common among MVHPs than GCHPs.

Histopathology

Two major subtypes of HPs can be distinguished: MVHP and GCHP; however, the distinction does not have clinical significance.

MVHPs are composed of superficial serrated epithelium and funnel-shaped, evenly spaced crypts with proliferative zones confined to crypt bases. Epithelial serrations are present in the surface epithelium and limited to the upper two thirds of the crypts, resulting in stellate lumina in cross-sectioned crypts, corresponding to the Kudo type II pit pattern {8836710;12502929}. The crypts do not show basal dilatation or substantial distortion. The epithelium matures early and is composed of a dual population of mature goblet cells and non-goblet columnar cells that contain fine apical mucin vacuoles (i.e. microvesicular). Nuclei are small, round to ovoid, and basally located. Immunostains for Ki-67 demonstrate symmetrical labelling of basal epithelial cells in the deep crypts.

GCHPs are small, and their morphological alterations are often so subtle that they are easily overlooked as epithelial hyperplasia or a reactive alteration. Most of the cells of the surface and crypt epithelium are goblet cells with small, uniform nuclei. The crypts are longer and wider than those of the normal mucosa, and they show occasional branching or tortuosity. The epithelium may show only slight serration confined to the surface epithelium and crypt orifices. Cross-sectioned crypt lumina are round rather than stellate {12502929}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a polypoid non-dysplastic serrated epithelial proliferation with largely preserved crypt architecture.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Small HPs of the distal colorectum have no substantial malignant potential and do not affect colonoscopic surveillance intervals. Closer surveillance of larger HPs has been recommended {32044092;32572858}, and increasing numbers and sizes of serrated polyps, including HPs, may eventually fulfil the diagnostic criteria of serrated polyposis, which requires regular surveillance.
