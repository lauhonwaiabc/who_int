16.1.1.5 MUTYH-associated polyposis (MUTYH)

Definition

MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants of MUTYH and characterized by colorectal polyposis and cancer.

MIM numbering

608456 MUTYH-associated polyposis; FAP2

ICD-11 coding

2B90.Y Other specified malignant neoplasms of colon

Related terminology

Not recommended: familial adenomatous polyposis 2 (FAP2).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adenomas and adenocarcinomas occur in the colorectum and duodenum {12606733;16042573;17161978;19414147}.

Clinical features

In MAP, adenomatous polyps develop in the large bowel during adulthood, typically numbering 10–100. MAP accounts for 15–40% of patients with 10–99 adenomas, and about 15% of those with > 100 adenomas. Almost half of patients with MAP present with colorectal cancer and at least two thirds develop colorectal cancer during their lifetimes (at a mean age of 48 years, range 21–77 years), some without obvious polyposis {16557584;21846783}. MAP accounts for 0.3–0.8% of all colorectal cancer cases {15931596;21846783}. Duodenal polyposis is observed in about 20% of cases by 40 years of age and 40% of those by > 70 years, with a concomitant increased risk of duodenal adenocarcinoma {16557584;33130102}. Sebaceous skin tumours may occur, indicating that MAP is a cause of Muir–Torre syndrome (see also section Lynch syndrome (MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, MSH6)). The risks for some other malignancies including ovary and bladder also appear to be increased {19732775;23035301;27194394;32821650;35422474;33419231}. Heterozygous carriers of pathogenic MUTYH variants may have a small increase in risk of colorectal cancer {20663686;31094179;32362383;33059073;34244858;35422474} and increased but small absolute risks of rare tumours including adrenal cancers and pancreatic islet cell tumours {34816434;38394468} (for a summary, see #48843Table 16.11).

Epidemiology

Approximately 1 in 50 north-western Europeans but only 1 in 300 eastern Asians are carriers of monoallelic pathogenic variants of MUTYH {27799157;39497414}. Hence, in some populations, as many as 1 in 10 000 individuals may be homozygotes or compound heterozygotes and at risk of developing MAP; but in other populations, MAP will be much rarer. Several population-specific founder variants in MUTYH have been identified, such as NM_001128425.1:c.536A>G p.(Tyr179Cys) and NM_001128425.1:c.1187G>A p.(Gly396Asp) in northern Europe, NM_001128425.1:c.1437_1439del p.(Glu480del) in southern Europe, NM_001128425.1:c.312C>A p.(Tyr104*) in Pakistan, NM_001128425.1:c.1438G>T p.(Glu480*) in India, and NM_001128425.1:c.467G>A p.(Trp156*) in eastern Asia {23035301}.

Etiology

MAP is caused by inherited biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in MUTYH {12606733;17161978}.

Pathogenesis

Biallelic pathogenic variants of MUTYH impair the glycosylase activity of MUTYH that is part of the base excision repair pathway in which it normally excises adenines misincorporated opposite 8-oxo-7,8-dihydro-2′-deoxyguanosine (8-oxoguanine), a stable product of oxidative DNA damage {19414147;28087410;32307808;34142156}. This leads to an increased mutation rate in normal and neoplastic gastrointestinal and other cells, characterized by G:C>T:A transversions and the single-base substitution (SBS) mutation signatures SBS18 and SBS36 {35803914}. MUTYH cooperates with OGG1 that removes 8-oxoguanine {32821650}. MUTYH deficiency appears to have different impacts on various tissues according to the degree of oxidative stress, based on a mouse model {31505270}. In biallelic MUTYH variant carriers, intestinal adenomas can arise through somatic G:C>T:A transversion mutation of APC {12606733}, and colorectal adenomas and cancer through somatic hotspot mutations in KRAS (NM_004985.5:c.34G>T p.(Gly12Cys)) and PIK3CA (NM_006218.4:c.1636C>A p.(Gln546Lys)) {15083190;35668106}. A small proportion of cancers in heterozygote germline variant carriers show acquired loss of heterozygosity for the second allele of MUTYH and mutation signatures consistent with MUTYH deficiency {34816434;38394468}.

Macroscopic appearance

The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in MAP are indistinguishable from sporadic tumours.

Histopathology

The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in MAP are mostly indistinguishable from sporadic counterparts, with the exception of marked lymphocytic infiltration around and within the tumour epithelium.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The colorectal adenomas and adenocarcinomas in MAP have characteristic G:C>T:A somatic mutation signatures (primarily SBS18 and SBS36) that indicate oxidative DNA damage and inactivation of MUTYH function {32025018;32025007;35668106}; as a result they may be more likely to have some otherwise uncommon somatic KRAS variants (e.g. NM_004985.5:c.34G>T p.(Gly12Cys)) that should arouse suspicion of MAP. Somatic mutations in mismatch repair genes arising in individuals with MAP might cause diagnostic confusion with Lynch syndrome (sometimes termed “Lynch-like syndrome”). Sequence and large rearrangement evaluation of MUTYH to demonstrate germline biallelic pathogenic loss-of-function variants is necessary for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of biallelic germline pathogenic variants in MUTYH.

Desirable: presence of colonic adenomatous polyps or other associated neoplasms; identification of the MUTYH-associated tumour mutation signatures (SBS18 and SBS36).

Staging

Colorectal cancers are staged as their sporadic counterparts. For duodenal neoplasms, Spigelman staging that is widely applied in familial adenomatous polyposis is not appropriate in MAP {33130102}.

Prognosis and prediction

As for sporadic counterparts
