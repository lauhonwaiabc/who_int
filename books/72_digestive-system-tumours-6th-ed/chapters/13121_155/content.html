13.1.2.1 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour is a rarely metastasizing fibroblastic/myofibroblastic neoplasm containing a prominent inflammatory infiltrate, chiefly lymphocytes and plasma cells.

ICD-O coding

88254/1 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F70.2 & XH9H83 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, NOS, of small intestine

2F70.3 & XH9H83 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, NOS, of colon

2C51.Z & XA43V8 & XH2668 Myofibroblastic sarcoma of mesentery

2C52 & XH2668 Myofibroblastic sarcoma of omentum

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflammatory pseudotumour; inflammatory fibrosarcoma; plasma cell granuloma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma (88255/3)

Localization

The small intestine and colon are the most common gastrointestinal sites, followed by the stomach. The oesophagus, anus, appendix, pancreas, liver, and biliary tree are rarely affected {7611533;34596604;37584005;38313745}. The submucosa, muscularis propria, or mesentery may be involved {11979371}. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma has a marked predilection for the mesentery of the small intestine and omentum {21164297}.

Clinical features

Patients may present with abdominal pain, bowel obstruction, hematemesis, intussusception, or fever {11979371;1746682}. Leukocytosis, anaemia, elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate, and hypergammaglobulinaemia are found in about half of affected patients {7611533;11979371;29375843}.

Epidemiology

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours are extremely rare, with children and young adults most frequently affected although the age range is broad {7611533;11979371}. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma shows a marked male predominance, but conventional tumours show no sex predilection {21164297}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

About two thirds of inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours harbour tyrosine kinase receptor gene rearrangements, most often involving the ALK locus at 2p23, with diverse fusion partners, resulting in activation and overexpression of the ALK C-terminus kinase region {24091028;25723109}. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma usually has RANBP2::ALKfusion, or (less often) RRBP1::ALK fusion {21164297;27874193}. Approximately 5–10% of inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours harbour ROS1 or NTRK3 gene fusions; other rare gene fusions involve PDGFRB and RET {24875859;25612511;25723109;26647767;27259007}. Loss of chromosome 19 and PIK3CA mutations are found in a subset of cases {34551905}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours are 30–120 mm in size, with firm white or yellow cut surfaces.

Histopathology

Histological examination reveals loose fascicles of uniform, plump spindle cells with vesicular chromatin, small nucleoli, and pale cytoplasm {7611533}. The stroma may be myxoid or collagenous, typically containing an inflammatory infiltrate dominated by lymphocytes and plasma cells, with fewer eosinophils and neutrophils. Some tumours exhibit a compact, fascicular architecture with minimal stroma. A subset of tumour cells may resemble ganglion cells. Mitotic activity is low, and necrosis is usually absent. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma is composed of epithelioid or round cells with large nucleoli and amphophilic or eosinophilic cytoplasm, embedded in an abundant myxoid stroma with prominent neutrophils {21164297}.

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours are positive for SMA in nearly all cases. Nearly 60% of inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours express ALK in a diffuse cytoplasmic pattern using specific anti-ALK antibody clones such as D5F3 {34551905} and 5A4 {38155480}. Desmin is expressed in more than half of cases, and keratin expression is observed in 20–30% of cases. ROS1 expression may also be detected, correlating with underlying ROS1 gene fusions {25612511;25723109}. KIT, DOG1, CD34, S100, SOX10, and EMA are usually negative.

Cytology

Aspirate smears show bland spindle cells with oval nuclei and small nucleoli in a background of lymphocytes and plasma cells, sometimes with ganglion-like cells {20730898;29673102}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Positive immunohistochemistry for ALK, or more rarely ROS1, is sufficient for diagnosis, and demonstration of a fusion gene is not usually required.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: loose fascicles of plump spindle cells without substantial pleomorphism; an inflammatory infiltrate of lymphocytes and plasma cells; SMA, often with ALK or (rarely) ROS1 expression.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most conventional gastrointestinal inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours have a low rate of local recurrence {11979371}. Distant metastases are rare (< 5%); histological features do not reliably predict clinical behaviour {17414097}. ALK-negative tumours may have a higher risk of metastases {17414097}. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma is an aggressive subtype that recurs rapidly, with disseminated intra-abdominal disease, variable liver metastases, and a high mortality rate {21164297}.
