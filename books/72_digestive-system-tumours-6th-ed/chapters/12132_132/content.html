12.1.3.2 Duodenal-type follicular lymphoma

Definition

Duodenal-type follicular lymphoma (DTFL) is a type of follicular lymphoma (FL) restricted to the GI tract, mainly to the second portion of the duodenum. It is a neoplasm of germinal-centre B cells showing a follicular architecture and is mostly limited to the mucosa. DTFLs are of low histological grade and have an indolent clinical course and excellent outcomes.

ICD-O coding

96951/3 Follicular lymphoma, duodenal type

ICD-11 coding

2A80.6 & XH9L76 Follicular lymphoma, duodenal type

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

DTFL is usually detected in the second (descending) portion of the duodenum. Additional lesions throughout the entire small intestine are common, while involvement of the stomach, colon, and rectum is rare {21561531;21383289;30126979}.

Clinical features

Patients are typically asymptomatic, and lesions are usually discovered incidentally during endoscopy performed for other reasons. Most have localized lesions (stage IE) scattered within the duodenum or (less commonly) along the small intestine, sometimes with regional lymphadenopathy. Endosonography is often negative or shows restriction of the process to the mucosa and submucosa {21383289}.

Epidemiology

DTFL accounts for approximately 4% of all gastrointestinal lymphomas, and it is identified in 1 in 3000–7000 gastrointestinal endoscopies {21383289;21561531}. In larger retrospective series, the median age of patients ranges from 52 to 65 years. There is no sex predilection {21383289;21561531;27009323;28504205;29986575;30126979}, although slight female predominance has been suggested in some studies {27009323;29986575;30126979}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

DTFL shares many genomic alterations with systemic FL including t(14;18)(q32;q21) (IGH::BCL2) and recurrent mutations in TNFRSF14, EZH2, KMT2D, and/or CREBBP {30126979}. However, DTFL has a lower frequency of KMT2D mutations, especially multiple mutations, than nodal/systemic FL, and less genetic complexity reminiscent of in situ follicular B-cell neoplasm {24162788}. These characteristics and a case report of a clonally related and synchronously occurring DTFL and in situ follicular B-cell neoplasm support the hypothesis that DTFL may represent an FL in situ lesion of the GI tract {31371415}. The gene expression profile of DTFL indicates an immune microenvironment of DTFL distinct from nodal/systemic FL especially with higher expression of CCL20 in DTFL, which is involved in inflammatory T helper 17 (Th17) cell recruitment {24602001;30126979}. A bias in IG gene usage towards IGHV4 (VH4) and IGHV5 (VH5) strongly suggests a role for chronic inflammation and antigen stimulation in the etiology of DTFL {17601964}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most patients present with grey to whitish granular lesions or nodules on endoscopy {29986575}. Larger polypoid lesions are rare.

Histopathology

Neoplastic lymphoid follicles are mainly composed of small to medium-sized centrocytes and morphologically resemble those seen in low-grade (grade 1–2) classic FL. They predominantly present in the mucosa, with only minor submucosal extension. Tumour cells often invade beyond the follicle into the villi, and the enlarged villi may show the glove-balloon sign {30582768}. This sign is a useful diagnostic feature, especially when only a small or core needle biopsy can be obtained and much of the structural information is missing. Tumour cells are positive for B-cell antigens and CD10, BCL6, and BCL2 exhibiting a phenotype similar to nodular/systemic FL. The follicular dendritic cell meshwork (CD21/CD23/CD35+) is pushed to the periphery of the neoplastic follicles (hollow network), in contrast to the usually preserved meshwork in nodal/systemic FL {22899287}.

Cytology

FNAB does not usually play a role in DTFL.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of t(14;18)(q32;q21) (IGH::BCL2) may be helpful in the diagnosis of unclear cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a B-cell lymphoma predominantly confined to the intestinal mucosa/submucosa, characterized by follicles composed predominantly of centrocytes positive for germinal-centre markers and BCL2.

Desirable: secondary involvement of nodal/systemic FL should be ruled out, especially if atypical clinicopathological features are observed.

Staging

Staging should follow the Lugano staging of gastrointestinal lymphomas, which has been adopted by the ninth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]]. Diagnosis is usually straightforward on histological examination, although gastrointestinal endoscopy with multiple biopsies and ultrasound endoscopy are important components of the diagnostic workup; clinical staging, including CT and bone marrow biopsy, may be useful to exclude secondary metastases from nodal/systemic FL.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is usually excellent. No disease-related deaths were observed in two large cohorts with 10-year and 77-month follow-up {21383289;30126979}. Histological transformation rarely occurs {37493666}. Retrospective data suggest that DTFL occurring in the second part of the duodenum has a better prognosis than localized FL occurring in other parts of the GI tract {21561531}. Other risk factors include age > 65 years, advanced stage, male sex, and abdominal symptoms. The preferred approach is follow-up. Radiation therapy, rituximab monotherapy, chemotherapy (+/−rituximab), and clarithromycin have been reported as effective treatments in mostly small series or case reports, but there are no data showing a benefit of treatment in asymptomatic patients {21383289;27009323;29377265;29986575}; SEER database analysis shows no benefit from surgery {32399459}.
