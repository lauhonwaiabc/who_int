16.1.1.6 Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome (GREM1)

Definition

Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome (HMPS) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline (constitutional) duplications upstream of GREM1 and characterized by diverse types of colorectal polyps.

MIM numbering

601228 Polyposis syndrome, hereditary mixed 1; HMPS1

ICD-11 coding

2E92.40 Polyposis syndrome

Related terminology

Not recommended: colorectal cancer, susceptibility to, 4 (CRCS4); colorectal cancer, susceptibility to, on chromosome 15.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Several different polyp types occur in the colon and rectum.

Clinical features

Patients with HMPS may develop polyps of more than one type, but also, and characteristically, individual polyps may contain components of more than one histological type {10646855;22561515;23805267}. It is a rare condition; the phenotype may depend on the underlying variant.

Typically, total polyp count is less than 100. Polyps present in early adulthood but have been observed in patients as young as 10 years. Polyp types include conventional adenomas, hyperplastic polyps, inflammatory polyps, serrated polyps, atypical juvenile polyps, prolapse-type polyps, and lymphoid aggregate–containing polyps. The characteristic polyps with mixed histology can include two, three, or even more of the individual types of histology in a single polyp {9024286;14572586;18807306;27984123}.

Extracolonic features, such as those observed in FAP, have not been seen {9024286}. Colorectal adenocarcinoma due to malignant change in polyps is the most frequent cancer type seen in HMPS families. Although other gastrointestinal cancers and cancers at other sites are seen in individuals and families, it is not yet possible to say whether these are incidental or due to HMPS.

It should be noted that null or missense genetic variants within the GREM1 protein-coding region are associated with a completely different phenotype, in which no tumour predisposition is seen {20610440}.

Epidemiology

HMPS is a rare syndrome with an emerging phenotype. Its prevalence is unknown but is likely to be < 1:100 000 in populations without a founder effect. The original description in a large Ashkenazi Jewish kindred would be consistent with a greater prevalence in that population {12696020;20407467}.

Etiology

HMPS has been found to be caused by tandem head-to-tail duplications affecting the GREM1 gene that include regulatory elements, leading to increased and ectopic expression of the BMP antagonist GREM1 {22561515;26493165;29804199}. This, in turn, has been proposed to lead to persistence and proliferation of progenitor cells outside of the stem cell niche {27984123;25419707}. In 1 case of early-onset colorectal cancer, but without any reported polyps, a 57 kb duplication encompassing the whole GREM1 gene and part of the adjacent SCG5 have been observed {21128281}.

It should be noted that null or missense genetic variants within the GREM1 protein-coding region are associated with a completely different phenotype, in which no tumour predisposition is seen {20610440}. Although patients with the original 40 kb duplication (GRCh37 chr15:32964939_33004759dupins30 [39 850 bp]) found in individuals of Ashkenazi background are the best described, other non-Ashkenazi families are being identified with different variants, such as a 16 kb duplication (GRCh37 chr15:g.32986220_33002449dup [16 229 bp]) in a Swedish kindred with a phenotype of mostly adenomatous polyps, but with only a few polyps of mixed histology; and a 24 kb duplication (GRCh37 chr15:g.32987207_33011059dupinsTG [23 854 bp]) in a family with a history suggestive of Lynch syndrome rather than polyposis, the index case of which had 20 polyps found, from age 46 to 63 years, including 11 adenomas and 5 hyperplastic polyps, but none with mixed histology {10092300;26493165;27984123;22561515;28242209;29804199}. A 57 kb duplication (GRCh37 chr15:32976000_33033000 [approximately]) has been described in an isolated case of sigmoid adenocarcinoma at age 35 years, without any polyps being reported {21128281}. Hence, cases or families with apparently pure adenomatous, serrated, or even hamartomatous polyposis in which a mixture of polyps, or no mixed polyps, or potentially just early-onset colorectal cancer, are at risk of delayed or missed diagnosis. An inclusive approach to diagnosis is, therefore, recommended.

Pathogenesis

Polyp formation is due to increased and ectopic expression of GREM1, a BMP antagonist {22561515;25419707}. While HMPS segregates in families as an autosomal dominant trait, no second hit is required as with classic tumour suppressor genes, because the underlying mechanism is one of overactivity of normal (unmutated) GREM1 protein in an inappropriate location, and therefore, loss of the normal allele is not observed as it would lead to a reduction in activity and thus a reduction in cell growth stimulus.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of HMPS is the same as that of its sporadic counterparts {9024286;10646855;22561515;23805267}.

Histopathology

Patients with HMPS develop a variety of different types of colorectal polyps, including conventional adenomas, hyperplastic polyps, serrated polyps, inflammatory polyps, atypical juvenile polyps, prolapse-type polyps, and lymphoid aggregate–containing polyps, with appearances similar to their sporadic counterparts {9024286;27984123;14572586;26947005;28242209} (see #48951Table 16.12). The most characteristic polyp shows mixed histology within the same polyp, and these can include two, three, or more histological types as described above. There is an increased risk of colorectal adenocarcinoma that is indistinguishable histopathologically from sporadic cancer {9024286;10646855;14572586;18807306;22561515;23805267;27984123}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of a duplication affecting GREM1 (NM_013372.7) involving regulatory elements between SCG5 and GREM1 {10646855;22561515;23805267}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of a duplication affecting GREM1 involving regulatory elements located between SCG5 and GREM1 is essential for diagnosis.

Desirable: different colorectal polyp types, including individual polyps with mixed histological types, seen endoscopically and confirmed by histopathological diagnosis, sometimes over several colonoscopies.

See #48952Table 16.13.
