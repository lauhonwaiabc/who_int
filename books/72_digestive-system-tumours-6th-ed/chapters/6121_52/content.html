6.1.2.1 Appendiceal mucinous neoplasms

Definition

Appendiceal mucinous neoplasm is characterized by a circumferential mucinous epithelial proliferation. Spread beyond the appendix can occur in the form of large mucin pools and diverticulum-like growth, but infiltrative-type invasion is lacking.

Related terminology

Not recommended: mucinous tumour of uncertain malignant potential; mucinous cystadenoma of the appendix; borderline tumour of the appendix; mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the appendix.

Subtype(s)

Low-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasm; high-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasm

Localization

Appendiceal mucinous neoplasms can occur throughout the appendix, with either localized or diffuse involvement.

Clinical features

Many appendiceal mucinous neoplasms are clinically asymptomatic and detected during abdominal imaging studies performed for other reasons. Some are identified when patients undergo appendectomy for presumed acute appendicitis. In this situation, the mucinous neoplasm may be an incidental finding in an otherwise inflamed appendix; such patients have typical symptoms of acute appendicitis with right lower quadrant pain, fever, and leukocytosis. Other patients present with symptoms related to appendiceal dilatation. Peritoneal dissemination may be heralded by progressive abdominal distension, new onset of an umbilical hernia, or a palpable mass on abdominal or pelvic examination. Imaging studies demonstrate appendiceal dilatation or a fluid-filled appendix that may be accompanied by a soft tissue mass. Curvilinear calcification of the wall can be observed.

Epidemiology

Appendiceal mucinous neoplasms are uncommon neoplasms, with an estimated incidence of < 0.05% among all gastrointestinal tumours {17524597}. They tend to occur in the sixth decade of life, but a wide age range has been described. Women are affected slightly more frequently (60%) than men, although this predilection may be related to the frequency with which incidental appendectomies are performed by gynaecological surgeons {32796173;35676331;35962281}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

KRAS and GNAS mutations occur in most low-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasms (LAMNs) (60–90% and 50–60%, respectively), and these frequently co-occur {21443421;23403822;28526814;26160192;24821835;29936255;24925222;31111538;32821969;33712927;39400480}. GNASmutations may play a role in the abundant mucin production that is characteristic of these tumours {24925222}. KRASmutations are common in high-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasms (HAMNs) also, whereas GNAS mutations are more variable {31491041;31111538;32821969;33712927}. Other mutations, in RNF43, SMAD4, FAT4, SMAD2, AKT1, MET, JAK3, PIK3CA, STK11, ATM, CTNNB1, and RB1, have been described in a minority of appendiceal mucinous neoplasms {24821835;31491041;32821969;39400480}. Microsatellite instability is not a feature of LAMN or HAMN, and BRAF mutations are rare {21443421;15354187;39400480}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most mucinous neoplasms produce either diffuse or localized appendiceal dilatation due to accumulation of mucin within the lumen {31857681;32796173;35472721;35676331}. Gross perforation with mucin extrusion may be evident. The wall may be attenuated or markedly thickened and partially calcified, and it should be dissected with care to avoid spreading luminal mucin onto the serosal surface.

Histopathology

Appendiceal mucinous neoplasms are classified on the basis of the degree of architectural complexity and cytological atypia present in the mucinous epithelium. LAMN is the most common subtype of appendiceal mucinous neoplasm {35676331}. It is characterized by circumferential replacement of the normal epithelium by a low-grade proliferation of columnar cells with abundant cytoplasmic mucin. The neoplastic epithelium may have a filiform, villous, undulating, or flat architectural appearance. In some cases, the single layer of neoplastic cells is attenuated or extensively denuded. Cells contain voluminous cytoplasm and basally oriented, slightly enlarged nuclei with hyperchromasia. Mitotic figures and apoptotic debris are infrequently encountered and, if present in abundance, raise the possibility of another type of lesion, such as conventional appendiceal adenoma. The amount of lamina propria is diminished compared with the non-neoplastic appendiceal mucosa, and lymphoid tissue is decreased or absent. Alterations in the appendiceal wall are usually present and facilitate distinction from mucinous adenoma. These include at least focal loss of the muscularis mucosae, dissecting mucin in the wall, expansile or diverticulum-like growth of neoplastic epithelium in the wall, and mucin and/or neoplastic mucinous epithelium outside the appendix {19641451;12883241;7828125;26492181}. These changes are accompanied by variable amounts of fibrosis, hyalinization, and calcification that obscure the separation of layers within the wall.

HAMN is rarer than LAMN {35676331}. It is defined by the presence of unequivocal high-grade cytological features in ≥ 10% of the tumour cells, in combination with an overall growth pattern similar to that of LAMN, with loss of the muscularis mucosae, subepithelial fibrosis, and expansile or diverticulum-like growth of neoplastic epithelium in the appendiceal wall {26492181;35472721;35676331}. Architecturally, the neoplastic epithelium may show micropapillary features, cribriform glands, piling up of the epithelial cells, or even a single layer of neoplastic cells. The epithelial cells contain enlarged, hyperchromatic nuclei that lack polarity and may show considerable pleomorphism. Mitotic figures are often frequent, and atypical mitotic figures may be seen. Single-cell necrosis and sloughed luminal necrotic cells are sometimes present. #48844Table 6.02 highlights the histological criteria for grading appendiceal mucinous neoplasms and their peritoneal metastases.

Appendiceal mucinous neoplasms should be distinguished from several important mimics. Ruptured diverticular disease of the appendix and delayed appendectomy following an episode of acute appendicitis (i.e. interval appendectomy) are often associated with organizing mucin on the appendiceal serosa, mural fibrosis, and hyperplastic epithelial changes {31857681}. In some cases, this mucin contains small clusters or strips of non-neoplastic epithelial cells that can be misinterpreted as LAMN. In contrast to LAMN, however, diverticular disease and interval appendectomy specimens contain goblet cell–rich epithelium as well as non-goblet epithelial cells and Paneth cells, well-formed crypts supported by lamina propria, preserved lymphoid tissue, and an intact normal-appearing muscularis mucosae {19623035;31857681}.

Sessile serrated lesions (SSLs) lack the filiform, villous, and undulating patterns typical of LAMN. SSLs are accompanied by normal or near-normal amounts of lamina propria with clear separation between the layers of the appendiceal wall and absence of mural and extra-appendiceal mucin; SSLs are not associated with pseudomyxoma peritonei. Some LAMNs are heterogeneous and contain areas resembling SSL. In such cases, the neoplasm is best classified as LAMN. Mucinous adenocarcinoma shows at least focal destructive invasion characterized by pools of mucin with floating clusters or strips of epithelium, cribriform glands, or infiltrative glands with a desmoplastic stromal response; these features allow distinction from HAMN. Dissemination to the peritoneal cavity occurs in both HAMN and mucinous adenocarcinoma, and it should not be the sole factor in distinguishing the two.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: typically circumferential proliferation of uniform population of mucin-containing columnar cells with decreased lamina propria accompanied by at least one of the following: disrupted muscularis mucosae, hyalinization of the appendiceal wall, pushing or diverticulum-like growth, mucin pools in the appendiceal wall, extra-appendiceal mucin with or without neoplastic epithelium.

Staging

According to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) staging system, LAMN is considered in situ (pTis) if confined to the submucosa or muscularis propria. A tumour that extends into the subserosa is staged as pT3, and one that perforates or involves the appendiceal serosa is considered pT4a. HAMN is staged using the same criteria as invasive appendiceal adenocarcinoma. For both LAMN and HAMN, mucin and/or epithelial cells that involve peritoneal surfaces beyond the appendix are staged as pM1a if the mucin is acellular and as pM1b if the mucin contains neoplastic epithelial cells. Key reporting elements for LAMN and HAMN in the peritoneum are further discussed in section Peritoneal metastatic appendiceal mucinous neoplasms.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of LAMNs is highly stage-dependent, with tumours limited to the appendix having an excellent prognosis and those with peritoneal dissemination having a variable prognosis {7828125;18852679;19641451;35676331}. The prognosis of disseminated tumours depends on the grade of the mucinous epithelium in the peritoneum {11443613;16699309;34731401;39435876}, the extent of disease, and the ability to achieve complete cytoreduction of macroscopically visible tumour within the abdomen {21304299}. The addition of hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) together with complete surgical cytoreduction has increased survival {10622499}. The 10-year survival rates of patients with acellular mucin in the peritoneum, low-grade mucinous carcinoma peritonei, and high-grade mucinous carcinoma peritonei are approximately 90%, 60%, and 40%, respectively {29159742}.

HAMNs are rare, and there are limited data regarding their natural history. Available data suggest that HAMNs limited to the appendix are cured by excision; a few studies have shown that no patients with HAMN confined to the appendix developed recurrence on multiyear follow-up, although the number of patients evaluated is limited {29572977;35472721;35676331}. HAMNs that have disseminated to the peritoneal cavity are likely to behave in a stage-dependent manner.
