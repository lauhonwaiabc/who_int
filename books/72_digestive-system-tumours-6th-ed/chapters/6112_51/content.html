6.1.1.2 Appendiceal serrated lesions

Definition

Appendiceal serrated lesions are epithelial neoplasms characterized by a serrated (saw-tooth or stellate) architecture of the crypt lumen, originating in the appendix.

ICD-O coding

See Subtype(s).

ICD-11 coding

DB35.0 & XA8PW4 Hyperplastic polyp of appendix

2E92.4Y & XA8PW4 & XH28K3 Sessile serrated lesion of appendix

2E61.Y & XA8PW4 & XH0BA6 Sessile serrated lesion with dysplasia of appendix

Related terminology

Acceptable: serrated polyp; non-dysplastic serrated lesion; dysplastic serrated lesion; sessile serrated lesion with dysplasia.

Not recommended: diffuse mucosal hyperplasia; traditional serrated adenoma; sessile serrated polyp/adenoma.

Subtype(s)

Appendiceal hyperplastic polyp (82134/0); sessile serrated lesion (82130/0); sessile serrated lesion with dysplasia (82130/2)

Localization

Serrated lesions and polyps can occur throughout the appendix.

Clinical features

Most sessile serrated lesions (SSLs) and hyperplastic polyps are incidental findings detected in appendices removed for other reasons {18059232}. Dysplastic serrated lesions, particularly those with traditional serrated adenoma (TSA)-like dysplasia, may form polypoid masses that can cause luminal obstruction and lead to appendicitis.

Epidemiology

Neoplasms of the appendix are rare, accounting for only 0.4% of the digestive tumours, and the incidence of appendiceal serrated lesions is unknown, with limited case reports published {34508554;37556749}. Appendiceal serrated lesions occur in men and women with near equal frequency {18059232;24439221} and are common in patients with serrated polyposis syndrome {36827991}. Median age varies, but diagnosed patients are generally older adults aged 60–80 years {18059232;24439221}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most non-dysplastic serrated neoplasms of the appendix resemble SSLs of the colon, and similarly, they frequently harbour alterations in genes involved in MAPK-related signalling, primarily KRAS and BRAF {18059232;24439221;31111538;33712927;35121004}. The frequencies of KRAS and BRAF mutations among appendiceal SSLs vary across studies, and they do not precisely mirror the molecular features of colonic SSLs. Data from most studies suggest that appendiceal SSLs show a greater proportion of KRAS mutations, while BRAF mutations are found at a lower frequency {18059232;24439221;31111538;33712927}. Some authors have suggested that appendiceal SSLs with KRAS mutations represent precursors to low-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasm (LAMN) with similar molecular alterations {35121004}.

The molecular features of appendiceal serrated lesions with dysplasia have not been well characterized, although available data indicate that they share some changes with non-dysplastic serrated lesions. Dysplastic lesions of the appendix harbour more frequent KRAS mutations (38–57%) than BRAF mutations (13–14%), and mismatch repair deficiency does not seem to be a prominent feature {18059232;24439221}. These observations suggest that mechanisms for progression of appendiceal serrated neoplasms are distinct from those of the colon.

Macroscopic appearance

Most appendiceal serrated lesions are grossly inconspicuous, although dysplastic polyps with a TSA-like component may form a luminal mass {24439221}. A dilated appendix and gross luminal mucin are rarely identified and should prompt consideration of an appendiceal mucinous neoplasm.

Histopathology

Non-dysplastic appendiceal serrated lesions resemble either colonic HPs or SSLs. Appendiceal HPs appear as slight mucosal protrusions that incompletely involve the luminal circumference. They are composed of elongated crypts with luminal serrations and increased numbers of goblet cells admixed with columnar cells filled with smaller mucin vacuoles {4757938;20231616}. The crypts tend to be slightly more dilated towards the lumen where serrations are more prominent. The deep crypts are not architecturally abnormal, and cytological dysplasia is not present. A circumferential proliferation of hyperplastic epithelium with increased goblet cells and presence of other cell types (e.g. Paneth cells) is generally encountered in the context of appendiceal inflammation (postinflammatory mucosal hyperplasia) and should not be classified as an HP or an SSL.

Appendiceal SSLs circumferentially involve the luminal mucosa and feature an abnormal proliferation of elongated crypts with prominent serration that extends to their bases. The crypts are variably dilated near the muscularis mucosae and may display abnormal L shapes and inverted T shapes. Finger-like or villous projections of epithelium may be seen. Mild cytological atypia with dystrophic goblet cells and a few mitotic figures are often present in the crypts of the deep mucosa. The epithelium is supported by abundant lamina propria, and the layers of the appendiceal wall are clearly demarcated.

Serrated lesions with dysplasia may contain both dysplastic and non-dysplastic elements; the latter generally resemble SSL. The dysplastic components show variable morphological features with conventional dysplasia, serrated dysplasia, or traditional serrated adenoma–like dysplasia; multiple patterns of dysplasia may be seen within a single lesion {18059232}. Conventional dysplasia usually displays a villous growth pattern with elongated, pseudostratified nuclei that show hyperchromasia, increased mitotic activity, and apoptotic debris similar to colorectal adenomas. Serrated dysplasia maintains the serrated architecture of the crypts with deep dilatation, but the crypts are lined by cuboidal to low columnar cells with hyperchromatic and enlarged nuclei, reduced cytoplasmic mucin, and increased mitotic figures. TSA-like dysplasia shows slit-like serration and villous growth of tall columnar cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm. The nuclei are elongated with mild hyperchromasia, but the degree of atypia is generally less than that of a conventional adenoma. Villous projections may contain abortive crypts along their lateral edges resembling ectopic crypt foci of TSAs in the colorectum. See #43520Table 6.01 for the histological and molecular features of these lesions.

Appendiceal SSLs and HPs should be distinguished from the reactive mucosal hyperplasia that can be seen in postinflammatory reparative settings, including diverticular disease of the appendix and interval appendectomy specimens {31857681;39084918}.

Serrated appendiceal lesions should be distinguished from LAMNs that occasionally show serrated architecture. In contrast to LAMNs, SSLs are composed of non-dysplastic epithelial cells. They retain the normal architecture of the appendix and typically have an intact muscularis mucosae with supportive lamina propria. When carcinoma develops, the luminal epithelium always shows overt dysplasia, and the tumour cells appear as infiltrative glands with variable serration and extracellular mucin; tumours with pushing invasion and submucosal fibrosis or dissemination to the peritoneum as pseudomyxoma peritonei are best classified as mucinous neoplasms. Tumours with heterogeneous areas resembling SSL and LAMN are best classified as LAMN.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Hyperplastic polyp

Essential: a polypoid proliferation of non-dysplastic serrated crypts harbouring goblet cells and non-goblet columnar cells containing tiny vesicles of mucin; absence of goblet cell–rich epithelium and/or other cell types (e.g. Paneth cells).

Sessile serrated lesion

Essential: a proliferation of architecturally distorted non-dysplastic crypts lined by goblet cells and non-goblet columnar cells containing tiny vesicles of mucin; preserved lamina propria.

Serrated dysplasia

Essential: a proliferation of serrated crypts lined by dysplastic epithelial cells; neoplastic cells may show conventional dysplasia, TSA-like dysplasia, serrated dysplasia, or dysplasia NOS.

 

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Dysplastic and non-dysplastic serrated lesions of the appendix are cured by appendectomy. Their presence indicates a high risk of colorectal neoplasia {34508554}.
