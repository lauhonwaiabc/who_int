13.1.2.8 Calcifying fibrous tumour

Definition

Calcifying fibrous tumour is a benign lesion composed of a hypocellular fibroblastic proliferation with dense stromal collagen, chronic inflammation, and variably prominent psammomatous and/or dystrophic calcification.

ICD-O coding

88170/0 Calcifying fibrous tumour

ICD-11 coding

2E84.Y & XA7MC7 & XH7TH6 Calcifying fibrous tumour of stomach

2E84.Y & XA6452 & XH7TH6 Calcifying fibrous tumour of small intestine

Related terminology

Acceptable: calcifying fibrous pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Calcifying fibrous tumour is anatomically ubiquitous with a predilection for the GI tract (especially stomach and small intestine), mesentery, and peritoneum {12232572;27196478;28153506;31355265}. Tumours usually arise in the submucosa or muscularis propria {20090503;28153506;30546412}.

Clinical features

Patients are usually asymptomatic but may occasionally present with anaemia or abdominal discomfort {12232572;28153506}.

Epidemiology

Calcifying fibrous tumours may be detected over a wide age range, with a peak in young to middle-aged adults and no strong sex predilection described in published case reports {27196478;33066662}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Tumours are usually sporadic with rare familial cases {12817428}. The molecular pathogenesis is unknown.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are well circumscribed and unencapsulated with a tan-white, firm, often gritty cut surface. The average size is 50 mm (range: 5–250 mm). Tumours are multifocal in around 5% of cases, mostly in the peritoneum {27196478}.

Histopathology

Calcifying fibrous tumour is composed of a hypocellular proliferation of slender spindle cells with fine chromatin and indistinct cytoplasm, embedded in an abundant hyalinized collagenous stroma with a sparse lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate. Dystrophic and/or psammomatous calcification is observed in most cases. By immunohistochemistry, the lesional cells are usually positive for CD34 and rarely for SMA, whereas KIT (CD117), DOG1, desmin, STAT6, β-catenin, and ALK are negative {11504838;12232572;31355265}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed, hypocellular, densely collagenous mass with a sparse lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, usually with dystrophic and/or psammomatous calcification.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Around 10% of patients experience local recurrence {31355265}. There is no potential for metastasis.
