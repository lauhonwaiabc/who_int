16.1.1.4 GAPPS (APC promoter) and other fundic gland polyposes

Definition

Gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach (GAPPS) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in the APC promoter.

MIM numbering

175100 Gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The body and fundus of the stomach are affected by a characteristic carpeting polyposis, with sparing of the antrum and relative sparing of the lesser curve. Notably, there is no proven involvement of the duodenum, small intestine, or colon.

Clinical features

Patients with GAPPS can either be asymptomatic or present with nonspecific gastrointestinal symptoms, such as abdominal pain, dyspepsia, and melaena. At endoscopy, characteristic carpeting polyposis with the polyps generally measuring < 10 mm in diameter is observed {21813476}. The youngest recorded age at presentation is 8 years; this case was discovered incidentally during an endoscopy performed for eosinophilic oesophagitis. The patient was found to have numerous fundic gland polyps with low-grade dysplasia {34581903}.

Epidemiology

GAPPS is an autosomal dominant syndrome with incomplete penetrance. Families have been identified in Asia, Australia, North America, and Europe {29968043;21813476;22027476;27343414;29141268;29112017;27087319;31409086;37663957;33242120}.

Etiology

GAPPS is caused by germline point mutations in the YY1 binding site of the APC promoter 1B {27087319}.

Pathogenesis

The point mutations that segregate with GAPPS (NM_001127511.3:c.-191T>C, NM_001127511.3:c.-192A>G, and NM_001127511.3:c.-195A>C) are all positioned within the YY1 binding motif of the APC gene, reducing the transcriptional level of APC {27087319}.

Macroscopic appearance

The gastric fundus and body are typically involved by numerous polyps, forming a characteristic carpeting pattern. An arbitrary number of > 100 has been proposed as a diagnostic criterion; however, some family members may present with fewer polyps {21813476;22027476}. The polyps are predominantly < 10 mm in size {29112017;21813476}, sessile, and have a smooth surface. In some cases, larger, dominant polyps up to 40 mm in size may be observed {29112017;27343414}, and in others, a gastric adenocarcinoma may be present.

Histopathology

A range of microscopic features have been described in the literature, including fundic gland polyps (FGPs), hyperproliferative aberrant pits, hyperplastic polyps, foveolar-type adenomas, and adenocarcinomas (tubular and mixed with a poorly cohesive component). Some lesions exhibit a mixture of the above features. The larger, dominant polyps often have (focal) dysplasia {27343414} or may be adenomatous {29112017}.

FGPs: FGPs have been described in all published reports, encompassing both typical FGPs as well as fundic gland–like polyps {29112017;21813476;22027476}. The latter display atypical features such as the absence of typical cystically dilated glands lined by a mixture of mucinous and oxyntic cells and an expanded or inverted foveolar compartment in contrast to those of FAP and non-syndromic FGPs {29112017;35092716}.

Hyperproliferative aberrant pits: These polypoid lesions typically show disorganized proliferation of specialized/oxyntic glands high up in the mucosa, involving the attenuated foveolar region around the gastric pits, with proliferative activity highlighted by Ki-67 immunoreactivity. Hyperproliferative aberrant pits appear to be the most frequent early pathological findings {29112017}, and there is a morphological continuum of these lesions progressing to focal foveolar dysplasia in FGPs and foveolar-type adenomas {29112017}.

Hyperplastic polyps: Occasional hyperplastic polyps have been described {21813476}. These lesions may demonstrate elongation, irregularity, dilatation, and branching of the foveolar compartment, giving the appearance of inverted foveolar hyperplasia {29112017}.

Preinvasive neoplastic lesions: Some of the FGPs are inherently neoplastic, showing focal dysplasia. Discrete foveolar/pyloric adenomas, often multifocal, may also be present {29112017;21813476;27343414;29141268}. The dysplastic epithelium typically exhibits eosinophilic cytoplasm basally located, enlarged nuclei; and an apical mucin cap, similar to changes described in foveolar dysplasia/adenomas. Both low-grade and high-grade dysplastic changes have been reported. High-grade dysplasia is characterized by advanced architectural complexity and crowding coupled with high-grade nuclear features comprising loss of polarity, round to oval vesicular nuclei, and prominent nucleoli as focal change in a background of low-grade dysplasia.

Adenocarcinomas: All adenocarcinomas described to date have been of tubular or mixed type. To date, typical signet-ring cell adenocarcinomas have not been reported.

Immunohistochemistry

Dysplastic lesions in the GAPPS setting are of gastric foveolar phenotype (with immunoreactivity for MUC5AC) {29112017;10980114;24548295;24525509}. The immunophenotype of adenocarcinoma is described in two reports. One describes expression of MUC5AC, CK7, CDX2 (very focal, strong), and CD10 (moderate) in the absence of CK20, MUC2, and MUC6 {29112017}. The other report (of the analysis of different markers) describes increased expression of nuclear β-catenin, Ki-67, and p53 {27406052}.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis is fundic gland polyposis in the setting of FAP (classic and attenuated forms). The characteristic sparing of the antrum in patients with GAPPS, combined with the absence of a colonic polyposis phenotype, is an important clinical feature that distinguishes GAPPS from (attenuated) FAP. Prolonged use of proton-pump inhibitors may be the cause of FGPs/polyposis without dysplasia and should be considered in the differential diagnosis {27634363}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A second APC hit occurs in most FGPs in GAPPS by loss of wildtype allele or truncating mutations in the APC gene, but this appears to be a late event, occurring in only a subset of the polyp’s epithelial cells.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: carpeting polyposis with a spectrum of polyps that include typical early microscopic lesions termed hyperproliferative aberrant pits.

Staging

Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging is applicable to individual malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is often poor in patients with gastric adenocarcinoma.

Primary criteria

Phenotypic features:

·    Predominantly fundic gland polyps and/or fundic gland–like polyps

·    Proximal (body and fundus) gastric polyposis with antral sparing; no evidence of colorectal or duodenal polyposisa

·    > 100 polyps carpeting the proximal stomach in the index patient or > 30 polyps in someone who has a first-degree relative with GAPPS

Proband or family member with either dysplastic fundic gland polyps or gastric adenocarcinoma
