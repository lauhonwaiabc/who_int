12.1.6.1 EBV-positive inflammatory follicular dendritic cell sarcoma of the digestive tract

Definition

EBV-positive inflammatory follicular dendritic cell (FDC) sarcoma is an indolent malignant neoplasm of spindled FDCs with a rich lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate and a consistent association with EBV.

ICD-O coding

97581/3 EBV-positive inflammatory follicular dendritic cell sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B31.Y Other specified histiocytic or dendritic cell neoplasms

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflammatory pseudotumour–like follicular/fibroblastic dendritic cell sarcoma; inflammatory pseudotumour–like follicular dendritic cell tumour; EBV-positive inflammatory pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

It most frequently involves the liver or spleen and rarely the GI tract with practically exclusive involvement of the colon, in the form of polypoid lesions {24868227;26477709;32327214;32506505;32814623;33264138;37512154;38882325;39370049}. Other sites of involvement have also been very rarely reported, such as the upper aerodigestive tract, lung, and mesentery {33264138;33553370}.

Clinical features

Patients are asymptomatic or present with abdominal discomfort, sometimes accompanied by constitutional symptoms such as malaise, weight loss, or low-grade fever. Laboratory investigations may reveal anaemia, elevated C-reactive protein, hypoalbuminaemia, hypergammaglobulinaemia, raised CA125, and (occasionally) peripheral eosinophilia {26332157;29287022}. Patients with colonic tumours are asymptomatic or present with haematochezia or positive faecal occult blood test. Rare patients present with paraneoplastic pemphigus {33264138}.

Epidemiology

EBV-positive inflammatory FDC sarcoma is rare, with most reported patients being Asian, raising the possibility of an ethnic predilection. It occurs predominantly in young to middle-aged adults (median age: 50–58 years), with marked female predominance, although sex predilection is not evident for the colonic tumours {24966952;33264138;35941667}.

Etiology

EBV is a significant etiological factor. The neoplastic cells are consistently associated with clonal EBV genome, frequently with a 30 bp deletion or point mutations in exon 3 of the LMP-1 gene {8651355;11301353;12866373;14720144}. Strain subtype A and type f variant of the virus have been reported in a case series from southern China {27146079}.

Pathogenesis

It has been postulated that the tumour may arise from a common EBV-infected mesenchymal cell that differentiates along the follicular or fibroblastic dendritic cell pathway {28447898}. The genetic changes of the tumour have not been well characterized. Limited studies have reported copy neutral loss of heterozygosity of 5q, gain of chromosome X, and somatic mutations in some genes, mostly of uncertain clinical significance {34514557;32787485}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours occurring in the solid organs are often circumscribed, consisting of fleshy tan tissue often interspersed with foci of haemorrhage or necrosis. Tumours involving the colon occur as mucosal pedunculated or sessile polyps, with or without surface ulceration.

Histopathology

The neoplastic spindle to oval cells with poorly defined borders are singly dispersed or form loose whorled fascicles in the background of a prominent lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, sometimes accompanied by scattered reactive lymphoid follicles. However, tumour cell–rich areas, usually with a fascicular or storiform growth pattern, may be present or even dominant in rare cases. The tumour cells show scant to moderate cytoplasm, and vesicular nuclei with stippled chromatin and small, centrally located, distinct nucleoli. Nuclear atypia is highly variable, even within individual cases, but at least some cells with overt atypia such as enlarged, irregularly folded or hyperchromatic nuclei are always found. Some tumour cells may even resemble Reed–Sternberg cells. Mitotic figures are rare. Necrosis and haemorrhage are often present and may be associated with a histiocytic reaction. The blood vessels frequently show fibrinoid deposits in the walls. Additional changes found in some cases include epithelioid granulomas, abundant eosinophils, xanthomatous cells, or monocytoid B-cell aggregates {24503752;33264138}.

The neoplastic cells often show variable staining for FDC markers, such as CD21, CD35, CD23, CXCL13, D2-40, and CNA.42, with the staining ranging from extensive to very focal. Rare cases may lack FDC markers but instead exhibit a fibroblastic/myoid immunophenotype (SMA+), suggesting fibroblastic reticular cell differentiation. EBV-LMP1 protein is frequently expressed. The background lymphocytes include a mixture of CD20+ B cells and CD3+ T cells. The plasma cells are polytypic, and richly express IgG4 in some cases {23714251}.

In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is consistently positive, typically showing slender bland-looking nuclei as well as large pleomorphic nuclei.

The major differential diagnoses include malignant lymphoma, inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, splenic spindle cell pseudoneoplastic lesions associated with mycobacterial infection in immunocompromised patients, and florid lymphoid hyperplasia or inflammatory polyp. Lack of cytological atypia in the lymphoid cells and immunohistochemical demonstration of a mixture of T cells, B cells, and polytypic plasma cells do not support a diagnosis of malignant lymphoma. For inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, the neoplastic spindle cells have well-defined amphophilic cytoplasm, express actin, often show tyrosine kinase receptor gene (most commonly ALK) translocation, and lack EBV. The spindle cells in mycobacterial pseudotumour usually lack nuclear atypia, FDC markers, and EBV, but harbour Mycobacterium avium-intracellulare and express histiocytic markers. Florid lymphoid hyperplasia or inflammatory polyp comprises predominantly lymphoid cells with no atypia, lack a significant spindle cell component, and are usually EBV-negative.

Cytology

Cytological preparations reveal hypercellular samples with dual populations of neoplastic spindled cells and inflammatory cells. Tumour cells appear as syncytial groups, whorls, fascicles, or single cells with variable nuclear atypia and prominent nucleoli. Multilobated and multinucleated forms can be seen {18064686}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In situ hybridization for EBER is positive.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: proliferation of atypical spindle to oval cells with indistinct cell borders, vesicular nuclei and distinct nucleoli; accompanied by a rich lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate; expression of FDC or sometimes, fibroblastic/myoid markers; EBER positivity.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Outcome data are limited, but tumours involving liver or spleen are indolent, with a tendency to develop repeated intra-abdominal recurrences over many years. Rare distant metastases, including in lymph node, lung, and spine, can occur {11395549;29287022}. Tumours occurring in the liver have a worse prognosis than those occurring in the spleen. Based on several series on liver tumours, the recurrence rate is 29% and the tumour mortality rate is 10% {26332157;24966952}. Tumours occurring in the colon, probably because of their polypoid nature and amenability to complete removal, have an excellent outcome {33264138}.
