12.1.4.5 Intestinal T-cell lymphoma NOS

Definition

Intestinal T-cell lymphoma NOS (ITCL-NOS) is an aggressive primary gastrointestinal T-cell lymphoma that lacks the clinical and pathological features of defined lymphoma entities arising within the GI tract.

ICD-O coding

97170/3 Intestinal T-cell lymphoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B2Y & XH9FT0 & XA6452 Intestinal T-cell lymphoma of small intestine

2B2Y & XH9FT0 & XA1B13 Intestinal T-cell lymphoma of large intestine

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ITCL-NOS can be confined to a single organ or involve multiple gastrointestinal sites, with the small intestine and colon being the most common {35551151;34072328;24128129;21798075}. Dissemination to regional lymph nodes and extragastrointestinal sites may be seen.

Clinical features

The symptoms and signs depend on the anatomical site and extent of involvement {36327820;35551151;34072328;20002766}. Approximately 20% of patients experience perforation. Lugano stage IV disease is observed in 11–43% of patients {36327820;35551151;34072328}.

Epidemiology

The geographical distribution of the uncommon ITCL-NOS is wide. In the USA and Europe, the frequency of ITCL-NOS is presumed to be low, because enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL) and monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma (MEITL) represent the vast majority of primary intestinal T-cell lymphomas {21566094}, whereas the proportion classified as ITCL-NOS in Asia ranges from 15% to 58% {39121027;36327820;35551151;34072328;21798075}. ITCL-NOS mainly affects middle-aged to elderly individuals (median age: 49–68 years), although cases have been reported in younger adults. The reported M:F ratio has ranged from 0.5:1 to 2.4:1 {39121027;36327820;35551151;34072328;21798075}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Rare cases have been reported in individuals with inflammatory bowel disease and autoimmune enteropathy {24128129;29446107}.

Pathogenesis

Insufficient information is available regarding the genetic landscape. JAK/STAT pathway mutations have been identified in the few evaluated cases {27389054}. There appears to be a lower frequency of SETD2, STAT5, and JAK3 mutations in ITCL-NOS than in MEITL {35551151}.

Macroscopic appearance

ITCL-NOS usually has an ulcerated, plaque-like appearance; protruding luminal masses can be seen in some cases {21423155;34072328}.

Histopathology

The lymphoma shows mucosal and often transmural infiltration, commonly accompanied by ulceration {39121027;36327820;34072328}. The neoplastic infiltrate can be patchy or diffuse. The lymphoma cells vary in size from medium to large, and they often show pleomorphism. Epitheliotropism may be present in a minority of cases {20002766}.

The neoplastic cells express CD3 and are generally CD4+/CD8− or CD4−/CD8− {39121027;36327820;35551151;34072328;24128129}. They are typically negative for CD56. TIA1 is often positive, but the expression of granzyme B, CD5, and CD30 is variable. TCRαβ is expressed in 25–56% of cases, and a subset is negative for T-cell receptor expression {36327820;35551151;24128129}. The possibility of some lymphomas originating from TCRγδ T cells cannot be ruled out at present. In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is negative.

Differential diagnosis

ITCL-NOS should be diagnosed only after excluding well-defined primary gastrointestinal T-cell and NK-cell lymphoid proliferations and lymphomas (e.g. EATL, MEITL, extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, indolent T-cell lymphoma of the GI tract, indolent NK-cell lymphoproliferative disorder of the GI tract) {31522873}. The diagnosis of ITCL-NOS should therefore be avoided when biopsies are limited, because cytoarchitectural features characteristic of specific lymphoma types may not be evident.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Studies of TR rearrangement may be helpful in excluding inflammatory disorders in selected circumstances.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a lymphoma with the bulk of disease localized to the GI tract; infiltration with medium-sized to large lymphoma cells; proven T-cell lineage; exclusion of defined types of T-cell and NK-cell lymphomas / lymphoproliferative disorders; EBV negative in neoplastic cells.

Staging

ITCL-NOS is staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the ninth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

ITCL-NOS is associated with a poor prognosis, but some studies report better outcomes (median survival: 17–68 months) than for EATL and MEITL {39121027;34072328;21798075}.
