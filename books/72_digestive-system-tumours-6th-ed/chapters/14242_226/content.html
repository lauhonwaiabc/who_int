14.2.4.2 Non-functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours

Definition

Non-functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (NF-PanNETs) are malignant well-differentiated epithelial neoplasms with neuroendocrine differentiation that arise in the pancreas, measure ≥ 5 mm and are not associated with a distinct hormonal syndrome. Microtumours are NF-PanNETs measuring < 5 mm.

ICD-O coding

81500/1 Pancreatic neuroendocrine microtumour

81500/3 Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour, non-functioning

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH3709 Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour, non-functioning

Related terminology

Acceptable: non-functioning pancreatic endocrine tumour.

Not recommended: carcinoid; low-grade endocrine carcinoma of the pancreas; islet cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Sixty per cent of surgically resected NF-PanNETs occur in the body or tail of the pancreas {22525418}.

Clinical features

A growing number of NF-PanNETs are detected incidentally, particularly if small or localized in the pancreatic tail {34554237;35348774;38375858}. The remainder manifest with symptoms due to local or metastatic tumour spread {20664472}. The latter present in about half of the patients at presentation {34554237}. The clinical diagnosis is supported by EUS, CT, MRI, and somatostatin receptor imaging (Octreoscan; 68Ga-DOTATOC PET) {26742109}. 68Ga-DOTATOC PET specifically locates and stages NF-PanNETs and their metastases {35930017;32824388}.

The most common sites of metastases are regional lymph nodes and the liver. Later in the course of the disease, metastases to the lung and bone may occur {20664472;2153046}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of clinically diagnosed sporadic NF-PanNET is much lower (0.2–2 cases per 1 million person-years) than that of microtumours detected in postmortem examinations {31117106;39243580;34554237;28448665}. The prevalence of microtumours ranges from 1% to 10%, depending on the amount of pancreatic tissue examined {2070707;31117106}. The prevalence of clinically detected NF-PanNETs seems to have steadily increased over the past 40 years {34554237;35348774;38375858}, most likely because of improved imaging. This conclusion is supported by earlier age at diagnosis, lower stage, and an increase in the relative prevalence of NF-PanNETs {34554237;35348774;38375858}. Sporadic NF-PanNETs are clinically detected over a wide age range (10–90 years), with a mean age of 40–65 years {22525418;39243580;34554237;10556340;35348774}. Both sexes are equally affected, with an M:F ratio of 1:1.15 {39243580;34554237;10556340;35348774}.

In multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) syndrome, 30–75% of patients have clinical evidence of a PanNET, and there is pancreatic involvement in nearly 100% of patients with MEN1 at autopsy {38521}. In these patients, tumours are usually multiple and non-functioning {12210027;16699310}, though at least one functioning PanNET may also be observed {39467535;32110650}. In von Hippel–Lindau syndrome, PanNETs are observed in 11–17% of patients, and the vast majority are non-functioning {18063061;33737209;34036514;38706378}. The incidence of NF-PanNETs is increased in patients with tuberous sclerosis, and these neoplasms occur at an early age {33897589;35612824}.

Etiology

Familial NF-PanNETs are found in several hereditary syndromes. When sequenced, some patients without a family history of cancer have been found to harbour deleterious germline variants {38662083}.

Pathogenesis

Precursors for NF-PanNET are unknown; the pancreatic ducts and the islets of Langerhans have both been suggested to be the site of origin. The pathogenesis of NF-PanNET is described in section Pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms: Introduction. During disease progression, accumulation of additional genomic alterations may accompany the grade progression {22575867}.

Macroscopic appearance

NF-PanNETs located in the head may narrow the pancreatic and common bile ducts and infiltrate the duodenum; large tumours in the tail of the gland may extend into peripancreatic soft tissues and spleen. Tumour thrombi within large veins may be found.

Because of their small size (< 5 mm), microtumours are barely visible grossly. The average greatest dimension of surgically resected NF-PanNETs ranges from 20 to 50 mm, and most tumours are well circumscribed {22525418}. Large tumours are often lobulated, with a heterogeneous and nodular-infiltrative appearance. The consistency of NF-PanNETs varies from soft and fleshy to hard and fibrous, and the colour varies from yellow-white to reddish tan. Necrosis is uncommon. About 10–20% of NF-PanNETs are cystic, usually with a unilocular cyst filled with serous fluid surrounded by a rim of neoplastic tissue {25763832;27216830;28586782;30704851;31160191}. NF-PanNETs with extensive sclerosis are often centred on the main pancreatic duct and are associated with upstream ductal dilatation {20713615}.

Histopathology

NF-PanNETs display the classic well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Most NF-PanNETs are well-circumscribed microscopically, but infiltrative growth with invasion into and beyond the adjacent pancreatic parenchyma can be seen {34969955}. Most NF-PanNETs have an organoid growth pattern that does not differ from that of most types of functioning PanNETs {25441311}. Trabecular, gyriform, solid-nesting, solid-paraganglioma-like, and even a glandular pattern may be observed. In tumours in which one hormone predominates, distinctive histological patterns may occur. Glucagon-positive NF-PanNETs frequently demonstrate cystic changes, trabecular pattern, or reticular pattern {20922407;39377913}. Somatostatin-positive NF-PanNETs may show paraganglioma-like pattern and/or glandular structures with psammoma bodies {18310290}. Serotonin-positive NF-PanNETs often display small cell nests and tubules embedded in dense stromal sclerosis, and they frequently arise adjacent to the main duct, causing duct obstruction and dilatation {21292301;22221702;20713615}.

Necrosis is uncommon and, when present, usually punctate. The stroma is richly vascular, but otherwise varies considerably, from simple, fine, capillary-sized vessels between neoplastic cell nests to broad areas of dense, hyalinized collagen. Hyaline globules, typically present in solid pseudopapillary neoplasms, can be seen in approximately 7% of PanNETs {21677537}. The sclerotic pattern, as the name implies, has abundant fibrous stroma {20713615}. Some tumours exhibit stromal calcifications (sometimes including psammoma bodies).

The neoplastic cells are typically cuboidal and eosinophilic, with finely granular cytoplasm. The nuclei are round to oval, with minimal atypia and coarsely clumped (salt-and-pepper) chromatin. The nuclei can be centrally located or, particularly in trabecular tumours, polarized.

Mitoses are generally sparse; in most cases there are < 5 mitoses/2 mm2 and almost always < 20 mitoses/2 mm2. Some NF-PanNETs contain small trapped non-neoplastic ductules and islets intermingled with the neoplastic cells {15166675;16418841}.

Several NF-PanNETs have distinctive histological patterns. Clear cell NF-PanNETs have foamy cytoplasm with innumerable cytoplasmic lipid vacuoles; oncocytic NF-PanNETs have abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm due to abundant intracellular mitochondria; pleomorphic NF-PanNETs have scattered, markedly enlarged nuclei, but these pleomorphic nuclei have no adverse prognostic significance, because they are associated with a normal N:C ratio and no increased proliferation rate {16096409}; the neoplastic cells of the rhabdoid pattern have glassy eosinophilic cytoplasm {38426360;29938394;24335139;12717248;24635775;16096409;32488621}. The sclerotic pattern, as the name implies, has abundant fibrous stroma {20713615}. Although clear cell NF-PanNETs were first reported in patients with VHL, they have also been observed in sporadic cases and in patients with MEN1 {11342771;16434893;22923265}.

The ultrastructure of NF-PanNETs is characterized by variable numbers of membrane-bound electron-dense neurosecretory granules, either non-polarized within the cytoplasm or oriented near the basal surfaces facing the capillaries. The granule morphology is often nonspecific.

Immunohistochemistry

NF-PanNETs express synaptophysin (usually diffuse and strong), INSM1, and chromogranin A (usually more focal and apical). They also express neuron-specific enolase, CD56, and CD57, which are not specific for neuroendocrine differentiation {29520563}. Many NF-PanNETs, although clinically non-functioning, immunolabel for peptide hormones in highly variable proportions of the neoplastic cells. About 40% of the tumours are multihormonal {16418841}. The hormones most often expressed are glucagon, PP, and somatostatin {16418841;39377913}. Rarely, the entire tumour fails to label for any hormone. Microtumours are more likely to show diffuse expression of a single peptide, most often glucagon, followed by PP {16699310}.

NF-PanNETs express the transcription factor insulin gene enhancer protein ISL1, which may be useful to support pancreatic origin for a metastatic neuroendocrine tumour (NET) of unknown primary {32813226;30456697;23348208}. Less useful for determining the site of origin are PDX1, PAX8, and CDX2 {25433656;26491904;21739268;34048981}. Immunolabelling for DAXX and ATRX reveals loss of expression in 45% of NF-PanNETs, and this loss is associated with the alternative lengthening of telomeres, which can be detected by either fluorescence DNA in situ hybridization or chromogenic RNA in situ hybridization {21252315;33849943;39522643;21719641}. There is generally no abnormal TP53 expression or loss of expression of RB1 or SMAD4 {22251937;25441311}. Among the various somatostatin receptors, SSTR2 is often strongly expressed in a membranous pattern, and its expression correlates with molecular imaging {17873898}. Occasionally, NF-PanNETs immunolabel for CEA and CA19-9 {12395898;12131156} or have scattered trypsin-positive cells {12395898}. PanNETs associated with VHL alterations typically express HIF1A and CAIX, as do associated foci of islet cell hyperplasia not identifiable on routinely stained sections in these patients {18580449;19238077}.

Grading

NF-PanNETs are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis includes pancreatic neuroendocrine carcinoma (PanNEC), and other solid cellular neoplasms of the pancreas, including acinar cell carcinoma, solid pseudopapillary neoplasm, pancreatoblastoma, and ductal adenocarcinoma (for diagnostic criteria, see #44756Table 14.04) {34048981}.

Metastasis from renal cell carcinoma may resemble a clear cell PanNET, particularly in patients with VHL, whereas metastases from hepatocellular carcinoma or adrenal cortical carcinoma may resemble oncocytic PanNETs {16491376;11342771}.

Microtumours can be distinguished from enlarged islets by immunohistochemical labelling for islet peptides, because microtumours lose the normal distribution of insulin (central location) and glucagon (peripheral location) that characterize islets {16699310}.

Cytology

FNA specimens from PanNETs are usually highly cellular with a clean background. Neoplastic cells are found in loosely cohesive clusters and individually. A plasmacytoid configuration is characteristic. The nuclei are uniform and round to oval, and they contain coarsely granular chromatin. Mitotic figures are usually not identified. Grading of NF-PanNETs in cytological specimens can underestimate the true grade because of limited material {25493252;25044931;29044913}. Sampling with newer end-cutting fine CNB needles has improved diagnostic yields {35124072}. NF-PanNETs are diagnosed as category 7 (malignant) using the WHO Reporting System for Pancreaticobiliary Cytopathology {38709670}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The most frequent recurring genetic alterations are inactivation of the genes MEN1, DAXX or ATRX, TSC1/TSC2, PTEN, SETD2, and ARID1A; hyperactivation of the PI3K/mTOR pathway; and alternative lengthening of telomeres {21252315;29486199;33849943;39522643;21719641}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: SSTR2 positive immunohistochemistry; DAXX and ATRX for prognostic assessment (selected cases).

Staging

The staging of pancreatic NENs is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) of the pancreas.

Prognosis and prediction

Microtumours are almost always indolent, although extremely rare examples of spread to adjacent lymph nodes have been described {30003325;35699685}. It is unknown whether all or only a subset of these tumours may progress to clinically relevant PanNETs.

All NF-PanNETs are considered malignant neoplasms, and approximately 55–75% spread beyond the pancreas, metastasize, or recur {2153046;22525418;30300897}. After surgical resection, the 5-year survival rate of NF-PanNETs is reportedly 65–86%, and the 10-year survival rate is 45–68% {22525418;22503317;18065733;12039924}. Most patients with metastatic PanNETs survive for several years after their metastases are diagnosed. The median survival time after recurrence is 2.3 years {22503317}. The 5-year survival rate of patients with metastatic PanNETs is 60%, and the 10-year survival rate is 35% {23579216}.

A number of factors have been shown to predict the prognosis for PanNETs. The most important histological factor predictive of outcome is proliferative rate, based on mitotic count and Ki-67 proliferation index {22525418;35249100} (see section Pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms: Introduction). Abundant data demonstrate the prognostic significance of grade, both in the setting of a resected primary tumour and for metastatic disease. In biopsies of metastases, the Ki-67 proliferation index may be difficult to assess accurately because of the limited tissue available or tumour heterogeneity regarding the highest proliferative activity {21566513;27490982}. PanNETs of all grades are prognostically separated from PanNECs {35174558;35616186} (see also section Pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms: Introduction). The 5-year survival rate for G2 PanNETs is 62%, for G3 PanNETs it is 29%, and for PanNECs it is 16% {22525418;35616186;24503751}.

Another predictor of outcome is tumour size. Tumours < 20 mm uncommonly demonstrate clinical aggressiveness, whereas tumours > 30 mm have a higher risk of metastasis {20664472;12210027}. Small (< 20 mm), low-grade NF-PanNETs have very indolent biology {35205787;35986682;36192215;37550460}. Therefore, some have suggested that small low-grade asymptomatic cases may be closely monitored radiographically rather than resected {26742109}. The likelihood of metastasis also increases with extent of invasive growth (especially vascular invasion), necrosis, and the status of regional lymph nodes {18059224}.

Cystic PanNETs have been associated with an improved prognosis but frequently lack adverse prognostic factors and often present at a lower stage {27216830;28586782;30704851;23073325}. Oncocytic NF-PanNETs appear to be aggressive {16491376;32488621}. DAXX/ATRX loss and, consequently, the presence of ALT are associated with poorer prognosis {21252315;33849943;39522643;21719641}. Immunolabelling for a peptide in the absence of a clinical syndrome has no prognostic relevance. The histological patterns of NF-PanNETs are not prognostically distinctive in general {32488621}. Other immunohistochemical and molecular factors reported as prognostic are listed in #57059Table 14.05.
