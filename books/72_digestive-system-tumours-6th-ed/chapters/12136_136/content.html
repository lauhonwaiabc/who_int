12.1.3.6 Burkitt lymphoma

Definition

Burkitt lymphoma (BL) is a mature aggressive B-cell neoplasm composed of monomorphic, medium-sized cells with a germinal-centre B-cell immunophenotype, a high proliferation index, and IG::MYC rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

96870/3 Burkitt lymphoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2A85.6 & XA4F42 Burkitt lymphoma of ileocaecal junction

2A85.6 & XA7MC7 Burkitt lymphoma of stomach

Related terminology

Not recommended: Burkitt cell leukaemia; atypical Burkitt lymphoma; Burkitt-like lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

Recommended subtypes

EBV-associated Burkitt lymphoma (96875/3); EBV-negative Burkitt lymphoma (96876/3)

Traditional subtypes

Endemic Burkitt lymphoma (96872/3); sporadic Burkitt lymphoma (96873/3); immunodeficiency-associated Burkitt lymphoma (96874/3)

Localization

In the GI tract, the most frequent site of involvement is the ileocaecal region, followed by the stomach, small intestine, ascending colon, and, rarely, transverse colon {36120205}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with bulky disease and high tumour burden. Specific clinical manifestations vary according to epidemiological subtype and site of involvement. Multiple tumours can occur {31693241}. The most common symptoms are vomiting, abdominal pain {32999657}, constipation, weight loss, and progressive anaemia. Fatal haemorrhage, haematemesis {32675976}, jejunal intussusception {28523628;39715939}, bowel obstruction {32782017;39099724}, and jaundice (with pancreatic localization) may also occur.

Epidemiology

The incidence of BL varies widely (2-fold to 50-fold) in different populations worldwide {31388351;31924557}. It is the most common childhood cancer in many countries in equatorial Africa and in Papua New Guinea, where it is considered endemic. Elsewhere, BL is less common in children and is considered sporadic. Immunodeficiency-associated BL is more common in the setting of HIV infection than in other forms of immunosuppression {7059918}.

Etiology

Infectious agents (e.g. EBV, Plasmodium falciparum, and HIV), environmental factors, and genetic predispositions (e.g. germline mutations in ATM, BLM, or SH2D1A) are known cofactors. Familial BL clusters, including rare cases with germline variants in TCF4 and CHD8, suggest a genetic contribution {33051549}.

Pathogenesis

In endemic BL, there is a strong epidemiological link with holoendemic malaria and EBV infection (> 95% of cases) {210392;2374749;24434689}, and recent data support concepts of polymicrobial disease pathogenesis {19897039;22333947;18767045;17019706;26276629}. This polymicrobial nature of endemic BL is further supported by the architecture of the B-cell receptor indicating features of antigen selection {26712879;21245480}. Children can be repeatedly infected with P. falciparum, resulting in chronic antigen stimulation of B-cells, suppression of T-cell immunity, and induction of activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) associated with forming MYC translocation {26276629}. Children who are exposed to mixed P. falciparum infection have a higher risk for BL than those exposed to a single P. falciparum strain {33918470}.

In sporadic BL, EBV can be detected in 20–30% of cases {22260300;7059918}; more sensitive tools identify EBV in a higher percentage {28298901}. The proportion of EBV-positive sporadic BL appears to be much higher in adults than in children {25321330}. In immunodeficiency-associated BL, EBV is identified in 25–40% of cases {8392401;1868025}. The variation in EBV association among the different forms of BL and different countries makes it difficult to determine the role of the virus in BL pathogenesis. EBV may affect host cell homeostasis in various ways by interfering with cellular microRNA expression {24731550;23269792;18802929;26325594;23503461}. However, recent studies have shown that the mutation and viral landscape of BL is more complex than previously reported {25827832;25463610}. Expression of the latency pattern in BL is heterogeneous from case to case and within a given case from cell to cell, suggesting selective pressure and alternative mechanisms to survive. Overall, the inverse correlation between the EBV viral load and the number of somatic mutations suggests that mutations may substitute for the virus in EBV-negative cases {23091298}.

The molecular hallmark of BL is the translocation of MYC to one of the IG loci. Most breakpoints originate from class switching or aberrant somatic hypermutation mediated by AID. BL exhibits a stable karyotype with few chromosomal imbalances. Approximately 10% of classic BL lacks an identifiable MYC rearrangement {15034863;16760442;18802929}; however, cryptic translocations may occur {31073073}. The expression of MYC mRNA and MYC protein in these cases suggests alternative mechanisms to deregulate MYC {20930934;20823134}. Mutations of the transcription factor TCF3 (E2A) or its negative regulator ID3 have been reported in about 70% of sporadic BL. These mutations activate B-cell receptor signalling, which sustains BL cell survival by engaging the PI3K pathway {23143597;23143595;22885699;22897848}. Mutations of MYC, CCND3, TP53, RHOA, SMARCA4, and ARID1A are recurrent mutations, found in 5–40% of BL {23091298}. Numbers of mutations overall and mutations in TCF3 or ID3 are lower in endemic BL than in sporadic BL {26468873}.

Molecular analyses suggest that EBV status is a defining characteristic of clinical BL subtypes, regardless of tumour epidemiology. EBV-positive cases show significantly higher levels of AID (AICDA) mRNA expression and somatic hypermutation activities, particularly in non-coding sequences close to the transcription start site, than EBV-negative BL {30617194}. Despite this, EBV-positive BL harbours fewer driver mutations, particularly in the apoptosis pathway {30617194}. Thus, in-depth genetic analyses suggest a dual mechanism of BL pathogenesis: mutationally driven versus virally driven {26468873}. A recent study identified three genetic BL subgroups pointing to molecular diversity of BL {36201743}. A methylome analysis of BL showed that changes in the methylation pattern and somatic mutations cooperate to deregulate key B-cell pathways {26437030}.

Macroscopic appearance

Soft, tan, fleshy tumours with haemorrhage and necrosis infiltrate the bowel and/or involved organs.

Histopathology

BL is characterized by a diffuse monotonous infiltrate of medium-sized lymphoid cells. The cells appear to be cohesive but often exhibit squared-off borders of retracted cytoplasm. The nuclei are round, with finely clumped chromatin and multiple, basophilic paracentrally located nucleoli. The cytoplasm is deeply basophilic and usually contains lipid vacuoles, which are better seen in imprint preparations or FNA specimens and can also be demonstrated by immunostaining for adipophilin {22952953}. The tumour has an extremely high proliferation rate, with many mitotic figures, as well as a high rate of spontaneous cell death (apoptosis). A starry-sky pattern is usually present. Very few reactive lymphocytes are seen. Some EBV-positive cases have a florid granulomatous reaction obscuring the tumour. These cases are characterized by a proinflammatory microenvironment, typically present with limited-stage disease, and have an especially good prognosis, sometimes even with spontaneous remission {8447509;15104301;33948980}. Some BLs show greater nuclear pleomorphism with more prominent nucleoli. Particularly in adults with immune deficiency/dysregulation, the tumour cells may exhibit plasmacytoid differentiation {1868025}. These morphological features are in line with gene expression profiling studies suggesting that the morphological spectrum of BL is broader than generally thought {16760442;39375391}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells typically express B-cell antigens (CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a, and PAX5), moderate to strong membrane IgM with light chain restriction, and germinal-centre markers (CD10, BCL6, CD38, HGAL, and MEF2B), and are negative for LMO2. CD77 and CD43 are also frequently positive {15258997;10191768;21718280}. Almost all BLs show strong expression of MYC protein in the majority (> 80%) of neoplastic cells {29954940}. The proliferation index is high, with nearly 100% of the cells positive for Ki-67. Weak to moderate BCL2 expression in a variable number of cells may be observed in some cases. The immunophenotype may be more variable in sporadic BL in older patients and at extranodal sites {15258997}. TdT is negative, allowing for the differential diagnosis from B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma with MYC translocation {21718280;27247372}. Strong expression of BCL2 and expression of cyclin D1 or TdT is not compatible with a diagnosis of BL; a lack of MYC expression would make the diagnosis of BL unlikely. However, cases with some variation in the size and shape of the nuclei can still be diagnosed as BL if they show a combination of an isolated IG::MYC translocation with a typical immunophenotype (CD10+, BCL6+, BCL2−, Ki-67 > 95%) {33655392}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, high-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 rearrangements (predominantly in adults), high-grade B-cell lymphoma NOS, and high-grade B-cell lymphoma with 11q aberration.

Cytology

In touch imprint preparations of surgical excised lesions, the nuclei are round with fine chromatin, multiple nucleoli, and mitotic figures. The cytoplasm is basophilic and contains numerous lipid vacuoles.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of MYC breakage or IG::MYC translocation in the absence of BCL2 and BCL6 translocations may support the diagnosis of BL in the appropriate morphological and immunophenotypic context. Documented absence of an IG::MYC fusion argues against the diagnosis of typical BL.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Classic features

Essential: medium-sized, monomorphic cells with basophilic cytoplasm and multiple small nucleoli; CD20 and CD10 positivity; absence or (rarely) weak expression of BCL2; Ki-67 index > 95%; strong expression of MYC (in > 80% of cells) and/or demonstration of MYC breakage or IG::MYC translocation.

Desirable: starry-sky pattern, cohesive growth pattern; BCL6 positivity, TdT negativity, CD38 positivity; exclusion of BCL2 and BCL6 rearrangements (mainly required in adult BL).

Staging

Paediatric BL is staged according to the system of Murphy et al. {6986967}. A revised International Pediatric Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma Staging System (IPNHLSS) has recently been proposed {25940716}.

Prognosis and prediction

BL is a highly aggressive but potentially curable tumour; intensive chemotherapy can result in long-term overall survival in 70–90% of patients, with children doing better than adults. Adverse prognostic factors include advanced-stage disease, bone marrow and CNS involvement, unresected tumour > 100 mm in diameter, and high serum LDH levels {22260323;23913575;27136063}. Gastric involvement, older age, male sex, Black race, and no chemotherapy have also been associated with inferior overall survival {32160410;32663292}. A combination of chemotherapy with rituximab has been shown to improve the prognosis for children with stage III/IV BL {35644197}. The overall survival rate in endemic BL has improved from no > 10–20% to almost 70% as a result of the introduction of the International Network for Cancer Treatment and Research (INCTR) protocol INCTR 03-06 in African institutions {22844968}.

Surgical intervention is indicated for patients presenting with intussusception and abdominal masses {39715939}; however, only chemotherapy is an important independent predictor of overall survival in children and adolescents {37968641}.
