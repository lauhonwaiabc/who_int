13.1.5.2 Schwann cell hamartoma

Definition

A benign intramucosal tumour composed of spindled or epithelioid cells showing nerve sheath differentiation.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DB3Y & XH27Y1 Nerve sheath tumour, NOS, of large intestine

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These lesions virtually always arise in the colon, and are usually left-sided {19065103;16160473}. Origin in the upper GI tract and bladder has been described {16160473;32460039}.

Clinical features

Almost all cases are detected incidentally during endoscopy, for screening or other reasons.

Epidemiology

Schwann cell hamartoma is an uncommon lesion with cases describing tumours usually occurring in older adults (sixth to seventh decades of life), with a female predominance {32460039}.

Etiology

There are no known syndromic associations.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown. It is not known whether these lesions are neoplastic.

Macroscopic appearance

These are small polypoid or sessile lesions, typically < 10 mm and averaging 2 mm. The overlying mucosa is usually intact.

Histopathology

Schwann cell hamartoma is an intramucosal lesion and may be circumscribed or have infiltrative edges {19065103;16160473;32460039}. It is composed of a pure population of bland Schwann cells that are spindled, have wavy nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, and palely eosinophilic cytoplasm. The cells typically entrap colonic crypts. There is at most mild cytological pleomorphism, and mitoses are typically absent. In contrast to conventional gastrointestinal schwannoma, Schwann cell hamartoma lacks peritumoural lymphoid aggregates, dense collagenous stroma, and nuclear pleomorphism.

Schwann cell hamartomas should be distinguished from mucosal benign epithelioid nerve sheath tumours {16160473}, which are essentially the same as epithelioid schwannoma of the soft tissues (see section Schwannoma).

The tumour cells of both subtypes show diffuse positivity for S100 and SOX10, and are negative for CD34, EMA, KIT, DOG1 and desmin.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindled or epithelioid cells showing Schwannian differentiation; diffuse S100/SOX10 expression.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Schwann cell hamartoma and mucosal benign epithelioid nerve sheath tumour are benign lesions. Excision is curative.
