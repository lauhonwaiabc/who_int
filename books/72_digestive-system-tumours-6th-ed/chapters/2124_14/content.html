2.1.2.4 Oesophageal undifferentiated carcinoma

Definition

Undifferentiated carcinoma of the oesophagus is a malignant epithelial tumour that lacks definite microscopic features of squamous, glandular, or neuroendocrine differentiation.

ICD-O coding

80200/3 Carcinoma, undifferentiated, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B70.Y & XH1YY4 Carcinoma, undifferentiated, NOS, of oesophagus

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

SWI/SNF complex–deficient undifferentiated carcinoma (80447/3); lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma (80820/3)

Localization

Undifferentiated carcinoma NOS and SWI/SNF complex–deficient undifferentiated carcinoma are most often located in the lower oesophagus or the oesophagogastric junction {25582499;33027072;37054973}.

Clinical features

Progressive dysphagia is the most common symptom, followed by gastro-oesophageal reflux, weight loss, and anaemia {25582499}. Rare cases have been discovered during endoscopic surveillance for Barrett oesophagus {25582499}.

Epidemiology

The reported relative prevalence of oesophageal undifferentiated carcinoma NOS varies widely, from 0.18% to 4% of oesophageal carcinomas {19196264;17437008}; this variation is likely related to a lack of uniform diagnostic criteria. In case series, patients were aged 39–86 years (with most presenting after their seventh decade of life) and were predominantly male {25582499;33027072}.

Etiology

Etiology and risk factors remain unclear but may be similar to those of oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (lymphoepithelioma-like) and adenocarcinoma (SWI/SNF complex–deficient undifferentiated carcinoma). Unlike similar tumours located in the head and neck region, lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma of the oesophagus is only occasionally positive for EBV {23573354;34328628}.

Pathogenesis

SWI/SNF complex–deficient undifferentiated carcinoma most likely results from the transformation of conventional oesophageal adenocarcinoma. In the largest study reported to date, 12 of 16 cases were associated with Barrett oesophagus, and some of those carcinomas showed focal glandular differentiation {25582499}. In an international study, 8 of 14 cases were associated with Barrett oesophagus and 3 of 8 showed adjacent high-grade Barrett dysplasia {33027072}. Lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma of the oesophagus is more likely associated with squamous cell carcinoma.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are exophytic, with raised edges, and they are centred in either the oesophagus or the oesophagogastric junction {25582499}.

Histopathology

Oesophageal undifferentiated carcinoma NOS comprises tumour cells that form variably sized nests and sheet-like arrangements. The cells are medium-sized to large, with poorly defined amphophilic to slightly eosinophilic cytoplasm imparting a syncytium-like appearance. Cytologically, the nuclei are oval and vesicular. Large pleomorphic nuclei and multinucleated giant cells mimicking osteoclasts or rhabdoid cells are occasionally found {25582499}. When present, cytokeratin positivity may help exclude non-epithelial neoplasms such as melanoma, lymphoma, and sarcoma. Other ancillary studies are recommended to exclude neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) (chromogranin A, synaptophysin, and INSM1), poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma (p63 and p40), and poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma (mucins).

Undifferentiated carcinomas frequently show loss of expression of members of the SWI/SNF complex, a family of chromatin-remodelling enzymes. Members of this family include ARID1A, SMARCA2 (BRM), SMARCA4 (BRG1), and SMARCB1 (INI1), which are all recognized tumour suppressors. One large cohort study reported loss of ARID1A, SMARCA4 (BRG1), SMARCA2 (BRM), and SMARCB1 (INI1) immunolabelling in 10.4%, 3.4%, 9.9%, and 2%, respectively, of oesophageal adenocarcinomas {31906887}. Dedicated case series have reported within-tumour immunolabelling heterogeneity and frequent loss of multiple SWI/SNF enzymes {33027072;34812766;31906887}. Despite the association with high-grade pathological features, SWI/SNF-deficient neoplasms do not uniformly exhibit undifferentiated or rhabdoid morphologies {37054973;37459679}.

Lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma {23573354} is considered to be a distinct subtype. Clinically, these cases resemble conventional squamous cell carcinoma {11577322;18031397}. The neoplasms are characterized by a sheet-like arrangement of large epithelioid cells with prominent nucleoli and indistinct cell borders. The tumour cells are surrounded by a characteristic lymphoplasmacytic inflammatory infiltrate.

Cytology

Undifferentiated carcinoma consists of malignant cells without evidence of lineage differentiation.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

SWI/SNF family alterations can be detected either using immunohistochemistry or next-generation sequencing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Oesophageal undifferentiated carcinoma NOS

Essential: malignant cells without evidence of lineage differentiation by histology; equivocal immunohistochemistry.

SWI/SNF-deficient oesophageal carcinoma

Essential: oesophageal undifferentiated carcinoma with loss of SWI/SNF family member (ARID1A, SMARCA4 [BRG1], SMARCA2 [BRM], and SMARCB1 [INI1]) immunolabelling.

Lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma

Essential: a sheet-like arrangement of undifferentiated epithelioid cells accompanied by a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate.

Staging

The staging of oesophageal undifferentiated carcinoma follows that of oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

In a series from the USA, most undifferentiated carcinomas were detected at an advanced stage; the prognosis was poor, with a 1-year survival rate of 25% {25582499}.
