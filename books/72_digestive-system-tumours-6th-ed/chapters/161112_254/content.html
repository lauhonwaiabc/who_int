16.1.1.12 Hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome (CDH1, CTNNA1)

Definition

Hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome (HGBCS) (previously hereditary diffuse gastric cancer) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in CDH1 or CTNNA1 and characterized by diffuse gastric cancer and lobular breast carcinoma.

MIM numbering

137215 Diffuse gastric and lobular breast cancer syndrome; DGLBC

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary diffuse gastric cancer syndrome; hereditary lobular breast cancer; diffuse gastric and lobular breast cancer syndrome; diffuse gastric cancer syndrome / lobular breast cancer syndrome; CDH1-related tumour susceptibility syndrome; E-cadherin–related tumour susceptibility syndrome.

Subtype(s)

CDH1-related hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome; CTNNA1-related hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome

Localization

HGBCS can affect all topographical regions of the gastric mucosa {15138207;15141383;30014492}.

Clinical features

HGBCS is characterized by diffuse-type gastric cancer (DGC) and/or lobular breast cancer (LBC) {32758476}, occasionally with cleft lip/palate. The age of clinical presentation of (advanced) DGC in index cases is extremely variable (14–85 years), even within families, with a median age around 42 years {36436516}. The overall risk before the age of 20 years is low {26182300}. The estimated DGC lifetime risk is 10–42% for men and 6–33% for women in unselected cohorts {31296550;31246251;38873722}. Lifetime risk is higher in families with a strong history of gastric cancer {26182300;38873722}, with such cases particularly prevalent in Europe {36436516}. These risk estimates pertain to advanced DGC, not to mucosal signet-ring cell carcinoma (SRCC) commonly found in patients with HGBCS, as virtually every patient with HGBCS will develop these {30014492}. Women with germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) in CDH1 face an additional 37–55% lifetime risk of LBC. However, DGC is the main cause of mortality in both sexes. The risk of DGC in carriers of CTNNA1 truncating variants is less clear, with early estimates of 49–57% likely inflated due to family selection bias {36038258}.

Epidemiology

It is estimated that about 10% of all gastric cancer cases (~100 000/year) present with familial aggregation {25638682}. Increased gastric cancer risk in families may be associated with shared genetic susceptibility, lifestyle, and environmental factors {25638682}. The prevalence of HGBCS is estimated to be < 1% of all gastric cancer cases (~10 000/year). Germline pathogenic CDH1 variants have been identified in families with HGBCS from most countries and many ethnicities, although the incidence of HGBCS is highly variable in different populations {29589180;23264079;26049741} and likely depends on differential environmental exposures {36436516}. The 2020 criteria {32758476} present high sensitivity and specificity to identify families with gastric cancer carrying CDH1 pathogenic variants. Criteria performance could be improved by adding new LBC-centred criteria {36436516}.

Etiology

CDH1 is located on the long arm of chromosome 16 (16q22.1), comprises 16 exons, and encodes E-cadherin, which is a transmembrane protein predominantly expressed at the basolateral membrane of epithelial cells and involved in homophilic cell–cell adhesion and transduction of mechanical force {23127608;25925582}. Its cytoplasmic domain interacts with numerous structural and regulatory proteins, including the catenin family {23547085}. These interactions influence cell survival signalling, the microtubule network, mitotic spindle orientation {38572612}, and the organization of the cortical actin cytoskeleton {23127608}, with a profound effect on cell shape, polarity, and motility {25925582}.

At least 400 different heterozygous CDH1 GPVs have been reported in families with HGBCS, across the full coding sequence {36436516}. Most are nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site single-nucleotide variants or large deletion copy-number variants, which are truncating or lead to transcription loss {36436516}. There are many CDH1 missense changes reported {29231860}, but as their impact in E-cadherin function remains controversial, these are classified as variants of unknown significance. CDH1 truncating variants are positively associated with DGC and LBC in probands and relatives, and no evidence of a positive association with these phenotypes was found for CDH1 missense variants of unknown significance {36436516}.

Germline truncating variants in CTNNA1 (which encodes α-catenin) have been identified in several HGBCS families, accounting for < 2% of families fulfilling clinical criteria {23208944;29330337;34425242;36038258}. Most CTNNA1 carrier families described so far present with DGC only. Given the well-established interaction between E-cadherin and α-catenin in the adherens junction {23547085}, it is likely that CTNNA1 constitutes a genuine minor hereditary DGC gene. Recently identified potential causative gene variants in RHOA and CTNND1 have been reported in individual families and require further investigation {38605661;38796558}. Infrequently, families meeting HGBCS criteria carry germline mutations in other cancer susceptibility genes like PALB2, MSH2, RECQL5, ATM, and BRCA2 {29706558;34871783}.

Pathogenesis

Initiation of DGC and LBC in HGBCS families requires inactivation of the second CDH1 allele. CDH1 promoter methylation is the most prevalent mechanism of second allele inactivation in primary tumours {19223545;19269290}. E-cadherin deficiency in the gastric epithelium does not lead to a general loss of epithelial cohesion, but instead disrupts mitotic spindle orientation by impairing the cortical localization of LGN {38572612}. This defect results in daughter cells of misoriented divisions detaching from the gastric epithelium and then disseminating into the surrounding matrix. While basally delaminated cells can reintegrate in organoid models, apically mispositioned cells compromise epithelial architecture, facilitating further detachment and loss of epithelial integrity {38572612}. The presence of hundreds of pT1a foci in some CDH1 pathogenic variant carriers suggests that mutations in other genes are not required for the establishment of these early foci {15138207}. The lack of correlation between age and the number of SRCC foci detected in the total gastrectomy specimens in CDH1 variant carriers suggests that a proportion of the indolent foci may be transient {30014492}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic features of the stomach differ between asymptomatic CDH1 pathogenic variant carriers undergoing prophylactic gastrectomy and index patients diagnosed with advanced DGC. In the former, the stomach nearly always appears normal to the naked eye and on palpation and slicing shows normal mucosal thickness. Most index patients present with cancers that are indistinguishable from sporadic DGC, often with linitis plastica, which can involve all topographical regions within the stomach.

Histopathology

Early-stage (pT1a) HGBCS is characterized by multiple foci of SRCC (< 0.1–10 mm) in the lamina propria of the superficial gastric mucosa, without nodal metastases {15138207;15141383;30014492}. At the neck-zone level, neoplastic cells are small, and they usually enlarge towards the surface of the gastric mucosa. Less commonly, larger foci of intramucosal carcinoma can involve superficial and deep mucosa. In prophylactic gastrectomy, histological examination of the entire gastric mucosa is recommended (one section per block) before the absence of neoplasia can be claimed. Because all topographical regions of the gastric mucosa can be affected, the surgical specimens should include a complete cuff of squamous oesophageal mucosa and distal duodenal mucosa. In most patients, there is no intestinal metaplasia or Helicobacter pylori infection. The International Gastric Cancer Linkage Consortium (IGCLC) has prepared a checklist for reporting the pathology of gastrectomy specimens from patients with HGBCS {32758476}.

Two precursors of pT1a SRCC are recognized: SRCC in situ (Tis) and pagetoid spread (not necessarily associated with an invasive carcinoma) of signet-ring cells (see #49904Box 16.06). Strictly following criteria for the identification of these precursors will diminish the risk of overdiagnosing nonspecific changes and distinguish precursors from mimics of SRCC in situ. On the basis of these precursors, a model for the development of DGC in carriers of deleterious germline CDH1 mutations has been proposed (see #49904Box 16.06) {15141383}.

Advanced DGC in the context of HGBCS shows the characteristic picture of diffuse-type, poorly cohesive gastric cancer. In most cases, there is at least a small component of typical signet-ring cells. However, most tumours are heterogeneous, displaying atypical cells with diffuse growth and also cords, (micro)glands, and small mucin lakes. Precursor lesions are an important clue for CDH1-related DGC and may be found in surrounding non-neoplastic mucosa, distant from the tumour bulk.

Most pT1a SRCCs show absent or reduced staining for E-cadherin {15141383;17363565;19269290;27573781}, in keeping with a clonal origin of the cancer foci, indicating that the second CDH1 allele has been downregulated or lost. E-cadherin immunohistochemistry is not recommended as a pre-screening test in HGBCS, because E-cadherin immunoreactivity is often present to some level and is also often diminished in sporadic DGC.

Confirmation of carcinoma in situ and pagetoid spread of signet-ring cells by an independent pathologist with experience in the field is strongly recommended and will help distinguish precursor lesions and tiny foci of early intramucosal carcinoma from mimics of signet-ring cells (telescoped normal glands, globoid cells in hyperplastic lesions, clear/glassy-cell change of mucous glands, and xanthomatous cells) {32758476}.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates are not taken from gastric lesions.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The genetic/molecular landscape that follows somatic inactivation of the second CDH1 allele in HGBCS is poorly understood. Intramucosal SRCC foci (pT1a) frequently show an indolent phenotype, characterized by signet-ring cells without immunoreactivity for Ki-67 or p53, whereas advanced DGC (pT > 1) usually have a more aggressive appearance, with pleomorphic cells immunoreactive for both proteins {27573781;17363565}. Progression of the intramucosal DGCs is associated with the expression of SRC, fibronectin, FAK (PTK2), and STAT3, as well as with epithelial–mesenchymal transition {17363565}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: detection of GPVs in CDH1 or CTNNA1.

Diagnostic criteria for CDH1 testing and actionable causative genes are presented in #49905Box 16.07. See summary in #59820Table 16.22.

Staging

In most prophylactic gastrectomies, only intramucosal cancers are diagnosed and staged as pT1aN0M0. Most index cases are diagnosed at an advanced stage.

Prognosis and prediction

The intramucosal SRCC foci may remain indolent for a long time and carry a very low risk for dissemination. Advanced HGBCS gastric cancer has a poor prognosis. The diagnosis of CDH1-related HGBCS offers the opportunity for presymptomatic genetic screening of at-risk family members and lifesaving cancer risk-reduction gastrectomy for carriers. With an increasing number of patients undergoing endoscopic surveillance in expert centres, there is a growing need to integrate endoscopic and histological findings for comprehensive risk assessment and management {36990606}. Enhanced screening to detect early LBC should be considered {32758476}. The clinical picture of CTNNA1 mutation–positive HGBCS families seems to be similar to that of CDH1-related HGBCS, except that typical precursor lesions of SRCC/DGC and LBC have not been reported {29330337;23208944}.

·       ≥ 1 case of DGC at any age and ≥ 1 case of LBC < 70 years in different family members

·       ≥ 2 cases of LBC in family members < 50 years

CDH1 testing is recommended when one of the above criteria has been met and cancer diagnosis has been confirmed. Histologically confirmed intestinal-type gastric cancer and non-LBC cases should not be used to fulfil testing criteria as these are not part of HGBCS. Individuals who fulfil criteria for genetic testing but are found to be negative for a CDH1 variant should be subsequently tested for CTNNA1.
