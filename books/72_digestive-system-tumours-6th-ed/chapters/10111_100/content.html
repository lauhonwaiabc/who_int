10.1.1.1 Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm of the bile ducts

Definition

Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm (IOPN) of the bile ducts is a non-invasive intraluminally growing epithelial tumour characterized by exophytic nodular projections composed of epithelium with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.

ICD-O coding

84550/2 Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm

ICD-11 coding

2E61.3 & XA6R80 & XH2BV2 Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm, NOS, of the bile duct

Related terminology

Not acceptable: oncocytic subtype of intraductal papillary neoplasm of the bile duct (IPNB).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

About 70% of IOPNs occur in the intrahepatic bile ducts, while 15% involve extrahepatic bile ducts, and 15% diffusely affect both the intrahepatic and extrahepatic bile ducts {38782100}.

Clinical features

IOPNs are often incidentally discovered, or patients present with an abdominal mass or jaundice {19540556;20464560;16741522}.

Epidemiology

IOPNs of the bile duct are extremely rare, and diagnosis tends to be incidental. IOPNs constitute approximately 9–15% of grossly visible intraductal precursor lesions of the bile duct {38128439;38782100;36821179}. Age at presentation ranges from 36 to 84 years, and there is no sex predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of IOPN of the bile ducts is similar to that of IOPNs in the pancreas {27591765;27282351;38950698} (see section Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm of the pancreas). IOPNs typically have fewer genetic alterations compared with IPNBs and resulting cholangiocarcinomas. For instance, KRAS, BRAF, GNAS, RNF43, PIK3CA, and SMAD4 mutations, which are reported to be related to IPNBs and cholangiocarcinomas, are not observed in IOPNs {23828315;31678302;32100878;36821179;37871652}. In contrast, translocations such as t(1;1)(p31;q24), which results in ATP1B1::PRKACB gene fusion, and t(1;19)(q24;p13), which results in ATP1B1::PRKACA gene fusion, as well as a single 400 kb deletion on the short arm of chromosome 19 leading to the DNAJB1::PRKACA gene fusion are common in IOPNs {31676785;31678302;36821179}. Recurrent ATP1B1::PRKACB, DNAJB1::PRKACA, and/or ATP1B1::PRKACA fusion genes are also observed in IOPN-associated invasive carcinomas {31678302}. These fusion genes increase protein kinase activity, leading to mitochondrial hyperplasia, and to the oncocytic histological features seen in IOPNs {36152045;37871652}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of IOPNs of the bile ducts is similar to that of IOPNs in the pancreas {8712298;19540556;30986801;38950698}. IOPNs typically present as large (mean size: 53 mm), tan-brown lesions containing friable papillary projections or solid nodules within dilated intrahepatic and/or extrahepatic bile ducts, with minimal intraductal mucin accumulation {19540556;21946282;38782100}.

Histopathology

The histopathology of IOPN of the bile ducts is similar to that of IOPNs in the pancreas {8712298;19540556;30986801;38950698}. Microscopically, the tumours form thick, complex, and arborizing papillae with delicate, myxoid, fibrovascular stroma. The papillae are lined by few layers of cuboidal to columnar cells with abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm; a large, round nucleus; and a prominent nucleolus {12412172;36821179}. The tumour cells may form cribriform structures with intraepithelial lumina containing mucin. Interspersed goblet cells are also commonly observed. The epithelium of adjacent papillae may fuse, producing a solid growth pattern. Occasionally, tumour cells with oncocytic cytoplasm are mixed with gastric foveolar–like tall columnar epithelial cells containing subnuclear oncocytic cytoplasm {38841868;37871652}. Intense stromal and intraepithelial infiltration by lymphocytes or neutrophils may also be observed. All IOPNs have high-grade dysplasia, defined by the architectural complexity and the degree of nuclear atypia.

Approximately 30–40% of IOPNs may show progression to invasive carcinoma {19540556;38782100}. The invasive carcinomas developing from IOPN are predominantly composed of small infiltrating tubules or solid nests of oncocytic cells. On rare occasions, the invasive carcinoma contains abundant stromal mucin accumulation mimicking colloid carcinoma {38782100}. Lymphovascular and perineural invasion as well as lymph node metastasis are also observed in similar frequency as in adenocarcinoma derived from intraductal papillary neoplasm of the bile ducts (IPNBs) {38782100}.

Immunohistochemically, IOPNs diffusely label for MUC5AC and MUC6 and focally label for MUC1, whereas MUC2 expression is largely restricted to goblet cells {20139757;22634127;35141839;16741522;23828315;36821179}. The mucin protein expression profiles of IOPNs of the bile ducts and pancreas are similar {28550497}. Additionally, there is consistent immunoreactivity for HepPar1 and KIT (CD117) {19540556;38782100}.

IOPN should be distinguished from IPNBs, particularly the pancreatobiliary subtype. The complex cribriform structures composed of intensely oncocytic cells and diffuse MUC6 expression are the characteristic features of IOPN.

Cytology

Unique cytological features of IOPN include sheets or papillary clusters of cuboidal or columnar cells with abundant granular cytoplasm, well-defined cell borders, large central nuclei, prominent eccentric nucleoli, and intercellular punched-out spaces {24264957;26415076}. IOPNs have significantly less intracellular mucin and produce less extracellular colloid-like mucin – features typically seen in IPNBs {23828315;26415076}. However, the high-grade dysplasia observed in IOPNs does not necessarily indicate the presence of invasive carcinoma.

The cellularity and complex tubular architecture of IOPNs, along with the paucity of mucin, may lead to their misdiagnosis as cholangiocarcinoma on cytological samples {24264957}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The diagnosis of IOPN of the bile ducts is based on imaging and histology and does not require molecular confirmation. However, identifying ATP1B1::PRKACB, DNAJB1::PRKACA, or ATP1B1::PRKACA rearrangements helps support the diagnosis of IOPN in selected cases (e.g. small biopsies) {31676785;31678302;37871652;38841868}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a non-invasive complex arborizing papillary neoplasm with oncocytic epithelial features; located within a dilated duct.

Desirable: immunolabelling for HepPar1.

Staging

IOPNs with high-grade dysplasia are staged as Tis. Staging of invasive carcinomas developing from IOPNs, depending on their location, follows the classifications for intrahepatic, perihilar, or distal cholangiocarcinoma [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

In invasive carcinomas developing from IOPNs of the bile ducts (in ~30–40% of cases) {19540556;38782100}, patient survival is better compared with that in invasive carcinomas developing from IPNBs {38782100}.
