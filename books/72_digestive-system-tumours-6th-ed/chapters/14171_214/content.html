14.1.7.1 Anal neuroendocrine neoplasms

Definition

Anal neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) are malignant epithelial neoplasms with neuroendocrine differentiation that arise in the anal canal.

Related terminology

Acceptable: well-differentiated endocrine tumour/neoplasm; poorly differentiated endocrine carcinoma; mixed exocrine–endocrine carcinoma.

Not recommended: carcinoid; composite carcinoid-adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (80130/3); small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (80410/3); mixed large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–adenocarcinoma (80132/3); mixed large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–squamous cell carcinoma (80133/3); mixed small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–adenocarcinoma (80451/3); mixed small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma–squamous cell carcinoma (80453/3); amphicrine-like carcinoma (85743/3); amphicrine-like adenocarcinoma (85744/3); amphicrine-like squamous cell carcinoma (85745/3)

Localization

NENs can occur in any portion of the anal canal.

Clinical features

Anal neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) is frequently diagnosed at advanced stages {34104815;32854635;24412088}. Patients may present with symptoms such as anal pain, discomfort, or bleeding {28270879}. Tumour-induced obstruction of the anal canal can lead to constipation {26198995}. On endoscopic examination, a mass or ulcer may be evident. Common sites of distant metastases include the liver, lungs, and bones {33551318;26237193}.

Epidemiology

Anal NENs are exceedingly rare, accounting for approximately 1% of all anal cancers in case series {10223561;29755775}. In the anal canal, small cell NECs (SCNECs) and large cell NECs (LCNECs) are far more common than primary well-differentiated NETs, which appear to be exceptionally rare {29755775;33551318}. NEC of the anal canal appears to be slightly more common in women {22531171;33551318;34104815}. Most NECs occur between the fourth and seventh decades of life {22531171;33551318;34104815}, although sporadic cases of young-onset NEC {32854635} and mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasm (MiNEN, previously known as MANEC) {34548300} have been reported.

Etiology

The etiology of anal NENs remains largely unknown. SCNEC of the anal canal has been associated with HIV infection {8384908;23730521;17279348}. Similar to squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), HPV infection is also considered a potential etiological factor {22531171;22524667;32854635}.

Pathogenesis

Neuroendocrine cells are present in the basal layer of the anal transitional zone mucosa as well as in rectal-type mucosa. Little is known about the development of NENs in the anal canal; however, a proportion of NECs are observed in association with SCC or adenocarcinoma in situ. There has been a reported case of NEC coexisting with squamous intraepithelial neoplasia in an 80-year-old woman; both elements were positive for HPV18 {22524667}.

To date, no large-scale genetic profiling studies have been conducted, apart from a few molecular case studies. Next-generation sequencing analyses of HPV-related anal NEC in isolated cases have identified a PIK3CA mutation. Another molecular case study reported amplifications of CCND1, FGF19, FGF3, and FGF4, along with mutations in KRAS and TP53 {32854635;39401914}.

Macroscopic appearance

NECs of the anal canal often presents as an irregular mass with depressions and ulcers.

Histopathology

NET

Anal NETs display the classic well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Anal NETs typically exhibit a nesting or trabecular pattern, with cells displaying granular cytoplasm {27889033}.

NEC

Anal NECs display the classic poorly differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage, either small or large cell type [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Anal NEC may present with concurrent mucosal adenocarcinoma or squamous dysplasia/carcinoma in situ {23730521;22524667}. Anal NECs are poorly differentiated cancers with a non-organoid, diffuse architecture, marked cellular pleomorphism, and brisk mitotic activity (usually exhibiting a very high Ki-67 proliferation index). SCNEC has small tumour cells with a high N:C ratio and nuclear moulding. LCNEC is composed of large tumour cells with large nuclei with vesicular chromatin and prominent nucleoli and amphophilic or eosinophilic cytoplasm. Pagetoid spread to the anal epithelium has been reported in LCNEC {15260855}. NECs of the anal canal show a variable positivity for neuroendocrine markers such as synaptophysin, chromogranin A and INSM1.

MiNEN

Anal MiNENs display the classic morphological features of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine neoplasia [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. MiNENs are rare in the anal canal and most often consist of NEC and adenocarcinoma {21461890;24412088;34548300;32480333}. MiNENs consisting of SCNEC and SCC can also occur.

Amphicrine-like carcinoma

Anal amphicrine-like carcinoma may occur as amphicrine-like adenocarcinoma with goblet cell features {37083794;34548300;32683504}.

Grading

Anal NENs are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

Differential diagnosis

Due to the rarity of primary anal NET, infiltration of NET from other sites, especially a rectal or presacral {34690926} NET, should be excluded before making the diagnosis.

Differentiation of NEC and MiNEN from other malignancies such as poorly differentiated / basaloid SCC, poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, melanoma, and basal cell carcinoma of the perianal skin is critical. Immunohistochemical markers (e.g. chromogranin A, synaptophysin, and INSM1) confirm the diagnosis of NEC or a NEC component in MiNEN {27889037}, if a compatible H&E morphology is present. Diffuse CD56 expression may occur in anal basaloid SCC {33989959}, but positive p63/p40 staining distinguishes basaloid SCC from NEC.

Anal NEC may express TTF1 {26198995}. Therefore, TTF1 (and other transcription factors) cannot be reliably used to differentiate anal NEC from metastatic NECs from other sites {31857137}.

Merkel cell carcinoma may occur in the anal canal {12792446;22329967}. Similar to NEC, Merkel cell carcinoma variably expresses neuroendocrine markers. However, unlike SCNEC, classic polyomavirus-positive Merkel cell carcinoma shows perinuclear dot-like staining for CK20 and is negative for TTF1 {30067951}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

As in other organs, the immunohistochemical loss of RB1 and diffuse overexpression or loss of p53 by immunohistochemical staining can support the diagnosis of (NEC) in morphologically challenging cases (e.g. those with minimal expression of neuroendocrine markers). However, a TP53/RB1 wildtype status does not rule out the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

NET

Essential: well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: SSTR2 expression; positive immunohistochemistry for hormones (e.g. serotonin, GLP-1).

NEC

Essential: poorly differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage with either small cell or large cell carcinoma pattern; sheets or trabeculae of poorly differentiated cells; neuroendocrine marker expression; high Ki-67 proliferation index and or mitotic count.

Desirable: reduced/absent SSTR2 expression; loss of RB1 expression or abnormal p53 expression.

MiNEN

Essential: a mixed tumour composed of two morphologically recognizable components; either type ≥ 30% of the neoplasm; neuroendocrine marker expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Desirable: assessment of Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count to grade the NET component or to prognostically stratify the NEC component; grading of the non-neuroendocrine component.

Amphicrine-like carcinoma

Essential: a mixed tumour with no morphologically distinct zones of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine differentiation; ≥ 30% of the neoplasm; neuroendocrine marker expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Desirable: assessment of Ki-67 proliferation index.

Staging

There is no TNM staging system specific for anal NET. The staging is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for anal carcinoma, similar to NEC, MiNEN, and amphicrine-like carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Anal NECs are highly malignant neoplasms with a poor prognosis {15043285}. In a recent report {33551318} including a total of 9 patients with NEC of the anal canal or anorectum, 5 patients presented with locoregional disease at diagnosis, while the remaining 4 had metastatic disease. Despite multimodal therapy, including chemotherapy, the progression-free survival time was an average of 7.3 months (range: 2.1–12.7 months) and the overall survival time was an average of 18.1 months (range: 7.1–25.5 months). However, there are case reports of anal NEC achieving long-term remission with chemoradiotherapy and/or surgery {34104815;32854635}.
