9.1.3.1 Bile duct adenoma

Definition

Bile duct adenoma (BDA) is a benign nodular lesion composed of a proliferation of small bile ducts.

ICD-O coding

81600/0 Bile duct adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2E92.7 & XH6KR6 Bile duct adenoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: peribiliary gland hamartoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

BDA is most commonly identified as a solitary subcapsular nodule (90%), but it can develop in any part of the liver.

Clinical features

BDA is frequently an incidental finding during surgery, at autopsy, or by a pathological examination of liver specimens resected for other reasons. It is sometimes submitted for frozen section diagnosis during surgery for exclusion of metastatic carcinoma.

Epidemiology

The frequency of BDA was 0.6% in a large autopsy study {205188} and 2.4% in a large resection cohort {25329860}. It is mostly found in adults and shows no specific sex predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The origin and pathogenesis of BDA are discussed controversially. Early investigators proposed that BDA is a reactive process to a focal injury, possibly postinflammatory or traumatic {3046396}. A later study suggested that BDA is a peribiliary gland hamartoma {8669534}; however, this has been questioned because of the common subcapsular localization and rare occurrence in children. More recently, BRAF p.Val600Glu (V600E) mutations were detected in a high proportion of cases (8 of 15 cases, 53%), suggesting that at least some BDAs are true neoplasms {24634053;25704541}.

Macroscopic appearance

BDA is a white, firm, well-circumscribed but unencapsulated nodule, usually < 10 mm (range: 1–20 mm). Multiple BDAs may occur.

Histopathology

BDA is composed of a disordered collection of uniform small tubules in a connective tissue stroma without infiltrative or pushing margin. The tubules have no or little lumen and are formed by cuboidal cells that have regular nuclei, resembling ductules. The tumour-associated stroma shows varying degrees of chronic inflammation and collagenization. Portal tracts are typically enclosed in the nodule. The cytokeratin immunophenotype of the ductal epithelium is identical to that of normal bile ducts, being positive for CK7 and CK19. Some BDAs can express mucin and have more collagenized stroma.

Clear cell and oncocytic patterns have been described {11420469;16918984}. Oncocytic BDA shows abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, central round nuclei, and dilated neoplastic tubules.

The main clinical significance of BDA resides in its possible confusion with metastatic adenocarcinoma, especially during intraoperative consultation. Small duct intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, particularly the cholangiolocellular subtype, can have very well differentiated small ductular structures with only mild atypia, but these entities can be distinguished on the basis of tumour size, cytoarchitectural patterns, and invasive growth. Immunostaining for Ki-67, p53, EZH2, and p16 (p16INK4a) may be helpful {27396933;24487593}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is low in BDA (average: 2%, never > 10%), and strong p53 staining is not seen. EZH2 is expressed in carcinomas but not BDAs; p16 (p16INK4a) is expressed in BDAs but not carcinomas. Like small duct intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, BDA can be positive for C-reactive protein and albumin in situ hybridization {26841202}.

BDA should also be distinguished from other benign biliary conditions. Reactive ductular proliferation is usually not circumscribed or nodular in configuration. However, in cirrhosis, ductular reaction can replace the hepatocytic nodule (parenchymal extinction in cirrhotic nodules) and form a vague nodule. Von Meyenburg complex and biliary adenofibroma are also mimics. However, BDA typically lacks cystic changes and intraluminal bile.

Cases of malignant transformation of BDA are reported but remain questionable, because the cases exhibited atypical features, such as larger size {22326450} and microcystic change {7757293}.

For more detailed information about the differential diagnosis of solid-microcystic glandular lesions of the liver, see#48807Table 9.16 in section Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, small duct.

Cytology

Cytology is not suitable for the diagnosis of BDA.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a small nodular proliferation of benign small bile ducts with frequent inclusion of portal tracts; absence of infiltrative or pushing margin.

Desirable: may have fibrotic stroma with chronic inflammation; expression of CK7 and CK19.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Malignant transformation is not documented for classic BDA.
