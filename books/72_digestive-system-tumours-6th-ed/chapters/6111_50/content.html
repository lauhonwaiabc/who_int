6.1.1.1 Conventional appendiceal adenoma

Definition

Conventional appendiceal adenoma is a benign neoplasm with conventional intestinal dysplasia, originating in the appendix.

ICD-O coding

81441/0 Adenoma, intestinal type, low-grade

81441/2 Adenoma, intestinal type, high-grade

ICD-11 coding

2E92.4Y & XA8PW4 & XH1VG3 Adenoma, intestinal type, low-grade, of appendix

2E61.Y & XA8PW4 & XH6X02 Adenoma, intestinal type, high-grade, of appendix

Related terminology

Acceptable: appendiceal adenoma; tubular adenoma; villous adenoma; tubulovillous adenoma.

Subtype(s)

Tubular adenoma, low-grade (82111/0); tubular adenoma, high-grade (82111/2); villous adenoma, low-grade (82611/0); villous adenoma, high-grade (82611/2); tubulovillous adenoma, low-grade (82632/0); tubulovillous adenoma, high-grade (82632/2)

Localization

These tumours are localized to the appendix proper and should be distinguished from adenomas of the caecum that extend into the appendiceal orifice or proximal appendix.

Clinical features

Conventional appendiceal adenomas are almost always asymptomatic and are discovered during the histological evaluation of surgical resection specimens obtained for other reasons. These lesions may present with appendicitis or, in rare instances, symptoms related to intussusception {39020129;21477328}. Rarely, large lesions may cause distal dilatation of the appendix and produce a radiographically apparent mass.

Epidemiology

Conventional appendiceal adenomas are uncommon, with an incidence ranging from 0.03% to 0.06% in appendectomy specimens obtained for indications of acute appendicitis {39020129}.

Etiology

Conventional appendiceal adenomas may arise sporadically or in association with heritable syndromes, particularly familial adenomatous polyposis.

Pathogenesis

Conventional adenomas of the appendix frequently have KRAS mutations accompanied by mutations in APC or RNF43, reflecting activation of the WNT signalling pathway {31111538}. Some cases, especially those with high-grade dysplasia (HGD), harbour TP53 mutations. Conventional adenomas can progress to appendiceal adenocarcinoma through an adenoma–carcinoma sequence analogous to colorectal adenocarcinomas derived from adenomas {36454563}.

Macroscopic appearance

Conventional appendiceal adenomas are often small, and most are not prospectively identified at the time of macroscopic examination. However, large polyps may appear as pedunculated, sessile, flat, or depressed lesions.

Histopathology

Like conventional colorectal adenomas, those of the appendix exhibit tubular, tubulovillous, or villous architecture. Enlarged crypts expand the mucosa and show crypt-to-crypt variability in size and shape. The epithelium consists of columnar cells with minimal or no cytoplasmic mucin, accompanied by occasional goblet cells. Neoplastic cells contain elongated, hyperchromatic nuclei with readily identifiable mitotic figures and scattered apoptotic cellular debris. Dysplasia is graded using a two-tiered classification system based on architectural complexity and cytological atypia. Adenomas with low-grade dysplasia lack architectural complexity and contain elongated, pseudostratified nuclei located near the basement membrane. Adenomas with HGD generally show architectural complexity characterized by somewhat papillary or cribriform growth, as well as high-grade cytological abnormalities with loss of cell polarity and nuclear stratification. The nuclei may be round with open chromatin and prominent nucleoli. Conventional adenomas of the appendix should be distinguished from mucinous neoplasms. Although conventional adenomas contain decreased amounts of lamina propria compared with normal appendiceal mucosa, they lack the mural changes of a mucinous neoplasm, such as atrophy of lymphoid tissue, fibromuscularization of the submucosa, loss of tissue planes between wall layers, and hyalinization of the muscularis propria. Adenomas are not associated with mucin pools in or around the appendix and are not precursors to low- or high-grade mucinous carcinoma peritonei (i.e. pseudomyxoma peritonei).

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: presence of a villous or tubular glandular neoplasm with conventional dysplasia but absence of invasion.

Staging

Adenomas with HGD are classified as pTis.

Prognosis and prediction

Conventional appendiceal adenomas are benign neoplasms. There is insufficient evidence to determine whether the risk of developing invasive carcinoma is associated with size, the proportion of villous component, or the presence of HGD.
