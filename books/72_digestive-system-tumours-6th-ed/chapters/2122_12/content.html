2.1.2.2 Oesophageal mucoepidermoid, adenoid cystic, and adenosquamous carcinomas

Definition

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma is a malignant tumour composed of a variable mixture of malignant mucous cells, epidermoid cells, and intermediate cells, resembling its salivary gland counterpart and characterized by MAML2 gene rearrangements.

Adenoid cystic carcinoma is a malignant tumour composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells arranged in cribriform, tubular, or solid architecture and punched-out spaces filled with hyaline material.

Adenosquamous carcinoma is a tumour composed of distinct malignant squamous and malignant glandular components.

Related terminology

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma occurs mostly in the middle and distal oesophagus {21587086}. Adenoid cystic carcinoma and adenosquamous carcinoma mostly arise in the mid oesophagus {24363531;27867543;26943443;25013491}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are similar to those of conventional squamous cell carcinoma, with dysphagia being the most common presenting symptom.

Epidemiology

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, and adenosquamous carcinoma are all rare, accounting for < 1% of primary oesophageal carcinomas. These tumours occur most commonly in men, at a median age of about 60 years, as case reports show {21587086;24363531;27867543;16115189;26943443;25013491;3004692}.

Etiology

Risk factors are similar to those reported for conventional oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (see section Oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma).

Pathogenesis

The cell of origin of oesophageal mucoepidermoid carcinoma and oesophageal adenoid cystic carcinoma is not known with certainty. However, neoplastic derivation from oesophageal submucosal glands appears most likely, given the shared embryological origins of oesophageal submucosal and salivary glands and the occasional restriction of these tumours to the oesophageal submucosa {21587086;7504701}. Adenosquamous carcinoma likely derives from a primary squamous cell carcinoma (or primary adenocarcinoma when it occurs in the distal oesophagus against a background of Barrett oesophagus), that undergoes divergent glandular or squamous differentiation from a common precursor, as shown by shared mutation analysis {11875011;15489653}. In rare instances, the lack of shared genetic ancestry supports a collision of clonally independent squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma {15489653}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically, mucoepidermoid carcinoma and adenosquamous carcinoma appear similar to conventional squamous cell carcinoma. Adenoid cystic carcinoma is either protruding or ulcerative {26943443;25013491;23236991}. At early stages, adenoid cystic carcinoma is often located in the submucosa with an intact overlying squamous oesophageal lining. Lesions have a mean size of 56 mm, but they can be highly variable in size {6318960;29259386;8411740}.

Histopathology

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma recapitulates its salivary gland counterpart in being composed of cysts and sheets of mucin-producing epithelial cells, epidermoid cells, and intermediate cells in variable proportions, depending on tumour grade. Rare examples contain prominent sclerotic collagen with lymphoid tissue and eosinophils {24944502}.

Adenoid cystic carcinoma consists of epithelial and myoepithelial cells in a bilayer with inner epithelial and outer myoepithelial cells organized in a variety of cribriform, tubular (glandular), or solid patterns. The cribriform pattern is characterized by sharp, punched-out spaces between the tumour cells forming cystic lumina that contain either basophilic glycosaminoglycans that are Alcian blue–positive (true glands) or hyalinized basal lamina material that is PASD-positive (pseudoglands). The solid pattern shows no lumina but is still composed of two cell types. Perineural infiltration and lymphovascular permeation are common {26943443}. The myoepithelial cells in adenoid cystic carcinoma are positive for p63, p40, S100, SMA, and calponin. The epithelial cells are positive for cytokeratin, CEA, and KIT (CD117) {25013491;22473577}. Detection of MYB gene rearrangements and overexpression of MYB protein on immunohistochemistry can further corroborate this diagnosis {26076064;38973399}. Basaloid squamous cell carcinoma may be considered in the differential diagnosis, because it often has a pseudoglandular pattern not unlike that of adenoid cystic carcinoma and a jigsaw-like arrangement of nests and cords {15387711}. However, basaloid squamous cell carcinoma is composed of only one cell type unlike the dual neoplastic population typical for adenoid cystic carcinoma.

The diagnosis of adenosquamous carcinoma requires distinct components of both adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma that are separate or intermingled with each other. Unlike mucoepidermoid carcinoma, where the squamous component shows a bland cellular morphology, the squamous cells in adenosquamous carcinoma are clearly malignant. Generally, the volume of each component is irrelevant, although the Japanese classification of oesophageal cancer requires ≥ 20% of either component, without which the tumour defaults to the designation of the primary component alone {28111536}. The squamous areas may exhibit any degree of differentiation, including areas with no clear keratinization or intercellular bridges {24363531}. The adenocarcinoma component is typically tubular or glandular with evidence of mucin production. Acantholytic change within a conventional squamous cell carcinoma can mimic gland formation and should not be mistaken for a glandular differentiation. Likewise, the presence of adenoid or microcystic changes within squamous foci of otherwise pure squamous cell carcinoma is insufficient for the diagnosis.

Cytology

The cytological appearance of these carcinomas arising in the oesophagus has not been documented in the literature.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MAML2 and MYB gene fusion arrangements can aid in the molecular diagnosis of oesophageal mucoepidermoid carcinoma {36481651} and oesophageal adenoid cystic carcinoma {32621256}, respectively.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

Essential: glandular mucin-producing cells, intermediate cells, and squamous cells in varying proportions depending on tumour grade.

Adenoid cystic carcinoma

Essential: a biphasic tumour composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells in tubules, nests, or solid sheets with punched-out spaces filled with hyaline material.

Adenosquamous carcinoma

Essential: distinct squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma components; absence of intermediate cells and cysts lined by mucous cells.

Staging

Staging of these tumours should follow the staging guidelines set out by primary site and/or treatment history (see section Adenocarcinoma of the oesophagus and oesophagogastric junction and section Oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma).

Prognosis and prediction

Diagnostic reporting of oesophageal mucoepidermoid carcinoma and oesophageal adenoid cystic carcinoma should follow the nomenclature of their salivary gland counterparts [[El-Naggar AK, Chan JKC, Grandis JR, et al., editors. WHO classification of head and neck tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2017. (WHO classification of tumours series, 4th ed.; vol. 9). https://publications.iarc.who.int/548. Bookref]]. Grading based on growth pattern, mitotic activity, and necrosis (Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Centre [MSKCC] system) predicts outcome for conventional salivary gland mucoepidermoid carcinoma {38554299}. Case series analysing outcomes of patients with oesophageal mucoepidermoid carcinoma or oesophageal adenoid cystic carcinoma are generally small and liable to inclusion bias, precluding well-controlled conclusions {21587086;26943443;3004692}. Studies analysing the National Cancer Database (NCDB) and SEER database have reached conflicting conclusions with regard to the outcomes of oesophageal adenosquamous carcinoma compared with those of conventional oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma and oesophageal adenocarcinoma {28280613;28848104}.
