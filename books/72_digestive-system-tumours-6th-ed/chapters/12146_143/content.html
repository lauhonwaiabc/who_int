12.1.4.6 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTL) is a clinically aggressive lymphoma characterized by cytotoxic T cells, usually γδ subtype, involving the spleen, liver, and bone marrow.

ICD-O coding

97160/3 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A90.8 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The spleen, liver, and bone marrow are involved. Lymph nodes are usually not involved {11554170;12907441;24128129}.

Clinical features

Patients with HSTL typically present with hepatosplenomegaly and systemic symptoms. Common abnormalities in laboratory tests include cytopenias and elevated serum levels of B2M, LDH, bilirubin, and liver function enzymes {26872013;33160933}. Peripheral blood involvement, uncommon at presentation, can occur later in the clinical course {11554170;12907441;24128129}. Hemophagocytic syndrome can occur {29337025;38268478}, and rarely patients may present with liver or multiorgan failure {30902844}. See #49817Table 12.04.

Epidemiology

HSTL is rare and represents 1.4–2% of peripheral T-cell lymphomas and < 1% of all lymphomas {24128129;34819748;38420697}. In England, the age-standardized incidence of HSTL is 0.1 cases per 1 million person-years {38420697}. Non-White populations appear to have a higher incidence {38420697;38946833}. The median patient age is in the fourth or fifth decade of life {26872013;33160933;3385626;38420697;38946833}. The T-cell receptor (TCR) γδ subtype of HSTL is more common in men, but the less common TCRαβ subtype is more common in women {11224598;12907441;24128129;33856526;34819748}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Chronic immune suppression and chronic antigenic exposure are likely involved in pathogenesis. About 20% of HSTL arise in patients on long-term immunosuppressive therapy for solid-organ transplantation {12907441;17509984;29337025}. Around 10% of cases occur in patients with immune disorders (e.g. Crohn disease) who have been treated with azathioprine or mercaptopurine, often in combination with TNF inhibitors {21122554;24621284;28038706}.

Isochromosome 7q is present in 40–70% of HSTL cases {11807981;29337025}. The common region of gain at 7q22 is associated with increased expression of the multidrug resistance gene ABCB1 {25057852}. Trisomy 8 occurs in 10–50% of cases {29337025}. Gene expression profiling studies have shown that HSTL exhibits a distinctive signature unifying TCRγδ and TCRαβ cases {22510872}. Gene mutation profiling has shown inactivating mutations in chromatin-modifying genes, such as SETD2 and INO80 {28122867}. Activating mutations in STAT5B and less often STAT3 implicate the JAK/STAT pathway {22510872;24947020;28122867}. Methylation profiling has shown hypermethylation of genes relevant to T-cell leukaemia pathobiology including LTA, CD5, CXCR6, GIMAP7, BCL11B, and SEPTIN9. There are also some methylation differences between TCRγδ and TCRαβ cases {30337361}. See #49818Table 12.05.

Macroscopic appearance

Liver and spleen show diffuse enlargement without an identifiable mass. The spleen is often markedly enlarged, typically > 1000 g or > 200 mm in diameter by radiological imaging {26872013}. The cut surface has a homogeneous appearance without nodules, reflecting diffuse red pulp involvement and atrophy of the white pulp.

Histopathology

HSTL typically involves cords and sinuses of the splenic red pulp {12907441;17509984;29337025}. The liver shows predominantly sinusoidal infiltration, and bone marrow is virtually always involved in a predominantly intrasinusoidal distribution {11554170;12907441;26872013}. HSTL cells typically expand bone marrow sinusoids. The neoplastic cells are monotonous with small to medium-sized nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, and pale cytoplasm {24128129}. The cytoplasm can have a jagged outline with cytoplasmic projections and peripheral basophilia likened to an oyster-shell appearance {38268478}. Cytological atypia with large-cell or blastoid changes can occur, especially with disease progression {11554170;12907441;17509984}.

The neoplastic cells are CD2+, CD3+, CD5− (or dim), CD4−, CD7+/−, CD8−/+, CD56+/−, and either TCRγδ+ (70–80%) or TCRαβ+ (15–20%) {12907441;29337025;33856526}. About 5% of cases can be negative for TCRs. Lymphoma cells usually express TIA1 and granzyme M; granzyme B is 30–40%, and perforin is seldom positive. Almost all cases are EBV-negative. See #49819Table 12.06 for immunophenotyping features.

Cytology

Diagnosis is often established by bone marrow examination. There is a limited role for FNAB of liver, or (rarely) spleen.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HSTL shows clonally rearranged T-cell receptor genes. Detection of isochromosome 7q and/or trisomy 8 and/or a typical gene mutation profile can be helpful in establishing the diagnosis {17509984;26872013}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a characteristic pattern of disease with involvement of spleen, liver, and bone marrow; uniform small to medium-sized lymphoid cells with indistinct nucleoli and lacking cytoplasmic granules; cytotoxic T-cell lineage.

Desirable: characteristic immunophenotype: CD4−, CD5−/+, CD8−/+, CD56+/−, TIA1+; usual expression of TCRγδ, but sometimes TCRαβ; isochromosome 7q; trisomy 8.

Staging

HSTL is staged according to the Lugano classification, adopted by the ninth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical course is aggressive. HSTL is refractory to anthracycline-based chemotherapy with a median survival of < 2 years {33160933;33856526;34819748;38946833}. Early use of high-dose therapy followed by allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation may improve survival {23107915;25234166}. Poorer prognosis has been associated with the TCRαβ subtype, trisomy 8, and evidence of liver dysfunction {26872013;33160933}.
