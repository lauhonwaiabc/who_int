5.0.1 Tumours of the jejunum and ileum: Introduction

In this sixth edition, tumours of the small bowel are split between those of the duodenum and ampulla (section Tumours of the duodenum and ampulla) and those of the jejunum and ileum (this chapter), based on differences in epidemiological and biological features. Tumours in the jejunum and the ileum share many features with their colorectal counterparts, although there is considerably less knowledge about small bowel tumours, since they are much less frequent and less accessible to endoscopy for biopsy or endoscopic resection, thus reducing the numbers of early diagnoses. The most commonly encountered malignancies of the small bowel are metastatic tumours, covered in section Metastases to other digestive system sites.

This chapter covers the most important epithelial tumours, including conventional adenomas, sporadically occurring hamartomatous Peutz–Jeghers polyps, and adenocarcinomas. Since there is very limited literature on the serrated pathway in the small bowel, the reader is referred to section Tumours of the colon and rectum. Mesenchymal tumours of the small bowel are described in section Mesenchymal tumours of the digestive system. Neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) of the jejunum and ileum are described in section Jejunal and ileal neuroendocrine neoplasms. Relevant genetic tumour syndromes affecting the small bowel are described in section Genetic tumour syndromes of the digestive system.

Of particular importance for susceptibility to development of small bowel polyps and adenocarcinomas is the risk associated with both inflammatory conditions (coeliac disease and Crohn disease) as well as hereditary genetic tumour syndromes. These predisposing conditions should be considered for most diagnoses of primary jejunal or ileal epithelial precursors and malignancies.
