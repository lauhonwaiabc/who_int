12.1.3.3 Follicular lymphoma

Definition

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is a neoplasm of germinal-centre B cells with varying proportions of centrocytes and centroblasts or large transformed cells and at least a partially follicular growth pattern.

ICD-O coding

96900/3 Follicular lymphoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2A80.Z Follicular lymphoma, NOS

2A80.6 Follicular lymphoma of small intestine

2A80.Y & XA1B13 Follicular lymphoma of large intestine

Related terminology

Acceptable: follicular lymphoma grades 1, 2, 3A, and 3B.

Subtype(s)

Classic follicular lymphoma (96903/3); follicular lymphoma with unusual cytological features (96902/3); follicular large B-cell lymphoma (96983/3)

Localization

Primary digestive FL occurs most often in the small and, to a lesser extent, large intestines, whereas its occurrence in the stomach, liver, pancreas, and biliary ducts is rare {35311939;31934218;38447995;21836483;18040146;20679881}. Multiple lesions are frequently found throughout the entire GI tract {20084529}. By definition, it is set apart from duodenal-type FL {21383289;21561531;29292593}, although the boundary is unclear for duodenal-type FL presenting with simultaneous or prevalent lesions arising in the small intestine.

Clinical features

Lymphomas in the small or large intestine may cause abdominal discomfort, intestinal obstruction, or intestinal bleeding if ulcerated. FL of the biliary tract may cause jaundice. Many localized digestive FLs are incidentally detected {35249892}.

Epidemiology

The GI tract is the most commonly involved site for primary extranodal FL. Primary digestive FL are rare and constitute < 4% of gastrointestinal lymphomas, but their incidence has increased over the past 40 years, and a recent study reports an age-adjusted average annual incidence of 0.111 cases per 100 000 person-years {38447995;20084529}. Most gastrointestinal FL are diagnosed in patients above 60 years of age {38447995}. The risk is increased in patients with inflammatory bowel disease treated with azathioprine or mercaptopurine {24879926}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The FL hallmark translocation t(14;18) is detected in about 90% of gastrointestinal FL {28504205;37493666}. Ongoing hypermutations and selective usage of IGHV (VH) gene segments suggest an antigen-driven pathogenesis {22899287}. The expression of homing molecules hints to local factors in the maintenance of localized disease {12507894}.

Macroscopic appearance

Endoscopic granular lesions are frequent in the small bowel, whereas submucosal or ulcerated tumours are mainly observed in the stomach and colon {37493666;28504205}. Multiple polyps in the small or large intestine (lymphomatous polyposis) may be seen.

Histopathology

Like their nodal counterparts, gastrointestinal FL displays an at least partly follicular proliferation of centrocytes and centroblasts. Follicles are often crowded and without any zonation or starry-sky pattern. Most cases can be subtyped as classic follicular lymphoma (grade 1/2 or rarely 3A), and follicular large B-cell lymphoma is exceptional {37493666}. The rare cases with an entirely diffuse growth pattern should still be composed of GC cells with characteristic morphology and immunophenotype.

The immunophenotype is similar to that of nodal FL, including expression of B-cell markers like CD20 and CD79A; and positivity for CD10, BCL6 and BCL2 (detailed in the fifth-edition Haematolymphoid tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637. Bookref]]), although primary gastrointestinal FL of the stomach and colorectum often downregulate CD10 and BCL6 {27513891;37493666;28504205}. In contrast with duodenal-type follicular lymphoma, in which follicular dendritic cell meshworks mostly display a peripheral pattern, the CD21 staining in ileal FL may be either diffuse strong, diffuse weak, or peripheral. The peripheral pattern is rare in colonic FL and virtually absent in gastric cases, whereas it may be observed in secondary gastrointestinal involvement by FL {37493666}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The t(14;18) translocation, resulting in the fusion of BCL2 and IGH genes, is detected in most cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a B-cell lymphoma composed of centrocytes and centroblasts with a dominance of centrocytes in the vast majority of cases and with a GC B-cell immunophenotype.

Desirable: at least partly follicular growth pattern.

Staging

A diagnosis of primary gastrointestinal FL warrants the exclusion of secondary gastrointestinal involvement by systemic FL {37648294;35074979}. Most primary cases are localized, but regional lymph node involvement is not rare. Staging is performed according to the Lugano classification {27574190}.

Prognosis and prediction

Most gastrointestinal FLs have an indolent course {35249892}. Their rate of high-grade transformation is much lower than that of nodal FL. Transformation may occur in all gastrointestinal localizations {37493666}.
