13.1.6.4 Synovial sarcoma

Definition

Synovial sarcoma is a spindle cell sarcoma showing variable epithelial differentiation, characterized by SS18rearrangements with one of three partner genes (SSX1, SSX2, SSX4).

ICD-O coding

90400/3 Synovial sarcoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5A.Y & XA7MC7 Synovial sarcoma of stomach

Related terminology

Not recommended: synovial cell sarcoma, synoviosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Synovial sarcoma, monophasic fibrous (90410/3); synovial sarcoma, biphasic (90430/3); poorly differentiated synovial sarcoma (90401/3)

Localization

Primary synovial sarcoma is rare in the GI tract. It arises most commonly in the stomach, but a small number of cases have been reported in the oesophagus, small intestine, and colorectum {37972927}.

Clinical features

Patients have presented with abdominal pain, respiratory symptoms, or swallowing symptoms, and with nonspecific complaints such as anaemia and fatigue. Tumours have also been detected as an incidental finding {37972927}.

Epidemiology

Synovial sarcoma is an uncommon sarcoma reported to account for 5–10% of all soft tissue sarcomas, and with an annual incidence of 1 case per 1.4 million adults {19514087;37972927}. Like its soft tissue counterpart, synovial sarcoma of the GI tract shows an equal sex distribution and is most common in middle age (median age: 50 years, range: 23–81 years) {37972927}. Approximately 30% of cases occur in patients aged < 20 years {19514087}.

Etiology

There are no known predisposing factors. Exceptionally, synovial sarcoma has been associated with a history of previous radiotherapy {29336183}.

Pathogenesis

Synovial sarcoma is driven by a unique chromosomal translocation that results in the formation of an oncogenic SS18::SSX1/2/4 fusion gene that drives tumorigenesis {21472259;26947017;29202462}. Otherwise, synovial sarcomas have a very low mutation burden relative to other sarcomas {29100075}. Recent evidence suggests that the SS18::SSX fusion protein disrupts epigenetic control and blocks mesenchymal differentiation by complementary mechanisms including competitive binding with, and displacement of, native SS18 in the SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex {23540691}, inducing dependency on BRD9-containing alternative SWI/SNF complexes (ncBAF complexes) {30397315;30431433} and colocalizing with factors such as the ATF2 transcription factor, TLE1 to repress ATF2 target genes {25614489}, and the KDM2B lysine demethylase at unmethylated CpG islands to reactivate repressed genes {29502955;29861296}.

Macroscopic appearance

GI tract synovial sarcoma is most commonly centred in the submucosa and demonstrates infiltrative and destructive growth into other layers {37972927}. The tumours may be polypoid or ulcerating {37972927;18223331}.

Histopathology

Most gastrointestinal synovial sarcomas are monophasic spindle cell tumours composed of relatively uniform spindle cells with almost no matrix or with a focal collagenous matrix. A minority of cases have a biphasic histology, with glandular elements and spindle cells, similar to the monophasic tumours. Mitotic counts vary, but most cases have a low-grade appearance with scarce mitoses. High-grade components with a round cell pattern and prominent mitotic activity may be present and portend a poorer prognosis {15364967}. Foci of calcification and/or ossification may be seen. Immunohistochemically, the lesion shows patchy, focal positivity for keratin and EMA in the monophasic spindle cell tumours and strong positivity in the glandular elements. Positivity for calretinin, S100 protein, DOG1, and rarely KIT (CD117) (usually focal) can occur, and this raises the differential diagnostic consideration of gastrointestinal stromal tumour. Similarly, immunoreactivity for CD99 and synaptophysin {37972927} can lead to diagnostic confusion with Ewing sarcoma, especially in high-grade tumours with a round cell pattern.

Immunohistochemistry with an SS18::SSX monoclonal antibody directed towards the fusion protein transcribed by SS18::SSX1/2/4 fusion transcripts is useful in confirming the diagnosis. Diffuse strong nuclear expression with SS18::SSX fusion–specific immunohistochemistry has been consistently demonstrated to be highly specific (perhaps perfectly so) for synovial sarcoma while maintaining a high sensitivity (> 95%) {32141887;32559341;37972927;37899499}. It may be used in conjunction with an SSX C-terminus–specific antibody (SSX-CT), which is highly sensitive (but imperfectly specific) to confirm synovial sarcoma {32141887;32559341;37972927;37899499}.

Cytology

Synovial sarcoma can be diagnosed on FNA. Preparation of a cell block is advised because a conclusive diagnosis relies heavily on ancillary immunohistochemical and molecular studies {23444168;32559341}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Like other synovial sarcomas, those in the GI tract are defined by SS18::SSX1 or SS18::SSX2 gene fusions (rarely, SS18::SSX4) {37972927;18223331;25699170}. These fusions can be detected by FISH or molecular assays. However, as SS18::SSX fusion protein–specific immunohistochemistry has proven so specific, cases with compatible morphology showing diffuse strong positivity for this marker require no molecular confirmation {32141887;32559341;37972927;37899499}. However, as this antibody is imperfectly sensitive, in cases without staining for SS18::SSX, molecular confirmation may be required to confirm the diagnosis if the morphology is suggestive (particularly if there is expression of SSX-CT) {32141887;32559341;37972927;37899499}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cell sarcoma showing variable epithelial differentiation; evidence of SS18 rearrangement confirmed by molecular testing or fusion-specific immunohistochemistry.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

In general, the prognosis is unpredictable and appears similar to that of synovial sarcoma in soft tissue with instances of local recurrence and distant metastases, although patients with tumour limited to the mucosa seem to pursue a favourable course {37972927}. The specific SS18 partner variant (SSX1, SSX2, or SSX4) does not seem to have a significant impact on prognosis {26217552}.
