16.1.1.18 Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia (GCGR)

Definition

Glucagon cell hyperplasia and neoplasia (GCHN) is an autosomal recessive syndrome with germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in GCGR and characterized by a spectrum of hyperplasia and neoplasia of glucagon-producing pancreatic islet cells.

MIM numbering

619290 Mahvash disease; MVAH

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 Neuroendocrine neoplasms of pancreas

Related terminology

Not recommended: glucagon cell adenomatosis; Mahvash disease.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GCHN arises throughout the pancreas.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with nonspecific symptoms such as abdominal pain, altered bowel habits, fatigue, and/or acute pancreatitis. Despite very high serum glucagon levels (in the same range or higher than seen in glucagonoma syndrome) {29702528;27933176;37260318}, no symptoms typical of such a syndrome occur, and in fact, there may be hypoglycaemia. Abdominal imaging may reveal lesions of varying sizes throughout the pancreas, which may also be somatostatin receptor (SSTR) reactive on PET imaging {18437091;25695890;36698259;37991855}. There are reports of individual patients with additional features including severe PTH-independent hypercalcaemia {30032256}; non-cirrhotic portal hypertension {37991855}; elevated amino acids detected on newborn screening {30294546}; and necrolytic migratory erythema {21763589}.

Epidemiology

GCHN is a rare disease; 15 cases have been reported to date. Both sexes seem to be equally affected. The age ranges from 7 to 68 years.

Etiology

Most of the examined patients with GCHN showed germline sequence variants of the GCGR gene, located on chromosome 17q25 {18957496;25695890;18437091;25914784}. GCGR point substitutions NM_000160.5:c.187G>A p.(Asp63Asn), NM_000160.5:c.674G>A p.(Arg225His), and NM_000160.5:c.1102G>A p.(Val368Met) were detected in GCHN, which are associated with impaired receptor function {34801547;37260318}. In addition, truncating frameshift variants in GCGR were also described {25695890;37991855}. Examinations in one index patient and his parents indicate that GCHN with mutation of GCGR follows an autosomal recessive inheritance {25695890}. The genetic disease mechanism in patients without GCGR variants remains unclear {25695890;23407487}.

Pathogenesis

GCHN harbour either homozygous GCGR sequence variants or at least two compound heterozygous sequence variants leading to premature stop codons or probably deficient protein expression. Deficient glucagon signalling in the liver causes a disturbance in a presumed feedback mechanism between the liver and the glucagon cells, which is probably mediated by amino acids {30938753}. This leads to glucagon cell hyperplasia, elevated serum glucagon levels, and finally to neuroendocrine tumours (NETs). Recently, liver transplantation in a patient with GCHN resulted in normalization of hyperglucagonaemia and gradual reduction of the hypertrophic pancreas, supporting pathogenic mechanism that have been discussed in animal models {37991855}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically the pancreas can be normal-sized or enlarged, with few large nodules of up to 20 mm in size on the cut surface accompanied by multiple white-yellow nodules ranging from 5 to 10 mm in diameter {18437091;18957496;25695890}.

Histopathology

Throughout the entire pancreatic tissue there are normal-sized and hypertrophic islets, randomly distributed between microtumours, and occasionally single large tumours, some with cystic transformation and focal calcification. The tumour cells are monomorphic and exhibit round to oval nuclei. The mitotic activity is very low. Micrometastases have been reported in regional lymph nodes but are rare {25695890}.

Immunohistochemically, the microtumours and the macrotumours are almost exclusively composed of glucagon-positive cells that occasionally coexpress PP {18957496}. In the hypertrophic islets, glucagon-positive cells outnumber insulin and somatostatin cells. All endocrine cells express SSTR2A. Ki-67 labelling is very low (< 1%) {18437091;18957496;25695890}. GCHN with GCGR mutation seems to have a more advanced phenotype regarding the number of hypertrophic islets, microtumours, and the occurrence of macrotumours. Lymph node metastases were found in 1 patient with wildtype GCGR {25695890}.

Cytology

Cytologically, the pancreatic NETs (PanNETs) are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterpart.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Sequencing all exons of GCGR including splice sites is obligatory before GCGR mutation can be excluded. To date, sequence variants of GCGR have been reported in exons 2, 4, 8, 10, 11, or 12 {37260318}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: markedly elevated serum glucagon levels; hypertrophic islets with glucagon cell hyperplasia merging with PanNETs, expressing predominantly or exclusively glucagon.

Desirable: identification of GPVs in GCGR; exclusion of MEN1 and other syndromes associated with pancreatic neuroendocrine cell hyperplasia and microtumours.

Staging

Staging follows the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems for NETs of the pancreas.

Prognosis and prediction

To date, all reported cases of GCHN have followed a benign clinical course except one. No recurrence of the disease symptoms has been noted in patients who underwent partial pancreatectomy {25695890}. However, lifelong follow-up of these patients is indicated, as the tumours have the potential to metastasize.
