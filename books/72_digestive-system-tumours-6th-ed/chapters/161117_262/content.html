16.1.1.17 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour predisposition syndrome (KIT, PDGFRA)

Definition

Familial gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) syndromes are autosomal dominant disorders characterized by germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in KIT and PDGFRA leading to gastrointestinal stromal tumours, inflammatory fibroid polyps, fibrous tumours, and disorders of mast cells, skin pigmentation, and GI tract motility.

MIM numbering

175510 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour / GIST-plus syndrome, somatic or familial (PDGFRA)

606764 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour, familial (KIT)

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: familial gastrointestinal stromal tumour syndrome (KIT- or PDGFRA-related).

Not recommended: GIST-plus syndrome; Devon polyposis syndrome; intestinal neurofibromatosis / neurofibromatosis 3b (INF/INF3b).

Subtype(s)

KIT-associated familial GIST syndrome; PDGFRA-associated familial GIST syndrome

Localization

In KIT-related syndromes, the GISTs arise throughout the GI tract. The GISTs arising in PDGFRA families occur in the stomach, whereas the inflammatory fibroid polyps, fibrous tumours, and GI tract fibrosis occur throughout the GI tract {27318444;25975287;36802986}.

Clinical features

Patients with germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) in KIT present with GISTs throughout the GI tract. They are also prone to motility disorders (particularly dysphagia) associated with diffuse interstitial cell of Cajal hyperplasia (ICCH); skin pigmentation disorders including hyperpigmentation, lentigines, and naevi particularly around the mouth, genitals, hands and feet; and mast cell disorders such as urticaria pigmentosa and systemic mastocytosis {27437068}. The KIT-associated syndrome is not associated with inflammatory fibroid polyps.

In addition to gastric GISTs, the PDGFRA syndrome is associated with inflammatory fibroid polyps, distinctive fibrous tumours, areas of mucosal/submucosal or muscularis propria fibrosis, and GI tract lipomas. PDGFRA variants are not associated with diffuse ICCH and dysmotility, pigmentation abnormalities, or mast cell disorders {18670346;27437068;25975287;17566086;29486293}. Some patients with PDGFRA-related syndrome have coarse facial features and large hands, and premature tooth loss has been reported in one kindred {14699510;17087943;29486293}.

Epidemiology

GPVs in KIT and PDGFRA are rare causes of syndromic GISTs compared with the SDH-deficient tumour syndromes (see section SDH-deficient tumour syndromes) and neurofibromatosis type 1 (see section Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)). For example, in 1 series of 499 patients with GIST, 16 (3.2%) were associated with SDH syndromes, 7 (1.4%) with neurofibromatosis type 1, and only 1 (0.2%) with GPVs in KIT {36593350}; and at the time of writing, < 60 kindreds have been reported {28314314;37311038;39350996}. While KIT-related tumours demonstrate equal sex distribution, PDGFRA-related tumours show a female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:3) {27437068}. Compared with their counterparts with sporadic disease, patients with GPVs in KIT and PDGFRA present on average 10 years earlier {27437068}.

Etiology

These syndromes are caused by GPVs in either KIT or PDGFRA. KIT-related kindreds outnumber PDGFRA kindreds by 10:1 {28314314;37311038;39350996}.

Pathogenesis

As in sporadic GISTs, activating GPVs in KIT and PDGFRA cause ligand-independent kinase activation and therefore tumorigenesis of GISTs (see section Gastrointestinal stromal tumour). While some GISTs arising in these syndromes also show secondary molecular events such as loss of heterozygosity, or chromosomal gains or losses, these are not required for tumorigenesis.

The involvement of KIT in the development and maintenance of interstitial cells of Cajal, melanocytes, and mast cells explains the development of ICCH, dysmotility, disorders of pigmentation, and mast cell diseases.

Telocytes, a recently described fibroblast-like CD34-positive/PDGFRA4-positive cell (previously termed “interstitial Cajal-like cells”), which proliferate and grow under the influence of PDGFRA, are reported to be the cell of origin of the fibroid polyps, fibrous tumours, and mucosal/submucosal fibrosis associated with PDGFRA variants {30117724}.

Macroscopic appearance

Other than multifocality, the GISTs and inflammatory fibroid polyps found in these syndromes are grossly indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts. The mucosal/submucosal fibrosis associated with PDGFRA may extend to the serosa or mesentery and be mistaken for a sclerosing malignancy or nonspecific fibrosis {18670346;25975287;30117724}.

Histopathology

The GISTs are identical to their sporadic counterparts and show normal preserved expression of SDHB. KIT-related tumours are usually spindled and distributed throughout the GI tract, while PDGFRA-related GISTs are confined to the stomach and more frequently show epithelioid or mixed differentiation {29170162}. Diffuse hyperplasia of the interstitial cells of Cajal is a clue to KIT syndrome but does not occur in association with PDGFRA {29170162}. The inflammatory fibroid polyps are similar to their sporadic counterparts but form a continuum with less cellular fibrous tumours {18670346;25975287;29486293;30117724}. The fibrous tumours are often densely collagenized but contain scattered CD34-positive/KIT-negative spindled cells, often with long, slender cytoplasmic processes. Discontinuous extension of the fibrosis away from the polyp into the adjacent submucosa, serosa, or mesentery is a clue to separating hereditary inflammatory fibrous polyps from sporadic ones {29486293}.

Cytology

The cytological features of the GISTs are the same as their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

GPVs in KIT and PDGFRA are rare, and there are no clear guidelines on who should be offered genetic counselling/testing. There is clearly a higher risk in younger patients, with multifocal disease, a family history, and coexistent inflammatory fibroid polyps, or other syndromic features such as skin pigmentation changes or mastocytosis.

Although there seems to be some selection towards more favourable genotypes, the pathogenic variants found in these syndromes overlap with those found in tumours in sporadic disease {27437068;23610110}. Therefore, a pragmatic approach to assessing the possibility of these syndromes in the setting of multiple GISTs or inflammatory fibroid polyps is to first sequence the tumours using the standard panels employed for somatic testing covering exons 8, 9, 11, 13, and 17 of KIT and exons 12, 14, and 18 of PDGFRA. If no KIT or PDGFRA variants are found, germline KIT or PDGFRA syndrome is effectively excluded, and therefore an SDH-deficient tumour syndrome or neurofibromatosis type 1 becomes the most likely cause of syndromic disease. If a pathogenic KIT or PDGFRA variant is identified, a decision can be made on whether to proceed to germline testing based on local resourcing and the clinical risk of syndromic disease. If two separate primary GISTs are found to harbour the same KIT or PDGFRA variants, then these syndromes should be actively considered.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: confirmation of GPVs in either KIT or PDGFRA.

Desirable: multifocal GISTs (for both KIT and PDGFRA); ICCH, pigment disorders, mast cell disorders, dysmotility (KIT only); inflammatory fibroid polyps; fibrous tumours (PDGFRA only).

Staging

Staging of syndromic GISTs is the same as that of their sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis and treatment of the GISTs is the same as that for sporadic tumours. However, because of the relatively small size and indolence of most of these GISTs and the propensity to multifocal disease, the decision of whether and when to operate is nuanced. The mitotic count and size can be used to predict the prognosis for individual tumours, and if tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy is required, the specifics of the KIT or PDGFRA variant present predict the likelihood of response or primary resistance {23598963;35791894;28710566}. Skin pigmentation abnormalities associated with KIT syndrome have also been reported to improve on tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy {28074523;28710566;32220019}. The inflammatory fibrous polyps and fibrous tumours associated with PDGFRA variants are benign {18670346;25975287;29486293}.
