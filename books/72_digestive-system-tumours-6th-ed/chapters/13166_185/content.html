13.1.6.6 Embryonal sarcoma of the liver

Definition

Embryonal sarcoma of the liver is a malignant hepatic neoplasm composed of undifferentiated mesenchymal cells.

ICD-O coding

89910/3 Embryonal sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5Y & XH42Q2 & XA5DY0 Embryonal sarcoma of liver

Related terminology

Acceptable: undifferentiated embryonal sarcoma.

Not recommended: malignant mesenchymoma of the liver.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Embryonal sarcoma usually arises in the right hepatic lobe {208754;24210186}.

Clinical features

Patients often present with abdominal distension, pain, fever, and weight loss; hepatic rupture with haemoperitoneum is uncommon {208754}. Leukocytosis and elevated serum alkaline phosphatase are common. Invasion of the inferior vena cava with extension to the right atrium may be observed {1702267}.

Epidemiology

Embryonal sarcoma is rare, although it is the most commonly described primary hepatic sarcoma in children. In reported cases, patients were typically aged 5–15 years, with no sex predilection {208754;27781381}; presentation in adulthood is rare {18361435}. Embryonal sarcoma has been reported in association with Li–Fraumeni syndrome {1702267}.

Etiology

Embryonal sarcoma is sporadic. Rarely, transformation from mesenchymal hamartoma has been reported {20925497}.

Pathogenesis

Undifferentiated embryonal sarcoma of the liver harbours alterations in the large microRNA cluster C19MC, just like mesenchymal hamartoma of the liver {17311249}. The most common alteration is t(11;19)(q13;q13.4), leading to MALAT1::C19MC fusion {24120702}, and this fusion drives overexpression of C19MC microRNAs {32310940}. In contrast to mesenchymal hamartoma, embryonal sarcoma uniformly harbours complex copy-number alterations, as well as TP53mutations in 90% of cases {11376805;20925497;32310940}.

Macroscopic appearance

Embryonal sarcoma is well circumscribed but unencapsulated. Tumours are large (100–300 mm). The cut surface is heterogeneous, with alternating solid, fleshy, and mucoid areas and foci of cystic degeneration, necrosis, and haemorrhage {208754}.

Histopathology

The tumour is typically composed of variably spindled, stellate, and pleomorphic giant cells loosely arranged in a myxoid stroma; fibrotic areas with solid, fascicular, or storiform growth may be observed {208754;1702267}. Cytoplasmic PAS-positive eosinophilic hyaline (lysosomal) globules in giant cells are a characteristic finding {208754}. Mitotic activity is easily detected, including atypical mitoses. Peripheral entrapment of bile ducts is often seen, as is extramedullary haematopoiesis. Association with mesenchymal hamartoma has been documented {8066077;9331300;11779051;20925497}.

Embryonal sarcoma shows no specific immunophenotype. Variable, often limited expression of keratins, desmin, and MSA may be seen {1702267;17378682;16785789}; a subset of cases are positive for glypican-3 (GPC3) {21937079}. The tumour cells are negative for EMA, HepPar1 (CPS1), AFP, myogenin, and MYOD1 {17378682}.

Cytology

Cytology specimens show spindle cells, some of which exhibit marked nuclear pleomorphism {38226775}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Embryonal sarcoma uniformly shows a complex karyotype and complex copy-number alterations {11376805;32310940}. The t(11;19)(q13;q13.4) characteristic of mesenchymal hamartoma has been detected in several cases {17311249}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindled, stellate, and pleomorphic giant cells loosely arranged in a myxoid stroma.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Although embryonal sarcoma of the liver may pursue an aggressive clinical course, with metastases to the lung and peritoneum, patients with surgically resectable tumours often have a favourable outcome {24210186;26925712;27427850;38556718;38567652}. Tumour size > 150 mm is an adverse prognostic indicator {27781381}.
