13.1.2.6 Inflammatory fibroid polyp

Definition

Inflammatory fibroid polyp is a benign, often polypoid, hypocellular fibroblastic neoplasm with a predilection for the stomach and ileum, containing a prominent inflammatory infiltrate, especially eosinophils.

ICD-O coding

88256/0 Inflammatory fibroid polyp

ICD-11 coding

2E84.Y & XA7MC7 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of stomach

2E84.Y & XA0QT6 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of ileum

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The stomach (especially the antrum) is the most commonly affected site, followed by the ileum, although inflammatory fibroid polyps may arise throughout the GI tract {721077;8214261}. Tumours are usually centred in the submucosa but can be restricted to the mucosa.

Clinical features

Tumours may be sessile or polypoid. Small upper GI tract tumours can be discovered incidentally at endoscopy. Larger tumours may present with abdominal pain, bleeding, or obstruction; half of patients with small intestinal tumours present with intussusception {23426127}.

Epidemiology

Inflammatory fibroid polyps are rare and represent < 0.1% of all gastric polypoid lesions. There is a slight female predominance and a peak incidence in middle-aged adults {20393746;32602395}.

Etiology

Most tumours are sporadic, but rare familial cases result from germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants in PDGFRA{6745724;25975287}. Pathogenic variant carriers often develop multiple tumours, including gastrointestinal stromal tumours {29486293}.

Pathogenesis

PDGFRA activating mutations are identified in many cases, most often in small intestine tumours {18686281;19448595;20393746}; exon 18 mutations, usually NM_006206.6:c.2525A>T p.(Asp842Val), predominate in gastric tumours, whereas exon 12 mutations are nearly always identified in small intestine tumours {22394371}. Germline PDGFRA mutations have been found in the rare familial cases {25975287}. Evolving data support that the lesional cells show telocyte differentiation; these CD34+/PDGFRA+ interstitial cells appear to be involved in neurotransmission {29486293;29981768;30117724}.

Macroscopic appearance

Inflammatory fibroid polyps may be sessile or polypoid. Tumours range widely in size; gastric tumours are usually < 30 mm, whereas ileal tumours are often larger. The cut surface is white and often fleshy.

Histopathology

Histological features include a poorly marginated, hypocellular lesion containing a haphazard arrangement of short spindled and stellate cells with fine chromatin, small or indistinct nucleoli, and scant eosinophilic cytoplasm. The stroma is often oedematous and sometimes myxoid or collagenous, with a prominent mixed inflammatory infiltrate, often rich in eosinophils and lymphocytes. Small and intermediate-sized, rounded blood vessels, some with concentric (onion-skin) fibrosis, are a typical finding.

By immunohistochemistry, CD34 and PDGFRA are positive in most cases {9160309;19448595}. More limited SMA expression is observed in a small subset of tumours. KIT, DOG1 (ANO1), desmin, S100, SOX10, and keratins are negative.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a hypocellular appearance, with short spindled to stellate cells in an oedematous or myxoid stroma; an inflammatory infiltrate of eosinophils and lymphocytes.

Desirable: expression of CD34.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Inflammatory fibroid polyps are benign.
