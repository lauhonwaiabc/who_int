Definition

Pancreatic mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNENs) are epithelial neoplasms composed of morphologically and immunohistochemically recognizable malignant neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine components.

ICD-O coding

81540/3 Mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasm

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH8E54 Mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasm (MiNEN) of pancreas

Related terminology

Acceptable: mixed adenoneuroendocrine carcinoma (only for specific subtypes).

Subtype(s)

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine tumour (82442/3); mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine carcinoma (81544/3); mixed acinar carcinoma–neuroendocrine tumour (82443/3); mixed acinar carcinoma–neuroendocrine carcinoma (81542/3); mixed acinar–neuroendocrine–ductal carcinoma (81543/3)

Localization

Pancreatic MiNENs can arise anywhere in the pancreas. Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs) are more frequently located in the pancreatic head. Since this specific subtype is also frequently found in the ampulla {36646615;37032087}, the possibility of an ampullary origin ought to be ruled out with careful evaluation.

Clinical features

The symptoms are usually nonspecific and related to tumour growth and/or metastatic dissemination and include abdominal pain, obstructive jaundice, weight loss, nausea, vomiting, and anaemia {36078951}.

Epidemiology

Pancreatic MiNENs represent < 2% of pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (PanNENs), with an incidence rate of 0.2 cases per 1 million person-years {39243580}. Considering all the different subtypes together, pancreatic MiNENs are slightly more frequent in men, and the average age at presentation is 71.3 years (range: 63.3–78.1 years) {39243580}.

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs are very uncommon, accounting for < 0.5% of all ductal adenocarcinomas {10839610;12647216}. They occur mainly in men (82.8% of patients are male, 18.2% are female), and the mean patient age is 61.7 years (range: 24–82 years) {36078951}.

Neuroendocrine differentiation at the immunohistochemical level is very common in acinar cell carcinomas. However, cases qualified as mixed (> 30% morphologically identifiable second component) account for about 15% of all acinar cell carcinomas {23026929} and have a similar sex distribution; the mean age at diagnosis is 55 years (range: 16–89 years) {15517367} [[Klimstra DS, Hruban RH, Siegel CS, et al. Tumors of the pancreas. Arlington (VA): American Registry of Pathology; 2023 (AFIP atlases of tumor and non-tumor pathology, series 5; fascicle 15). Bookref]].

Both mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–neuroendocrine tumour (NET) and acinar carcinoma–NET are extremely rare, and no epidemiological information is available.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

For mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NEC, only limited molecular data are available, and no association with genetic syndromes has been documented. KRAS mutations have been identified in 58.3% of cases, which also showed loss of RB1 (Rb) expression on immunohistochemistry and upregulation of several genes involved in WNT and MAPK pathways (i.e. MMP7), which were not observed in the wildtype KRAS group {37866661}. In addition, several molecular pathways were found to be upregulated compared with normal pancreatic ductal epithelium, including the JAK-STAT, hedgehog, PI3K, RAS, MAPK, cell-cycle apoptosis, DNA damage repair, and chromatin modification pathways. Among them, the DNA damage repair pathway had the highest score of upregulation, whereas the driver gene pathway had the lowest score {37866661}.

For mixed acinar carcinoma NEN, no association with genetic syndromes has been documented, but 1 patient with history of breast cancer with a germline BRCA2 mutation presenting a pancreatic mixed acinar carcinoma–NEC has been recently described {35819745}. Mixed acinar carcinoma–NEN seems to share the genetic changes of acinar cell carcinomas, including APC/β-catenin pathway alterations and BRAF fusions {24590585;25266736}. Mutations found in pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PanNETs), such as DAXX, ATRX, and MEN1 mutations, are not typically observed.

Macroscopic appearance

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs and mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NETs are solid tan to yellow tumours measuring 20–100 mm in diameter.

Mixed acinar carcinoma–NENs are similar to acinar carcinomas; they are large (mean size: 90 mm), nodular, and well-circumscribed neoplasms with a fleshy, focally necrotic cut surface.

Histopathology

MiNENs display the classic morphological features of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine neoplasias [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]].

In mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs, the ductal adenocarcinoma component is moderately to poorly differentiated and resembles conventional ductal adenocarcinoma. Mucin production is visible, and intracellular or luminal mucins can be stained with PAS, mucicarmine, or Alcian blue histochemical stains. The NEC component can be either of small or large cell type and is characterized by solid architecture with areas of necrosis and a mitotic count of > 20 mitoses/2 mm2, along with high Ki-67 index. The two components are distinct. The ductal adenocarcinoma component is positive for CEA and EMA; the neuroendocrine component is positive for synaptophysin, INSM1, and/or chromogranin A, and the Ki-67 proliferative index is high (40–90%).

In mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NETs, which are very rare, the exocrine component shows the same morphological and immunohistochemical features described above, while the neuroendocrine component appears as a well-differentiated NET with low proliferation in terms of both mitotic count and Ki-67 index. Neuroendocrine markers are well expressed, and focal expression of peptide hormones can be observed. Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NETs must be distinguished from ductal adenocarcinomas with entrapped islets, which can be intimately attached to well-differentiated neoplastic glands. Entrapped islets are ovoid and show the normal islet distribution of peptide hormones with insulin-immunoreactive cells mainly located in the islet centre and glucagon-positive and somatostatin-positive cells at the islet periphery. Conversely, mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NET must be distinguished from PanNETs with entrapped ductules, which are biologically similar to other PanNETs and may even be more benevolent than ordinary PanNETs {32488621}; therefore the distinction is of vital importance. In these cases, ductular structures are often mature, small, bland, and typically confined by the boundaries of the neuroendocrine lesion; they do not show aberrant p53 immunostaining and are positive for SMAD4; nuclear Ki-67 expression is restricted to scattered cells.

As for acinar carcinoma NENs, it should be reiterated here that some degree of neuroendocrine differentiation at the immunohistochemical level, either in forms of scattered cells / clusters or in an amphicrine pattern (the same cells showing dual differentiation), is very common in acinar cell carcinomas in general. For such cases, the tumour is essentially classified as acinar carcinoma. However, if a distinct neuroendocrine component is present in an acinar cell carcinoma and constitutes > 30% of the tumour, then the lesion can be classified conceptually as MiNEN and reported as mixed acinar carcinoma–NEC or mixed acinar carcinoma–NET depending on the morphology of the neuroendocrine component. Immunohistochemistry for acinar (trypsin and BCL10) and neuroendocrine (synaptophysin, chromogranin, and INSM1) markers demonstrates distinct compartments in the tumour tissue, although overlaps and unexpected patterns can be observed [[Klimstra DS, Hruban RH, Siegel CS, et al. Tumors of the pancreas. Arlington (VA): American Registry of Pathology; 2023 (AFIP atlases of tumor and non-tumor pathology, series 5; fascicle 15). Bookref]]. Cases of acinar cell carcinoma with intimately admixed and not separated neuroendocrine component estimated to be > 30% can conceptually fit the novel definition of amphicrine-like carcinoma (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

It should be also noted here that pancreatoblastomas could by default be conceptually considered as MiNEN in the sense that they are characterized by the presence of a mixture of multiple cell lineages, the most common of which are acinar and neuroendocrine. They can also occur in adults. For this reason, before a case is classified as mixed acinar carcinoma–NEN, it is advisable to carefully search for the pathognomonic finding of squamoid nests and possibly also perform β-catenin immunohistochemistry in order to highlight the nuclear labelling in the subtle forms of morules (so-called squamoid nests) {31581358}. The presence of Gardner syndrome should also raise that possibility.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant since the diagnosis is made on resected specimen

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mixed neoplasm composed of at least two components of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine; either type ≥ 30% of the neoplasm; neuroendocrine markers expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Desirable: assessment of Ki-67 proliferation index and mitotic count to grade the NET component or to prognostically stratify the NEC component; grading of the non-neuroendocrine component.

Staging

The pathological staging of pancreatic MiNENs is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for pancreatic carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs usually metastasize to lymph nodes and liver early in the course of the disease and behave very aggressively. The reported median disease-free survival (DFS) duration was 12 months (range: 0–216 months): 1-year DFS was 62.5% of cases, 2-year DFS was 41.6% of cases, and 5-year DFS was 16.6% of cases. The median overall survival (OS) was 12 months (range: 0–288 months): 1-year OS was 84% of cases, 3-year OS was 27.7% of cases, and 5-year OS was 23.5% of cases {36078951}. Patients harbouring wildtype KRAS seem to show a shorter mean OS than patients with KRAS-mutant mixed ductal adenocarcinoma–NECs (17 months vs 28 months) {37866661}.

Acinar carcinoma–NENs essentially behave like an acinar cell carcinoma. After surgery, patients with mixed acinar carcinoma–NENs show the same 5-year survival rate (30–50%) as patients with pure acinar cell carcinomas.
