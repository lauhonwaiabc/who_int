14.2.4.3.3 VIPoma

Definition

VIPoma is a functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET) composed of vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP)-producing cells and associated with unregulated VIP secretion causing WDHA syndrome (watery diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, and achlorhydria; also known as Verner–Morrison syndrome).

ICD-O coding

81550/3 VIPoma

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH72E5 VIPoma of pancreas

Related terminology

Acceptable: VIP-producing pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour.

Not recommended: diarrhoeagenic tumour; islet cell tumour with watery diarrhoea.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately 70% of pancreatic VIPomas are located in the pancreatic body or tail, while the remaining 30% are found within the pancreatic head {9854582;10089056;17510774;31609932;35644576}.

Clinical features

Patients with VIPoma most commonly present with a profound secretory-type diarrhoea that persists after a 48-hour fasting as part of the WDHA syndrome {10089056;13571250;17510774;31127985;35644576}. However, the diarrhoea may be present for several years before diagnosis. Typically, the stool amount exceeds 700 mL per day and can exceed 3000 mL per day in 70% of patients. This results in losses of fluid and electrolytes, such as potassium. Other symptoms include lethargy, nausea, vomiting, muscle weakness, and cramps due to dehydration and hypokalaemia. Flushing has been reported in 8–20% of patients {31127985;31268974;34404601}. Tetany can also be present due to hypomagnesaemia. If severe, losses in fluid and electrolytes can lead to cardiac arrhythmias, myopathy, and hypovolaemic shock {3035922;34404601;9854582;10089056}. Children with VIPoma may present with failure to thrive in addition to chronic diarrhoea. The diagnosis is confirmed by demonstrating high plasma VIP levels. A VIP level of > 60 pmol/L after fasting is typically indicative of a VIPoma {730072}. CT, MRI, EUS, Octreoscan, and 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT can be used to detect primary and metastatic VIPomas {9854582;20664472}.

Epidemiology

VIPomas of the pancreas account for 0.6–1.5% of PanNENs and approximately 2–6% of all functioning PanNETs {17896148;24121170;24499825;26704781}. The incidence is 0.05–0.2 cases per 1 million person-years, with no sex predilection {26742109;31268974;31609932}. The mean age of patients with VIPoma is 50.5 years.

Etiology

There are no known etiological factors. Approximately 3–10% of pancreatic VIPomas occur in the context of MEN1 syndrome, but they are present in < 1% of patients with MEN1 {10089056;9854582;15657529;33649917}.

Pathogenesis

Pancreatic VIPomas originate from non-B islet cells of the pancreas that secrete VIP {13571250}. These tumours can produce excessive amounts of VIP, which causes intestinal secretion by activating G protein–coupled receptors on the intestinal epithelial cells, leading to watery diarrhoea and, consequently, dehydration and electrolyte imbalance {181379;8103215}.

Genetic studies have identified mutations in MEN1, VHL, PTEN, and SMAD4 in a subset of VIPomas {10327057;11523053}. Frequent alterations in 11q, Xq, and the Y chromosome have also been reported {10595906}. Loss of heterozygosity in chromosomes 3q and 6q may also be involved in tumour progression {11523053;11395364}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pancreatic VIPomas are usually solitary, well circumscribed, and large (mean size: 44–54 mm), with a solid and/or cystic composition {2839087;17510774;10089056;31268974}.

Histopathology

VIPomas display the classic well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. The tumour cells are arranged in a solid, trabecular, or tubuloacinar pattern {2839087;35644576}. Variable oncocytic or clear cell change can occur. Lymphatic and venous invasion are common.

Immunohistochemistry

Most tumour cells express keratins (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, and CK19), neuroendocrine markers (synaptophysin, chromogranin A, INSM1), and SSTR2. For a subset of cases, low-molecular-weight keratin immunohistochemistry can highlight the presence of paranuclear fibrous bodies {35644576}. In addition, VIPomas can demonstrate nuclear PDX1 expression. Immunolabelling for VIP is found in 90% of cases, but the extent of labelling is variable and may be attributed to rapid protein turnover. Coexpression of PP and calcitonin have also been described {22406608;2839087}.

Grading

VIPomas are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

Most VIPomas are G2 and G1 tumours {35644576}, but G3 tumours can occur {31268974;34404601}.

Cytology

The VIPoma cytology is the same as that of other PanNETs and is diagnosed as category 7 (malignant) when using the WHO Reporting System for Pancreaticobiliary Cytopathology (WHO System) {38709670}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinical evidence of VIP hyperproduction; well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: VIP expression; SSTR2 positive immunohistochemistry; DAXX and ATRX for prognostic assessment (selected cases).

Staging

The staging of pancreatic VIPomas is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for this site.

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 50–80% of patients present with distant metastases at diagnosis, predominantly in the liver and the ovary {10089056;3035922;9854582}. The management of VIP excess (using somatostatin analogues, peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT), or cytoreductive therapies) is also a critical factor {31268974}. Postoperative 5-year survival rates are 60% and 94%, respectively, for patients with and without metastatic disease {10089056}.
