2.1.1.1 Oesophageal squamous papilloma

Definition

Oesophageal squamous papilloma is a benign squamous epithelial proliferation with a papillary growth pattern.

ICD-O coding

80520/0 Squamous cell papilloma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E92.0 & XH50T2 Squamous cell papilloma of oesophagus

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Squamous cell papillomatosis (80600/0)

Localization

In a large series from the USA, 58% of squamous papillomas were located in the lower oesophagus {27001250}. In Asian populations, squamous papilloma is more frequently found in the middle oesophagus. In a series from Taiwan, China, slightly more than half (54%) of squamous papillomas were located in the middle oesophagus {26900297}.

Clinical features

Most patients with squamous papilloma are asymptomatic. Endoscopically, squamous papillomas appear as small, white exophytic growths with vessels crossing on a wart-like surface {26900297}. They have been observed in patients on follow-up for eosinophilic oesophagitis.

Epidemiology

Squamous papilloma is uncommon. Its prevalence in endoscopic series ranges from 0.01% to 0.45% {26135647;26900297;15853063}. Squamous papilloma typically presents in middle age (median age: 50 years) {26135647}. In a large endoscopy series from north-eastern France, the M:F ratio was 1.3:1 {26135647}. However, in many other populations, squamous papilloma shows a female predilection (with M:F ratios ranging from 0.2:1 to 0.8:1) {26900297;15853063}. Squamous papillomatosis, loosely defined as multiple lesions, has been described in paediatric {26538585} and elderly {28746592} patients. Approximately 53 cases have been reported in the English-language literature {37120568}.

Etiology

Chronic mucosal irritation can result from chemical factors (e.g. gastro-oesophageal reflux, alcohol consumption, cigarette smoking, chemotherapy, and caustic injury) or mechanical factors (e.g. minor trauma, variceal sclerotherapy, self-expandable metal stents, chronic food impaction, nasogastric intubation, and bougie-assisted oesophageal dilation) {26135647}. The etiological role of gastro-oesophageal reflux in squamous papilloma may explain why these lesions are frequently located in the lower oesophagus {26135647}. Squamous papillomatosis may occur in patients with focal dermal hypoplasia (also known as Goltz syndrome) {28405153;25314164;21272558} or angioma serpiginosum {17342156}.

Pathogenesis

Although it was implicated in the past, most recent data indicate that HPV is not a factor {37908288;37728123}. A single case has been reported of oesophageal papillomatosis hypothesized to be related to the high incidence of early-onset gastro-oesophageal reflux {24789149}. The hyperregenerative responses may stimulate proliferation of the squamous mucosa resulting in squamous papilloma. In another study, HRAS or KRAS mutations were identified in two thirds of cases {31960612}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically, squamous papillomas usually have a white, elevated, warty surface. The papillomas are often small (median diameter: 3 mm) {26135647}, but giant squamous papillomas have been reported {19659792}.

Histopathology

Microscopic examination reveals a papillary proliferation of squamous epithelium with a fibrovascular core of lamina propria. Scattered vacuolated cells with morphological features of koilocytes are often seen. Atypical nuclear features are absent, and no viral inclusions are present. The proliferation patterns of squamous epithelium are most often exophytic {8393303}, but they can also be endophytic or spiked. Rarely, oesophageal squamous dysplasia has been reported to arise in the setting of papillomatosis {39344881}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a papillomatous proliferation consisting of benign squamous epithelium with fibrovascular cores.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Squamous papilloma does not recur after resection. Squamous cell carcinoma has been rarely reported to be associated with squamous papilloma or squamous papillomatosis {26135647;28470005}.
