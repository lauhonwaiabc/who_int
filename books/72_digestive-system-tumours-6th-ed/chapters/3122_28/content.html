3.1.2.2 Gastric squamous cell carcinoma

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the stomach is a gastric carcinoma showing squamous cell differentiation, characterized by keratinocyte-type cells with intercellular bridges and/or keratinization.

ICD-O coding

80700/3 Squamous cell carcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B72.Y & XH0945 Squamous cell carcinoma, NOS, of stomach

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Verrucous squamous cell carcinoma (80510/3)

Localization

The upper, lower, and middle portions of the stomach are affected in decreasing order of frequency {24765539;27559983}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are the same as those of other gastric cancers.

Epidemiology

Limited data are available. Primary gastric SCC is rare, accounting for 0.04–0.5% of all gastric cancers {37719040;30788163}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Various mechanisms have been proposed, including malignant progression of squamous metaplasia, development from pluripotent stem cells or mucosal ectopic squamous nests, and squamous transdifferentiation of adenocarcinoma {24765539;25925374;37719040}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is the same as that of other gastric cancers.

Histopathology

The histology is similar to that of SCC of the oesophagus (see section Oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma) and other organs. The tumour shows uniform squamous cell differentiation without adenocarcinoma or other histology on thorough sampling. Exhaustive sampling to exclude another component is crucial, as is the exclusion of metastasis from SCC from another organ. Distal extension of an oesophageal SCC should be ruled out.

Verrucous SCC has been recognized as a morphological subtype; the tumour is characterized by very well differentiated keratinizing squamous cells with minimal atypia, demonstrating coarse surface papillary projections and bulbous downgrowth {30761414}.

Cytology

Peritoneal lavage cytology is used for staging gastric cancer and monitoring chemotherapy (see section Gastric adenocarcinoma).

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Limited data are available (see section Gastric adenocarcinoma).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: histological evidence of squamous cell differentiation; proof of invasion.

Desirable: exclusion of metastasis from another organ.

Staging

Gastric SCC frequently manifests at an advanced stage, associated with poor outcome. Spread to the liver is frequent in advanced disease {29390350;27559983;37719040}. For TNM staging, see section Gastric adenocarcinoma).

Prognosis and prediction

Because of the rarity of this entity, no specific prognostic features have been identified. Disease stage at diagnosis is probably the main determinant of prognosis {29390350;27559983;37719040}. Gastric SCC seems to behave more aggressively than adenocarcinoma of the same disease stage {29390350;30788163}.
