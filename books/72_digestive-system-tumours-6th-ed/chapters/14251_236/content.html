14.2.5.1 Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour

Definition

Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour (CoGNET), previously known as gangliocytic paraganglioma, is a triphasic tumour composed of neuroendocrine epithelial cells, Schwannian spindle cells, and ganglion cells.

ICD-O coding

86830/3 Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B80.0Y & XH3JF3 Composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour of duodenum

Related terminology

Acceptable: mixed ganglioneuroma–neuroendocrine neoplasm (MiGNEN); gangliocytic neuroendocrine tumour (NET).

Not recommended: duodenal ganglioneuroma; duodenal paraganglioma; gangliocytic paraganglioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CoGNET almost exclusively arises in the second part of the duodenum (periampullary region) {25886293;31572665;32668278}. Extremely rare cases have been reported in the jejunum, pylorus, oesophagus, pancreas, appendix, lung, thymus, and cauda equina {12653602;17653595;26447134;28096810;28764742;32205710;39601995;35285002}.

Clinical features

Patients with CoGNET usually present with gastrointestinal bleeding due to mucosal ulceration or nonspecific epigastric pain {30565068}. Biliary and intestinal obstruction have been reported {19517054}. Most CoGNETs are clinically non-functioning, although high serum levels of somatostatin, PP, and serotonin have been reported {8633524;24047035;27034808}. A few CoGNETs have been reported in the setting of neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1), and this possibility should be considered clinically {11876254;2894342}. There is no evidence to suggest that CoGNET is associated with other hereditary syndromes {31789631;29239034}.

Epidemiology

CoGNETs are rare and occur across a broad age range (15–92 years) {30522176;30101131;30565068}, but are most frequent in the sixth decade of life {30101131;30522176;31572665}. A slight male predominance (M:F ratio: ~3:2) {30101131;31572665} was reported in some series but not in others {30565068}.

Etiology

Although a few cases have been reported in the setting of neurofibromatosis type 1 {11876254;2894342}, the majority of CoGNETs are sporadic, with no known risk factors or syndromic associations.

Pathogenesis

CoGNET is thought to be derived from a defective sheet of intraembryonic endodermal progenitor cells, progenitor neuroectodermal cell rests, and smooth muscle {6139954;2578747;11819111;2750701}. About 20% of CoGNETs harbour somatic gain-of-function pathogenic variants in EPAS1 (HIF2A) {27130043}.

Macroscopic appearance

CoGNETs are solitary, polypoid, pedunculated or sessile lesions {2877566}, 20–30 mm in median size (range: 7–190 mm) depending on site {30565068;31572665}. In the pancreatic head, CoGNETs are larger (median size: 40 mm) {30565068}. Tumours are typically centred in the submucosa and are well demarcated but unencapsulated, with borders infiltrating the mucosa and/or muscularis propria in more than half of the cases {21599949}. The cut surface varies from tan-grey to yellow-white. The overlying mucosa is often ulcerated, even in the absence of mucosal invasion.

Histopathology

CoGNETs display three morphologically distinct cell components: (1) an epithelial cell component, arranged in nests and gland-like structures with granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, round to oval nuclei, speckled chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli; (2) a spindle cell Schwannian component, in slender fascicles with elongated plump nuclei and attenuated eosinophilic cytoplasm, wrapping around nests of epithelial cells; and (3) a ganglion cell component, scattered or in clusters, intermixed with the epithelial and spindle cell components. The proportions of the three components vary widely; ganglion cells may be difficult to identify. Mitoses are rare. Cytological atypia is minimal, and necrosis is absent. Any one or all three components may be present in metastases.

Immunohistochemistry

Almost all epithelial cells express cytokeratins {32668278}, chromogranin A, synaptophysin, NSE, and PR. PP and somatostatin may also be expressed. Spindle cells express SOX10, S100, and NSE. Ganglion cells express NSE and synaptophysin but are negative for SOX10 {32668278}. CoGNETs are usually negative for or show only very focal, weak expression of GATA3 and tyrosine hydroxylase {32668278}.

Grading

CoGNETs are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification).

Differential diagnosis

Exclusion of gastrointestinal stromal tumour, leiomyoma, schwannoma, NET, adenocarcinoma, ganglioneuroma, and paraganglioma is based on specific markers (KIT [c-KIT] and DOG1 for gastrointestinal stromal tumour, and desmin for leiomyoma) and adequate sampling. Ganglioneuroma lacks the epithelial component of CoGNET, whereas conventional paraganglioma lacks the ganglion and Schwannian components and is usually cytokeratin-negative {27130043;22931260;30101131;32668278}. CoGNET is often misdiagnosed as conventional well-differentiated NET (G1). Increasing evidence indicates that CoGNET is more closely related to NET than to conventional paraganglioma {32668278;27130043}. Immunohistochemistry for PR and PP, although not specific, may be useful {25886293}.

Cytology

Aspirates are often hypercellular and predominantly composed of epithelial cells, with a minor component of spindle and ganglion cells {22102547;33764657}. Rare cases with predominant spindle cells have been described.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: triphasic differentiation with variable neuroendocrine epithelial cells, spindle cells with Schwannian differentiation, and ganglion cells.

Desirable: location in the periampullary region of the duodenum.

Staging

The staging of duodenal CoGNET is in line with the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) / Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) systems for this site as well as for CoGNET at the other (exceedingly rare) sites.

Prognosis and prediction

Most CoGNETs are indolent and do not recur after complete removal. However, lymph node metastases have been reported in 10–14.5% of cases and liver metastases in 1.8% {26910321;30101131;30565068}. In a recent study, the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates for periampullary CoGNETs were 100%, 83.3%, and 55.6%, respectively {30565068}. No known histopathological factors are predictive of metastasis. Tumour size > 20 mm, young age, and depth of invasion could be risk factors for lymph node metastasis {21599949;26717062;26910321;27656281;28096810;28213067;30101131}. Pancreatic CoGNETs are thought to have a greater potential for metastasis {28764742}. Because of the lack of validated predictive markers for metastasis, all CoGNETs should be regarded as malignant; and long-term follow-up is recommended.
