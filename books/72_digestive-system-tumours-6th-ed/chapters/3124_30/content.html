3.1.2.4 Gastric undifferentiated carcinoma

Definition

Undifferentiated carcinoma of the stomach is a primary gastric carcinoma lacking specific cytological or architectural features of differentiation.

ICD-O coding

80200/3 Carcinoma, undifferentiated, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B72.Y & XH1YY4 Carcinoma, undifferentiated, NOS, of stomach

Related terminology

Acceptable: anaplastic carcinoma; pleomorphic carcinoma.

Not recommended: giant cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

No specific localization within the stomach is observed, but diffuse involvement of more than one region is common.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a large ulcerated and extensively necrotic tumour mass, which is often associated with synchronous regional metastases. Most patients with initially localized disease develop distant metastases shortly after diagnosis, and most patients die of their disease within 1 year {24503755;26551623}.

Epidemiology

Limited data are available. Undifferentiated gastric carcinoma is rare and is reported to account for about 0.2% of all gastric carcinomas. Men are predominantly affected, and the mean age is 60 years {24254063}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The common presence of glandular components in otherwise undifferentiated tumours suggests an origin via dedifferentiation. In some cases, the undifferentiated phenotype is driven by components of the SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex. In particular, the loss of SMARCB1 (INI1), SMARCA2, SMARCA4 (BRG1), and ARID1A has been reported, and co-inactivation of more than one component may occur {26551623;32413172;34812766}. In a subset of cases, SWI/SNF loss appears to be superimposed on mismatch repair deficiency or related to EBV {24503755;26551623}.

Macroscopic appearance

Undifferentiated carcinoma is usually encountered as a large, ulcerated, necrotic mass {26551623}.

Histopathology

Undifferentiated carcinoma is composed of anaplastic, large to medium-sized polygonal cells, arranged in solid or discohesive sheets, with frequent pleomorphic tumour giant cells. A rhabdoid phenotype may be the predominant pattern; these cells are frequently binucleated or multinucleated. Rarer histological patterns include spindled sarcomatoid morphology and undifferentiated carcinoma with osteoclast-like giant cells; the osteoclast-like cells are non-neoplastic {7726143;22825525}. Abundant necrosis is common. Extensive sampling is worthwhile to detect diagnostic areas adenocarcinoma. The stroma may be myxoid or mucinous. Immunohistochemistry, which is not specific, shows variable positivity for pankeratin. EMA staining may be helpful in keratin-poor examples. Vimentin is consistently expressed, frequently with a perinuclear dot-like pattern {24503755;26551623}. Common differential diagnoses to consider based on the morphology include EBV-associated carcinoma with lymphoid stroma, aggressive lymphomas (including anaplastic large cell lymphoma), metastatic melanoma, germ cell neoplasms, and sarcomas with epithelioid morphology.

Cytology

Peritoneal lavage cytology is used for gastric cancer staging and monitoring chemotherapy (see section Gastric adenocarcinoma).

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Limited data are available (see section Gastric adenocarcinoma).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a carcinoma with malignant epithelial cells without histological evidence of differentiation.

Staging

Undifferentiated gastric carcinoma frequently presents at an advanced stage with widespread metastatic disease {34812766;36464671}. For TNM staging, see section Gastric adenocarcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Because of the rarity of this entity and its highly aggressive nature, data on prognostic factors are limited. Disease stage at diagnosis, loss of SWI/SNF protein expression, and the underlying genotype appear to be major prognostic variables {33481850;34812766;36464671}.
