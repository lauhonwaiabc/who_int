7.1.1.3 Colorectal traditional serrated adenoma

Definition

Colorectal traditional serrated adenoma (TSA) is a benign epithelial neoplasm originating in the colorectum, characterized by serrated crypts, ectopic crypt foci, eosinophilic cytoplasm, and pencillate pale nuclei.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately 70% of TSAs occur in the distal colon and rectum {25216220}.

Clinical features

Most TSAs are asymptomatic and detected during screening or surveillance colonoscopy. Larger polyps may bleed and prompt detection with faecal occult blood testing {27021193}.

Epidemiology

TSAs are relatively uncommon accounting for < 1% of all colorectal polyps {24418851;31413858;26001333}. However, this is likely an underestimate because TSAs with conventional dysplasia may be classified as tubular or villous adenomas and those associated with non-dysplastic serrated precursors may be classified as sessile serrated lesions (SSLs) with dysplasia.

Etiology

See section Colorectal carcinoma.

Pathogenesis

Two distinct pathways have been identified in the progression of TSA that can be distinguished by their initiating mutations in either BRAF or KRAS.

Activating mutations of BRAF occur in 50% of TSAs {25216220;25127095}. TSAs with a component of SSL, or (less commonly) microvesicular hyperplastic polyp, tend to occur in proximal colon to rectosigmoid colon; they harbour BRAF and RNF43mutations (causing WNT pathway activation), as well as high levels of CpG island methylator phenotype {32052185;30179900;25216220;25127095;28614199;26924569;27305845;28543708}. A further step in progression may involve downregulation of SMOC1 expression via CpG island hypermethylation, associated with protuberant TSA morphology and high-grade dysplasia {38429655;29435136}, along with TP53 mutation and nuclear β-catenin accumulation, but it does not involve mismatch repair deficiency {25216220;24603588}. TSAs with BRAF mutations predominantly develop serrated dysplasia. Loss of p16 expression is observed only in BRAF-mutant TSAs.

Activating mutations of KRAS are present in 30% of TSAs {25216220;25127095}. These are often located in the left colon and more frequently develop without an associated serrated lesion (i.e. de novo) or may be accompanied by a goblet cell–rich hyperplastic polyp. In the distal colorectum, TSAs show protuberant morphology and ectopic crypt foci more frequently, as well as contain RSPO fusions {32052185}. TSAs with KRAS mutations tend to progress to conventional adenomatous dysplasia.

Macroscopic appearance

TSAs of the proximal colon may appear as sessile plaque-like lesions whereas those of the distal colon are usually polypoid, broad-based lesions with a surface texture resembling a pinecone or coral pattern {25216220;25127095;24225759}.

Histopathology

TSAs in their most characteristic form show a tubulovillous or villous architecture and crypts with slit-like serration lined by tall columnar cells with dense eosinophilic cytoplasm. The lesional cells show pencillate nuclei with smooth nuclear membranes and pale chromatin that is distinct from the dense hyperchromasia seen in conventional adenomas. Mitotic figures and apoptotic debris are inconspicuous. Ectopic crypt formations, defined as rounded epithelial protrusions into the lamina propria that are not anchored to the muscularis mucosae, may be present on the lateral aspects of villous projections of protuberant TSAs, but they are rarely present in flat TSAs. These ectopic crypt foci represent the proliferative compartment of the epithelium and show increased Ki-67 staining by immunohistochemistry. Most TSAs contain only scattered goblet cells, although mucin / goblet cell–rich TSAs do occur; this feature is of no clinical consequence {28295534}. Some serrated lesions with characteristic endoscopic appearances have been termed as “superficially serrated adenomas” {29789649} and share features of early forms of TSA {30007084} and should be classified as such. These polyps may present as small solitary lesions or the flat shoulder component of a large protuberant TSA. They primarily consist of straight adenomatous crypts with superficial serration and can be misdiagnosed as tubular adenoma or SSL with dysplasia.

Up to 50% of TSAs are accompanied by a non-dysplastic serrated precursor lesion that may take the form of a microvesicular hyperplastic polyp, a goblet cell–rich hyperplastic polyp, or an SSL {25589791}. Whether the BRAF-mutated polyps of the proximal colon are more appropriately classified as SSLs with TSA-like dysplasia or as TSA is somewhat controversial; any presence of the distinctive appearance of eosinophilic cytoplasm and/or ectopic crypts in an SSL warrants the diagnosis of TSA.

There is insufficient evidence of grading of TSA. Although most TSAs show only pale nuclei with mild nuclear atypia, some can display foci with loss of serration and acquisition of marked nuclear hyperchromasia that mimics adenomatous (conventional) dysplasia. Rarely, lesions may retain the serrated appearance but show nuclei with high-grade atypia {25216220;24225759;24603588}. Polyps with features of both TSA and conventional adenoma (usually tubulovillous adenoma) have been termed “mixed adenomas” or “serrated tubulovillous adenomas”. These terms are not recommended, and they probably represent progression of TSA. This phenomenon is almost always seen in polyps that meet the size criteria for an advanced adenoma (> 10 mm), which determines the recommended surveillance interval. An adenomatous polyp with any amount of TSA component should be classified as TSA with low-grade or high-grade dysplasia on the basis of nuclear cytology.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a non-invasive flat or polypoid lesion; slit-like crypt serrations and/or ectopic crypts.

Desirable: a dense eosinophilic cytoplasm; pale pencillate nuclei.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

TSAs are associated with an increased risk of large metachronous serrated polyps and colorectal carcinoma, especially when they are large {28927878;22710576;25399542;22763141;26677986}. Recommendations regarding appropriate surveillance intervals are based on a low level of evidence and vary by country {22710576;22763141;28450390}. The presence of synchronous conventional adenomas may affect surveillance intervals {26855297}.
