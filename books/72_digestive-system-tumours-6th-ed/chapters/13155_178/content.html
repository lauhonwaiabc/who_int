13.1.5.5 Ganglioneuroma and ganglioneuromatosis

Definition

Ganglioneuroma is a benign tumour composed of mature ganglion cells, Schwann cells, and unmyelinated axons.

ICD-O coding

94900/0 Ganglioneuroma, NOS

94910/0 Ganglioneuromatosis

ICD-11 coding

2E92.4Y & XH03L9 Ganglioneuroma of large intestine

2E92.4Y & XH6LR5 Ganglioneuromatosis of large intestine

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Polypoid ganglioneuroma (94900/0); diffuse ganglioneuromatosis (94910/0)

Localization

Most cases of polypoid ganglioneuroma and ganglioneuromatosis occur within the large intestine with a predilection for the left side and the rectum. Rarely tumours arise within the appendix.

Clinical features

Ganglioneuromas arise in three main clinical settings: (1) solitary polypoid ganglioneuroma, which has no known syndromic associations; (2) multiple polypoid ganglioneuromas, also known as ganglioneuromatous polyposis and is associated with PTEN hamartoma (Cowden) syndrome; and (3) diffuse ganglioneuromatosis, which occurs in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2B (MEN2B) or (less commonly) neurofibromatosis type 1, and usually shows diffuse mural involvement {19118783}.

Polypoid ganglioneuromas present as small mucosal polyps, and are usually detected incidentally during endoscopy. The clinical presentation of diffuse ganglioneuromatosis is quite variable, including acute intestinal obstruction, megacolon, and intestinal motility disorders; or it may be detected incidentally. Approximately 40% of individuals with MEN2B have diffuse ganglioneuromatosis of the GI tract. Most patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2B (MEN2B) manifest gastrointestinal symptoms starting in infancy or early childhood.

Epidemiology

This is a rare disorder and occurs across a wide age range, and no sex predilection has been identified among published cases {26424825;33785023}.

Etiology

Multiple polypoid ganglioneuromas / ganglioneuromatous polyposis is often associated with PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (Cowden syndrome) {9259288}. Intestinal ganglioneuromatosis has a well-established association with MEN2B, and is less commonly related to neurofibromatosis type 1. MEN2B is associated with germline mutations in the RETproto-oncogene and is associated with a 70–100% risk of medullary thyroid carcinoma {8570194}. Individuals with MEN2B often have mucosal neuromas of the lips and tongue, ganglioneuromatosis of the GI tract, distinctive facies with enlarged lips, and a marfanoid body habitus {980061}.

Pathogenesis

For syndromic tumours, see <<16.1.1>>Genetic tumour syndromes of the digestive system.

Macroscopic appearance

Ganglioneuromas typically present as small mucosal polyps of < 20 mm. Diffuse ganglioneuromatosis is deeper and more diffuse and may only be identified on resection specimens.

Histopathology

The solitary lesions are typically polypoid and/or sessile, and are composed of ganglion cells, spindled Schwann cells, and axons within the lamina propria. Cystic glands can be present, reminiscent of juvenile polyps in some cases, and scattered eosinophils may be present. In contrast, diffuse ganglioneuromatosis is characterized by a poorly demarcated, nodular or diffuse proliferation of nerve fibres, ganglion cells, and supporting cells of the enteric nervous system. Their growth pattern varies, from fusiform, hyperplastic expansions of the myenteric plexus to confluent transmural ganglioneuromatous proliferations that distort the myenteric plexus and infiltrate the bowel wall.

Immunohistochemical markers of neural differentiation (neurofilament, neuron-specific enolase, synaptophysin, and PHOX2B) can be used to highlight the ganglion cells {23715162}. S100 is diffusely positive in the Schwannian component and delineates the ganglion cells, which are S100-negative. Neurofilament positive axons may also be present.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not clinically relevant, unless an inherited tumour syndrome is suspected.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mixture of Schwann cells and ganglion cells.

Desirable: S100 positive in Schwann cells and negative in ganglion cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Both solitary and multifocal forms follow a benign course. Clinical recognition and accurate diagnosis allow for identification of individuals with PTEN hamartoma syndrome, MEN2B, or neurofibromatosis type 1, who are at risk of developing other neoplasms and require genetic screening.
