2.0.1 Tumours of the oesophagus: Introduction

The sixth-edition Digestive system tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series features substantial revisions to the sections on malignant oesophageal tumours of epithelial origin and their precursor lesions. These updates reflect our deepened understanding of pathophysiology, improved prognostic insights, and significant advances in case management strategies. Recent molecular and genetic discoveries have informed a more nuanced classification system, enabling more precise therapeutic approaches and potentially improving clinical outcomes.

Oesophageal epidermoid metaplasia is a new entry in this edition. This epithelial condition has been regularly detected in association with oesophageal squamous neoplasia and in patients at risk of developing it. Furthermore, molecular alterations such as TP53 mutations have been found concurrently in both epidermoid metaplasia and associated squamous cell carcinoma, suggesting a precursor relationship.

The sections on Barrett dysplasia, oesophageal squamous dysplasia, adenocarcinomas of the oesophagus and oesophagogastric junction, and oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma have been revised to reflect worldwide epidemiological changes and evolving clinical practices. The histopathology descriptions have been expanded to address feedback from the previous edition.

Given the major role of endoluminal therapy for early oesophageal neoplasms, the Paris classification is highlighted where appropriate, as pathologists must be fully aware of the clinical classification of endoscopically detected lesions.

We now have a better understanding of the molecular underpinnings of carcinogenesis in oesophageal adenocarcinoma and its precursor lesions, and the relevant sections have been updated accordingly. In particular, the role of p53 immunohistochemistry in Barrett oesophagus and related dysplasia is discussed.

Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy has become a mainstay in the treatment of both types of oesophageal carcinoma. In addition, targeted therapies and immunotherapies have become increasingly common for advanced oesophageal carcinoma. Sections on both these carcinomas elaborate upon the clinical application of biomarkers to guide the care of patients with advanced neoplasms.

The section on undifferentiated carcinoma has been expanded and revised, reflecting our growing experience with this category of neoplasms and our enhanced molecular understanding. This edition has established a subset of undifferentiated gastro-oesophageal carcinomas, which have unique morphology and genetic deficiencies of various members of the SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex.

In this edition, adenosquamous, mucoepidermoid carcinomas and adenoid cystic carcinomas are grouped into a single section, given their shared characteristics.

Finally, the topic of neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) of the oesophagus has been removed from this section and is discussed in a unique chapter covering all NENs encountered in the digestive system (section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system).
