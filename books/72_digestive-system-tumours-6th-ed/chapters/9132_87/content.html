9.1.3.2 Biliary adenofibroma

Definition

Biliary adenofibroma (BAF) is a benign intrahepatic, solid-microcystic, epithelial neoplasm, comprising variably dilated tubular structures lined by non–mucin-secreting biliary epithelium and supported by a fibrotic stromal scaffolding.

ICD-O coding

90130/0 Adenofibroma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E92.7 & XH91Y8 Adenofibroma, NOS, of liver or intrahepatic bile ducts

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

No preferential location within the liver

Clinical features

Abdominal pain is usually the presenting symptom, but some cases are discovered incidentally.

Epidemiology

BAF is a rare benign neoplasm, with < 35 cases reported in the medical literature {21151526;28266931;40026322}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

BAF is considered a primary epithelial neoplasm with a secondarily induced stroma {12717255}. A close morphological resemblance to biliary microhamartoma (von Meyenburg complex) and a similar expression pattern of foregut epithelial antigens (D10-positive and 1F6-negative) have been noted, although there is no evidence of transformation of biliary microhamartoma to BAF. The immunophenotype is different from that of peribiliary glands, which makes them an unlikely source of this tumour {12717255}.

Multiplex PCR assay did not identify the presence of common cancer hotspot mutations in four BAFs {28266931}. By array comparative genomic hybridization, multiple clonal cytogenetic alternations were found, providing support for the neoplastic nature of BAF. The amplifications of CCND1 and ERBB2 suggest that BAF may have the ability to behave aggressively. A mutation in CDKN2A or NRAS was identified in 2 cases with malignant transformation {28078134;35178267}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is typically solitary and 17–160 mm in diameter. It is unencapsulated, well circumscribed, and round to oval. On cut section, typically both solid and microcystic areas appear in varying proportions, with the latter often sponge-like in appearance. Rare cases show multiple macrocysts > 10 mm in diameter.

Histopathology

BAF is composed of glandular structures shaped into acini, tubules, and cysts embedded in abundant fibrotic stroma. The tubules are often dilated, branched, and may exhibit complex configurations. The cysts are of various sizes; some feature intraluminal polypoid projections. The single layer of non–mucin-secreting epithelium is composed of cuboidal to low columnar cells and is frequently flattened. The lining cells typically have amphophilic cytoplasm, bland round nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli, and minimal contour irregularity. Apocrine snouts are notable in some cases. The background stroma is collagenous and relatively hypocellular with scattered, bland, spindle-shaped myofibroblasts. A patchy mononuclear inflammatory infiltrate can be present.

The biliary phenotype of the epithelial lining is reflected in its immunoreactivity for EMA, CK7, CK19, and CA19-9. The Ki-67 proliferation index is never > 10% in the epithelial component and is always < 1% in the stromal part.

A range of premalignant changes leading to invasive carcinoma is noted in half of the reported cases of epithelial dysplasia including elongated hyperchromatic nuclei and nuclear pseudostratification {28266931}, as well as architectural disturbance with intracystic, complex, papillary proliferations and cribriform formations {25374710;26184026}. The carcinoma arising from BAF is a conventional adenocarcinoma, probably representing small duct–type intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (SD-iCCA) {26778388;26885426;28078134}. A rare microcystic-cribriform carcinoma was observed in 1 case {27631648}.

A differential diagnosis is the tubulocystic differentiation of SD-iCCA, in which BAF-like areas are often present {35697170}. Identical mutations in chromatin-remodelling genes (e.g. ARID1A, BAP1, IDH2, and PBRM1) or FGFR2::MCU fusion gene were detected in both adenocarcinoma and BAF-like areas, indicating a malignant nature of the BAF-like components in those cases {38946053}. In cases where the diagnosis of BAF is considered, careful exclusion of the tubulocystic differentiation of SD-iCCA is advised.

Cytology

Cytology is not suitable for the diagnosis of BAF.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a solid-microcystic benign epithelial neoplasm; microcystic and tubuloacinar glandular structures lined by non–mucin-secreting biliary epithelium; supporting fibrotic stroma.

Note: recently, the entity of BAF and its distinction from cholangiocarcinoma with tubulocystic growth has been questioned; therefore, caution and meticulous exclusion of malignant growth must be exerted before making the definitive diagnosis of BAF.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 50% of reported cases of BAF had malignant transformation, which is characterized by the appearance of cellular and architectural atypia. Incomplete excision is followed by recurrence {28266931}.
