14.2.4.3.5 Somatostatinoma

Definition

Somatostatinoma is a functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (PanNET) composed of somatostatin-producing cells associated with uncontrolled somatostatin secretion causing somatostatinoma syndrome.

ICD-O coding

81560/3 Somatostatinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C10.1 & XH5VH0 Somatostatinoma of pancreas

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Somatostatinomas can arise throughout the pancreas, but approximately two thirds arise within the pancreatic head {10374671;17645474;33144470}.

Clinical features

The clinical manifestations of excess somatostatin secretion are less dramatic and distinctive compared with the classic clinical features observed in other functioning PanNETs {1969977;10374671;19719976;17645474}. The classic triad of somatostatinoma syndrome includes diabetes / glucose intolerance (resulting from insulin inhibition), cholelithiasis (due to CCK inhibition), and diarrhoea/steatorrhea (secondary to a decrease of pancreatic enzymes and bicarbonate secretion). Many patients with somatostatinomas also experience gastric hypochlorhydria due to decreased gastric acid secretion. However, these symptoms rarely present simultaneously, complicating the differentiation between symptoms caused by elevated plasma somatostatin levels and symptoms caused by tumour localization or mass effects. Tumour mass–related symptoms may include abdominal pain, weight loss, jaundice, nausea, and vomiting. Given this overlap, somatostatinoma syndrome needs to be confirmed by a fasting plasma somatostatin level that is ≥ 3 times above the upper reference limit of > 25 pmol/L (> 60 pg/mL). In cases where results are inconclusive, secretin or calcium stimulation test can be useful, although these tests lack standardization {25905263}. Due to these challenges in clinical diagnosis, the initial identification is frequently based on imaging studies, such as CT, MRI, and EUS. Adjunct tests include somatostatin receptor scintigraphy, and PET using specific tracers: 18F-FDOPA, 11C-5-HTP, 68Ga-DOTATOC, and 68Ga-DOTATATE {20652423}.

Epidemiology

Somatostatinomas are extremely rare neoplasms, with an annual incidence of 1 case per 40 million people, accounting for < 1% of all functioning PanNETs {17645474}. The mean patient age at diagnosis is 50 years (range: 22–72 years), with only 44.4% of patients aged < 50 years. Somatostatinomas show a female predilection {10374671;33144470}.

Etiology

The etiology of pancreatic somatostatinoma remains unknown. Pancreatic somatostatinomas are generally considered sporadic, with no clear hereditary predisposition. In contrast, non-functioning somatostatin-producing tumours have been described in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) and von Hippel–Lindau syndrome (VHL) {10374671;15657529;7496080;11040195}. Neurofibromatosis type 1 has also been associated with non-functioning somatostatin-producing tumours, but unlike MEN1 and VHL, these tumours are typically found in the ampulla of Vater or duodenum rather than in the pancreas {7745981}. See #49164Table 14.06.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of pancreatic somatostatinoma is unknown. Activating somatic mutations in EPAS1 (also called HIF2A) have been described in patients with polycythaemia, multiple paragangliomas, and duodenal or pancreatic somatostatin-producing D-cell tumours (polycythaemia-paraganglioma-somatostatinoma syndrome, also called Pacak–Zhuang syndrome) {23361906}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pancreatic somatostatinomas are grossly indistinguishable from other PanNETs. These neoplasms are solitary, well circumscribed, and large (average diameter: 50–60 mm) {10374671;20043348}. On cross-section, they are unencapsulated and composed of yellow to tan parenchyma. They usually have a multinodular architecture and demonstrate invasive growth into adjacent structures, such as the duodenum and spleen.

Histopathology

Pancreatic somatostatinomas display the well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Somatostatinomas may display an occasional paraganglioma-like pattern {18310290}. Their architecture is often described as trabecular, solid, or acinar, and are composed of cuboidal to round cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm and uniform nuclei. Unlike duodenal somatostatin-producing neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), pancreatic somatostatinomas usually do not show a tubular or glandular architectural pattern with intraglandular psammomatous calcifications. Vascular and perineural invasion are frequent, but extensive necrosis is typically absent.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells of pancreatic somatostatinomas are positive for synaptophysin and INSM1. Chromogranin A is less consistently expressed and may even be absent. Somatostatin is expressed in most tumour cells, with various degrees of intensity. Most tumours also contain scattered cells immunoreactive for other hormones, such as PP, calcitonin, gastrin, ACTH, glucagon, and insulin.

Grading

Pancreatic somatostatinomas are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification). The Ki-67 proliferation index and mitotic count have rarely been reported in literature. However, the available evidence suggests that most pancreatic somatostatinomas are classified as G2 NETs.

Cytology

The cytology of somatostatinoma is the same as that of other PanNETs and is diagnosed as category 7 (malignant) using the WHO Reporting System for Pancreaticobiliary Cytopathology (WHO System) {38709670}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinical and laboratory evidence of somatostatin excess; well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: somatostatin immunohistochemistry expression; SSTR2 positive immunohistochemistry; DAXX and ATRX for prognostic assessment (selected cases).

Staging

The staging of pancreatic somatostatinomas is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for NETs of the pancreas.

Prognosis and prediction

There are insufficient survival data for patients with pancreatic somatostatinomas. However, the 5-year overall survival rate ranges from 60–100% in patients with localized disease to 15–60% in patients with distant metastases. Large tumour size (> 30 mm) and lymph node involvement are markers of poor prognosis {10374671}.
