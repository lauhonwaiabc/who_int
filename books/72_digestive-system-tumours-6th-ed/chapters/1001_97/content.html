10.0.1 Tumours of the gallbladder and extrahepatic bile ducts: Introduction

This chapter covers epithelial neoplasms of the gallbladder and extrahepatic bile ducts (EHBDs) with the exception of neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs). NENs are now outlined in a separate chapter in this volume. Also, the rare primary haematolymphoid neoplasms and mesenchymal tumours are described in their respective chapters.

Carcinomas of the gallbladder and EHBDs display significant geographical variation, with high incidences of gallbladder carcinomas reported in Chile (among the Indigenous Mapuche), India, and south-eastern Asia. EHBD carcinomas are especially frequent in eastern Asian countries, especially Thailand and the Republic of Korea.

There are many parallels among non-invasive epithelial neoplasms of the gallbladder, the large intrahepatic and the extrahepatic bile ducts, and the pancreas. They often share directions of differentiation and genetic alterations, and they all can progress to invasive cancer. For these reasons, in the sixth edition we have tried to further harmonize the nomenclature across these anatomical sites. We recognize that this harmonization is provisional, since many of these entities are rare and evidence is limited.

In the gallbladder, mass-forming intraepithelial neoplasms have been provisionally designated as intracholecystic neoplasms, which are further subcategorized. In the extrahepatic bile ducts, non-invasive mass-forming epithelial tumours now include intraductal papillary neoplasm, intraductal tubulopapillary neoplasm, and intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm as separate entities. The same mass-forming neoplasms may arise in the intrahepatic biliary tree. They are described in this chapter and are only referred to in the chapter on liver, with the exception of mucinous cystic neoplasm, which is far more prevalent in the liver. Like in the previous edition, microscopic intraepithelial neoplasia in the biliary tract is designated as biliary intraepithelial neoplasia. All precursor lesions are now graded according to a two-tiered classification system, with the former low-grade and intermediate-grade dysplasias now classified as low-grade {26559377}, consistent with tumours in the pancreas and the intestine.

Many tumours developing in the gallbladder, the EHBDs, and the large intrahepatic bile ducts share common pathogenetic mechanisms, with a substantial role for chronic inflammation, mainly related to gallstones, helminthic infections, or specific chronic inflammatory diseases (e.g. primary sclerosing cholangitis). Longstanding chronic inflammation promotes the development of the above-mentioned precursor lesions {23809246}.

Although adenocarcinoma is the most common type of biliary tract carcinoma, tumours may display a wide range of histological subtypes. Carcinomas of the gallbladder and EHBDs are characterized by the accumulation of genetic abnormalities, with TP53 mutations being the most common, and others, such as ERBB2 (HER2) amplifications, offering options for targeted treatment. In addition to common molecular alterations shared by gallbladder and EHBD carcinomas, some specific alterations have been described in EHBD carcinomas.
