3.1.1.7 Oxyntic gland adenoma

Definition

Oxyntic gland adenoma (OGA) is an intramucosal neoplasm composed of cells with mild cytological atypia, predominantly differentiating into chief cells, along with a variable proportion of cells exhibiting differentiation towards parietal cells and mucous neck cells.

ICD-O coding

81402/0 Oxyntic gland adenoma, low-grade

81402/2 Oxyntic gland adenoma, high-grade

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

OGA occurs predominantly in non-atrophic oxyntic mucosa (80%), similarly to gastric adenocarcinoma of fundic gland type (GA-FG) {29648578}.

Clinical features

In Japan, most lesions are identified during endoscopic screening; elsewhere, most patients present with reflux symptoms {22472957;26335020}. Endoscopy reveals a polypoid, subsurface tumour or flat lesion with faded, whitish mucosa {24338239;25820416;31375767}. Vasodilatation or branched vessels may be visible on the tumour surface {24338239;26962407;31375767}.

Epidemiology

This is a rare neoplasm. In individuals undergoing upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, the described prevalence of OGA/GA-FG is 0.01–0.36% {26962407;36792200;38294208}. In gastric cancer cases, one study reported the occurrence of new cases of OGA/GA-FG as only 3 cases (0.05%) out of > 6000 resected specimens, while another study reported 6 cases (1.6%) out of 373 specimens {23110017;24011952}. Furthermore, out of 506 early gastric cancer cases with resection by endoscopic submucosal dissection, 5 cases (0.98%) were identified as OGA/GA-FG {26185396}. The relative incidence of OGA and GA-FG is 3:7 {38162862}. The M:F ratio is 1:1.12, and most cases occur in patients aged 60–70 years {38294208}.

Etiology

The etiology remains unclear, but since most cases occur in individuals who are either Helicobacter pylori–naïve or have undergone eradication therapy, OGAs/GA-FGs are believed to be H. pylori–independent {38162862}.

Pathogenesis

Data on the molecular features of OGA/GA-FG are limited. GNAS mutations are a recurring genetic abnormality {25288233;31375767;39382261}. TP53 mutations are absent. There are conflicting reports of WNT/β-catenin pathway activation {25288233;36094595}.

Macroscopic appearance

OGAs typically manifest as small nodules (< 10 mm) {22472957;26335020;31375767}.

Histopathology

These tumours are in the spectrum of oxyntic gland neoplasms {31375767}. OGAs are formed by clusters of tightly packed glands. Glandular anastomosis, branching, dilation, and even cribriforming may be present. The tumour glands may show multilayering of cells and nuclear stratification. OGA is composed of cells that differentiate into chief cells, parietal cells, or both. A subset of cells differentiating into mucous neck cells may also be present. The proportion of each cell subtype differs among cases. The chief cell–predominant pattern is the most common and is characterized by glandular proliferation of monotonous columnar cells with basophilic cytoplasm. In the mixed cell pattern, in addition to cells with chief cell differentiation, a subset of cells with parietal cell differentiation are scattered in the superficial region of the tumour, more reminiscent of normal oxyntic glands. Cytonuclear atypia is usually minimal, with only mild nuclear enlargement and subtle nuclear irregularity. Mitotic figures are rare and the Ki-67 index is low (< 5%) {38162862}. Typically, the tumour is located mainly in the deeper mucosa and is covered by normal foveolar epithelium. A pushing interface with the surrounding mucosa and prolapse-type misplacement are characteristic of OGA and should not be misinterpreted as evidence of GA-FG. The diagnosis of the latter is reserved for those with submucosal invasion and atypical cellular differentiation.

Although the diagnosis of histologically typical cases does not require immunohistochemistry, OGAs are diffusely positive for MUC6, while pepsinogen I can be used to highlight chief cells and H+/K+ ATPase is expressed by parietal cells {20410811}. In biopsy specimens, OGA can be challenging to differentiate from normal oxyntic glands, but the diffuse strong positivity of cyclin D1 in OGA is helpful in the differentiation {39863113}. Regarding differential diagnosis, some of the architectural patterns (e.g. clustered and solid glands and anastomosing cords) can mimic a neuroendocrine tumour (NET) {26335020}. Although some lesions display synaptophysin expression, chromogranin A is negative. A subset of familial adenomatous polyposis–related pyloric gland adenomas show morphological overlap with OGA {27767239}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a small intramucosal lesion consisting of cells with mild cytonuclear atypia differentiating predominantly into chief cells and to a lesser extent into parietal cells.

Desirable: expression of cyclin D1; in selected cases, immunohistochemical confirmation of chief cell differentiation by pepsinogen I positivity and parietal cell differentiation by H+/K+ ATPase positivity may be helpful.

Staging

If high-grade dysplasia is present, this is staged as pTis.

Prognosis and prediction

OGAs are slow-growing and follow a benign course {29648578;31375767}. As these lesions progress, they may invade the submucosa, at which point they are classified as GA-FG. Even in such cases, endoscopic resection is sufficient treatment, as GA-FG is generally considered a low-grade malignancy {31375767;38162862}.
