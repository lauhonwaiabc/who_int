14.1.3.1 Duodenal and ampullary neuroendocrine neoplasms

Definition

Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) of the duodenum and ampulla are malignant epithelial tumours with neuroendocrine differentiation that arise in the duodenum or in the ampullary region.

Localization

More than 90% of duodenal neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) are located in the first or second part of the duodenum {26910321;28516311}. Ampullary NETs account for approximately 20–30% of all NETs {26910321;28516311}. Non-functioning gastrin cell NETs occur almost exclusively in the first part of the duodenum, while gastrinomas may be located in the duodenal bulb or (less commonly) in more distal parts of the duodenum or in the ampullary region {26649731;35553369}. Somatostatin cell NETs are preferentially located in the regions of major or minor duodenal papilla/ampulla {18310290;30913089}. Neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) and MiNEN are almost exclusive to the ampullary region {26910321}.

Clinical features

Most non-functioning NENs are clinically asymptomatic and discovered incidentally during endoscopy. If they are clinically apparent, it is because of bleeding or size with duodenal obstruction, abdominal pain, or nausea/vomiting. Ampullary NENs may lead to obstructive jaundice (most frequent clinical sign in 74% of cases) and rarely to acute pancreatitis {1458269;36646615}. Functioning tumours are rare (< 25%) {16253893}. Gastrinomas account for one third to half of duodenal gastrin cell NETs and, by definition, are associated with Zollinger–Ellison syndrome, characterized by hypergastrinaemia, gastric hypersecretion, refractory peptic ulcer disease, and diarrhoea due to uncontrolled secretion of gastrin {26649731;29326808}. Duodenal/ampullary NETs with somatostatinoma syndrome (diabetes mellitus, diarrhoea/steatorrhoea, gallstones, hypo/achlorhydria, and anaemia) are exceedingly rare {11606861;18310290}. Tumours with immunohistochemical somatostatin expression in the absence of the pertinent syndrome should be designated as somatostatin cell NETs and not as somatostatinomas. Carcinoid syndrome (due to a duodenal serotonin-producing tumour), hypoglycaemic syndrome (due to an insulinoma), glucagonoma syndrome, Cushing syndrome (due to an ACTH-producing NEN) or acromegaly (due to a GHRH-producing NET) are exceptionally rare {24006218;89070;15986979;28862247;19301154}.

Epidemiology

Duodenal and ampullary NENs are rare. However, the incidence of duodenal NETs has significantly increased in recent decades (from 0.027 cases per 100 000 population in 1983 to 1.1 cases per 100 000 population in 2010) according to data from the US SEER registry {26013986}. This increase is primarily due to early endoscopic detection {26013986}. A study from Japan reported that approximately 5% of all digestive system NENs are found in the duodenum {33189127}. Duodenal NECs are rarer than duodenal NETs. Duodenal-ampullary NETs, NECs, and MiNENs show a slight male predilection (M:F ratio: 1.25:1) {26013986;26910321;36646615}. The median patient age at diagnosis is 60 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 48–60 years) for NETs and 66 years (IQR: 58–71 years) for NECs based on a pathology case series {26649731}, and 63 years (IQR: 51–69 years) for MiNENs according to a systematic review {36646615}. The median age of patients with duodenal gastrin cell NETs is 64 years (IQR: 36–88 years) {26910321}, whereas somatostatin cell NETs, the predominant histological subtype of ampullary NETs, are diagnosed at a median age of 55 years (IQR: 44–63 years) {35553369} and affect women more frequently than men {36535044}.

Etiology

The etiology of sporadic duodenal and ampullary NENs is unknown. The role of Helicobacter pylori gastritis and a long-term use of proton-pump inhibitors in the development of sporadic non-functioning gastrin cell NETs has been suggested {17122515}.

Genetic factors

A minority (10–20%) of duodenal NENs arise in the setting of genetic tumour syndromes {16253893;30054824}. Patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) may develop multiple duodenal gastrinomas and rarely somatostatin cell tumours {17006979;17135306}. Nearly one third of duodenal gastrinomas occur in patients with MEN1 syndrome {1678681;17006979;32138393;34629807}, and most MEN1-associated gastrinomas occur in the duodenum rather than in the pancreas {17006979;1968616}. MEN1 mutations are detected in 40% of sporadic duodenal gastrinomas {10634374}. Ampullary somatostatin cell tumours may be associated with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) (in ~20% of cases) [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]] {18310290;20300877;35553369}. Exceptional cases of duodenal/ampullary NETs associated with von Hippel–Lindau syndrome are described {11201368;23913169}. Rare activating mosaic mutations of the EPAS1 (encoding HIF2α) lead to somatostatin cell tumours in combination with paragangliomas and polycythaemia {22931260;27679736}. Other molecular alterations identified in duodenal NETs include WNT pathway alterations (CTNNB1 mutations and APC methylation) and CDKN2A methylation {11095446;11559529;18632876}. Loss of DAXX expression has been reported in few duodenal NETs {23954140}. A duodenal NEC harbouring immunohistochemical loss of both MSH2 and MSH6 expression and microsatellite instability has been reported {25465415}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of sporadic NENs of the duodenum and the ampullary region is not known. The MEN1-related gastrinomas in the duodenum are associated with hyperplastic gastrin and somatostatin cell changes within the adjacent mucosa and Brunner glands {15887103;17135306;18058264;21095540}. No gastrin or somatostatin cell hyperplasia was found in patients with sporadic duodenal gastrinomas {15887103}; however increased number of gastrin cells were described in the mucosa adjacent to duodenal non-functioning G cell NETs {17122515}. About 50% of MEN1-associated duodenal NETs reveal allelic loss of the wildtype MEN1 allele, indicating that other mechanisms frequently inactivate the wildtype allele {17135306}. Allelic loss occurs at the same frequency in multifocal tiny lesions (300 μm), defining these as neoplasms, whereas hyperplastic gastrin cells showed retention of both alleles {17135306}. Although MEN1-related duodenal gastrinomas exhibit an immune-excluded microenvironment, their tumour and stromal cells express proinflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin 17B, which stimulate the neuroendocrine phenotype {34750164;38229586}. In NETs associated with NF1, somatic inactivation of the wildtype NF1 allele and/or loss of chromosome 22 was identified in a subset of cases {29849115}.

Macroscopic appearance

Duodenal and ampullary NETs are usually small (< 20 mm; median size: 10 mm), polypoid, submucosal lesions with an intact or focally ulcerated overlying mucosa; only rarely are they large, infiltrating tumours {10189128;18310290;26910321}. At endoscopy, most duodenal NETs have the appearance of small, erythematous or pale, rounded lesions {35233848}. NECs and MiNENs present as polypoid or ulcerated masses with a median size of 25 mm (IQR: 17–35 mm) and 23 mm (IQR: 15–35 mm), respectively {15832081;26910321;36646615}. The median size is 7 mm for both functioning and non-functioning gastrin cell NETs {26649731} and 20 mm for ampullary somatostatin cell NETs {35553369}. MEN1-associated gastrinomas and somatostatin cell NETs are typically tiny and multicentric {17006979;18310290}. NF1-associated somatostatin cell NETs are rarely multiple, involving both the ampulla of Vater and the minor papilla {30913089}.

Histopathology

NET

NETs display the well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]] and are composed of uniform tumour cells with round to oval nuclei with finely granular (salt-and-pepper) chromatin and an organoid architecture {32138393}. They usually show little pleomorphism, and necrosis is very rarely seen.

Duodenal gastrin cell NETs (either functioning or non-functioning) are typically arranged in a predominantly trabecular pattern, with solid or pseudoglandular architecture in some cases {26649731}. Some gastrinomas (MEN1-associated or sporadic) may rarely contain psammoma bodies {15887103;26649731}. Ampullary somatostatin cell NETs have a predominantly tubuloglandular pattern {2422964;18310290;26910321;30913089} and often contain psammoma bodies, especially in cases associated with NF1, and, rarely, focal luminal mucin {2422964;18310290;26910321;30913089}. A few somatostatin cell NETs are associated with stromal amyloid deposits {34541974;34549672}. The rare duodenal serotonin-producing NETs may show a nested architecture with peripheral palisading, akin to jejunal-ileal serotonin-producing NETs {32138393;26910321}. NETs with ectopic secretion generally exhibit the conventional NET morphology {24006218}. A clear cell ampullary NET described was associated with von Hippel–Lindau syndrome {23913169}.

NEC

NECs show the poorly differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage and are subdivided into small cell or large cell subtypes [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. NECs are high-grade carcinomas growing in sheets or poorly formed trabeculae or nests. Mitotic activity is brisk (often > 20 mitoses/2 mm2), and necrosis is frequent {26910321}.

MiNEN

Duodenal and ampullary MiNEN display the classic morphological features of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine neoplasia [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Endocrine and neuroendocrine tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 10). https://publications.iarc.who.int/645. Bookref]]. Some NETs and NECs arise in association with an adenoma {15832081;30001239}. MiNENs typically consist of both neuroendocrine components (usually NEC) and non-neuroendocrine components (typically adenocarcinoma) {36646615}.

Amphicrine-like carcinoma

Duodenal and ampullary amphicrine-like carcinoma may occur as amphicrine-like adenocarcinoma also with goblet cell features {36646615;25702653;22918208}.

Immunohistochemistry

NETs and NECs show the typical immunohistochemistry of NEN at other sites. Chromogranin A expression may be lost in a few somatostatin cell NETs, but stain diffusely though faintly within microgranules of NECs. The demonstration of keratin expression is useful in the distinction of NETs from paraganglioma/phaeochromocytoma. NENs are more likely to express CK8 and CK18 (e.g. as demonstrated by keratin AE1/AE3 and CAM5.2 staining) than CK7. However, CK7 and MUC1 are frequently expressed by ampullary somatostatin cell NETs {26910321;30913089}.

Hormone immunohistochemistry (gastrin, somatostatin, serotonin, insulin, glucagon, ACTH, and GHRH) is useful in the case of hyperfunctioning syndrome (e.g. gastrin in patients with Zollinger–Ellison syndrome) or for NET subtyping (especially gastrin and somatostatin). Duodenal NETs often express more than one hormone {14582691;2476943;32138393}. Non-functioning gastrin cell NETs, somatostatin cell NETs, and serotonin-producing NETs are defined as NETs that are exclusively or predominantly (> 50% of tumour cells) composed of gastrin-positive cells, somatostatin-positive cells, or serotonin-positive cells, respectively, with other hormones expressed in minor cell populations {18310290;26649731;32138393}. Additional hormones that may be infrequently expressed by non-functioning duodenal/ampullary NETs include calcitonin, CCK, insulin, and pancreatic polypeptide {2476943;10189128;32138393}. Duodenal NETs frequently express the transcription factors insulin gene enhancer protein ISL1 and PDX1, while CDX2 is negative or weak and focal {15517368;21739268;33849943}. Like pancreatic NETs, ampullary NETs may be S100-positive {38849197}. While gastrin cell tumours as a rule express SSTR2A and SSTR5, somatostatin cell NETs are typically negative for SSTR2A but retain SSTR5 expression {26910321}. Loss of RB1 expression may be useful to support a diagnosis of NEC, especially in the differential with G3 NETs {15517368}.

Grading

Duodenal and ampullary NENs are graded using the same system used for other gastroenteropancreatic NENs (see section Neuroendocrine neoplasms of the digestive system: Introduction and classification). Most duodenal and ampullary NETs (of any subtype) are G1 {26910321}; G3 NETs are very rare {34499237}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis for duodenal and ampullary NETs is similar to that of other gastrointestinal NETs and includes paragangliomas, which are usually negative for cytokeratin and positive for GATA3. The typical tubuloglandular pattern and the frequent CK7 and MUC1 expression of ampullary somatostatin cell NETs may cause confusion with well-differentiated adenocarcinomas. Unlike adenocarcinomas, however, somatostatin cell NETs are composed of uniform cells with bland nuclei and few mitotic figures. In endoscopic biopsies, they can also be confused with normal Brunner glands; well-differentiated adenocarcinoma; and composite gangliocytoma/neuroma and neuroendocrine tumour (CoGNET), which has, by definition, a gangliocytoma/neuroma component.

Small cell NEC should also be distinguished from other NENs, including NETs and large cell NECs, and from other small cell neoplasms, including lymphomas, melanomas, and sarcomas. Differential diagnosis of large cell NECs includes G3 NETs, small cell NECs, and poorly differentiated non-neuroendocrine carcinomas.

Ampullary NENs should be distinguished from pancreatic NENs invading the duodenum and ampulla by correlation with endoscopic and radiological findings.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

NET

Essential: well-differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: SSTR2 expression; positive immunohistochemistry for selected hormone products (e.g. somatostatin, gastrin) for subtyping.

NEC

Essential: poorly differentiated neuroendocrine morphology of epithelial lineage; neuroendocrine marker expression; high Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count.

Desirable: loss of RB1 expression or abnormal p53 expression.

MiNEN

Essential: a mixed tumour composed of two morphologically recognizable components; either type ≥ 30% of the neoplasm; neuroendocrine marker expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Desirable: assessment of Ki-67 proliferation index and/or mitotic count to grade the NET component or to prognostically stratify the NEC component; grading of the non-neuroendocrine component.

Amphicrine-like carcinoma

Essential: a mixed tumour with no morphologically distinct zones of neuroendocrine and non-neuroendocrine differentiation; ≥ 30% of the neoplasm; neuroendocrine marker expression in the neuroendocrine component.

Desirable: assessment of Ki-67 proliferation index.

Staging

The staging of duodenal and ampullary NETs is in line with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems for this site. For NECs, MiNENs, and amphicrine-like carcinomas, the staging follows the criteria of duodenal and ampullary carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Duodenal NETs may exhibit metastases, even when small in size {18520436;32088004;32839579}. Regional lymph node metastases are frequent in duodenal/ampullary NETs (30–80%), whereas distant metastases are much rarer (< 10%) {26910321;28516311;32088004;36535044}. The liver is the most common site of distant metastasis {17992565}. Ampullary somatostatin cell NETs commonly show deep invasion, lymphatic invasion, and lymph node metastases {26910321}. The metastatic potential of gastrin cell NETs is variable; gastrinomas are more aggressive (with more frequent lymph node and distant metastases) than non-functioning gastrin cell tumours {26649731;26910321}. NECs and MiNENs are generally deeply infiltrating and frequently show lymphatic and venous invasion, as well as lymph node and/or distant metastases {26910321;36646615}.

The 5-year overall survival rate for patients with duodenal NEN is reported to be 76% (95% confidence interval: 74.4–77.7), based on data from the SEER database (2000–2018) {39285515}. Histological differentiation is the most important prognostic factor in duodenal/ampullary NENs. The prognosis for patients with NETs is generally favourable and significantly better than that of patients with NEC, who typically have a poor prognosis {21043824;26910321}. Adverse prognostic factors in duodenal NEN cohort studies include tumour size ≥ 20 mm, vascular invasion, and distant metastasis {24448999;26910321;32088004}. G2 NETs are associated with a worse patient outcome compared with G1 tumours {26910321}. Risk factors for lymph node metastasis in duodenal NETs encompass histological subtype (gastrinoma, somatostatin cell tumour), invasion beyond the submucosa, lymphatic and/or vascular invasion, tumour G2, and tumour size ≥ 11 mm {26910321;28315861;33347833;36130981}. Nevertheless, lymph node metastases are not independent predictors of poor prognosis in patients with duodenal/ampullary NET {10189128;22476195;26910321;32088004;38685134}. Ampullary NENs seem to be more aggressive than duodenal NENs, partly due to a higher proportion of NECs in the ampullary region {24114680;32088004;34312800}. Negative prognostic factors for ampullary NETs include older age at diagnosis (> 60 years), tumour G2, pancreatic invasion, and distant metastasis {34312800;35553369}. Although tumour size is not a clear prognostic factor in ampullary NETs, it is associated with lymph node metastasis, particularly when using a cut-off value of 16 mm {35553369;32839579}. More than half of patients with ampullary NEC die from the disease, with a median survival of 10 months {15832081;35553369}. Similarly, patients with ampullary MiNENs show a poor prognosis, with disease recurrence and cancer-related death occurring in nearly half of cases over a median follow-up of 12 months {36646615}.
