13.1.3.2 Rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

Rhabdomyosarcoma (RMS) is a malignant neoplasm showing pure skeletal muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

89000/3 Rhabdomyosarcoma, NOS

89100/3 Rhabdomyosarcoma, embryonal type

89120/3 Rhabdomyosarcoma, spindle cell / sclerosing type

89200/3 Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B55.Y & XH83G1 & XA0KT3 Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, NOS, of biliary tract

2B55.Y & XH83G1 & XA5DY0 Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, NOS, of liver

2B55.Y & XH83G1 & XA6S21 Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, NOS, of retroperitoneum

Related terminology

Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma

Not recommended: myosarcoma; malignant rhabdomyoma; rhabdosarcoma; sarcoma botryoides.

Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

Not recommended: monomorphous round cell rhabdomyosarcoma.

Spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma

Acceptable: spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma; sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS) most commonly arises in the head and neck, including the orbit, and the genitourinary system. The GI tract, including biliary tract, liver, retroperitoneum, and abdomen, may occasionally be involved. The other types essentially never affect the gastrointestinal system (refer to Soft tissue and bone tumoursvolume of this series).

Clinical features

Clinical features of GI tract involvement include jaundice for ERMS affecting the biliary tree and constipation/obstruction for alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS) of the perineal area. RMSs arising at other sites typically present as a painless mass, which may or may not be rapidly growing and may or may not cause additional symptoms, depending on adjacent structures.

Epidemiology

Although RMSs are exceedingly rare in the digestive tract, peribiliary ERMS is most frequent {39272968;31610788}.

Etiology

ERMS is associated with several syndromes such as Costello syndrome (HRAS mutations), neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1 mutations), Noonan syndrome, and DICER1 syndrome {34095712}.

Pathogenesis

ERMS is characterized by multiple whole-chromosome aberrations including gains of chromosomes 2, 8, 11, 12, 13, and/or 20 {8764111;11807989} and loss of 11p15.5 {2798419}. About half of the cases show somatic mutations involving the RAS pathway (NRAS, KRAS, HRAS, NF1, FGFR4) {24436047}, and loss of function mutations involving the BCOR gene are seen in some cases {38722224}. About 10% of cases harbour TP53 mutations in which case they often morphologically exhibit diffuse anaplasia {33302115}. Botryoid-type ERMS is frequently associated with DICER1 mutations {34017064;39210566}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, the botryoid subtype of ERMS shows polypoid nodules directly underlying the epithelial surface in hollow viscera, including the biliary tree. Other types of ERMS, ARMS, and pleomorphic RMS have a fleshy cut appearance, whereas spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma (SCSRMS) has a whitish-grey and whorled cut surface.

Histopathology

The various types of RMS have different histological features; ERMS is the type detected in the GI tract. It is composed of rhabdomyoblasts that recapitulate various stages of myogenesis. Key histological features include variable cellularity (densely cellular areas alternating with loose, hypocellular areas), a combination of primitive round to spindled cells and variably differentiated rhabdomyoblasts, and a loose myxoid to myxocollagenous stroma. Occasionally, cells with cross-striations may be seen. Heterologous elements, including a cartilaginous component, may be seen in DICER1-associated ERMS {39210566}. Botryoid-type ERMS, a variant of ERMS with a distinctive grape-like gross appearance, shows condensation of the neoplastic cells beneath a zone of loose stroma and overlying intact surface epithelium. A smaller subset of cases shows a more prominent spindle cell component in which case a type of spindle cell RMS may be on the differential diagnosis. Focal anaplasia (scattered anaplastic cells admixed within numerous non-anaplastic tumour cells) or diffuse anaplasia (clusters of sheets of anaplastic cells) may be seen in ERMS and is defined by the presence of significantly enlarged, atypical tumour cells and/or atypical mitotic figures {18985676}.

Most RMSs show relatively strong desmin positivity, while this can be patchy in SCSRMS. Myogenin (nuclear) is typically diffusely positive in ARMS, while it is scattered in ERMS and SCSRMS. MYOD1 (nuclear) is diffusely positive, especially in SCSRMS. Pleomorphic RMS tends to be strongly and diffusely positive for desmin, while MYOD1 and myogenin are less frequently positive. ARMS may show aberrant expression of keratins and neuroendocrine markers such as synaptophysin or CD56 {18487991}. Spindle cell RMS with FUS/EWSR1::TFCP2 or MEIS1::NCOA2 fusion may show expression of keratins and/or ALK.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The diagnosis of ARMS is supported by identifying FOXO1 gene rearrangements. Molecular testing may be useful to diagnose other specific rare subtypes of RMS or to exclude entities in the differential diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

  • The features are dependent on type, but all cases show histological or immunohistochemical evidence of skeletal muscle differentiation
  • For ERMS: primitive round to spindle cell morphology, differentiating rhabdomyoblasts; multifocal, scattered myogenin staining
  • For ARMS: monotonous round cells; strong and diffuse myogenin staining; presence of PAX3, PAX7, or FOXO1rearrangements
  • For SCSRMS: purely spindled, hyalinized, and sclerosing patterns, or a combination of both
  • For spindle cell RMS with FUS/EWSR1::TFCP2 or MEIS1::NCOA2 fusion: presence of TFCP2 or MEIS1::NCOA2 fusion; mixed spindled and epithelioid morphology (or one these patterns)
  • For infantile spindle cell RMS: NCOA2 or VGLL2 fusion with various partners including TEAD1, SRF, and CITED2

Desirable:

  • For SCSRMS: NM_002478.5 c.365T>G p.(Leu122Arg) variant in MYOD1

Staging

The Intergroup Rhabdomyosarcoma Study Group (IRSG) staging system {22378628;35129294;33306276} is commonly used for paediatric RMS, which factors into account the site of tumour origin. Tumours involving the bile ducts, orbit, head and neck, or genitourinary tract are associated with a more favourable prognosis, while other sites are considered unfavourable.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis and prediction vary by subtype and stage. ERMS has generally a favourable prognosis, especially in patients aged 1–9 years, but the prognosis worsens with age. Presence of TP53 mutations or chromosome 3q loss may be associated with worse behaviour {37738543;31699828}.
