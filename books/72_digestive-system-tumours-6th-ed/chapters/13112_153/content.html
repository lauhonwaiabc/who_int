13.1.1.2 SDH-deficient gastrointestinal stromal tumour

Definition

Succinate dehydrogenase (SDH)-deficient gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) is a mesenchymal neoplasm that shows differentiation towards the interstitial cells of Cajal and is driven by dysfunction of the mitochondrial complex 2 (known as succinate dehydrogenase deficiency).

ICD-O coding

89361/3 Succinate dehydrogenase-deficient gastrointestinal stromal tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B5B.0 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour of the stomach

2B5B.1 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour of small intestine

Related terminology

Not recommended: paediatric wildtype GIST; type 2 GIST.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

With the exception of a single case report, all well-characterized SDH-deficient GISTs reported to date have arisen in the stomach {20305538;21997692;28324028}.

Clinical features

Although gastric location, multinodularity/multifocality, young age at onset, and lymph node metastases are clues to the diagnosis of SDH-deficient GIST, this tumour usually has clinical signs and symptoms that are indistinguishable from those of other GISTs {20305538}.

Epidemiology

SDH-deficient GISTs account for 3% of all GISTs (5–7.5% of gastric GISTs) in unselected adult populations {20305538;21997692}. They account for the overwhelming majority of GISTs diagnosed in children and the majority of GISTs diagnosed in young adults, with a median age of 22 years and female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:3) {20305538;21997692;22804613;27011036;21173220}.

Etiology

About 30% of SDH-deficient GISTs are associated with germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) in SDHA, and 20% with GPVs in SDHB, SDHC, or SDHD {23060355;22955521;23282968;29239034}. The remainder, approximately 50%, are associated with postzygotic systemic hypermethylation of the SDHC promoter locus (i.e. SDHC epimutation), the molecular signature of Carney triad – the syndromic but non-hereditary association of SDH-deficient GIST, SDH-deficient paraganglioma, and pulmonary chondroma {25540324;24859990;29239034;35285003}.

Although somatic-only biallelic inactivation of both alleles of one of the SDH genes has been reported in SDH-deficient tumours in the absence of syndromic disease, this is an extremely rare event {17634472;29239034;35285003}. Therefore, the diagnosis of SDH-deficient GIST is virtually pathognomonic of an SDH-deficient tumour syndrome (see section SDH-deficient tumour syndromes).

Pathogenesis

The succinate dehydrogenase complex, also known as the mitochondrial complex 2, comprises four protein subunits: SDHC and SDHD (the anchoring components), and SDHA and SDHB (the catalytic components). Additionally, SDHAF2 is required for flavination of the SDHA protein. If any component of the SDH complex is lost or dysfunctional, the entire complex becomes unstable, and the SDHB subunit is released into the cytoplasm where it rapidly degrades, accounting for the loss of expression of SDHB protein {19576851;22544211;22835832;29239034}. Dysfunction of this key enzyme causes accumulation of succinate, which acts as an oncometabolite behaving as a competitor of 2-oxoglutarate–dependent dioxygenases involved in a broad spectrum of tumorigenic pathways that include hypoxic regulation and epigenetic reprogramming including MGMT promoter hypermethylation {15987702;23550148;31666694;32234487;30616628;33116851;36198483}.

Macroscopic appearance

SDH-deficient GISTs present as relatively circumscribed but unencapsulated tumours centred in the muscularis propria and pushing into the gastric mucosal or serosal surface. Unlike other GISTs, SDH-deficient GISTs are commonly multifocal, demonstrate a multilobulated/plexiform growth pattern, and show frequent lymph node metastases {20305538;21753704;21997692;22804613}.

Histopathology

There are several morphological, clinical, and molecular clues to the separation of SDH-deficient GISTs from other GISTs (summarized in #48654Table 13.02). These include a younger age of onset, gastric location, multifocality, a plexiform or multinodular growth pattern, frequent lymph node metastases, a rich capillary network, and epithelioid predominance sometimes with a vaguely nested architecture {20305538;21753704;29239034}.

Loss of expression of SDHB (termed SDH deficiency) occurs in all SDH-deficient GISTs regardless of which SDH subunit is inactivated and is required for diagnosis. In addition to loss of expression of SDHB, SDH-deficient GISTs associated with SDHA mutation also show loss of expression of SDHA by immunohistochemistry, whereas tumours associated with SDHB, SDHC, or SDHD mutation, and SDHC epimutation, demonstrate preserved expression of SDHA {23060355;22955521;23282968}.

SDHB and SDHA immunohistochemistry may be difficult to interpret, particularly in small biopsies. Normal positive staining is strong, cytoplasmic, and distinctly granular (mitochondrial) and should always be identified in non-neoplastic cells, which act as internal positive controls, before a tumour is considered SDH-deficient/negative. In some SDH-deficient tumours, interpretation may be particularly challenging because a nonspecific diffuse cytoplasmic blush may be retained. In this setting, the weak and cytoplasmically diffuse, rather than granular, expression in the neoplastic cells that contrasts with the internal positive controls is a useful clue to the diagnosis of true SDH deficiency {19576851;29239034}.

Although SDH-deficient GISTs are not associated with KIT or PDGFRA mutations, they always show diffuse strong immunohistochemical expression of both KIT and DOG1 {20305538;21753704;29239034}. This can be useful in the differential diagnosis with PDGFRA-mutant GISTs, which may resemble SDH-deficient GISTs due to their frequent gastric location and epithelioid cell predominance but commonly show only weak/focal, or absent, KIT expression.

Cytology

The diagnosis of SDH-deficient GIST can be suggested on cytology if the morphology is compatible and there is material for immunohistochemistry. However, as firm diagnosis requires loss of SDHB expression in the presence of an internal positive control, extreme care is warranted when interpreting SDHB immunohistochemistry on small biopsies and cell block preparations, which may be subject to both false positive and false negative interpretations.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

SDH-deficient GISTs demonstrate a distinctly different gene expression profile to other GISTs {18483389;33806389} and lack somatic KIT or PDGFRA mutations {20305538;21997692}. Virtually all SDH-deficient GISTs are syndromic, being associated with germline SDHA mutations (30%), SDHB, SDHC, SDHD mutations (20%), or SDHC epimutation (50%). Therefore, all patients with SDH-deficient GISTs should be referred for genetic counselling/testing for SDH-deficient tumour syndromes, which are characterized by SDH-deficient GIST, paraganglioma/phaeochromocytoma, SDH-deficient renal carcinoma, and pulmonary chondroma (see section SDH-deficient tumour syndromes).

Immunohistochemistry for SDHB and SDHA can be very useful to assess the pathogenicity of genetic variants of uncertain significance in the SDH genes identified by this testing {25720320}. It is emphasized that, unlike SDHB, SDHC, SDHD, or SDHAF2, GPVs in SDHA are common incidental findings (estimated to occur in up to 0.3% of some populations) {21752896}. Furthermore, SDHA pathogenic variants show a low lifetime penetrance (estimated as 1.7%) {30201732}. Therefore, the clinical significance of SDHA variants, particularly if identified by large panel sequencing in the absence of SDH-deficient neoplasia, should be carefully considered {21752896;23060355}; and recommendations for surveillance and cascade testing of families with SDHA mutations vary {29978154;34021277}.

Carney triad (defined clinically as the syndromic but not hereditary association of SDH-deficient GIST, paraganglioma, and pulmonary chondroma) is characterized molecularly by postzygotic epimutation (promoter hypermethylation) of SDHC {24859990;25540324;33624983}. However, this can only be identified by specific DNA methylation assays that are not widely available. Therefore, it is reasonable to assign a presumptive clinical diagnosis of Carney triad to any individual with an SDH-deficient GIST if no GPVs in SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD, or SDHAF2 are identified on specific testing even in the absence of paraganglioma or pulmonary chondroma. It should be noted that some patients previously considered to have Carney triad have now been shown to have mosaicism for GPVs in SDH at low variant allele frequencies {31046099} or deep intronic pathogenic variants in the SDH genes that were previously undetected by older technologies {33195952}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a compatible morphology with loss of expression of SDHB (with satisfactory internal positive controls) and expression of KIT and/or DOG1 by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Although SDH-deficient GISTs are staged using the same TNM system as other GISTs, it is important to appreciate that, unlike other GISTs, the prognosis for SDH-deficient GISTs cannot be predicted by size and mitotic count.

Prognosis and prediction

Unlike other GISTs, the prognosis for SDH-deficient GISTs cannot be predicted by size and mitotic count {20305538;22804613;27340750;29239034;21997692}. That is, small mitotically inactive tumours frequently metastasize, and lymph node spread (rare in other GISTs) is common. When metastases do occur, they may be indolent or even lie dormant for years or decades. Despite a metastatic rate of up to 50% at 2 years and 74% at 5 years {37477937}, disease-specific survivals of greater than 80% at 10 and 15 years have been reported {37477937}.

It can be difficult to distinguish indolent disease from partial response to investigational therapies. However, current evidence suggests that SDH-deficient GISTs do not respond to imatinib but may show some response to next-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as regorafenib {36816783;37559050}. The presence of MGMT promoter hypermethylation has been used to justify the use of alkylating agents such as temozolomide with promising early results {30616628;33116851;36198483;35116662;36151992;34426440}.

The accumulation of additional molecular abnormalities such as KRAS and TP53 mutations, or chromosome 1q amplification has been associated with worse outcomes {37477937;27011036;29967109}. Focal loss of KIT and DOG1 expression by immunohistochemistry has been reported very rarely and always in association with morphologically dedifferentiated aggressive tumours {29967109;31072206;37781202}. It is probable that reports of poor prognosis for SDH-deficient gastric sarcomatoid malignancies lacking KIT and DOG1 expression represent dedifferentiated SDH-deficient GISTs {32501622}.

In view of the strongly syndromic nature of SDH-deficient neoplasia and the tendency for multifocality and metachronous disease, surveillance and lifelong follow-up for recurrence, metachronous disease, and other SDH-deficient neoplasms are indicated even in individuals in whom no germline sequence variant has been identified {29239034;34021277}.
