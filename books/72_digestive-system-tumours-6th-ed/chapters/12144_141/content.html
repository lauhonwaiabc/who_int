12.1.4.4 Monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma (MEITL) is an aggressive primary intestinal T-cell lymphoma of intraepithelial T lymphocytes, characterized by monomorphic cytomorphology, in most cases, and epitheliotropism, typically lacking an association with coeliac disease.

ICD-O coding

97172/3 Monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2B2Y & XH1AG7 & XA6452 Monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma of small intestine

Related terminology

Not recommended: type II enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MEITL arises most commonly in the small intestine, and less commonly in the colon, duodenum, or stomach; 20–35% of cases may show multifocal gastrointestinal tumours {23399895;22641357;27457239;21921780;31493002}. Tumour cells may disseminate to mesenteric lymph nodes, as well as to the lung, liver, brain, and skin {27862162;33028779;32641648;21566094;29650734;33646526}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with abdominal pain; gastrointestinal bleeding, obstruction, or perforation; diarrhoea; and weight loss {23399895;22641357;21921780}.

Epidemiology

MEITL is an uncommon lymphoma and with worldwide distribution {21921780;21753698;21423155}. Enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL) and MEITL together represent 2%, 6%, and 9% of all peripheral T-cell lymphomas in Asia, North America, and Europe, respectively {21566094}. MEITL cases greatly outnumber EATL cases in Asia; the reverse is true in Europe; and the incidence rates are approximately equal in North America. MEITL predominantly occurs in adults, with a reported median age of 54–67 years and a male predominance (M:F ratio: 2:1) {23399895;21921780;27600764;21323966;22641357;31914529;31493002;29052597}.

Etiology

There is no association with coeliac disease, because studies have shown an absence of coeliac serologies {23399895}. Rare reports of MEITL occurring in patients with coeliac disease may be coincidental events {31361171;21566094;29052597}.

Pathogenesis

MEITL classically originates from resident intestinal γδ T-cell populations involved in mucosal immunity, although some cases may be of αβ T-cell lineage or phenotypically T-cell receptor–silent. There is clonal TR rearrangement, even in T-cell receptor–silent cases {30576040;33017466}. The genomic landscape of MEITL bears some similarities with that of EATL. Like EATL, MEITL shows gains of 9q34.3-qter {20399483;26854024;26226842;34895266} and losses at 16q12.1 {26854024;26226842}, whereas alterations of 1q32.2-q41 and 5q34-q35.5 have been reported in MEITL by some {30576040} but not others {26854024;17484883}. Alterations such as gains of 9q22.31, 4p15.1, 7q34, 8p11.23, and 12p13.31, as well as loss of 7p14.1 {26226842}, are more specific to MEITL. MEITL typically shows gains (and rarely translocations) of MYC {22654438;23399895;33017466}.

The mutation spectrum of MEITL includes SETD2 alterations and JAK/STAT deregulation as the pivotal pathways in oncogenesis {26854024;28424246;27600764;33260897;35551151;35708139}. SETD2 is almost ubiquitously affected by nonsense mutations, frameshift insertions, or deletions, resulting in loss of function of this gene. SETD2 is an H3 p.Lys36 (H3K36)–specific methyltransferase and interacts with RNA polymerase II. SETD2 is a key regulator of the transition from early double-negative to double-positive T cells during thymic T-cell development and probably directs the choice between αβ and γδ T-cell development {35226431}. This notion is supported by the finding of increased intestinal γδ T cells in SETD2-knockout mice. Similar SETD2 alterations are found in hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma that is also prototypically of γδ T-cell derivation {25586472}. Besides SETD2 inactivation, JAK/STAT pathway activation via mutations (predominantly in STAT5B and JAK3) indicates constitutive cytokine-mediated signalling as a major driver of oncogenesis in MEITL, similar to EATL and other subtypes of T-cell and NK-cell lymphomas. Additional recurrently mutated genes in MEITL include TP53, GNAI2, KRAS, and BRAF {27600764;31914529;26854024;35551151;35708139}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour often appears as a large ulcerated mass showing full-thickness involvement of the intestinal wall, with or without perforation. Some tumours show coarse, cobble stone–like thickening of the mucosa.

Histopathology

In most cases, the neoplastic cells are monotonous and small to medium-sized, with round nuclei, dispersed chromatin, and moderate pale to clear cytoplasm. At times, the neoplastic cells may appear high-grade with a starry-sky appearance, or (rarely) the lymphoma may show larger, pleomorphic cells with open chromatin and conspicuous nucleoli {35551151}. Necrosis and an inflammatory background are uncommon. In the peripheral zone, the tumour invades the mucosa, inducing distortion, destruction, or expansion of villi, accompanied by epitheliotropic lymphoma cells.

The distant intestinal mucosa displays normal architecture and may show an increase in morphologically bland, small intraepithelial lymphocytes, mimicking coeliac disease or lymphocytic colitis {23399895;21921780;27457239}.

The most common immunophenotypic profile is CD2+, CD3+, CD4−, CD5−, CD7+, CD8+, CD56+ {23399895;21921780}. However, CD8 and CD56 may be negative in 10–20% and 5–6% of cases, respectively {31493002;21921780;35551151}. Pooled data from several series show that 47% of cases express TCRγ and 35% express TCRβ, with occasional cases showing dual or negative T-cell receptor expression {23399895;21921780;27600764;22641357;33260897;29052597}. The cytotoxic marker TIA1 is expressed in almost all cases, whereas positivity for granzyme B or perforin is less consistent {26226842;23399895;31914529;27600764}. A high frequency of CD103 and CD8a homodimer expression has been reported {23399895;27600764}, whereas CD30 expression is rare {31493002}. Extensive nuclear expression of MATK and cytoplasmic expression of Syk are seen in practically all MEITLs, whereas these are negative in EATLs {21233830;29052597}. Aberrant expression of CD20 is reported in 20% of cases {23399895}. H3 p.Lys36me3 (H3K36me3) immunohistochemistry can serve as a surrogate method for detecting SETD2 alterations {35708139}.

The intraepithelial lymphocytes in the distant zone usually express CD3, CD8, and TIA1, and they can be positive or negative for CD56 {23399895;27457239;21921780}. These cells may represent precursors of MEITL.

Differential diagnosis

See #49464Table 12.03.

Cytology

Pleural or peritoneal fluid cytological specimens show non-cohesive atypical lymphoid cells with cytoplasmic azurophilic granules seen on Giemsa staining {33387400;28681573}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is negative {23399895;21921780;31493002}. TR clonality assays may support the diagnosis in equivocal cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a lymphoma with the bulk of disease localized to the GI tract; dense infiltration by relatively monotonous medium-sized or (occasionally) large lymphoma cells; typically lacking necrosis and an inflammatory background; epitheliotropism is common; no histological evidence of coeliac disease in uninvolved mucosa; proven T lineage, with expression of cytotoxic markers, and often CD5−, CD4−, CD8+, CD56+; EBV negative.

Desirable: in problematic cases, demonstration of JAK3 and/or STAT5 mutations and/or SETD2 inactivation are helpful in differentiating MEITL from EATL.

Staging

MEITL is staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the ninth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical outcome is poor, with a median overall survival of 7 months, 1-year overall survival rate of 36–57%, and 3-year overall survival rate of 13–26% {22641357;23399895;31493002;35708139}. The poor survival is attributable to intestinal perforation, the propensity of lymphoma to recur locally and to spread to various sites, and primary resistance to CHOP-like polychemotherapy {31493002}. Poor performance status and TP53 and STAT5B mutations are associated with adverse outcomes {35708139}.
