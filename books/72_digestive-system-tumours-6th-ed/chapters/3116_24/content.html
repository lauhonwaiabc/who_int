3.1.1.6 Gastric pyloric gland adenoma

Definition

Pyloric gland adenoma (PGA) of the stomach is a non-invasive gastric epithelial neoplasm consisting of dysplastic glands with pyloric/mucous neck cell–type differentiation.

ICD-O coding

81404/0 Pyloric gland adenoma, low-grade

81404/2 Pyloric gland adenoma, high-grade

ICD-11 coding

2E92.1 & XA6P89 & XH0WQ8 Pyloric gland adenoma, low-grade

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PGAs usually arise in the gastric body or fundus {12715167;18830123;29278427}.

Clinical features

Sporadic PGAs are usually asymptomatic and discovered during oesophagogastroduodenoscopy performed to investigate other lesions related to underlying gastritis. Many sporadic PGAs occur in elderly women with autoimmune gastritis, suggesting an association {29278427;12715167;18830123}. Syndromic PGAs occur in younger patients than sporadic PGAs and are associated with polyposis syndromes {24525509;25832318;27767239}.

Epidemiology

PGAs are rare neoplasms, with case reports indicating that sporadic PGAs account for about 3% of gastric epithelial polyps (excluding fundic gland polyps) {12715167}. PGAs occur with similar frequency in men and women. In a series of 67 cases, the mean age at diagnosis was 66 years {29278427}.

Etiology

Sporadic PGAs are closely associated with autoimmune and/or Helicobacter pylori gastritis {12715167}. Syndromic PGAs are seen in polyposis syndromes, including familial adenomatous polyposis, gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach (GAPPS) {37500088}, McCune–Albright syndrome {28188442}, juvenile polyposis {25390638}, and Lynch syndrome {24518125}.

Pathogenesis

Sporadic gastric PGAs typically arise in the setting of pyloric gland metaplasia in oxyntic mucosa with chronic atrophic gastritis {18830123;25092673;12715167}. They can occur wherever heterotopic gastric mucosa is present or pyloric gland metaplasia develops {10526011;15838648;15838649;27526016}. In contrast, syndromic PGAs are not associated with an inflammatory or metaplastic background. However, both sporadic and syndromic familial adenomatous polyposis–associated PGAs consistently feature GNAS, KRAS, and APC mutations {23208952;25832318;27767239;31783043}.

Macroscopic appearance

PGAs are polypoid lesions or masses with an average size of 20 mm (range: 3–100 mm) {12715167;29278427}. Inverted growth patterns have also been described {30574615}.

Histopathology

PGAs are composed of closely packed glands, mainly consisting of pyloric/mucous neck cell types, lined by uniform cuboidal or low columnar epithelium. Large lesions may include dilated glands. The neoplastic cells feature a defined ground-glass appearance, with clear or lightly eosinophilic cytoplasm. PGAs may contain chief or parietal-type cells, making the differential diagnosis from oxyntic gland adenoma difficult {27767239;23782334}. The lack of a well-defined apical mucin cap distinguishes PGAs from foveolar-type adenomas/dysplasia. However, surface epithelial cells in PGAs exhibit columnar morphology and differentiate into foveolar epithelium. When this foveolar differentiation is pronounced, distinguishing PGAs from foveolar-type adenoma/dysplasia can be challenging. Notably, it has been reported that flat (non-polypoid) foveolar-type adenomas harbouring KRAS and APC mutations can transform into PGAs upon the acquisition of GNAS mutations {35968980}. The round to ovoid nuclei are basally located, with inconspicuous nucleoli. High-grade PGAs consistently show crowded dense nuclei, loss of nuclear polarity, and a high proliferation index {20827489}. High-grade dysplasia /intramucosal adenocarcinoma is observed in as many as half of the cases in some studies {12715167;20827489}, but submucosal invasion does not exceed 10% of the cases {18830123;29278427}.

Consistent with their pyloric and mucous neck cell–type differentiation, the glandular component consistently expresses MUC6 {18830123;20827489;29278427} and partially expresses pepsinogen I and MIST1 {23782334}. MUC5AC is predominantly expressed in surface cells but may also be variably expressed throughout the lesion. As many as 10% of PGAs exhibit focal expression of MUC2 and/or CDX2 {20827489}. Occasional nuclear β-catenin staining has also been reported {25832318}. However, because of the variable staining patterns in routine diagnostics, immunohistochemistry targeting mucins is not required for diagnosis.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a polypoid proliferation of closely packed glands composed of pyloric/mucous neck-type cells with foamy, ground-glass cytoplasm without an apical mucin cap.

Staging

If high-grade dysplasia is present, it is staged as pTis.

Prognosis and prediction

Complete resection is indicated. Progression to high-grade dysplasia /adenocarcinoma is more frequent in elderly patients with autoimmune gastritis. The neoplastic risk increases with larger size, tubulovillous architecture, and a mixed immunophenotype. The prognosis depends on any carcinomatous component; however, after complete endoscopic or surgical resection, the overall local recurrence rate is < 10% {29278427}.
