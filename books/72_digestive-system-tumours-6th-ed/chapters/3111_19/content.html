3.1.1.1 Fundic gland polyps

Definition

Fundic gland polyps (FGPs) are benign gastric epithelial polyps consisting of disordered and microcystic oxyntic glands covered by normal-appearing or altered foveolar epithelium.

ICD-O coding

82135/0 Fundic gland polyp

ICD-11 coding

DA44.1 Fundic gland polyp of stomach

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Sporadic fundic gland polyps; syndromic fundic gland polyps

Localization

FGPs arise exclusively in the oxyntic mucosa of the gastric body/fundus.

Clinical features

FGPs are usually asymptomatic and discovered during upper endoscopy performed to investigate unrelated pathologies such as gastro-oesophageal reflux disease {11150384;19465154;19566871}. Syndromic etiologies should be considered when multiple FGPs are detected in young patients {23583466}.

Epidemiology

Sporadic FGPs are among the most common gastric polyps encountered by endoscopists {12702980}. The relative incidence of sporadic FGPs has been steadily increasing due to the widespread use of proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs) and the reduced occurrence of Helicobacter pylori–associated hyperplastic polyps due to the decreasing prevalence of this infection {17059515;31734951}. FGPs are most frequently encountered in the fifth to sixth decades of life, often with a female predominance {19465154;31087669}. Patients with syndromic FGPs may present as early as 8 years of age, but typically present in their third decade of life {4043660;11321399;18237868}.

Etiology

FGPs arise in sporadic and syndromic settings. Sporadic FGPs most often occur in patients taking high doses of PPIs but also arise in the absence of PPI use {31087669}. In fact, the existence of a direct etiological relationship between PPI use and an increased risk of developing FGPs has been challenged {27211501;27634363;38829941}.

Associated syndromes include familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach (GAPPS), and MUTYH-associated polyposis {26633;19506731;21813476;24525509}.

Pathogenesis

FGPs develop in a background of normal oxyntic mucosa with a negative association with H. pylori infection {19491866;23583466}. They have been classically viewed as hamartomatous lesions but are now categorized as neoplastic due to distinct alterations of the WNT pathway seen in both sporadic and syndromic FGPs {11238048;11956818}. Sporadic FGPs harbour CTNNB1 mutations and are devoid of APC mutations {11238048;11956818}. Epigenetic changes involving methylation of CpG islands have been implicated in the development of some FGPs associated with PPI use {15491970}. FAP-associated FGPs are characterized by biallelic APC mutations and wildtype CTNNB1 {11238048}.

Sporadic FGPs with dysplasia have been reported to harbour APC mutations, typically with wildtype CTNNB1, rendering them genetically similar to FAP-associated FGPs {12414520}.

Both sporadic and FAP-related FGPs, regardless of the presence or absence of dysplasia, lack the DNA content abnormality characteristic of other GI tract adenomatous precursor lesions, which may partly explain their low rate of progression into advanced neoplasia {36805791}.

Macroscopic appearance

FGPs usually appear as small, sessile, and well-circumscribed polyps.

Histopathology

FGPs consist of architecturally disordered oxyntic glands, with variable glandular buds, irregular tortuous glands, oxyntic gland cysts, and shortened foveolae {3020962;31087669;35092716}. Despite sharing similarities in morphology, sporadic and syndromic FGPs exhibit subtle differences; for example, hyperproliferative aberrant glands, inverted foveolar hyperplasia, and hypermaturation are seen in GAPPS-associated FGPs {21813476;35092716}. FGPs associated with PPI therapy develop due to cystic glandular expansion caused by foveolar cell proliferation, parietal cell protrusion, and hyperplasia {17059515;31087669}.

Immunohistochemistry is not recommended in the routine diagnostic setting, including the differential diagnosis of FAP-related and sporadic cases, as both subtypes have similarly low rates of β-catenin nuclear positivity {29436014}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: hyperplastic expansion and disordered architecture of the deeper epithelial compartment of the oxyntic mucosa with dilatation of one or more oxyntic glands; parietal cell hyperplasia.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Sporadic FGPs associated with PPI therapy disappear when the therapy is discontinued. There have been no reliable reports of malignant transformation occurring in the sporadic setting.

Dysplasia, confined to the foveolar epithelium, is rare and almost invariably low-grade in sporadic FGPs. Although dysplasia is more common and sometimes high-grade in syndromic FGPs, progression into carcinoma is exceptionally rare {18237868;19465154;21813476;24548295}. Regenerative changes due to erosions on polyps should not be misinterpreted as dysplasia, with an abrupt border between atypical and adjacent normal epithelium proposed as the most reproducible feature of dysplasia {34424570}.
