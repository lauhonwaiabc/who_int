11.1.1.1 Simple mucinous cyst

Definition

Simple mucinous cyst is a > 10 mm cavity-forming lesion composed of gastric-type epithelium with an absence of papillary architecture and ovarian-type stroma.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DC30.0 Cyst of pancreas

Related terminology

Not recommended: simple cyst; pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia involving retention cysts; cystic mucinous duct lesion; mucinous non-neoplastic cysts {25775066;26559377}.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Simple mucinous cysts are typically solitary lesions (95%) with a slight predilection (60%) for the body and tail of the gland {33511431}.

Clinical features

Approximately half of all patients are asymptomatic at the time of diagnosis, while the other half are reported to present with abdominal pain, jaundice, or other symptoms. On CT, the cysts are unilocular or paucilocular and typically do not communicate with the main pancreatic duct {28567485}. Contrast enhancement may be observed within a thickened cyst wall or internal septations, but mural nodules, pancreatic atrophy, and parenchymal calcification are typically absent {22015974;28567485}. By MRI, simple mucinous cysts are smoothly marginated with fluid content that is hyperintense on T2-weighted images and hypointense on T1-weighted images, with no enhancing soft tissue elements {28567485;34518632}. The radiological differential diagnosis usually includes mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN), pseudocyst, retention cyst, lymphoepithelial cyst, and macrocystic (oligocystic) serous cystadenoma. Preoperative pancreatic cyst fluid often has high carcinoembryonic antigen levels, and the cyst fluid can harbour KRAS mutations {27740966}.

Epidemiology

Simple mucinous cysts account for < 1% of pancreatectomies, < 1% of pancreatic cyst resections, and, overall, represent 5–10% of pancreatic resections for a mucin-producing cyst {15185076;19954814;22015974;22695095}. Patients are predominantly female (70%), with a mean age of 57 years (range: 22–85 years) {8530832;11850544;19954814;22820665;27740966;28567485;34518632}.

Etiology

There are no known etiological factors, although there are a few documented cases in patients with a family history of pancreatic cancer {22015974}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of simple mucinous cysts is unknown. The identification of somatic alterations in KMT2C/MLL3, KRAS, BRAF, RNF43, CDKN2A, TP53, and SMAD4 suggest that simple mucinous cysts represent true cystic neoplasms and are molecularly related to other precursor neoplasms of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma {27246174;27740966;32380013;33511431}. Alternatively, clonal analyses using AR (HUMARA) suggests that simple mucinous cysts are polyclonal in origin, arguing a potentially non-neoplastic nature {19954814}. However, intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs) of the pancreas can also contain several independent clones {31175866}.

Macroscopic appearance

Simple mucinous cysts are unilocular or paucilocular with a mean greatest dimension of 50 mm (range: 10–120 mm). The cysts typically do not connect to the main or branch pancreatic ducts {33511431}. Cyst contents frequently consist of clear or serous fluid, but the cysts can also contain mucinous, serosanguinous, or brown/green thick fluid {27740966}. The cyst wall is often thin, and the inner lining is smooth with no to minimal focal papillary excrescences {27740966}.

Histopathology

Most simple mucinous cysts are lined by a single layer of cuboidal to columnar epithelium that is at least focally mucin-producing, with gastric-type mucin. The cells have only mild nuclear atypia (low-grade dysplasia) {34518632}. The epithelium is predominantly flat with minimal papilla formation. However, in up to 8% of cases, simple mucinous cysts can exhibit a tufted appearance that lacks apical mucin and has increased nuclear atypia characterized by nuclear pseudostratification and loss of nuclear polarity (high-grade dysplasia) {27740966}. A paucicellular fibrotic wall of variable thickness surrounds the cyst epithelium and lacks ovarian-type stroma (diagnostic of a mucinous cystic neoplasm) or a band of lymphoid tissue (suggestive of a lymphoepithelial cyst). Degenerative changes within the cyst wall have been reported in two thirds of cases and consist of hyalinization, myxoid stroma, haemorrhage, calcification, aggregates of macrophages, and granulation-like tissue {27740966}. The background pancreas often harbours low-grade pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) along with periductal fibrosis, atrophy, squamous metaplasia, or acinar-ductal metaplasia. However, discrete ductal stenosis, as seen with calculi and obstructive pancreatitis, is not observed as these lesions would be classified as retention cysts.

The histological differential diagnosis of simple mucinous cysts includes PanIN, IPMNs, MCNs, and non-neoplastic entities, such as retention cysts. The epithelium of simple mucinous cysts mimics the pale mucin seen in low-grade PanIN, but PanIN lesions are microscopic and by definition < 5 mm, while simple mucinous cysts are > 10 mm {26559377}. The papillary growth and gastric foveolar morphology of a gastric-type IPMN also contrasts the smooth and flat lining of a simple mucinous cyst. Furthermore, growth within the pancreatic ductal system would favour an IPMN over a simple mucous cyst. Although MCNs and simple mucinous cysts frequently occur in female patients, an underlying ovarian-type stroma is a defining feature of MCNs. Finally, focal gastric-type mucinous epithelium can be found in retention cysts, and therefore, the distinction between retention cysts and simple mucinous cysts is dependent on the combination of clinical and pathological findings associated with ductal obstruction and subsequent chronic pancreatitis.

Immunolabelling of simple mucinous cysts reveals diffuse expression for CK7 and other cytokeratins, such as AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, and CK19 {22820665}. Consistent with their gastric phenotype, nearly all simple mucinous cysts express MUC5AC and/or MUC6 {19954814;34518632}. MUC1 is positive in 40% of cases, while focal positivity can be seen for CDX2 and MUC2 {34518632}. ER and PR are negative within the cyst wall and contrast with the positive immunoreactivity observed in ovarian-type stroma, as is characteristic of an MCN.

Cytology

Preoperative fine-needle aspirates of corresponding cyst fluid are often classified as non-diagnostic {32380013}. The remaining cases are characterized by predominantly thin mucin and flat honeycomb sheets/nests of cuboidal or columnar mucinous cells that lack nuclear crowding, nuclear moulding, and loss of polarity {22750967}. Rare cases show papillary, acinar, or single-cell architecture. The cytoplasm of the epithelial cells is delicate, vacuolated, and variable in amount. Nuclei are round or ovoid with smooth contours and contain fine granular chromatin with 1–2 inconspicuous nucleoli. Although the presence of mucin and mucin-producing epithelium excludes the radiological differential diagnosis of pseudocyst, these findings can also be seen in IPMNs, MCNs, retention cysts, and GI tract contamination.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not clinically relevant in postoperative specimens as the diagnosis relies on accurate histological examination of the lesion. In the preoperative setting, molecular testing of pancreatic cyst fluid obtained by EUS-FNAB may help establish the differential diagnosis of a mucin-producing cyst, such as a simple mucinous cyst. Detectable genomic alterations have been described in up to a third of simple mucinous cysts with the most prevalent mutation involving KRAS {27246174;27740966}. However, KRAS mutations in pancreatic cyst fluid can also be found in other mucin-producing cysts, such as IPMNs and MCNs {37212422}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cystic lesion of > 10 mm with flat, columnar, mucin-producing epithelium and without an underlying ovarian-type stroma.

Staging

Simple mucinous cysts with high-grade dysplasia are categorized as pTis (carcinoma in situ) as per current staging protocols [[American Joint Committee on Cancer, American College of Surgeons. AJCC Cancer Staging System. Version 9. Chicago (IL): American College of Surgeons; 2020. Bookref]]} [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

Follow-up data show that virtually all patients diagnosed with a simple mucinous cyst after surgical resection and no other neoplastic pathology are alive without evidence of disease after follow-up {34518632}. The reported presence of high-grade dysplasia and invasive carcinoma (advanced neoplasia) in association with a simple mucinous cyst may dictate that patients should undergo postoperative surveillance protocols {27740966}.
