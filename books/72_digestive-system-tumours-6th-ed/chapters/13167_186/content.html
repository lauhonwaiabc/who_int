13.1.6.7 GLI1-altered neoplasms (including gastroblastoma)

Definition

GLI1-altered neoplasms are low-grade biphasic epitheliomesenchymal neoplasms.

ICD-O coding

89760/1 Gastroblastoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F70.1 & XH4VQ1 Gastroblastoma of stomach

Related terminology

Acceptable: gastroblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GLI1-altered neoplasms most commonly arises in the distal stomach. Rare cases have been reported in the proximal stomach and small bowel {28731043;36823781;38260988;38934567;39451014}. Individual cases of a morphologically similar neoplasm have been reported in the oesophagus {38934567} and liver {37992966}.

Clinical features

Patients present with features attributable to a mass; often abdominal pain and occasional haematochezia {19718790;20203230;28731043;31931637;38622073;38260988;39451014}. Tumours range in size from 18 to 150 mm in maximum dimension and are most often localized. Gastroblastoma may present with metastases to either regional lymph nodes or viscera, yet still pursue an indolent clinical course.

Epidemiology

GLI1-altered neoplasms are most commonly found in the GI tract. Gastroblastoma affects patients aged 9–73 years (median: 30.5 years) and occurs roughly equally in both sexes {35968961}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Initially somatic MALAT1::GLI1 fusions were recurrently identified in cases of gastroblastoma and thought to be the main transcriptomic basis. However, alternate fusions have been identified more frequently with ACTB::GLI1 being most often detected followed by less common fusion genes PTCH1::GLI1, GLI1::AARS1, MRTFA::GLI1, and DDIT3::GLI1 {28731043;38934567;39451014}. MALAT1::GLI1 and ACTB::GLI1 fusions are also present in plexiform fibromyxomas of the stomach {34040702}, a benign neoplasm.

Macroscopic appearance

Gastroblastomas typically appear as lobulated, multinodular, fleshy, and (sometimes) haemorrhagic masses of the muscularis propria.

Histopathology

Gastroblastoma is usually a biphasic neoplasm, with variable proportions of monotonous spindle cells and uniform epithelial cells arranged in nests, trabeculae, and pseudorosettes. Two rare cases with monophasic epithelial features were recently reported {39451014}. The spindle cell component is composed of slender tumour cells arranged in sheets and bundles in an often-myxoid and occasionally calcified background. The epithelial component is characterized by predominantly bland cells with moderate amounts of pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and ovoid nuclei with open chromatin. Rarely, foci of nuclear enlargement including scattered bizarre-appearing cells, may be present. Mitoses are rare and necrosis generally absent. The immunophenotype of the spindle component includes expression of CD10 and CD56 while KIT, DOG1, CD34, and SMA are absent. The epithelial component often expresses keratins and less often CD10. Nuclear expression of GLI1 protein is seen, reflecting underlying fusion events.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of GLI1 rearrangement by FISH or identification of one of the recognized GLI1 fusion genes (e.g. ACTB::GLI1 or MALAT1::GLI1), typically by next-generation sequencing, is crucial to establish the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: gastric origin; biphasic epithelial and spindle cell histopathology; expression of cytokeratin in the epithelial component.

Desirable: demonstration of GLI1 fusion.

Staging

Gastroblastoma is staged using the same system as gastric adenocarcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

The number of reported cases is too few to define robust prognostic and predictive features. Most reported cases have been managed with surgical resection, and the clinical course has been indolent, even in patients presenting with lymph node and visceral metastases. One case associated with multiple recurrences had a survival interval of ≥ 26 years {31931637}, and the 3 patients from the original description of the tumour had uneventful follow-up at 3.5, 5, and 14 years postresection {19718790}.
