3.1.2.3 Gastric adenosquamous carcinoma

Definition

Adenosquamous carcinoma of the stomach is a gastric carcinoma composed of distinct adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) components, with either component accounting for ≥ 25% of the tumour.

ICD-O coding

85600/3 Adenosquamous carcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B72.Y & XH7873 Adenosquamous carcinoma of stomach

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Gastric adenosquamous carcinoma may occur in all areas of the stomach {28676632;39003328}.

Distal extension from the more common adenosquamous carcinomas of the lower oesophagus should be excluded by correlation with available macroscopic information (endoscopic or gross evaluation).

Clinical features

The clinical features of gastric adenosquamous carcinoma are similar to those of other types of gastric cancer. Most tumours present as large masses, with a mean size of 60 mm {25889482;28676632;3942965}.

Epidemiology

Gastric adenosquamous carcinoma is rare, accounting for < 0.4% of all gastric cancers {22788926;25567665}. Men are predominantly affected, with a mean age of 62 years {3942965;25889482}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The definitive pathogenesis remains controversial. Tumours may originate from pluripotent stem cells or derive from squamous metaplasia in pre-existing gastric adenocarcinoma {25889482}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is similar to that of other gastric cancers.

Histopathology

Gastric adenosquamous carcinoma shows distinct components of adenocarcinoma and SCC in variable proportions; either component should account for≥ 25% of the tumour {22788926}. The squamous cell component mirrors conventional oesophageal SCC (see section Oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma). The presence of adenoid or microcystic changes within otherwise pure squamous cell carcinoma is insufficient for the diagnosis of gastric adenosquamous carcinoma.

Mucin staining is useful for highlighting adenocarcinoma areas, and p63/p40 or high-molecular-weight keratin (34βE12) and CK5/6 immunohistochemistry may confirm the presence and extent of the squamous cell component.

Cytology

Peritoneal lavage cytology is used for gastric cancer staging and monitoring chemotherapy (see section Gastric adenocarcinoma).

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Limited data are available (see section Gastric adenocarcinoma).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a carcinoma with both glandular and squamous cell components.

Staging

Spread of tumour to regional nodes and to liver, lung, or peritoneum is common {3942965}. Lymph node metastases are mostly from the adenocarcinoma component, but the squamous cell component or both components may be seen in the metastases {25889482;25567665;28676632}. For TNM staging, see section Gastric adenocarcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Because of the rarity of this entity, data on specific prognostic factors are limited and stage is an independent determinant of outcome {33380867}.

Gastric adenosquamous carcinoma seems to behave more aggressively than gastric adenocarcinoma of the same stage {30788163;31737107}. An increased proportion of the squamous cell component has been associated with worse prognosis {32899039;39003328}.
