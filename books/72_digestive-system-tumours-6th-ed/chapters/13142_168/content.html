13.1.4.2 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma (EHE) is a malignant vascular neoplasm composed of epithelioid endothelial cells within a myxohyaline stroma; most cases are characterized by WWTR1::CAMTA1.

ICD-O coding

91330/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5Y & XH9GF8 & XA5DY0 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, NOS, of liver

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion (91331/3); epithelioid haemangioendothelioma with YAP1::TFE3 fusion (91332/3)

Localization

EHE of the digestive system accounts for roughly a quarter of all cases, mainly arises in the liver, and is exceedingly rare at other sites. Hepatic EHE often presents as multifocal disease {10091730}, and about 70% of patients present with metastatic disease, primarily involving the lungs and bones {34381186;37686662}.

Clinical features

Hepatic EHE is often discovered incidentally {10091730}, but some patients present with abdominal pain, weight loss, or ascites. Multifocal hepatic involvement is identified in > 75% of patients, often involving the right and left lobes {10091730;17019735}. Radiological findings are variable; in about 30% of cases, the nodules show a targetoid appearance on MRI, with peripheral high signal intensity on T1-weighted images and low signal intensity on T2-weighted images, referred to as the bright-dark ring sign {17019735;29159525}. Calcifications can display a characteristic pattern on CT but not MRI {1592925}.

Epidemiology

EHE overall is reported to have an incidence of 0.038 cases per 100 000 person-years and a prevalence of < 1 per 1 million {34090171}. Hepatic EHE shows a slight female predominance and a peak incidence in middle-aged adults, with a wide age range; children are rarely affected {10091730}. There are no data concerning geographical distribution and risk factors. US cancer registry data show no consistent epidemiological patterns of disease {34850580;39502705}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

A WWTR1::CAMTA1 gene fusion, resulting from a t(1;3)(p36;q25) translocation, is a characteristic feature found in > 90% of hepatic EHEs {21584898;21885404}. Rare cases of YAP1::TFE3-fused EHE {34381186} have been described. The fusions activate the Hippo signalling pathway. Multifocal hepatic disease is monoclonal {22429593}. Infrequently, other gene fusions can be detected, but their significance is unclear and they are most likely passenger mutations {37686662}. Tumours may also have additional, secondary mutations in other genes, including CDKN2A, CDKN2B, RB1, APC, and FANCA {31577358}. Clonality studies suggest that multifocal liver involvement usually represents metastatic disease rather than separate primary tumours {22429593}.

Macroscopic appearance

Hepatic epithelioid haemangioendothelioma ranges in size from small (subcentimetre) nodules to large coalescing masses > 100 mm. The cut surface is typically white and firm.

Histopathology

EHE is composed of epithelioid tumour cells embedded in a distinctive myxoid or hyalinized/sclerotic stroma. In the liver, the tumour cells may have a more spindled or stellate appearance and show greater cytological pleomorphism than their soft tissue counterparts. Nuclei are typically round with fine chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. The cells have moderate amounts of eosinophilic cytoplasm and often have intracytoplasmic vacuoles that may contain erythrocytes. The growth pattern is variable; tumour cells are most often found as single cells or in cords within the characteristic myxohyaline extracellular matrix but may also show solid growth. Some tumours show a striking sinusoidal pattern, associated with preserved but atrophic hepatic plates. The tumours may also show angiocentric growth, invading the portal and hepatic veins; microscopic disease may extend significantly along the veins beyond the gross margins of the tumour. Occasional tumours show marked nuclear atypia, including pleomorphic and multinucleated cells. The mitotic count is highly variable; many tumours contain rare mitotic figures. Necrosis can also be seen. Other stromal features include scattered calcifications or metaplastic ossification, haemorrhage, and cystic degeneration {6088383}.

By immunohistochemistry, tumour cells are positive for endothelial markers, including CD31, CD34, podoplanin (D2-40), and ERG {21317715}. Immunoreactivity for keratins is frequently observed in a subset of cells {11014572}, potentially leading to confusion with carcinomas, especially desmoplastic cases of cholangiocarcinoma. CAMTA1 is positive in approximately 90% of cases {25879300;26414223}, correlating with underlying gene rearrangement.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of WWTR1::CAMTA1 helps distinguish EHE from angiosarcoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cords or nests of epithelioid cells with cytoplasmic vacuolization set within a myxohyaline or fibrous stroma; expression of one or more markers of endothelial differentiation (ERG, CD31, CD34).

Desirable: identification of CAMTA1 expression or a WWTR1::CAMTA1 gene fusion may be especially useful in cases with unusual clinical or histological findings.

Staging

For hepatic EHE, the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) eighth-edition staging is not applicable. Instead, it is recommended that the size of the largest lesion is recorded if possible.

Prognosis and prediction

Hepatic EHE has a better prognosis than angiosarcoma of the liver. The 5-year survival rate is 60–75% {38555494}. Predictors of poor prognosis include Ki-67 proliferative index of > 10% {30340559;38741104}, male sex {37625241}, and the presence of metastatic disease at presentation {37625241;34028371}. A site-agnostic nomogram has also been developed that uses age, tumour stage, differentiation, surgical treatment, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy {36245980}.
