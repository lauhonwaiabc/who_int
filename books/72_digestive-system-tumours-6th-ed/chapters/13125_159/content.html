13.1.2.5 Liposarcoma

Definition

Liposarcomas are malignant mesenchymal neoplasms showing adipocytic differentiation.

ICD-O coding

88510/3 Liposarcoma, well-differentiated, NOS

88580/3 Dedifferentiated liposarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B59.Y & XA0828 & XH7Y61 Liposarcoma, well-differentiated, of oesophagus

2B59.Y & XA0828 & XH1C03 Dedifferentiated liposarcoma of oesophagus

Related terminology

Not recommended: atypical lipomatous tumour; giant fibrovascular polyp.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Essentially, all primary well-differentiated and dedifferentiated liposarcomas of the GI tract occur in the oesophagus, where they commonly present as large, polypoid masses, previously termed “giant fibrovascular polyps” {28984298;23352209}. Involvement of other portions of the GI tract by liposarcoma essentially always reflects extension from a primary intra-abdominal or retroperitoneal well-differentiated or dedifferentiated liposarcoma; very rarely, metastases from myxoid and pleomorphic liposarcoma involve the GI tract.

Clinical features

Polypoid oesophageal liposarcomas may present dramatically, with reports of patients regurgitating tumours or (rarely) dying from asphyxiation {28984298;23352209}.

Epidemiology

Oesophageal well-differentiated and dedifferentiated liposarcomas are rare, with > 80 reported cases {38024013;26875081;32410132;22924136}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Well-differentiated and dedifferentiated liposarcomas are characterized by supernumerary ring and giant marker chromosomes, typically as the sole change or concomitant with a few other numerical or structural abnormalities {15527898}. Both supernumerary rings and giant markers invariably contain amplified sequences originating from the 12q14-q15 region, MDM2 (12q15) being the main driver gene. Several other genes located in the 12q14-q15 region, including TSPAN31, CDK4 (12q14.1), HMGA2 (12q14.3), YEATS4 {20601955;19737942;18214854}, CPM {19820690}, and FRS2 (12q15) {21793095}, are frequently coamplified with MDM2 {29368794;29220294;26643872}. In addition to 12q14-q15–amplified sequences, coamplification of at least one other genomic segment is characteristic {9892106}. The chromosomal origin of these coamplified regions varies. The most frequent is 1q21-q25. Another striking feature of these supernumerary chromosomes is that they consistently contain a neocentromere {18823700;25517748}. Overexpression of MDM2 protein resulting from genomic amplification inactivates p53; MDM2 targets p53 degradation towards the proteasome and inhibits tumour-suppressing p53-mediated transactivation.

Macroscopic appearance

Well-differentiated and dedifferentiated oesophageal liposarcomas typically manifest as polypoid, variably sized masses, some with ulcerated squamous mucosa. The tumours themselves appear partially fatty and partially fibrous; dedifferentiated liposarcomas display larger areas with a solid, non-fatty appearance, sometimes with necrosis and haemorrhage.

Histopathology

Well-differentiated liposarcomas can be subdivided into three main subtypes: adipocytic (lipoma-like), sclerosing, and inflammatory {17197914}. The presence of more than one coexistent morphological pattern is common. Lipoma-like tumours are composed of mature adipocytic cells in which, unlike in lipoma, substantial variation in cell size and nuclear atypia in fat cells or stromal spindle cells is present. Scattered hyperchromatic stromal spindle cells are easily identified within fibrous septa or blood vessel walls. Occasionally, fat cells assume hibernoma-like features {27272683}. A varying number of lipoblasts (from many to none) may be found. Importantly, the mere presence of lipoblasts neither makes nor is required for a diagnosis of liposarcoma. Sclerosing well-differentiated liposarcomas are composed chiefly of fibrous septa containing atypical stromal cells, with only a minor adipocytic component. Inflammatory changes are common in oesophageal liposarcomas, likely secondary to mechanical trauma, potentially mimicking a non-neoplastic inflammatory polyp. Dedifferentiated liposarcomas show an abrupt transition from well-differentiated liposarcoma to a typically undifferentiated, highly cellular, mitotically active spindle cell sarcoma, or are composed solely of spindle cell sarcoma in recurrent lesions from patients with prior well-differentiated liposarcoma. MDM2 and/or CDK4 nuclear immunopositivity is present in most cases {16160477;19737942;17895748}. In lipoma-like atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma, MDM2 and CDK4 expression may prove difficult to evaluate, making chromogenic in situ hybridization {30520819} or FISH valid alternatives {27508976}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MDM2 (and/or CDK4) amplification {17895748;25862836;21117066} serves to distinguish well-differentiated liposarcoma from benign adipose tumours, and MDM2 (and/or CDK4) amplification is also present in dedifferentiated liposarcoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

  • For well-differentiated liposarcoma: mature adipose tissue transected by variably prominent fibrous septa containing enlarged, hyperchromatic stromal cells; lipoblasts are not required
  • For dedifferentiated liposarcoma: high-grade spindle cell sarcoma arising from pre-existing well-differentiated liposarcoma, either synchronously or in a local recurrence

Desirable:

  • In selected or challenging cases: MDM2 and/or CDK4 nuclear expression or evidence of MDM2 and/or CDK4gene amplification

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Well-differentiated oesophageal liposarcomas may recur locally unless completely resected with negative margins, and rarely dedifferentiate. Dedifferentiated oesophageal liposarcomas may metastasize and result in death from disease {28984298}.
