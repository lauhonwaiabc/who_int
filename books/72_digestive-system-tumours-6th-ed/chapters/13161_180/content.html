13.1.6.1 PEComa

Definition

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumours (PEComas) are mesenchymal neoplasms composed of perivascular epithelioid cells (PECs), distinctive epithelioid cells that are often closely associated with blood vessel walls and express both melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Angiomyolipoma (88600/0); epithelioid angiomyolipoma (88600/1)

Localization

Digestive tract PEComas usually arise in the colon, liver, small intestine, and pancreas; gastric and other gastrointestinal sites are rare {24061520;27428182;23786777}. PEComas may also involve the mesenteries and peritoneum, often as metastases. Angiomyolipomas are nearly exclusively renal, hepatic, and pancreatic. Some patients, usually with tuberous sclerosis, present with both hepatic and renal angiomyolipomas {9888702}.

Clinical features

Gastric and intestinal PEComas form mural masses that may contain or entirely consist of a polypoid mucosal component. Tumours can become symptomatic, with abdominal pain or bleeding. Mesenteric and hepatic PEComas can form pedunculated nodular masses. Malignant GI tract PEComas often metastasize to the liver, peritoneum, lymph nodes, and lungs {24061520}. Hepatic angiomyolipoma is usually discovered incidentally {23292871}. Some patients present with vague abdominal symptoms or right upper quadrant pain. Most lesions are solitary; multifocal disease may be associated with tuberous sclerosis. Rarely, patients present with hepatic rupture and haemoperitoneum {21283992}. On CT, hepatic angiomyolipomas are hypodense, with peripheral enhancement in the arterial phase; on MRI, the tumours are typically hypointense on T1-weighted images and hyperintense on T2-weighted images {18516533}.

Epidemiology

Gastrointestinal and hepatic PEComas and angiomyolipomas are rare. GI tract PEComas affect children and adults across a wide age range, with a mean age of 35–40 years and a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:1.5) {24061520;27428182}. Hepatic angiomyolipoma shows a marked female predominance, arising mostly in middle-aged adults {6496843;9888702;23292871}.

Etiology

Most hepatic angiomyolipomas are sporadic; 5–10% of patients have tuberous sclerosis {9888702;22251200}. Other gastrointestinal PEComas are rarely associated with tuberous sclerosis {24061520}.

Pathogenesis

PEComas (including hepatic angiomyolipomas) are known to harbour TSC2 mutations (biallelic inactivation), resulting in activation of the mTOR signalling pathway; TSC1 is rarely mutated {25651471;18085521;25234729;29512829}. A minority of PEComas contain TFE3 gene fusions, most often with SFPQ, followed in frequency by ASPSCR1, NONO, PRCC, DVL2, RBM10, or RBMX {22456611;20871214;38597260}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most gastrointestinal PEComas are circumscribed, unencapsulated masses that can extend into the adjacent mesenteric tissue. Tumour size ranges from < 10 mm to > 200 mm, with a median size of 60 mm {24061520}. The cut surface varies from pale tan to greyish brown. Hepatic angiomyolipomas also show a large size range, from small lesions to large masses occupying much of the liver {6496843;9888702}. The cut surface is yellow or tan and often fleshy, with variable necrosis and haemorrhage.

Histopathology

PEComas typically show a nested architecture and are composed of spindled to epithelioid cells with abundant granular eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm and round nuclei with small nucleoli. Some are predominantly spindled. In the sclerosing pattern, tumour cells are arranged in cords within a dense collagenous stroma. Typical PEComa may contain occasional multinucleated cells and show limited pleomorphism (symplastic change). Mitotic activity is usually low (< 1 mitosis/mm2). Malignant PEComas are characterized by a variable combination of mitotic activity, necrosis, and nuclear pleomorphism {22619565;16327428;24061520}. Most hepatic angiomyolipomas contain an admixture of lipid-distended PECs, conventional PEC, and thick-walled blood vessels; the relative proportions of these components vary considerably, although epithelioid cells are often a dominant component. The epithelioid cells are often intimately associated with the blood vessel walls. Some tumours are composed predominantly of spindled (leiomyomatous variant), lipid-distended (lipomatous variant), or epithelioid cells (epithelioid variant). Extramedullary haematopoiesis and chronic inflammation may be present {20637427}. Necrosis and focal haemorrhage are common in large tumours. The mitotic count is usually low. TFE3-rearranged PEComas typically grow in nested or alveolar patterns and are composed of large epithelioid cells with macronucleoli and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm {22456611;20871214;38597260}.

By immunohistochemistry, PEComas (including angiomyolipomas) variably express both smooth muscle (desmin and/or SMA, each 60–75%) and melanocytic markers (HMB45, melan-A, PNL2, MITF, tyrosinase) {16327428;24061520;10846546;9888702;11800647}. Epithelioid cells show more extensive expression of melanocytic markers than the spindle cell component. Cathepsin K is frequently positive {23379905}. KIT (CD117)–positive PEComas must be distinguished from gastrointestinal stromal tumour {24061520;11914628}. TFE3 positivity may be encountered {24061520}, especially in TFE3-rearranged examples {20871214;38597260}, which lack expression of keratins, S100 protein, and SOX10.

HMB45 staining should be assessed in apparent hepatic lipomas, because these lesions are often angiomyolipomas instead. Positive staining for glutamine synthetase (GS) or negative staining for liver fatty acid–binding protein should not be mistaken for evidence of hepatic adenoma {29512829}; HepPar1 (CPS1) and arginase-1 (ARG1) are negative.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Specific molecular genetic information on GI tract PEComas is limited.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

  • For PEComa: epithelioid and/or spindle cells with granular eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm; nested, trabecular, or sheet-like architecture
  • For angiomyolipoma: variable admixture of adipocytes, thick-walled blood vessels, and epithelioid cells

Desirable:

  • Demonstration of melanocytic and smooth muscle markers

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Marked nuclear atypia, diffuse pleomorphism, and mitotic activity (> 1 mitosis/mm2) are the strongest risk factors for metastatic behaviour of GI tract PEComas {24061520}, but quantification of risk remains problematic due to limited data. Nearly all hepatic angiomyolipomas are benign {6496843;9888702;23292871}. Rare primary malignant hepatic PEComas have been reported {18391749;18723294}. Histological atypia is not a reliable criterion for malignancy in hepatic angiomyolipomas {29512829}. A proposed risk stratification system for renal epithelioid angiomyolipomas is not applicable to their hepatic counterparts {21263237}.
