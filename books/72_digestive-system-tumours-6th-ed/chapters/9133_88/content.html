9.1.3.3 Mucinous cystic neoplasm

Definition

Mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN) is a cyst-forming, non-invasive epithelial tumour without communication with the bile duct lumina, composed of cuboidal to columnar, variably mucin-producing epithelium, associated with ovarian-type subepithelial stroma.

ICD-O coding

84701/0 Mucinous cystic neoplasm with low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia

84700/2 Mucinous cystic neoplasm with high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia

ICD-11 coding

2E92.7 & XH6NK7 Mucinous cystic neoplasm with low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia of liver or intrahepatic bile ducts

Related terminology

Not recommended: hepatobiliary cystadenoma; cystadenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MCNs are mostly intrahepatic {21516077;29016404;28570009} and occur particularly in the left hepatic lobe (most commonly in segment IV). Two cases were reported in the gallbladder {29536429;23827932}. MCNs are most often solitary.

Clinical features

MCNs nearly always cause symptoms at the time of presentation, typically abdominal pain and swelling. Serum levels of CA19-9 may be elevated, particularly if there is an associated invasive carcinoma. Cystic fluid CA19-9 and CEA levels are higher and help distinguish MCN from non-neoplastic lesions {26058559;15467680}. Imaging shows a multilocular cystic mass with smaller cysts in the cyst wall. Irregular thickness of the wall, internal septation, and papillary projections are observed when an associated invasive carcinoma is present {28224034;27195178}.

Epidemiology

MCNs are rare, with an incidence of 1 case per 20 000–100 000 person-years {21516077;7943529}, and they constituted 10.5% of the cases in a series of 258 liver cysts of ≥ 10 mm {35778790}. This neoplasm occurs only in women {21516077}. With the current requirement for ovarian-type stroma, cases previously reported in male patients as hepatobiliary cystadenoma or cystadenocarcinoma {7943529} are likely to represent intraductal papillary neoplasms of the bile ducts. The mean age is 52 years {35778790}. MCNs with an associated invasive carcinoma tend to arise in older individuals (mean age: 61 years) than non-invasive MCNs (mean age: 50.4 years) {29016404}.

Etiology

The cause of MCN is unknown. The predominance in middle-aged to older women suggests a hormonal influence {26058559}.

Pathogenesis

KRAS mutations are identified in 20% of MCNs, whereas GNAS, RNF43, and PIK3CA are wildtype in all cases {29016404;28570009;32880620}. KRAS mutations are uncommon in cases of low-grade dysplasia (identified in 5%) but are found in most cases with high-grade dysplasia. KRAS-mutated MCNs more commonly have a multilocular cystic appearance and expression of EMA, MUC2, and MUC5AC compared with KRAS-wildtype cases {28570009}. Gradual telomere shortening occurs during the progression of MCN to invasive carcinoma {39214240}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, MCNs are well-demarcated, multilocular cystic lesions, ranging from 50 to 290 mm in size (mean: 110 mm) {7943529;29016404;28570009;25792461}. Most MCNs are separate from the biliary tree, but polypoid intraluminal extension into bile ducts can occur. The inner surface is usually smooth or trabeculated, but rarely papillary projections can be seen. The cysts may contain mucinous, clear, or haemorrhagic fluid. An associated invasive carcinoma may appear as a solid area of greyish-white tumour.

Histopathology

MCNs are well delimited by a fibrous capsule. The inner surface of the cysts is lined by columnar, cuboidal, or flattened epithelial cells with pale eosinophilic to mucinous cytoplasm and basally oriented nuclei; polypoid or papillary projections may be present. Mucin can be demonstrated by histochemical staining in some regions, but in 50% of cases, part of the lining is composed of non-mucinous biliary-type epithelium, resembling the lining of non-neoplastic bile ducts {29016404;27673548}. The epithelial cells express CK7, CK8, CK18, CK19, EMA, CEA, and MUC5AC {29016404}. Gastric and intestinal differentiation and squamous metaplasia may also occur. About half of the tumours contain scattered neuroendocrine cells {29016404}.

Underlying the epithelium is the entity-defining ovarian-like hypercellular stroma, which in turn is surrounded by more collagenized fibrous tissue {29016404;28570009}. The stromal cells may be focally luteinized, and hyalinization may occur in large MCNs or those in older patients. However, only half of all cases demonstrate diffuse ovarian-like stroma (present in > 75% of the cyst wall), whereas such stroma is focal in the remaining cases {29016404}. Stromal inflammation and degenerative changes, such as haemorrhage, calcification, and necrosis, are common {29016404}. The stromal cells are immunoreactive for ER, PR, and α-inhibin {29016404;28570009;24491354}.

The vast majority of MCNs have low-grade dysplasia. Only rare cases display high-grade dysplasia, which is characterized by substantial architectural atypia as well as nuclear pleomorphism and numerous mitotic figures, or progress to invasive carcinoma, which typically occurs in large tumours with gross papillary projections {24509187;29016404}. Invasive carcinomas, developing from MCN, which are rare (occurring in 6% of cases), are adenocarcinomas with tubulopapillary or tubular growth patterns and a desmoplastic reaction, resembling large duct intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma {29016404;28570009}. Invasion may occur focally, so resection specimens must be extensively sampled and carefully evaluated {29016404}.

Other diseases can mimic MCN clinically and pathologically (see #43828Table 9.13), but the presence of ovarian-like stroma is essential for the diagnosis of MCN, and its absence is mandatory for the diagnosis of other lesions {24491354;21442059}. Intrahepatic cystic carcinomas that communicate with bile ducts and do not contain ovarian-like stroma, previously reported as hepatobiliary cystadenocarcinomas {7943529}, are most likely invasive carcinoma developing from intraductal papillary neoplasms {28109714;16741522}.

Cytology

FNA samples are characterized by aggregates of cuboidal to columnar epithelium, occasionally with papillary arrangement. The background can be watery or composed of abundant thick mucin containing macrophages {10790236}. The neoplastic cells show varying degrees of architectural and nuclear atypia. The ovarian-type stromal component is usually not seen {11836701}, making it difficult to distinguish MCN from intraductal papillary neoplasms with a cystic appearance on FNA.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a grossly visible, multilocular, cystic lesion, occurring in a female patient, generally with no connection to the bile duct system; cuboidal to columnar neoplastic epithelia and ovarian-like stroma.

Staging

Staging of adenocarcinoma originating from MCN follows the TNM classifications for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma or carcinoma of the extrahepatic bile ducts or of the gallbladder, depending on its location [[Brierley JD, Giuliani M, O’Sullivan B, et al., editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 9th ed. Oxford (UK): John Wiley & Sons; 2025. Bookref]].

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for patients with MCN is excellent, if complete resection is achieved {7943529;38368313}. The prognosis for patients with invasive adenocarcinoma developing from MCN is worse, but it is thought to be better than that for patients with conventional intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. Persistence/recurrence of MCN is not uncommon after incomplete excision.
