13.1.6.9 Undifferentiated sarcoma

Definition

Undifferentiated sarcoma (US) is a malignant neoplasm showing no identifiable cellular differentiation and no definable molecular signature.

ICD-O coding

88050/3 Undifferentiated sarcoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH6HY6 & XA9607 Undifferentiated sarcoma of gastrointestinal tract

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

US is primarily a disease of the peripheral soft tissue (extremities) of middle-aged and elderly adults. It is only rarely seen in visceral sites and parenchymal organs; site-specific distribution of primary US in the GI tract has not been documented.

Clinical features

Other than a frequently rapid growth rate and bleeding, primary US of the GI tract has no characteristic clinical features that distinguish it from other types of sarcomas.

Epidemiology

There are no epidemiological data on primary US of the GI tract. Most often, cases reporting US occurring in the GI tract represent metastasis from soft tissue tumours of a defined tumour type when thoroughly analysed, making genuine primary US of the GI tract a vanishingly rare diagnosis. A review of cases from 2007 retrieved 50 primary and 5 metastatic USs from different parts of the GI tract and added 5 cases of their own {17874130;30546898}. Most cases in earlier literature predated modern diagnostic tools and probably represented other well-defined tumours, and occurrence of primary US of the GI tract remains uncertain.

Etiology

Similar to their peripheral soft tissue counterparts, there are no specific etiological risk factors for US of the GI tract. In the thorax and upper oesophagus, US has been reported following radiotherapy for thoracic Hodgkin disease, and in the rectum, following radiotherapy for prostate cancer or other pelvic lesions {20308666;3179948}.

Pathogenesis

There are no substantial molecular data specific to primary US of the GI tract. Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas (UPSs) of soft tissue typically display extensive structural genomic rearrangements involving most if not all chromosomes, resulting in complex karyotypes with chromosome numbers ranging from near-haploid to hyperoctaploid {10362810;25516889;19290004;21085701}. Compared with other sarcoma subtypes, UPS demonstrates substantial copy-number heterogeneity and does not form distinct methylation subgroups {29100075;30889380}. Targets of frequent deletions and disruptive rearrangements include CDKN2A and CDKN2B in 9p, PTEN in 10q, RB1 in 13q, and TP53 in 17p, each affected in 10–20% of cases {10748288;11103795;20884963;21125665;29100075;30889380}. RB1 and TP53, as well as ATRX, are among the few genes that recurrently show pathogenic variants at the nucleotide level {29100075}. At the transcriptomic level, UPS cannot be distinguished from high-grade myxofibrosarcoma {29100075}.

Macroscopic appearance

Primary US of the GI tract comprises a heterogeneous group with no distinctive macroscopic features, other than the frequent presence of necrosis and haemorrhage. The tumour may be localized submucosally, intramurally, or within the external layers of the gut wall. Contiguous extension from intra-abdominal sarcomas should be excluded on gross and histological examination. A predominantly polypoid growth pattern is characteristic of sarcomatoid carcinoma and metastatic pleomorphic US {17874130}.

Histopathology

The majority of USs of the GI tract are morphologically high-grade. They are broadly divided into pleomorphic, spindle cell, round cell, and epithelioid patterns, but none have specific defining features other than their lack of an identifiable line of differentiation {18446665}. Pleomorphic US, which represents the largest group, closely resembles UPS and pleomorphic types of other specific sarcomas and is often patternless, with frequent bizarre multinucleated tumour giant cells. Spindle cell US most often shows a fascicular architecture with variably amphophilic or pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and tapering nuclei. Round cell US consists of relatively uniform, round, or ovoid cells with high N:C ratio, variably resembling Ewing sarcoma or other specific types of round cell sarcomas, which are now subtyped into specific molecular subgroups. These include CIC-, BCOR- and NUTM1-rearranged sarcomas, as well as the group of round cell sarcomas associated with EWSR1 fusions involving non-ETS family of genes (e.g. PATZ1, NFATC2, SP3, SMARCA5, and POU5F1). USs with epithelioid morphology have been the least studied to date {21357725}. Morphologically, these lesions closely resemble metastatic carcinoma or melanoma, but generally lack nesting, and have amphophilic or pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and large vesicular nuclei. Some appear to show SMARCA4 inactivation {26343384;30451731}. Importantly, genomic profiling may allow specific classification of US {19290004;15221942;12640106;21412072}.

Differential diagnosis is directed towards separating true primary US of the GI tract from metastatic tumours on the one hand and specific well-defined entities on the other; this necessitates the application of ancillary diagnostic tools {1317996}. Tumours that most often mimic US and should be excluded are primary or metastatic dedifferentiated liposarcomas, metastatic keratin-poor pleomorphic carcinomas of pulmonary, gynaecological or other origin, or anaplastic/undifferentiated melanomas {26448190;33428337;33506327}. Comparative genotyping illustrating shared mutation with a prior or concurrent tumour represents a strong surrogate for clonal origin, and therefore, metastatic nature of the tumour {26448190;33428337;33506327}. Primary sarcomatoid carcinoma is rare in the tubular GI tract but is not uncommon in the pancreaticobiliary system, some of which may lack expression of pankeratins raising the possibility of US {32388398}. Presence of an intraepithelial (adenomatous) or minor differentiated invasive component by thorough sampling and supplementary genotyping help in their identification {38155543}. After all these mimics have been excluded, the possibility of metastatic soft tissue US should be excluded by clinical history {17874130}. A predominantly or purely polypoid growth pattern is seen frequently in sarcomatoid carcinoma and in metastatic US {17874130}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Undifferentiated pleomorphic and epithelioid cell sarcomas are characterized by complex genetic aberrations, which currently lack diagnostic specificity. However, molecular genetics is essential to exclude specific masqueraders that harbour defining molecular alterations. This is particularly relevant for identifying dedifferentiated liposarcoma (MDM2amplification) or specific translocation-associated sarcomas that may present with pleomorphic morphology {20581836;12640106;26448190;33428337;33506327}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle, pleomorphic, epithelioid, or round cell (most often high-grade) tumour with no evidence of cellular differentiation by morphology and immunohistochemistry; absence of specific molecular alterations and translocations that define other well-characterized sarcomas with pleomorphic morphology; absence of tumour outside the GI tract.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic data are limited by the rarity of clearly defined primary US of the GI tract. UPS arising in the extremities or trunk of adults have a reported 5-year metastasis-free survival rate of 83% whereas those with as epithelioid morphology are more aggressive {11408500;21357725}. The prognosis for putative US of the GI tract is largely defined by whether it represents primary or metastatic (UICC stage IV) disease, and whether all mimics have been reliably ruled out.
