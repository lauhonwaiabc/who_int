2.1.1.4 Epidermoid metaplasia of the oesophagus

Definition

Epidermoid metaplasia of the oesophagus is a sharply demarcated area of epithelial hyperplasia with a prominent granular cell layer, and hyperorthokeratosis.

ICD-O coding

8052F/0 Oesophageal epidermoid metaplasia

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Not recommended: oesophageal leukoplakia {4420581}.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Epidermoid metaplasia can occur anywhere in the oesophagus. Approximately half the cases are identified in the lower third of the oesophagus; involvement limited to only the upper third of the oesophagus is uncommon {23765246;33734117}.

Clinical features

Approximately half of all patients present with dysphagia {23765246;33734117}. Other cases are detected incidentally on follow-up for Barrett oesophagus, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, or oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma {33734117}.

Epidemiology

Epidermoid metaplasia is found in 0.18% of routine oesophageal pathology specimens {26426946}. Among the case series reported, the prevalence of oesophageal squamous neoplasia found adjacent to epidermoid metaplasia is 3.5–17% {26426946;23765246}. The mean age of patients at diagnosis is in the seventh decade of life, and there is no sex predominance {23765246;26426946;33734117}.

Etiology

Although the exact cause is unknown, tobacco smoking, alcohol consumption, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, and (rarely) oesophageal lichen planus are associated with the development of oesophageal epidermoid metaplasia {21830395;23765246;23879628;26426946;28731047;33734117}.

Pathogenesis

Based on risk factors, it is postulated that epidermoid metaplasia is due to chronic oesophageal injury {16928229}. Recurrent damage to the oesophagus leads to its transformation into a keratinized, skin-like tissue as a mechanism of cellular adaptation to chronic stress. TP53 mutations have been found in epidermoid metaplasia and both synchronous and metachronous squamous neoplasia {28731047}.

Macroscopic appearance

Endoscopically, abnormal areas present as well-demarcated white patches with a cobblestone appearance, involving the middle-to-distal oesophagus {26426946;23765246}.

Histopathology

At low magnification, these lesions are sharply demarcated from the normal squamous mucosa. The mucosa appears undulating with flattened epithelial rete pegs. A distinctive feature is the presence of a granular cell layer with overlying compact hyperorthokeratosis {26426946;23765246}. Additionally, there may be focal areas of parakeratosis between the hypergranulosis and hyperorthokeratosis. Squamous dysplasia and malignancy can be found adjacent to or in association with epidermoid metaplasia.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not clinically relevant to the diagnosis of oesophageal epidermoid metaplasia.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an epithelial hyperplasia without atypia, a prominent granular cell layer, and superficial hyperorthokeratosis.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

There is epidemiological evidence to suggest that epidermoid metaplasia is a premalignant condition for oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma, but the actual risk of progression is unknown. A high prevalence of associated squamous neoplasia has been detected {26426946;23765246;33734117}.
