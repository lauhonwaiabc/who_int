9.1.4.6 Mesonephric adenocarcinoma of the vagina

Definition

Mesonephric adenocarcinoma is a malignant neoplasm with mesonephric (Wolffian) differentiation.

ICD-O coding

9110/3 Mesonephric adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C71.0 & XH5WG5 Adenocarcinoma of vagina & Mesonephric adenocarcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: mesonephroma, malignant

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lateral walls of the vagina

Clinical features

Most patients present with abnormal vaginal bleeding or a vaginal mass {6137744; 18028380; 29970606; 15105647}.

Epidemiology

These are extremely rare vaginal neoplasms.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Mesonephric adenocarcinomas of the vagina arise from vaginal mesonephric remnants. See section Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type, of the uterine cervix, which has the same derivation.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic features are those of a mass.

Histopathology

Mesonephric adenocarcinoma typically exhibits various architectural patterns (including tubular, ductal, solid, papillary, retiform, and sex cord–like) within the same tumour. Eosinophilic luminal material may be present. The solid foci may be partly or entirely spindled and may contain heterologous elements, such as cartilage. The cells are usually cuboidal or columnar, with eosinophilic cytoplasm. The nuclei often have irregular nuclear membranes and grooves, and nuclear pseudoinclusions may be seen. Mesonephric remnants may be seen in the background.

Immunohistochemically, there is usually positivity for pancytokeratins, CD10 (apical/luminal staining), EMA, and PAX8 {22456609; 12548163; 12877729}. GATA3, TTF1, and calretinin are variably positive {22456609; 26135559; 11224609}. ER and PR are usually completely negative {22456609; 11224609}. p16 is negative or patchy/weak and HPV is negative {22456609}.

Refer also to section Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type, of the uterine cervix.

Cytology

See section Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type, of the uterine cervix.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No vaginal mesonephric carcinomas have been studied at the molecular level; however, cervical mesonephric carcinomas have characteristic molecular alterations, most notably KRAS mutations {26336887}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: admixture of architectural patterns.

Desirable: association with adjacent mesonephric remnants and/or mesonephric hyperplasia; positivity for CD10, GATA3, and/or TTF1; negativity for ER/PR and napsin A; HPV negativity.

Staging

The staging is the same as for other vaginal neoplasms.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis and prediction are unknown because of the limited number of cases described in the literature.
