10.1.1.2 Condyloma acuminatum

Definition

Condyloma acuminatum is a benign verrucous papillary lesion caused by HPV.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

1A95.1 & XA2GU7 Genital warts & Female genital organs

Related terminology

Acceptable: genital wart; anogenital wart; low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion with condylomatous features

Subtype(s)

Giant condyloma acuminatum

Localization

Condyloma acuminatum is more often localized on the vulvar skin, vestibule, perineum, and perianal skin, but it can also be detected on the vaginal mucosa and (more uncommonly) on the cervix. Rarely, the lesion may involve the urethra {24729927}.

Clinical features

Genital warts may occur singly but are more often found in clusters. They are usually small (1–5 mm in diameter) but can also grow or spread to become large masses in the genital or anal area. They are generally asymptomatic but may cause itching, redness, or discomfort, especially when they occur around the anus. Multiple, extensive, non-regressive, and sometimes giant lesions are more often associated with immunosuppression {23754510}.

Epidemiology

Condylomata acuminata are more commonly detected in young sexually active women. The majority of the lesions are estimated to be preventable by the quadrivalent or the nine-valent vaccines {31255301}.

Etiology

Condyloma acuminatum is a result of productive infection with HPV, usually low-risk types. HPV6 and HPV11 are the most prevalent types associated with the lesion. Occasionally, high-risk HPV types are identified {31288232; 31046696}. Lesions indistinguishable from vulvar condyloma in children can occur secondary to non-genital HPV (i.e. HPV2) {17414099}.

Pathogenesis

Condyloma acuminatum is an HPV-induced proliferation of squamous epithelial cells, like other HPV-associated lesions.

Macroscopic appearance

Condylomata acuminata are exophytic, verruciform, or papular lesions.

Histopathology

Acanthosis and papillomatosis, with formation of papillary structures, in addition to a variable degree of parakeratosis, hypergranulosis, and hyperkeratosis, are the key features. The rete ridges are typically thick. Koilocytic change (keratinocytes with perinuclear clearing and enlarged nuclei that may be hyperchromatic) is variable, depending on the location and age of the lesion, and may be difficult to detect. Mitotic figures and apoptosis can also be observed. The stroma/dermis often exhibits newly formed blood vessels and a chronic inflammatory infiltrate. Occasionally, the condyloma acuminatum may be indistinguishable from seborrhoeic keratosis, especially in young women and girls {24556611; 27792064}. Giant condyloma acuminatum, also known as Buschke–Löwenstein tumour, a rare subtype occasionally associated with immunosuppression, may be locally aggressive and have a potential for malignant transformation {31141268; 28487788}. Vestibular papillomatosis, an anatomical subtype of the vestibular mucosa, may occasionally be confused with condyloma but lacks acanthosis and koilocytosis {27136629}.

Cytology

Low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (koilocytic atypia), atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance, or atypical parakeratosis is frequently seen in Pap smears when the lesions involve the uterine cervix or the vagina.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV, usually low-risk types, can be identified by PCR or in situ hybridization. HPV6 and HPV11 are the most prevalent types {31288232; 31046696}. Immunostaining with p16 usually shows irregular, patchy, non–block-type staining {25862555}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: acanthosis and papillomatosis, with formation of papillary structures and thickened rete ridges; parakeratosis, hyperkeratosis, and variable degrees of koilocytic atypia.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Condyloma acuminatum is a benign lesion. The disease can be managed by observation, local excision, cautery, or topical imiquimod. The majority of cases resolve spontaneously or do not recur after removal. Recurrences are more frequent when the lesions are multifocal {31286210} or in immunosuppressed patients {28637906}. Condyloma acuminatum increases the risk of HIV transmission {30137482}.
