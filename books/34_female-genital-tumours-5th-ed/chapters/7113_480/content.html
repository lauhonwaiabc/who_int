7.1.1.3 Invasive and metastatic hydatidiform moles

Definition

Invasive hydatidiform mole is a hydatidiform mole (usually complete or less often partial) that invades the myometrium and/or uterine vessels. Metastatic hydatidiform mole may manifest as lesions containing abnormal molar chorionic villi at sites beyond the uterine cavity, most commonly the vaginal wall / pelvis.

ICD-O coding

9100/1 Invasive hydatidiform mole

ICD-11 coding

2C75.0 & XH46G2 Malignant trophoblastic neoplasms of placenta & Invasive hydatidiform mole

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterus and extrauterine sites in metastatic disease

Clinical features

Vaginal bleeding with persistent elevation of serum hCG after primary evacuation of a hydatidiform mole is characteristic {3010701; 25318458; 25607129}.

Epidemiology

No epidemiological data are available.

Etiology

In the majority of cases, invasive moles develop from complete hydatidiform moles {26535984}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The lesion consists of invasive molar tissue extending into the myometrium. Hydropic villi may be visible, and uterine perforation may occur.

Histopathology

Most invasive hydatidiform moles are complete hydatidiform moles, which retain the villous histological characteristics but demonstrate myometrial and/or vascular invasion. Invasive partial moles also occur {25318458; 8737309}. In cases of metastatic mole, the features are those of complete hydatidiform molar villi in extrauterine locations.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: villi with abnormal trophoblastic proliferation within myometrium and/or uterine or extrauterine vessels.

Staging

See TNM staging of gestational trophoblastic neoplasms [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]].

Prognosis and prediction

In the absence of immediate complications (e.g. severe haemorrhage), chemotherapy is highly effective, with a cure rate of > 80%, depending on the extent of the disease {19369669; 26535984; 25607129}.
