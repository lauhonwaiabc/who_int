8.2.1.5 Microglandular hyperplasia

Definition

Microglandular hyperplasia is a non-neoplastic hyperplastic endocervical lesion comprising closely packed, small glands lined by mucinous epithelium.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA15.Y Other specified acquired abnormalities of cervix uteri

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cervix

Clinical features

Microglandular hyperplasia is usually an incidental finding, or it may present with contact bleeding.

Epidemiology

Microglandular hyperplasia is typically identified in women of reproductive age.

Etiology

This lesion is often associated with pregnancy and progestogen therapy.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Microglandular hyperplasia may be multifocal. Larger lesions may be polypoid and ulcerated.

Histopathology

There are clusters of crowded small glands lined by cuboidal to columnar mucinous epithelium. Subnuclear vacuolation and reserve cell hyperplasia are often present. The nuclei are uniform, with absent or scant mitoses. The stroma may be inflamed and hyalinized. Rare cases demonstrate solid or trabecular growth patterns, cytological atypia, signet-ring–like appearances, or increased mitotic activity {2535775; 25083971}. Immunohistochemistry is unnecessary for diagnosis, although the glandular epithelium is usually positive for ER, PAX2, and cyclin D1 and negative for p16, vimentin, and CEA, and it shows a Ki-67 proliferation index of < 10% {25473758; 12383213; 12819393}.

Cytology

Cytological changes are nonspecific but can lead to a misdiagnosis of adenocarcinoma or high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion {7924805}. There is often a spectrum of clearly benign to 3D clusters of atypical glandular cells. Nuclei are frequently enlarged, hyperchromatic, and overlapping, but they have smooth nuclear contours and fine chromatin. Repair and degenerative changes (including pseudoparakeratosis) may also be present.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clusters of crowded small glands lined by cuboidal to columnar mucinous epithelium.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

This is a benign lesion.
