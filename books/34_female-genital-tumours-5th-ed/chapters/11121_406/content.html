11.1.2.1 Small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

Definition

Small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (SCNEC) is a high-grade carcinoma composed of small to medium-sized cells with scant cytoplasm and neuroendocrine differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8041/3 Small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C7Z & XH0U20 Malignant neoplasms of female genital organs, unspecified & Neuroendocrine carcinoma NOS

2C7Z & XH0YB0 Malignant neoplasms of female genital organs, unspecified & Small cell carcinoma NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: small cell carcinoma; neuroendocrine carcinoma, small cell type

Not recommended: high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SCNEC can occur anywhere in the gynaecological tract, most commonly in the cervix {28735441; 1384368}.

Clinical features

Cervical and endometrial tumours typically present with abnormal vaginal bleeding, and ovarian tumours usually present with symptoms related to a pelvic mass. Vaginal and vulvar tumours usually present with a mass.

Epidemiology

Cervical SCNEC is diagnosed at a mean age of 48.1 years {25341572}, whereas SCNEC at other sites typically presents after menopause {26945341; 24144291; 29019870; 25444431}. SCNEC accounts for < 1% of all gynaecological malignancies {24144291}.

Etiology

Most SCNECs of the cervix are associated with high-risk HPV infection, primarily with types 16 and 18 {29248196}. The etiology of SCNECs at other gynaecological sites is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Some cervical SCNECs have recurrent genetic alterations involving the MAPK, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, and p53/BRCA pathways, as well as loss of heterozygosity involving the short arm of chromosome 3 (3p) {29505425}. Abnormal mismatch repair protein expression has been described in endometrial SCNECs {26945341}. Molecular alterations in SCNECs of the ovary, fallopian tube, vulva, and vagina have not been studied.

Macroscopic appearance

SCNEC presents as a mass lesion.

Histopathology

SCNEC is composed of sheets of highly atypical cells with scant to indiscernible cytoplasm, ovoid to slightly spindled nuclei with hyperchromatic and dispersed chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli. Nuclear moulding, numerous mitoses, apoptotic bodies, extensive necrosis, and crush artefact are frequent features {27098591}. Cells are arranged in solid, nested, trabecular, pseudoglandular, and rosette-like patterns. Vascular invasion is common. Areas of large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC) or other tumour histotypes may be present {24875120; 21621706; 28484583}. Ovarian SCNEC may be associated with teratoma {25990936; 9788270}.

Chromogranin, synaptophysin, and CD56 are the most frequently used immunohistochemical markers. No marker is expressed in 100% of SCNECs. Synaptophysin is most consistently expressed and chromogranin is most specific {20182342}. CD56 is less specific and should only be regarded as evidence of neuroendocrine differentiation in the proper histological context. Broad-spectrum cytokeratins are usually positive. TTF1 and CDX2 expression does not exclude a gynaecological origin. Diffuse block-type p16 immunoreactivity is common in cervical SCNECs because of the association with high-risk HPV, but it may also occur in SCNECs arising elsewhere. SMARCA4, SMARCA2, and SMARCB1 are retained. Detection of high-risk HPV may be useful in helping to confirm a cervical primary {26945341; 29248196}.

Cytology

Smears are usually moderately to highly cellular. The cells are arranged in sheets and as single dispersed cells with scant cytoplasm. The nuclei have finely granular/stippled chromatin, often with nuclear moulding and chromatin streak artefact {20891001}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: high-grade tumour cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and scant cytoplasm.

Desirable: immunohistochemically confirmed neuroendocrine differentiation is desirable but not required for SCNEC when histological criteria are met {26945341}.

Staging

The staging is as for other carcinomas at the corresponding site.

Prognosis and prediction

SCNECs of the gynaecological tract are aggressive, with a high propensity for lymphatic and haematogenous spread. Distant metastasis at presentation is common. Mortality is high, even among patients diagnosed at an early stage {25341572}.
