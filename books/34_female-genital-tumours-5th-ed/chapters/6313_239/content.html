6.3.1.3 Intravenous leiomyomatosis

Definition

Intravenous leiomyomatosis is an intravascular growth of benign smooth muscle cells in the absence of or beyond the confines of a leiomyoma, sometimes with pelvic or extrapelvic extension.

ICD-O coding

8890/1 Intravenous leiomyomatosis

ICD-11 coding

2E86.0 & XH60C2 Leiomyoma of uterus & Intravascular leiomyomatosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: intravascular leiomyomatosis

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intravenous leiomyomatosis is more commonly seen in the uterus; it rarely involves the broad ligament, pelvic veins, and vena cava {25675187}.

Clinical features

Patients have symptoms similar to those encountered in patients with leiomyomas {21777942; 8112951; 3202247; 29699902}. Less commonly, they present with chest pain, dyspnoea, syncope, or pulmonary embolism due to right heart or pulmonary artery involvement {25675187; 11904348; 30898968}. Pelvic MRI may help to detect early-stage disease {28643137; 17179374}; CT-angiography and contrast-enhanced CT are useful if there is extension to extrapelvic vasculature {29395222; 26858203}.

Epidemiology

Intravenous leiomyomatosis is uncommon {25675187; 29699902; 8112951; 3692675}. It occurs over a broad age range (median patient age: 45 years).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The monoclonality of this benign smooth muscle proliferation is supported by X chromosome inactivation. t(12;14), involving HMGA2, and HMGA2 protein overexpression have been identified in a subset of cases, suggesting a pathogenetic relationship with uterine leiomyomas {11904348; 12508249; 26892441}. Recurrent 22q and 1p regional losses and 12q gains have also been reported, but no MED12 mutations, unlike in leiomyomas {25033729}.

Macroscopic appearance

Multiple white, rubbery, and nodular myometrial masses are sometimes seen as worm-like tumour plugs in vessels, but this may be a subtle finding or not grossly visible {3202247}.

Histopathology

There is intravascular growth of benign smooth muscle cells, resembling typical leiomyoma or its subtypes, in the absence of or outside a leiomyoma {3202247; 25675187}; hydropic change, hyalinization, and thick-walled vessels are frequent {8112951; 3202247}. Rarely, endometrial stroma and glands may be seen admixed with the smooth muscle component (termed intravascular adenomyomatosis) {24076777}. Smooth muscle markers are generally positive and CD10 is negative.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: intravascular growth of benign smooth muscle tumour cells in the absence of or outside a leiomyoma.

Staging

This neoplasm is not usually staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Extrauterine extension occurs in about 30% of patients, involving pelvic veins, the inferior vena cava, and (rarely) heart or pulmonary vessels, leading to sudden death {3675155; 29351101}. Recurrence, the risk of which is about 10%, may occur years later, either within veins or rarely as benign metastasizing leiomyoma {25675187; 21777942; 3202247; 1203870}. For unresectable lesions, tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, or GnRH could be used {18280477}.
