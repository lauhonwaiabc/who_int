10.1.2.4 Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-independent, of the vulva

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), HPV-independent, is an HPV-independent malignant squamous tumour with stromal invasion.

ICD-O coding

8086/3 Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-independent

ICD-11 coding

2C70.2 & XH2138 Squamous cell carcinoma of vulva & Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-negative

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

External female genitalia, including labia majora, labia minora, vestibule, clitoris, vaginal introitus, perineum, and urethral meatus

Clinical features

Peak incidence is in about the eighth decade of life {28815579}, but HPV-independent vulvar SCC can also occur in younger women, particularly in the setting of lichen sclerosus or lichen planus. Tumours present as asymptomatic masses or as ulcers. Some patients experience pruritus, a burning sensation, pain, or bleeding, especially in advanced disease. A high FIGO stage and/or multiple primary tumours are common at presentation.

Epidemiology

General epidemiology features of vulvar cancer (independent of HPV status) are provided in section Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-associated, of the vulva. Risk factors are poorly defined, but HPV-independent vulvar SCC is often associated with lichen sclerosus or lichen planus {24999271}. The reported proportion of vulvar SCCs that are HPV-independent varies widely (25–80%) and is highly dependent on geographical differences in HPV distribution {23886586}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Despite the strong association between HPV-independent SCC and vulvar dermatoses, the role of persistent chronic inflammation is presently unclear.

Pathogenesis

Differentiated vulvar intraepithelial neoplasia (VIN) is the best-defined precursor lesion {15614161; 21946295; 27834349}. Approximately 80% of HPV-independent vulvar SCCs feature somatic mutations in TP53 {30291345}, but activating mutations in NOTCH1/2, HRAS, and PIK3CA have also been reported to be common {28899974; 27834349}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are frequently exophytic masses, which may be ulcerated.

Histopathology

Typically, HPV-independent SCCs are well-differentiated keratinizing carcinomas with keratin pearl formation. A subset of these tumours display a more infiltrative growth pattern, with budding of small nests or individual atypical tumour cells at the invasive border, often coexisting with extensive desmoplasia {24064972}. Other features include acantholytic (adenoid or pseudoglandular) or spindle cell (pseudosarcomatous) differentiation with a prominent fibromyxoid stromal reaction {16814852}. Other histological patterns may be seen, including non-keratinizing, basaloid, and warty patterns. Definitive distinction between HPV-associated and HPV-independent vulvar SCC cannot be made confidently on H&E sections {28815579}. Differentiated VIN, lichen sclerosus, or lichen planus is frequently found in the adjacent surface epithelium.

Unequivocal invasion is required for the diagnosis of vulvar SCC. This may be challenging in superficially invasive tumours {24076774} and in biopsies. Ideally, the absence of HPV should be confirmed by validated molecular approaches. Alternatively, negative or non–block-type p16 immunostaining is an acceptable surrogate marker for the absence of high-risk HPV {26630231}. The presence of an HPV-independent precancerous lesion in the adjacent epithelium is desirable but not essential.

Verrucous carcinomas are part of the spectrum of HPV-independent carcinomas. These tumours are very well differentiated, show cohesive or verruciform growth patterns with invasion typically displaying bulbous pegs with a broad front, and display variable keratinization {27834349}. These tumours show minimal atypia, abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, lack of abnormal mitotic figures, and wildtype p53 staining.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

DNA sequencing can identify TP53 mutations. Immunohistochemically, proliferating keratinocytes show either nuclear p53 overexpression (missense mutations) or no expression (truncating stop/gain mutations). p16 staining is typically negative.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infiltrating, angulated, irregularly sized and shaped nests, anastomosing cords, and solid sheets; nuclear pleomorphism and mitoses; a desmoplastic or inflammatory stroma; p16 negativity and/or negative molecular testing for HPV.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and FIGO staging systems are applicable.

Prognosis and prediction

Stage and lymph node involvement are the most important prognostic factors, and surgical resection with tumour-free margins is the primary treatment choice. HPV-independent vulvar SCC has a worse prognosis than HPV-associated vulvar SCC {26630231}. TP53 status has been suggested to refine prognosis in HPV-independent vulvar SCC {28899974}. Unilateral or bilateral inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy or sentinel node biopsy is recommended when depth of invasion is > 1 mm, because lymph node status (including the number of involved nodes, size of metastasis, and extranodal growth) significantly affects clinical outcome. Recurrence rates are higher than in HPV-associated SCC, ranging from 12% to 37% {12965138}, and are possibly related to residual dermatoses {27396942; 21802125}. Multiple local recurrences and rapid progression are common. Treatment of (residual) dermatosis may reduce the risk of malignant transformation {26069940}.
