3.4.0.4 Endosalpingiosis of the peritoneum

Definition

Endosalpingiosis is the presence of glands lined by benign tubal-type epithelium outside the fallopian tube.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

DC51.Y Other specified disorders of peritoneum or retroperitoneum

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peritoneum, pelvic or abdominal organs, skin, nerves, and lymph nodes {29739266; 19020998; 31122517; 27841144; 23249836; 20631604}

Clinical features

It is usually an incidental finding {31033796; 31122517; 19020998}.

Epidemiology

It occurs in as many as 12.5% of women, with a wide age range {27261327}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown. Endosalpingiosis of the peritoneum has been suggested to originate through metaplastic transformation, direct implantation of exfoliated tubal epithelium, or transit via lymphatics {31033796}.

Macroscopic appearance

It usually occurs as multiple small nodules or cysts {31122517}.

Histopathology

Endosalpingiosis is lined by a single layer of tubal-like epithelium, sometimes associated with psammoma bodies. Small papillae or epithelial proliferation without cell detachment can be seen {31033796}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign tubal-type epithelium located outside of the fallopian tube.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Endosalpingiosis is benign. Rarely, serous neoplasms have been observed in association with endosalpingiosis {22613998; 31157685}.
