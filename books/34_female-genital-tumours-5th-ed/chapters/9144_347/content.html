9.1.4.4 Mucinous carcinoma, gastric type, of the vagina

Definition

Mucinous carcinoma, gastric type, of the vagina is a primary vaginal mucinous adenocarcinoma exhibiting gastric differentiation, analogous to its counterpart in the cervix.

ICD-O coding

8482/3 Mucinous carcinoma, gastric type

ICD-11 coding

2C71.0 & XH1S75 Adenocarcinoma of vagina & Mucinous adenocarcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours show variable localization within the vagina.

Clinical features

In reported series, patients were aged 41–69 years (mean: 56 years) {29664741; 29901522}. Patients present with a vaginal mass or related symptoms such as vaginal discharge or urinary symptoms.

Epidemiology

These are rare neoplasms {29664741; 29901522}.

Etiology

The etiology is largely unknown, although there is a potential link with congenital genitourinary malformations and adenosis exhibiting gastric differentiation {22317878; 29664741}. There is no association with HPV or in utero exposure to diethylstilbestrol (DES).

Pathogenesis

This tumour may derive from gastric-type adenosis via atypical adenosis {29664741}. TP53 mutation may be implicated in pathogenesis.

Macroscopic appearance

In the reported cases, the tumours have ranged in size from 2.8 to 4 cm.

Histopathology

Histology shows a mucinous adenocarcinoma exhibiting gastric differentiation, identical to cervical gastric-type adenocarcinoma {17460448; 29901522}. The glands typically display voluminous clear/pale, eosinophilic or foamy cytoplasm, prominent cell membranes, and variable nuclear atypia. Intestinal differentiation (goblet cells) and neuroendocrine differentiation are sometimes seen. Benign and atypical adenosis is sometimes present {29664741}. Immunohistochemistry typically shows positive staining with MUC6, HIK1083, CDX2, CK7, CEA, CA19-9, and CA125 and variable staining with PAX8 and CK20. Hormone receptors are usually negative and p16 negative or focally positive. Approximately half of cases exhibit p53 mutation-type staining.

Cytology

See section Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, gastric type, of the uterine cervix.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: prototypical morphology and immunophenotype, as described for cervical gastric-type adenocarcinoma (see section Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, gastric type, of the uterine cervix).

Staging

The staging is the same as for other primary vaginal tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Data are limited, but the clinical behaviour appears to be aggressive {29664741}.
