3.2.1.2 Low-grade serous carcinoma of the peritoneum

Definition

Low-grade serous carcinoma (LGSC) of the peritoneum is an invasive serous neoplasm with low-grade malignant features and no gross or microscopic disease in the ovaries.

ICD-O coding

8460/3 Low-grade serous carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F74 & XH12V5 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of peritoneum & Low-grade serous carcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peritoneum

Clinical features

Most patients present with abdominal pain or distension, although in rare cases patients can present with lymphadenopathy {15316322; 15577686} or be asymptomatic {26076164}.

Epidemiology

Patient age ranges from 27 to 82 years (median: 52 years) {21397305}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Peritoneal LGSC can occur de novo or after the diagnosis of serous borderline tumour. The MAPK pathway appears to play an important role in the pathogenesis of this tumour {27141071}.

Macroscopic appearance

LGSC typically occurs as multiple nodules of variable size involving the peritoneum or omentum; however, rare cases are not associated with a distinct gross abnormality but are gritty or firm on tissue palpation or sectioning.

Histopathology

LGSC shows neoplastic cells that are relatively uniform, with mild to moderate cytological atypia and a low mitotic count {15087669}. A variety of architectural patterns can be seen, including papillary, micropapillary, cribriform, solid, glandular, and nested patterns, as well as single cells. Extracytoplasmic or intracytoplasmic mucin, as well as a variable number of psammoma bodies, can be seen. Invasion into underlying tissues such as omentum, pelvic tissue, or abdominal/pelvic organs is present. There is no involvement of the ovaries {21397305}.

Cytology

Cytology samples show variable cellularity, large 3D papillary groups, and a variable number of single cells, as well as variable pleomorphism, N:C ratio, nuclear size, chromatin pattern, and psammoma bodies. Mitoses are rare {23858317}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an invasive epithelial proliferation in the form of papillary/glandular structures; nests or single cells with mild to moderate cytological atypia; low mitotic count; gross or microscopic involvement of the ovaries excludes this diagnosis.

Staging

LGSC is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

A reported 72% of patients have persistent disease or progressive disease after the completion of primary treatment, despite an optimal cytoreduction rate of 75%. Like its ovarian counterpart, peritoneal LGSC shows a low response rate to conventional platinum-based chemotherapy {21397305}. The presence of KRAS mutations in peritoneal serous borderline tumour and LGSC also appears to be an adverse prognostic factor {29027536}.
