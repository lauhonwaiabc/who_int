8.2.2.7 Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type, of the uterine cervix

Definition

Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type, is a malignant neoplasm with mesonephric (Wolffian) differentiation.

ICD-O coding

9110/3 Mesonephric adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C77.1 & XH5WG5 Adenocarcinoma of cervix uteri & Mesonephric adenocarcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Mesonephric carcinomas are typically associated with mesonephric remnants along the course of the embryological mesonephric duct located deep in the cervical wall at the lateral positions {2252101; 15105647; 22456609}.

Clinical features

The average patient age at presentation is 53 years {7573674; 11224609; 15105647; 26336887}. Symptoms include abnormal vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain, uterine prolapse, and dyspareunia {11224609; 15105647; 7573674}. Patients can present with abnormal Pap smears or symptoms related to metastasis {11224609; 7573674; 18580312; 27172762}.

Epidemiology

These neoplasms are rare, accounting for < 1% of cervical adenocarcinomas {29135516}.

Etiology

There is no association with high-risk HPV {22456609; 11021808}.

Pathogenesis

These neoplasms are thought to arise from vestiges of the embryological male reproductive system (Wolffian/mesonephric duct). Most harbour KRAS mutations and gain in chromosome 1q (with a subset showing concurrent loss of 1p). Two thirds have mutations in chromatin remodelling genes (ARID1A/B or SMARCA4) and one third in BCOR/BCORL1 {26336887}. A minority have mutations in TP53 or CTNNB1 {31266530; 26336887}. They have a low mutation burden and absence of microsatellite instability {31266530}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour epicentre is usually within the cervical wall, where mesonephric remnants reside, with extension to overlying cervical mucosa {11224609}. Full-thickness invasion, circumferential involvement, ulceration, and extension into the lower uterine segment are not uncommon {11224609; 15105647; 7573674}. Some tumours can form an exophytic or polypoid mass {15105647; 7573674}. On cut section, tumours are firm and white or grey, and they can be diffusely haemorrhagic {15105647; 7573674}.

Histopathology

The classic pattern is tubular, with back-to-back tubules lined by cuboidal cells with lumina filled with dense eosinophilic secretions (PAS-positive and mucicarmine-positive) {7573674}. The ductal (pseudoendometrioid) pattern consists of angulated glands lined by columnar cells. Additional patterns include papillary, retiform, sex cord–like, hobnail, glomeruloid, sieve-like, spindled, and solid {30148742; 7573674; 18580312; 2252101}. Nuclei are uniform, with coarse or vesicular chromatin or grooves and inconspicuous nucleoli {11224609; 15105647}. Mitotic activity is variable. Importantly, squamous differentiation and cytoplasmic mucin are absent. Sarcomatous differentiation, including chondrosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and osteosarcoma, supports a diagnosis of mesonephric carcinosarcoma {7573674; 30148742; 11224609; 15105647}. There is no grading system applicable to mesonephric carcinomas. Distinction from benign mesonephric remnants and hyperplasia is based on architectural crowding, haphazard infiltrative growth, elevated mitotic activity, intraluminal necrotic debris, and nuclear atypia {11224609}.

Tumours are positive for GATA3, PAX8, and CD10 (luminal staining pattern); completely negative for ER; and usually negative for napsin A and AMACR {25851711; 25272301; 26135559; 30148742; 12548163; 31361605}. They can be positive for TTF1 (rarely) and calretinin {11224609; 15105647; 30148742; 22456609}. p53 is wildtype {25272303}. p16 is not diffuse and HPV is not detected {25272301}.

Cytology

Loosely cohesive clusters of uniform cuboidal cells with hyperchromatic nuclei but smooth nuclear contours can suggest mesonephric differentiation on a Pap test, but distinguishing between mesonephric hyperplasia and mesonephric carcinoma requires histological examination. Additionally, high-grade mesonephric adenocarcinomas can result in a Pap diagnosis of malignancy on the basis of nuclear atypia, but classification as mesonephric would depend on histological and immunohistochemical findings {16181310; 31318491}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an admixture of architecture patterns; optically clear nuclei with grooves; p16 is not diffuse.

Desirable: association with adjacent mesonephric remnants and/or mesonephric hyperplasia; positivity for GATA3 and/or TTF1; negativity for ER and napsin A; HPV negativity.

Staging

This entity is staged the same way as other cervical carcinomas.

Prognosis and prediction

In limited case series, approximately one third of cases recurred. Recurrences can occur over long periods (mean: 4 years; range: 1–11 years) and can include both local (pelvic and abdominal) and distant sites (lung, mediastinum) {26336887; 11224609; 15105647; 7573674}.
