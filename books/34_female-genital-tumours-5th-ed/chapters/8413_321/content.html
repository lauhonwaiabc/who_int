8.4.1.3 Adenosarcoma of the uterine cervix

Definition

Adenosarcoma is a biphasic neoplasm composed of a benign epithelial component and a sarcomatous, usually low-grade, stromal component.

ICD-O coding

8933/3 Adenosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2C77.Y & XH5544 Other specified malignant neoplasms of cervix uteri & Adenosarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This tumour occurs in the uterine cervix, as well as in the uterus and the vagina. See section Adenosarcoma of the uterine corpus and section Adenosarcoma of the vagina.

Clinical features

Patients present across a wide age range (mean: 48 years) but tend to be younger than patients with uterine corpus tumours {27771166}. Clinical presentations include vaginal bleeding or discharge, pelvic pain, and a mass/polyp discovered on examination {18552169; 30122564}.

Epidemiology

Adenosarcoma is rare. It accounts for 0.16% of all cervical cancers and approximately 10% of adenosarcomas of the female genital tract {27771166}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. No relation to HPV has been documented {18552169}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours can be polypoid/papillary or nodular and endophytic {30122564}. The median size is 3.0 cm (range: 2.0–5.0 cm) {27771166}.

Histopathology

At low-power examination, a phyllodes-like architectural pattern with intraglandular polypoid projections and periglandular stromal cuffing is typical. Benign or mildly atypical glands are frequently lined by endocervical-type epithelium, occasionally with areas of squamous differentiation. The stromal component is usually low-grade endometrial stromal or fibroblastic in appearance. Mitotic figures are usually present; however, the diagnosis can be considered in the absence of mitotic figures when typical architecture is seen {18941402}. Heterologous stromal elements (fetal-type cartilage and rhabdomyoblasts) and sex cord–like areas can be present {18941402; 8600073; 17292949; 11748995}. The sarcomatous component may overgrow the epithelial component (referred to as sarcomatous overgrowth); there may also be transformation into high-grade sarcoma {2535774; 18941402}.

The differential diagnosis includes benign endometrial or cervical polyps with unusual morphology (focal phyllodes-like architecture, increased stromal cellularity surrounding glands) on the one hand and adenosarcoma with a low degree of stromal mitotic activity on the other. These lesions are much more common than adenofibroma, and a diagnosis of adenofibroma should be made with caution {25118811; 26715175; 20179434}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign or mildly atypical glands and sarcomatous, usually low-grade stroma (biphasic pattern) or periglandular stromal cuffing (phyllodes-like or cystic pattern).

Desirable: stromal mitoses.

Staging

The staging is the same as for other cervical tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

For completely resected tumours confined to the cervix, the prognosis is favourable {27771166}. Local recurrences can occur, but distant metastasis is rare. Deeper cervical wall invasion, sarcomatous overgrowth, and lymphovascular space invasion adversely affect prognosis {18941402; 8600073; 31396483}. The presence of tumour stalk is an independent protective factor for recurrence {31396483}.
