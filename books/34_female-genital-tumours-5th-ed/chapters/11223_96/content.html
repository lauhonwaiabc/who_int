1.12.2.3 Neuroectodermal-type tumours

Definition

Neuroectodermal-type tumours are malignant tumours of central neuroectodermal derivation.

ICD-O coding

9084/3 Teratoma with malignant transformation

ICD-11 coding

2F76 & XH7K24 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of female genital organs & Neuroectodermal tumour NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovary

Clinical features

The median patient age is 22 years (range: 6–70 years) – typically younger than seen with uterine tumours. Patients frequently present with a pelvic mass. Abdominal pain, ascites, and vaginal bleeding are less common manifestations. Rarely, patients present with extraovarian disease {8393302; 9808135; 22311546; 28296680; 30085939; 30074494}.

Epidemiology

These are rare tumours.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The frequent association of central-type primitive neuroectodermal tumour and glioma with teratoma suggests a germ cell derivation {8393302; 28296680}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically large and solid or solid and cystic {28296680; 8393302}. The solid component is white, tan, or pink. Haemorrhage and necrosis can be prominent. Teratomatous elements, such as waxy sebaceous or gelatinous material, hair, bone, or teeth may be present {28296680; 8393302; 7114357}.

Histopathology

These tumours are characterized either by a small round cell proliferation or by a variable degree of neuronal or glial differentiation. Differentiated central-type tumours may look like ependymoma, astrocytoma, oligodendroglioma, or neurocytoma {19483626; 9100074; 30074494; 6204919; 29107097}. Less-differentiated tumours have an appearance reminiscent of medulloblastoma, ependymoblastoma, medulloepithelioma, or glioblastoma. A component of mature teratoma may be present. In general, tumours show membranous CD99 and nuclear FLI1 expression, and they are often positive for CD56, NSE, and synaptophysin. They are rarely positive for broad-spectrum cytokeratin, and they are negative for desmin {28296680; 30074494}. About 50% of tumours (most commonly differentiated ones) express GFAP {28296680}. Ependymomas may be positive for ER and PR {30074494}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primitive tumour with a variable degree of neuroectodermal differentiation, as seen in brain tumours; positivity for CD99, FLI1, and GFAP.

Staging

This entity is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Limited clinical data suggest that stage is the most important prognostic factor for these tumours {8393302; 28296680}. Differentiated tumours are associated with better prognosis {8393302; 28296680; 30074494}.
