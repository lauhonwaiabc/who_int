13.1.2.4 Cellular angiofibroma

Definition

Cellular angiofibroma is a benign, cellular, and richly vascularized fibroblastic neoplasm that usually arises in the superficial soft tissues of the vulva or inguinoscrotal region.

ICD-O coding

9160/0 Cellular angiofibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH4E06 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Cellular angiofibroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiomyofibroblastoma-like tumour

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours typically arise in the superficial soft tissues of the vulvovaginal region and (in men) in the inguinoscrotal or paratesticular region. Rare examples have been described in other sites.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a slow-growing, painless mass. The most frequent preoperative diagnosis is Bartholin gland cyst {20852591; 15489646; 9199640}.

Epidemiology

Cellular angiofibroma is a rare neoplasm arising in adults. Women and men are roughly equally affected, with peak incidence in the fifth decade of life for women and the seventh decade for men {20852591; 15489646; 9422311; 9199640}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Partial or complete losses of chromosome 13 (including the RB1 locus) and/or chromosome 16 are found in cellular angiofibroma as well as in spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma and myofibroblastoma, suggesting pathogenetic similarity in these tumours {17727484; 20852591; 22790852; 28193293}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours range in size from 0.6 to 25 cm. Those in women are generally smaller (median size: 2.8 cm) than those in men (median size: 7.0 cm). The tumours appear as round, oval, or lobulated, well-circumscribed nodules. The consistency of the lesion varies from soft to rubbery, and the cut surface is solid with a greyish-pink to yellowish-brown colour {15489646; 9422311}. Foci of haemorrhage or necrosis are exceptional {15489646}.

Histopathology

The tumours are generally well circumscribed. Tumours are composed of uniform, short, spindle-shaped cells in an oedematous to fibrous stroma containing short bundles of delicate collagen fibres and numerous small to medium-sized thick-walled blood vessels with rounded, irregularly ectatic or branching lumina. The spindle cell component is usually moderately to highly cellular and randomly distributed throughout the lesion, occasionally with a fascicular arrangement or nuclear palisading. Spindle cells have short, oval to fusiform nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli and scant, palely eosinophilic cytoplasm with ill-defined borders. Mitoses are generally sparse but can be more frequent in some cases. The stroma consists primarily of wispy collagen, with occasional short bundles of densely eosinophilic collagen fibres. Variable stromal oedema, hyalinization, or myxoid change is often seen. Perivascular lymphoid aggregates may be present. Mast cells are frequent. Small aggregates or individual adipocytes are observed in close to 50% of cases. Degenerative changes such as slight nuclear enlargement and hyperchromasia, intravascular thrombi, and cystic change can be seen {15489646; 9422311; 15469474}.

Morphological sarcomatous transformation has been described rarely, mostly in the vulva {20305534; 20852591; 19783726}. Abrupt transition from cellular angiofibroma to a discrete sarcomatous nodule composed of multivacuolated lipoblasts has been reported, as have pleomorphic and hyperchromatic spindle cells showing morphological features of pleomorphic liposarcoma, atypical lipomatous tumour, or pleomorphic spindle cell sarcoma. Other rare cases show severely atypical cells scattered in conventional cellular angiofibroma. No necrosis or haemorrhage is observed. Mitoses in sarcomatous areas are often few {20305534; 20852591}.

By immunohistochemistry, CD34 expression has been documented in 30–60% of tumours. Variable expression of SMA and desmin is identified in 10–20% of cases. ER and PR are expressed in many cases {15489646; 9422311; 15469474}. RB1 loss is frequently detected {22790852}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Deletions of RB1, located in chromosome band 13q14, can be detected but are usually not required {17727484; 20852591; 28193293}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: usually in vulvovaginal or inguinal regions; bland spindle cells, short bundles of delicate collagen fibres, and numerous small to medium-sized thick-walled vessels, with or without adipose tissue.

Desirable: loss of RB1 may be helpful in selected cases.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence is very infrequent {15489646; 9422311; 15469474}. To date, the rare cases with atypia or sarcomatous transformation have not developed recurrence or metastasis {20305534; 20852591; 19783726}.
