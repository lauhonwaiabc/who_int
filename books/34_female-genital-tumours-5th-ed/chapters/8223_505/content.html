8.2.2.3 Adenocarcinoma in situ, HPV-independent, of the uterine cervix

Definition

Adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), HPV-independent, is a non-invasive glandular neoplasm unrelated to HPV. Gastric-type differentiation is characteristic.

ICD-O coding

8140/2 Adenocarcinoma in situ NOS

8484/2 Adenocarcinoma in situ, HPV-independent

ICD-11 coding

2C77.1 & XH2L30 Adenocarcinoma of cervix uteri & Adenocarcinoma in situ NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: gastric-type adenocarcinoma in situ; atypical lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Gastric-type AIS (gAIS) is typically at or just proximal to the transformation zone {28394803; 27334141} and can extend into the lower uterine segment and endometrium {28394803}.

Clinical features

These neoplasms can present with abnormal Pap smears or watery discharge {27334141; 28394803; 28449203}. Asymptomatic lesions are found incidentally on hysterectomy or cervical excisions performed for other indications {28394803}.

Epidemiology

Patients present over a wide age range (25–74 years; mean: 51 years). Although AIS is virtually always associated with HPV {22323075}, HPV-independent cases have been rarely reported {23552379}. However, because most cases of AIS are asymptomatic and diagnosed through cytology-based and/or HPV-based cervical screening, reports are likely to underrepresent precursors of HPV-independent types of invasive adenocarcinoma.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

gAIS does not form visible mass lesions.

Histopathology

gAIS is characterized by cuboidal to columnar cells with distinct cell borders, eosinophilic to pale and/or foamy vacuolated cytoplasm, and nuclear atypia that ranges from mild to severe {28394803; 23752085}. Mitoses and apoptosis are present but not conspicuous, and they are usually identified under high magnification. Intestinal (goblet cell) differentiation (to varying degrees) is often observed. The proliferation involves pre-existing normal endocervical glands and thus lacks haphazard gland crowding or confluence. However, intraglandular complexity (cribriform, papillary) can occur. On occasion, the lesion grows proximally to involve endometrial glands {29683820}. A related lesion, atypical lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia, is defined as cytological atypia within the confines of a lobulated proliferation consistent with lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia (see section Lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia). It is likely that these entities represent a spectrum of gAIS. By immunohistochemistry, gAIS and lobular endocervical glandular hyperplasia are positive for CK7 and the pyloric gland marker HIK1083 (MUC6 lacks specificity). PAX8 and CDX2 are often positive. ER and PR are negative or only weakly positive. p16 expression is either absent or patchy. Abnormal p53 immunohistochemical staining can support the diagnosis {28394803; 15143335}. An in situ equivalent for clear cell carcinoma has not been well characterized.

Cytology

HPV-independent AIS can appear morphologically similar to HPV-associated AIS with enlarged, hyperchromatic nuclei and feathering. However, it may be distinguished by the presence of yellow gastric-type mucin, which is best seen on conventional Pap smears {29982289}. Because mucin is paler on liquid-based preparations, it can be difficult to distinguish yellow gastric-type mucin from pink normal endocervical mucin.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: proliferation confined to normal endocervical glands; glandular epithelium with distinct cell borders and eosinophilic to pale mucinous cytoplasm; nuclear atypia and proliferation; intraglandular complexity allowed; negative/patchy p16 or negative HPV testing; negative ER and PR.

Desirable: positivity for HIK1083, CK7, and MUC6 (MUC6 is nonspecific).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The biological behaviour and malignant potential of gAIS is unknown. Complete excision with cone biopsy is recommended.
