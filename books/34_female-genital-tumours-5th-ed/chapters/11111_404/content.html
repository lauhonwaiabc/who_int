11.1.1.1 Neuroendocrine tumour

Definition

Neuroendocrine tumour (NET) is a low-grade or intermediate-grade epithelial neoplasm with morphological and immunohistochemical features of neuroendocrine differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8240/3 Neuroendocrine tumour NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C7Z & XH9LV8 Malignant neoplasms of female genital organs, unspecified & Neuroendocrine tumour, grade 1

Related terminology

Acceptable: carcinoid tumour; typical carcinoid tumour; atypical carcinoid tumour

Subtype(s)

Neuroendocrine tumour, grade 1; neuroendocrine tumour, grade 2

Localization

NETs arise in the cervix, uterine corpus, fallopian tube, vagina, and vulva. These neoplasms also occur in the ovary, where the term “carcinoid tumour” is used.

Clinical features

The presentation is variable and depends on the site of origin. Patients with cervical lesions may present with an abnormal cervical smear, vaginal bleeding, and/or a visible cervical lesion. In addition, serum chromogranin A and 24-hour urinary 5-HIAA may be elevated {28878659; 21375671}. Carcinoid syndrome can occur if metastatic disease involves the liver {10566674}. Patients with uterine tumours may present with abdominal distension or abnormal vaginal bleeding {7679854; 9777628}, whereas vulvar tumours usually present with a painless mass {22895270}. The neoplasms reported in the vagina or fallopian tubes have been incidental findings {3345952; 29019870}.

Epidemiology

These are extremely rare neoplasms at all sites within the female genital tract except the ovary. Patients with cervical tumours are usually in their fourth or fifth decade of life {28878659; 10566674; 31635755}. Patients with vulvar tumours have ranged in age from 38 to 56 years {22895270}, and the rare uterine corpus tumours have been in elderly women {7679854; 9777628}. Two fallopian tube tumours, not associated with teratomas, were detected in a 49-year-old woman and a 61-year-old woman {29019870}. A single example of vaginal mucinous NET was reported in a 32-year-old woman {3345952}.

Etiology

HPV16 has been detected in a single carcinoid tumour of the uterine cervix {29555602}, and HPV16 and HPV18 have been found in atypical carcinoids at this anatomical site {9889022; 29555602}.

Pathogenesis

These tumours appear to originate from neuroendocrine cells located in the cervical glandular and squamous epithelium {28878659}, in the major and minor vulvar vestibular glands {22895270}, endometrial epithelium {7679854}, and fallopian tube epithelium {29019870}.

Macroscopic appearance

Cervical tumours are usually ≤ 2 cm and polypoid or indurated {570452; 28878659}, whereas tumours arising in the endometrium have been as large as 5 cm {7679854}. Vulvar tumours are typically well circumscribed and small (0.8–1.1 cm), with no involvement of the overlying epithelium {22895270}. Fallopian tube tumours have been incidental microscopic findings {29019870}.

Histopathology

The tumours show a trabecular, ribbon-like, glandular, or nested growth pattern. The neoplastic cells are monotonous and cuboidal/columnar/polygonal, with a variable amount of cytoplasm. The nuclei can be either regular with uniform chromatin and small nucleoli (grade 1 tumours) or variable in size and shape with irregularly distributed chromatin (grade 2 tumours). Grade 1 tumours show rare mitotic figures, whereas grade 2 tumours can show 5–10 mitotic figures/2 mm2 (5–10 mitotic figures/10 HPF of 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area), as well as foci of necrosis {9111090}. Clearing of the cytoplasm may be seen {22895270}. Immunohistochemically, the tumours express at least one of the neuroendocrine markers synaptophysin, chromogranin, and CD56. In the cervix, the tumours can be associated with a component of in situ or invasive squamous carcinoma or adenocarcinoma {9111090}.

Cytology

Cytology shows cuboidal/columnar/polygonal cells with variable amounts of pale, granular cytoplasm and monotonous or variable nuclei, growing in a solid, tubular, honeycomb, or rosette-like pattern {21394936}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: low- to intermediate-grade tumour with a monotonous cell population arranged in a trabecular, nested, ribbon-like, or glandular pattern; immunohistochemical confirmation of neuroendocrine differentiation.

Staging

TNM or FIGO staging is used as for other female genital tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

These are rare neoplasms with no large studies. The prognosis depends predominantly on the stage. Cervical tumours tend to present as FIGO stage I disease {28878659; 31635755}. Grade 2 tumours have been reported to be less responsive to chemotherapy than high-grade cervical neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs) {31635755}. The experience with endometrial, vaginal, vulvar, and fallopian tube tumours is very limited {22895270; 7679854; 9777628; 3345952; 29019870}.
