4.2.0.6 Mucinous metaplasia

Definition

Mucinous metaplasia is replacement of part of the normal lining of the fallopian tube by bland mucinous cells.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA17 Acquired abnormalities of fallopian tube

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fallopian tube

Clinical features

This is an underreported entity. It is mostly an incidental finding. It can be seen in association with gynaecological neoplasia. This may be a result of extensive sampling of the fallopian tube {25025442; 21131836}. Mucinous metaplasia is usually unilateral and unifocal {21131836}.

Epidemiology

The epidemiology is unknown.

Etiology

Mucinous metaplasia may occur in association with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome {21615613}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

There is no macroscopic lesion.

Histopathology

The tubal lining is partly replaced by a monolayer of bland mucinous cells. Accompanying chronic inflammation and other metaplasias, such as transitional cell metaplasia, may be seen {21131836}. The differential diagnosis includes spread of well-differentiated mucinous tumours to the tubal mucosa, which can produce an appearance that is indistinguishable, but the immunoprofile reflects the primary carcinoma {25025442; 24901398}. Mucinous metaplasia is positive for CK7 and CK20. In one report HIK1083, claudin-18, and MUC6 expression was reported {27662036}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland proliferation of mucinous cells in the absence of a mucinous carcinoma.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Mucinous metaplasia is benign.
