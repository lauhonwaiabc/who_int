14.0.1.2 Congenital melanocytic naevus

Definition

Congenital melanocytic naevus is a benign melanocytic neoplasm present at birth or appearing in the first year of life.

ICD-O coding

8761/0 Congenital melanocytic naevus NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F20.2 Congenital melanocytic naevus

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Giant pigmented naevus NOS

Localization

Giant congenital melanocytic naevus with garment distribution frequently affects the genitalia. Small lesions can involve the vulva.

Clinical features

Congenital melanocytic naevi are well-defined (but not sharply demarcated), tan to brown papules, nodules, or plaques. Within the first 6 years, congenital melanocytic naevi may continue to grow and change colour {3777972}. They usually darken, get thicker, and increase the volume of terminal hairs. Satellitosis is often seen. Most congenital melanocytic naevi arise in the vulvar skin. Small congenital naevi are < 1.5 cm in diameter, intermediate congenital naevi 1.5–20 cm, and giant congenital naevi > 20 cm {23182059; 19503980}.

Epidemiology

Small congenital melanocytic naevi affect approximately 1% of newborn infants.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most large congenital melanocytic naevi harbour exon 3 NRAS mutations; smaller congenital melanocytic naevi can also harbour BRAF p.Val600Glu mutations {31111470}. Congenital naevi are related to the migration of neural crest precursors (melanoblasts) during embryogenesis {24460190}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not clinically relevant

Histopathology

Junctional congenital melanocytic naevi display intraepidermal single and nested melanocytes in a background of variably hyperplastic epidermis. The dermal and compound types exhibit single and nested small dermal melanocytes lacking cytological atypia and demonstrating maturation. Perivascular and periadnexal growth can be noted. Melanocytes can be seen infiltrating arrector pili and/or nerves. If biopsy is performed within the first weeks of life, during the naevi’s active growth phase, a concerning pagetoid scatter and high dermal mitotic activity (as many as 27 mitoses/mm2) can be present {23348144}. Longstanding congenital melanocytic naevi can develop dermal proliferating nodules, some of which are composed of atypical melanocytes and show mitotic activity.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a pigmented lesion present at birth or appearing within the first year of life.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Giant congenital melanocytic naevus is the main risk factor for developing melanoma in childhood {30782194}. The risk of developing melanoma appears to be proportional to the size of the lesion. In giant lesions, the lifetime risk can be approaching 10%. The highest incidence of melanoma arising in congenital melanocytic naevi is within the first 5 years of life, with a second peak from puberty into early adulthood {23182059; 27849631; 18671780; 16792745}.
