6.3.1.5 Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential of the uterine corpus

Definition

Smooth muscle tumours of uncertain malignant potential (STUMPs) show morphological features that exceed criteria for leiomyoma or its subtypes, yet are insufficient for a diagnosis of leiomyosarcoma, and behave in a malignant fashion in only a minority of cases.

ICD-O coding

8897/1 Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential

ICD-11 coding

2F76 & XH1EN1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of female genital organs & Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Not recommended: atypical smooth muscle neoplasm

Subtype(s)

Spindle STUMP; myxoid STUMP; epithelioid STUMP

Localization

Uterus (arising in myometrium)

Clinical features

Patients’ symptomatology is similar to that of patients with typical leiomyomas in the uterus.

Epidemiology

These smooth muscle tumours are rare. They typically occur in reproductive-aged or postmenopausal patients, with a mean patient age of approximately 43 years, a decade younger than patients with leiomyosarcoma {29537683}. Patients diagnosed with a STUMP who develop recurrences are also as much as 10 years younger than those with an uneventful follow-up {19342083; 19417585; 21865091}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

STUMPs are genomically heterogeneous, with features ranging from very simple profiles characterized by a few chromosomal alterations to high chromosomal instability and complexity, similar to uterine leiomyosarcoma (but chromosomal gains are not as frequent) {25932961; 29327710; 31189997}. These findings have been reported in spindle cell and myxoid subtypes in which copy-number variation is important in tumour pathogenesis and may serve as a useful diagnostic tool {25932961; 26637855}. MED12 mutations occur in as many as 10% of these smooth muscle tumours {22768200; 26037152; 24196187}.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross features of these smooth muscle tumours often overlap with those seen in typical leiomyomas. The size of tumours associated with recurrence is similar to that of those that do not recur, but some neoplasms associated with recurrence have been noted to have irregular borders {29537683}.

Histopathology

This category of smooth muscle tumours is morphologically heterogeneous. It has been stated that tumours should have one of the criteria used for the diagnosis of leiomyosarcoma. Other parameters that may be useful include the finding of atypical mitoses, vascular involvement, and infiltrative/irregular margins {29537683; 18162776}. When an unusual smooth muscle tumour is encountered, every effort should be made to establish a diagnosis of either leiomyoma (usually one of its subtypes) or leiomyosarcoma by integrating gross and microscopic features with generous sampling {26715170}.

This is a challenging diagnostic area, and the following are general guidelines for spindled cell smooth muscle tumours to be placed in this group, but these should not be taken as strict diagnostic criteria:

(1) Tumours with focal/multifocal or diffuse nuclear atypia, with 2–4 mitoses/mm2 (equating to 5–9 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area), and lacking tumour cell necrosis. Approximately 12–17% of reported tumours with these features have recurred. It should be noted that some of these tumours with fewer mitoses have also recurred (see #10909Table #10909).

(2) Tumours with tumour cell necrosis (seen in ~28%) but no other worrisome features. These have also recurred. Given the difficulty of assessment of tumour cell necrosis and its confusion with early infarct-type necrosis {17667546; 23552382}, this diagnosis is appropriate for any bland-appearing smooth muscle tumour containing unequivocal tumour cell necrosis or necrosis of uncertain type.

(3) Tumours lacking cytological atypia and tumour cell necrosis, but with > 6 mitoses/mm2 (equating to ≥ 15 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area). Although none of the 42 such cases reported to date have recurred, experience is limited and these tumours are best placed in this category {8179071; 19417585; 29537683}.

(4) Tumours with diffuse nuclear atypia and uncertain mitotic counts, often due to brisk prominent karyorrhexis. PHH3 immunohistochemistry may be helpful to evaluate mitoses in these cases {27864989; 19483635; 26191133}.

Within the epithelioid and myxoid categories, the criteria are more strict. Tumours that exceed the criteria for leiomyoma (#10818Box #10818) but fall below the threshold of leiomyosarcoma (#13669Box #13669) fall into the STUMP category {9130984; 56982; 26866354; 28787279}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

See #10909Table #10909.

Staging

These tumours are not usually staged.

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence rates of STUMPs have ranged from 7% to 28%, depending on the criteria applied (see #10909Table #10909). Epithelioid and myxoid subtypes may be more likely to recur {18162776; 29537683}. The time to recurrence is longer (average: 47 months) than for leiomyosarcomas, and the recurrent tumour may look histologically similar to the initial tumour or to leiomyosarcoma. The median survival time for patients with recurrences is 5.5 years {21865091}. Immunohistochemical markers such as p16, p53, Ki-67, p21, BCL2, ER, and PR have been used to enhance the diagnosis and prognosis in these tumours without success {18580309; 23552380; 28609585}. Loss of ATRX or DAXX expression is associated with an adverse outcome {27499896; 30423196; 26891131}. A genomic index of > 35, chromosome 5p and 17p gain, and chromosome 13 loss have also been shown to be poor prognostic factors {25932961; 29327710}.
