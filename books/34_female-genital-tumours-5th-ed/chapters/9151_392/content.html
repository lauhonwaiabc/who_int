9.1.5.1 Adenocarcinoma of Skene gland origin

Definition

Adenocarcinoma of Skene gland origin is an adenocarcinoma arising in the Skene glands, morphologically and immunohistochemically similar to prostatic adenocarcinoma.

ICD-O coding

8140/3 Carcinoma of Skene, Cowper, and Littré glands

ICD-11 coding

2C71.0 & XH22Z8 Adenocarcinoma of vagina & Carcinoma of Skene, Cowper, and Littré glands

Related terminology

Acceptable: Skene or periurethral gland adenocarcinoma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Periurethral/paraurethral, urethral, anterior vaginal wall, and posterior bladder neck {2990157; 14668555; 9584326; 7525428; 3114949; 28872768; 7507278; 20870432}

Clinical features

The usual presentation is with a mass and (less frequently) haematuria or incontinence. Serum PSA may be elevated {2990157; 14668555; 20870432; 7525428}.

Epidemiology

This extremely rare tumour has been reported in patients ranging in age from 46 to 88 years (median: 70 years) {2990157; 14668555; 9584326; 7525428; 3114949; 28872768; 7507278; 20870432}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These tumours arise from the periurethral Skene glands, the female equivalent of the prostatic glands in males.

Macroscopic appearance

It is a polypoid or flat tumour, 1.0–3.5 cm in size {7525428}.

Histopathology

These neoplasms are usually morphologically similar to acinar prostatic adenocarcinoma. Typically, there is a population of well-formed to poorly formed glands lined by cuboidal or columnar epithelium, with pale or eosinophilic cytoplasm, round nuclei, and prominent nucleoli. A cribriform pattern is common, and individual glands, solid areas, or a single-file pattern can be seen. The degree of nuclear atypia is variable. Goblet cells are rarely seen. Residual benign Skene glands may be present. Immunohistochemically, usual Skene gland adenocarcinoma is positive for prostatic markers: PSA, PAP, NKX3-1, P501S, AMACR, and PIN4 cocktail (high-molecular-weight cytokeratin, p63, and AMACR) {2990157; 14668555; 9584326; 7525428; 3114949; 28872768; 7507278; 20870432}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: adenocarcinoma morphologically resembling a prostatic adenocarcinoma.

Desirable: positivity for prostatic markers.

Staging

The staging is the same as for other urethral neoplasms.

Prognosis and prediction

Experience with this neoplasm is limited; a single case has been reported with metastases involving inguinal lymph nodes.
