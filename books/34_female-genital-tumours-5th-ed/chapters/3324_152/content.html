3.3.2.4 Extragastrointestinal stromal tumour

Definition

Extragastrointestinal stromal tumour (EGIST) is a mesenchymal neoplasm with variable behaviour, characterized by differentiation towards the interstitial cells of Cajal, but occurring outside the gastrointestinal tract.

ICD-O coding

8936/3 Gastrointestinal stromal tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F74 & XH8RP6 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of peritoneum & Gastrointestinal stromal tumour NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

EGISTs mainly affect the omentum, mesentery, and retroperitoneum {19440146; 28288036}. They may represent metastasis from an unrecognized primary or a detached mass from the gastrointestinal tract. Exceptionally, EGISTs can involve solid organs, such as the liver and pancreas.

Clinical features

Patients commonly present with an abdominopelvic mass, abdominal discomfort, and/or pain. Asymptomatic small tumours are detected incidentally during surgery or radiological examination.

Epidemiology

EGISTs are rare and may occur at any age.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most EGISTs harbour gain-of-function mutations of the KIT or PDGFRA oncogene.

Macroscopic appearance

EGISTs can be solitary or multiple, and they may be of considerable size. The cut section can be solid or cystic with haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

The histopathological features of EGISTs are similar to those of their counterpart in the gastrointestinal tract. Solitary tumours are similar to gastric gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GISTs), whereas multinodular EGISTs are more likely to show histological features similar to those of small intestinal GISTs. Some cases have indeterminate histology {19440146; 28288036}. The histological patterns include spindled, epithelioid, vacuolated, nested, and myxoid {19440146; 28288036}.

Immunophenotypically, > 90% of EGISTs are positive for KIT (CD117). DOG1 antibody may rescue diagnostically as many as 50% of KIT-negative GISTs {15215166; 18223323; 19606013; 20716168}. Most spindle cell GISTs are positive for CD34, whereas epithelioid examples are less consistently positive.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Approximately 70% of EGISTs harbour mutations of the KIT or PDGFRA gene {19440146; 28288036}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: circumscribed morphologically uniform spindle cell or epithelioid tumour with KIT (CD117) and/or DOG1 immunopositivity.

Staging

Risk stratification is preferred to anatomical staging {29846513}.

Prognosis and prediction

Inoperable EGISTs have a worse prognosis {28288036}. Tumours with small intestinal GIST features are more likely to have an aggressive course {10824931; 19440146}. Brisk mitotic activity and necrosis are also adverse prognostic factors {10824931; 19440146; 28288036; 29599305}.
