14.0.1.1 Acquired melanocytic naevus

Definition

Acquired melanocytic naevus is an acquired benign proliferation of melanocytes with features similar to those found in extragenital skin or mucosa.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Naevus NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F20.0 Common acquired melanocytic naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign melanocytic naevus; common melanocytic naevus

Not recommended: naevocellular naevus

Subtype(s)

Junctional naevus NOS; intradermal naevus; compound naevus

Localization

Melanocytic naevi usually affect the labia majora, labia minora, and clitoris, and less frequently the perineum or mons {1606765}. Melanocytic naevi arising in a background of lichen sclerosus have been reported mainly in mucosal areas such as the labia minora and clitoris {11790170; 15097951; 24018819}.

Clinical features

Naevi usually appear during infancy or in individuals of childbearing age, occuring as well-defined flat or papular pigmented lesions. Accelerated growth may be observed during pregnancy. Melanocytic naevi usually present as single, tan or brown lesions that are uniform in colour, symmetrical, and well-demarcated with regular borders, measuring as large as 5–6 mm in diameter {2298946}. Vulvar mucosal melanocytic naevi are usually flat-topped or macular, whereas naevi in hair-bearing skin tend to be dome-shaped, flat-topped, or (less commonly) macular.

Epidemiology

Melanocytic neoplasms in the genitalia account for 10–12% of vulvar pigmented lesions, with melanocytic naevi representing 2.3% of these lesions {2298946; 1606765}. All types of melanocytic naevi can be found, including acquired or congenital melanocytic naevi, dysplastic naevi, blue naevi, and atypical melanocytic naevi of the genital type. They are frequently an incidental finding during self-examination or a medical examination for another reason (pregnancy, etc.). Among children and adolescents, the incidence is 3.5% {24373784}, with a median patient age at onset of 5.5 years. In adults, vulvar melanocytic naevi are generally found in women in the reproductive age group {28778635; 25267379}. Acquired melanocytic naevi account for 2.3% of vulvar pigmented lesions {2298946; 1606765}. Dermal naevi predominate {3597916}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

BRAF p.Val600Glu mutations have been detected {24842760}.

Macroscopic appearance

Acquired melanocytic naevi are homogeneously pigmented lesions that are typically solitary and flat, papular, or plaque-like. They can reach 1 cm in size.

Histopathology

Similar to naevi occurring elsewhere, genital naevi are composed of variable proportions of junctional and/or subepithelial/dermal components. Regularly sized and distributed melanocytic nests and single cells in the epidermis characterize junctional melanocytic naevus. In dermal melanocytic naevus, dermal melanocytes are seen displaying zonal maturation as the melanocytes become smaller and more spindled and lose pigment with depth. Lesions displaying both described junctional and subepithelial/dermal components are classified as compound melanocytic naevi. Immunohistochemical melanocytic markers such as S100, SOX10, and melan-A (MART1) are positive. HMB45 demonstrates a top-heavy staining profile.

Melanocytic naevi superimposed on lichen sclerosus can share some architectural features (i.e. confluent growth pattern and impression of pagetoid growth pattern in atrophic epithelium or epidermis) with recurrent naevi and melanoma. However, they are sharply demarcated and symmetrical, and pagetoid spread (if present) is rather focal. The trizonal pattern (confluent growth of predominantly single cells in the epidermis with dermal sclerosis and an underlying dermal naevus) and dermal melanocytic maturation can be seen. No mitotic figures are expected, and the Ki-67 proliferation index is < 5%. HMB45 in the dermal component is seen in the nests entrapped in the sclerosis.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: naevus cells with symmetry and evidence of cellular maturation (compound and intradermal/stromal).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most naevi have a completely indolent course, and recurrence after excision is rare. Melanoma ex naevus is an exceptional event in this location.
