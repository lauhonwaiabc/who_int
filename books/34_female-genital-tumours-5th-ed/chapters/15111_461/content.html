15.1.1.1 Metastasis to the lower female genital tract

Definition

Metastases to the lower female genital tract are tumours spreading to the uterus, vagina, or vulva from other primary sites.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

Code according to the primary neoplasm.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Metastases to the uterine corpus or cervix are uncommon {6322966; 3955507; 29750707}, whereas metastases to the vagina are much more common than primary vaginal malignancies [[Fu YS, Reagan JW. Pathology of the uterine cervix, vagina, and vulva. Philadelphia (PA): Saunders; 1989. pp. 193–224.]]. Metastases to the vulva are also rare, accounting for 5–10% of all vulvar malignancies {12766584}.

Clinical features

Patients may present with bleeding, abnormal cervical cytology {3955507; 15943996; 21979600; 22961708}, or a mass accompanied by ulceration or pain {12766584}. Patients may be asymptomatic if the infiltration affects the myometrium only. Occasionally, such metastasis may represent the first manifestation of disease.

Epidemiology

The reported patient age range is wide (10–86 years) {6322966; 12766584; 30918738}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most secondary tumours in the uterine corpus occur by direct extension from neoplasms of neighbouring organs. Among extragenital primary tumours metastatic to the uterine corpus, the most frequently reported are mammary lobular carcinoma, gastric signet-ring cell carcinoma, and colonic carcinomas {29750707; 30918738; 15943994}. Contiguous spread to the cervix from an endometrial primary carcinoma is most common {14501374; 12883240}, followed by metastases from the ovary, gastrointestinal tract, breast, and kidney {3955507}. Metastases to the vagina most commonly originate from cervical carcinomas. Other sites of origin include the endometrium, colorectum, ovary, vulva, urinary tract, and breast, as well as cutaneous melanoma, uterine leiomyosarcoma, and gestational trophoblastic tumours {12649667; 18720923}. Metastases to the vulva arise mainly from the gynaecological tract {12766584} but also from the gastrointestinal tract, skin (melanoma), and breast {12766584}.

Macroscopic appearance

The metastases may be grossly undetectable, or they may present as single or multiple nodules that may be ulcerated {12766584}.

Histopathology

Features suggesting metastasis to the uterine corpus include a histological pattern that is unusual for primary endometrial carcinoma, diffuse replacement of endometrial stroma with sparing of benign endometrial glands, lack of premalignant changes in endometrial glands, and disproportionate carcinomatous involvement of the serosa or outer myometrium. Features suggesting metastasis to the cervix include signet-ring cells, lack of an in situ component, and extensive lymphovascular invasion. Metastatic endometrial endometrioid carcinoma may show deceptively altered morphology and simulate primary cervical adenocarcinoma (including gastric-type adenocarcinoma) or even benign lesions such as mesonephric hyperplasia {12493029; 12883240; 20414103}. The histopathology of metastases to the vagina varies with the type of tumour. Vulvar metastases commonly involve the dermis and subcutis. Pagetoid spread of rectal or bladder carcinoma to the lower genital tract has been reported {28461034; 22510759} and may mimic vulvar Paget disease {22510759}. Immunohistochemistry may help to confirm the diagnosis and the origin of the metastasis (see #13294Table #13294).

Cytology

Isolated or clustered malignant tumour cells in a background of normal cervical cells without tumour cell diathesis are suggestive of metastatic nature {27434279}. Melanoma and sarcoma cells have been reported in cervical smears {30354020; 12040638}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The diagnostic molecular pathology depends on the primary neoplasm.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: unusual histopathology pattern for the site; clinical correlation.

Desirable: ancillary immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Stage according to the primary neoplasm.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is poor because there are often accompanying systemic metastases {2171127; 12766584; 21941962}.
