1.11.1.7 Signet-ring stromal tumour

Definition

Signet-ring stromal tumour is a benign stromal tumour containing cells with signet-ring morphology in a background of fibromatous stroma.

ICD-O coding

8590/0 Signet-ring stromal tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F76 & XH69N5 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of female genital organs & Signet-ring stromal tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovary

Clinical features

Presentation is usually with symptoms attributable to an ovarian mass. Hormonal manifestations are usually absent.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare. They have occurred in adults with ages ranging from 21 to 83 years {7483017; 14668550; 20664453; 26509912; 27513075; 30676431}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The exact pathogenesis is uncertain, but signet-ring stromal tumour probably represents a degenerative cytoplasmic change within a stromal tumour.

Macroscopic appearance

Signet-ring stromal tumour is typically unilateral, but rare bilateral cases have been reported {30676431; 20664453}. The cut surface is usually solid, whitish-yellow, and firm.

Histopathology

Variable numbers of signet-ring cells are present in a background stromal component, which often resembles cellular fibroma {7483017; 14668550}. The nuclei are eccentrically located, small, uniform, and bland. Small nucleoli and occasional nuclear grooves may be present. Mitotic figures are rare or absent. The signet-ring cells contain a single large cytoplasmic vacuole, which does not contain mucin, glycogen, or lipid. Eosinophilic hyaline globules may be present. A collision tumour with components of signet-ring stromal tumour and steroid cell tumour has been reported {27513075}. PAS and mucin histochemical stains are negative. Immunohistochemically, the tumour cells are usually positive for calretinin, SF1, and SMA. They may be focally positive for pancytokeratin and are typically negative for inhibin and EMA. Nuclear β-catenin and cyclin D1 expression, along with CD10 immunoreactivity, has been reported {26509912}. Abnormalities of CTNNB1 have been reported in occasional cases {26509912}. Distinction from metastatic signet-ring cell carcinoma is based on negative EMA and absence of intracytoplasmic mucin.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: signet-ring cells within a background fibromatous stroma; lack of expression of EMA; absence of intracytoplasmic mucin.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The reported cases have shown benign behaviour.
