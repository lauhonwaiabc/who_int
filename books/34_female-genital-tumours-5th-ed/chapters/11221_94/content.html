1.12.2.1 Struma ovarii

Definition

Struma ovarii is a mature teratoma in which thyroid tissue is the predominant or sole component.

ICD-O coding

9090/0 Struma ovarii NOS

9090/3 Struma ovarii, malignant

ICD-11 coding

2F76 & XH22M4 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of female genital organs & Struma ovarii NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovary

Clinical features

Struma ovarii is usually an incidental finding (with peak incidence in the fifth decade of life), but patients may present with signs and symptoms related to a pelvic mass {19471561; 7802134}. Ascites (with or without pleural effusion) is seen in about one third of patients, and hyperthyroidism in < 10% {19696610}.

Epidemiology

Struma ovarii was the most common type of monodermal teratoma (~3%) in a pathological case series {8037292}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

BRAF and KRAS mutations and RET/PTC and PAX8-PPARG rearrangements have been identified, respectively, in papillary (including follicular variant) carcinoma and follicular carcinoma arising in struma ovarii {18058267; 17721188; 26362194; 31538107}.

Macroscopic appearance

Struma ovarii is typically unilateral, measures < 10 cm, and has a solid beefy (sometimes nodular) red to brown cut surface resembling normal thyroid or goitre. It may have cysts or (rarely) be entirely cystic {8037292}. A dermoid cyst may be seen {7802134}.

Histopathology

Struma ovarii usually resembles normal thyroid tissue, with variably sized follicles (most frequently macrofollicles) lined by cuboidal to flat cells and filled with colloid. Other patterns include solid, trabecular, pseudopapillary, pseudotubular {7802134}, and predominantly cystic. The predominantly cystic pattern mimics a serous cystadenoma, with only rare follicles seen in its wall {8037292}. Hyperplastic changes (proliferative struma) and adenomatous changes may be seen {8253550}. Cytoplasm is typically scant and pale but may occasionally be abundant and clear or oxyphilic. Nuclei are typically bland, round, and small (rarely they may be optically clear), and mitoses are rare. Stroma is typically scant and collagenous but can be prominent and oedematous. Stromal luteinization may be seen peripherally {3804558}. Papillary, follicular (including highly differentiated) {19696610; 19620939; 18753973; 18058267; 17721188; 22288964; 18317221; 8253550; 26374222}, or anaplastic carcinoma may rarely develop {26630220}. Struma ovarii may be seen in association with mature cystic teratoma, carcinoid (typically trabecular), Brenner tumour, or mucinous cystadenoma {22558485; 30134344; 7802134}. Tumour cells express thyroglobulin, TTF1, and PAX8 {17285841; 16512597}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign thyroid tissue alone or constituting > 50% of a dermoid cyst.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The great majority of tumours are clinically benign, but behaviour cannot always be predicted on the basis of the histological appearances. Struma ovarii may have a protracted clinical course when associated with secondary malignancies {19696610; 26374222; 20407319; 22288964; 19620939}. Other adverse prognostic factors include adhesions, surface defects, and > 1 L of peritoneal fluid {19696610}. No single histological feature correlates with prognosis {22288964}. The presence of peritoneal implants of well-differentiated thyroid tissue in a patient with a histologically benign struma ovarii, known as strumosis, is now thought to represent metastasis from a highly differentiated follicular carcinoma arising in struma ovarii {22288964; 18317221}.
