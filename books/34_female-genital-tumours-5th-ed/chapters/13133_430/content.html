13.1.3.3 Angiosarcoma

Definition

Angiosarcoma is a malignant vascular neoplasm that variably recapitulates the morphological and immunohistochemical features of endothelial cells.

ICD-O coding

9120/3 Angiosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B56.Y & XH6264 Angiosarcoma, other specified primary site & Haemangiosarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: haemangiosarcoma; lymphangiosarcoma; malignant haemangioendothelioma; malignant angioendothelioma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Angiosarcoma is more common in the uterus and ovary, but the vulva and vagina can be involved {24257655; 28595254; 19396453; 9591733}.

Clinical features

Patients present with vaginal bleeding, anaemia, and/or nonspecific gastrointestinal symptoms {24257655}. Vulvar tumours may present as white papules {26704338}.

Epidemiology

The median patient age at presentation is 47 years (range: 17–87 years) {24257655}.

Etiology

Most cases are sporadic. In a subset, radiation therapy and chronic lymphoedema are predisposing factors {28795130; 17581409; 16343203}.

Pathogenesis

Angiosarcomas are genetically heterogeneous. Most harbour complex karyotypes, without recurrent chromosomal changes {20217954; 25231439}. High-level MYC gene amplifications (at 8q24) occur in almost all postradiation and chronic lymphoedema–associated secondary angiosarcomas, and rarely in primary angiosarcomas {20949568; 20008140; 24329959; 22121953}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours form a large, friable haemorrhagic mass with poorly defined margins and an ulcerated surface, but flat plaque-like tumours have been reported in the cervix {31815894}.

Histopathology

Most angiosarcomas have a lobulated architecture with closely packed, anastomosing abortive vascular channels; cystic spaces; and solid sheets of spindled and epithelioid cells with haemorrhage and necrosis. Tumour cells are pleomorphic and contain large, hyperchromatic nuclei. Mitoses are usually numerous. Prominent stromal hyalinization may be present. Immunohistochemically, the tumour cells stain for CD31, CD34, factor VIII, and ERG. Some angiosarcomas express D2-40 or cytokeratins. ER and PR are negative {24257655}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of MYC amplification may be helpful, although only in the setting of radiation-associated or lymphoedema-associated angiosarcoma {22121953; 21909081; 20949568}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: architecturally complex vascular structures lined by atypical endothelial cells; solid patterns and epithelioid morphology may be seen.

Desirable: immunoreactivity for endothelial markers (in selected cases).

Staging

Staging of angiosarcoma is not recommended, because this neoplasm’s typically aggressive natural history is not consistent with soft tissue staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

As is the case for soft tissue tumours in general, prognosis depends on stage (extent of disease). Higher-stage tumours are associated with a mean overall survival time of < 30 months {24257655}. Much of the evidence is from uterine and cervical cases. Factors such as epithelioid change, necrosis, and margin status, which have been shown to be prognostically significant for angiosarcomas in general, require specific validation at gynaecological sites {20485141; 17974557}. Older patient age, retroperitoneal site, and large tumour size are associated with poor outcome {9630175; 17974557; 20485141}. The most frequent sites of distant metastasis are the lungs, followed by lymph nodes, soft tissues, bone, liver, and other sites (including the brain) {9630175; 15166670}.
