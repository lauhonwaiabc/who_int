9.0.1 Tumours of the vagina: Introduction

Benign, premalignant, and malignant lesions are generally much less common in the vagina than at other sites in the female genital tract (see Epidemiology in section Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-associated, of the vagina). As is the case in the cervix, vaginal epithelial pathology is dominated by HPV infection and its neoplastic consequences. However, it is now clear that some primary vaginal carcinomas (a minority of squamous carcinomas and most adenocarcinomas) are unrelated to HPV infection; moreover, the presence of HPV is a favourable prognostic factor for vaginal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) {22226684}. This is reflected in this edition of the WHO classification, in which HPV-associated and HPV-independent SCCs and adenocarcinomas are recognized in most of the lower genital tract sites.

The classification of squamous lesions of the vagina parallels that of the cervix, with HPV-associated and HPV-independent SCCs recognized as separate entities. Although there are currently no major differences in treatment between HPV-associated and HPV-independent tumours, it is recommended that the type of vaginal SCC (HPV-associated or HPV-independent) be documented on the pathology report. Nevertheless, a morphological diagnosis without differentiating the two categories is an acceptable alternative where the facilities to make this distinction are not available. Similarly, adenocarcinomas include the very rare HPV-associated neoplasms and various HPV-independent tumours that are also rare and include the newly described primary vaginal gastric-type adenocarcinoma {29664741}; broadly, the same categories of adenocarcinomas are included as for the cervix.

Various benign epithelial lesions (including the potentially premalignant adenosis) and mixed epithelial and mesenchymal tumours are included in this chapter, but pure mesenchymal tumours are now included in section Mesenchymal tumours of the lower genital tract, which classifies and discusses mesenchymal tumours of the lower genital tract as a whole. Similarly, melanocytic lesions are included in section Melanocytic lesions, and metastases in section Metastasis. section Genetic tumour syndromes of the female genital tract includes tumours of the vagina that are associated with hereditary tumour syndromes.
