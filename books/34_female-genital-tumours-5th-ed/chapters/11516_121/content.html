1.15.1.6 Stromal hyperplasia and hyperthecosis

Definition

Stromal hyperplasia is a non-neoplastic proliferation of ovarian stromal cells. Stromal hyperthecosis in addition shows increased luteinized cells.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

5A80.Y Other specified ovarian dysfunction

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovarian stroma

Clinical features

Stromal hyperplasia and hyperthecosis are typically incidental and almost always bilateral. Endocrine manifestations occur frequently in patients with stromal hyperthecosis but rarely in stromal hyperplasia {28432270; 3900841}.

Epidemiology

Stromal hyperplasia alone is uncommon. Stromal hyperthecosis occurs in one third of patients aged > 55 years and is associated with stromal hyperplasia.

Etiology

Rarely, stromal hyperthecosis can be familial or associated with HAIR-AN syndrome (hyperandrogenism, insulin resistance, and acanthosis nigricans) {3900841}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

There is nodular or diffuse enlargement of the cortex, medulla, or both.

Histopathology

There is a dense proliferation of uniform stromal spindle cells in both lesions. Hyperthecosis contains single cells, clusters, or small (< 1 cm) nodules of luteinized stromal cells. The stromal proliferation incorporates normal ovarian structures, unlike in sex cord–stromal tumours, which are more frequently unilateral and form discrete masses that displace normal ovarian structures {17717430}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: stromal proliferation incorporating normal ovarian structures; dense proliferation of uniform stromal spindle cells; single cells, clusters, or small nodules (< 1 cm) of luteinized stromal cells in hyperthecosis.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Stromal hyperplasia and hyperthecosis show benign behaviour.
