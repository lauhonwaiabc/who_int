3.2.1.1 Serous borderline tumour of the peritoneum

Definition

Serous borderline tumour (SBT) of the peritoneum is a non-invasive low-grade proliferative serous epithelial neoplasm primarily arising in the peritoneum without ovarian involvement.

ICD-O coding

8442/1 Serous borderline tumour NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F74 & XH3ZK9 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of peritoneum & Serous cystadenoma, borderline malignancy

Related terminology

Not recommended: atypical proliferative serous tumour; serous tumour of low malignant potential

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peritoneum/omentum

Clinical features

Patients present with symptoms attributable to an abdominal/pelvic mass. Disease may be localized or disseminated throughout the abdominal/pelvic cavity. Ascites and adhesions may be present.

Epidemiology

The mean patient age at diagnosis is 31–33 years (range: 16–67 years) {1599025; 2305929}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of peritoneal SBT in the absence of ovarian disease is presumably similar to that of ovarian SBT. However, papillary tubal hyperplasia of the fallopian tube {21997682} and peritoneal endosalpingiosis are potential precursors. In cases with both ovarian and peritoneal involvement by SBT, secondary peritoneal involvement from a primary ovarian SBT and independent primary tumours have been postulated; a large proportion of peritoneal SBTs have shown KRAS/BRAF mutations identical to those in the concurrent ovarian SBT {24307542}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours may be of variable size and line the serosa. Granularity (due to psammoma bodies) and adhesions may be present.

Histopathology

Peritoneal SBTs histologically resemble SBT that arises in the ovary. By definition, these tumours are not associated with ovarian SBT. It is important to exclude prior ovarian disease before making this diagnosis. The term “implants” should be restricted to cases with ovarian disease. In addition, it is necessary to distinguish peritoneal SBT from peritoneal low-grade serous carcinoma, particularly cases with prominent psammoma bodies (psammomatous carcinoma) {15897738; 27428735; 2305929; 28248817}.

Cytology

Peritoneal washings typically contain numerous papillary clusters of relatively uniform epithelial cells. Psammoma bodies may be present.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelial proliferation with a branching pattern, non-invasive architecture, and low-grade atypia; no ovarian SBT.

Staging

Peritoneal SBT is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Primary peritoneal SBTs have a more favourable outcome than low-grade serous carcinomas, but they carry a risk of recurrence and progression to low-grade serous carcinoma over long-term follow-up {1599025; 2305929}.
