9.1.3.3 Vaginal adenosis

Definition

Vaginal adenosis is the presence of benign glandular epithelium (unassociated with endometrioid-type stroma) involving the superficial lamina propria or vaginal epithelium.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA14.Y Other specified acquired abnormalities of vagina

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adenosis associated with diethylstilbestrol (DES) exposure is typically in the upper two thirds of the vagina, whereas sporadic forms occur throughout, although there may be overlap {1033667; 30544435}.

Clinical features

Adenosis occurs over a wide age range. Patients may present with vaginal discharge or pain, or they may be asymptomatic and the adenosis discovered as an incidental finding {30544435; 3699812; 1157359; 29664741}.

Epidemiology

Adenosis is uncommon. Historically, it was more frequent in patients exposed in utero to DES {4829509; 1117962; 22682699}.

Etiology

In utero DES exposure was a common cause of adenosis before 1970 {6537153; 4829509; 1117962; 5549830}. Adenosis in patients not exposed to DES is most commonly idiopathic but can be congenital, sometimes associated with genitourinary malformations {4829509; 1033667; 29664741; 22317878} or rarely secondary to topical fluorouracil or CO2 laser treatment {1893363; 20173507}.

Pathogenesis

Vaginal adenosis is speculated to result from a failure of squamous differentiation in Müllerian duct epithelium due to disruption of p63 (TRP63, transformation-related protein 63) {22682699}. Adenosis may also be a response to mucosal trauma {20173507}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, adenosis may exhibit a red granular or nodular appearance or may be occult {4829509; 30544435; 3699812}.

Histopathology

Histology shows glands located within the superficial stroma or replacing the squamous epithelium. The glands are tuboendometrioid or mucinous; the mucinous pattern may be endocervical or gastric {6537153; 4829509; 3699812; 29664741}. The tuboendometrioid pattern is characterized by non-mucinous cells, sometimes with cilia; endometrioid-type stroma must be absent. Gastric-type adenosis displays pale/eosinophilic cytoplasm, prominent cell membranes, and sometimes intestinal differentiation with goblet cells {29664741}. Atypical tuboendometrioid and gastric adenosis (adenosis with nuclear atypia) has been reported {6537153; 29664741; 22317878}.

Cytology

Cytology reveals mucinous or non-mucinous columnar cells on vaginal smear {7195652}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign glandular structures within the superficial vagina; no endometrioid-type stroma.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Adenosis can regress secondary to squamous metaplasia {7195652}. Tuboendometrioid and gastric adenosis, respectively, may progress via atypical adenosis to clear cell and gastric-type adenocarcinoma {6537153; 1157359; 29664741; 22317878}.
