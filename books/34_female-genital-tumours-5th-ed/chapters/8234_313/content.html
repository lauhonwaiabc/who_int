8.2.3.4 Carcinoma of the uterine cervix, unclassifiable

Definition

Carcinoma of the uterine cervix, unclassifiable, is a malignant epithelial tumour of the cervix that cannot be further subclassified; diagnosis requires the exclusion of potential histological mimics, including primary and metastatic tumours.

ICD-O coding

8020/3 Carcinoma, undifferentiated, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C77.Z Malignant neoplasms of cervix uteri, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: undifferentiated carcinoma; poorly differentiated carcinoma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cervix

Clinical features

The clinical features are similar to those of cervical carcinoma in general. In a SEER analysis, the mean patient age was 42 years (range: 32–58 years), and 70% of cases were American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) stage I {17718897}.

Epidemiology

These account for a very small percentage of cervical carcinomas.

Etiology

Data are limited, but most cases are HPV-associated and/or block-type p16-positive {25884453}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is usually HPV-associated.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is similar to that of cervical carcinoma generally.

Histopathology

The tumours demonstrate an epithelial phenotype but lack histological or immunohistochemical features of other specific subtypes of cervical carcinoma (e.g. squamous carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and neuroendocrine carcinoma [NEC]). In some cases this may be a consequence of limited or non-representative tumour sampling. Diffuse p63 expression should elicit consideration of a poorly differentiated squamous carcinoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primary malignant epithelial neoplasm lacking specific differentiating features; molecular analysis and/or p16 immunohistochemistry may help in determining HPV-associated etiology.

Staging

The staging is the same as for other cervical tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

In a SEER analysis, the 10-year cause-specific survival rate was 84.9%, not statistically different from that of cervical squamous carcinoma {17718897}.
