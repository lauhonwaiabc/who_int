1.12.2.5 Somatic neoplasms arising from teratomas

Definition

Somatic neoplasms arising from teratomas are benign or malignant tumours arising from any of the elements within a teratoma.

ICD-O coding

9084/3 Teratoma with malignant transformation

ICD-11 coding

2C73.3 & XH33E8 Malignant teratoma of ovary & Teratoma with malignant transformation

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovary

Clinical features

Tumours are typically unilateral and occur at an older age (average: 55 years), typically two decades later than uncomplicated teratomas {18971155; 17240432; 19038764; 13493921}. Large or malignant tumours may present with signs and symptoms related to a mass {17240432; 2712191}. Diameter > 10 cm and elevated CEA in patients aged ≥ 45 years are suggestive of malignancy {18971155; 17240432}; sarcomas occur more often in younger patients than carcinomas.

Epidemiology

Cutaneous neoplasms occur in about 2% of dermoid cysts, with squamous cell carcinoma being most common – accounting for about 80% of malignant tumours within teratomas. Melanomas, melanocytic naevi, basal cell carcinomas, and sebaceous neoplasms are much less common {13493921; 13752348; 16990706; 1774106; 8886884; 15166669; 11844060; 6893919; 4757597}. Adenocarcinoma is the second most common carcinoma, accounting for 7% of malignancies in teratomas {13493921}, and sarcoma accounts for 8% {13493921; 9421078}. Other benign and malignant tumours have also been reported, including low-grade malignant mucinous neoplasms, choroid plexus papilloma, Paget disease, glomus tumour, benign soft tissue tumours, and lymphomas {1774106; 20034987; 8503739; 1718175; 15028031; 10975942; 28700440; 28864175}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically large and solid or solid and cystic, with or without a recognizable dermoid cyst. They may protrude into the cyst wall or cause thickening of the wall {18971155; 17240432}. Extraovarian spread is common.

Histopathology

Squamous cell carcinoma shows a varied morphology, ranging from well differentiated and keratinizing to poorly differentiated to anaplastic (including sarcomatoid). Melanomas may show usual (epithelioid and spindle) or unusual appearances, including pseudopapillary architecture, follicle-like spaces, and a myxoid background {16990706}. Sebaceous lesions include sebaceous hyperplasia, adenoma, sebaceoma, and carcinoma {1774106}. Adenocarcinomas most commonly arise from gastrointestinal-type epithelium {7821857} and respiratory-type epithelium {23026196; 15656343; 13493921; 1653182}. Benign and low-grade mucinous epithelial neoplasms with mucin extravasation (more common within the ovary but also seen outside it) resembling appendiceal primaries may arise in teratomas and mimic metastases {17527072; 18344868; 12717249}. Finding a teratomatous component is critical. An associated appendiceal primary should be excluded, because rare collision tumours are reported {31409389}. Sarcomas include the subtypes described in soft tissues {22833084; 21623194; 19696615; 12759630}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

The essential and desirable diagnostic criteria are based on the histological type of the somatic neoplasm (see Histopathology, above).

Staging

Malignant tumours are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis is highly dependent on stage, and most data are derived from squamous cell carcinoma. Overall, patients with tumours limited to the ovary have a favourable outcome. The 5-year overall survival rate is 15–52% for all stages combined {2712191} and 75.7% for stage I tumours {18971155; 21430455; 17240432; 19038764}. The prognosis of advanced disease is worse than that of more common ovarian cancers {18971155; 17240432; 19038764; 17877681; 31092447}.
