16.0.0.7 Carney complex

Definition

Carney complex (CNC) is a multiple neoplasia syndrome characterized by pigmented lesions of the skin and mucosa, myxomatous tumours of the heart and skin, and multiple endocrine and non-endocrine neoplasms.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

160980 Carney complex, type 1; CNC1

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Genital lentigines are the most common gynaecological manifestation {26130139}. Ovarian lesions, myxoid uterine leiomyoma, atypical mesenchymal neoplasm of the cervix, vulvar blue naevus, and vulvar myxoma have been reported {11095480; 21934476; 23327071; 6578600; 9415461; 11737445}.

Clinical features

Genital lentigines, vulvar myxoma, follicular cysts, cystic teratomas, and serous cystadenomas are usually asymptomatic {11737445; 11095480; 26130139}. The cutaneous and mucosal lentigines/lesions are small. Reported symptoms and signs are variable and depend on the lesion {23327071; 21934476; 9415461; 11737445}. Leiomyomas with myxoid change can be encountered {11737445; 11095480; 23327071; 21934476; 9415461}. Full criteria for diagnosis have been developed {26130139}.

Epidemiology

CNC is rare, with an unknown prevalence {26130139}. Its gynaecological manifestations are usually found at a young age {11737445; 11095480; 21934476; 23327071; 26130139}.

Etiology

CNC is usually inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, but it can occur de novo. Most cases are due to a germline mutation in the PRKAR1A gene (located on chromosome 17q) {26130139}.

Pathogenesis

PRKAR1A defects lead to an increased cell proliferation in cAMP-responsive tissues and tumour formation {26130139}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is nonspecific.

Histopathology

All the reported lesions have shown histological features compatible with their histotypes {11737445; 11095480; 21934476; 23327071}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The identification of a pathogenic germline mutation of the PRKAR1A gene helps confirm the diagnosis, although such a mutation may be absent in 20–30% of cases {26130139; 20301463}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: either two major criteria or one major criterion and one supplemental one.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most of the gynaecological lesions associated with CNC are benign.
