3.3.2.1 Desmoid fibromatosis

Definition

Desmoid fibromatosis is a locally aggressive but non-metastasizing deep-seated (myo)fibroblastic neoplasm with infiltrative growth and a propensity for local recurrence.

ICD-O coding

8822/1 Abdominal fibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

2F74 & XH6116 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of peritoneum & Abdominal (mesenteric) fibromatosis

Related terminology

Not recommended: desmoid tumour; extra-abdominal fibromatosis

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours most commonly arise from the pelvic floor {10945361}.

Clinical features

As many as 50% of patients present with pain, whereas urinary symptoms are the chief presentation in a smaller subset of patients, and obstructed labour may occur in pregnant patients {10945361; 16343190; 22315870}. Nearly one third of cases are asymptomatic. The mean size is 11 cm (range: 3–30 cm) {10945361}.

Epidemiology

Desmoid fibromatosis is uncommon. Patients are usually women of reproductive age (mean age: 30 years; range: 17–62 years) {10945361}.

Etiology

The etiology is multifactorial, including hormonal factors (including pregnancy), physical factors (surgery) {10945361}, and genetic factors (sporadic somatic CTNNB1 mutations and germline APC mutations in Gardner syndrome).

Pathogenesis

Activating mutations in CTNNB1 (the gene that encodes β-catenin) and inactivating mutations in APC (a tumour suppressor gene) cause decreased proteosomal degradation and increased nuclear accumulation of β-catenin, which ultimately promotes cell proliferation and survival through upregulation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway {19099242; 19436199; 28575679; 17952864}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are solitary, firm, and relatively circumscribed grossly. The cut surface is whorled and white, with occasional myxoid/cystic change.

Histopathology

There is an infiltrative proliferation of bland fibroblasts/myofibroblasts with variable mitotic activity arranged in long sweeping fascicles. Lymphoid aggregates are often present at the advancing tumour edge. Extensive hyalinization or keloid-like collagen may be present {27124915}. The tumour cells are immunoreactive for actin, with occasional desmin staining. The vast majority of tumours show nuclear expression of β-catenin {17711447; 15832090}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Because most sporadic cases harbour point mutations in CTNNB1, mutation analysis may be useful when morphological features are not diagnostic and β-catenin immunohistochemistry is equivocal.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fibroblasts and myofibroblasts without atypia arranged in long sweeping fascicles; infiltrative growth.

Desirable: nuclear β-catenin expression.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Almost 40% of tumours recur after surgery, often within 2 years, but the natural course is unpredictable {10945361}. Currently, conservative management (specifically with close surveillance) has been recommended, with medical treatment (antihormone or targeted therapy, chemotherapy) in cases with progression {28961825; 25315103}. Surgery may be considered in resectable cases not associated with compromised function. Some studies have suggested that CTNNB1 (β-catenin) mutation may be predictive of tumour recurrence {21323417; 23913621; 20197769; 18832571}.
