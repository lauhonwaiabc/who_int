9.1.5.2 Mixed tumour of the vagina

Definition

Mixed tumour of the vagina is a benign tumour composed of an admixture of spindle and epithelial (glandular or squamous) elements.

ICD-O coding

8940/0 Mixed tumour NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F33.Y & XH2KC1 Benign neoplasm of other specified female genital organs & Pleomorphic adenoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: spindle cell epithelioma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Posterior and distal vaginal wall close to the hymen

Clinical features

Patient age has a wide range (mean: 40 years). Patients are asymptomatic or report nonspecific symptoms related to a mass.

Epidemiology

These are rare vaginal neoplasms.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Mixed tumours typically range in size from 1.5 to 5 cm and are solid, soft, rubbery, and mostly well circumscribed.

Histopathology

Mixed tumours are composed of spindled and epithelial elements in varying proportions and arrangements. A cellular spindle cell component, with fascicular, corded, nested, and/or reticular growths, typically predominates and is sharply demarcated from the epithelial elements, glandular (typically mucinous) and/or mature squamous epithelium {15154010; 7682380}. Eosinophilic hyaline material may be present within a myxomatous or collagenous stroma. The cells show minimal nuclear atypia, and mitotic activity varies (although it is usually low). There is often coexpression of a variety of epithelial and mesenchymal markers, including AE1/AE3, CK7, EMA, CD10, SMA, desmin, h-caldesmon, WT1, calretinin, BCL2, and CD34 {15154010; 25998318; 7682380}. The spindle cell elements typically strongly express epithelial markers.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign epithelial and spindle cell components.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign lesions but may recur if incompletely excised.
