3.5.4 Gliomatosis

Definition

Gliomatosis is characterized by the presence of mature glial tissue in the peritoneum.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: gliomatosis peritonei

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peritoneum, omentum, lymph nodes {24421855}

Clinical features

Patients usually have symptoms related to an ovarian teratoma. However, gliomatosis can cause abdominal/pelvic pain due to the formation of masses, or abnormalities may be seen on imaging studies when gliomatosis occurs as recurrent disease after ovarian immature teratomas have been treated and the patient has normal serum markers. In these circumstances, gliomatosis becomes part of the so-called growing teratoma syndrome {26564007}.

Epidemiology

Gliomatosis is rare. It occurs over a wide age range but is more common in young patients. It can be associated with immature teratomas, mature cystic teratomas, mixed germ cell tumours, and ventriculoperitoneal shunts {26564007}.

Etiology

Proposed theories include maturation of implanted ovarian teratomatous elements and metaplasia of pluripotent Müllerian stem cells or supraperitoneal mesenchymal cells {15188134; 11438453}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

It typically occurs as miliary lesions, measuring a few millimetres in size; however, larger lesions can be encountered.

Histopathology

There are discrete nodules of mature glial tissue that are occasionally associated with inflammation, haemorrhage, and fibrosis. Infrequently, gliomatosis is associated with endometriosis {24421855}. Immunohistochemistry shows the nodules to be positive for GFAP and SOX2 and variably positive for S100 {26564007}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mature glial cell aggregates; absence of other teratomatous elements.

Staging

See comment on the staging of gliomatosis peritonei in section Immature teratoma of the ovary.

Prognosis and prediction

Gliomatosis has a favourable outcome {25594144; 26033179}. However, morbidity related to the expansive and compressive nature of enlarged glial masses {28656118} and rare malignant transformation, usually to high-grade gliomas, have been documented {2048608; 9195578}.
