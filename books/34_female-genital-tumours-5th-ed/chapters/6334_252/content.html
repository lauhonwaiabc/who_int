6.3.3.4 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour (IMT) is a distinctive, mesenchymal neoplasm composed of myofibroblastic and fibroblastic spindle cells accompanied by an inflammatory infiltrate.

ICD-O coding

8825/1 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F76 & XH66Z0 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of female genital organs & Myofibroblastic tumour NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflammatory pseudotumour

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma

Localization

IMT has been described in multiple anatomical sites, including the peritoneum and viscera. In the female genital tract, IMT usually arises in the corpus {16160478; 28664932; 25321329; 28490045} and much less commonly in the cervix {25321329; 28664932}.

Clinical features

Large tumours may cause abnormal uterine bleeding {16160478; 25321329; 28664932; 28490045}, but these may also be an incidental finding at the time of hysterectomy or caesarean section {26262452; 28490045; 28731868; 29505427}. Tumours in the peritoneum may cause symptoms related to an abdominopelvic mass or are discovered as an incidental finding.

Epidemiology

Uterine IMTs are rare (< 100 cases reported) {28787279}. They typically occur in adult, premenopausal women (median age: 38 years; range: 6–78 years) {25321329; 28664932}. Some cases are associated with pregnancy and involve the placenta {28731868; 26262452; 29505427; 31917155; 32271187}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most (~95%) IMTs tested for ALK immunohistochemistry are positive for this marker, typically correlating with alterations of the ALK gene {22646268}. ALK rearrangements (2p23.2-p23.1) are detected by FISH in about 75% of cases. Common fusion partners include IGFBP5, THBS1, and TIMP3. Tumours negative by FISH often have complex genetic rearrangements, such as intrachromosomal inversions, which can be detected by RNA sequencing {28490045; 29794871; 28664932; 30741845; 28731868}. ALK-negative uterine IMTs are rare and probably underrecognized, with only one case each harbouring an ETV6-NTRK3 or RET fusion {29900760; 31917155}. ROS1 and PDGFRB fusions have not yet been identified in uterine tumours. Peritoneal IMTs also frequently harbour genetic fusion involving receptor tyrosine kinases {27874193}; ALK is most commonly involved, with variable 5′ fusion partners {22646268; 28490045; 28664932; 25321329}, followed by rare NTRK3 fusion {29900760}. RANBP2-ALK and RRBP1-ALK fusions have been reported in an aggressive form of IMT with epithelioid morphology (see description below) {21164297; 27874193}.

Macroscopic appearance

Uterine IMTs have a mean size of 7.5 cm (range: 1–20 cm) {16160478; 25321329; 28664932; 29794871}. Most tumours are intramural, but polypoid intracavitary growth can occur {28664932}. They display a gelatinous and soft or firm and whorled (simulating a smooth muscle tumour) cut surface, with frequent irregular margins {16160478; 25321329; 29310405}. Haemorrhage and necrosis are observed in about one third of tumours {16160478; 25321329; 28664932}.

Histopathology

At low power, these tumours may be well circumscribed or may show infiltrative margins. Three main histological patterns can be seen, which may be admixed {28664932; 28490045; 25321329}: (1) a loose myxoid pattern, characterized by hypocellular areas featuring individually dispersed cells in an abundant myxoid matrix, imparting a fasciitis-like or tissue culture–like appearance (potentially mimicking a myxoid smooth muscle tumour); (2) a fascicular/compact pattern, characterized by cellular areas arranged in intersecting fascicles or less commonly storiform architecture (mimicking a conventional smooth muscle tumour and less commonly an endometrial stromal tumour) {30015720; 29794871}; and (3) a hyalinized pattern, associated with dense collagenous matrix with inconspicuous tumour cells {16160478; 25321329; 28664932}. Tumour cells are spindled, containing wispy eosinophilic cytoplasm and elongated nuclei with evenly dispersed to open chromatin. Ganglion-like cells, epithelioid cells, and Touton giant cells may be present. Nuclear atypia is usually minimal, but severe atypia may occur. Mitotic figures can be present. The inflammatory component is usually lymphoplasmacytic but may be mixed, and it varies in intensity and distribution within each tumour. Stromal decidualization can be observed during pregnancy {28664932}.

ALK-positive IMT with epithelioid morphology has been described using the term “epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma” {21164297}. This subtype is typically intra-abdominal, mostly arising from mesentery or omentum. It has also been described in the ovary, but not elsewhere in the female genital tract {28787324}. It is composed of sheets of round epithelioid cells with vesicular nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm, often with abundant myxoid stroma and inflammation, as well as a minor spindle cell component.

Immunohistochemistry

ALK granular cytoplasmic positivity is highly sensitive and specific for the diagnosis of uterine IMT; however, the degree of positivity varies, with some tumours showing only focal staining {28664932; 28490045; 30015720; 28731868; 29794871}. IMTs frequently express SMA, desmin, and caldesmon (and they are therefore often misdiagnosed as smooth muscle tumours if only these markers are considered). They also frequently express CD10, but they are negative for keratin, S100, CD34, and KIT (CD117) {25321329; 28664932}. The tumours are typically p53-wildtype {28130839}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Immunohistochemistry for ALK is usually sufficient for diagnosis, although demonstration of fusion genes may be useful in selected cases {30741845}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland spindle cells with myxoid and fascicular growth; lymphoplasmacytic inflammation; ALK immunohistochemical expression (with rare exceptions).

Desirable: ALK rearrangement.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most uterine IMTs are benign and confined to the uterus; however, a minority present with extrauterine spread and/or recur {25321329; 28664932; 29794871; 24371717}. There is insufficient evidence to establish prognostic variables for uterine-confined tumours. Necrosis, tumour size > 7 cm, moderate to severe atypia, high mitotic activity, and lymphovascular invasion have been associated with aggressive course {25321329; 28664932}. IMTs that harbour ALK rearrangement may potentially respond to targeted therapy using tyrosine kinase inhibitors {28787259; 29352732}. Peritoneal epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcomas are highly aggressive, with rapid local recurrence and tumour-related mortality in a significant number of patients {28787324; 21164297}.
