3.4.0.8 Other tumour-like lesions of the peritoneum

Definition

Squamous or cartilaginous metaplasia is a mesothelial transformation into squamous epithelium or mature hyaline cartilage. Endocervicosis is an uneven arrangement of endocervical-type glands and cysts in ectopic sites. Keratin granulomas represent a reactive process to laminated keratin.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peritoneum

Clinical features

These are all incidental findings.

Epidemiology

The epidemiology depends on the etiology.

Etiology

Squamous metaplasia and cartilaginous metaplasia are due to irritation of the peritoneum in patients with peritoneal dialysis or pelvic disease {18753977; 12118117}. Keratin granulomas are caused by keratin debris / non-viable squamous cells displaced from endometrial/ovarian endometrioid adenocarcinoma with squamous differentiation, atypical polypoid adenomyoma, cervical squamous cell carcinoma, or ruptured ovarian dermoid cyst {1698340}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Keratin granulomas and squamous metaplasia are usually 2–3 mm nodules {1698340; 15186277}. Cartilaginous metaplasia may form nodules as large as 2.5 cm {12118117; 11109160}.

Histopathology

Keratin granulomas show laminated keratin or ghost squamous cells surrounded by foreign body–type giant cells, histiocytes, and inflammatory cells. Squamous metaplasia may have a diffuse or micronodular pattern; the former is associated with band-like subsurface fibrosis in peritoneal dialysis patients and the latter with inflammatory conditions {18753977}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ectopic location of the above types of metaplastic tissue with benign features.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These are all benign lesions.
