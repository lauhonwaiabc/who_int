9.1.3.5 Cysts of the vagina

Definition

Vaginal cysts are subepithelial cysts lined by squamous, Müllerian, mesonephric, or transitional epithelium.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA14.Y Other specified acquired abnormalities of vagina

Related terminology

Acceptable: Müllerian cyst; mesonephric cyst; Gartner duct cyst; inclusion cyst; urothelial cyst

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Müllerian cysts are most common in the anterior or lateral wall; mesonephric cysts are typically located in the lateral wall.

Clinical features

Vaginal cysts are most commonly diagnosed in adults (average patient age: 38 years; range: 19–75 years) {3957551; 18390079}. Some patients are asymptomatic and the cyst is an incidental finding, but larger cysts may present with a lump, pain, or urinary symptoms.

Epidemiology

Inclusion cysts {1143723} and Müllerian cysts {3957551} are the most common.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Mesonephric (Gartner duct) cysts result from dilatation of mesonephric duct remnants. Some squamous-lined inclusion cysts are seen at sites of prior trauma and are secondary to epithelial entrapment. Urothelial-lined cysts may derive from periurethral glands.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, there is a simple thin-walled cyst, typically containing mucinous or serous fluid.

Histopathology

Müllerian cysts are lined by mucinous, tubal (ciliated), or endometrioid-type epithelium. Mesonephric cysts are lined by non-mucinous, non-ciliated cuboidal to flattened epithelium. Squamous inclusion cysts are lined by stratified squamous epithelium. Urothelial cysts are lined at least in part by transitional epithelium {1143723; 3957551; 12913681; 2212683; 18390079}. Immunohistochemically, Müllerian cysts are typically positive for PAX8, ER, and PR; mesonephric cysts are negative for hormone receptors and positive for GATA3.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign cysts lined by a variety of epithelial types.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision is curative.
