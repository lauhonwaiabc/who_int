5.0.1 Tumours of the broad ligament and other uterine ligaments: Introduction

Tumours of the broad ligament are uncommon. Most are mesenchymal or mixed epithelial and mesenchymal tumours that resemble their counterparts in the uterine corpus. Leiomyoma and adenomyoma are the most common benign entities, and leiomyosarcoma is the most common sarcoma. Although papillary cystadenoma of the broad ligament is rare, recognition of this entity is clinically significant because papillary cystadenoma is strongly associated with von Hippel–Lindau syndrome and may be the sentinel tumour for some patients. Wolffian tumour (also known as female adnexal tumour of probable Wolffian origin) may present a diagnostic challenge, because it can morphologically mimic a wide variety of much more common ovarian epithelial and sex cord–stromal neoplasms. Although a mesonephric (Wolffian) origin has long been presumed, these tumours remain an enigma, with recent studies demonstrating that they neither share the molecular signature of mesonephric carcinomas arising elsewhere in the female genital tract nor show significant immunoexpression of GATA3, a robust marker of mesonephric differentiation.
