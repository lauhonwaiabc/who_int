3.3.2.3 Calcifying fibrous tumour

Definition

Calcifying fibrous tumour (CFT) is a benign lesion composed of abundant hyalinized collagen, lymphoplasmacytic infiltration, and calcifications.

ICD-O coding

8817/0 Calcifying fibrous tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F74 & XH7TH6 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of peritoneum & Calcifying fibrous tumour

Related terminology

Not recommended: calcifying fibrous pseudotumour

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CFT usually occurs in the gastrointestinal tract, peritoneum, pleura, and mesentery {27196478; 12232572}, and it has been rarely reported in the ampulla of fallopian tube {24300534}.

Clinical features

Tumours are typically identified incidentally but may cause symptoms related to an abdominal mass {28153506}. Patients with multiple tumours have been reported {27196478}.

Epidemiology

CFT seems to have a female predilection (M:F ratio: 0.79:1), most commonly in infants, young children, and young adults. The peritoneum is the fifth most common site of involvement, after the stomach, small intestine, pleura, and mesentery {27196478}.

Etiology

CFT may be related to trauma.

Pathogenesis

When CFT occurs at an early age, genetic predisposition has been suggested {27196478; 12817428}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most lesions are solitary, but they can be multiple. CFT appears as a well-defined, solid, and firm mass, with a mean size of 4.6 cm and a wide size range {27196478}. The cut surface is tan-white and gritty.

Histopathology

The tumour is characterized by abundant, hypocellular, hyalinized collagen, with scattered calcifications that may be psammomatous, and a chronic lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate. It also contains scattered spindle cells with bland nuclei, fine chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli. Tumours are usually diffusely positive for factor XIIIa and CD34.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: abundant dense well-circumscribed hyalinized collagen with lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, spindle cells, and psammomatous or dystrophic calcification.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

CFT is benign and curable by local surgical resection, but a 10% recurrence rate has been reported {27196478}.
