16.0.0.11 Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma

Definition

Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma (HLRCC) is an autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndrome with early-onset development of cutaneous and uterine leiomyomas, as well as renal cell carcinoma (RCC; most frequently type 2 papillary RCC), associated with germline FH (fumarase) gene mutations.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

150800 Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer; HLRCC

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Not recommended: hereditary multiple cutaneous leiomyomas; multiple cutaneous and uterine leiomyomas; Reed syndrome {11549574}

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Skin, uterus, and kidney (see #13464Table #13464)

Clinical features

Tumours usually present with an earlier onset (see #13464Table #13464).

Epidemiology

HLRCC is rare, with uncertain prevalence; > 300 families have been reported to date {20301430}, and 0.24% of unselected women with uterine leiomyoma are affected {30741757}.

Etiology

HLRCC is caused by autosomal dominant inherited germline mutation of the fumarate hydratase gene (FH; chromosome 1q42.3-q43) {11283798; 21398687; 23211287}. More than 80 different FH pathogenic variants are known {15937070; 21398687}. There is no genotype–phenotype correlation {15937070}, but there is high penetrance {15724016}. Fumarate hydratase deficiency in uterine leiomyoma can also occur due to sporadic genetic alterations in the FH gene, unrelated to HLRCC {28664937; 26574848}.

Pathogenesis

The Krebs cycle enzyme fumarate hydratase converts fumarate to L-malate. Decreased fumarate hydratase activity results in fumarate accumulation, leading to tumorigenesis due to stabilization of HIFα {21398687}.

Macroscopic appearance

See #13464Table #13464.

Histopathology

Uterine leiomyomas show a staghorn vascular pattern, alveolar pattern oedema, scattered bizarre nuclei, ovoid nuclei arranged in chains, eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions, and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli surrounded by perinucleolar haloes (see #13464Table #13464) {27454940; 26574848; 24309325}.

Immunohistochemistry

These tumours usually show loss of immunostaining for fumarate hydratase, although some cases may display retained expression due to FH missense mutation {31162287}. Positive immunostaining for 2SC may be more sensitive for fumarate hydratase deficiency but is currently not widely available {24441663}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The identification of FH germline mutation or deletion is necessary for definitive diagnosis {15937070}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

HLRCC can be suspected or confirmed if the criteria in #10888Box #10888 are met.

Staging

RCC should be staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

RCCs arising in this syndrome are aggressive {17895761; 12772087}. Although the smooth muscle tumours may show atypical cytological features, there are only rare reports of biologically malignant behaviour {27454940; 26574848; 16477632}.

Clinically, the diagnosis should be suspected if the major criterion or ≥ 2 minor criteria are fulfilled {20618355}:

 

Major criterion

·         Multiple, histologically confirmed cutaneous piloleiomyomas

Minor criteria

·         Severely symptomatic uterine leiomyoma before the age of 40 years (surgical therapy needed)

·         Renal cell carcinoma before the age of 40 years

·         A first-degree family member fulfilling one of the criteria above

The diagnosis is confirmed if a pathogenic variant of FH is detected or if both of the following criteria are fulfilled {20301430}:

 

·         ≥ 2 cutaneous leiomyomas (with ≥ 1 histologically confirmed) without a family history of HLRCC or
≥ 1 cutaneous leiomyoma with a family history of HLRCC

·         ≥ 1 renal cell carcinoma with compatible morphology and immunohistochemistry (with or without a family history of HLRCC)

Clinical features

Mean age of onset is 25 years; can be solitary or multiple; predominantly involves trunk or extremities; number can increase with time {15937070; 12772087}

Occurs in almost 100% of women with HLRCC, at a mean age of 30 years (range: 18–53 years); multiple, large, bulky, and highly symptomatic, often resulting in early surgical intervention {15937070; 12772087}

Affects 14% of HLRCC patients, typically presenting in the fourth or fifth decade of life as a unilateral solitary renal tumour {17895761; 12772087; 15937070; 11248088}; often asymptomatic or less frequently presents with back pain, palpable mass, or haematuria {20301430}

 

Macroscopic appearance

Skin-coloured or light-brown nodules {15937070}, 0.4–2.5 cm in size {12772087}

Multiple and large (up to 10 cm) {12772087}; the cut surface is firm, tan to white, and whorled (similar to sporadic leiomyomas)

Variable cystic and solid parts; measures 2.3–22 cm {17895761; 11248088}

 

Histopathology

Nodular smooth muscle proliferation growing in interlacing bundles with centrally located blunt-edged nuclei, similar to sporadic tumours {11248088}

Conventional, cellular, or with bizarre nuclei; often displays characteristic features such as staghorn vasculature, alveolar oedema, ovoid nuclei arranged in chains, eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions, and nuclei with prominent eosinophilic nucleoli surrounded by haloes; some cases can also display Schwannian-like features {29332133; 28664937}

Can show a variety of growth patterns (papillary, tubular, tubulopapillary, tubulocystic, collecting duct–like, eosinophilic SDHB-like RCC, and mixed growth {11248088; 17895761; 15937070; 12772087; 28165631}), but the characteristic finding is prominent inclusion-like eosinophilic nucleoli surrounded by a perinucleolar halo {11248088; 17895761; 15937070; 12772087; 28165631}

Rarely, unilateral or bilateral nodular hyperplasia of adrenal gland can occur {22982371}
