13.1.4.1 Leiomyoma of the lower genital tract

Definition

Leiomyoma is a benign smooth muscle tumour.

ICD-O coding

8890/0 Leiomyoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E86.1 & XH4CY6 Leiomyoma of other or unspecified sites & Leiomyoma NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid leiomyoma; myxoid leiomyoma

Localization

They most frequently arise in the vulva {28786880}.

Clinical features

Lower genital tract leiomyomas are usually < 5 cm. They may present as a slow-growing, painless mass; many are clinically suspected to be a cyst.

Epidemiology

Leiomyomas are the most common mesenchymal tumour of the lower genital tract. Most patients are in the fourth to sixth decade of life {418977; 450336; 8669526; 28786880}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Patients with Alport syndrome and germline deletion of COL4A5 and COL4A6 can develop diffuse leiomyomatosis of the oesophagus, tracheobronchial tree, vulva, and uterus {8356449; 2026365}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Leiomyomas of the lower genital tract are typically well circumscribed, nodular, and rubbery, with a white to tan bulging cut surface, analogous to their uterine counterparts. Cystic change, softening from oedema, and (in large tumours) focal to patchy haemorrhage can be present {8669526; 28786880}.

Histopathology

Leiomyomas are composed of intersecting fascicles of cytologically bland spindle cells with ample eosinophilic cytoplasm and elongated tapered to blunt-ended nuclei. Most have minimal cytological atypia and a mitotic count of < 2.3 mitoses/mm2 (equating to < 5 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.53 mm in diameter and 0.22 mm2 in area). Ischaemic or hyaline necrosis may occur, but tumour cell necrosis is absent. Lower genital tract tumours tend to have hyalinized or myxoedematous stroma focally or diffusely {418977; 450336; 8669526; 28786880}.

Subtypes such as myxoid leiomyoma, epithelioid leiomyoma, leiomyoma with bizarre nuclei, and mitotically active leiomyoma are rare {10782419; 12701642; 10203920}. The classification criteria for subtypes are probably similar to those for the uterus, but data are limited. Differing criteria for risk stratification have been proposed {418977; 450336; 8669526; 28786880}. A recent study comparing the predictive accuracy of proposed criteria showed that uterine smooth muscle tumour criteria outperformed others, and the authors endorsed these criteria {28786880}. Malignant potential in atypical smooth muscle neoplasms of the lower genital tract can thus be defined per uterine criteria.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: conventional leiomyoma: intersecting fascicles of spindle cells exhibiting mild to moderate cytological atypia, a mitotic count of < 4.6 mitoses/mm2 (equating to < 10 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.53 mm in diameter and 0.22 mm2 in area), and an absence of tumour cell necrosis.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Leiomyomas are benign and complete resection is curative. Local recurrence is possible if leiomyoma is incompletely excised, but recent data indicate that the risk is low {8669526; 28786880}.
