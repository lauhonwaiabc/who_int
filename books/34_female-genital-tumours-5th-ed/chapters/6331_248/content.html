6.3.3.1 Uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumour

Definition

Uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumour is a uterine neoplasm with morphological patterns that resemble those seen in ovarian sex cord tumours, without a component of recognizable endometrial stroma.

ICD-O coding

8590/1 Uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F76 & XH8CW8 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of female genital organs & Uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterine corpus or rarely cervix

Clinical features

These tumours typically occur in middle-aged women (mean age: 50 years), who usually present with abnormal vaginal bleeding or pelvic pain, but uterine tumour resembling ovarian sex cord tumour can also be an incidental finding {28656712; 961630; 19542872; 25150955}.

Epidemiology

These are rare neoplasms (< 1% of uterine mesenchymal tumours).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These tumours have been hypothesized to arise from pluripotent mesenchymal, endometrial stromal, or displaced ovarian sex cord cells {20070149}, but the pathogenesis is uncertain. Rearrangements involving either ESR1 or GREB1 have been reported, with a variety of partners, including NCOA1, NCOA2, NCOA3, CTNNB1, NR4A3, and SS18 {30350331; 30273195; 31094921; 31464709}. The tumours do not harbour the molecular alterations seen in low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma (JAZF1-SUZ12), adult granulosa cell tumour (FOXL2 alterations), or Sertoli–Leydig cell tumour (DICER1 alterations) {19542872; 26598979; 25581731}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically intramural and/or submucosal, forming a polypoid mass with an average size of 6 cm. They commonly display well-circumscribed margins and have a predominantly solid, variably soft to firm, and yellow or tan cut surface.

Histopathology

These tumours are typically well circumscribed, but some have an irregular edge with entrapment of surrounding smooth muscle. They grow in sheets, cords, nests, insulae, trabeculae, or tubules, sometimes with a retiform appearance {19309399}. Cells have scant to abundant cytoplasm and may be pale (including foamy) or eosinophilic, sometimes resulting in a rhabdoid appearance. Nuclei are ovoid and show minimal cytological atypia, and mitotic activity is low, with the exception of malignant tumours, which may show prominent cytological atypia and brisk mitotic activity. Vascular invasion may be seen occasionally. Tumours have a polyphenotypic immunohistochemical profile, with variable positivity for sex cord markers (inhibin, calretinin, WT1, CD56, CD99, SF1, FOXL2, and melan-A), epithelial markers, ER, PR, smooth muscle markers (actin, desmin, and h-caldesmon), and CD10 {17182656; 21084963; 26598979; 16118629; 25581731; 27311867}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sex cord patterns without a component of endometrial stromal tumour.

Desirable: immunoreactivity for sex cord–stromal markers.

Staging

This entity is staged as uterine sarcoma using the FIGO, Union for International Cancer Control (UICC), and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Uterine tumours resembling ovarian sex cord tumours are benign in most cases but should be considered to have a low malignant potential because they may recur. However, no definitive prognostic morphological features have been identified {28656712; 25150955}.
