9.1.4.2 Endometrioid carcinoma of the vagina

Definition

Endometrioid carcinoma of the vagina is a primary vaginal adenocarcinoma with histological features identical to those of endometrioid carcinoma of the endometrium.

ICD-O coding

8380/3 Endometrioid adenocarcinoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C71.0 & XH0SD2 Adenocarcinoma of vagina & Endometrioid adenocarcinoma NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

They most commonly occur in the upper vagina.

Clinical features

Endometrioid carcinomas of the vagina predominantly occur in postmenopausal women, with a median patient age of 57 years in the largest series {17895749}. Patients present with abnormal vaginal bleeding, or an asymptomatic mass is identified on examination. These tumours frequently occur in women who have had a prior hysterectomy for benign disease.

Epidemiology

No epidemiological data are available.

Etiology

Most endometrioid carcinomas of the vagina arise in association with endometriosis or in women with a history of endometriosis.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is most commonly polypoid or papillary but may be flat, and tumours as large as 7 cm have been reported. The cut surface is solid or solid and cystic.

Histopathology

The microscopic features are the same as those of endometrioid carcinomas elsewhere in the gynaecological tract, and many of the same variations in morphology seen at other sites have also been documented in the vagina, including squamous differentiation, mucinous differentiation, secretory change, small non-villous papillae, and the minimal deviation pattern {17895749}. Adjacent endometriosis may be seen.

Cytology

Endometrioid adenocarcinoma is notable for its crowded clusters of cells with markedly increased N:C ratios and hyperchromatic, irregular nuclei that are smaller than those seen in endocervical adenocarcinoma. Large cytoplasmic vacuoles stuffed with neutrophils are also characteristic. However, none of the features are entirely specific.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

The essential and desirable diagnostic criteria are similar to those in endometrium (see section Endometrioid carcinoma of the uterine corpus). The presence of endometriosis is supportive of the diagnosis.

Staging

The staging is the same as for other vaginal carcinomas.

Prognosis and prediction

Data are limited, but the prognosis is favourable overall. Outcome is related to stage, but recurrences of low-stage tumours have occurred.
