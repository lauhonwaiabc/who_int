8.2.1.11 Tuboendometrioid metaplasia

Definition

In tuboendometrioid metaplasia, there is a change from endocervical epithelium to tubal (ciliated) and/or endometrioid-type epithelium.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA15.Y Other specified acquired abnormalities of cervix uteri

Related terminology

Acceptable: tubal metaplasia

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cervix (usually endocervix)

Clinical features

Tuboendometrioid metaplasia is generally an incidental finding but may rarely present with abnormal cervical cytology {9018644; 26715169}.

Epidemiology

Tuboendometrioid metaplasia is most commonly found in women of reproductive age.

Etiology

Tuboendometrioid metaplasia is thought to be associated with previous cervical instrumentation, most commonly in the form of loop or cone excision. One study has suggested an association with diethylstilbestrol (DES) exposure {16175088}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tuboendometrioid metaplasia is generally an incidental finding, but rare cases may present with a macroscopic lesion {7741110}.

Histopathology

These lesions are composed of architecturally normal endocervical glands containing ciliated cells, pseudostratified non-ciliated cells, and intercalated or peg cells similar to the epithelium of the fallopian tube. Subtle stromal condensation around the glands is typical. Cytological atypia should be absent, and mitoses are rare {7741110}. ER is invariably positive and p16 shows patchy staining, but never a diffuse pattern of positivity such as that seen in adenocarcinoma in situ.

Cytology

Tubal metaplasia occurs as strips of glandular cells with round to oval nuclei, smooth nuclear membranes, and even chromatin. The cells lack true nuclear stratification and crowding, but the impression of nuclear palisading and pseudostratification can lead to a misdiagnosis of adenocarcinoma in situ. Although not always apparent, cilia and terminal bars are characteristic of tubal metaplasia {1546503; 17173299}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: architecturally normal endocervical glands containing ciliated cells, pseudostratified non-ciliated cells, and intercalated or peg cells similar to the epithelium of the fallopian tube.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Tuboendometrioid metaplasia is benign.
