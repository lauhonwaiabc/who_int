6.1.2.3 Clear cell carcinoma of the uterine corpus

Definition

Clear cell carcinoma is a carcinoma demonstrating papillary, tubulocystic, and/or solid architectural patterns and variably pleomorphic polygonal, cuboidal, flat, or hobnail cells with clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm.

ICD-O coding

8310/3 Clear cell adenocarcinoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C76.2 Endometrial clear cell adenocarcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterus

Clinical features

The mean patient age is in the mid to late seventh decade of life {23359866; 28945214}. Postmenopausal bleeding is the most frequent presenting symptom, but cervical cytology screening may be abnormal, especially in advanced-stage disease {27173475}. About 50–60% of the tumours are early-stage at presentation {16495918; 28945214; 26854651}. Patients may have an increased risk of venous thromboembolic events {21464728; 19370896}.

Epidemiology

This is a rare tumour, accounting for < 10% of all endometrial carcinomas {16495918; 26854651}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There is no known histotype-specific molecular profile; tumour subsets demonstrate molecular heterogeneity and can overlap with serous carcinoma, endometrioid carcinoma, neither, or both {25308272; 28485815; 28718916; 31269413}. Recurrent somatic mutations include mutations in TP53 (36–59%), PPP2R1A (16–37%), PIK3CA (11–36%), PIK3R1 (16–21%), KRAS (11–14%), ARID1A (15.9–27%), and SPOP (14–18%) {28718916; 28485815; 25308272; 31269413; 30585826; 14976538}. Reported findings on PTEN mutation (0–14%) and exonuclease domain of POLE mutation (0–7%) are conflicting {28718916; 25308272; 30585826; 14976538}. A reported 11–14% of cases show a microsatellite instability–high profile {14976538; 28485815}, but the reported frequency of DNA mismatch repair protein deficiency has varied significantly (0–33%) {30585826; 28718916; 25308272; 31269413}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most cases produce a friable mass without features distinct from those of other endometrial carcinomas {28945214}.

Histopathology

Strict adherence to diagnostic criteria, architectural and cytological, is required to distinguish clear cell carcinoma from potential histological mimics and to maximize diagnostic reproducibility {22790851; 25851704}. The major architectural patterns (tubulocystic, papillary, and solid) are frequently admixed {943228; 23359866; 30550483}. Papillae are typically short and rounded and often have hyalinized stroma. Tubulocystic areas display variably confluent glands and cysts. Constituent tumour cells show cuboidal, polygonal, hobnail, or flat appearances, with clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm. However, neither cytoplasmic clearing nor hobnail cells are required for the diagnosis. Tall columnar cells, abundant stratification, intraglandular tufting, and detached budding are either absent or only present focally; squamous differentiation is absent {30550483; 23359866}. Nuclear pleomorphism is variable, but at least focal moderate to severe atypia is typically present {30550483; 23359866}. There may be striking intratumoural and intertumoural variability in mitotic activity, but most tumours display < 5 mitoses/2 mm2 (equating to < 6 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area) {943228; 23359866; 30550483}. Immunohistochemically, tumours are positive for HNF1β, napsin A, and AMACR (P504S) in 67–100%, 56–93%, and 75–88% of cases, respectively, usually in the majority of cells {25308272; 28796747; 24145649; 17442376; 22495362; 24103020; 24119561; 31269413; 23807777}. ER and PR are usually negative or only focally positive {24103020; 23807777}; 22–72% of cases display mutation-pattern p53 staining {24103020; 9635673; 23524907; 27167671; 23807777}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an admixture of tubulocystic, papillary, and/or solid patterns; clear to eosinophilic cuboidal, polygonal, hobnail, or flat cells.

Desirable: confirmation by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

This entity is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of tumours of the uterus – endometrium [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]) and the FIGO staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

The overall 5-year survival rate is 55–78% {23359866; 28945214; 31500893}. Most relapses occur outside the pelvis {8859187; 27668398}. Advanced patient age and tumour stage are accepted poor prognostic factors {28945214; 8859187; 23359866; 16495918}. Other possible prognostic factors, with preliminary or conflicting data, include The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) subgroupings (in particular the presence of POLE mutations) {28718916}; high expression of L1CAM {28941294}, IMP3 {23465280}, and cyclin E {26050345}; loss of expression of ARID1A (BAF250a) {29451900; 23524907}; aberrant p53 phenotype {23524907; 29451900}; positive peritoneal cytology in otherwise early-stage disease {28541637}; race {29129389; 24552897}; adjuvant therapeutic modalities {29129389; 25019570; 27668398}; tumour size {29129389}; tumour architectural patterns {8859187}; and lymphovascular invasion {28945214; 2013441; 26854651}.
