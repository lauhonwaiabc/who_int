3.4.0.6 Ectopic decidua

Definition

Ectopic decidua is the extrauterine location of decidual tissue.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

JA01.Z Ectopic pregnancy, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: deciduosis; decidualized endometriosis

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

By definition, this occurs outside the uterine body {26926901}.

Clinical features

Ectopic decidua is usually an asymptomatic, incidental finding {28351025; 12068174}.

Epidemiology

Ectopic decidua usually occurs in women of reproductive age. Microscopically, it occurs in all women during pregnancy; macroscopically, it can be seen in as many as 10% of pregnant women {26717495}.

Etiology

It occurs during pregnancy or progestin use.

Pathogenesis

It probably originates from decidualization in endometriosis. In addition, it could arise in decidual metaplasia of subserosal mesenchymal cells or lymphatic dissemination of endometrial cells {26926901}.

Macroscopic appearance

Ectopic decidua presents as single or multiple small nodules of variable colour, 0.2–2 cm in size {26717495}. Rarely, it can be larger {26926901}.

Histopathology

There is solid growth of polygonal / focally spindled cells with well-delineated borders; eosinophilic, basophilic, or amphophilic cytoplasm; and nuclei with fine chromatin and prominent nucleoli. Necrosis, low mitotic activity, signet-ring cells, focal nuclear pleomorphism, and hyperchromasia can be seen. Immunohistochemical studies show that the cells are positive for ER and PR and typically negative for keratin and calretinin {26717495; 26926901}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nodules of decidualized cells outside the uterine body.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

It is benign and usually regresses postpartum {26926901}.
