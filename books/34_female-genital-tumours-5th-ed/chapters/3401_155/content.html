3.4.0.1 Mesothelial hyperplasia

Definition

Mesothelial hyperplasia is a non-neoplastic mesothelial proliferation.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Peritoneum {26696597}

Clinical features

This is usually silent clinically and seen at surgery or on biopsy for other conditions.

Epidemiology

Mesothelial hyperplasia is a common incidental finding.

Etiology

Mesothelial hyperplasia is a reactive condition that occurs in response to pelvic inflammation, peritoneal effusion, endometriosis, and tumours {8418078; 21421699; 24901399}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

It can show small nodules or coat the peritoneum {24901399}.

Histopathology

The hyperplastic mesothelial cells grow as small aggregates/sheets, single cells, cords, and tubulopapillary structures admixed with inflammation. An infiltrative appearance, lymphovascular invasion, enlarged nuclei, multinucleation, prominent nucleoli, scattered mitoses, and deciduoid features may be seen {21421699; 23855881; 24901399}. Cells are typically positive for desmin and negative for EMA, GLUT1, and IMP3, whereas mesothelioma shows the opposite staining pattern {26288396}. Unlike well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumour, mesothelial hyperplasia lacks expression of L1CAM {30171198}. Unlike mesothelioma, mesothelial hyperplasia shows no loss of BAP1 by immunohistochemistry {26288396}.

Cytology

In cytology samples, the mesothelial cells show mild to moderate nuclear pleomorphism, multinucleation, mitotic figures, cytoplasmic vacuoles, and psammoma bodies {23858317}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Mesothelial hyperplasia lacks BAP1 mutations and homozygous deletion of CDKN2A (p16) by FISH {26288396}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign cytological features and lack of invasive growth.

Desirable: BAP1 immunohistochemistry and CDKN2A (p16) FISH in selected cases.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

This is a benign lesion.
