8.2.1.3 Nabothian cyst

Definition

A nabothian cyst is a mucus-filled cystic structure in the cervical wall, lined by endocervical epithelium.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA15.2 Nabothian cyst

Related terminology

Acceptable: Nabothian follicle

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cervix

Clinical features

Most lesions are asymptomatic, but they may present as polyps or be associated with chronic cervicitis and mucous discharge. Large or deeply situated nabothian cysts can be clinically concerning for a malignant process.

Epidemiology

Nabothian cysts are common, especially in multiparous women.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Obstruction of an endocervical gland leads to mucus retention and cyst formation.

Macroscopic appearance

Nabothian cysts are single or multiple, filled with mucus, and usually 2–10 mm in diameter. They are generally near the mucosal surface but are occasionally located deep in the cervical wall {2807713}.

Histopathology

The cystic spaces are round to slightly irregular and lined by a single layer of non-atypical attenuated to columnar endocervical-type epithelium. There is sometimes tubal metaplasia. No associated stromal reaction is noted. Deep nabothian cysts may be rarely identified {2807713}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cystic spaces are round to slightly irregular and lined by a single layer of non-atypical attenuated to columnar endocervical-type epithelium.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Nabothian cyst is benign.
