3.4.0.7 Splenosis

Definition

Splenosis is the presence of benign splenic tissue in the peritoneal cavity outside the spleen.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

3B81.4 Splenosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: autotransplanted splenic tissue

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Abdomen and/or pelvis {28543860}

Clinical features

Splenosis is usually an incidental finding, although it can occasionally produce pelvic pain, intestinal obstruction, bleeding, etc. {28543860; 21867352} or can be mistaken for endometriosis or malignancy {21867352; 29651643}.

Epidemiology

Splenosis is uncommon in women {29651643} and is typically seen in patients ranging in age from 29 to 60 years {30931139; 28543860; 23809236}.

Etiology

It results from the disruption of the spleen due to trauma, splenectomy, or spontaneous rupture due to haematological or infectious processes {21867352}.

Pathogenesis

Splenosis is caused by direct seeding of splenic tissue or haematogenous spread {21867352}. The interval between the causative incident and the diagnosis of splenosis ranges from a few months to > 50 years {21867352; 30363257}.

Macroscopic appearance

Splenosis presents as dark-red to bluish, solitary or multiple lesions ranging from a few millimetres to > 10 cm, although lesions > 3 cm are rare {28543860; 26076145; 29651643}.

Histopathology

The tissue may mimic normal spleen or show a distorted architecture {28543860}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: splenic tissue outside the spleen.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

This lesion is benign and usually does not require treatment unless symptomatic {21867352}.
