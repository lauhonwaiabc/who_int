7.1.1.2 Partial hydatidiform mole

Definition

Partial hydatidiform mole (PHM) is an abnormal gestation with trophoblastic proliferation and villous hydrops involving part of the chorionic villi, usually with embryonic development. The presence of a diandric, monogynic triploid genome is the fundamental etiology in most cases.

ICD-O coding

9103/0 Partial hydatidiform mole

ICD-11 coding

JA02.1 & XH5325 Incomplete or partial hydatidiform mole & Partial hydatidiform mole

Related terminology

Acceptable: partial molar pregnancy; partial mole

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Uterus and less commonly ectopic in the fallopian tube {15968202}

Clinical features

Most patients present in the late first or early second trimester with missed or incomplete abortion. Vaginal bleeding is less common than with complete mole {7070731; 26588240}. Serum β-hCG is normal or mildly elevated {19369669}. Most cases are not suspected clinically prior to evacuation. Ultrasound may show focal placental cystic changes, and a fetus may be detectable {2644599; 7255725}.

Epidemiology

No reliable epidemiology data are available. See section Complete hydatidiform mole. The incidence of hydatidiform moles shows a distinct geographical distribution, being highest in south-eastern Asia, the Middle East, and South America and lowest in western Europe and North America {29451409; 14602247; 26586414; 12972736}. However, the incidence rates of complete and partial moles are not separated in most studies, and epidemiological data specific to partial mole are lacking. Improvement in diagnostic accuracy in recent years may result in increased detection rates and incidence of PHM {27258730; 26586414; 29451409}. Maternal age is probably not a significant risk factor for PHM {14602247; 26777992}.

Etiology

Partial moles have excess paternal chromosome complements in most cases, resulting in diandric monogynic triploidy. Rare tetraploid cases with three paternal haploid chromosome sets also exist {22123726; 25078332; 23887308}.

Pathogenesis

Most PHMs (> 95%) arise as a result of two spermatozoa fertilizing one egg (dispermic/heterozygous PHM); the remaining cases result from reduplication of the paternal chromosome set (monospermic/homozygous PHM) due to failure of meiosis I or II after fertilization of one egg by one spermatozoon {7125594; 23887308; 28135560}. The exact mechanism by which the excess paternal genome leads to abnormal villous development is not yet understood.

Macroscopic appearance

Villous tissue may appear grossly normal or may show focal hydropic vesicles. Gestational sac, fetal parts, or an intact fetus may be present and show intrauterine growth restriction and malformations {2063893; 11746161}.

Histopathology

PHM typically demonstrates an admixture of two villous populations: small, fibrotic villi and enlarged, irregularly shaped villi with scalloped contours, a variable degree of hydrops, and mild to moderate circumferential trophoblastic proliferation. Cistern formation and trophoblastic pseudoinclusions are common {12469234; 23518914}. Fetal blood vessels and nucleated red blood cells are often present. Immunohistochemistry for p57 shows nuclear staining in cytotrophoblast and villous stromal cells {30500280; 24907943}. The morphological features of PHM are not entirely specific and may show significant overlap with those of complete hydatidiform mole, hydropic abortion, trisomy syndromes and other chromosomal abnormalities, placental mesenchymal dysplasia, and twin gestations with complete hydatidiform mole and a coexisting normal fetus. Digynic triploidy may also mimic PHM microscopically and by DNA ploidy analysis or karyotyping.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Short tandem repeat (STR) DNA genotyping provides precise diagnosis by identifying diandric triploidy {23518914; 28757408; 25078332}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic morphological features; diandric triploidy by STR genotyping; definitive diagnosis requires molecular analysis.

Desirable: retained nuclear p57 immunohistochemical expression to exclude complete mole.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 0.5–5% of PHMs are followed by persistent gestational trophoblastic disease, mostly invasive hydatidiform mole {26588240; 17086803; 17165438; 20673583}. The risk of choriocarcinoma after PHM is < 0.5% {10892763}.
