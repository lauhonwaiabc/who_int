10.1.3.1.4 Phyllodes tumour

Definition

Phyllodes tumour is a biphasic neoplasm composed of mammary-like epithelium with a prominent leaf-like growth and stromal hypercellularity.

ICD-O coding

9020/1 Phyllodes tumour NOS

9020/0 Phyllodes tumour, benign

9020/1 Phyllodes tumour, borderline

9020/3 Phyllodes tumour, malignant

ICD-11 coding

2F33.Y & XH50P7 Benign neoplasm of other specified female genital organs & Phyllodes tumour, benign

GA13.Y & XH5NK4 Other specified acquired abnormalities of vulva or perineum & Phyllodes tumour, borderline

2C70.Y & XH8HJ7 Other specified malignant neoplasms of vulva & Phyllodes tumour, malignant

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The labium majus is the most common site of occurrence {25357118}.

Clinical features

The average patient age at presentation is 39 years (range: 17–69 years) {20035149; 25357118; 23354802; 21293278}. Patients present with a vulvar mass that may be rapidly growing {20035149; 25357118; 23354802; 21293278}.

Epidemiology

These are very uncommon vulvar neoplasms

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These are thought to arise from anogenital mammary-like glands.

Macroscopic appearance

These are variably circumscribed masses with a solid or solid and cystic cut surface. Leaf-like structures may be evident grossly. The average size is 3.3 cm (range: 0.7–6.6 cm) {20035149; 25357118; 23354802; 21293278}.

Histopathology

Phyllodes tumours contain bland glandular elements lined by mammary-like epithelium with a prominent leaf-like stromal growth protruding into the glandular lumina. They typically display regional variability in stromal cellularity, with band-like areas of hypercellularity and increased mitotic activity around the epithelial components. Phyllodes tumours are classified as benign, borderline, or malignant depending on the degree of stromal atypia and mitotic activity; it is recommended that similar criteria be used as are used in the breast {26768026}. See the WHO classification of breast tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.fr/581.]].

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: biphasic histology; mammary-like epithelium with a prominent leaf-like architecture; hypercellular stroma.

Staging

Because the stromal component of phyllodes tumours represents the neoplastic component, malignant phyllodes tumours may be staged as sarcomas using the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]].

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 20% of reported cases have recurred locally, including some histologically “benign” tumours. There have been no reported cases of distant metastases {20035149; 25357118}. It is recommended that similar criteria be used to predict malignant behaviour as are used in the breast; however, because these are uncommon vulvar neoplasms, the criteria predicting malignant behaviour are not well established {26768026}.
