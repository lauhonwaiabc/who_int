3.5.2 Pseudomyxoma peritonei

Definition

The term “pseudomyxoma peritonei” refers to deposits of mucinous tumour within the peritoneal cavity secondary to a mucin-producing epithelial neoplasm, usually of appendiceal origin.

ICD-O coding

8480/6 Pseudomyxoma peritonei

ICD-11 coding

2D91 & XH83J5 Malignant neoplasm metastasis in peritoneum & Pseudomyxoma peritonei

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucinous carcinoma peritonei

Not recommended: disseminated peritoneal adenomucinosis; peritoneal mucinous carcinoma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

All sites of the peritoneal cavity can be affected. The mucinous tumour deposits may accumulate in dependent areas in the pelvic cavity due to gravity, or in sites at which ascitic fluid is resorbed, such as the omentum and the right hemidiaphragm, a pattern known as the redistribution phenomenon.

Clinical features

There can be symptoms of appendicitis, increasing abdominal girth, abdominal pain, or mucin within a hernia sac.

Epidemiology

Pseudomyxoma peritonei affects women somewhat more often than men, usually in the fourth to seventh decade of life {28746986}.

Etiology

Most cases arise from an appendiceal mucinous neoplasm. Extra-appendiceal primary sites include the colon, urachus, ovarian teratomas, pancreas, and gallbladder {28449606; 23785561; 30531244; 31082160}.

Pathogenesis

The genetic alterations in pseudomyxoma peritonei reflect the primary organ. Most of the data relate to appendiceal tumours that have spread to the peritoneum {28746986}. The vast majority of low-grade tumours have KRAS mutations, and most have GNAS mutations, which lead to constitutive activation of the PKA pathway {24944587; 25274248; 26475379}. High-grade tumours often have mutations similar to those in low-grade tumours, but with greater frequency and a higher tumour mutation burden. They often have additional mutations in other genes, including TP53, SMAD4, MYC, DAXX, and MYB {30962504; 24618609}. A notable exception is GNAS mutations, which are less common in high-grade tumours, suggesting that high-grade tumours do not necessarily arise from low-grade tumours {24944587}.

Macroscopic appearance

Mucinous globules of tumour usually range in consistency from watery mucus to more solid mucinous masses, depending on the degree of fibrosis and tumour cellularity.

Histopathology

To be considered pseudomyxoma peritonei, tumours must consist of abundant mucin, with varying degrees of tumour cellularity. Histological grading is based on tumour cellularity, cytological and architectural atypia, and the presence of signet-ring cells (see #10830Table #10830) {24633196; 26492181}. However, true signet-ring cells must be distinguished from pseudo–signet-ring cells, which are degenerated tumour cells floating in mucin (usually a focal finding). For this reason, signet-ring cells may be more prognostically important when they infiltrate tissue rather than only float in mucin {24814804}. Rarely, grade 3 tumours have other growth patterns, such as sheets of tumour cells.

Cytology

There is mucin with varying numbers of atypical cells, singly and in 3D clusters.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: accumulation of intra-abdominal mucin due to mucinous epithelial neoplasia (at least 50% of the neoplasm consists of extracellular mucin).

Staging

Staging is according to the primary site of the neoplasm. For appendiceal primaries, the stage of pseudomyxoma peritonei depends on the presence or absence of mucinous epithelium within the mucinous deposits (see #10831Table #10831).

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic factors include the cellularity of the peritoneal mucin, histological tumour grade, and whether complete cytoreduction can be achieved. Patients with low-grade tumours and complete cytoreduction have a median survival time of 59–170 months, whereas patients with grade 2 and 3 tumours have median survival times of 45–59 months and 18.9–26 months, respectively {30017331; 31090036; 26597367; 30523470}.
