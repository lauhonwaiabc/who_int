4.2.0.5 Placental site nodule occurring in the fallopian tube

Definition

Placental site nodule is a tumour-like benign lesion of chorionic-type intermediate trophoblast.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA17 & XH1RM5 Acquired abnormalities of fallopian tube & Placental site trophoblastic tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fallopian tube

Clinical features

Placental site nodule occurring in the fallopian tube is an incidental finding, usually weeks or even years after pregnancy {23564702; 8912838}.

Epidemiology

Placental site nodule occurring in the fallopian tube is very infrequent.

Etiology

Placental site nodule occurring in the fallopian tube originates from remnants of an ectopic placental implantation site.

Pathogenesis

Clinicopathological and molecular studies demonstrate that placental site nodule/plaque most likely develops from the chorionic-type intermediate trophoblastic cells (associated with chorion laeve) rather than from the intermediate trophoblastic cells in the implantation sites.

Macroscopic appearance

The lesion presents as a nodule of fallopian tube.

Histopathology

Placental site nodule/plaque presents as a small discrete nodule or multiple lesions containing nests of trophoblastic cells embedded in abundant eosinophilic extracellular matrix (see section Placental site nodule and plaque). It is frequently associated with a relatively intense lymphocytic infiltrate surrounding the lesion. Immunohistochemistry using the markers specific to intermediate trophoblast (especially the chorionic-type), including HSD3B1 and p63, is useful in differential diagnosis. These markers are also positive in epithelioid trophoblastic tumour and other trophoblastic neoplasms.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a small discrete nodule or multiple lesions containing nests of trophoblastic cells embedded in abundant eosinophilic extracellular matrix.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Placental site nodule/plaque can be transformed into epithelioid trophoblastic tumour, which has malignant potential.
