1.15.1.7 Fibromatosis and massive oedema

Definition

Fibromatosis and massive oedema are tumour-like enlargement of the ovaries due to fibroblastic proliferation with collagen deposition (fibromatosis) or stromal accumulation of oedema fluid (massive oedema).

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

FB51.Z Fibroblastic disorders, unspecified

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ovarian stroma

Clinical features

Fibromatosis and massive oedema present in premenopausal patients, with menstrual abnormalities, abdominal pain, or androgenic manifestations {6490313}. Most cases are unilateral {6490313; 8729041}.

Epidemiology

These are rare lesions.

Etiology

See below.

Pathogenesis

Ovarian fibromatosis is a stromal proliferation unrelated to the fibromatosis of soft tissue type {9421079}. It may rarely be associated with overgrowth disorders {23333153}. Massive oedema is associated with ovarian torsion, and obstruction of venous/lymphatic drainage is thought to play an etiological role {6490313; 17717430}. It is also associated with fibromatosis and benign/malignant neoplastic conditions, possibly through similar mechanisms {6490313; 15099983; 24901402}.

Macroscopic appearance

The ovary is enlarged (average: 8 cm), with a smooth nodular surface and a cut surface that is firm (fibromatosis) or watery (massive oedema) {6490313; 8729041}.

Histopathology

Fibromatosis is a proliferation of ovarian stromal fibroblastic cells with variable collagen deposition. Massive oedema shows markedly oedematous stroma that spares the outermost cortex. Luteinized stromal cells can be seen. Both processes preserve pre-existing ovarian structures {6490313; 8729041; 17717430}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fibromatosis: fibroblastic stromal proliferation with collagen deposition; massive oedema: stromal accumulation of oedema fluid.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign lesions.
