6.3.1.1 Uterine leiomyoma

Definition

Uterine leiomyomas are benign mesenchymal tumours of smooth muscle derivation with a wide range of morphological patterns.

ICD-O coding

8890/0 Leiomyoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E86.0 Leiomyoma of uterus

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Cellular leiomyoma; leiomyoma with bizarre nuclei (symplastic); fumarate hydratase–deficient leiomyoma; mitotically active leiomyoma; hydropic leiomyoma; apoplectic leiomyoma; lipoleiomyoma; epithelioid leiomyoma; myxoid leiomyoma; dissecting leiomyoma; diffuse leiomyomatosis

Localization

Uterine leiomyomas may be intramural, submucosal, or subserosal.

Clinical features

Leiomyomas are asymptomatic, but one third of patients present with menorrhagia; pelvic pain; or symptoms related to size, location, and number of tumours. Leiomyomas occur more frequently in patients who are receiving progestogen therapy {21398323; 3182168; 12636944}.

Epidemiology

Uterine leiomyomas usually affect women in their fifth decade of life. Leiomyoma, including subtypes, is the most common uterine tumour. The subtypes account for approximately 10% of leiomyomas. These tumours are most prevalent among African-American women and least common among Asian women {18534913; 28296146}. Geographical or racial variability may partly be related to different epigenetic factors {23818951; 22428009; 25922306}. Genome-wide association studies have also identified a single nucleotide polymorphism of 17q25.3 that leads to overexpression of FAS in white people {23040493}, as well as three loci (10q24.33, 22q13.1, and 11p15.5) in Asians, all of which increase susceptibility to leiomyomas {21460842}. Other risk factors include a family history, premenopausal state, hypertension, and > 5 years since most recently giving birth. The use of oral contraceptives decreases the risk of leiomyomas {9757871; 8813312}. Patients with hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma (HLRCC) present with symptomatic uterine leiomyomas and cutaneous leiomyomas in the second to third decade of life.

Etiology

Rare tumours arise in the setting of HLRCC (see section Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma).

Pathogenesis

Most leiomyomas originate from a single transformed somatic stem cell {22633281; 16202732; 8529956}, although multiple concurrent tumours may have a common clonal origin with distinct secondary subclonal genetic aberrations {25964426; 24713395}. Leiomyomas can harbour MED12 mutations, HMGA2 and HMGA1 rearrangements, COL4A5 and COL4A6 deletions, and FH mutations (see #10817Table #10817) {25106763}. These alterations are mutually exclusive {28592321}. Other, less common alterations include chromosome 7q22 deletion (CUX1) {22965931; 26787895}, 22q deletion (DEPDC5 and SMARCB1) {23738515; 26787895; 24525513}, and 1p deletion (NPHP4) {24412114; 19602464}. Spindle and myxoid leiomyomas have relatively simple genomic profiles {25932961; 31189997}; in contrast, leiomyomas with bizarre nuclei have both simple genomic profiles (biallelic inactivation, loss of heterozygosity, and FH mutations) and complex genomic profiles (TP53 and RB1 alterations) {28863073; 28664937; 27363490}. A novel t(10;17)(q22;q21) KAT6B-KANSL1 fusion has been described in cellular leiomyomas, and approximately one third of hydropic leiomyomas overexpress HMGA2 as a result of t(12;14)(q15;q24) {31027501; 30292626; 23738515}.

Macroscopic appearance

Leiomyomas are often multiple. They are well circumscribed but unencapsulated; they range widely in size and characteristically have a bulging, firm, whorled, white cut surface. Some tumours are soft, in particular if oedematous, highly cellular, or epithelioid. Cellular leiomyomas and lipoleiomyomas often have a tan to yellow cut surface. Large tumours may show haemorrhage and infarction. Cystic change may be seen. Leiomyomas in pregnant patients may have a beefy-red appearance (so-called red degeneration). Progestogen therapy may induce multiple discrete haemorrhagic foci (apoplectic change) {26685083}. Mitotically active leiomyomas are often submucosal. The serosal component of cotyledonoid dissecting leiomyoma may appear as beefy bulbous protrusions mimicking placental tissue. Myxoid leiomyoma has a gelatinous and sticky cut surface, which does not exude oedematous fluid on compression. Diffuse leiomyomatosis shows diffuse involvement of the myometrium by numerous ill-defined or confluent small nodules.

Histopathology

Most leiomyomas have a well-demarcated border and are composed of spindle cells arranged in intersecting fascicles. Cells have indistinct borders, eosinophilic fibrillary cytoplasm, and cigar-shaped nuclei with small nucleoli and rare mitoses. Nuclear palisading resembling schwannoma may be seen. Prominent hyalinization may lead to compartmentalization of tumour cells, imparting a pseudoepithelioid appearance {26715170}. Infarct-type necrosis (defined by the presence of a band of granulation tissue with or without associated haemorrhage or fibrosis between viable and non-viable tumour) may occur. The non-viable areas have a mummified appearance in which both tumour cells and blood vessels are devitalized. At an early stage, only single or groups of apoptotic cells are seen; in longstanding leiomyoma, calcification may occur {17667546; 20179432}. Tumours treated with GnRH agonists may show an irregular border, increased cellularity, infarction and hyalinization, a massive lymphoid infiltrate, and vascular changes {8406417; 9100066; 14615835; 9563785}. Uterine artery embolization usually results in infarct-type necrosis and marked acute inflammation {12548162; 20407327; 15958862}. Antifibrinolytic agents such as tranexamic acid, used in the treatment of menorrhagia and/or leiomyomas, can also induce infarction {17667546; 27801754}. No tumour cell necrosis is seen. Extensive sampling may be necessary in some tumours to exclude malignancy or other tumours. A number of histological subtypes are recognized.

Cellular leiomyoma shows significantly increased cellularity compared with the surrounding myometrium {7793473}. The neoplastic cells have a diffuse arrangement (often in the centre) or grow in fascicles (typically at the periphery). Thick-walled vessels and cleft-like spaces are common. Cells typically have scant cytoplasm and lack atypia, and mitoses are rare. The border is usually irregular and merges with the surrounding myometrium {647634; 24901397}.

Leiomyoma with bizarre nuclei (symplastic leiomyoma) contains bizarre cells arranged in a multifocal to diffuse distribution in a background of typical leiomyoma {25140893}. Most tumours have a well-circumscribed border. The bizarre cells may be mononucleated or multinucleated and may have eosinophilic or globular cytoplasm, smudged chromatin, and nuclear pseudoinclusions {27015034; 28664937; 28700441}. Mitotic count is typically low (< 2 mitoses/mm2, equating to < 5 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area), but karyorrhectic nuclei, which may mimic atypical mitoses, are not uncommon {9351564; 25140893}.

Fumarate hydratase–deficient leiomyoma shows staghorn vasculature, alveolar-pattern oedema, scattered bizarre nuclei, ovoid nuclei sometimes arranged in chains, eosinophilic cytoplasmic (rhabdoid) inclusions, and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli surrounded by perinucleolar haloes {27454940; 26574848; 24309325}. These features are not entirely specific – they may also be seen in conventional and cellular leiomyomas and leiomyoma with bizarre nuclei {28664937; 27454940; 26457356; 23211287}. Fumarate hydratase–deficient leiomyoma can be due to somatic or germline FH mutations (HLRCC); both mechanisms of fumarate hydratase loss result in similar histological findings.

Mitotically active leiomyomas are usually seen in women of reproductive age. They are associated with secretory endometrium, pregnancy, and drugs (progestogens and tamoxifen). They have increased mitotic activity, with a range of 2.5–6 mitoses/mm2 (equating to 6–14 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area). Cellularity may be increased, but there is no cytological atypia or tumour cell necrosis {2307449; 3337336; 1728856}. Extensive sampling is very important in these tumours.

Hydropic leiomyoma has conspicuous zonal, watery oedema, which separates tumour cells into thin and delicate cords or nests and may surround thick-walled vessels in a perinodular pattern {30292626; 1309411}.

Leiomyoma with apoplectic changes displays multiple stellate zones of haemorrhage surrounded by a hypercellular rim of cells – some with eosinophilic cytoplasm, pyknotic nuclei, and increased mitoses. The cells in these areas may be associated with a myxoid background. Most importantly, away from these areas, the smooth muscle cells have a banal appearance, imparting overall a zonation phenomenon {3182168; 4073354; 26685083}. These changes are induced by progestogen therapy and pregnancy; there may be hydropic change and cells with a deciduoid or signet-ring appearance {21398323}.

Lipoleiomyoma has variable numbers of mature adipocytes admixed with the smooth muscle cells {16810060; 25625021}. Some tumours may have a chondroid appearance or resemble a hibernoma {15842648; 9891251}. The cells have no significant atypia and only rare mitoses {1582747}.

Epithelioid leiomyomas display round or polygonal cells with appreciable eosinophilic granular or clear cytoplasm arranged in sheets, cords, trabeculae, or nests. They may be admixed with a spindle cell component {56982; 9130984}. Tumours with a plexiform growth and measuring < 1 cm are referred to as plexiform tumourlets {6092289}. Tumours have < 0.8 mitoses/mm2 (equating to < 2 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.55 mm in diameter and 0.24 mm2 in area). Rarely, cells have a rhabdoid morphology {9130984; 16175075}.

Myxoid leiomyoma is a well-circumscribed and hypocellular tumour with cells widely separated by myxoid acid-mucin stroma (Alcian blue–positive). Cells lack cytological atypia and mitotic activity {22175894; 28787279}.

Dissecting leiomyoma shows irregular dissection by nodules of bland smooth muscle cells of the myometrium, often with conspicuous hydropic change and occasionally with intravenous extension {8944038; 10478662; 23370645}. There may be extension outside the uterus (cotyledonoid dissecting leiomyoma) {22710314}.

Diffuse leiomyomatosis is characterized by innumerable, poorly circumscribed hypercellular nodules of bland smooth muscle cells diffusely involving the myometrium {3692674; 8835266}. It may be seen in patients with HLRCC {31162287}.

Leiomyoma subtypes can coexist. Heterologous elements such as bone {26458678}, cartilage {19225797}, skeletal muscle {1877531; 10088557}, haematopoietic or lymphoid cells {2670790}, and osteoclastic-type giant cells {26166717; 25851705} may rarely be found.

Immunohistochemistry

Leiomyomas express desmin, h-caldesmon, SMA, MSA, calponin, and SMMHC {19626649; 20004935}. They are also positive for ER, PR, WT1 (nuclear), and oxytocin receptor {19734847; 14576480}. HDAC8 is a sensitive marker for epithelioid tumours {16538051; 12653950}. CD10 (a marker of endometrial stromal derivation) is expressed in as many as 40% of cellular leiomyomas {11396634; 11914617}. Transgelin has been found to be expressed more frequently in smooth muscle than endometrial stromal tumours, but experience with this marker is limited {25991737}. Expression of p16 and p53 is rarely seen in usual leiomyomas, but these markers are not infrequently positive in leiomyomas with bizarre nuclei {18580309; 30845657}. Hypercellular areas in apoplectic leiomyomas may show increased p16 expression {28609585}. HMGA2 expression may be seen {30292626}. Loss of fumarate hydratase staining may be observed in tumours harbouring an underlying germline or somatic mutation involving the FH gene {31162287; 26457356; 24309325}. However, some tumours with missense FH mutations may retain fumarate hydratase staining {30741757; 26574848; 28664937; 26457356}. 2SC expression is sensitive and specific for an underlying fumarate hydratase deficiency {26574848; 24441663; 24309325}.

Prognosis and prediction

Usual leiomyomas and subtypes typically have a benign course, although experience with some subtypes is limited {8179071; 19417585; 20179432}. Tumours may recur after myomectomy {25140893; 28700441; 56982; 9130984}. Young women with symptomatic leiomyomas containing fumarate hydratase–deficient morphology should be referred for genetic counselling to exclude HLRCC {30741757}.
