4.1.3.2 High-grade serous carcinoma of the fallopian tube

Definition

High-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC) is a high-grade epithelial neoplasm demonstrating serous differentiation originating in the fallopian tube. The non-invasive form is called serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (STIC). Precursor lesions with less atypia are termed serous tubal intraepithelial lesions (STILs).

ICD-O coding

8461/3 High-grade serous carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C74.0 & XH24N6 Adenocarcinoma of fallopian tube & High-grade serous carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: adenocarcinoma of the tube

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fallopian tube

Clinical features

Clinical features include the rare triad of hydrops tubae profluens (pain, watery discharge, and pelvic mass) and the presence of a sausage-like adnexal mass. This tumour typically affects the fallopian tube, including the fimbria, and it is bilateral in 5% of cases {10053109; 11066048}.

Epidemiology

The epidemiology is similar to that of other extrauterine HGSCs (see section High-grade serous carcinoma of the ovary). The occurrence rate of STIC in fallopian tubes from prophylactic salpingo-oophorectomy is as high as 5–10% in the high-risk population (BRCA carriers), about 40% in association with invasive pelvic HGSC, and < 1% in the general population {30424539; 28218502; 31360879}.

Etiology

This tumour is thought to arise from genotoxic injury to fimbrial epithelium during reproductive life or menopause. Risk is also increased with hormone replacement therapy {22561401; 28931330; 28054715; 27625309}. A reported 16–43% of cases have been associated with germline BRCA mutations {11263928; 14615451}. There is evidence for lineage continuity between STIC and STIL in disseminated HGSC {30043522; 21990067}.

Pathogenesis

The presence of TP53 mutations in non-ciliated tubal epithelium (common) is thought to be followed by (uncommon) expansile growth linked to loss of function of other regulatory genes (including BRCA1/2, PIK3CA, and PTEN) {23780408; 28931330}. A deleterious TP53 mutation is detected in > 90% of cases and is an early event in high-grade serous carcinogenesis, reflected in aberrant p53 expression (diffuse or nearly diffuse nuclear staining or complete loss of nuclear staining) {26166714}.

Macroscopic appearance

There is hydrosalpinx with papillary or solid intraluminal growth and fusion of the fimbrial end. Detection of small STICs or carcinomas is maximized using the SEE-FIM (sectioning and extensively examining the fimbriated end) protocol {16434898}. Sometimes these tumours are microscopic and not visible grossly.

Histopathology

The histopathology is identical to that of other HGSCs (see section High-grade serous carcinoma of the ovary) and may include classic papillary and SET (solid, endometrial-like, transitional) cytomorphology. STIC is a non-invasive subtype of HGSC, usually localized to the tuboperitoneal junction {25675178}. It is characterized by abnormal morphological features (high N:C ratio, nuclear enlargement, pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, lack of ciliated cells, loss of polarity with or without epithelial stratification, and occasional mitotic figures), aberrant p53 expression, and increased Ki-67 immunostaining (> 10%). STIL is a related lesion that shows variable morphology, p53 expression, and Ki-67 immunostaining, falling short of the full criteria for STIC {21989347; 22498942}.

Cytology

Vaginal/cervical cytology is positive in 14% of cases {12789923}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not usually necessary for clinical diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the presence of STIC or any mucosal HGSC or obliteration of part or all of a tube by the tumour mass signifies a primary tubal lesion (see #10931Table #10931); for STIC: abnormal morphological features (high N:C ratio, nuclear enlargement, pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, lack of ciliated cells, loss of polarity with or without epithelial stratification, and occasional mitotic figures), aberrant p53 expression (> 75%, strong or moderate to complete absence), and increased Ki-67 immunostaining (> 10%).

Staging

This entity is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification (see TNM staging of ovarian, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneal carcinoma [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]]).

Prognosis and prediction

The 5-year survival rate is > 80% with stage I disease, in particular with adherent fimbria {25149685; 10053109; 11066048; 29281053}. Isolated STIC shows subsequent development of HGSC in 4.5% of patients {26407480}.
