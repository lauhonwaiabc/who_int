8.2.1.1 Endocervical polyp

Definition

Endocervical polyp is an exophytic lesion lined by benign endocervical epithelium covering a fibrovascular core.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

GA15.0 Polyp of cervix uteri

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Endocervix

Clinical features

Endocervical polyp is usually asymptomatic but may be associated with vaginal bleeding (especially postcoital) and/or discharge.

Epidemiology

These are common lesions that may occur at any age, but they are more common among women aged > 40 years {14010498}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Most are solitary and < 1 cm {14010498}.

Histopathology

Endocervical polyps are composed of fibrovascular tissue lined by benign endocervical-type epithelium, with glands permeating the central stroma {14010498}. There is commonly squamous metaplasia or microglandular change. Rarely, surface papillary proliferation or stromal decidual change may occur. The surface of polyps may be inflamed or eroded and associated with reactive/reparative changes. Squamous intraepithelial lesion may be present {18753960}.

Cytology

Reactive or reparative changes can be seen in the setting of an endocervical polyp. Repair in squamous cells is characterized by monolayer sheets with round to oval nuclei and smooth nuclear contours. Nucleoli may be present, and streaming can be seen but is less apparent in liquid-based preparations. Reactive endocervical cells are notable for mild nuclear enlargement and slight crowding but have round to oval nuclei with smooth nuclear contour and even chromatin. Marked reactive changes in a polyp can lead to a cytological diagnosis of atypical squamous cells, atypical endocervical cells, or atypical glandular cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fibrovascular connective tissue lined by benign endocervical-type mucinous epithelium, often with squamous metaplasia; glands are typically also present within the polyp stroma.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Endocervical polyps are benign lesions, although they can rarely be involved by in situ lesions or invasive carcinomas, either solely or as part of more-generalized cervical neoplasia {18753960}.
