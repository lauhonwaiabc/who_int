6.3.2.2 Low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma

Definition

Low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma is a malignant stromal tumour with cells resembling proliferative-phase endometrial stroma and displaying infiltrative (permeative) growth with or without lymphovascular invasion.

ICD-O coding

8931/3 Endometrial stromal sarcoma, low grade

ICD-11 coding

2B5C.0 & XH1S94 Endometrial stromal sarcoma of uterus & Endometrial stromal sarcoma, low grade

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours involve the uterine corpus much more commonly than the cervix {8253545; 24121169}.

Clinical features

Patients present with abnormal uterine bleeding, pelvic pain, a uterine mass, and/or lung metastases or adnexal and nodal metastasis {21276609}.

Epidemiology

Tumours occur over a wide age range (mean patient age: 52 years; range: 16–83 years) {18813312}. Risk factors include prolonged estrogen or tamoxifen intake and pelvic irradiation {2985476; 7665715; 3756818}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Two thirds of low-grade endometrial stromal sarcomas harbour genetic fusions involving polycomb family genes {21836477; 11371647; 12850374; 15043312; 16049311; 16397222; 17197920; 17667554; 18580489; 22918161; 23211293; 24592973; 25288234; 27154512; 27219024}, with JAZF1-SUZ12 being most common, followed by JAZF1-PHF1, EPC1-PHF1, and MEAF6-PHF1 {22761769; 24530230}. MBTD1-EZHIP (CXorf67), BRD8-PHF1, EPC2-PHF1, and EPC1-SUZ12 have also been documented, but experience is limited (< 3 reported cases each) {23959973; 28758277; 29721194; 30144186}, with BRD8-PHF1 also implicated in a high-grade tumour {30789359}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours often form intracavitary or intramural, poorly defined, coalescent, yellow to tan nodules (most being 5–10 cm in size), typically associated with overt myometrial infiltration and/or intravascular tumour plugs, the latter sometimes more striking in parametrial veins {18813312; 2327549; 854242; 6490315}.

Histopathology

Irregular, densely cellular islands of tumour cells with a diffuse growth permeate the myometrium (tongue-like) and may be associated with lymphovascular invasion (sometimes prominent). Cells have uniform, oval to fusiform nuclei with no or minimal atypia, scant cytoplasm, and a delicate arteriolar network. Sometimes tumour cells whorl around the vessels. The mitotic activity is usually low but can be brisk {16049311}. Hyaline plaques, foamy histiocytes, cystic change, and necrosis can be seen {2327549; 5939046}. Common variant features include smooth muscle differentiation {8786208; 9706980; 12218570; 15043312; 21836477; 17667554; 23211293}, fibromyxoid/fibrous change {12218570; 10542938; 15043312; 16049311; 21836477}, and sex cord–like differentiation {16049311; 17667554; 21836477; 23211293}. Bizarre nuclei, epithelioid/rhabdoid change, endometrioid glands, and pseudopapillae are uncommon {11376025; 18580489; 21836477}. Other reported variations (e.g. skeletal muscle / adipocytic differentiation and clear cell change) are not seen in molecularly confirmed cases {21836477}.

Tumours usually show diffuse strong expression of CD10, IFITM1, ER, and PR, with focal cyclin D1 positivity (with rare exceptions) {11353058; 11532038; 11914617; 10737385; 22982899; 28700435}. Tumours may be positive for wide-spectrum keratins {1706303; 29406332}. Desmin and h-caldesmon typically highlight smooth muscle differentiation {16538051} and are often positive in sex cord–like elements, which also express inhibin, calretinin, melan-A, WT1, and CD99 {17347285; 10374776; 16118629; 15007645; 11914617; 15213600}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of the characteristic gene fusions may be helpful in selected cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: proliferative-phase endometrial stromal-type tumour cells permeating the myometrium, with or without lymphovascular invasion.

Staging

Low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma is staged as uterine sarcoma using the FIGO, American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC), and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) systems.

Prognosis and prediction

This is a relatively indolent sarcoma, with a protracted clinical course even in patients with recurrent disease. Stage is the most important prognostic factor, with a 5-year survival rate of > 90% associated with stage I/II versus 50% with stage III/IV {19236512}. Some tumours showing myometrial infiltration exceeding that allowed in a nodule but relatively limited when compared with most sarcomas have been designated as endometrial stromal tumours with limited infiltration; however, they should be diagnosed as low-grade endometrial stromal sarcomas for all purposes, because they may also recur {30807369}.
