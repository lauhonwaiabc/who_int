13.1.5.2 Rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

Rhabdomyosarcomas are a family of malignant mesenchymal tumours exhibiting skeletal muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8900/3 Rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

8910/3 Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

8920/3 Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

8901/3 Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B55.Y & XH0GA1 Rhabdomyosarcoma, other specified primary site & Rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

2B55 & XH7099 Rhabdomyosarcoma & Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

2B55 & XH5SX9 Rhabdomyosarcoma, primary site & Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

2B55 & XH83G1 Rhabdomyosarcoma & Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: sarcoma botryoides; mixed embryonal and alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The frequency at different sites within the female genital tract (FGT) varies by histological subtype. Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma is typically seen in the corpus. In the lower FGT, embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS) is most common in the vagina in children and in the cervix and corpus in adolescents and adults; alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS) is most common in the vulva {23111355; 28634755; 3278956; 29320751; 17325479}.

Clinical features

Patients with rhabdomyosarcomas often present with bleeding and are found to have a mass on pelvic examination. Cervical and vaginal ERMS often presents as a polyp {22157934} and may protrude through the introitus, sometimes with a grape-like or botryoid appearance {29421342}. Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma typically occurs in postmenopausal patients, who often present with abnormal vaginal bleeding {20173498; 9421077}.

Epidemiology

ERMS represents > 75% of lower FGT rhabdomyosarcomas {28634755}. Most ERMSs arise in children, whereas ARMS and pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma occur at an older age.

Etiology

Individuals with DICER1 syndrome (germline DICER1 mutations) are at risk for cervical ERMS {22157934; 27241106} (see section DICER1 syndrome).

Pathogenesis

In ERMS, RAS signalling pathway mutations activate the MAPK effector pathway to repress myogenin expression and block myogenesis {29973406; 24436047; 26138366}. In ARMS, t(2;13) or t(1;13) translocations produce PAX3-FOXO1 or PAX7-FOXO1 fusion genes encoding transcription factors that activate PAX3/PAX7 target genes involved in the regulation of proliferation, survival, differentiation, and motility {30617281}. Molecular studies of a single case of pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma revealed PIK3CA and TP53 mutations {29320751}. Somatic DICER1 mutations have been reported in a small subset of cervical ERMSs {22180160; 26461232}.

Macroscopic appearance

Rhabdomyosarcoma usually has a fleshy greyish-tan appearance. Botryoid ERMS often presents as clusters of submucosal, multiple, polypoid, grape-like masses {24113309}.

Histopathology

ERMS is usually polypoid and partially covered by squamous or glandular epithelium {24113309}. It often has alternating cellularity, with hypocellular myxoedematous areas and closely packed primitive cells beneath the surface epithelium and around pre-existent entrapped glands (the latter in the cervix and corpus). In botryoid tumours, a hypercellular cambium layer beneath the surface epithelium (which may be subtle) and cuffing around inactive pre-existing entrapped endometrial glands may be seen. If hypocellular areas with oedematous/myxoid matrix are predominant, the tumour may be confused with a polyp. Tumour cells have round, ovoid, or spindle-shaped nuclei and scant cytoplasm. Strap-shaped cells are variably seen but are often not prominent. Cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, rhabdomyoblasts with cytoplasmic cross-striations, and multinucleated giant cells are occasionally present. Tumours show brisk mitotic and apoptotic rates. Anaplasia (lobated, hyperchromatic nuclei > 3 times the size of those of adjacent cells) may occur {8470759}. Approximately 40% of tumours contain fetal-type cartilage {23348207; 3278956}. Some tumours can also contain areas with a focal alveolar appearance, but molecular analysis is necessary to identify whether such cases contain a fusion gene {18580325; 29320751}.

ARMS is composed of small round blue cells with variable numbers of rhabdomyoblasts growing in loose cohesive nests or alveoli, separated by collagenous stroma {21615615; 24113309}. The cells show dyscohesion centrally and adhere to septa at the periphery. Multinucleate cells may be present. A solid pattern without fibrous septa may be seen. Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma shows an admixture of round, polygonal, bizarre, or spindle cells, with marked atypia and with or without giant cells and rhabdomyoblasts {17325479; 29320751; 20173498; 24113309}.

Rhabdomyosarcomas are positive for myogenin (sparse if embryonal) {24113309} and MYOD1; both are specific. Myogenin and MYOD1 expression is usually more diffuse in ARMS than ERMS. MSA, desmin, myoglobin, and myosin may be positive, but SMA is negative. Wide-spectrum keratins and neuroendocrine markers can be positive in ARMS {18611935}. Rhabdomyosarcoma arising in the context of other neoplasms (e.g. as sarcomatous overgrowth in an adenosarcoma or as heterologous sarcoma in a carcinosarcoma) should not be typed.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of fusion genes may be helpful in selected cases (see Pathogenesis, above).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ERMS: alternating cellularity of primitive cells with subepithelial condensation (cambium layer), with or without obvious skeletal muscle differentiation; ARMS: alveolar pattern (classic or solid); pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma: presence of bizarre cells with marked atypia, with or without giant cells and rhabdomyoblasts; all subtypes: skeletal muscle marker positivity (myogenin and MYOD1).

Desirable: molecular confirmation (in selected cases).

Staging

Pathological staging is based on tumour, nodal, and metastatic status, and clinical group is based on extent of disease {16921036}. In the Intergroup Rhabdomyosarcoma Study Group (IRSG) clinical staging system, the FGT is a favourable site and non-metastatic tumours are considered stage 1.

Prognosis and prediction

Rhabdomyosarcoma of the lower FGT is associated with an overall 5-year survival rate of about 70%, which is consistent with the majority being of embryonal type {28634755}, because ERMS has a better prognosis than pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma and ARMS {12879475; 17325479; 20173498; 20687274}. Younger patient age, non-metastatic disease, ERMS histology, and treatment by cancer-directed surgery are associated with improved survival {11413538; 28634755; 29159540}. Tumours with ARMS histology but without gene fusions are associated with an outcome similar to that associated with ERMS histology {20351326; 23526739}. Anaplasia in ERMS is associated with poor prognosis {8470759}.
