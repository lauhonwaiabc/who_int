6.0.1.5 Mucinous cystic neoplasm, low-grade

Definition

Low-grade mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN) is a cyst-forming and mucin-producing epithelial neoplasm with distinctive ovarian-like subepithelial stroma and low-grade dysplasia.

Clinical features and imaging

Low-grade MCN almost exclusively occurs in middle-aged women and predominantly involves the body or tail of the pancreas or the intrahepatic biliary system. Imaging studies show a multiloculated, thick-walled cystic lesion without connection to the pancreatic duct or biliary duct {15185076; 20924309}. The features suggestive of invasive carcinoma, such as irregular thickening of the cyst wall and intracystic mural nodules, are absent {25517958}.

Histopathology

MCNs arising in the pancreas and hepatobiliary duct system share the same histopathological features. The tumours are multilocular cysts lined by epithelium and underlying ovarian-type stroma. The lining cells are predominantly columnar mucin-producing cells, although non-mucinous cuboidal cells can be seen in as many as 50% of MCNs arising in the hepatobiliary system {27673548}. Attenuation of the epithelium from pressure can also diminish the cytoplasmic mucin and reduce the cyst-lining epithelium to flat to cuboidal non-mucinous cells. Erosion of the lining is also not uncommon. Low-grade MCNs show mild to moderate cytoarchitectural atypia without complex papillary projections or severe cytopathological atypia {11342768}. The presence of ovarian-like stroma is required for the diagnosis, which is characterized by densely packed spindle-shaped cells with round or elongated nuclei and scant cytoplasm.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Usually low cellularity
  • Variable amount of thin or thick mucin with histiocytes
  • Bland or mildly atypical epithelial cells in small sheets and tissue fragments or singly
  • Columnar or cuboidal cells that may or may not contain cytoplasmic mucin
  • Round or oval nuclei with smooth or mildly irregular nuclear membranes, evenly distributed chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli
  • Background coagulative-type necrosis is absent, but erosion can cause the cyst fluid to be inflammatory with degenerated cells

Reference(s): {27926784}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

A variable amount of background mucin and sparse mucin-containing columnar cells are the classic cytopathological findings, which along with the imaging finding of a multiloculated, thick-walled cyst in the body or tail of the pancreas in a middle-aged woman support the diagnosis of MCN {24777782; 32589187; 27926784}. In cases with no or scant mucin identified, a high CEA level (≥ 192 ng/mL) in cyst fluid supports the diagnosis of a mucinous cyst {15131794}. MCNs share similar cytopathological features with intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs), especially side-branch IPMN, and distinction between the two is difficult if not impossible in the absence of ovarian-like stroma; thus, it is acceptable to render a cytopathological diagnosis of “neoplastic mucinous cyst”, to include MCN and IPMN, in the differential diagnosis {25775066}. The use of microforceps biopsy that obtains a bite of the cyst wall can help make a specific diagnosis of MCN by obtaining a tissue sample demonstrating the ovarian-type stroma {29660844}.

It is critically important to differentiate low-grade MCN from high-grade MCN. The FNAB material should be carefully examined to assess the degree of cytoarchitectural atypia, although it is well known that high-grade atypia can be underrepresented in the FNAB of MCN {30257067; 23939829; 32589187}. MCN may undergo substantial degenerative changes and mucin-producing cells may be absent, making it difficult to distinguish from a pseudocyst {26576582; 31538798}. Other entities in the differential diagnosis include serous cystadenoma, lymphoepithelial cyst, and secondary dilatation of the duct due to proximal obstruction {18089490}.

Ancillary testing

Cyst fluid chemical analysis is helpful in the differential diagnosis between MCN and a non-mucinous cyst, including pseudocysts. A high CEA level (≥ 192 ng/mL) supports a mucinous cyst, whereas a low amylase level (< 250 U/L) helps to exclude a pseudocyst {15131794; 24965184}. Elevated pancreatic cyst fluid levels of amylase cannot be used to differentiate IPMN from MCN {23395570}. In a prospective study, IPMN and MCN showed a range of amylase levels from 0 to 280 000 U/L {32122771}.

Molecular testing may have a potential role in the diagnosis of low-grade MCN. MCNs harbour KRAS mutations; however, KRAS mutations are identified in only a small subset of low-grade MCNs {29189063; 23187835}. Other gene alterations such as TP53 and SMAD4 mutations are absent in low-grade cysts. Identification of GNAS mutation supports the diagnosis of IPMN {21775669; 24938521}.
