4.10.6 Rosai-Dorfman disease

Definition

Rosai–Dorfman disease (RDD) is a histiocytic proliferation characterized by large S100-positive histiocytes with emperipolesis.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Rosai–Dorfman disease

ICD-11 coding

EK92 Histiocytoses of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Not recommended: sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Primary intraosseous RDD most commonly affects the metaphysis of long bones and craniofacial skeleton {28401384}. In 71% of cases, one bone is affected; the remainder involve multiple bones {28401384}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Patients usually present with pain localized to the involved anatomical site; pathological fracture is rare.

Imaging

Imaging studies reveal a well-defined lytic, sometimes expansile, septated mass. Cortical thickening and periosteal reaction are present in a minority of cases {20679880}.

Epidemiology

RDD is a rare disease that usually presents in the lymph nodes, but it may affect extranodal sites in > 40% of cases; bone involvement occurs in 2–10% of patients {26966089}. Primary bone involvement is very rare, is associated with equal sex distribution, and may affect patients of any age (mean: 31 years) {28401384}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Constitutive activation of the RAS/MAPK pathway probably plays a role in a subset of cases, because this pathway is important in monocyte maturation {28614806}. Mutually exclusive missense mutations in NRAS, KRAS, MAP2K1, ARAF, and BRAF have been reported in 33–40% of tumours and are considered driver genetic aberrations {29720485; 30014527; 29748446}. Germline mutations in SLC29A3 and FAS (TNFRSF6) are associated with patients who have the rare familial form of the disease {29720485}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range in size from 1 to 7 cm; most are < 5 cm and are well defined and tan-grey, with a soft or gritty texture.

Histopathology

The tumour is poorly defined, replaces the marrow, infiltrates haversian systems, and is associated with local bone resorption {20679880}. The mass is characterized by sheets and clusters of large histiocytes with nuclei that range from round or oval to reniform, with fine or vesicular chromatin and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli. The cytoplasm is abundant and pale eosinophilic, and it demonstrates conspicuous emperipolesis (lymphocytophagocytosis) of lymphocytes, plasma cells, or neutrophils. The tumour cells are enmeshed in a fibrotic stroma that contains a prominent mixed inflammatory infiltrate composed of lymphocytes, plasma cells (often containing Russell bodies), neutrophils, foamy macrophages, and rare eosinophils. Microabscesses may be present. The neoplastic histiocytes are variable in number and distribution, and they may be undersampled on biopsy. The histiocytes express S100 and are negative for CD1a and CD207 {26966089}. The differential diagnosis includes osteomyelitis, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, Erdheim–Chester disease, and IgG4-associated sclerosing lesion because RDD may also contain numerous IgG4-positive plasma cells {26966089}.

Cytology

FNA reveals large histiocytes in a mixed inflammatory background. Emperipolesis of inflammatory cells can usually be seen in the cytoplasm of the tumour cells {24192148}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large S100-positive, CD1a-negative histiocytes with cytoplasmic emperipolesis; plasma cell–rich inflammatory infiltrate.

Staging

There is no applicable staging system. Imaging studies (e.g. FDG PET-CT) to identify additional sites of disease should be considered {29720485}.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis is good; one or more other organs become involved in 58% of patients {28401384}. Bone lesions are effectively treated for local control by curettage {20679880}.
