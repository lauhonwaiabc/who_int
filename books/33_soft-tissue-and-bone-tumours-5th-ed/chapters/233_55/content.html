2.3.3 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Definition

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour is a rare dermal and subcutaneous neoplasm showing plexiform architecture and biphasic morphology, composed of nodules of histiocytoid cells and bundles of myofibroblastic spindle cells.

ICD-O coding

8835/1 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH4GL1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is a strong predilection for the upper limbs followed by the lower limbs. The trunk and the head and neck area are less often affected {17870015; 2847569; 1723956}.

Clinical features

The tumours present as slow-growing, ill-defined plaques and nodules, typically measuring 1–3 cm in diameter {2847569}.

Epidemiology

The age range is wide, but children and young adults are typically affected, with a median age of 14.5–20 years and an equal sex distribution {17870015; 2847569; 1723956; 10366148}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours form a nondescript mass at the dermal–subcutaneous junction, usually < 3 cm in size.

Histopathology

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumours are centred at the dermal–subcutaneous junction. They show an infiltrative architecture with overall plexiform outlines and may extend into skeletal muscle. The tumours are composed of small nodules of histiocytoid cells and osteoclast-like multinucleated giant cells, surrounded by fascicles of spindle cells in varying proportions. There may be haemorrhage, haemosiderin deposition, and a chronic inflammatory infiltrate. Lymphovascular invasion can rarely be seen. Additional rare findings include myxoid or hyalinized stromal changes and metaplastic bone formation. By immunohistochemistry, the spindle cells express SMA {22441367}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: plexiform architecture; involvement of dermis and/or subcutaneous adipose tissue; nodules composed of histiocytoid epithelioid cells and osteoclast-like giant cells; fascicles of myofibroblastic spindle cells.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour is associated with a risk for local recurrence ranging from 12.5% to 37.5% and rare lymph node metastasis {17870015; 2847569; 1723956; 10366148}. Distant metastasis to the lung is exceptional {9130995; 10366148}.
