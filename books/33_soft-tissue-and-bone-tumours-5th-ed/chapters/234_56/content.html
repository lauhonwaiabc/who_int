2.3.4 Giant cell tumour of soft tissue

Definition

Giant cell tumour of soft tissue is morphologically similar to but genetically unrelated to giant cell tumour of bone.

ICD-O coding

9251/1 Giant cell tumour of soft parts

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH81M1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Giant cell tumour of soft parts NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: giant cell tumour of low malignant potential.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Giant cell tumour of soft tissue usually occurs in superficial soft tissues of the upper and lower extremities (70% of tumours). Affected less frequently are the trunk (20%) and head and neck (7%) regions {10496598; 10716152; 10680893; 19615796; 21563092}. Occasional cases have been reported in other anatomical locations {21139924; 23644334; 29867073}.

Clinical features

The tumours usually present as painless growing masses {10716152; 10680893}, with an average duration of 6 months {10680893}. Peripheral mineralization is common.

Epidemiology

Giant cell tumour of soft tissue occurs predominantly in the fifth decade of life, but it can affect patients ranging in age from 5 to 89 years. Giant cell tumour of soft tissue shows no apparent difference in incidence with regard to sex or ethnicity {10496598; 10716152; 10680893}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Giant cell tumour of soft tissue lacks the mutations of the H3-3A (H3F3A) gene that are present in the vast majority of giant cell tumours of bone, suggesting a different pathogenesis {28084336; 28477388}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range in size from 0.7 to 10 cm (mean: 3 cm) {10496598; 10716152; 10680893}. Subcutaneous adipose tissue or dermis is involved in 70% of tumours; 30% are situated deep to superficial fascia. Giant cell tumour of soft tissue is a well-circumscribed, mostly solid, nodular mass with a fleshy, reddish-brown or grey cut surface. Gritty regions of mineralized bone are frequently present at the periphery {10716152}.

Histopathology

Giant cell tumour of soft tissue displays a multinodular architecture (85%). Cellular nodules are separated by fibrous septa of varying thickness, containing haemosiderin-laden macrophages {10716152}. The nodules are composed of a mixture of round to oval mononuclear cells and osteoclast-like multinucleated giant cells, with both cell types immersed in a richly vascularized stroma. Mitotic activity is readily seen in giant cell tumour of soft tissue {10496598; 10716152; 10680893}. Nuclear pleomorphism and bizarre giant cells are absent, and necrosis is rarely found {10496598; 10716152; 10680893}. Metaplastic bone formation is present in approximately 50% of tumours, most often in the form of a peripheral shell of woven bone. Aneurysmal bone cyst–like changes may be seen. Vascular invasion is identified in about 30% of tumours {10496598; 10680893}. Additional histological features include stromal haemorrhage (50%) and regressive changes in the form of marked stromal fibrosis and clusters of foamy macrophages (70%). Immunohistochemistry is not helpful.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multinodular superficial soft tissue neoplasm; histiocytoid mononuclear cell population with osteoclastic giant cells; haemosiderin deposition and metaplastic bone formation are commonly observed.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Giant cell tumour of soft tissue is associated with a local recurrence rate of 12%, with very rare metastasis {10496598; 10716152; 10680893}.
