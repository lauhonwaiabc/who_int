10.2.4.1 Haemangioma of bone

Definition

Haemangioma of bone (HA-B), including epithelioid haemangioma (EH), is a benign vascular tumour infrequently involving the calvarium or facial bone including the orbit. It is genetically characterized by a fusion gene and can be locally aggressive.

ICD-O coding

9120/0 Haemangioma

9125/0 Epithelioid haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.0Y & XH5AW4 Neoplastic haemangioma of other specified site & Haemangioma, NOS

2E81.0Y & XH10T4 Neoplastic haemangioma of other specified site & Epithelioid haemangioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: intraosseous haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid haemangioma of bone (EH-B)

Localization

The craniofacial skeleton including the orbit can be affected {33481536;16754225;28210401;31874384;33034932}. Lesions can be multifocal {34742485;31708630;33034932}.

Clinical features

Lesions grow slowly and are often asymptomatic. They can present as a swelling/mass and can cause a range of symptoms including pain, displacement (with proptosis and diplopia), ophthalmoparesis, and epiphora {33481536;16754225;31874384}.

Imaging shows an expansile, often lobulated lesion with a polka-dot pattern that is hyperintense on MRI {31708630;34742485}.

Epidemiology

HA-Bs are relatively common and often incidental findings. They can be found at any age. Lesions often present in adulthood. The spine is the most common site followed by the craniofacial bones. The orbit is very rarely affected {28210401;31874384;34742485;34203801}.

EH-B is rare, involving orbital bone exceptionally {31708630;28210401}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

HA-Bs are fusion gene–driven tumours, of which the genetic drivers are:

for HA-B: EWSR1::NFATC1 or EWSR1::NFATC2 {34081036;23480895;34742485} for EH-B: FOS or FOSB rearrangements with variable partners (e.g. ZFP36::FOSB, WWTR1::FOSB, FOS::LMNA, and FOS::VIM) {32271190;33034932}.

Macroscopic appearance

These lesions are situated in the medullary cavity with a sponge-like haemorrhagic appearance and a delineated sclerotic border {34742485}.

Histopathology

HA-B shows variable thin- and thick-walled vessels of different calibre without remarkable endothelial lining. A stromal reaction can be prominent. A vascular malformation can be difficult to differentiate.

EH-B is distinguished by a lobular architecture of well-formed vessels with a pericytic cuff. In the centre of the lesion, the plump monomorphic epithelioid cells are sometimes tightly packed. There is a zonation pattern with peripheral maturation. The eosinophilic cytoplasm may possess vacuoles. Cellular atypia and necrosis can be confused with a malignant tumour {34742485;33034932;32271190}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry accentuates the organoid vasculature by highlighting the endothelial cells with CD31, CD34, ERG, and FLI1 staining. Pericytes / smooth muscle cells express αSMA {34742485;31950476}.

In EH-B, FOS or FOSB is positive {32271190}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HA-B: EWSR1::NFATC1 or EWSR1::NFATC2

EH-B: FOS or FOSB rearrangement with variable fusion partners

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone (orbit) as primary site; compatible radiology; for HA-B: thin-walled blood vessels lined by a single layer of non-atypical endothelial cells; for EH-B: large epithelioid endothelial cells with densely eosinophilic cytoplasm.

Desirable: detection of a fusion gene (see Diagnostic molecular pathology); for EH-B: lobular architecture with distinct vasoformation; FOS and FOSB positivity by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is excellent. Recurrences are rare. Multifocality should not be interpreted as metastases {33481536;16754225;31874384;31708630;34742485}.
