8.1.2.3 Orbital glial heterotopia

Definition

Orbital glial heterotopia is an isolated orbital focus of non-teratomatous CNS tissue occurring entirely outside the intracranial and intraspinal cavities in the absence of a skull defect and microphthalmia with cyst.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.2 Structural developmental anomalies of orbit

Related terminology

Acceptable: ectopic glial-meningeal tissue; neuroglial heterotopia.

Not recommended: heterotopic brain choristoma; ectopic brain tissue.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Glial heterotopia can rarely be found in the orbit (without any dural connection) {31348112} and only single reports of extraorbital lesions (lower eyelid, retina) exist {24492734;12523898}.

Clinical features

Based on the approximately 30 reported cases, the most common signs are swelling, strabismus, and proptosis. One case of blindness due to orbital glial heterotopia has been reported {22088230}. Microphthalmia and anophthalmia can also be present {35253133}; however, the symptoms depend on the location and the size of the orbital lesion. The lesion is usually unilateral, but a patient with bilateral orbital lesions has been described {30505127}. Leukocoria has been clinically noted in a case of intraocular glial heterotopia mimicking a retinoblastoma {12523898}. CT or MRI is recommended for localization of the lesion.

The differential diagnosis may include encephalocoele and microphthalmia with cyst. In order to suggest the diagnosis of orbital glial heterotopia, it must be shown clinically that there is no skull defect to suggest an encephalocoele and that the eye is otherwise without evidence of microphthalmia with cyst.

Epidemiology

For orbital lesions the age at presentation is typically before 5 years {31348112}. Some of the reported cases were described in neonates. Glial heterotopia is rarely found in adults {27958209;19229751}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unclear. Embryological hypotheses include abnormal migration of neural tissue and abnormal maturation of neural crests.

Macroscopic appearance

The reported size of lesions has varied between a few and 80 mm.

Histopathology

The lesions are composed of benign and mature brain tissue including glial and neural components. Microcalcifications and psammoma bodies may be present {20818257}. The tissue is typically positive for S100 and GFAP {8110681}. Neurofilament protein may identify axons within the tissue. Ependyma, choroid plexus, and cerebellar tissue are rarely present. One report described an orbital lesion composed of glial tissue intermixed with muscle fibres {18569821}.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mature brain tissue in the orbit without connection to the dura/meninges; absence of a skull defect and absence of microphthalmia with cyst.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

No malignant transformation has been reported. The lesions can exhibit slow growth. A diagnostic and therapeutic approach is necessary to rule out other tumours and to prevent further ocular sequelae {22088230}. Complete excision should be achieved but is often not possible. Subtotally excised lesions often remain stable {27179534} but can recur {18569821}.
