10.1.1.2 Fibrous histiocytoma

Definition

Fibrous histiocytoma is a common benign mesenchymal tumour consisting of a combination of fibroblasts, myofibroblasts, reactive dendrocytic cells, and macrophages in between collagen bundles.

ICD-O coding

8831/0 Deep fibrous histiocytoma

8832/0 Classic dermatofibroma

8832/0 Cellular dermatofibroma

ICD-11 coding

2F23.0 Dermatofibroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: dermatofibroma (skin).

Subtype(s)

Eyelid: deep fibrous histiocytoma; classic dermatofibroma; cellular dermatofibroma; aneurysmal dermatofibroma; atypical dermatofibroma

Conjunctiva: none

Localization

Fibrous histiocytoma of the ocular area is mostly located within the bulbar conjunctiva and limbus {33764259}. The next commonest site is the upper eyelid skin (dermatofibroma) {30183445}. Very rare sites include the caruncle {33764259}, lacrimal sac {9030950}, and uveal tract (choroid) {9683939}. While the orbit was thought to be a common site {6281162}, most of these cases are judged to be orbital solitary fibrous tumours {12867407;21056898}.

Clinical features

Eyelid: Most lesions occur on the upper eyelid as a painless nodule that can be white, yellow, skin coloured, or reddish {30183445}.

Conjunctiva: Most cases comprise a painless, yellowish to tan-coloured, dome-shaped mass, often lacking a feeder vessel at the base, but sometimes with vascularization over the surface {33764259;16671910;17157587;27282913;25971163}.

Epidemiology

Eyelid: The age range is 5–68 years with an equal sex distribution {30183445}.

Conjunctiva: Fibrous histiocytoma is rare, accounting for only 0.2% of all conjunctival tumours {15350332}. The age of onset ranges from infancy to the eighth decade of life (mean age: 31 years), with a male preponderance {33764259}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

In non-eyelid skin, recurrent gene fusions involving protein kinase C isoforms (PRKCA, PRKCB, and PRKCD) with various genes encoding membrane-associated proteins (e.g. LAMTOR1, PDPN, CD63, and KIRREL) have been identified in a minority of cases {26121314;24721208;30094855;26432191}. These data provide proof of the neoplastic, clonal nature of dermatofibroma (see the related section in the Skin tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Skin Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 12). https://publications.iarc.who.int/650.]]), which is lacking for conjunctival and eyelid fibrous histiocytoma.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

The usual histology is a reticular dermis–based, poorly defined, polymorphous population of cells, comprising spindled to rounded neoplastic cells with surrounding dermal dendrocytes and macrophages {20520936}. There are characteristic collagen balls in the lesion. A storiform growth pattern may be seen. The epidermis is usually hyperplastic {32767564;19955877;25727075}. Early lesions are cellular and older lesions appear more fibrotic with fewer macrophages. Lipidized tumour contains histiocytes with vacuolated cytoplasm {10770432}. Because of the relative lack of reticular dermis from eyelid skin, some cases of eyelid-skin dermatofibroma deviate from the classic appearance and show histological variance. Some cases are well defined, basophilic, and cellular, lack peripheral collagen trapping (collagen balls), lack a zone of collagen separating the tumour from the epidermis, and do not exhibit overlying epidermal hyperplasia {30183445}. These differences are thought to reflect an origin within the papillary dermis rather than from the reticular dermis {30183445}. For further details on cutaneous subtypes, refer to the related section in the Skin tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Skin Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 12). https://publications.iarc.who.int/650.]].

The histology of conjunctival and limbal fibrous histiocytomas is very similar to that described above, with classic and other morphological patterns {30893190;17157587}, with some cases being lipidized {30574484}.

Immunohistochemistry

There are no specific markers for ocular fibrous histiocytoma {27408454}. CD68 and CD163 stain in interspersed histiocytes. SMA stains in the myofibroblastic component. Factor XIIIa stains in interspersed reactive dendrocytic cells {17667121;21271031;24608257;27636240}.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis includes nodular episcleritis, juvenile xanthogranuloma, spindle cell carcinoma, and solitary fibrous tumour {17667121;21271031;1704795}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: combination of fibroblasts, myofibroblasts, reactive dendrocytic cells, and macrophages in between collagen bundles.

Desirable: supportive immunohistochemical data.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision is usually curative, with no recurrence {33764259;24608257;2838836;17157587;27636240}.
