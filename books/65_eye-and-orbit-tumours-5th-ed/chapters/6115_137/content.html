6.1.1.5 Intraductal carcinoma

Definition

Intraductal carcinoma (IDC) is a biphasic carcinoma characterized by round tumour lobules with variable architecture and low-grade cytology that are surrounded by a single continuous layer of myoepithelial cells

ICD-O coding

8500/2 Intraductal carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2E6A.Y & XH1H31 Carcinoma in situ of other and unspecified part of the eye and adnexa & Intraductal carcinoma, noninfiltrating, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: intercalated duct carcinoma; low-grade intraductal carcinoma.

Not recommended: low-grade cribriform cystadenocarcinoma; low-grade salivary duct carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

One case has been described in the lacrimal gland {24195727}. Most cases occur in the major salivary glands {15252310;29443014} and oral cavity {6298331;15043319}.

Clinical features

The lacrimal tumour caused proptosis with inferomedial globe displacement {24195727}.

Epidemiology

This is a rare type of lacrimal carcinoma with only a single case described so far {24195727}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The single lacrimal case was not analysed for molecular changes. Intraductal carcinoma of the salivary glands tends to harbour NCOA4::RET and TRIM33::RET fusions {29574881;30045065;31162284}. RET gene rearrangement was shown to be present in both luminal and myoepithelial cells, suggesting both components are neoplastic {33086236}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not reported

Histopathology

Unencapsulated, well-circumscribed carcinoma consists of round lobules with variable solid, papillary-cystic, cribriform, and micropapillary architecture, surrounded by a single, continuous, flat layer of myoepithelial cells. The ductal cells are large and polygonal with abundant amphiphilic or oncocytic cytoplasm and mild nuclear atypia. Small nucleoli and intranuclear inclusions may be seen. Eosinophilic secretions fill the cystic spaces. The surrounding stroma may be densely fibrotic and contain lymphocytic infiltrate. Intercalated duct, oncocytic, and apocrine subtypes have been described in the salivary IDCs. The 1 lacrimal case had intercalated morphology {24195727}.

Immunohistochemistry

Intraductal cells of the intercalated and oncocytic types are positive for CK19, S100, SOX10, and mammaglobin, and negative for AR and GCDFP-15. The opposite staining pattern is seen in apocrine IDCs. Myoepithelial cells are positive for p63, p40, CK5, CK14, calponin, SMA, and SMMHC.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of RET gene rearrangement may help with diagnosis; however, break-apart FISH is often falsely negative in IDC with NCOA4::RET fusion owing to a tight inversion {29443014;30045065;33086236}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: round lobules of ductal cells surrounded entirely by a single layer of myoepithelial cells.

Desirable: presence of RET gene rearrangement.

Staging

IDC is currently regarded as in situ disease by Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) staging of invasive carcinoma ex IDC, according to the UICC TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

The single lacrimal IDC did not recur in the 6 years after complete excision {24195727}.
