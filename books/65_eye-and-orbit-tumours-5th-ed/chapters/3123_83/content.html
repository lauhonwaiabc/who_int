3.1.2.3 Iris naevus

Definition

Iris naevus is a benign usually pigmented neoplasm that arises from melanocytes of the iris stroma.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Iris naevus

8720/0 Circumscribed iris naevus

8720/1 Diffuse iris naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F36.1 & XH4L78 Benign neoplasm of iris & Pigmented naevus, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Circumscribed iris naevus; diffuse iris naevus

Localization

Most iris naevi are located in the lower half of the iris and tend to be round and small, typically about 3 mm in diameter. Less commonly naevi are located close to the pupillary margin or they are larger, more elongated, or triangular in shape, and in a small number of patients they may be located in the upper half of the iris.

Clinical features

An iris naevus appears as a pigmented or non-pigmented spot or nodule on the iris. The mean age at diagnosis is 51 years {22035581}. They can be flat or elevated, may be associated with a cyst, and penetrate deeper into the iris stroma than a freckle would. They fairly often cause uveal ectropion (dragging of the pigmented iris epithelium towards the anterior iris surface) or corectopia. They can be classified into diffuse, sectoral, incomplete sectoral, and solitary in extent {25359901}. Circumscribed iris naevi are discrete and often nodular. Diffuse iris naevi involve an entire sector or (rarely) the entire iris. Magnocellular naevus, or melanocytoma, is described in a separate chapter.

Epidemiology

Iris naevi show a predilection for White people and become apparent during puberty or young adulthood {14693769;22035581} without significant sex predilection.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown but may be multifactorial; sunlight exposure may play a role {16401785}.

Pathogenesis

It has been postulated that the formation of iris naevi depends on a time-related embryogenetic alteration of the location, migration, and maturation of normal melanocytes along peripheral nerves, explaining the morphology and extension of benign melanocytic iris lesions {27002320;25359901;2400347}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of an iris naevus is generally a brown lesion if pigmented, or a light-beige well-circumscribed lesion if non-pigmented.

Histopathology

Iris naevus cells range from slender spindle to rounded polygonal cells. The nucleolar size of iris naevi is smaller than that of iris melanomas. Differential diagnosis with an iris melanoma can still be challenging {1305027}. If epithelioid cells predominate, an iris melanoma should be suspected {3977695}.

Immunohistochemistry

A naevus will show rare cycling cells with Ki-67 expression; however, a low-grade iris melanoma may show almost similar findings. Melan-A, S100, and SOX10 immunohistochemistry is positive; HMB45 is usually only focally positive. Nuclear BAP1 is retained; somatic and germline mutations of this gene are frequently found in uveal melanoma, leading to loss of nuclear immunostaining.

Differential diagnosis

This includes metastasis, cysts, and non-melanocytic lesions such as leiomyoma, xanthogranuloma, and neurofibroma {22035581}.

Cytology

FNABs of iris naevi are paucicellular and show predominantly small spindle to round cells with or without pigmentation and with small or absent nucleoli. If a melanocytoma, the cells show prominent intracellular pigmentation with centrally located round nuclei and indistinct nucleoli.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular genetic studies of iris naevi have identified mutations in GNAQ and GNA11 as well as in NRAS, PTEN, KIT, and TP53 {28700778;29371009}. Some iris naevi harbour BAP1 and EIF1AX mutations with or without monosomy 3; these tumours are designated iris melanocytic tumours of uncertain malignant potential {29371009;30318043}. PRAME has been identified as an independent prognostic biomarker in uveal melanoma that identifies gene expression profile class 1 or disomy 3 tumours with increased risk of metastasis. Although PRAME has not been validated for iris melanocytic naevi, it might further increase the accuracy of prognostic testing {26933176;27486988}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cellular, bland melanocytic lesion clinically originating from, or histopathologically mainly located in, the iris.

Desirable: presence of spindle type A melanocytes; immunopositivity for melanocytic markers; retained nuclear BAP1 expression.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Most naevi show no growth, or very slow growth, over the years. The prognosis for vision and life is excellent. The risk of malignant transformation of an iris naevus to melanoma is < 2%. Diffuse iris naevus is statistically associated with a higher risk of transformation into iris melanoma as compared with a circumscribed naevus {23290981}. Periodic examinations with photographic documentation and ultrasound biomicroscopy are recommended to monitor any changes in size or other features that might suggest progression to iris melanoma. This evolution theory is supported by the observation that borderline malignant iris naevi have been reported to harbour mutations that predict clinical and histopathological borderline malignant status {29371009;23290981;14693769}. Clinical factors predictive of iris naevus growth into melanoma are summarized in the mnemonic ABCDEF: A, age < 40 years; B, blood (hyphaema episodes); C, clock-hour inferior; D, diffuse configuration; E, ectropion uveae; and F, feathery margin {23290981}.
