10.2.2.1 Chondroma

Definition

Orbital chondroma is a benign chondrocytic neoplasm, producing hyaline or myxoid cartilage, and arising periosteally or in orbital soft tissue.

ICD-O coding

9220/0 Chondroma

ICD-11 coding

2E89.1 & XH0NS4 Benign tumours of uncertain differentiation, soft tissue & Chondroma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: chondroma of soft parts; extraskeletal chondroma; periosteal chondroma.

Not recommended: juxtacortical chondroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Orbital chondromas arise in the superomedial orbit in the vicinity of the trochlea, which is the only cartilaginous structure found normally in the orbit {5981161;26265654;17117112;17446102;1578865}.

Clinical features

Tumours are solitary and present as a painless firm mass in the superomedial orbit. Ptosis and restrictive strabismus have been reported {17446102;17117112;26265654}.

Epidemiology

Orbital chondroma is extremely rare, accounting for 1 of 645 orbital masses in one survey {6497741}. Of 3 reported cases in the English-language literature, all included male patients, aged 9–27 years {26265654;17117112;17446102}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Genetic analysis of orbital chondroma has not been performed. Location in the region of the trochlea suggests an origin from residual chondroid progenitor cells in this area.

Cytogenetic analyses of limited numbers of soft tissue chondromas of other sites reveal variable aberrations, including rearrangements of 12q13-q15 and trisomy 5. In a set of tumours with rearrangements of 12q13-q15, a truncated or full-length HMGA2 transcript was found {8402563;10748295;11793371;14614053;25250651}. In about 50% of the cases, FN1 gene rearrangements were reported, with FGFR1 and FGFR2 as fusion partners {31273315}. Periosteal chondromas may harbour mutations in IDH1 {21598255}.

Macroscopic appearance

Orbital chondroma is a 10–20 mm, well-circumscribed, nodular, solid greyish-bluish-white mass. Myxoid/cystic change may be present.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of lobules of well-formed hyaline and/or myxoid cartilage delineated by fibrous septa. Chondrocytes are arranged in clusters and reside within lacunar spaces or float in a flocculent myxoid matrix. The nuclei are usually small, round, and dark, but may be enlarged with fine or coarse chromatin and small nucleoli, and exhibit mild pleomorphism. Coarse calcifications and endochondral ossification may be present {17446102}. Mitotic activity is absent or very low and abnormal mitotic figures are not observed. If periosteal, there should be no invasion of the underlying cortex.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: soft tissue mass composed of well-delineated cartilage nodules; matrix is hyaline or myxoid and may calcify; chondrocytes show limited atypia and no or little mitotic activity.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Marginal excision is curative. Recurrence has not been reported, although experience is limited.
