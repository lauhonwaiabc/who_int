3.2.1.2 Uveal melanocytoma

Definition

Uveal melanocytoma is a benign uveal melanocytic neoplasm, consisting of plump polygonal cells with dense intracytoplasmic melanin that obscures the nuclei, which show bland nuclear morphology after bleaching of melanin.

ICD-O coding

8726/0 Uveal melanocytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.0 Benign neoplasm of choroid

2F36.1 Benign neoplasm of iris

2F36.2 Benign neoplasm of ciliary body

Related terminology

Acceptable: magnocellular naevus of the uvea.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Melanocytomas occur more frequently in the iris {15767055;20829742} and ciliary body {10768345} than in the choroid; they are also found adjacent to the optic nerve head {20829742;16500211}.

Clinical features

Uveal melanocytoma may clinically be similar to an ordinary iris, ciliary body, or choroidal naevus, or a small to medium-sized melanoma {11786063;17621182}. Most choroidal melanocytomas form a discoid or dome-shaped dark-grey to black mass that elevates the retina. Overlying orange pigment and subretinal fluid can sometimes be seen, which makes the distinction from a choroidal melanoma difficult {19125075}. Over time, some melanocytomas may spontaneously regress in size. Those in the ciliary body may be associated with cataract, pigment dispersion, or secondary glaucoma.

Epidemiology

Melanocytomas are rare neoplasms and their true incidence in the uvea is not known. The age at diagnosis is variable, ranging from infants to age 90 years {9027679;17621182}. The frequency in both sexes and in different races is nearly equal {19125075}. Unlike uveal melanomas, melanocytomas do not show a predilection for White people.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The histogenesis is similar to that of uveal naevi. Uveal melanocytomas originate from neural-crest cells and may harbour GNAQ and (possibly) GNA11 mutations, suggesting a role in their development from normal uveal melanocytes {23685997;27239460;35835335}.

Ciliary body melanocytomas have shown a somatic GNAQ p.Q209L missense sequence variant within exon 5 {23685997;27239460;35835335}. The same GNAQ variant is typical for uveal naevi and uveal melanoma {23685997}. Mutation of GNA11, BAP1, SF3B1, and BRAF has so far not been identified {23685997;27239460;35835335}. However, only a few uveal melanocytomas have been evaluated genetically. GNA11 mutation is reported in melanocytomas in other locations {34750314}.

Macroscopic appearance

On gross examination melanocytomas appear dark brown to jet black and generally measure 1–5 mm in thickness. Ciliary body melanocytomas tend to be larger than melanocytomas in other locations before they are detected clinically.

Histopathology

Uveal melanocytomas are composed of large, polygonal cells with abundant intracytoplasmic melanin pigment that obscures cellular and nuclear details. Bleaching of melanin is required to adequately evaluate the tumour cells, which show a low N:C ratio, oval to round bland nuclei, and inconspicuous nucleoli. Although partial necrosis may be seen in larger melanocytomas, mitoses are absent and Ki-67 immunohistochemistry does not show cycling cells {11942551}, unlike in uveal melanoma and pigmented adenocarcinomas of the ciliary body. BAP1 nuclear expression is retained.

The differential diagnosis includes uveal melanomas and naevi. Adenoma and adenocarcinoma of the ciliary or retinal pigmented epithelium, although rare, should be considered. The melanocytic lineage immunohistochemical markers melan-A, SOX10, and S100 may be useful in challenging cases to rule out pigmented epithelial tumours.

Cytology

Diagnostic FNAB of melanocytomas shows large, ovoid cells with small centrally located nuclei and heavily pigmented cytoplasm {18323281}. The cells are immunoreactive for melanocytic markers and show rare or no cells in cycle with Ki-67 immunostaining.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a deeply pigmented uveal melanocytic tumour with polygonal cells, abundant cytoplasm, small centrally located nuclei, and few indistinct nucleoli.

Desirable: bleaching of melanin and immunohistochemical confirmation of melanocytic lineage with absence of cytokeratin expression.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for life is excellent, although in rare instances malignant transformation to uveal melanoma can occur {1078332;8010703;23685997;15965181}. BAP1 mutation was demonstrated in 2 cases of optic nerve melanocytoma with malignant transformation, with 1 of them also harbouring a GNAQ mutation. This suggests that mutations in BAP1 may accompany malignant transformation in ocular melanocytomas {27239460}.
