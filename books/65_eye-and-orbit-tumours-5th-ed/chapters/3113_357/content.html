3.1.1.3 Pigmented epithelial cyst

Definition

Pigmented epithelial cysts arise from the posterior pigment epithelium of the iris.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A91 Cyst of iris, ciliary body or anterior chamber

9A82 Cyst in the anterior chamber of the eye

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The cysts are most common in the peripheral iris but also can be free-floating in the anterior chamber or vitreous cavities {19702716}.

Clinical features

Pigmented epithelial cysts are single or multiple dark-brown lesions that bulge the iris stroma anteriorly and can be detected after mydriasis or via ultrasound biomicroscopy or anterior segment optical coherence tomography at the posterior iris. Drug-associated cysts are smaller and located at the pupillary margin {7070779}.

The clinical differential diagnosis includes: uveitis-associated cysts that arise from interepithelial fluid accumulation in the iris and ciliary body {8976700}, cystic benign naevi, cystic uveal melanoma {6888862}, metastases {28882598}, intraocular foreign body {19702716}, and intraocular parasites {28882598}.

Epidemiology

These cysts tend to occur in young adults, with one third of cases diagnosed in the third or fourth decade of life {28161919}.

Etiology

Pigmented epithelial cysts are idiopathic, but some cases are secondary to longstanding use of miotic or PGF2α-analogue eye drops {28882598;7070779}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of medication-related iris cysts is unclear. In contrast to the other cysts, they resolve after discontinuation of the drug {28882598}.

Macroscopic appearance

Dark-brown cysts

Histopathology

Pigmented cysts are lined by a monolayer of iris pigmented epithelial cells, which can be columnar or cuboidal.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cyst lined by bland cuboidal or columnar posterior iris pigmented epithelium.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

An enlarging cyst might cause visual deterioration and precipitate angle-closure glaucoma. Complete excision is curative.
