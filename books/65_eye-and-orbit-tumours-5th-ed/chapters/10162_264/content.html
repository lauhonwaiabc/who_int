10.1.6.2 Infantile haemangioma

Definition

Infantile haemangioma (IH) is a benign vascular tumour characterized by a rapid proliferative phase during infancy followed by gradual involution.

ICD-O coding

9131/0 Infantile haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.2Y & XH3U29 Other specified benign vascular neoplasms of infancy or childhood & Capillary haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: capillary haemangioma; juvenile haemangioma; strawberry naevus; capillary-cavernous haemangioma; infantile haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

IH most commonly involves the skin of the upper eyelid / orbit and less frequently the conjunctiva, sclera, and iris {28540013;21788081;34632621;22486338}.

Clinical features

IH appears soon after birth as a distinct or diffuse red mass and undergoes rapid growth (up to 2 years), with most tumours involuting by 48 months {21788081;33069287}. Approximately 20% of children have additional non-periocular haemangiomas {22776927}.

Epidemiology

IHs have a female predilection and more common in White populations and premature infants {22776927}. This lesion accounts for 1–5% of all ocular adnexal tumours {15121380;27725148;30450316} [[Heegaard S, Grossniklaus H, editors. Eye Pathology: An Illustrated Guide. Heidelberg (Germany): Springer; 2014.]], 40–80% of all vascular eyelid tumours {26028084;30450316}, and 15–20% of vascular conjunctival tumours {21788081;27725148}.

Etiology

The etiology of IH is not well understood. Germline genetic variants and familial clustering have been reported as risk factors {27940781}.

Pathogenesis

IH is caused by the uncontrolled proliferation of clonally expanded vascular endothelial cells {11254674}. Causative genes have not been described. Hypoxia has been postulated to play a role in the pathogenesis of IH, supported by the demonstration of GLUT1, IGF2, VEGF-A, and HIF1α in endothelial cells {26940670;12032304;21079070}. Immunophenotypic similarity to placental microvessels has raised a theory that IH may result from embolization of placental cells or stem cells differentiating towards placental vascular cells {11346333}.

Macroscopic appearance

IH usually occurs as a lobulated vascular-appearing mass.

Histopathology

IH in the proliferative stage is composed of capillary-sized vascular channels lined by plump endothelial cells intermixed with pericytes, arranged in lobules around larger vessels. In the regressive stages, the lobules are progressively replaced by fibrosis.

Immunohistochemistry

Endothelial cells express GLUT1, CD31, ERG, and CD34 and lack staining with D2-40 {24355066}.

Differential diagnosis

Congenital non-progressive haemangiomas and lobular capillary haemangioma (see related section) are distinguished from IH based on clinicopathological correlation and GLUT1 immunohistochemistry.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: proliferative stage: lobules of capillaries each of which is surrounded by a layer of pericytes; regressive stage: fibrous tissue replacing capillary lobules.

Desirable: endothelial cells are GLUT1-positive.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

IH involutes spontaneously; some lesions can be associated with amblyopia from compression of the eyeball and obscuration of vision. Eyelid lesions respond to oral and topical propranolol {22967870;21810027}.
