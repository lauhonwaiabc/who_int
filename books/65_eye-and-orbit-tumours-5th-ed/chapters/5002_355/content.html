5.0.0.2 Optic nerve choristoma

Definition

Optic nerve choristoma is a rare, benign, and probably developmental/malformative choristomatous lesion composed of smooth muscle and adipose tissue within and around the optic nerve.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA13.7Y Other specified congenital malformation of optic disc

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is equal left:right optic nerve involvement. The location within the nerve may be intracanalicular, intraorbital, and intracranial involving the optic chiasm {11987274}.

Clinical features

There is a painless, slow, progressive decrease in visual acuity, associated with optic atrophy. In rare cases, the visual loss may be rapid and progressive. MRI shows enlargement of the nerve and hyperintense signal that disappears with fat suppression, suggestive of a fat-containing lesion. Differential diagnostic considerations may include optic nerve glioma, optic nerve meningioma, lipoma, an inflammatory process, and a secondary process involving the optic nerve {28774539}.

Epidemiology

Rare single case reports in the literature show an approximately equal sex distribution and an age range from 9 to 63 years.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

This is believed to be a developmental malformation with heterotopic smooth muscle and adipose tissue within and around the optic nerve. Optic disc colobomas may be associated with heterotopic adipose tissue and/or smooth muscle {6847467}. A rare case showed mixed features of both optic nerve coloboma and morning glory anomaly.

Macroscopic appearance

Optic nerve choristoma may appear as a yellow/tan mass.

Histopathology

Histology shows adipose tissue and bundles of smooth muscle in variable amounts within or adjacent to atrophic optic nerve tissue. Immunohistochemistry for SMA highlights the smooth muscle component, and GFAP staining highlights optic nerve tissue {11987274;15806516;28774539;29755281}.

Cytology

Limited experience

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: variable amounts of adipose tissue and smooth muscle.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

There is slow growth, with benign, progressive visual loss.
