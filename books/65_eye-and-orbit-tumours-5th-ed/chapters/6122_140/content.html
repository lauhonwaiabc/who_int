6.1.2.2 Primary ductal adenocarcinoma of the lacrimal gland

Definition

Primary ductal adenocarcinoma (PDA) of the lacrimal gland is a rare high-grade adenocarcinoma and a counterpart of salivary duct carcinoma, resembling invasive ductal carcinoma of the breast.

ICD-O coding

8500/3 Primary ductal adenocarcinoma of the lacrimal gland

ICD-11 coding

2D03.0 & XH7KH3 Adenocarcinoma of the lacrimal apparatus & Infiltrating duct carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: unclassified adenocarcinoma; ductal carcinoma/adenocarcinoma; lacrimal duct carcinoma.

Not recommended: cystadenocarcinoma; mucinous cystadenocarcinoma; intestinal-type adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PDA arises throughout the main lacrimal gland and (rarely) in accessory lacrimal glands.

Clinical features

PDA of the lacrimal gland presents with painless proptosis (31%), palpable mass (25%), swelling (22%), diplopia (16%), mechanical ptosis (15%), pain (13%), and decreased vision (12%) {31837385}. The average duration of symptoms is 25 months. Left and right lacrimal glands are involved equally, without any bilateral cases reported to date. The average size of the tumours ranges from 11 to 50 mm, with a mean diameter of 30 mm.

Orbital imaging (CT, MRI) features include an irregularly shaped, heterogeneous, enhancing mass in contrast T1-weighted MRI. Bony erosions, muscle involvement, and calcifications may be present.

Epidemiology

PDA is rare, accounting for only 2% of all epithelial lacrimal gland tumours {19667339}. The exact incidence is not known, with < 40 cases reported to date {31837385;33186791;35030152}. It appears to occur more commonly in men than women (M:F ratio: 3:1), with an average age at diagnosis of 58 years (range: 34–78 years) {23613093;26669339;31837385}. Interestingly, this predominance in men is greater than that seen in the salivary counterpart. It remains unclear whether this is a true trend, or whether women are underrepresented owing to misdiagnosis of these tumours as metastatic ductal adenocarcinoma of the breast.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of this tumour is currently unknown, although at least a subset of cases show ERBB2 (HER2) overexpression by immunohistochemistry and/or ERBB2 gene amplification similar to that seen in salivary duct carcinoma {27808478;23613093;25999735;31837385}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour appears as a poorly defined mass surrounding the lacrimal gland and within the soft tissue.

Histopathology

Tumour cells are polygonal with granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, pleomorphic and vesicular nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and mitoses. The cells are arranged in ductal structures of varying patterns (glandular, cribriform, and solid) with frequent central comedonecrosis resembling intraductal adenocarcinoma of the breast and salivary gland {31837385}; extracellular mucin may also be present {16271319}. Perineural, vascular, lymphatic, and bony invasion have been reported, all in approximately 25% of cases {31837385}. Dense hyalinization and sclerosis with interspersed myoepithelial cells favour an adenocarcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma over a PDA.

Positive immunohistochemical markers include markers for epithelia (AE1/AE3, CK7, CK19, EMA), apocrine differentiation (AR, GCDFP-15), and cell growth (ERBB2). ER, PR, and PSA are usually negative, and the Ki-67 proliferation index may reach 70% {27808478;23613093;25999735}. Expression of p53 and cyclin D1 is also reported {25999735;23613093;33186791}. Myoepithelial markers including SMA, p63, and calponin are negative in most of the cases.

Differential diagnoses are adenocarcinoma NOS, adenocarcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma, and mucoepidermoid carcinoma.

Cytology

Individual tumour cells display moderate to marked pleomorphism, contain round nuclei with prominent nucleoli and abundant eosinophilic/granular cytoplasm, and form gland-like structures in a necrotic background.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Amplification of ERBB2, which is present in approximately 25% of salivary duct carcinomas, has also been reported in lacrimal duct carcinoma {27808478}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an invasive adenocarcinoma with ductal differentiation, arising de novo (not arising from a benign tumour such as pleomorphic adenoma) in the lacrimal gland.

Desirable: tumour comedonecrosis.

Staging

PDA of the lacrimal gland is staged according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM classification for carcinoma of the lacrimal gland.

Prognosis and prediction

The reported 5-year survival rate of patients with PDA is 44% (indicating a more aggressive course than that of adenoid cystic carcinoma of the lacrimal gland, but a less-aggressive course than that of salivary duct carcinoma with a 5-year mortality rate of 60–80%) {23613093;26669339;31837385}. Based on a large case series with literature review, lymph node and distant metastases are seen in 24% and 50% of patients, respectively. Treatments include complete excision, lymph node dissection, orbital bone resection, and radiotherapy {15807311;26669339}. Poor prognosis for PDA may be associated with overexpression of ERBB2 and p53 {23613093}. GCDFP-15, AR, and ERBB2 have been proposed as biomarkers for treatment guidance and prognosis in PDA {25999735}.
