10.1.8.3 Synovial sarcoma

Definition

Synovial sarcoma (SS) is a spindle cell mesenchymal neoplasm showing variable epithelial differentiation and characterized by a specific SS18::SSX fusion gene.

ICD-O coding

9040/3 Synovial sarcoma, NOS

9041/3 Monophasic synovial sarcoma

9043/3 Biphasic synovial sarcoma

9040/3 Poorly differentiated synovial sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5A.Y Synovial sarcoma, other specified primary site

XH9B22 Synovial sarcoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: synovioma; synovial cell sarcoma; synoviosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Monophasic synovial sarcoma; biphasic synovial sarcoma; poorly differentiated synovial sarcoma

Localization

Most SS cases (73%) arise in the extremities and 7% in the head and neck {32618221}. Primary ocular adnexal SS has been described in the epibulbar region, anterior orbit, and intraconal space, where the tumour is frequently adherent to extraocular muscles. Bone destruction is uncommon {27697479}. Secondary involvement of the orbit by metastatic SS can occur rarely {22743696}.

Clinical features

SS can present insidiously with pain, proptosis, periorbital swelling, and diplopia {33587418;27697479}. Imaging generally reveals a circumscribed mass with occasional calcification {27697479}.

Epidemiology

Overall, SS has an even sex distribution and can occur at any age. Over 50% of cases present in adolescents and young adults {32618221}. Ocular adnexal SS is rare, has a female predilection, and occurs at a mean age of 25 years {33587418}.

Etiology

There are no known predisposing factors, although rarely SS has been reported after radiotherapy {29336183}.

Pathogenesis

In nearly all cases, SS bears a unique chromosomal t(X;18)(p11;q11) translocation, in which SS18 on chromosome 18 is fused to an SSX gene on the X chromosome, resulting in the oncogenic SS18::SSX fusion gene {11107170;26947017;29202462}. The fusion protein may block mesenchymal differentiation by various epigenetic mechanisms, which include binding and displacing native SS18 in the SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex {23540691}. Macroscopic appearance

SS demonstrates a yellow-tan to grey cut surface with occasional foci of necrosis, calcification, and haemorrhage {32800827}.

Histopathology

There are three main histological patterns of SS: monophasic (most common), biphasic (as many as one third of cases), and poorly differentiated.

Monophasic SS is composed of uniform spindle cells with ovoid hyperchromatic, frequently overlapping nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, and sparse cytoplasm, arranged in cellular sheets and fascicles. There may be areas of dense fibrosis and myxoid change. A staghorn-shaped vascular pattern may be observed. Foci of calcification and/or ossification are identified in as many as one third of SS cases {25438927}.

Biphasic SS contains epithelial and spindle-cell components in varying proportions. The epithelial cells of SS have ovoid vesicular nuclei with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and occasional keratinization, and are arranged in nests and cords {25438927}.

Poorly differentiated SS can feature small round hyperchromatic cells, reminiscent of Ewing family tumours, or fascicular spindle cells, reminiscent of malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour {25438927}. Severe nuclear atypia, necrosis, and brisk mitotic activity can be seen {15364967}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry with the SS18::SSX fusion–specific antibody and the C-terminus SSX-specific antibody shows strong diffuse nuclear staining in SS with high (> 95%) sensitivity and specificity {32141887}. TLE1 shows diffuse nuclear staining in as many as 95% of SS cases, but can be expressed in histological mimics, including malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour and solitary fibrous tumour. Most SS lesions express CD99 and BCL2. Epithelial cells in biphasic SS express EMA and cytokeratins. S100 and SMA are variably expressed {12409719}. SMARCB1 (INI1) and H3 p.K27me3 may be partially or completely lost in a subset of SS cases {20305614;32212796}.

Cytology

SS may show clusters of and single round, oval, and spindle cells with pericapillary arrangement and occasionally pink background stroma. Glandular formation or epithelial components may be seen in biphasic SS {31161693}. Conclusive diagnosis requires molecular confirmation {23444168}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Approximately two thirds of SS lesions carry an SS18::SSX1 fusion, one third carry SS18::SSX2, and a few carry SS18::SSX4 {11107170;17334349}. SSX1 fusions with SS18L1, EWSR1, MN1, and NEDD4 are rare {34504309;31595587}. Detection of SS18::SSX fusion by either molecular assay or immunohistochemistry should be performed in all SS cases when possible {26970672}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: monomorphic small blue / spindle cell sarcoma showing variable epithelial/glandular differentiation; diffuse, strong nuclear immunoreactivity for TLE1.

Desirable: diffuse, strong nuclear immunoreactivity for SS18::SSX fusion protein OR demonstration of an SS18::SSX gene fusion.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

The overall survival for patients with SS is 138 months, with 5-, 10-, and 20-year survival rates of 59%, 51%, and 43%, respectively {32618221}. SS can metastasize to lungs, bone, and regional lymph nodes. Unfavourable prognostic factors include higher tumour stage at the time of presentation, size > 50 mm, higher French Fédération Nationale des Centres de Lutte Contre le Cancer (FNCLCC) grade, non-extremity site, and older age {15364967;18997619;32618221}. The specific SS18 partner does not appear to significantly alter prognosis {11782370;15364967}.
