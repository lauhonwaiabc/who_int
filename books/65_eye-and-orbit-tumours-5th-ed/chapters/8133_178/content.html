8.1.3.3 Yolk sac tumour

Definition

Yolk sac tumour (YST) is a malignant germ cell neoplasm with variable histological patterns reflecting stages in the phylogenetic development of extraembryonic membranes such as the allantois and the yolk sac.

ICD-O coding

9071/3 Yolk sac tumour

ICD-11 coding

2D04 & XH09W7 Malignant neoplasm of orbit & Yolk sac tumour

Related terminology

Not recommended: endodermal sinus tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

YST occurs in the orbit, most often in the extraconal space, with possible extension into the nasopharynx, paranasal sinuses, or cranium {26481249}.

Clinical features

Orbital YST usually presents in early childhood with an average presentation age of 27 months {26481249;18219360;32594074;35258404}. One orbital YST arose at age 14 years {22096462}, but all others arose at ≤ 4 years of age. The M:F ratio is 1:1.6 (7 male patients vs 11 female patients). Common clinical features include progressive proptosis, ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, vision loss, relative afferent pupillary defect, exposure keratopathy, and inflammatory symptoms and signs {26481249}. Orbital YST may be well delineated or infiltrative on CT or MRI {26481249}. Serum AFP was elevated in the cases where it was measured {12426694;18030121;35258404;32594074}.

Epidemiology

Orbital YST is rare, with only 19 reported cases {26481249;18219360;32594074;35258404}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Orbital YST is postulated to arise from ectopic extraembryonic germ cells or defective embryogenesis {26481249;8066543}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not available

Histopathology

Orbital YSTs feature a meshwork of spaces lined by flat to cuboidal epithelium with marked cellular anaplasia and conspicuous mitoses {6677841}. There may be a microcystic, macrocystic, reticular, alveolar-glandular, or myxomatous pattern {26481249}. Schiller–Duval bodies (fibrovascular cores covered by a layer of embryonic epithelium) may be present or absent {26481249}. The stroma and tumour cells contain hyaline globules that are PAS-positive and diastase resistant {6677841;26481249}. There is strong cytoplasmic staining for AFP, CAM5.2 {26481249}, and glypican-3 (GPC3) {32594074}, and nuclear expression of SALL4 {32594074}, similarly to other extragonadal YSTs {19574883}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant / not described for orbital YSTs

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: primitive germ cell tumour exhibiting stages in the phylogenetic development of extraembryonic membranes.

Desirable: positive immunohistochemical staining for AFP, GPC3, or SALL4.

Staging

Not clinically relevant / not described for orbital YSTs

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic and management guidelines are not available for primary orbital YST. Proptosis allowing early tumour detection may result in a better prognosis for orbital YST than other extragonadal YSTs {6677841}. Chemotherapy and radiotherapy may provide long-term disease-free survival {6677841;18835732}.
