10.2.1.1 Osteoma

Definition

Osteoma is a benign tumour arising on the surface of bone and is composed primarily of lamellar mature cortical-type bone. The term “bone island” is used when an osteoma develops within the medullary cavity.

ICD-O coding

9180/0 Osteoma

ICD-11 coding

2E83.Z & XH4818 Benign osteogenic tumours of unspecified site & Osteoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: ivory exostosis; parosteal osteoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Osteomas predominantly affect craniofacial bones formed by membranous ossification {18154576}.

Clinical features

Osteomas are usually slow growing and asymptomatic {18154576}. On radiological imaging, osteomas are round, dense, well-defined ivory-like lesions which are attached to the underlying bone without cortical invasion. The presence of multiple osteomas suggests the diagnosis of Gardner syndrome {8956591}, which is a subtype of familial adenomatous polyposis.

Epidemiology

Since osteomas commonly represent incidental findings, their actual prevalence is difficult to determine but has been reported to reach 6.4% in select case series {25719573}.

Etiology

The etiology of sporadic cases is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Most osteomas develop sporadically and a small subset of cases is associated with Gardner syndrome, a phenotypic variant of familial adenomatous polyposis caused by mutations in the APC gene resulting in aberrant activation of the WNT/β-catenin signalling pathway. In 61.1% of sporadic osteomas, hotspot mutations in the CTNNB1 gene (encoding β-catenin) were found {34725446}.

Macroscopic appearance

Osteomas arise on the surface of the bone and are typically well-circumscribed tumours with a broad attachment to the underlying bone.

Histopathology

Osteomas are predominantly composed of mature lamellar/cortical-type bone and can be divided histologically into compact and (less frequent) spongious subtypes.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; tumour arises on the surface of bone or within the medullary cavity; tumour composed of lamellar/cortical-type bone.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Asymptomatic cases generally do not require treatment and follow an indolent clinical course; symptomatic cases can be conservatively excised.
