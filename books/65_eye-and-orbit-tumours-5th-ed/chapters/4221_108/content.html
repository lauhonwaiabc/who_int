4.2.2.1 Adenoma and adenocarcinoma of the retinal pigment epithelium

Definition

Adenomas and adenocarcinomas of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) are benign and malignant neoplasms derived from the RPE.

ICD-O coding

8140/0 Retinal pigment epithelial adenoma

8140/3 Retinal pigment epithelial adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH3DV3 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Adenoma, NOS

2D02.0 Adenocarcinoma of retinal pigment epithelium

Related terminology

Acceptable: epithelioma of the retinal pigment epithelium; retinal pigment epithelial carcinoma; malignant epithelioma of the retinal pigment epithelium.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RPE adenomas typically affect the peripheral retina but may occur adjacent to the optic disc {14246189;1454346}. RPE adenocarcinomas have no predilection for laterality or retinal location.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with blurred vision, but can be asymptomatic. Choroidal melanoma is a common referring diagnosis. RPE neoplasms are solitary, unilateral tumours located on the inner surface of the choroid. They typically are oval and dome shaped with an abrupt vertical elevation at the edge of the tumour and a derby-hat configuration. They are 6 mm in mean basal diameter and 3.5 mm in thickness. Although adenomas and adenocarcinomas of the RPE typically are dark grey or jet black in colour, about 8% are amelanotic or yellow. Perforation of the overlying retina occurs frequently, and many cases develop a retinal blood supply with dilated and tortuous retinal feeder arteries and draining veins. In contrast to uveal melanoma, more than half of RPE neoplasms often have prominent yellow intraretinal and subretinal exudates, and 22% have exudative retinal detachments {32417354;28399346}. B-scan ultrasonography discloses mostly dome-shaped or derby hat–shaped lesions that are acoustically solid and lack extraocular extension or choroidal excavation. They often are hypofluorescent in the early stages of intravenous fluorescein angiography, are isofluorescent or hyperfluorescent in the mid/late phases with leakage or staining, and also lack the double circulation pattern of internal vascularity found in many melanomas. Optical coherence tomography of RPE adenocarcinomas typically shows retinal invasion and posterior shadowing, high anterior reflectivity with an irregular or rugged surface and occasional vitreous seeds. Some tumours appear to contain optically empty cavities {32417354;33981695}.

Epidemiology

These tumours occur in adults. The mean patient age at diagnosis is 51 years (range: 18–83 years) {32417354}. There is no race predilection {10326956;28399346}. Two thirds of cases occur in women {32417354}.

Etiology

These are acquired neuroepithelial neoplasms that arise from the RPE. About one fifth are associated with congenital hypertrophy of the RPE. Several RPE adenocarcinomas that have arisen from CHRPE have been confirmed histopathologically {32417354;19744732;11296028;15983818}.

Pathogenesis

Some cases may develop from reactive RPE hyperplasia in blind disorganized eyes after trauma or inflammation {28399346}. About 12% of patients have a history of ocular trauma, a potential nidus for RPE hyperplasia {32417354}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most RPE tumours appear as heavily pigmented oval and dome-shaped lesions with abruptly elevated margins situated on the inner surface of the choroid. Exudative retinal detachment, lipid exudation, retinal invasion with perforation, and vitreous seeding often are present.

Histopathology

Contiguous with the adjacent RPE, RPE tumours are situated on the inner surface of the Bruch membrane. The location on the inner surface of the choroid is one of the most important features that serve to differentiate RPE tumours from choroidal melanoma on routine histopathological examination. Invasion, and often perforation, of the overlying retina occurs in all cases and vitreous seeding may be present. Much of the tumour is often situated anterior to the plane of the surrounding retina. Choroidal invasion occurs in some cases and is evidence of aggressive behaviour. It is one of the histological criteria for malignancy, which also include nuclear atypia and mitotic activity, which is minimal or absent in most cases.

RPE tumours may be solid, vacuolated, or combined {4111985;11782230}. Tumours arising from the peripheral RPE often have vacuolated cells similar to those in pigmented ciliary epithelial tumours {29344497}. The cells of posterior RPE tumours typically are arranged in linear arrays or strands on prominent PAS-positive connective tissue septa. The tumour cells also can form tubules, lobules, or pseudoglandular structures. The cells of RPE adenocarcinoma often are quite atypical with nuclear pleomorphism and prominent nucleoli and are easily confused with epithelioid melanoma cells, especially on FNAB. Mitotic activity typically is low. Foci of heavily pigmented RPE cells consistent with residual CHRPE are observed at the base of some tumours {19744732}. Transmission electron microscopy discloses intercellular junctions in RPE tumours {29344497;4111985}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunoreactivity is variable and somewhat nonspecific. RPE tumours typically show variable immunoreactivity for the epithelial markers CK7, AE1/AE3, CAM 5.2, and EMA as well as vimentin, but also may stain for melanocytic markers that are expressed by uveal melanoma. In one large series of RPE neoplasms, 100% stained with S100, 44% with melan-A (MART1), and 22% with HMB45 {32417354}.

The commonly used melanocytic marker SOX10 stains both melanocytes and RPE cells. Although staining for epithelial markers is suggestive, uveal melanomas occasionally are immunoreactive for cytokeratin {9484713}.

Considering this significant immunohistochemical overlap between RPE tumours and uveal melanoma, care should be taken in interpreting immunohistochemical reaction of pigmented intraocular tumours. Routine histopathological features including tumour location on the inner surface of the choroid, a characteristic linear arrangement of cells on PAS-positive septa, and scant mitotic activity should be the major diagnostic criteria for diagnosis of RPE tumours, not immunohistochemistry.

Differential diagnosis

Differentiating RPE adenomas from adenocarcinomas generally is impossible clinically and is often challenging histopathologically. Histopathological features that favour adenocarcinoma include cellular pleomorphism and nuclear atypia, mitotic activity, choroidal invasion and other evidence of aggressive behaviour.

Cytology

Cytological assessment can be difficult. Adenocarcinoma cells and epithelioid melanoma cells can appear similar and there is significant immunohistochemical overlap between RPE tumours and uveal melanoma {24993572}. It is very important to correlate the cytological findings with clinical and imaging findings.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tumour location on the inner surface of the choroid; characteristic linear arrangement of cells on septa; scant mitotic activity.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis for life is excellent if the tumour has not spread extraocularly. There are very few documented cases of metastatic RPE carcinoma {33981695;18303161;28126270}. Poor vision and enucleation are associated with larger thicker tumours and exudative retinal detachment {32417354}.
