2.1.1.1 Ductal hidrocystoma

Definition

Hidrocystomas are benign, sweat gland–derived cysts lined by a dual layer of cuboidal to columnar eosinophilic cells with variable decapitation secretions.

ICD-O coding

8404/0 Ductal hidrocystoma

8404/0 Apocrine hidrocystoma

8404/0 Eccrine hidrocystoma

ICD-11 coding

EK70.3 Hidrocystoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: apocrine gland cyst; eccrine ductal cyst.

Not recommended: sudoriferous cyst.

Subtype(s)

Apocrine hidrocystoma; eccrine hidrocystoma

Localization

Hidrocystomas most commonly involve the lower eyelid (40%), followed by the lateral canthus (30%), upper eyelid (18%), and medial canthus (13%) {28722532}. Apocrine hidrocystomas, derived from glands of Moll, typically involve the eyelid margin or the skin immediately adjacent to the mucocutaneous junction/cilia {21168812;25794031}. Eccrine hidrocystomas may arise in the pretarsal or preseptal eyelid skin.

Clinical features

Hidrocystomas are skin-coloured to blue-tinged and sometimes pigmented (brown), smooth-surfaced, soft, compressible cysts that usually transilluminate. Cysts may be multilocular, multiple, and bilateral and enlarge over months to years, typically presenting at < 5 mm in this location.

Epidemiology

Hidrocystomas typically appear in adults (mean ages of 56 years and 64 years in two large studies) {28722532;21168812}. There is no consistent sex or race predilection.

Etiology

Hidrocystomas are largely derived from ectasias of the apocrine or eccrine units (ductal or secretory epithelium). Some apocrine hidrocystomas may be neoplastic {17102015}.

Pathogenesis

Apocrine hidrocystomas do not have clear inciting factors. Some cases of bilateral, multiple apocrine hidrocystomas are associated with Schöpf–Schulz–Passarge syndrome {28722532}.

Eccrine hidrocystomas may be associated with retention of sweat and development may be affected by heat and humidity.

Macroscopic appearance

Cysts are thin and smooth walled, and contain clear to occasionally milky or brownish serous fluid.

Histopathology

Apocrine hidrocystomas (most common in the eyelids) can be multiloculated and are lined by an epithelial-myoepithelial bilayer. The apocrine epithelium is high cuboidal to columnar with glassy, deeply eosinophilic cytoplasm; apical decapitation snouts are present, but may be focal. A dense fibrous wall is common. Some lesions may demonstrate oncocytic change {25794031}. Focal proliferative epithelial changes may be seen, manifesting as epithelial multilayering or papillary projections without a fibrous core {30893188;18001413}. Brown or black luminal pigment in the form of lipofuscin may be encountered {24247572;25794031}.

Eccrine hidrocystomas are unilocular and are lined by a low-cuboidal epithelial bilayer with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm lacking apical snouts. Hybrid cysts derived from the junction of the pilosebaceous and apocrine/eccrine units are also relatively common, and these compromise the eccrine unit–type epithelial-myoepithelial layer, merging with areas of an epidermal cyst epithelial lining, containing both keratin and serous fluid {18356717}.

Immunohistochemistry

Apocrine hidrocystoma expresses GCDFP-15 and CK7 in the epithelium and SMA in myoepithelial cells. Eccrine hidrocystoma lacks expression of these markers {21168812}. (Immunohistochemistry is not required for diagnosis.)

Differential diagnosis

Apocrine cystadenoma, low-grade cystadenocarcinoma, and endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma are the main diagnostic considerations and can occur in association with hidrocystoma. Goblet cells / intracytoplasmic mucin, papillae with fibrous cores, and complex cribriform lining should lead to consideration of these entities (see section Apocrine carcinoma and section Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma) {18001413;16160476}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cyst lined by a sweat ductal bilayer containing serous secretion; lack of goblet cells or complex architecture.

Desirable: apocrine secretion in apocrine hidrocystoma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Simple excision is typically curative (recurrence rate of 0–3%). Injection, electrodesiccation, and laser therapy have also been successfully utilized.
