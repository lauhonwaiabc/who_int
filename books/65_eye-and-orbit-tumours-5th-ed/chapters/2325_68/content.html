2.3.2.5 Apocrine carcinoma

Definition

Apocrine carcinoma is an epithelial neoplasm exhibiting apocrine differentiation, typically characterized by decapitation secretion and/or the presence of cytoplasmic zymogen granules.

ICD-O coding

8401/3 Apocrine carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH9L77 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Apocrine adenocarcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: apocrine adenocarcinoma; apocrine gland carcinoma.

Not recommended: Moll gland carcinoma; Moll gland adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The eyelid is by far the most common location. The skin of the upper and lower eyelids is equally affected {21913192;24217473}.

Clinical features

The tumour appears as a solitary nodule that is bluish/brown in colour. Symptoms and signs include ulceration, bleeding, excess lacrimation, lash loss, swelling, congestion and discharge, rapid growth, or bleeding {31220897;29313402}. Some cases can resemble a chalazion, which can delay diagnosis and treatment {22571743}.

Epidemiology

There is a male predilection and an age range of 44–91 years {31220897}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Some cases of apocrine carcinoma with invasion are associated with a benign apocrine lesion or an apocrine carcinoma in situ, suggesting malignant evolution {17102015;8233400}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions appear as a solid or solid-cystic, poorly circumscribed bluish/brown nodule.

Histopathology

Apocrine carcinoma is usually highly infiltrative with a sclerotic stroma. It can exhibit glandular, tubular, papillary, tubulopapillary, or solid growth patterns. The tumour cells have plentiful eosinophilic cytoplasm, which can be granular and sometimes partly vacuolated. The nuclei are oval and vacuolated and show prominent eosinophilic nucleoli. Decapitation secretion is seen but can be focal. Pleomorphism and mitotic figures are present and vary according to the tumour differentiation. The tumour can show perineural, blood vessel, and lymphatic invasion {24217473;18347508}.

Apocrine carcinoma can be seen in association with various precursor benign and carcinoma in situ lesions. Benign lesions include apocrine gland hyperplasia, adenoma, and cystadenoma {17102015;8233400}. If apocrine carcinoma in situ is present, it has striking resemblance to breast ductal carcinoma in situ and is surrounded by an intact myoepithelial cell layer.

Histochemistry

Apocrine tumours contain PASD-positive cytoplasmic granules. Some cases show cytoplasmic iron.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells express AE1, AE3, CAM5.2, CK7, GCDFP-15, GATA3, CEA, S100, EMA, ER, PR, AR, and lysozyme {8599433;27746659;24342430;18347508}.

Differential diagnosis

Metastatic apocrine breast carcinoma and endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma require exclusion. The presence of eyelid apocrine precursor lesions such as an apocrine adenoma/cystadenoma or apocrine carcinoma in situ would exclude metastatic apocrine breast carcinoma. In their absence, comprehensive clinical examination is mandatory to exclude metastatic apocrine breast carcinoma. Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma expresses INSM1 and MUC2 and has mucinous elements, whereas apocrine carcinoma is negative for these markers and does not produce mucin {34086646}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an invasive carcinoma with apocrine differentiation (histologically or immunohistochemically confirmed); exclusion of metastatic breast apocrine carcinoma.

Desirable: the presence of a benign apocrine lesion or an apocrine in situ carcinoma, to support a primary eyelid origin.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification for eyelid carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Cases localized to the eyelid skin can be surgically excised with adequate margins. Tumours that extend into the orbit have been treated successfully with orbital exenteration. Adjuvant postoperative radiotherapy has been used in some cases if there is concern of a high risk of local recurrence, for instance if perineural invasion is present. Rare cases of eyelid apocrine carcinoma have displayed rapid local recurrence and metastasis to regional lymph nodes or distant sites. Some of these cases have been treated successfully by chemotherapy {31220897;24217473;24765311;6329262}.
