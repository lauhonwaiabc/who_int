6.1.2.7 Acinic cell carcinoma

Definition

Acinic cell carcinoma is a rare malignant lacrimal gland neoplasm displaying predominantly serous acinar differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8550/3 Acinic cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D0Y & XH3PG9 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Acinar cell carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: acinic cell adenocarcinoma; acinar cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The rare cases occurring in the lacrimal gland have involved the palpebral {24959368} and orbital lobes {1804375;3955504}, and a few cases extended to adjacent soft tissues {7540387} or intracranially {11766029}.

Clinical features

Patients may be asymptomatic {31762749} or present with a slow-growing painless mass causing inferior globe displacement {11766029} or proptosis {3955504}.

Imaging

CT shows a circumscribed mass located in the lacrimal fossa {31762749}. MRI provides better detail of the involvement of adjacent structures.

Epidemiology

This is an extremely rare lacrimal gland neoplasm with slight female predominance. The majority of the patients are in their fifties; however, a case was reported in an 18-year-old patient {7540387}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Studies performed in salivary gland cases revealed the recurrent genomic rearrangement t(4;9)(q13;q31) as a characteristic genetic driver event in acinic cell carcinomas, enabling upregulation of the transcription factor NR4A3 through enhancer hijacking {30664630;31094928}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are often well-circumscribed, tan-greyish, firm to soft solid masses with occasional cystic areas. Infiltrative borders and multinodularity are mostly seen in recurrences {26685679}.

Histopathology

Microscopically, the tumour may show solid (trabecular or acinar arrangements), microcystic, papillary-cystic (lattice-like), and follicular growth patterns. A prominent lymphoid infiltrate may be seen in the background. The typical tumour cell is characterized by a polygonal shape, abundant basophilic granular cytoplasm with poorly defined borders, central/eccentric small nuclei, and distinct nucleoli {3955504;11766029}; oncocytic and clear cells might be present. Cytoplasmic granules are PAS-positive and diastase-resistant. Tumours with high-grade transformation exhibit areas of nuclear enlargement and pleomorphism, increased mitoses, necrosis, and perineural and vascular invasion {33825717;23821210}.

Immunohistochemistry

DOG1 and SOX10 positivity is found in tumours with acinar and intercalated duct cell features, but it is not necessary for diagnosis. Recently described immunohistochemistry for NR4A3 or NR4A2 has been reported to be more specific and sensitive for diagnosis {31094928;32910350}.

Acinic cell carcinomas are usually negative for p63, S100, and mammaglobin, which assists differentiation from secretory carcinoma, a tumour that has a characteristic ETV6::NTRK3 translocation.

Differential diagnosis

This includes normal lacrimal gland tissue, secretory carcinoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma, and papillary cystadenocarcinoma, especially cases with papillary-cystic areas and stromal lymphoid aggregates. The most important differential is with secretory carcinoma, as on further testing most lacrimal gland acinic carcinomas turn out to be secretory carcinomas.

Cytology

Cellular smears composed of large, polygonal, serous cells with round, uniform nuclei and delicate, vacuolated, or finely granular basophilic cytoplasm. Stripped nuclei and small lymphocytes can be seen in the background. Uncommonly, mitoses, necrosis, and marked nuclear atypia may indicate high-grade transformation.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not needed in most cases. However, challenging cases may require demonstrating NR4A subfamily rearrangements via immunohistochemistry {32910350;31094928}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tumour cells with acinar differentiation; zymogen granules in focal areas.

Desirable: nuclear staining for NR4A3 (NOR1) or NR4A2 (NUUR1); molecular demonstration of NR4A3 rearrangement.

Staging

Stage according to the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Acinic cell carcinomas are low-grade malignancies, with a total any-year disease-specific survival rate of nearly 90%. Histological features associated with poor prognosis include extracapsular extension, increased mitoses, a desmoplastic and lymphoid stromal reaction, perineural and vascular invasion, and high-grade features {27474461;26685679}.
