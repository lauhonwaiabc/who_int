10.1.8.4 Alveolar soft part sarcoma

Definition

Alveolar soft part sarcoma (ASPS) of the orbit, similarly to those at other sites, is a high-grade tumour of uncertain histogenesis having a distinctive alveolar arrangement of epithelioid tumour cells and characterized by the der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25) rearrangement resulting in the ASPSCR1::TFE3 gene fusion.

ICD-O coding

9581/3 Alveolar soft part sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH8V95 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Alveolar soft part sarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ASPS of the orbit is located close to the extraocular muscles, sometimes intraconal, in the medial part of the orbit, supraorbital, or involving the eyelid {32085747;29285193;25022380}.

Clinical features

ASPS presents as a slowly progressive, painless proptosis of the eye with eyelid swelling and dilatation of conjunctival vessels, but rarely compressing the optic nerve {32085747}. Radiologically, the tumours are well-circumscribed ovoid masses, isodense or hypodense on CT {26231949}. MRI demonstrates intermediate signal intensity and marked enhancement {25415714}.

Epidemiology

While ASPS is rare, representing 0.5–0.9% of all soft tissue sarcomas, orbital involvement by the tumour is relatively frequent. Although any age group may be affected, peak occurrence is in the third decade of life {29285193}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. No cases have reported in association with a history of irradiation or a germline cancer predisposition syndrome.

Pathogenesis

The ASPSCR1::TFE3 translocation appears to be the initiating genetic event in ASPS {11244503}, defined by a specific alteration, der(17)t(X;17)(p11.2;q25). This translocation results in fusion of the TFE3 transcription factor gene (from Xp11.2) with ASPSCR1 (also known as ASPL) at 17q25.3 {11244503}. The ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion protein localizes to the nucleus, causing c-Met activation of c-Met signalling. Depending on the TFE3 intron involved in the genomic rearrangement, ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion transcripts can differ by the presence or absence of one additional exon of TFE3.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours vary from 20 to 50 mm (median 34.5 mm) {31836117;32085747;23960876;34630814;29285193;28844593}. They are usually circumscribed, but can be invasive {32085747;29285193;23661945;25022380;21478594}. The tumours are often soft, and vary from grey or yellow to tan and reddish in colour. The centre of larger lesions often shows haemorrhage or necrosis {32085747;29285193;23661945;25022380;21478594}.

Histopathology

Microscopy often shows an organoid or nest-like pattern with central discohesion resulting in the characteristic alveolar pattern with a rich sinusoidal vascular network seen throughout the tumour separating the nests from each other. Solid and haemangiopericytoma-like patterns may be encountered focally in many tumours. Cells are large and polygonal with well-defined cell borders, eosinophilic granular cytoplasm, a rounded central or eccentric vesicular nucleus, and a prominent nucleolus. Vascular invasion is very common. Cytoplasmic clearing, rhabdoid cells, a pseudoglandular pattern, and cystic change with myxoid material may be seen {22173238;9522219}. Many tumours show intracytoplasmic rod-like/rhomboid crystalline structures in a sheaf-like or stacked configuration {22173238;15500657}. These crystals may be irregular, are PAS-positive/diastase resistant, and are immunoreactive for MCT1 and CD147 {11943706;21835426;7571976}. Glycogen is usually present.

Immunohistochemistry

ASPS shows immunoreactivity for TFE3 (nuclear), cathepsin K (100%), and CD147 (100%). Immunoreactivity for S100, calretinin, desmin, and NSE is variable {17071801;12766578;1879268;21279521;21602817;24746209}.

Cytology

In cytological preparations, smears reveal large cells, mostly single and granular, which may have vacuoles {19787801;1879268}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of the fusion transcript ASPSCR1::TFE3 {26516944;23828314;21279521} may be diagnostically helpful. This gene fusion is also seen in paediatric renal cell carcinomas {11438465}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: organoid/nesting pattern of polygonal cells; rich sinusoidal capillary network; diffuse, nuclear TFE3 expression by immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: intracytoplasmic crystals; demonstration of TFE3 gene rearrangement or ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system can be used.

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence is seen in 18.6–47.7% of orbital ASPSs {32085747}. Metastasis can occur long (as long as 6 years) after the initial diagnosis and most commonly affects lungs, brain, and bones {25022380}.
