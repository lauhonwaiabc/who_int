7.2.1.1 Papillomas (squamous and inverted)

Definition

Papillomas of the lacrimal drainage system are benign neoplasms characterized by an exophytic or an inverted growth of acanthotic, maturing multilayered epithelium.

ICD-O coding

8052/0 Squamous papilloma, exophytic

8053/0 Squamous papilloma, inverted

8052/0 Squamous papilloma, mixed

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH0TP8 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Sinonasal papilloma, exophytic

2F36.Y & XH8FH7 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Sinonasal papilloma, inverted

Related terminology

Not recommended: Schneiderian papilloma; everted papilloma; fungiform papilloma; endophytic papilloma; transitional papilloma.

Subtype(s)

Exophytic papilloma; inverted papilloma; mixed papilloma

Localization

Papillomas usually arise from the epithelium in the lacrimal sac and may secondarily involve the lower canaliculus, nasolacrimal duct, and nasal cavity as they grow. Inverted papillomas (IPs) may also originate from the nasolacrimal duct {7947444;20958177;26649215}.

Clinical features

Most are unilateral and present with persistent/chronic epiphora, recurrent dacryocystitis, and/or a non-tender mass above the medial tendon. Serosanguineous punctal discharge, epistaxis, and pain are rare. About 40% of cases have been discovered incidentally during dacryocystorhinostomy or dacryocystectomy {28860918;24260768;7413136;12855966;4352147;16550051;23960982;28399337}.

Epidemiology

Papillomas usually arise in the fourth to fifth decades of life but can occur at any age, with no sex predilection; IP is the most common {6277817;4352147;7947444;20958177}.

Etiology

Low-risk HPV6 and HPV11 genotypes have been reported {7947444;9215061;29341451;34452388}. Statistically associated increased risk with cigarette smoking and dust and aerosol exposure has also been shown {32501879;25879369}.

Pathogenesis

IPs are histopathologically identical to inverted sinonasal papillomas and their pathogenesis is similarly related {23960982}. Over 70% have activating EGFR mutations in the tyrosine kinase domains {31743131}.

Macroscopic appearance

Squamous papillomas (SPs) appear as single polypoidal or confluent, firm, fleshy greyish-pink/tan masses. IPs are firm, tan/reddish polyps often with a convoluted cerebriform surface {28321771}.

Histopathology

SPs are composed of outward/exophytic proliferations of finger-like projections of multilayered epithelium 5–20 cells thick, variably squamous, squamotransitional, or columnar, but most often squamous, with a central fibrovascular core {28321771}.

IPs show an inward/endophytic growth of epithelial nests from the surface epithelium, surrounded by an intact basement membrane; mixed endophytic/exophytic tumours are not uncommon {24260768;28399337}. The epithelium is the same as in SPs but is more frequently transitional {23960982;28321771}. Pools of mucin with neutrophils are common {23960982}. Stromal changes include submucosal oedema and chronic inflammation {28321771}. Condylomatous changes (marked acanthosis with scattered koilocyte-like cells) are features of HPV infection {31743131}. Mitoses are rare and not atypical; although varying grades of dysplasia have been observed in papillomas {28860918;19568358}, malignant change in SPs is extremely rare {3957337}. IPs can erode bone, but this alone does not indicate malignant transformation; there are no histopathological features predictive of subsequent malignant transformation {28321771}.

Immunohistochemistry

Papillomas are positive for pancytokeratin {2321771}.

Differential diagnosis

Exclude squamous cell carcinoma and mucoepidermoid carcinoma.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Squamous papilloma

Essential: proliferative, exophytic, papillary growth; lined by an acanthotic multilayered epithelium; no evidence of high-grade squamous dysplasia; absence of inverted, destructive, or infiltrative growth.

Inverted papilloma

Essential: prominent endophytic growth of hyperplastic epithelium into the stroma with an intact basement membrane; absence of malignant cellular features and necrosis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete en bloc surgical excision is the treatment of choice for both SPs and IPs of the lacrimal drainage system. Recurrence and progression to malignancy is distinctly uncommon in SP, if completely excised {32501879}. IP recurrence rate is 11–14%; smoking increases the risk to 50% {32501879;25879369}. Invasion into the orbit, paranasal sinuses, and intracranial cavity can occur with risk increasing in recurrent cases {32501879}. The rate of malignancy of IP confined to the lacrimal drainage system is 22%, increasing to 27% when orbitally invasive cases are included {32501879}. The types of carcinomas identified include squamous cell carcinoma, mucin-producing carcinoma, and Schneiderian/transitional carcinoma {28399337;32501879}.

There are currently no histopathological, immunohistochemical, or molecular features predictive of aggressive behaviour.
