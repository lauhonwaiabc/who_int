7.2.2.2 Adenocarcinoma NOS of the lacrimal drainage system

Definition

Adenocarcinoma NOS of the lacrimal sac/duct is a rare primary malignant epithelial tumour with glandular differentiation, not otherwise classifiable.

ICD-O coding

8140/3 Adenocarcinoma, NOS, of the lacrimal drainage system

8147/3 Basaloid / basal cell adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D03.0 Adenocarcinoma of the lacrimal apparatus

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Basaloid/basal cell adenocarcinoma

Note: Oncocytic and ductal adenocarcinomas are covered in section Other salivary gland–type carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system.

Localization

Adenocarcinoma of the lacrimal drainage system usually originates in the lacrimal sac and/or nasolacrimal duct, but it can extend into the canaliculi {12782801;20958177;28399337}. Involvement of the adjacent sinonasal cavities or orbit, particularly if poorly differentiated {31684800}, is relatively common {32506982}.

Clinical features

Nearly all patients with adenocarcinoma present with epiphora and/or a firm, slow-growing, immobile lacrimal fossa mass lesion in the region of the medial canthus. Pain and bloodstained epiphora are uncommonly noticed but are concerning features for malignancy {32506982;24040460;21036121;25699235;21747898}. Some tumours are incidentally found at dacryocystorhinostomy or present as recurrent dacryocystitis {18569829;16354642;20958177;28399337}. Non-axial proptosis, blepharoptosis, periorbital oedema, and regional lymphadenopathy may be found in more-advanced cases {12782801;16354642;20958177;27222017}.

Epidemiology

Adenocarcinoma is rare (< 30 reported cases), accounts for < 5% of cases of malignant epithelial tumours, and is a tumour of middle-aged and older patients {17897525;16354642;28399337;27222017}. It is typically diagnosed in the sixth to seventh decades of life (mean age: 67 years) and shows a slight male predilection {28399337;8874432;32506982}.

Etiology

Although HPV infection is increasingly reported as an etiological agent in the development of nasolacrimal drainage carcinomas {17362365}, HPV infection has not been specifically demonstrated in adenocarcinoma.

Pathogenesis

Adenocarcinoma is believed to arise from the seromucinous accessory salivary-type glands within the lacrimal sac and/or nasolacrimal duct {15060716;8874432}.

Macroscopic appearance

These are lacrimal fossa mass lesions, often with invasion of surrounding tissue.

Histopathology

Adenocarcinoma of the lacrimal drainage system may show sheets, cords, nests, or single cell infiltration of pleomorphic cells forming lumina (glandular differentiation) with variable mucin production {16354642;24040460;8874432;7947444}. Cribriform and tubular structures may be seen. Cytomorphologically, cells are often cuboidal with eosinophilic granular cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli; nuclei may be highly pleomorphic to more uniform {24040460;28399337;32964761}. Mitotic figures, an inflammatory cell infiltrate, invasion into the fibrous lacrimal sac wall, and lymphovascular and perineural invasion are often present {16354642;24040460;8874432;4352147}.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumours are typically immunoreactive for pancytokeratin (AE1/AE3), CK7, AR, EMA, and GCDFP-15. CK20, ER, PR, chromogranin A, and synaptophysin do not often stain in these tumours, while ERBB2 expression is variable {18569829;24040460;25699235;29503943;32760849}.

Differential diagnosis

Nasolacrimal adenocarcinoma may be encountered as part of a biphasic tumour {30481853} or occur in the setting of malignant transformation of a benign precursor tumour (carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma or oncocytoma) {12782801}. Adenocarcinomas in the NOS category may be low-grade or high-grade, well differentiated or poorly differentiated, and show variable oncocytic/apocrine {32964761} and mucinous features. Ductal adenocarcinoma resembles salivary duct carcinoma {24040460;29503943}. Metastases should also be excluded.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Fusion assays based on next-generation sequencing or FISH may be useful to exclude other diagnoses defined by specific translocations (e.g. mucoepidermoid or adenoid cystic carcinoma).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pleomorphic, gland-forming neoplasm, often with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, and not categorized as another specific salivary-type adenocarcinoma.

Desirable: AR immunoreactivity; proof of absence of other translocation-defined salivary-type neoplasia.

Staging

While there is currently no separate TNM classification for carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system, the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system for lacrimal gland tumours can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Given the paucity and heterogenicity of cases, significant prognostic data is not available {20958177;28399337;21036121;27222017}. Current practices are based on information from a few case series {6277817;4352147;7947444} and individual case reports. However, recurrence with local destruction seems to be relatively common {20958177;28399337;21036121;12855966;30481853;16354642}, and metastases can occur, usually to lung or bone {32506982}. Certain low-grade subtypes such as basaloid adenocarcinoma may fare better {28837389}. AR-expressing tumours have been treated with androgen deprivation {32760849;25699235}.
