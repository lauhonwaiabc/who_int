1.3.2.1 Conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions

Definition

Conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions (C-MILs) represent a spectrum of morphological changes ranging from melanocytic hyperplasia through degrees of melanocytic atypia to melanoma in situ.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Low-grade conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesion

8720/2 High-grade conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesion

ICD-11 coding

2E63.1 Melanoma in situ of conjunctiva

Related terminology

Acceptable: conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia (C-MIN); primary acquired melanosis (PAM); melanoma in situ.

Not recommended: precancerous melanosis; premalignant melanosis; intraepithelial melanocytic proliferation with atypia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These lesions are generally unilateral but often multifocal; they vary in size, involving a mean of three clock-hours of the conjunctiva {27009410;17884168;31796811}. In decreasing frequency, C-MILs involve the bulbar, limbal, forniceal, and palpebral conjunctiva; they can also extend to the cornea and occur in the caruncle {17884168}.

Clinical features

C-MIL is usually unilateral and appears as an acquired lesion in young to middle-aged adults. The lesions appear as flat, irregular brown discolourations of the conjunctiva, which may change (wax and wane) over time. The pigmentation is mobile over the substantia propria and Tenon fascia and episclera. In rare cases, C-MIL can be amelanotic, making it difficult to recognize clinically {19128387;17884168}.

Imaging of conjunctival melanocytic lesions involves regular anterior segment photodocumentation (including with eversion of eyelids), slit-lamp microscopy, and potentially ultrasound biomicroscopy. Recent developments include anterior segment optical coherence tomography {29444155;32926848} and in vivo reflectance confocal microscopy {28654937}.

Epidemiology

All of these lesions are most common in middle-aged and elderly people with a light complexion, but they can occasionally occur in individuals with darker pigmentation, and even in teenagers or young adults. The precise incidence of pigmented conjunctival lesions is difficult to estimate. In a North American analysis of 311 eyes with PAM (C-MIL), 96% of the cases were in White individuals and 4% were in Black, Hispanic, or Asian individuals, with a patient age range of 15–90 years (mean: 56 years) {17884168;31796811}. There is no sex predilection {19128387;17884168}.

Etiology

No environmental cause has been clearly established, but atypical C-MILs develop most frequently in the interpalpebral zone, suggesting an association with ultraviolet (UV) radiation {12788119}.

Pathogenesis

C-MIL arises from basal conjunctival melanocytes {26556007}. Little is known about the underlying genetic alterations, although TERT promoter mutations were found in 2 of 25 (8%) cases of PAM with atypia {25159205}. BRAF p.V600E alterations occur in approximately 40% of conjunctival melanomas, but some studies suggest that these cases arise from naevi with mutations rather than from C-MIL {28938534;26425792}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

The key microscopic features in C-MIL include increased cellularity of intraepithelial conjunctival melanocytes with increasing cytological atypia, while the basal membrane remains intact {21128776;3972395;33130046}. The spectrum of cytological features ranges from small melanocytes with nuclear hyperchromasia and scant cytoplasm to severely atypical large pleomorphic epithelioid cells with ample cytoplasm and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli. Atypical architectural patterns may be difficult to discern in the context of thin conjunctival epithelium. They include basilar single cell hyperplasia, basilar nesting, intraepithelial nests, pagetoid proliferation of single cells, and a confluent growth pattern at the epithelial–stromal junction {21128776;3972395;33130046}.

Terms commonly used to classify these lesions include “primary acquired melanosis (PAM) with atypia” and “conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia (C-MIN)”, along with an emerging simplified classification into low-grade C-MIL, high-grade C-MIL, and conjunctival melanoma in situ. Various grading or scoring systems have been used for C-MIL {3972395;2649838;17255762;19128387;19761427;23222568;26556007;33130046}. The grading of the cytological atypia in PAM (mild, moderate, or severe) was like that used in the skin and other mucosal sites {3972395;18985522}. The C-MIN scoring system was based on architectural features (i.e. horizontal and vertical spread) and cytological atypia, with a C-MIN score of 0 representing hypermelanosis only (see section Benign epithelial melanosis of the conjunctiva); of 1 corresponding to PAM without atypia; of 2–4 being PAM with atypia; and of ≥ 5 equating to melanoma in situ {19761427}.

The fourth edition of the WHO classification of tumours of the eye proposed a simplified terminology: (1) low-grade C-MIL, corresponding to PAM without atypia or with mild atypia and lesions with C-MIN score of 1–2; (2) high-grade C-MIL, corresponding to PAM with moderate to severe atypia and C-MIN scores of 3–5; and (3) conjunctival melanoma in situ, corresponding to PAM with severe atypia generally involving > 75% of the epithelium and a C-MIN score of > 5 (see #35628Table 2.03) {37924948}. This C-MIL system results in similar interobserver agreement to PAM and C-MIN terminology {33130046}. The C-MIL definition of conjunctival melanoma in situ is concordant with that of the current American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system (pTis) [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]].

Immunohistochemistry

Atypical conjunctival melanocytes are immunoreactive for melan-A, S100, HMB45, MITF, and SOX10. Cyclin D1 is also of value in highlighting the atypical melanocytes {35356600}. The role of PRAME immunohistochemistry requires further examination {34089198;34268800}. Melanocytic proliferation can be assessed by Ki-67 staining {31836103}.

Differential diagnosis

Potential mimics of C-MIL include benign epithelial melanosis of the conjunctiva (see section Benign epithelial melanosis of the conjunctiva); oculodermal melanocytosis; conjunctival naevi, particularly those with junctional activity (see section Junctional, compound, and subepithelial conjunctival naevi); invasive melanoma (see section Conjunctival melanoma); pigmented conjunctival squamous intraepithelial neoplasia (see section Conjunctival squamous intraepithelial neoplasia) {29267580}; systemic conditions, such as Addison disease {23222568}; postinflammatory hyperpigmentation; and conjunctival tattooing {18378314}. Extension of C-MIL into conjunctival epithelial invaginations in the underlying lamina propria (pseudoglands of Henle) should not be misdiagnosed as invasive conjunctival melanoma.

Cytology

Not commonly performed

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not regularly used, although assessment of copy-number variations involving RREB1 (6p24), MYB (6q23), and CCND1 (11q13) have been found to be of value in difficult cases {20100177;23137666}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: varying degrees of increased cellularity and atypia of conjunctival melanocytes within the epithelium extending upward from the epithelial base, displacing squamous and/or goblet cells; no evidence of invasive growth.

Desirable: immunohistochemistry to confirm melanocytic phenotype, particularly in morphologically challenging cases.

Staging

The AJCC / Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system is used (pTis = conjunctival melanoma in situ).

Prognosis and prediction

PAM/C-MIL is a precursor of 50–70% of all conjunctival melanomas {23222568}; consequently, there is an absolute clinical need to know when to treat patients with surgery, cryotherapy, and/or topical chemotherapeutics (e.g. topical mitomycin C) {19128387;17884168}. Epithelioid cell morphology with cytological atypia, cell nesting, and pagetoid spread is associated with an increased risk of recurrence and progression to melanoma {18985522;3972395;17255762;21128776;17884168}. In contrast, C-MIL composed solely of hyperchromatic melanocytes with scant cytoplasm and nuclei devoid of discernible nucleoli is less likely to be associated with invasive melanoma {17255762;17884168}. For prognosis and prediction regarding conjunctival melanoma see section Conjunctival melanoma.
