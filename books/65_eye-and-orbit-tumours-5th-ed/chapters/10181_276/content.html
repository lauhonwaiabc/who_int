10.1.8.1 Angiomyxoma

Definition

Angiomyxoma is a benign mesenchymal tumour comprising spindle or stellate cells and thin-walled arborizing vessels in a myxoid stroma.

ICD-O coding

8841/0 Angiomyxoma

8841/0 Cutaneous myxoma (superficial angiomyxoma)

8841/0 Aggressive angiomyxoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH9HK9 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Angiomyxoma

2E84.0 & XH58A9 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of skin & Superficial angiomyxoma

2F7C & XH4V74 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Deep angiomyxoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: for superficial angiomyxoma: cutaneous myxoma; for deep angiomyxoma: aggressive angiomyxoma.

Subtype(s)

Superficial angiomyxoma; deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma

Localization

Superficial angiomyxomas occur in the conjunctiva {29631843} and eyelids {32495538;15953464}; deep angiomyxomas occur in the soft tissues of the orbit {20562663;17467533}.

Clinical features

Eyelid angiomyxomas present as multinodular, polypoid, or pedunculated slow-growing dermal/subcutaneous masses involving the eyelid margin {15953464;21708097;32495538}. In the conjunctiva, they appear as well-circumscribed, fleshy masses {29631843}. Orbital angiomyxomas present with proptosis and visual disturbances {20562663;17467533}.

Epidemiology

Angiomyxomas arise in patients across a wide age range, but predominantly in the third to fifth decades of life {32495538;29631843;21708097;20562663;17467533}, with no sex predilection.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Multiple superficial angiomyxomas may be seen in Carney complex, an autosomal dominant syndrome with germline mutation in PRKAR1A encoding a PKA subunit {15371594}.

Pathogenesis

Angiomyxomas of the eye region have not been reported to be associated with Carney complex, although it is suspected that PRKAR1A inactivation may play a role in the pathogenesis of sporadic tumours given the lack of protein expression on immunohistochemistry in 80% of cases {26130139;34532875}.

Macroscopic appearance

Angiomyxomas form poorly circumscribed, lobulated masses with a gelatinous surface {32495538;20562663}.

Histopathology

Superficial angiomyxomas are poorly circumscribed, forming lobules separated by collagenous septa, composed of spindle-shaped to stellate cells with bland ovoid nuclei in an abundant myxoid and variably collagenous stroma {32495538;20562663;29631843}. Thin-walled non-arborizing vessels are prominent {31078543;32495538;17467533}. Stromal neutrophils may be seen {15953464;21708097}.

Deep angiomyxomas have increased cellularity and thick-walled vessels showing medial hypertrophy and perivascular hyalinization {20562663}.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells express vimentin and CD34, and are negative for S100, desmin, SMA, and cytokeratins {32495538}. There is loss of PRKAR1A expression, particularly in Carney complex–associated lesions {31078543}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes nerve sheath myxoma (S100 positive) {33403177}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: hypocellular, myxoid, lobulated tumour; bland stellate to spindle-shaped cells; non-arborizing capillary-like vasculature.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision with wide margins is recommended {29631843;20562663}. There is frequent recurrence (30–40%) due to infiltrative growth and the inability to excise completely, requiring prolonged follow-up {3389450;21708097;17467533;20562663}.
