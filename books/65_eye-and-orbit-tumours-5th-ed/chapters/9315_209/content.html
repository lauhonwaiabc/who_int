9.3.1.5 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) NOS is a lymphoma consisting of medium-sized to large B cells with a diffuse growth pattern. This is a morphologically and molecularly heterogeneous entity that does not meet the diagnostic criteria for specific large B-cell lymphoma neoplasms.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Morphological subtypes: diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, centroblastic subtype; diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, immunoblastic subtype; diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, anaplastic subtype; other rare variants

Molecular subtypes: germinal-centre B-cell subtype (GCB); activated B-cell subtype (ABC)

Genetic subtype: diffuse large B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL6 rearrangements

Localization

Ocular adnexal DLBCL may occur in the eyelid, conjunctiva, orbital soft tissues, and lacrimal system. The orbit is most frequently affected (~50% of cases) {28399341;25393033;32574779;31768360}. Often the lesion extends into adjacent ocular adnexal structures.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a unilateral rapidly expanding orbital mass; it may be asymptomatic or some (~30%) experience pain or irritation {25393033}.

Epidemiology

DLBCL accounts for 5–15% of ocular adnexal lymphomas and is the third most common lymphoma subtype in the ocular adnexa. Most patients are about 70 years old, with a slight female predominance {28399341;9709754;17255761;32732342;25393033}.

Etiology

Two thirds of ocular adnexal DLBCLs are primary, but one third are associated with secondary systemic involvement {28399341;25393033}. Although DLBCL-NOS typically arises de novo, it may represent transformation from an underlying known or occult indolent B-cell lymphoma, such as a follicular or marginal zone lymphoma. No predisposing risk factors are known.

Pathogenesis

In contrast to the eyelid, conjunctiva, and lacrimal gland, normal orbital soft tissue has long been considered an extralymphatic site since it lacks lymphatics or lymphoid tissue {28399341;32516408}. Therefore, it remains unclear where the originating precursor neoplastic B cells arise and expand in DLBCL.

DLBCL-NOS can be largely divided into two different molecular groups: GCB-like (31%) and ABC (69%) {12075054;25393033}; however, 15–30% of DLBCLs cannot be classified definitively.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface of the tumour reveals a lobulated salmon-coloured mass.

Histopathology

Ocular adnexal DLBCL shows diffuse effacement of normal architecture and comprises large non-cohesive cells with large vesicular nuclei with multiple irregular eosinophilic nucleoli and atypical mitoses.

Immunophenotype

Ocular adnexal DLBCLs are CD20+. However, CD20 may be negative in biopsies taken after rituximab treatment. Alternative B-cell markers such as CD19, CD22, CD79a, and/or PAX5 should be employed to confirm B-cell differentiation and support the differential diagnosis. Ki-67 is > 50%. The tumour has few reactive T cells.

Immunohistochemical surrogates have been developed for subtyping of DLBCL-NOS according to cell of origin. The Hans algorithm is popular and uses three markers (CD10, BCL6, IRF4) to distinguish the GCB versus non-GCB subtypes {14504078}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes non-haematological malignancies, such as metastatic poorly differentiated carcinoma, metastatic melanoma, and seminoma, and less frequently other haematolymphoid malignancies with a dispersed monotonous pattern such as Burkitt lymphoma; histiocytic sarcoma; myeloid sarcoma; and some cases of Hodgkin lymphoma {26414904}.

Cytology

Cytology is not suited for the diagnosis of orbital inflammatory disease or haematolymphoid disease.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Clonality analysis may help to demonstrate the clonal B-cell population.

MYD88 and CD79B missense mutations may be identified in 30–70% and 10% of ocular adnexal DLBCLs, respectively {27102345;34706861}. The ABC-subtype DLBCLs are enriched for BCR pathway mutations such as in MYD88 (mostly p.L265P), CD79B, PIM1, and PRDM1 {25805586}.

High-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 rearrangements may be encountered in 16% of ocular adnexal DLBCLs {34706861}. EBV positivity is rare {20142557;10417924}. Cases displaying an isolated MYC rearrangement or dual rearrangements of MYC and BCL6 do fall within the spectrum of DLBCL-NOS; if a MYC or a simultaneous MYC and BCL6 rearrangement is detected, it should be reported as “DLBCL-NOS with MYC or MYC and BCL6 rearrangement” {30523716;30523719;29475959}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large B-cell lymphoma with diffuse or vaguely nodular growth pattern; mature B-cell phenotype; exclusion of any other class of large B-cell lymphoma with ocular adnexal involvement; exclusion of immunodeficiency / dysregulation-related settings.

Desirable: cell-of-origin subtyping; demonstration of a clonal B-cell population or an MYD88 mutation when histology is not definitive; reporting of isolated MYC or dual MYC and BCL6 rearrangements; absence of EBV-associated markers; cytogenetic testing.

Staging

In primary ocular adnexal DLCBL, disease-specific mortality is significantly associated with TNM T category {25393033;28880986}. However, for all such DLBCLs, Lugano staging appears to be superior {25393033;28880986}.

Prognosis and prediction

Younger age (below 60 years) and total resection are associated with increased survival {28880986}. The ABC subtype of DLBCL-NOS has an inferior outcome in response to standard therapies when compared with the GCB subtype. In ocular adnexal DLBCL, outcomes between the two subtypes are similar {25393033}. Ocular adnexal DLBCLs with MYD88 and CD79B mutations are associated with a poor prognosis {34706861}. Concurrent high expression of BCL2 and MYC in DLBCL (dual expressers) carries an adverse prognosis {34706861;22665537;22851565;28525305;23335369}.
