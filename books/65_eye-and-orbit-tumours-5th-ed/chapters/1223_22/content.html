1.2.2.3 Adenosquamous carcinoma

Definition

Adenosquamous carcinoma is an invasive carcinoma arising from dysplastic conjunctival epithelium, with biphenotypic differentiation comprising squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and adenocarcinoma.

ICD-O coding

8560/3 Adenosquamous carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D00.Y & XH7873 Other specified malignant neoplasm of conjunctiva & Adenosquamous carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: mucoepidermoid carcinoma; squamous carcinoma with mucinous differentiation.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adenosquamous carcinoma can occur at the limbal, bulbar, forniceal, and palpebral conjunctiva {991088;7947443;27171853;31932683;16950250;23960993}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a conjunctival mass of short duration, usually several weeks to months. The tumour may be nodular, multinodular, and fleshy pink-red, and can be associated with mild irritation {31932683;26902565}. Other presentations include leukoplakic, infiltrative, ulcerative, or papillomatous lesions {991088;23960993;24363552;24932264;31356465;31932683;7947443;27171853;16950250;26902565}. Involvement of the tarsal and forniceal conjunctiva is common (~60% of cases) {31932683;26902565}. For SCC with mucinous differentiation, see section Conjunctival squamous cell carcinoma.

Epidemiology

This tumour is rare. The mean patient age at presentation is 64 years, and there is a male predilection {7947443;24363552;991088}.

Etiology

Chronic exposure to ultraviolet B (UVB) radiation may be a risk factor for adenosquamous carcinoma {32717381;8676629}, because of the described association of adenosquamous carcinoma with UV radiation–induced conjunctival intraepithelial neoplasia {31932683;27171853;2817062}. No association with HPV has been demonstrated so far {31932683}.

Pathogenesis

Adenosquamous carcinoma often arises from conjunctival SCC in situ or conjunctival intraepithelial neoplasia with mucinous differentiation {27171853;2817062;31932683}. All reported cases to date have lacked MAML2 gene rearrangements {31932683;32476556}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours generally have both nodular and infiltrative components, with variable keratinization.

Histopathology

Adenosquamous carcinoma exhibits biphenotypic differentiation comprising SCC and adenocarcinoma. The ratio of these two components varies between cases. The SCC can be keratinizing or non-keratinizing, of variable differentiation, and arranged as infiltrative cords or sheets {991088;16950250;23960993;24932264;31356465;32476556;31932683}. The adenocarcinoma component can be tubuloglandular or comprise confluent sheets of atypical cells with intracytoplasmic mucin, resembling signet-ring cells and atypical goblet cells {991088;6677843;7055460;16950250;23960993;24363552;31932683;31356465;24932264}. The bordering conjunctival epithelium demonstrates SCC in situ or conjunctival intraepithelial neoplasia with mucinous differentiation {31932683;27171853;2817062}. The mucin within the tubuloglandular areas and the confluent intracellular vacuoles is positive for mucicarmine, Alcian blue, and PAS with diastase {23960993;31932683}.

Immunohistochemistry

The SCC component is positive for p40, CK5/6, and CK17. The adenocarcinoma component is positive for CK7, EMA (MUC1), and CEA, and with BerEP4 {23960993;32476556;31932683}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes pseudoglandular hyperplasia {11150278} and SCC with mucinous differentiation. Pseudoglandular hyperplasia does not show cytological atypia. Squamous carcinoma with mucinous differentiation does not feature true neoplastic glands / ducts / confluent sheets of cells with intracytoplasmic mucin and, instead, shows scattered small groups or individual cells with intracytoplasmic mucin among malignant squamous cells (see section Conjunctival squamous cell carcinoma). Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of the accessory and main lacrimal glands can sometimes enter the differential diagnosis, particularly in tumours without a well-established in situ component (see section Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of the lacrimal gland).

Cytology

FNAB or impression cytology is not recommended for this entity.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic studies are limited, and no particular diagnostic molecular changes have been proposed {32476556;31932683}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a conjunctival tumour with both SCC and adenocarcinoma components.

Desirable: presence of adjacent SCC in situ or conjunctival intraepithelial neoplasia with mucinous differentiation; keratinization of the squamous carcinoma component.

Staging

Adenosquamous carcinoma is staged using the most recent American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification system for conjunctival carcinomas.

Prognosis and prediction

This tumour is aggressive, with extensive local recurrence that can involve the orbit and the globe after local (or even wider) resection (i.e. enucleation or exenteration) {7947443;24363552;16950250}. Lymph node metastasis is uncommon, and death related to this tumour is rare {31932683;32698034;26902565}.
