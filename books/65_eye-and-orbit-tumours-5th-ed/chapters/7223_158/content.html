7.2.2.3 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of the lacrimal drainage system

Definition

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) of the lacrimal drainage system is a malignant glandular epithelial neoplasm characterized by squamous (epidermoid) cells, mucous cells, columnar cells, and intermediate cells.

ICD-O coding

8430/3 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D03.1 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of lacrimal apparatus

Related terminology

Not recommended: mixed epidermoid and mucus-secreting carcinoma; mucoepidermoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

At this anatomical site, MEC arises in the lacrimal sac.

Clinical features

MEC presents with unilateral epiphora which can occasionally be sanguineous, usually followed by the development of a palpable mass in the medial canthal area {32506982;32258022;27534787}. This mass is usually firm, solid, and non-inflamed. Other clinical features include eye pain and irritation, recurrent dacryocystitis, and nasal congestion {32506982;32258022;27534787}. The average duration of symptoms is 18 months (range: 3–36 months) {32506982;32258022;27534787}.

Epidemiology

MEC of the lacrimal sac occurs in adults with a median age of 50 years (range: 30–78 years) {32506982}. There is a slight male predominance {32506982;27534787}.

Etiology

In the salivary gland, MEC is associated with radiation exposure {33327563}. This has not been reported in the lacrimal sac.

Pathogenesis

Unlike in the analogous tumour at other body sites including salivary and lacrimal gland, a CRTC1::MAML2 or CRTC3::MAML2 fusion has not been identified {32258022} [[Hyrcza MD, Gilbert RW, Yu E, et al. Two cases have arisen in HPV-associated papillomas but HPV is not considered to be involved in malignant transformation [[Hyrcza MD, Gilbert RW, Yu E, et al. Macroscopic appearance

Lacrimal sac mass

Histopathology

MEC of the lacrimal sac is composed of variable proportions of three cells types: squamous, mucous secreting, and intermediate. The squamous cells have medium to large nuclei with eosinophilic cytoplasm and intercellular bridges between cells, but keratinization is usually absent. Mucous cells form small glands, tubules, and cysts; the cysts contain inspissated mucin (which may calcify) and are lined by mucous cells, looking deceptively bland. The intermediate (or transitional) cells are oval, with a round nucleus and faintly eosinophilic cytoplasm. In addition, columnar, clear, and oncocytic cells may be admixed. PAS or Alcian blue staining may be required to visualize mucin. The stroma may be hyalinized with calcification and ossification. A granulomatous reaction to extravasated mucus may be seen.

There are several systems for grading salivary gland MEC {27721274;31021855;34231055}. Most divide tumours into three levels (low-grade, intermediate-grade, and high-grade). The systems are based on the proportions of cystic areas, proportion of necrosis, perineural invasion, mitoses, nuclear pleomorphism, invasion front, and lymphovascular invasion. More recently a binary grading system separating low- and intermediate-grade tumours from high-grade tumours using the Brandwein system has been shown to best stratify patients into groups based on risk of recurrence {31021855}.

Immunohistochemistry

Lesions are positive for EMA (common in high-grade tumours), MUC2, MUC4 (common in low-grade tumours), MUC5AC, and MUC5B. MECs are usually positive for CK5/6, CK7, and CK14, and with BerEP4, and negative for CK20. The tumour cells can be focally positive for vimentin, CEA, and p63 {26121166}. Myoepithelial markers are negative.

Differential diagnosis

Exclusion of squamous cell carcinoma, sebaceous carcinoma, adenosquamous carcinoma, and metastatic carcinomas is important. There should be no in situ component.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Unlike in MEC at other body sites, a CRTC1::MAML2 or CRTC3::MAML2 fusion has not been identified in lacrimal sac tumours {32258022}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: squamous cells, intermediate cells, and mucous cells in varying proportions; for lower-grade tumours: cystic, mucous-cell rich, and well circumscribed; for higher-grade tumours: solid with a higher proportion of squamous and intermediate cells.

Staging

While there is currently no separate TNM classification for carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system, the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system for lacrimal gland tumours can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours are locally aggressive but rarely metastasize. Of the 26 cases in the literature the majority were treated successfully with combined surgery and radiotherapy with a few patients requiring more extensive surgery. Local recurrence has been reported in 4 patients but there have been no tumour-related deaths (mean follow-up: 39 months, range: 6–98 months) {32506982}.
