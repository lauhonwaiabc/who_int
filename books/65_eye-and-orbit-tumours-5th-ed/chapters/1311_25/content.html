1.3.1.1 Benign epithelial melanosis of the conjunctiva

Definition

Benign epithelial melanosis of the conjunctiva is characterized by increased production of melanin without significant proliferation of conjunctival melanocytes, which remain normal in size and location, and transfer melanin into adjacent keratinocytes.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A61.6 Conjunctival or subconjunctival degenerations or deposits

Related terminology

Acceptable: constitutional melanosis; complexion-related melanosis.

Not recommended: non-proliferative melanocytic pigmentary patch; ephelis; freckle.

Subtype(s)

Congenital conjunctival epithelial melanosis; acquired conjunctival epithelial melanosis

Localization

Conjunctival epithelial melanosis is usually bilateral and can be unifocal or multifocal. It is most common in the perilimbal region, but it can also occur in the bulbar conjunctiva and extend to the palpebral conjunctiva {19128387;26556007;21417906;34424954}.

Clinical features

Congenital (primary) conjunctival melanosis may be visible from birth as mottled or patchy intraepithelial pigmented lesions that are not fixed to the sclera and may extend onto the peripheral corneal epithelium. The lesions can darken and may increase in size during childhood and adolescence {12187419;34424954}.

Acquired (secondary) conjunctival epithelial melanosis occurs with increasing age and ionizing ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure, and can be associated with underlying systemic conditions, for example, treatment with calcium blockers and/or in the setting of Addison disease {19128387;34424954}.

Epidemiology

Conjunctival epithelial melanosis is common. It is frequent in people with Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI, occurring with lower frequency in White populations {19128387;34424954}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown but probably multifactorial. Proposed causes include UV irradiation, hormonal changes, chemical irritation, or chronic inflammatory conjunctival disorders {8309267;23222568;34424954}.

Pathogenesis

Like in hyperpigmentation of the skin, the eumelanin-to-pheomelanin ratio and the size of melanosomes within the conjunctival melanocytes, and how they react to external factors, have been proposed to play a role {33999447}. Melanocytes transfer melanosomes to keratinocytes to such an extent that the pigmented keratinocytes can outnumber the melanocytes. Mechanisms relating to this transfer are unclear and require investigation {33005614}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Conjunctival epithelial melanosis appears microscopically as increased melanin pigmentation predominantly within keratinocytes. The melanocytes are normal in number or show only a slightly increased density and are located predominantly within basal epithelium. They retain normal dendritic morphology. Features such as nuclear enlargement, prominent nucleoli, and mitoses are not present {19128387;33130046}. Melanophages may be present in the underlying conjunctival stroma.

Immunohistochemistry

Conjunctival melanocytes are positive for melanocytic markers, including melan-A, MITF, HMB45, and SOX10.

Differential diagnosis

This includes other conjunctival melanocytic lesions, such as naevi and conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia/lesions, as well as other ocular surface lesions, such as pterygia or squamous cell carcinoma in situ with hyperpigmentation {19128387;21417906;26556007;33130046}.

Cytology

See Histopathology. FNAB or impression cytology is usually insufficient for definitive diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Usually not required

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mottled or patchy intraepithelial pigmented lesion not fixed to the sclera; histological features demonstrating conjunctival melanocytes without significant increase in number or atypia.

Desirable: immunohistochemistry – melan-A, MITF, HMB45, and/or SOX10 expression (preferably using a red chromogen).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is very good. Malignant transformation into conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia / conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions / primary acquired melanosis with atypia and/or conjunctival melanoma is exceedingly rare {21417906}.
