9.3.2.2 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL) is an extranodal lymphoma of NK or T-cell lineage, characterized by vascular damage and destruction, prominent necrosis, cytotoxic phenotype, and association with EBV.

ICD-O coding

9719/3 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A90.6 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type

Related terminology

Acceptable: extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type.

Not recommended: angiocentric lymphoma; lethal midline granuloma; malignant midline reticulosis; polymorphic reticulosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ocular adnexal (OA) ENKTL usually arises from secondary spread from the paranasal sinuses or nasal cavity but metastasis from cutaneous or systemic disease is possible. It may involve the eyelid, conjunctiva/caruncle, orbital soft tissues, and lacrimal system. The orbit and lacrimal gland are most frequently involved. Intraocular and CNS involvement may occur in advanced cases. Primary orbital involvement without evidence of nasal mucosal involvement is rare {10571346;18438613;16360212;28399341;22681504;28966461;34763717}.

Clinical features

OA-ENKTL may present as proptosis and/or diplopia with limited ocular motility, reduced vision, pain, and chemosis. Nasal cavity involvement may present as nasal blockage and epistaxis {10571346;18438613;16360212;22681504;28966461;30144455;34763717}.

Epidemiology

OA-ENKTL accounts for < 1–2% of all OA lymphomas. It is prevalent in eastern Asia and in the Indigenous peoples of Central and South America, although cases in White populations are documented. It generally occurs in people aged > 50 years, but has been reported in younger patients, and shows male predominance {17846377;10571346;9709754;27894880;21336457;27118749;28399341}.

Etiology

ENKTL is strongly associated with EBV infection, which is present in a clonal episomal form with a type II latency pattern, and shows a 30 bp deletion in the LMP1 gene {22681504;8067515;8388175;9192774;9720504;10414505;22314189;22371452;31822801;32183952} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

ENKTL can occur in immunosuppressed patients, including after transplantation {10930998;11275994;14631361}. Other risk factors include lifestyle and environmental factors, with increased risk reported among farmers and pesticide users {17066445}.

Pathogenesis

A variety of complex genomic alterations have been reported, with region deletion 6q21-25 at chromosome 6q being the most common {16049916;19965620;29097495}. In the 6q21 region, several tumour suppressor genes, including PRDM1, ATG5, AIM1, HACE1, and FOXO3, have been identified {19194464;21690554;25612622}. Recurrent mutations have also been reported in the JAK/STAT pathway genes (STAT3, JAK3, STAT5B), epigenetic regulators (BCOR, KMT2D, ARID1A, EP300), tumour suppressor genes (TP53, MGA), and RNA helicase (DDX3X) {11733360;22705984;25980440;26192917} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

Macroscopic appearance

OA-ENKTL presents as a fleshy mass often with haemorrhage, necrosis, and/or ulceration.

Histopathology

OA-ENKTL shows diffuse infiltration of variably sized atypical lymphoid cells with an angiocentric-angiodestructive growth pattern and coagulative necrosis; scattered apoptotic bodies and a heavy admixture of small lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils, and histiocytes; and numerous abnormal mitoses.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic cells are CD2+, cytoplasmic CD3ε+, CD56+, CD43+, and CD8+/−; and positive for cytotoxic markers (TIA1, granzyme B, and perforin). They are usually negative for other NK- and T-cell antigens (CD16, CD57, surface CD3, CD4, CD5).

EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) in situ hybridization positivity is essential.

Differential diagnosis

EBV-negative extranodal cytotoxic T-cell lymphoma is classed as PTCL-NOS.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In situ hybridization for EBER is positive in the majority of tumour cells. TR gene rearrangement studies may be performed for select problematic cases {25188863}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: extranodal infiltration by lymphoma cells with variable morphology; NK/cytotoxic T-cell phenotype; EBER-positive.

Desirable: angiocentric-angiodestructive growth; necrosis.

Staging

The Lugano system is used to stage systemic ENKTL {25113753}.

For primary OA-ENKTL, the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and eighth-edition TNM staging systems better predict outcome and are preferred over the Ann Arbor staging system {31768360;28399341;27894880;24180616}.

Prognosis and prediction

With systemic chemoradiotherapy, the 5-year overall survival rate in early stage disease is 54% {26873565;32105608;33054461}. Patients > 60 years, advanced systemic disease, and higher levels of circulating EBV DNA have a significantly worse prognosis {26873565;32105608;33718104}.
