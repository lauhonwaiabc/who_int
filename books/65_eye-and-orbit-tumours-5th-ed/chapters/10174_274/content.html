10.1.7.4 PEComa

Definition

PEComas are mesenchymal neoplasms composed of perivascular epithelioid cells (PECs) – distinctive epithelioid cells closely associated with blood vessel walls which express both melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

ICD-O coding

8714/0 Perivascular epithelioid tumour, benign

8714/3 Perivascular epithelioid tumour, malignant

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH4CC6 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Perivascular epithelioid tumour, benign

2B5F.2 & XH9WD1 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Perivascular epithelioid tumour, malignant

Related terminology

Acceptable: angiomyolipoma; epithelioid angiomyolipoma.

Not recommended: clear cell myomelanocytic tumour; clear cell sugar tumour.

Subtype(s)

Perivascular epithelioid tumour, malignant

Localization

PEComas may arise in orbital soft tissues as intraconal or extraconal masses {15803216;30320097;28905842;18625958;34291749;26925281;28576480;28063419;26754205;26975036}, in the choroid {32676967} and ciliary body {27231690;20236689}, or superficially involving the eyelid or canthus {31609834;20236689;32676967}. Metastatic PEComa to the orbit has also been reported {30002939}.

Clinical features

PEComas in and around the eye usually present as slow-growing masses {32676967}. Less frequent symptoms include proptosis {28576480;28905842;30320097}, lid swelling {31609834;20236689}, and loss of vision with intraocular tumours {27231690}.

Epidemiology

PEComas arising in orbital/periorbital locations are very rare; 19 cases have been reported in the literature. The age range at presentation for ophthalmic PEComas is broad (2–54 years), and there is a slight male predilection.

Etiology

Most PEComas are sporadic, but a small subset is associated with tuberous sclerosis {11406657;16327428;18223480}. One PEComa arising in the eyelid was associated with tuberous sclerosis {31609834}.

Pathogenesis

Loss of heterozygosity involving the TSC2 locus and resulting in activation of the mTOR pathway occurs in most sporadic and tuberous sclerosis–associated tumours {18085521}. Deletion of 16p, the location of the TSC2 gene, indicates the oncogenetic relationship of PEComas and angiomyolipomas as TSC2-linked neoplasms. TP53 mutations have been identified in 63% of TSC2-mutated PEComas {25651471}. A small subset of PEComas harbour TFE3 gene fusions, which correlate with strong nuclear immunoreactivity for TFE3 {20871214}. The most common fusion partner with TFE3 is SFPQ (PSF) {26274027;19606011;25651471}. DVL2::TFE3 and NONO::TFE3 gene fusions have also been identified in PEComa arising in soft tissue {26975036}. TFE3-rearranged PEComa lacks TSC2 mutations / loss of heterozygosity and thus appears to be pathogenetically distinct {25651471;22456611}. NONO::TFE3 fusions have been reported in one canthal and two orbital PEComas {34291749;26975036;32676967}. PRCC::TFE3 fusion was reported in a choroidal PEComa {32676967}.

Macroscopic appearance

PEComas are well circumscribed with a firm or fleshy cut surface, and are brown-yellow in colour. Orbital tumours can have ulceration or haemorrhage {30002939}.

Histopathology

PEComas typically show a nested growth pattern and are composed of epithelioid or spindled cells, or a mix thereof. Most orbital PEComas have an epithelioid cytomorphology with abundant granular eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm and round nuclei with small nucleoli. The nests are surrounded by thin-walled capillaries. PEComa usually shows a distinctive perivascular pattern of growth, with tumour cells radially arranged around vessels, replacing the vessel wall and approaching the endothelium. Hyalinization of the tumour stroma may be present {30320097}.

Malignant PEComas generally show variable degrees of nuclear atypia/pleomorphism, mitotic activity, and necrosis {28905842;30002939;28576480}.

Immunohistochemistry

PEComas characteristically express both melanocytic markers, such as HMB45 (the most sensitive immunomarker), melan-A, and MITF, and muscle markers, such as SMA, desmin, and caldesmon {19604538;18223480;24061520;30001237;11800647}. Expression of melanocytic markers is more common in epithelioid than spindled tumour cells. Desmin and h-caldesmon are often less extensively positive than SMA {18223480}. Some tumours lack expression of muscle markers {19604538}.

Approximately 15% of cases show strong nuclear staining for TFE3 {26975036;16327428;24061520;20871214}. PEComas with TFE3 rearrangement tend to occur in younger patients, have a prominent alveolar growth pattern and epithelioid morphology, and often lack expression of smooth muscle markers {26975036;22456611;26274027;25651471}.

Differential diagnosis

In the orbit, the differential diagnosis of PEComa includes alveolar soft part sarcoma, paraganglioma, and melanoma.

Cytology

Cytology has not been reported for primary cases. Cytological features in 1 case metastatic to the orbit showed epithelioid cells with prominent nucleoli in a haemorrhagic background and an absence of melanin pigmentation {30002939}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

For cases with strong TFE3 protein expression, identification of TFE3 gene rearrangement or a corresponding fusion gene can help confirm the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid and/or spindle cells with granular eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm; nested, trabecular, or sheet-like architecture with frequent perivascular orientation; variable coexpression of melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

Desirable: TFE3 expression if smooth muscle markers are negative.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

While there is limited data specific to ophthalmic PEComa, across all anatomical sites, clinically malignant PEComas are typically large (> 50 mm); show marked nuclear atypia and pleomorphism, conspicuous mitoses, necrosis, and infiltrative margins {16327428;30001237;22619565}; and pursue an aggressive clinical course. The most common metastatic sites are the liver, lymph nodes, lungs, and bone.
