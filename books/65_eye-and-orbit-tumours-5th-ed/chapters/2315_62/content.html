2.3.1.5 Trichilemmoma

Definition

Trichilemmoma is a benign tumour that differentiates towards the outer root sheath epithelium of the hair follicle infundibulum (trichilemmal differentiation).

ICD-O coding

8102/0 Trichilemmoma

8102/0 Desmoplastic trichilemmoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH5AU2 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Trichilemmoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Desmoplastic trichilemmoma

Localization

The central face is the most common site of involvement, including the eyebrow and eyelid {6990903}. Rarely, the lid margin or inner canthus can be involved. Lesions can be solitary or multiple.

Clinical features

Trichilemmoma is typically a slow-growing flesh-coloured papule or nodule of 1–5 mm, although it rarely may be > 10 mm {6990903}. Trichilemmoma can also present as a papillomatous lesion mimicking verruca vulgaris. The desmoplastic subtype may mimic invasive carcinoma {6990903}.

Epidemiology

Trichilemmoma affects a wide age range, although the majority of cases are seen in adults. There is a predominance in White people and an equal sex distribution {6990903}. Lesions are typically solitary. Multiple lesions or paediatric presentation raises the possibility of Cowden syndrome {24136893} (a member of the PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome) {25132236}, in which the prevalence of trichilemmomas is 6–38% {21194675;21659347;20600018;23335809}.

Etiology

The etiology of sporadic lesions is unknown. Owing to the papillomatous appearance of some trichilemmomas, a link to HPV infection has been suggested, although there is limited published evidence {9021542;12920361}.

Multiple lesions are associated with Cowden syndrome (a member of the PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome) {24136893}, an autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutations in PTEN.

Pathogenesis

For solitary lesions, the pathogenesis is unknown.

For trichilemmomas occurring in Cowden syndrome, mutations in the PTEN gene cause absence of PTEN inhibition, causing AKT activation, resulting in phosphorylation of proteins such as mTOR that affect cell growth, cell-cycle entry, and cell survival {12040186}.

Macroscopic appearance

Skin-coloured firm papule or wart-like lesion

Histopathology

Conventional trichilemmoma: At low magnification, the lesion can be exophytic / wart like or bulbous, can be associated with a pre-existing hair follicle, often shows an epidermal collarette, and is connected to the epidermis. There may be hyperkeratosis and parakeratosis of the overlying epidermis. The tumour is lobulated and often surrounded by an eosinophilic, thickened basement membrane, resembling the basement membrane of the lower hair follicle. The lobules are composed of eosinophilic, bland squamoid cells, some of which exhibit trichilemmal differentiation resembling the hair follicle outer root sheath cells. This is characterized by cytoplasmic glycogen vacuolation within bland, monomorphic cells, without mitotic activity or pleomorphism. The edges of the lobules display a palisade with columnar cells that can show reverse polarity. Squamous eddies / morules are often present in the lesion {4711118;6990903}.

Desmoplastic trichilemmoma: This has identical features to a conventional trichilemmoma except there is a central sclerotic area of excessive basement membrane–like material embedded within which are irregular epithelial strands that show little glycogen vacuolation. These strands mimic invasive carcinoma {2319039;1373583}.

Immunohistochemistry

Trichilemmomas express CD34 in the tumour epithelial cells (which can be rather focal) and p16 {19461237;18976402}, and are negative with BerEP4.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes inverted follicular keratosis, viral wart, tumour of the follicular infundibulum, eccrine poroma, basal cell carcinoma with trichilemmal differentiation, invasive squamous cell carcinoma, trichilemmal carcinoma {2730855}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not performed routinely for solitary tumours in adults. For multiple trichilemmomas or those presenting in children, germline testing for PTEN mutations may be indicated in the appropriate clinical context. Loss of PTEN, seen with immunohistochemistry, is more frequent in syndromic tumours and may be useful as an initial screening tool {22486434}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for conventional trichilemmoma: bland squamoproliferative lesion connected to the epidermis with a palisade, showing trichilemmal differentiation characterized by cytoplasmic glycogen vacuolation; for desmoplastic trichilemmoma: as for conventional trichilemmoma, with a central area of excessive basement membrane–like material containing irregular epithelial strands, mimicking invasive carcinoma.

Desirable: demonstration of CD34 immunohistochemical positivity in morphologically challenging lesions; loss of PTEN staining in suspected Cowden syndrome.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Trichilemmoma is a benign lesion. Surgical excision is curative.
