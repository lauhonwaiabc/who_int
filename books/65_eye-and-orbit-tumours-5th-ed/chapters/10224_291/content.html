10.2.2.4 Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma

Definition

Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma is a high-grade, malignant, biphasic, primitive mesenchymal tumour with a well-differentiated, organized hyaline cartilage component.

ICD-O coding

9240/3 Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B50.Z & XH8X47 Chondrosarcoma of bone or articular cartilage of unspecified sites & Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma occurs in the orbital bones and soft tissues {15121382;29755276}; it can be adherent to the optic nerve or entirely intraneural {29863559;20813303;22761622}. It may also involve the lacrimal gland {29342030} and the lid {2044980}.

Clinical features

Orbital mesenchymal chondrosarcoma presents with nonspecific symptoms such as proptosis, ptosis, diplopia, loss of vision, and pain {18188507;28812954;18307151}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 50 cases of primary orbital mesenchymal chondrosarcoma have been reported {32835558;32257101;18188507}. There is a predilection for the second and third decades of life and for women, although mesenchymal chondrosarcoma also occurs in young children {29755276;15121382}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma arises from immature cartilage precursors and develops via HEY1::NCOA2 fusion {35342947}. The fusion of HEY1 at 8q21.13 and NCOA2 at 8q13.3 results in the HEY1::NCOA2 fusion gene, which appears to play a role in extraskeletal mesenchymal chondrosarcoma {22034177;29582189}. IRF2BP2::CDX1 fusion is reported in a single case of mesenchymal chondrosarcoma {29582189}. However, not much information on fusion genes is available on orbital mesenchymal chondrosarcomas.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is lobulated and well defined, and contains areas of cartilage, calcification, and haemorrhage {5096926;32835558;8293407}.

Histopathology

Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma reveals two components: foci of well-differentiated hyaline cartilage that have a zonal appearance as endochondral ossification {20123451;20138330} and a mesenchymal tumour composed of undifferentiated small round or spindled cells with a high N:C ratio. These two areas can be indistinct or separated. Areas of necrosis and brisk mitotic activity may be observed. There is almost always a distinctive haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern in the mesenchymal portion of the tumour {5096926;8293407}.

Immunohistochemistry

While immunostains are not specific, SOX9 and focal S100 are often positive in the round cells. CD99 can be positive. The tumour is usually negative for CD34, cytokeratin, EMA, and muscle markers. NKX3-1 (coding gene located at 8p21.1) immunohistochemistry expression in variable {31972596;33302222;32779239}.

Differential diagnosis

Orbital mesenchymal chondrosarcoma diagnosis is challenging if the biopsy sample is small and there is no cartilage component. The main differential diagnoses for the round cell tumour component include synovial sarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and other round cell tumours, and if the tumour shows spindle cells, then synovial sarcoma and malignant solitary fibrous tumour have to be considered {29755276}.

Cytology

There is a biphasic appearance of medium-sized cells, oval to spindled in CNS tumours, that have a high N:C ratio and basophilic extracellular matrix {12925982;20123451;20138330}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of a HEY1::NCOA2 fusion {22034177;29582189} may be helpful in the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: presence of both a cartilage component and the undifferentiated small round to spindled cells.

Desirable: staghorn vasculature; round cells positive for SOX9 and focal S100; detection of a HEY1::NCOA2 fusion gene.

Staging

Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma of the orbit is staged according to the TNM system described by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC), based on the tumour size, involvement of lymph nodes, and presence of metastases.

Prognosis and prediction

Overall, the prognosis for orbital mesenchymal chondrosarcoma is reported to be slightly better than that of other locations {29755276}. Local recurrence and distant metastasis can occur several years after treatment and, therefore, a long follow-up is indicated {35186692;22761622;18188507}.
