2.3.2.4 Microcystic adnexal carcinoma

Definition

Microcystic adnexal carcinoma (MAC) is a locally infiltrative adnexal carcinoma with bland cytology and focal sweat ductal / syringomatoid and/or follicular/trichoepithelial differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8407/3 Microcystic adnexal carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH17P2 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Microcystic adnexal carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: sclerosing sweat duct carcinoma.

Not recommended: malignant syringoma; eccrine epithelioma; syringomatoid carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MAC occurs in the upper and lower eyelids and the medial and lateral canthal areas. The lower eyelid is where most cases have been recorded {20065232;16491033;10201598;6329262}.

Clinical features

MAC presents as a yellow or skin-coloured firm indurated small nodule or hard plaque which can be associated with telangiectasia and marked eyelid distortion or retraction {20065232} and (rarely) ulceration and madarosis {15778661}. Its growth can be insidious, and the diagnosis can be delayed by years {20065232}. Periocular pain or numbness indicates perineural or intraneural invasion.

Epidemiology

Eyelid MAC occurs predominantly in White people. The age range is 18–95 years (average: 52 years). There is a female predilection {20065232;16491033;10201598;6329262;20151289;9613389;7710401;14767643}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

MAC can show sweat duct and/or follicular differentiation {7093897;2187025;3513708;1689137;21252639;7691906}. Next-generation sequencing revealed a low mutation burden, and p53 and JAK/STAT signalling pathway mutations, but no ultraviolet (UV) radiation signature {31857679}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

MAC is an infiltrative epithelial neoplasm that involves the deep dermis and typically extends to the subcutis. It is composed of small, bland, monomorphic epithelial cells forming small nests, strands, or columns associated with various proportions of trichoepithelioma-like keratocysts and/or syringomatoid, duct-like structures composed of two layers of small cuboidal cells (empty or filled with eosinophilic material). Clear cell differentiation can be seen.

Perineural or muscle invasion is common. Mitoses are rare or absent. The stroma can be sclerotic or tumour cells may infiltrate without much stromal alteration. MAC expresses cytokeratins (e.g. AE1/AE3, CK5/6) and p63. Ductal differentiation can be highlighted by staining for EMA or CEA {25873411;1689137}. Staining for GCDFP-15 and with BerEP4 is negative. CK20-positive Merkel cells typically seen in trichoepithelioma are absent {26016446}.

Diagnosis of MAC on a superficial biopsy can be impossible as the histological features may resemble trichoepithelioma (infundibular keratocysts), syringoma (tadpole-like ductal structures), or infiltrating basal cell carcinoma (infiltrating nests and strands) {10949455;18065959;11257620;22722464;1689137;21131826}. In some cases diagnosis may have to be deferred to a re-excision {26760686}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a highly infiltrative tumour composed of small, bland, monomorphic epithelial cells forming small nests, strands, or columns associated with various proportions of trichoepithelioma-like keratocysts and/or syringomatoid, duct-like structures composed of two layers of small cuboidal cells.

Desirable: perineural invasion.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system for non-melanoma eyelid carcinoma applies.

Prognosis and prediction

MAC is a locally aggressive carcinoma. If treated by standard wide local excision, there is a high rate of recurrence {16491033;32600650} with long-term follow-up. Mohs micrographic surgery is more effective at clearing tumour and is associated with a lower recurrence compared with standard surgical excision {16491033;26974419}. Exenteration may be required for orbit invasive tumours {20065232}. Adjuvant radiotherapy has been used after surgery, in the presence of perineural invasion, or as a primary modality in unresectable cases {20065232;32600650;9349155;26974419}. Regional lymph node and distant metastases are extremely rare {14657634;11074698;10426893;16549917;17122497;11298716}.
