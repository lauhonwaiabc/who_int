2.2.2.3 Merkel cell carcinoma of the eyelid

Definition

Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) is the eponymous name for primary cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma.

ICD-O coding

8247/3 Merkel cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C34 & XH81N8 Cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma & Merkel cell carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: primary cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma.

Not recommended: trabecular carcinoma; Toker tumour; primary small cell carcinoma of the skin.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority of MCCs occur on sun-damaged skin, most commonly in the head and neck region (mainly the cheek and eyelids) and less frequently on the extremities and trunk.

Clinical features

MCC is a rapidly growing, indurated, reddish-blue, non-ulcerating nodule on sun-exposed skin of elderly White patients. Lesions are usually painless, with occasional ulceration {29229574}. Eyelid tumours are often misdiagnosed as chalazion, delaying diagnosis and treatment {30871952;24287584}.

Epidemiology

The estimated incidence is 0.25–0.32 cases per 100 000 person-years {14576661; 21144740}. MCC is relatively more common in men, and the median age at diagnosis is 73.6 years. Incidence increases several-fold in individuals aged > 85 years, patients with AIDS, solid-organ transplant recipients, and patients with leukaemia.

Etiology

Exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation, immunosuppression, advanced age, and infection with Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) are risk factors.

Pathogenesis

Although the precise interaction of the risk factors in development of MCC is not clear, MCPyV is reported to be linked to development of MCC in 60–80% of tumours. Viral integration in the genome acts on T-antigen transcripts enhancing viral replication and clonal expansion of MCC cells. Viral-negative MCC reveals UV radiation damage in the form of CC to TT transition at dipyrimidine bases, and mutations in RB1, TP53, and NOTCH family genes, deregulation of p53 protein expression, and aberrations in the P13K/AKT/mTOR pathway. Mutation tumour burdens are high in viral-negative MCC {32040877;33960199;36763882}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions usually occur as well-circumscribed dermal/subcutaneous nodules, with occasional cutaneous ulceration in larger tumours.

Histopathology

A high-grade small blue cell neoplasm is seen, composed of uniform cells with round/oval nuclei, scant cytoplasm, distinct nuclear membranes, finely granular chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli. Sheets of tumour cells diffusely infiltrate the dermis and subcutaneous tissue. There is a trabecular growth pattern, and ribbons or festoons can be seen in the periphery of the mass {17126260}. Pseudorosettes are rare. Pagetoid spread within the epidermis may be present; rarely, the tumour is entirely confined in the epidermis {10698220}. Mitotic figures are numerous. Large lesions may show ulceration, zonal necrosis, and angiolymphatic invasion. Areas of squamoid or adnexal differentiation, and infrequently sarcomatoid differentiation, are observed in a minority of cases {11486166}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry shows positivity for CK20, low-molecular-weight keratins (dot-like pattern), and neuroendocrine markers (synaptophysin, chromogranin, INSM1, CD56); and negativity for CK7, CD45, thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), melanocytic markers, and CDX2. Staining with CM2B4 (MCPyV antibody, MCPyV nuclear stain) is positive in most cases {33128463}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes metastatic lung small cell carcinoma, other neuroendocrine tumours, lymphoma/leukaemia, melanoma, round cell sarcomas, and poorly differentiated carcinoma {19395876}.

Cytology

Dispersed cells with scant cytoplasm, round nuclei, finely granular chromatin, and no nucleoli are seen. Apoptotic bodies and background necrosis may be present {24678011}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MCPyV by PCR varies from 24% {18650846} to 85% {18633441}. Studies have shown gains of chromosomes 1, 5, 6, 18q, and 20, and loss of 10q23; rearrangements of 1p and 3q; and trisomy 6 and 1p deletion. Mutations in TP53 and HRAS have also been observed {26818033}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a blue cell tumour in the dermis with salt-and-pepper neuroendocrine cytology; immunophenotype of neuroendocrine differentiation (INSM1, chromogranin, and synaptophysin positivity; paranuclear dot-like staining for cytokeratin); negative for TTF-1.

Desirable: MCPyV positivity.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) eighth-edition TNM staging system is used, separating clinical and pathological staging groups and providing more accurate prognostication {27198511}.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis in patients with MCC is mostly affected by metastasis: the 5-year overall survival rate is 50.6% for patients with local disease, 35.4% for patients with nodal metastasis, and 13.5% for patients with distant metastasis {27198511}. T category, based on tumour size, has a significant impact on prognosis: the 5-year overall survival rates are 55.8% for T1, 41.1% for T2/3, and 31.8% for T4 in patients with local disease only. Additional features associated with worse prognosis are infiltrative growth pattern, lymphovascular invasion, presence of tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, older age, male sex, tumour site, and immunosuppression {25784178;19638070}. Sentinel lymph node biopsy is recommended by some authors {24287584;12003618}.
