7.1.0.1 Dacryops cyst

Definition

Dacryops cyst results from dilatation of the lacrimal gland duct/ductules.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A10.Y Other specified disorders of lacrimal gland

Related terminology

Not recommended: ranula of the lacrimal gland.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Dacryops cysts are most common in the palpebral lobe (simple dacryops); less common in the deeper orbital lobe, accessory lacrimal glands of Krause and Wolfring, ectopic (choristomatous) lacrimal glands; and least common in the glands of Popoff in the caruncle {6330636;23114708;31512543;31914837}.

Clinical features

Dacryops cyst commonly presents as an asymptomatic swelling, eyelid fullness, or mild irritation. Symptoms differ based on location.

On CT, these are usually well-defined, commonly uniloculated fluid-dense lesions anterior to the orbital rim, with absence of nodular enhancement, and bony changes. T2-weighted MRI helps in the delineation of the cystic lesion, which is isointense with the vitreous.

Epidemiology

Lesions are rare, with reported incidence varying from 1% to 17% among lacrimal gland lesions and from 0.5% to 2% among orbital lesions. They commonly arise in the fourth to fifth decade of life but can occur at any age (reported range: 18 months to 78 years), with a variable sex preponderance {31821863;23114708;15121380}. They are commonly unilateral but may be bilateral {31593037;15615995;22909413}.

Etiology

Most cysts are thought to be caused by persistent chronic inflammation, trauma, chemical injury, or infection {22571599;23114708}.

Pathogenesis

Proposed mechanisms for duct dilatation resulting in cyst formation include lacrimal gland hypersecretion or outflow obstruction by chronic periductal inflammation or fibrosis of the parenchyma, and dysfunction of the periductal/ductular neural plexus {1454325;31512543;23114708;23846204}.

Macroscopic appearance

Cysts appear as a uniloculated to (rarely) multiloculated smooth-walled cystic mass.

Histopathology

The cyst is lined by non-keratinized, double-layered columnar to cuboidal epithelium with dispersed goblet cells. The luminal cells may show focal apical projections and microvilli. Lumina may show globular eosinophilic to mucoid secretions. The wall of the cyst shows remnants of lacrimal gland acinar and variably dilated ductal structures. There may be patchy chronic inflammation and fibrosis.

Immunohistochemistry

CK7 and CK18 highlight the lining epithelium; the latter is more intense superficially. Epithelial cells are negative for CK17, SMA, S100, and GCDFP-15. The luminal surfaces of luminal cells have been reported to strongly express IgA {23114708;31821863;23846204}.

Differential diagnosis

Dacryoadenitis, prolapsed orbital fat, prolapsed lacrimal gland, dermolipoma, epithelial tumours of the lacrimal gland, foreign body granuloma, respiratory cyst, conjunctival inclusion cyst, dermoid or epidermoid cyst, mucocoele, sarcoidosis, and lymphoma can all mimic dacryops cysts {23114708;31512543}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cystic lesion lined by non-keratinized double-layered epithelium, with dispersed goblet cells; presence of lacrimal gland acini and variably dilated ducts in the wall of the cyst.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The outcome is good after complete surgical removal and marsupialization. However, recurrences, fistula formation after marsupialization, and secondary tear deficiency have been reported {21956544;23114708;31512543}.
