4.3.2.3 Glioneuronal hamartoma of the ciliary body

Definition

Glioneuronal hamartoma of the ciliary body is a tumour-like neuroglial lesion occurring at the margin of the optic cup, often with an adjacent chorioretinal coloboma.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA11.Y Other specified structural developmental anomalies of the anterior segment of eye

Related terminology

Acceptable: glioneuroma of the ciliary body.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Localization is in the ciliary body, typically in the temporal quadrant, with frequent extension to the iris and retina {6703991;2622623;4697214;4697215}.

Clinical features

Lesions appear as a tan, slowly growing mass, usually detected before the first year of life, often at birth. Extraocular extension may be present {4697215;2622623}. A chorioretinal coloboma may coexist but not be clinically detectable {2622623;4697215}. The eye may secondarily be smaller or larger than the contralateral eye, the cornea and lens may be cloudy {4697215;6703991}, a persistent pupillary membrane or hyaloid artery may be present {4697214;4697215}, and the intraocular pressure may be elevated {2622623;4697215}. The tumour enhances with contrast medium on CT {2622623}.

Epidemiology

Lesions are very rare. Fewer than 10 cases have been published {6703991;2622623;4697214;4697215}.

Etiology

Lesions arise as a congenital developmental mass {2622623;4697215}. There is no association with neurofibromatosis type 1 or 2.

Pathogenesis

One possibility is that the developing choristoma interferes with the normal development of the adjacent iris, ciliary body, choroid, and retina, and causes a secondary coloboma. Alternatively, faulty differentiation of the optic cup could result in formation of both heterotopic brain tissue and a coloboma {2622623}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions appear as a pink, greyish, or yellowish mass in the ciliary body.

Histopathology

The tumour consists of fibrillary astrocytes, an eosinophilic matrix of glial and neuronal processes, clusters of neuronal cells with small round nuclei, and scattered large ganglion cells. Calcospherites, dysplastic rosettes, and tubules composed of several rows of cells that resemble Homer Wright and Flexner–Wintersteiner rosettes may be present. Mitotic figures are very rare or absent. The glial component is immunopositive for GFAP and the neuronal component for synaptophysin and neurofilament proteins {2622623}. The ciliary epithelium over the mass shows reactive proliferation. The adjacent retina may be dysplastic and may extend to the chorioretinal coloboma when one is present {2622623;4697215}. Glioneuroma of the ciliary body must be distinguished from teratoid medulloepithelioma containing mature brain tissue {655220;2622623;4697214}.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not reported

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: histologically mature neuroglial tissue unassociated with neoplastic neuroepithelium; an adjacent coloboma, when present, is a helpful sign.

Staging

Not available

Prognosis and prediction

Lesions are curable early on by surgical excision, with negligible risk of recurrence.
