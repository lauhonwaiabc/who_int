4.2.1.2 Reactive hyperplasia of the retinal pigment epithelium

Definition

Reactive hyperplasia of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) is a non-neoplastic proliferation of RPE cells.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9B7Y Other specified disorders of the retina

Related terminology

Acceptable: nodular hyperplasia of the retinal pigment epithelium.

Not recommended: pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia; pseudoadenomatous hyperplasia; reactive hyperplastic lesion of the retinal pigment epithelium; ringschwiele.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Reactive hyperplasia of the RPE can occur anywhere in the retina {11782230}.

Clinical features

RPE hyperplasia is classified as localized, multifocal, or diffuse. It presents as darkly pigmented deposits. The pigment may be associated with retinal blood vessels, resulting in a spiculated appearance.

Nodular reactive RPE hyperplasia can be confused with adenomatous RPE tumours or choroidal melanoma. On fluorescein angiography, reactive RPE hyperplasia shows early hypofluorescence, with areas of hyperfluorescence in the late phase. It shows high reflectivity on ultrasound {11782230}.

Epidemiology

The incidence is unknown. Reactive hyperplasia of the RPE can occur in patients of any age and either sex. Multifocal hyperplasia may be seen in older people.

Etiology

The etiology of primary hyperplasia is unknown. Secondary hyperplasia can occur secondary to congenital abnormalities, inflammation, degenerative diseases, intoxications, radiation-induced choroiditis, chronic retinal detachment, subretinal haemorrhage, sequelae of traumatic retinopathy, or retinitis pigmentosa {5965122;11782230}. There may be a chronic vitreous base and zonular traction.

Pathogenesis

The proliferation of RPE cells is induced by growth factors.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Reactive RPE hyperplasia can range from simple duplication of cells to massive proliferations with intervening basement membrane material; in advanced, late-stage cases, the hyperplasia may be accompanied by collections of connective and osteoid tissue {5965122}.

In nodular reactive proliferation of the RPE, the cells are arranged in a tubular pseudoadenomatous pattern, with a large amount of basement membrane–like material. Areas of extracellular material alternate with areas of cellular proliferation. Neoplastic features, mitotic figures, and necrosis are absent {5049451}.

Differential diagnosis

Reactive hyperplasia of the RPE must be distinguished from choroidal melanoma, RPE adenoma, RPE adenocarcinoma, congenital hypertrophy of the RPE, combined hamartoma of the retina and RPE, and simple hamartoma of the RPE.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: darkly pigmented deposits in the retina, which may be associated with retinal blood vessels and/or nodular; proliferation of the RPE, often with intervening basement membrane material.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These lesions are benign; the survival prognosis is excellent. The visual prognosis depends on the underlying process. Longstanding RPE hyperplasia in disorganized, blind eyes may rarely give rise to RPE adenocarcinoma {9565054;18303161;8871153;17613862}.
