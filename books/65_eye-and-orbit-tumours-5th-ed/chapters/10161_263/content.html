10.1.6.1 Masson tumour

Definition

Masson tumour is a benign vascular lesion composed of endothelial proliferation, usually within the blood vessel.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms

Related terminology

Acceptable: intravascular papillary endothelial hyperplasia.

Not recommended: intravascular angiomatosis; vegetant intravascular haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

Primary (pure) Masson tumour; secondary (mixed) Masson tumour; extravascular Masson tumour

Localization

Masson tumour rarely affects periocular tissues. The eyelids are most commonly involved, followed by the orbit and conjunctiva {34615394}.

Clinical features

Eyelid and conjunctival lesions usually present as a painless, slow-growing, solid or cystic, red-purple vascular mass {34615394}. Orbital lesions may present with proptosis.

Imaging

MRI demonstrates a well-circumscribed homogeneously enhancing mass, which is isointense on T1-weighted imaging and hyperintense on T2-weighted imaging {31903303}.

Epidemiology

It is estimated that Masson tumour accounts for approximately 2% of all benign and malignant vascular tumours of the skin and subcutaneous tissues {22974134}. This lesion has no clear sex, age, or race predilection. There is a wide age distribution, ranging from 9 months to 80 years {22974134;34615394}.

Etiology

Masson tumour is currently believed to be a reactive lesion, associated with organized thrombus formation and recanalization within a dilated vascular space, pre-existing vascular abnormality, or haematoma {24294378}.

Pathogenesis

Vascular proliferation may be driven by thrombus-induced hypoxia, as supported by the upregulation of HIF1α and VEGF expression in the proliferating vascular endothelium {24294378;27323848}.

Macroscopic appearance

The lesion appears grossly as a circumscribed red-purple mass with a cystic component.

Histopathology

Masson tumour is a circumscribed lesion composed of papillary, interconnecting fibrous trabeculae lined by a single layer of flattened or plump endothelial cells in a background of fibrin thrombi. Primary lesions are observed within a dilated normal vascular channel, most frequently a vein. Secondary lesions arise as focal changes in the setting of a pre-existing vascular abnormality, such as a vascular malformation. A third, rare, subtype appears as an extravascular papillary endothelial proliferation in an organizing haematoma {34615394;6666836}.

Immunohistochemistry (generally not required for diagnosis) demonstrates positivity of lesional cells for CD31, CD34, ERG, and SMA {35297528}.

Differential diagnosis

An absence of infiltrative margins, significant nuclear atypia, frequent mitotic figures, and necrosis help distinguish Masson tumour from angiosarcoma {31903303;947306;6685471}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: circumscribed papillary lesion lined by benign endothelial cells with hyalinized cores in a background of an organizing haematoma/thrombus.

Desirable: intravascular location.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The lesion is benign with no documented malignant transformation. Complete excision is curative {34615394}. Orbital lesions can be rarely associated with visual loss from optic nerve compression {22974134}.
