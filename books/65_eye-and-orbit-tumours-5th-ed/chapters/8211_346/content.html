8.2.1.1 Primary orbital melanoma

Definition

Primary orbital melanoma is a primary malignant melanocytic neoplasm of the orbital soft tissue, without a prior history of conjunctival, uveal, or non-ocular melanoma.

ICD-O coding

8720/3 Primary orbital melanoma

ICD-11 coding

2D04 & XH1G74 Malignant neoplasm of orbit & Blue naevus, malignant

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumour is located in the orbital soft tissues, can affect any quadrant, and can be extraconal or intraconal {8643249;29326884;29388848;15121380;30605742;32900269}. It has been reported in the left orbit in 58% of cases, and the right in 42%.

Clinical features

The commonest presentations are unilateral proptosis, decreased visual acuity, periorbital pain, and diplopia. Other signs include ptosis, eyelid swelling, visual disturbances, and relative afferent pupillary defect. Imaging shows a well-circumscribed enhancing lesion, resembling a benign vascular lesion. The duration of symptoms varies between 3 and 156 months. There may be signs of ocular or oculodermal melanocytosis {8643249;29326884;29388848;15121380;30605742;32900269}.

Epidemiology

The age range of presentation is 5–91 years, with a median of 45 years. There is a male predilection (58% male and 42% female patients). Most patients are White European (95%), with very occasional cases recorded in South Asian and African Black patients {8643249;29326884;29388848;15121380;30605742;32900269}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Primary orbital melanomas are thought to arise from melanocytic cells of the leptomeninges or ciliary nerves, or from ectopic intraorbital nests of melanocytes. They can occur de novo but are usually in association with pigmentary changes of the periocular tissues – such as naevus of Ota, cellular blue naevus, or oculodermal melanocytosis. The underlying genomic alterations vary and include some that are similar but not identical to those occurring in ocular melanomas. These include mutations in GNAQ, GNA11, EIF1AX, and SF3B1, with copy-number variations including chromosomal gains in 6p, 8q, 17q, 6q, and 20p, and losses in 1p, 9p, 16q, and 17p {30558566;30605742}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface shows a well-defined tumour that can be amelanotic or variably pigmented. The tumour can be extraconal or intraconal. There may be involvement of orbital fat, fascial planes, extraocular muscle, and moulding against the sclera.

Histopathology

Two types of melanoma cells can be identified – spindle and epithelioid – identical to uveal melanoma (see section Choroidal and ciliary body melanomas) {8643249;30605742}, giving rise to spindle, mixed, or epithelioid melanomas. Mitotic figures are present, and necrosis is seen in some cases. The melanoma cells can exhibit a packeted arrangement of clones separated by extracellular matrix {8643249;30605742}. Some cases show balloon cell change {30605742}. Melanophages are commonly seen throughout the tumour. The background orbital soft tissue may show orbital melanocytosis, oculodermal melanocytosis, or orbital blue naevi (conventional and cellular) {8643249;30605742;34886849}. These are characterized by benign pigmented or non-pigmented melanocytic cells that can be spindle or multipolar, scattered individually or as groups or larger cohesive swathes, associated with sclerotic stroma and melanophages.

Immunohistochemistry

The melanoma cells are positive for S100, melan-A, HMB45, and SOX10. Some cases are nuclear BAP1-negative {30558566;30605742}.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis includes spread or metastasis of conjunctival, uveal, or non-ocular melanoma to the orbit (secondary orbital melanoma) {28690979} and orbital clear cell sarcoma {30605742}. The latter can be tested for the presence or absence of the EWSR1::ATF1 fusion gene.

Cytology

No cases reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A mutation panel comprising the following aids in the diagnosis of primary orbital melanoma in the context of the clinical history and findings: GNAQ, GNA11, BRAF, NRAS, TERT promoter, SF3B1, and EIF1AX {30558566;30605742}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cytologically malignant melanocytic cells arising in the orbital soft tissue; no previous history of conjunctival, uveal, or non-ocular melanoma.

Desirable: immunohistochemical positivity with melanocytic markers; presence of blue naevus (conventional or cellular), or oculodermal melanocytosis in background orbital soft tissue.

Staging

American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging for orbital tumours can be applied after detailed imaging investigations are undertaken to confirm the primary site of origin of the tumour and the extent of its spread.

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases are treated by exenteration, followed by adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy in a smaller number of cases {32900269}. Adjuvant radiotherapy confers a better survival advantage. Proton beam therapy can control local orbital recurrences {32900269}. Metastases develop in 36% of patients, 15% develop local recurrence, and 32% die of metastatic disease {32900269}.

Some genetic evidence suggests that mutations in SF3B1 and EIF1AX confer a more favourable prognosis {30558566}.
