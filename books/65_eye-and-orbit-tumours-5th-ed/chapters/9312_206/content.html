9.3.1.2 Follicular lymphoma

Definition

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is a B-cell neoplasm of germinal-centre B cells, composed of centrocytes and/or large transformed cells and centroblasts, usually with a pattern that is at least partially follicular.

ICD-O coding

9690/3 Follicular lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A80.Z Follicular lymphoma, unspecified

Related terminology

Not applicable

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ocular adnexal FL most frequently involves the orbital soft tissue, conjunctiva, and lacrimal gland {24763920;17255761}. Involvement of more than one ocular adnexal site can occur, although patients usually have unilateral disease {24763920;17255761}.

Clinical features

The predominant clinical signs are an enlarging mass of the ocular adnexal soft tissue and displacement of the eyeball {24763920;32906630}.

Epidemiology

The frequency of FL varies among studies. FL in one series accounts for 23% of ocular adnexal lymphomas, whereas in other series FL makes up 5–13 % of cases {17255761;14685876;16134191;32994547;32732342}. Secondary ocular adnexal FL is relatively common. In these patients the lymph nodes are the most common primary site. Most patients are middle-aged to older adults, often in their fifties or sixties, with a female predominance {24763920;17255761}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The cell of origin is a germinal-centre B cell.

The t(14;18)(q32;q21) translocation juxtaposes the BCL2 oncogene with the IGH locus, placing BCL2 under transcriptional control of immunoglobulin enhancers, leading to constitutive overexpression of the antiapoptotic BCL2, and is seen in as many as 80% of all FLs {25125069}; genetic features of ocular adnexal FL have not been widely studied. TP53 mutations and MYC rearrangements have been reported in high-grade cases {32906630}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface of tissues involved by FL may display a vaguely nodular pattern.

Histopathology

FL is characterized by closely packed, usually uniform follicles lacking polarization and often displaying a back-to-back arrangement. They are composed of an admixture of centrocytes and centroblasts, and uncommonly consist entirely of centrocytes or centroblasts. Centrocytes are small cells with scant cytoplasm and cleaved, twisted, indented, elongated, angulated nuclei having inconspicuous nucleoli and less dense chromatin. Centroblasts are large cells with round to oval nuclei, vesicular chromatin, and one to three small, peripheral nucleoli. Except in grade 3 FL, mitoses are infrequent. Tingible-body macrophages are absent or scant. Mantle zones may be thin or even absent.

Grading

Grading of classic FL (FL composed of centrocytes with a variable admixture of centroblasts) is currently considered optional. FL composed of solid aggregates of centroblasts is diagnosed as follicular large B-cell lymphoma [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic B cells express the pan–B-cell markers CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a, PAX5, and monotypic surface immunoglobulin, which is most often IgM. Germinal centre markers expressed include CD10, BCL6, GCET1, GCET2 (HGAL), LMO2, AID, MEF2B, and stathmin {34942689;25203428;23509938}. BCL2 expression helps in the distinction from reactive follicles. However, BCL2 expression may be absent in a subset of FLs, especially follicular large B-cell lymphoma {23642737}. The Ki-67 labelling index tends to correlate with the proportion of centroblasts.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis includes follicular hyperplasia (including in IgG4-related disease) and other small B-cell lymphomas. In the differential with follicular hyperplasia, crowded, poorly delineated follicles with a relatively monotonous composition expressing BCL2 and having a low proliferation index favour FL, as does the presence of centrocytes expressing germinal centre markers outside follicles. Hyperplastic follicles are typically well delineated, with intact mantles, are not crowded, and are composed of cells of a range of sizes with polarization, lacking BCL2, and having a high proliferation index.

Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma with follicular colonization can mimic FL but is composed of CD5− and CD10− B cells, and any residual reactive germinal centre cells have the immunophenotype of reactive follicles (BCL2−, high proliferation index). Small lymphocytic lymphoma / chronic lymphocytic leukaemia with prominent proliferation centres can mimic FL but is CD5+, lacks cells with the angulated nuclei of centrocytes, and lacks germinal centre markers.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Immunoglobulin heavy and light chain gene rearrangement in FL allows the distinction from follicular hyperplasia.

BCL2::IGH fusion or BCL2 rearrangement analysis can help distinguish FL from follicular hyperplasia and other small B-cell lymphomas.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: B-cell lymphoma with an admixture of centrocytes and/or centroblasts / large transformed cells; germinal centre immunophenotype, with positivity of markers such as CD10, BCL6, MEF2B, GCET1, GCET2, or LMO2.

Desirable: an at least partly follicular pattern; BCL2 rearrangement.

Staging

The TNM staging system is used to stage primary ocular adnexal FL [[Amin MB, Edge SB, Greene FL, et al., editors. AJCC Cancer Staging Manual. 8th ed. New York (NY): Springer; 2017.]]. Most patients with primary ocular adnexal FL have disease localized to the ocular adnexa [[Ferry JA, Fung CY, Lucarelli MJ, et al. Ocular Adnexal Lymphoma: Long-Term Outcome, Patterns of Failure and Prognostic Factors in 174 Patients. J Hematopathol. 2021;14:41â€“52. doi:10.1007/S12308-020-00424-6.]].

Prognosis and prediction

Ocular adnexal FL is a relatively indolent disease. The 10-year disease-specific survival has been reported from 69% to 91% {24763920}. Relapses are common, most often to lymph nodes but also to a variety of extranodal sites. Large cell transformation is uncommon and may be associated with a more aggressive course [[Ferry JA, Fung CY, Lucarelli MJ, et al. Ocular Adnexal Lymphoma: Long-Term Outcome, Patterns of Failure and Prognostic Factors in 174 Patients. J Hematopathol. 2021;14:41â€“52. doi:10.1007/S12308-020-00424-6.]]. External beam radiation (EBRT) in the mid-20 Gy range results in excellent disease control in localized lesions {22551232;20564102;16243446;22915662} and some recent studies have demonstrated that a dosage as low as 4 Gy can be effective {34318737;28370694}. Patients with widespread symptomatic disease are treated with various regimens of immunochemotherapy {16123223;30184451}. Neither Ann Arbor staging nor Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status has shown predictive values in ocular adnexal FL {24763920}.
