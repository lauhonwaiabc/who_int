10.1.1.1 Nodular and proliferative fasciitis, proliferative myositis

Definition

Nodular fasciitis is a rapidly growing, self-limited or transient fibroblastic/myofibroblastic neoplasm composed of plump spindled cells arranged in a tissue culture–like architectural pattern, and usually harbouring a USP6 rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

8828/0 Nodular fasciitis

8828/0 Proliferative fasciitis

8828/0 Proliferative myositis

ICD-11 coding

FB51.2 & XH5LM1 Pseudosarcomatous fibromatosis & Nodular fasciitis

FB51.2 & XH6JX7 Pseudosarcomatous fibromatosis & Proliferative fasciitis

FB51.Y & XH87F9 Other specified fibroblastic disorders & Proliferative myositis

Related terminology

Not recommended: pseudosarcomatous fasciitis; pseudosarcomatous fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

Proliferative fasciitis/myositis; intravascular and cranial fasciitis (probably not applicable to ocular adnexa)

Localization

Typical nodular fasciitis arises from superficial fascia and extends into the subcutis; it may also be intramuscular or dermal {153036;20716998} and usually arises in the upper extremities, trunk, and head and neck. Within the ocular adnexa, nodular fasciitis most commonly develops in the anterior periorbital tissues including eyelids and canthi, or as episcleral nodules; deep orbital lesions also occur {5905201;22611507;25393905;26882059}.

Proliferative fasciitis is very rare, but has been described a handful of times in the orbit, with 1 epibulbar case reported {26226235;34470541;33481533}.

Clinical features

Nodular fasciitis of the ocular adnexa is a rapidly growing (over weeks to months) epibulbar, anterior periorbital, or orbital mass that is typically well circumscribed, but may be poorly defined within the orbit. CT and MRI demonstrate an enhancing mass, rarely associated with bone remodelling {25393905}. Epibulbar lesions may mimic nodular episcleritis {27462253}. Lesions are usually < 10 mm on the epibulbar surface, and < 30 mm in the orbit {5905201}.

Proliferative fasciitis is very rare in this location {34470541;33481533}.

Proliferative myositis of the ocular/periocular tissues has not been documented.

Epidemiology

Nodular fasciitis is rare in the orbit and ocular adnexa. This lesion occurs in all age groups but is more prevalent in young adults; a quarter of cases occur in children and orbital lesions are more common in children.

Etiology

Historically these were postulated to be incited by trauma; currently the etiology is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Recurrent USP6 gene rearrangements have been reported in about 70% of cases {28752842}, including in nodular fasciitis of the orbit {26882059}.

Macroscopic appearance

The lesions are typically a well-circumscribed, unencapsulated mass with a myxoid cut surface.

Histopathology

Nodular fasciitis is composed of plump spindle and stellate cells (fibroblasts and/or myofibroblasts) without overt cytological atypia. Mitotic activity can be frequent, without atypical mitoses. Cellularity often varies. Feathery tissue culture–like areas with myxoid stroma are characteristic, often with extravasated red blood cells, but more cellular areas can form fascicles and sometimes have a storiform pattern. Stroma can be myxoid with microcystic-like changes and/or collagenous or keloidal-like. Osteoclastic giant cells and chronic inflammatory cells can be present.

Proliferative fasciitis and proliferative myositis contain plump myofibroblastic/fibroblastic spindle cells similar to those in nodular fasciitis and also demonstrate large ganglion-like cells with rounded nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant amphophilic to basophilic cytoplasm {1058047}. The ganglion cells can be variably distributed and usually have one nucleus, but may be multinucleated. Proliferative myositis may contain metaplastic bone.

Immunohistochemistry

SMA and MSA expression in a tram-track pattern is characteristic but not specific for these lesions. The ganglion-like cells are often negative for actins {2058761;1586481}.

Cytology

Cytologically, nodular fasciitis features bland spindle cells with bipolar and dendritic processes, ovoid nuclei, and occasional small nucleoli in a tissue culture–like arrangement, and variably myxoid stroma {8249520}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not always necessary, but in uncommon sites such as the ocular adnexa, USP6 rearrangement may be confirmatory {28752842;30139549}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for nodular fasciitis: loose intersecting fascicles of plump myofibroblasts in a tissue culture–like arrangement, variably myxoid stroma, and extravasated erythrocytes; for proliferative fasciitis and myositis: myofibroblastic/fibroblastic proliferation with ganglion-like cells and variable myxoid stroma.

Desirable: USP6 rearrangement in select cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Surgical excision is usually undertaken for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes, particularly given the common clinical concern for malignancy. Recurrence in large series outside the ocular adnexa is very rare after excision {6279273}. An orbital case has been successfully treated with intralesional steroids {32646258}. Since periorbital involvement is relatively rare, specific data regarding recurrence is not available in the orbit / ocular adnexa {11281591;26882059}.
