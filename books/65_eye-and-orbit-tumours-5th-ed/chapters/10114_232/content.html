10.1.1.4 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour (IMT) is a distinctive neoplasm of low metastatic potential composed of myofibroblastic and fibroblastic spindle cells accompanied by an inflammatory infiltrate of plasma cells, lymphocytes, and/or eosinophils.

ICD-O coding

8825/1 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH66Z0 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Myofibroblastic tumour, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflammatory pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In the ocular/periocular region, the orbit is a favoured site, and the lacrimal gland may also be involved {28824333;29755269}.

Clinical features

Symptoms and signs of orbital IMT include proptosis, ptosis, diplopia, visual loss, swelling, and pain {29755269;17728559;31880682}.

Epidemiology

IMT has a wide age distribution (0 to 81 years). No consistent sex predilection is observed {21042957;22014665}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

As many as 60% of tumours have an ALK gene (2p23) rearrangement with a wide variety of fusion partners. Among head and neck IMTs, ALK fusion with TIMP3 has been most commonly detected, and ROS1 fusion is seen rarely {31215130;34001695}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are usually < 50 mm, firm, and fleshy with a tan-white whorled to myxoid cut surface, occasionally showing haemorrhage and calcification.

Histopathology

Three principal patterns may coexist: a myxoid fasciitis or granulation tissue–like pattern, a hypercellular spindle-cell pattern, and a hypocellular fibrous (fibromatosis-like) pattern {7611533;17414097;31950474}. The spindle cells associate with a variable inflammatory infiltrate and fibromyxoid stroma {7611533;17414097;31950474}. Necrosis is infrequent. Mitotic activity is variable but generally low {7611533;24756612;17414097}.

Immunohistochemistry

Most IMTs, especially in patients < 40 years of age, express ALK, correlating with ALK gene rearrangements {26469330;26647767;27443585}. ROS1 may show cytoplasmic reactivity in ROS1-rearranged IMT {26647767;25612511}. The spindle cells generally show a patchy, variable reactivity for SMA, MSA, calponin, and desmin, with focal keratin reactivity in 30% of cases {7611533;31950474}.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis of IMT depends on the predominant histological pattern and includes idiopathic orbital inflammatory disease, IgG4-related disease, and nodular fasciitis (see related sections).

Cytology

The cytology of IMT features bland spindle cells with oval nuclei and small nucleoli, occasionally with ganglion-like cells, in a background of lymphocytes and plasma cells {20730898;27087029}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular assays for ALK, ROS1, and other gene rearrangements may be used, particularly if targeted therapy is being considered {26647767;30002191;31690781}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: loose or compact proliferation of spindle cells with myofibroblastic differentiation; fibrous or myxoid stroma; associated chronic inflammatory cell infiltrate.

Desirable: demonstration of ALK or ROS1 expression by immunohistochemistry; rearrangement of ALK or other implicated receptor tyrosine kinase genes (in select cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The natural history of IMT is variable. Overall, approximately 25% of IMTs recur, related to anatomical site and resectability {24756612;27443585;30410939;7611533}. Most patients with IMT remain recurrence-free after surgery with or without steroids {34854544}. Targeted therapies (i.e. ALK inhibitors) may improve the outcome {28787259;29286567;31690781}.
