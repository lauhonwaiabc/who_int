9.5.0.3 Erdheim-Chester disease

Definition

Erdheim–Chester Disease (ECD) is a clonal haematopoietic neoplasm characterized by accumulation of mature histiocytes, usually involving several organs, associated with inflammation and fibrosis.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Erdheim–Chester disease

ICD-11 coding

2B31.Y & XH1VJ3 Other specified histiocytic or dendritic cell neoplasms & Erdheim–Chester disease

Related terminology

Not recommended: lipogranulomatosis; lipoid granulomatosis; polyostotic sclerosing histiocytosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ECD is a multisystem disease and can affect virtually any organ, including bone, while lymph nodes, spleen, and liver are generally spared {32187362;29396850;33901419}. Periocular, orbital, and retro-orbital manifestations are common {32187362;32107533;31950179;34637661}. Extraorbital extension to the pituitary gland or cavernous sinus may occur {20413768}. Rare ophthalmic localizations include the conjunctiva, cornea, extraocular musculature, and lacrimal glands, and subretinal or choroidal infiltration {26114850;28153505;32227455}.

Clinical features

ECD may be asymptomatic, but more often patients present with systemic symptoms with fatigue, fever, and/or site-related symptoms, most of which are considered to be related to the inflammation, and fibrosis associated with histiocytic infiltration of the tissues. Visual abnormalities occur in < 5% of patients {28153505}. Ocular motility disturbances, exophthalmos, and retro-orbital pain are typically associated with diffuse infiltration of retro-orbital soft tissues affecting intraconal and extraconal compartments {32187362;31950179}. Vision loss {27322549} can result from posterior visual pathway involvement or optic nerve compression. Other manifestations include anterior uveitis, vitritis, optic disc oedema, eyelid oedema, choroidal infiltration, recurrent serous retinal detachment, retinal drusen-like deposits, and retinal pigment epithelial changes {34081930}. ECD can also present as periocular yellowish plaques (xanthelasma).

Imaging

Imaging includes 18F-FDG PET-CT of the entire body or 99mTc bone scanning; CT and MRI (with contrast) for evaluation of the pituitary gland and orbital or CNS involvement {32107533;32187362;33057928}; and fundus fluorescein angiography and optical coherence tomography imaging to assess choroidal infiltration and intraocular disease progression {28153505}.

Epidemiology

ECD is a rare but probably underdiagnosed disease; the actual incidence is unknown. It predominantly affects middle-aged men. Rare paediatric cases have been reported {28078666;29192649;31259822}.

Etiology

ECD is a clonal neoplastic disorder without known genetic predisposition {22879539;26566875;31768065}. It is marked by xanthomatous histiocytes of monocyte/macrophage lineage expressing MAPK pathway–activating genetic alterations {26966089;29192649;22879539}. An underlying clonal haematopoiesis has been described in 42% of cases {33067622;31221777}, which is significantly higher than that in the age-related general population.

Pathogenesis

The detection of oncogenic RAF or RAS mutations in bone marrow CD34+ cells and circulating offspring cells points to early haematopoietic stem/progenitor cells as histiocyte precursor cells {28566492;28512190;33067622}. MAPK mutations seem to favour the generation of myeloid cells with an intrinsic hyperinflammatory phenotype {34077954}. Monocytes coexpressing clonal haematopoiesis-associated mutations are found in many ECD patients. Sequential acquisition of driver mutations may lead to development of secondary myeloid neoplasms/malignancies {28566492;33067622;28679734;30084011;32852896}.

Macroscopic appearance

Biopsies are usually firm and fibrotic. Periorbital skin biopsies are yellowish in colour.

Histopathology

Sheets of xanthomatous (foamy) and lipid-laden histiocytes admixed with lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate are seen. Fibrosis and multinucleated giant cells (Touton giant cells) are frequent {29192649}. The proliferative index is usually low.

Histopathological findings of ECD do not discriminate between ECD and xanthogranulomatous-type histiocytoses. The most typical picture is an infiltrate of foamy macrophages, associated with Touton giant cells, lymphocytes, plasma cells, and neutrophils {26966089;29192649}. In xanthelasma, Touton cells are more abundant in ECD than in other etiologies {26785805}. Tissue fibrosis is frequently observed and may predominate, leading to a misdiagnosis of a reactive fibroinflammatory process. Admixed Langerhans cell histiocytosis may be present in the same biopsy or other tissues, in cases of mixed histiocytosis {24894769;32969291;26785805;32107533;32187362;31950179;27898473}. Occasionally, a morphological and immunophenotypical overlap with Rosai–Dorfman disease or with reticulohistiocytosis may be seen {31123032;31955466}.

Immunohistochemistry

Histiocytes express CD68, CD163, CD14, factor XIIIa, fascin, and phosphorylated ERK, but are negative for CD1a, langerin (CD207) {32187362;34785791;28566492}, and OCT2 {34781270;33177341}. S100 is usually weak or negative. Emperipolesis is rare {29526544;32187362}.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis includes Rosai–Dorfman disease, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, adult-onset xanthogranuloma, juvenile xanthogranuloma, necrobiotic xanthogranuloma {16622091;17135338;19961259}, adult-onset asthma and periocular xanthogranuloma {34637661}, non-malignant xanthelasma, metastatic tumours {33782321}, and malignant histiocytosis {29720485;33710041;34637661}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

MAPK pathway–activating somatic mutations are common and may aid in diagnosis and treatment {33901419}. Half of patients carry BRAF p.V600E mutations {26966089;29526544}, which are associated with retro-orbital involvement {29396850}. Negative staining with VE1 {22859608} should be confirmed by molecular testing as variant allele frequency may be low.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: collections of bland, foamy histiocytes in the appropriate clinical and radiological context.

Desirable: MAPK pathway–activating somatic mutations.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Usually, there is progressive disease with a variable prognosis and substantial mortality, depending on the organs involved and response to therapy. 18F-FDG PET-CT imaging is used for assessment of disease extent and response evaluation {32187362}. CNS involvement is an independent predictor of death {32107533}. Patients with ECD obtain clinical benefit with interferon alfa-2a or pegylated interferon alfa {32187362;33901419}. However, in recent years, MAPK pathway alterations have been recognized and targeted therapies with kinase inhibitors are frequently used {25422482;28667012}. Note that these drugs have been registered for use in this context in just a few countries. BRAF or MEK targeted therapy significantly increases 5-year overall survival from 43% to 83% {32187362;26566875}. Approximately 10–15% of ECD cases co-develop an additional haematological malignancy, negatively affecting prognosis.
