9.2.1.1 Reactive lymphoid hyperplasia

Definition

Reactive lymphoid hyperplasia (RLH) is a benign, reactive, polyclonal lymphoid proliferation without cytological or architectural atypia, which may resemble B-cell lymphoma.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A22.Z Orbital inflammation, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: lymphoid hyperplasia; (florid) follicular hyperplasia.

Not recommended: benign lymphoid proliferation; pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RLH may involve the ocular adnexa including eyelids, conjunctiva, orbital soft tissues, lacrimal gland, and drainage ducts {15767083;31261162;2394438;20704489}, as well as intraocular structures, such as the choroid {21900853}.

Clinical features

Signs vary according to the tissues involved; however, RLH typically results in a unilateral, red or orange, painless swelling or tumour mass, leading to corresponding symptoms, such as proptosis, eyelid or conjunctival mass, decreased ocular motility and visual acuity, and diplopia, among others {17550749}. Manifestations may be more severe in immunodeficient patients {16168486;17601445}. Because RLH is clinically and radiologically indistinguishable from low-grade lymphoma, a tissue biopsy is recommended in all cases {24307715}.

Epidemiology

RLH accounts for approximately 8–19% of cases categorized as ocular adnexal lymphoproliferative disease {27127077;28399341}. It affects patients in their fifth to seventh decades of life, with no sex or race predilection {27127077}. RLH is uncommon in children {23248544;24162623;27899370}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown, but RLH is probably caused by chronic antigen stimulation (either exogenous or endogenous antigens) leading to chronic inflammation and ultimately RLH. Implicated exogenous antigens include antigens associated with Chlamydia psittaci, Chlamydophila pneumoniae, Chlamydia trachomatis, Helicobacter pylori, EBV, and HTLV-1 {27127077}. Implicated endogenous antigens include those occurring in autoimmune diseases {18405875}.

Pathogenesis

It is proposed that either endogenous or exogenous chronic antigen stimulation leads to polyclonal expansion of B cells and T cells {15286744}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopic appearance is variable according to the site of involvement.

Histopathology

RLH shows a polymorphous infiltrate of mature lymphocytes within the stroma, as well as scattered irregular secondary lymphoid follicles with regular germinal centres and narrow marginal and mantle zones {27127077;9709754}.

Immunohistochemistry

The infiltrate comprises a mixture of B and T cells arranged physiologically. The germinal centres are immunoreactive for CD10 and BCL6 but negative for BCL2 {27127077}.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis includes reactive lesions such as sarcoidosis, idiopathic orbital inflammation, IgG4-related disease {25185258}, Sjögren disease involving the lacrimal gland {16168486}, and small cell B-cell lymphomas (extranodal marginal zone lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma).

Cytology

The distinction between reactive B-cell–rich lymphoid proliferations and small cell B-cell lymphoma is challenging on FNA specimens and is not advised as a primary diagnostic modality. However, flow cytometry immunophenotyping on aspiration material may be a helpful adjunct to confirm polytypic light chain expression by reactive B cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Occasional cases may show distortion of normal architecture or cytological atypia, raising the question of lymphoma. Molecular studies may be helpful in such cases to demonstrate polyclonal or oligoclonal immunoglobulin gene rearrangement {11454995;12799255;16049528}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of appropriate histological features and supporting ancillary studies (immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, and clonal analyses); no diagnostic evidence of lymphoma or other neoplasm (see #38459Table 10.01).

Desirable: identification of an infectious agent or evidence of underlying autoimmune disease, immunodeficiency, or other inflammatory condition on clinical and/or laboratory data.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

RLH has a favourable outcome, which is dependent on the size of the lesion and available therapy. Most cases resolve spontaneously or respond to antibiotics or low-dose radiotherapy. Some larger lesions have been treated with intralesional steroids and/or rituximab {17550749;18772675}.
