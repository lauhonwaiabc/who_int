10.1.1.3 Solitary fibrous tumour

Definition

Solitary fibrous tumour (SFT) is a fibroblastic neoplasm characterized by a prominent, branching, dilated (staghorn) vasculature and a NAB2::STAT6 rearrangement.

Related terminology

Not recommended: haemangiopericytoma; giant cell angiofibroma; benign solitary fibrous tumour.

Subtype(s)

Fat-forming (lipomatous) solitary fibrous tumour; giant cell–rich solitary fibrous tumour; dedifferentiated solitary fibrous tumour

Localization

Orbital SFT can affect intraconal and extraconal spaces {28877055;32493043}. Other periocular sites include the lacrimal gland, lacrimal sac, conjunctiva, sclera, and eyelids {18272558;26889300}. Fat-forming SFT has been reported in the orbit {11014579;17285842;18617906}, while giant cell–rich SFT is more common in periorbital soft tissue {19966660;23667375;29283151;33879215}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation depends on the location of the tumour. The commonest presentation is periorbital fullness, orbital mass (with or without proptosis), globe displacement, limitation of extraocular motility, diplopia, ptosis, and blurred vision {18272558;21056898;32493043}. Intraconal and orbital apex tumours may cause optic disc oedema, afferent pupillary defect, and choroidal folds {31876648;32493043}. MRI has value in the diagnosis and delineation of soft tissue involvement in SFT, while CT is important in detecting bone involvement {22997401}. The tumour is isointense with homogeneous appearance on T1-weighted MRI and shows a well-defined isodense enhancing mass with contrast on CT {22997401;32493043}.

Epidemiology

Orbital SFT is most common in the mid-forties {14499820;32493043;19085297} and is rare in childhood {21743364;19966660}. Sex distribution has been variably reported between being equally distributed and having a male predilection with an M:F ratio of 3:1 {14499820;28877055;32493043}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

SFT is characterized by a paracentric inversion involving chromosome 12q, resulting in the fusion of the NAB2 and STAT6 genes {23313954;23761323;23313952}. The fusion protein is thought to drive tumorigenesis via feed-forward activation of the EGR1 pathway {23313952}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, SFTs are usually partially encapsulated and well circumscribed, and exhibit a whitish, multinodular firm cut surface with possible myxoid areas and/or haemorrhage {28877055;33879215}.

Histopathology

SFT has a variable histopathological appearance showing hypocellular to hypercellular areas of proliferating ovoid to spindle cells with patternless arrangement, separated by collagen fibres of variable thickness (keloid type in classic SFTs) with a storiform pattern {32493043;33879215}. Cell pleomorphism and multinucleated giant cells (giant cell–rich SFT) occur in a subset of cases {7573691}. The stroma contains thin-walled and hyalinized branching staghorn-shaped blood vessels {33879215}. Hyalinized stroma is also reported {32493043}. Dedifferentiated SFTs show transition to a high-grade sarcoma {29848035}.

Immunohistochemistry

STAT6 is the most sensitive immunohistochemical marker {23575898;24030747;33879215;34278302}. Other markers may be used in combination if STAT6 immunohistochemistry is not available: CD34 (expressed in 81–95% of cases), BCL2 (expressed in > 90% of cases), and CD99, which is less sensitive {33879215}.

Differential diagnosis

In the ocular adnexa this includes peripheral nerve sheath tumours, leiomyoma, nodular fasciitis, adipocytic tumours, and synovial sarcoma (see related sections).

Cytology

Cytopathology has limited value in the evaluation of SFT (accurate and specific in 9% of cases). Therefore, positivity of STAT6 is essential for diagnosis, particularly in small specimens {28914981;34049841}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

NAB2::STAT6 fusions are pathognomonic of SFT, but the detection of fusion types is difficult without multiplexed sequencing assays. STAT6 immunohistochemistry is a sensitive and specific surrogate for all fusions {24030747;24702701;23575898}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindled to ovoid cells arranged around a branching and hyalinized vasculature; variable stromal collagen deposition; STAT6 expression by immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: demonstration of a NAB2::STAT6 fusion (in select cases).

Staging

Risk stratification models are preferred over anatomical staging {22575866;28731041}.

Prognosis and prediction

The most widely used models for metastatic risk of thoracic and extrathoracic SFTs (see #53044Table 11.07) incorporate mitotic count (≥ 2 mitoses/mm2), patient age (≥ 55 years), tumour size, and necrosis to classify SFTs into low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups {22575866;28731041;29935303}. Staining of p53 and a higher proliferation index using Ki-67 (> 5%) are associated with a worse prognosis {21056898;32843765}. Ocular adnexal SFT carries a relatively high risk of regional recurrence (~25%) and a low risk of distant metastasis (~2%), which can occur many years after initial surgery {32529350}. Thus, long-term follow-up is recommended {19085297;25761539;29780261;33499815}.
