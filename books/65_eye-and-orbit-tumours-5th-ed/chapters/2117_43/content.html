2.1.1.7 Phakomatous choristoma

Definition

Phakomatous choristoma is a benign congenital developmental lesion of lenticular anlage, composed of extraocular crystalline lens tissue and its basement membrane.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.0Y Other specified structural developmental anomalies of eyelids

Related terminology

Not recommended: Zimmerman tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Phakomatous choristoma generally occurs deep in the medial lower eyelid. Cases with exclusive involvement of the inferior anterior orbit, lacrimal sac, nasolacrimal duct, and extraocular muscles have been reported {30407999;10406159;5542122;34750316}.

Clinical features

Patients present in infancy with a smooth, firm, slowly growing subcutaneous mass in the medial lower eyelid, already present at birth. On imaging, the tumour is solid, well circumscribed, and contrast enhancing, without bone erosion {34750316;26070367}.

Epidemiology

Phakomatous choristoma is extremely rare, with < 30 cases reported worldwide. There is a slight male predominance (68%) {34750316}.

Etiology

This is a developmental abnormality.

Pathogenesis

Two hypotheses have been proposed as to the origin of the lesion: formation of an additional lens vesicle locus from surface ectoderm {5542122}, and displacement or migration of cells from the lens placode {973823;16102745}.

Macroscopic appearance

Phakomatous choristoma macroscopically appears as an ovoid, white-grey, firm mass, measuring as large as 20 mm {1390539;34750316}.

Histopathology

Phakomatous choristoma has a characteristic morphological appearance and consists of islands of cuboidal to polygonal cells, reminiscent of lens epithelium, and bladder (Wedl) cells surrounded by a thick layer of eosinophilic PAS-positive material analogous to lens capsule. Psammomatous calcification can be seen. Dense collagenous stroma is present in the background {5542122}.

Immunohistochemistry is not required for diagnosis. The epithelial and bladder cells stain positively for vimentin, S100, PAX8, and lens crystalline proteins, and are negative for cytokeratins {34750316;36249298}. Electron microscopy demonstrates 10 nm whorled cytoplasmic microfilaments within degenerating epithelial cells {973823}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: extraocular crystalline lens tissue and dense collagen bundles surrounded by a PAS-positive membrane localized to the eyelid/orbit.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Phakomatous choristoma is benign. Malignant transformation has not been described. Simple resection is curative {34750316}. Rare associations include myopia, colobomatous hypoplastic optic disc, and staphyloma {16424545}.
