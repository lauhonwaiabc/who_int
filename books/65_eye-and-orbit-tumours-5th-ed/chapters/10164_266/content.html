10.1.6.4 Epithelioid haemangioma

Definition

Epithelioid haemangioma (EH) is a benign vascular neoplasm composed of well-formed capillary-sized vessels lined by plump epithelioid endothelial cells in a variable background of chronic inflammation containing numerous eosinophils.

ICD-O coding

9125/0 Epithelioid haemangioma

9125/0 Cellular epithelioid haemangioma

9125/0 Atypical epithelioid haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.0Y & XH10T4 Neoplastic haemangioma of other specified site & Epithelioid haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiolymphoid hyperplasia with eosinophilia; histiocytoid haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

Cellular epithelioid haemangioma; atypical epithelioid haemangioma

Localization

EH can involve the eyelid skin and superficial and deep orbital soft tissues {4008683;24676051;27171790;31768366}.

Clinical features

Clinical signs and symptoms are related to the mass effect, and include eyelid fullness, ptosis, globe displacement/proptosis, and ocular motility restriction {24676051;27171790}. The lesion can be either poorly defined or well circumscribed on imaging {24676051;27171790}.

Epidemiology

EH may occur at any age (reported range 11–86 years) with a peak incidence in the fourth decade of life {24676051;27171790}. There is no significant sex predilection {27171790;24676051}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

EH is characterized by recurrent fusions involving the FOS or FOSB genes in as many as one half of cases {25043949;26135557}. FOS-negative and FOSB-negative tumours may harbour an alternative GATA6::FOXO1 fusion {33318580}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, EH is circumscribed and nodular or multinodular.

Histopathology

EH typically manifests as a lobular or multinodular proliferation of small vessels, frequently surrounding a central small artery with more mature vessels at the periphery of the lobules {15087672;21948309;3116865}. The endothelial cells have a distinctive appearance with an abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, large rounded nuclei, and a central nucleolus, and are associated with a pericytic layer. The endothelial cells may contain large cytoplasmic vacuoles (blister cells) and may protrude into the lumen with a tombstone pattern {15087672;21948309;10768347}. A variably intense infiltrate of lymphocytes with occasional lymphoid follicles, eosinophils, and plasma cells is noted in an oedematous stroma {3116865;15087672}. Mitotic figures are rare {15087672}.

Immunohistochemistry

The endothelial cells express CD31, FLI1, ERG, and to a lesser extent CD34 {15087672;26135557}. Expression of cytokeratain and EMA may be noted focally {8333560;21904630}. Expression of FOSB may be helpful in distinguishing from malignant epithelioid vascular tumours {28009608}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology includes evaluation for FOS or FOSB gene rearrangements in some cases {29266025;26135557;29527734}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobular vascular proliferation of epithelioid endothelial cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and enlarged round nuclei; stroma with chronic inflammation and eosinophils.

Desirable: demonstration of FOSB expression or rearrangements of the FOS and/or FOSB genes may be useful in a subset of cases.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete surgical excision is the treatment of choice, but lesions may recur {26685720;24676051}. Oral propranolol has been used in several reported recurrent or incompletely excised orbital lesions in young children {22186982;26954109}.
