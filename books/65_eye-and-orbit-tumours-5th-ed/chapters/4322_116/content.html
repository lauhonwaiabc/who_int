4.3.2.2 Medulloepithelioma of the ciliary body

Definition

Medulloepithelioma of the ciliary body is a primary intraocular embryonal neuroectodermal tumour characterized by cords of multilayered neuroepithelial cells resembling the medullary epithelium of the optic cup.

Subtype(s)

Medulloepithelioma, benign; medulloepithelioma, malignant; teratoid medulloepithelioma, benign; teratoid medulloepithelioma, malignant

Localization

Most medulloepitheliomas are ciliary body tumours located on the surface of the ciliary body {22288972}. Rarely they can develop in the optic nerve {20497433;4827430;12045984;14974835} and, exceptionally, in the retina {9025863}.

Clinical features

Medulloepithelioma presents typically with poor vision or blindness (27%), leukocoria (20%), strabismus (12%), or a red, painful eye (20%) {23796765}. Clinical diagnosis is challenging: diagnoses on referral include medulloepithelioma (12%), an undetermined mass (22%), cataract (20%), glaucoma (17%), and retinoblastoma (7%) {23796765;31124483}. Unusual clinical presentations include translucent neoplastic cysts in the posterior or anterior chamber {16445454}. More than half of patients have intratumoural cysts, which are a helpful diagnostic clue on imaging studies. One fifth show notching or coloboma of the lens caused by segmental loss of lens zonules {23796765;25136238;31124483}. A neoplastic membrane of tumour tissue is present behind the lens in half of patients and may cause leukocoria {23796765;21505878;25136238}. One half lead to neovascularization of the iris, and about 45% to secondary glaucoma {23796765}. Treatment and diagnosis often are delayed. In one series, the average age at presentation was about 4 years, but the tumour was diagnosed at the age of 5 years {655220}. Before diagnosis, some patients are treated for cataract or glaucoma. An epibulbar mass may be present from extraocular extension, which has been observed after local resection {3211466} or misguided tube shunt surgery for neovascular glaucoma {23699787}. Some medulloepitheliomas are found incidentally in blind, painful eyes after enucleation or evisceration.

Epidemiology

Most medulloepitheliomas occur in children who are typically 2–5 years of age, but exceptional ones are reported in adults (range: 0–66 years) {27239464;23218809}. Sixty-three per cent occur in females and there is no race predilection {23796765}.

Etiology

Intraocular medulloepitheliomas are congenital tumours thought to arise from anlagen of medullary epithelium that lines the forebrain and optic vesicle {655220;9003333;25545484}. No risk factors for intraocular medulloepithelioma are known. Most are sporadic and unassociated with any congenital malformation or cytogenic abnormality. However, a minority (5%) arise in patients with pleuropulmonary blastoma familial tumour and dysplasia predisposition syndrome (MIM number 601200) caused by germline mutations in DICER1 {21156700;25032694;23613510}. Conversely, in this syndrome, only < 1% of patients have developed an intraocular medulloepithelioma.

Pathogenesis

The neoplastic multilayered medullary epithelium appears to arise from a simple premedullary epithelial monolayer of cells that are S100-positive {25174896}. Immunostaining for CRX and the neuronal marker NeuN suggests that the anteriormost layers of the optic cup retain the potential for retinal and neuroglial differentiation. Although intraocular medulloepitheliomas share histomorphological features with CNS medulloepitheliomas, the evidence suggests that they do not share a common origin and biological behaviour. The latter is a rare entity grouped with several other primitive neuroectodermal tumours as embryonal tumours with multilayered rosettes. Although both intraocular and CNS medulloepitheliomas express LIN28A, the former show numerous copy-number aberrations that involve entire chromosomes or chromosome arms. In addition, they show no amplification of the C19MC cluster of microRNA typical of CNS medulloepitheliomas, and they have strikingly different DNA methylation patterns {26183384;25748578;26959146}. Conversely, RNA expression profiling of genes in the sonic hedgehog and WNT signalling pathways displays common upregulated target genes in both intraocular and CNS tumours, suggesting these pathways as critical drivers of both medulloepithelioma types {34785636}.

Cytogenetic analysis of intraocular medulloepitheliomas shows recurrent aberrations in chromosomes 1p, 4, 8, and 16p {26183384}. Mutation of DICER1 in some sporadic intraocular medulloepitheliomas is not associated with pleuropulmonary blastoma syndrome {25791583;26841698;25807141}. Expression profiling shows significantly upregulated and downregulated microRNAs in a fashion different from that in CNS medulloepitheliomas. Instead, the microRNA dysregulation profiles are similar to those seen in glioblastoma and retinoblastoma {25807141}. Mutation of KMT2D has been reported {26841698}. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of DNA methylation showed that intraocular medulloepitheliomas have signatures distinct from CNS medulloepitheliomas {26183384}. The targetable epigenetic tumour protein EZH2 is enriched in intraocular medulloepithelioma {27842164}.

Macroscopic appearance

Medulloepithelioma appears as a greyish-white mass typically with intralesional cysts behind the lens on the surface of the ciliary body, sometimes with freely floating cysts.

Histopathology

Medulloepitheliomas reside on the inner surface of the ciliary body and extend into the vitreous cavity and posterior chamber. Involvement of the iris and anterior chamber occurs occasionally. Aggressive lesions can invade the stroma of the ciliary body and the sclera to extend extraocularly. Sheets of tumour can grow posteriorly on the inner surface of the retina and reach the posterior pole of the eye. These tumours range in size from 2 to 30 mm (mean: 11 mm) {23796765}. They are composed of anastomosing cords, bands, and ribbons of polarized neuroepithelial cells that resemble embryonic medullary epithelium. A meshwork of thin interweaving strands of neuroepithelium characterizes a diktyomatous pattern. The polarized neuroepithelial elements secrete a relatively acellular myxoid stroma rich in hyaluronidase-sensitive mucopolysaccharide (neoplastic vitreous).

The side of the neuroepithelium abutting the myxoid stroma is lined by a thin basement membrane, whereas the lumina of the neuroepithelial clefts and rosettes contains zonulae adherentes analogous to the external limiting membrane of the retina. Approximately half of medulloepitheliomas contain intralesional cystoid spaces, and one third contain areas of intrinsic pigmentation {23796765;11831926}. Sheets of poorly differentiated neuroblastic cells resembling retinoblastoma is a histopathological criterion of malignancy. Such areas may contain Homer Wright or Flexner–Wintersteiner rosettes. Additional criteria for malignancy include invasion of adjacent tissues including the uvea, sclera, cornea, and optic nerve, extraocular extension, sarcomatous dedifferentiation in areas of heterologous tissue, cellular pleomorphism, atypia, and mitotic activity. Altogether, 66–80% of medulloepitheliomas are classified as malignant {655220;23796765}.

Because the histopathological criteria for malignancy do not correlate with clinical behaviour, and molecular genetic studies have disclosed no differences between histologically benign and malignant medulloepitheliomas, a simple grading scheme has been proposed to replace the prevailing dichotomous classification {27239464}. The proposed scheme divides intraocular medulloepitheliomas into grade 1, corresponding to the current WHO designation “benign”; grade 2 with progression evidenced by pleomorphism, mitotic activity, and local invasion; and grade 3 with metastatic potential evidenced by extrascleral extension or existing metastasis. Metastatic disease occurs only after extraocular extension.

Non-teratoid medulloepitheliomas consist exclusively of neuroepithelial elements. Teratoid medulloepitheliomas additionally contain heterotopic elements, such as cartilage and striated muscle. Medulloepitheliomas are therefore designated as teratoid if they contain heteroplastic elements including hyaline cartilage, muscle, myoblasts, and brain-like tissue. About one third of all intraocular medulloepitheliomas are teratoid {23796765}: about one fifth of benign and one half of malignant ones {655220;23796765}. The presence of heteroplastic elements does not alter prognosis. Hyaline cartilage is the most common heteroplastic element. Cartilage has even developed in non-teratoid tumours that recurred after local resection.

Pigmented tumours occasionally occur {9548346;25278807}.

Immunohistochemistry

The non-teratoid component of medulloepitheliomas typically is immunoreactive for vimentin and NSE {3211466}. Limited and sometimes conflicting results are reported for chromogranin, synaptophysin, GFAP, S100, and HMB45. Immunoreactivity for pancytokeratin and CK18 with no reactivity for CK7, CK20, and EMA has been described {25251824;22288972}. Almost all medulloepitheliomas stain positively for LIN28A as CNS medulloepitheliomas do, and the intensity of staining may correlate with aggressive behaviour {25748578}. As regards intraocular tumours, LIN28A might be specific for medulloepithelioma, because 19 of 20 were found to stain positive, while 0 of 10 retinoblastomas were immunopositive. PAX8 also showed diffuse immunoreactivity in ciliary body medulloepitheliomas, and this may be another useful immunostain for distinguishing poorly differentiated ones from retinoblastoma {33002562}.

Cytology

The polymorphous nature of medulloepithelioma can reduce the sensitivity and specificity of aspiration cytology {21062379;28217870;22288972;12713484}. In many reports, diagnosis is limited to a malignant round cell tumour, although more-specific rosettes and focal epithelial-like fragments with cellular polarity and multilayering occasionally are observed {21062379}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour composed of non-pigmented or pigmented cords and bands of polarized neuroepithelial cells with a myxoid matrix located on the surface of the ciliary body.

Desirable: teratoid subtypes contain heteroplastic elements, most often cartilage.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Most eyes with a medulloepithelioma are still enucleated. Prognosis for vision is thus poor. Recurrence often follows local resection because of clinically inapparent tumour cells {3211466}. Plaque brachytherapy is sometimes used, but its role is not clearly defined. Prognosis for life is excellent if the tumour remains intraocular. Metastases are infrequent but can develop after extraocular extension: in one series, 4 of 41 medulloepitheliomas developed metastases, all of which had extraocular extension because of a delayed diagnosis {23796765}.
