2.3.1.1 Sebaceous hyperplasia, sebaceous adenoma, and sebaceoma

Definition

Sebaceous hyperplasia is a benign lesion comprising hyperplastic lobules of mature sebaceous glands emanating from a central hair follicle.

Sebaceous adenoma and sebaceoma are a spectrum of benign sebaceous tumours, in which sebocytes predominate over basaloid cells in sebaceous adenoma, whereas basaloid cells predominate over sebocytes in sebaceoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Sebaceous hyperplasia, sebaceous adenoma, and sebaceoma occur most commonly on the skin of the head and neck. They only rarely occur in the ocular adnexa.

Clinical features

Sebaceous hyperplasia, sebaceous adenoma, and sebaceoma are well-circumscribed yellow-tan nodules {34023105;31821583}. Lesions associated with Muir–Torre syndrome may be multifocal.

Epidemiology

Sebaceous hyperplasia occurs in older patients. Sebaceous adenomas develop in patients aged > 40 years and account for 0.2% of all eyelid lesions {25595178}. Sebaceoma occurs in older patients (> 70 years) and is slightly more common in women {34023105;4129561;6703260} and is very rarely reported on the eyelids {23207023;24862791;12142607}.

Etiology

Sebaceous adenoma and sebaceoma show an association with Muir–Torre syndrome (MIM number 158320), whereas sebaceous hyperplasia does not. Muir–Torre syndrome is a rare autosomal dominant cancer predisposition syndrome, defined by the association of cutaneous lesions and Lynch syndrome–associated visceral malignancies {34023105;18670585}. Muir–Torre syndrome is caused by inherited germline abnormalities of DNA mismatch repair protein genes {23149194;3300561}. Sebaceous adenomas and sebaceomas developing in this setting may show loss of mismatch repair protein expression, including MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2 {25427047;26892655;21499234;20055911}, although this may also occur sporadically, emphasizing the need to integrate clinical factors into patient assessment {24603434;23212176}.

Pathogenesis

Limited sequencing studies have shown mutations in TP53, CDKN2A, EGFR, CTNNB1, and KRAS {27311873}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Sebaceous hyperplasia consists of multiple, enlarged lobules of mature sebaceous glands grouped around a central hair follicle. Each hyperplastic lobule consists of mature sebocytes, with a non-atypical germinative basaloid layer at the periphery that is no more than 2 cell layers thick.

Sebaceous adenoma and sebaceoma do not show a relationship to hair follicles and are well-circumscribed nodules that may show a direct connection to the epidermis. They consist of an admixture of basaloid cells and mature sebocytes. In sebaceous adenoma, sebocytes predominate over basaloid cells, whereas in sebaceoma, basaloid cells predominate over sebocytes. In sebaceous adenoma, the sebocytes are usually central within the lobules and the basaloid cells are sited peripherally. In sebaceoma, the sebocytes are randomly scattered (as individuals or groups) among the basaloid cells. Sebaceoma may also contain ducts and cysts containing sebaceous debris. The basaloid cells have round or oval vesicular nuclei and little cytoplasm and may show mitotic activity. Mild atypia may be seen. The sebocytes are larger in size, with frothy cytoplasm with a central, hyperchromatic, crenated nucleus.

Differential diagnosis

Whereas sebaceous hyperplasia and sebaceous adenoma are typically readily recognized, sebaceoma with many basaloid cells may be difficult to distinguish from sebaceous carcinoma and basal cell carcinoma. Sebaceous carcinomas often show intraepithelial extension, infiltrative growth, and cytological atypia. In contrast to most sebaceomas, basal cell carcinomas show peripheral palisading, retraction artefact, mucinous stroma, and isolated necrotic basaloid keratinocytes.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Sebaceous hyperplasia

Essential: hyperplastic lobules of mature sebaceous glands attached to a central hair follicle, with a basaloid layer no more than two cell layers thick; no cytological atypia.

Sebaceous adenoma

Essential: a well-circumscribed sebaceous tumour where sebocytes outnumber basaloid cells; the sebocytes are central and the basaloid cells are at the periphery; the sebocytes are cytologically bland.

Sebaceoma

Essential: a well-circumscribed sebaceous tumour where basaloid cells outnumber sebocytes; the sebocytes are randomly scattered (individual or grouped) among the basaloid cells; the sebocytes are cytologically bland.

Desirable: presence of ducts and cysts containing sebaceous debris.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Sebaceous hyperplasia, sebaceous adenoma, and sebaceoma are benign lesions, and simple excision is typically curative. In sebaceoma, anecdotal examples have shown malignant transformation into sebaceous carcinoma {26268140;23550845;33957741}. A subset of patients with sebaceous adenoma and sebaceoma have Muir–Torre syndrome and may experience morbidity and mortality from associated visceral malignancies {25595178;14752307}.
