8.1.2.4 Mesenchymal hamartoma of the orbit

Definition

Orbital mesenchymal hamartomas are a family of benign malformations of mesenchymal cells found in the orbit including rhabdomyomatous mesenchymal hamartoma (RMH), chondromesenchymal hamartoma (CMH), and fibrous hamartoma of infancy (FHI).

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.2 & XH2P15 Structural developmental anomalies of orbit & Mesenchymal hamartoma

Related terminology

Rhabdomyomatous mesenchymal hamartoma

Acceptable: striated muscle hamartoma.

Not recommended: hamartoma of cutaneous adnexa and mesenchyme; congenital midline hamartoma.

Chondromesenchymal hamartoma

Acceptable: chondroid hamartoma.

Not recommended: mesenchymoma; nasal hamartoma.

Fibrous hamartoma of infancy

Not recommended: subdermal fibromatous tumour of infancy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RMH presents most often in the subcutis of the head and neck {34904686}. Three primary orbital RMHs have been reported {17446511;25105519}. CMH typically occurs in the sinonasal tract in children (nasal CMH), with possible orbital and skull involvement {30167348}. There are two reports of primary orbital CMH {7322488;19617800}, and several reports of nasal cases extending into the orbit {17519672;21158582;30167348}. FHI presents in the axilla, back, or upper arm in about one half of cases and is distributed widely over the body in the other half {28059097}. Two FHIs have arisen in the orbit {28059097;26917076}.

Clinical features

All three subtypes can present as a painless orbital mass resulting variably in proptosis, ptosis, and altered ocular motility. Although nasal CMH is benign, it can be locally invasive with potential intracranial or paranasal sinus invasion {30167348}. MRI distinguishes primary orbital CMH from nasal CMH {19617800}.

Epidemiology

RMH is rare, and 80% of tumours have a clinical onset before 18 years of age {34904686}. Both reported orbital RMHs were in children {17446511;25105519}. Nasal CMHs are rare {30167348;34164613} with the majority presenting in newborn infants and young children and more rarely in adolescents {30167348}. The two reported orbital CMHs were in young children {7322488;17519672}. FHI is also rare, with 91% of cases in children < 2 years of age and an M:F ratio of 2.7 {30167348}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

RMH is considered a congenital hamartoma in 80% of cases and an acquired tumour in 20% {34904686}. Nasal CMH is regarded as a congenital hamartoma analogous to mesenchymal hamartoma of the chest wall {9537469}. Nasal CMH is associated with pathogenic germline DICER1 variants that give rise to DICER1-related tumour predisposition syndrome {34164613}. A review of 58 reported cases of nasal CMH showed that 38% had at least one additional DICER1-associated tumour {34164613}. A recent study of 12 FHI tumours, none from the orbit, identified EGFR exon insertion/deletion mutations in all cases {27631514}.

Macroscopic appearance

Nasal CMHs are usually removed in fragments that vary from firm and white, typical of cartilage, to soft, fleshy, and tan {9537469}. The primary orbital CMH reported by Bowen et al. was “pearly white, firm, well-circumscribed, and composed of several lobules” {7322488}. Dermal and subcutaneous FHIs are mostly rounded or bosselated with firm, glistening, greyish-white tissue intermixed with islands of yellow fat {14254080}.

Histopathology

RMH shows randomly arranged mature striated muscle fibres with an admixture of adipose tissue, collagen, blood vessels, and peripheral nerves {11297501;17446511}. Orbital RMH may be composed predominantly of bland spindle-shaped cells with scattered skeletal muscle fibres and intermixed collagen {25105519}. Nasal CMH shows irregular islands of hyaline cartilage with variable components of mesenchyme and fibro-osseous tissue {9537569;11231491}. Both of the reported primary orbital CMHs had mature hyaline cartilage {7322488;19617800}. FHI has varying proportions of mature adipose tissue, intersecting fascicles of fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells, and myxoid nodules of primitive-appearing spindled to stellate mesenchymal cells {14254080;28059097} with 30% of cases having hyalinized zones {28059097}.

Cytology

FNAB of FHI shows fibroblastic-appearing cells, fatty tissue, and collagenous material without significant nuclear atypia or mitoses {9990282}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A diagnosis of CMH should prompt referral for DICER1 genetic testing and lifelong surveillance if positive {34164613}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Rhabdomyomatous mesenchymal hamartoma

Essential: randomly arranged mature striated muscle fibres and benign admixed adipose tissue, collagen, and blood vessels.

Desirable: peripheral nerves.

Chondromesenchymal hamartoma

Essential: irregular islands of benign hyaline cartilage with variable components of mesenchyme.

Desirable: DICER1 testing.

Fibrous hamartoma of infancy

Essential: triphasic morphology with varying proportions of mature adipose tissue, bundles of cytologically bland fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells, and nodules of primitive mesenchyme.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Nasal CMH may recur if not completely excised {30167348}, and there are rare reports of malignant transformation of adult tumours {23348387;34321260}. FHIs may recur in about 1% of cases, but there were no metastases in a series of 145 cases {28059097}.
