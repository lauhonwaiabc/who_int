10.1.6.7 Angiosarcoma of soft tissue

Definition

Angiosarcoma (AS) is a rare aggressive sarcoma that recapitulates features of endothelial cells.

ICD-O coding

9120/3 Angiosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B56.Y & XH6264 Angiosarcoma, other specified primary site & Haemangiosarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: haemangiosarcoma; lymphangiosarcoma; malignant haemangioendothelioma; malignant angioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

AS associated with ultraviolet (UV) radiation occurs in skin, mainly of the scalp and face, including the eyelids. Deep periocular tissues can be involved secondarily or as a primary process {30643767}.

Clinical features

AS usually presents as a rapidly growing haemorrhagic mass or plaque. Symptoms are site-dependent, including visual loss and ptosis {30643767}.

Epidemiology

Periocular AS is rare {33268934}. Eyelid AS typically presents about the seventh decade of life {30643767}. Primary orbital AS has a wide age distribution with a median age of 24 years {6687155}.

Etiology

UV or other radiation exposure is a risk factor. The etiology is unknown for de novo tumours {31562204;20949568;32042194}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of AS is complex, heterogeneous, and subtype specific. Altered genes include TP53, KDR, PLCG1, ASXL1, PIK3CA, COL19A1, LPPRC, CACNA1S, GRIN2A, NOTCH2, and PTPRB. Genes affecting the MAPK pathway may be involved. MYC and FLT4 amplification are mainly reported in radiation-induced AS. A high mutation burden is reported in head and neck AS {20949568;32042194;24633157;26440310;31562204}.

Macroscopic appearance

AS is diffuse or (multi)nodular, haemorrhagic, and spongy or more solid and fleshy, often with necrosis. Small lesions can be subtle.

Histopathology

The spectrum of AS ranges from well to poorly differentiated lesions. Features can vary within a single neoplasm. Poorly defined variably formed anastomosing vascular channels dissect local tissues, with variable, occasionally predominant solid growth. Atypical spindle or epithelioid endothelial cells are usually multilayered with enlarged, often hyperchromatic nuclei and frequently distinct nucleoli. Pleomorphism can be striking. Mitotic figures, haemorrhage, and necrosis are more frequent in less-differentiated tumours {31950476}.

Immunohistochemistry

The most reliable immunohistochemical markers are CD31 (strong cytoplasmic and membranous staining) and ERG in combination, with variable CD34. Most ASs also stain with D2-40.

Differential diagnosis

Loss of pericytic layers by α-SMA distinguishes AS from benign vascular lesions. AS with keratin expression can mimic carcinomas {31950476}.

Cytology

Cytology demonstrates loosely cohesive clusters and dispersed atypical often oval to spindled cells with pleomorphic variably hyperchromatic nuclei, nucleoli, and mitotic and apoptotic figures. Sometimes cytoplasmic vacuoles are seen {34708939}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

MYC amplification may support the diagnosis of irradiation-induced AS and to a lesser extent (40%) UV-associated AS {21909081;24091875}.

Methylation profiling can be helpful in discriminating AS from other (vascular) lesions {33479225}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: at least focal vasoformative growth; multilayering of endothelial cells in a morphologically well-differentiated neoplasm; nuclear atypia, increased mitoses; CD31 and ERG positivity.

Staging

Staging is not recommended as the aggressive nature of AS does not align with soft tissue staging systems.

Prognosis and prediction

AS of soft tissue is highly aggressive. Five-year overall survival is about 25–50% {31950476;32042194;20485141}.
