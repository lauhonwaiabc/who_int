2.3.1.3 Syringocystadenoma papilliferum

Definition

Syringocystadenoma papilliferum (SCAP) is a benign apocrine neoplasm, composed of two layers of cells (inner epithelial and outer myoepithelial layer) forming dilated papillary and cystic structures with the stroma containing abundant plasma cells.

ICD-O coding

8406/0 Syringocystadenoma papilliferum

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH1PY0 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Syringocystadenoma papilliferum

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most cases are located on the lower lid margin. Smaller numbers of cases are seen on the upper lid or canthal area {29053036;26265653}.

Clinical features

The duration of the lesion before presentation ranges from 2 months to 70 years (mean: 24 years). The lesion size range is 4–25 mm. The main symptoms are swelling, pruritis, and bleeding. The appearance can be nodular, papular, verrucous, cystic, ulcerated, or crusted, or an umbilicated pore that oozes fluid {29053036;26265653}. SCAP can be misdiagnosed as basal cell carcinoma, sebaceous cyst, recurrent chalazion, and hidrocystoma {29053036;26265653}.

Epidemiology

The age range is 8–82 years (mean: 44 years). The M:F ratio is 1:2, and the lesion mostly affects White ethnic groups {29053036;26265653}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Activation of mutations in the MAPK signalling pathway, affecting BRAF, KRAS, and HRAS, are present in SCAP and tubular adenoma {25532942;29241739;35158743}. These activating mutations increase cell growth, differentiation, and survival.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

SCAP is an endophytic crateriform lesion, connected focally to the epidermis / hair follicle and composed of two layers of cells forming dilated papillary and cystic structures. The luminal cells are columnar with rounded or oval nuclei near the basal pole of the cell and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm with decapitation secretion. Mucinous metaplasia may be observed. The outer layer consists of basal/myoepithelial cells. The overlying squamous epithelium often displays prominent hyperplasia. The epidermis surrounding SCAP is often papillomatous or wart like, particularly in cases associated with naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn. The stroma contains numerous plasma cells.

Immunohistochemistry

BRAF p.V600E (antibody clone VE1) can be assessed by immunohistochemistry {29206289} and is seen in SCAP and tubular adenoma. The luminal cells of SCAP and tubular adenoma are positive for CEA and EMA, whereas the outer cell layer is typically reactive for myoepithelial markers, including SMA, calponin, SOX10, and p63 [[Requena L, SangÃ¼eza O. Differential diagnosis

The histopathological differential diagnosis of SCAP includes hidradenoma papilliferum {26863059}. Hidradenoma papilliferum has papillary formations similar to those of SCAP, and the epithelial cells lining these structures also show evidence of secretion by decapitation. However, hidradenoma papilliferum is not connected to the epidermis and lacks plasma cells.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRAF p.V600E mutations are present in 50–64% of SCAPs {35158743}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tubular structures with papillary projections and plasma cells in the stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

SCAP is a benign neoplasm. Excision with clear margins is curative. Incompletely excised lesions can recur {31686976;29053036;26265653}. There is 1 reported case of malignant transformation of SCAP, harbouring invasive squamous cell carcinoma {29217053}.
