6.1.2.4 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of the lacrimal gland

Definition

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) of the lacrimal gland is a malignant epithelial tumour characterized by squamous (epidermoid) cells, mucous cells, columnar cells, and intermediate cells.

ICD-O coding

8430/3 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D0Y & XH1J36 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Mucoepidermoid carcinoma

2D03.1 Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of lacrimal apparatus

Related terminology

Not recommended: mixed epidermoid and mucus-secreting carcinoma; mucoepidermoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MEC can arise throughout the main lacrimal gland and in accessory lacrimal glands.

Clinical features

MEC usually presents as a slow-growing mass in the lacrimal gland region, clinically indistinguishable from more-common tumours such as pleomorphic adenoma {24526859;24145905}. Occasionally it may cause painful proptosis. On CT, MEC is usually a well-circumscribed enhancing mass in the lacrimal gland region {24526859}.

Epidemiology

MEC is relatively rare, accounting for 1.5–4% of all epithelial tumours and about 7% of all malignant tumours in the lacrimal gland {24145905;24526859}. The mean age at presentation is 50–52 years, although it may occur at younger ages {24893972}.

Etiology

Etiological factors have not been described for lacrimal gland carcinoma. MEC of the salivary gland is associated with radiation exposure {33327563}.

Pathogenesis

Lacrimal gland and salivary gland MECs share a common histogenesis: they are composed of varying proportions of luminal and myoepithelial cells. This is supported by reports describing MEC arising in pleomorphic adenoma {25005213;34939520;34652307}.

A CRTC1::MAML2 gene fusion is present in the majority of tumours and is considered a key oncogenic driver {29076877;33830080}. This fusion transactivates expression of the EGFR (HER1) ligand {33830080}. RAS and PIK3 family mutations also involved in the EGFR pathway have been detected in poorly differentiated tumours {32129900}. In the salivary gland, ERBB2 and EGFR are upregulated in high-grade MEC {23855785}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours usually appear circumscribed, with a greyish-white lobulated cut surface. Cystic areas may be evident depending on tumour grade. Calcification has been described {24145905}.

Histopathology

MEC of the lacrimal gland is composed of variable proportions of three cells types: squamous, mucin-secreting, and intermediate. The squamous cells have medium to large nuclei with eosinophilic cytoplasm and intercellular bridges between cells, but keratinization is rare. Mucous cells form small glands, tubules, and cysts; the cysts contain inspissated mucin (which may calcify) and are lined by mucous cells, looking deceptively bland. The intermediate (or transitional) cells are oval, with a round nucleus and faintly eosinophilic cytoplasm. In addition, columnar, clear, and oncocytic cells may be admixed. PAS or Alcian blue may be required to visualize mucin. The stroma may be hyalinized with calcification and ossification. A granulomatous reaction to extravasated mucus may be seen.

There are several systems for grading salivary gland MEC {27721274;31021855;34231055}. Most divide tumours into three levels (low-grade, intermediate-grade, and high-grade). The systems are based on the proportions of cystic areas, proportion of necrosis, perineural invasion, mitoses, nuclear pleomorphism, invasion front, and lymphovascular invasion. More recently a binary grading system separating low- and intermediate-grade tumours from high-grade tumours using the Brandwein system has been shown to best stratify patients into groups based on risk of recurrence {31021855}.

The differential diagnosis includes squamous cell carcinoma, sebaceous carcinoma, adenosquamous carcinoma, and pleomorphic adenoma.

Cytology

In the salivary gland, the cytology of low-grade lesions consists predominantly of mucus and macrophages with occasional bland epithelial cells {9258615}. Higher-grade lesions show an admixture of cell types, and pleomorphism, mitoses, and necrosis can be present. There are few reports of the cytology of lacrimal gland MEC although associated inflammation can cause diagnostic error {22551376}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A CRTC1::MAML2 fusion protein has been identified in lacrimal gland MECs {22323114}; this fusion, resulting from a t(11;19)(q21;p13.1) translocation, is present in 80% of salivary gland MECs {34231055} and may be used as diagnostic maker.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: squamous cells, intermediate cells, and mucous cells in varying proportions; for lower-grade tumours: cystic, mucous cell–rich, and well circumscribed; for higher-grade tumours: solid, higher proportion of squamous and intermediate cells.

Desirable: CRTC1::MAML2 fusion on genetic analysis.

Staging

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma is staged according to the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification for carcinoma of the lacrimal gland.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic factors have not been formally reported for MEC of the lacrimal gland. In the salivary glands, the prognosis for MEC varies with tumour grade. Low/intermediate-grade tumours have a survival rate of 97% whereas high-grade tumours have poorer survival at 67% {24771140}.

The CTRC1::MAML2 fusion is associated with better prognosis {34630791;33830080}. RAS and PIK3 mutations may be associated with higher-grade tumours {32129900}.
