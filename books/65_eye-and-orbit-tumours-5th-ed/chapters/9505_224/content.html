9.5.0.5 Xanthelasma

Definition

Xanthelasma are benign, subcutaneous, localized lesions composed of foamy histiocytes and Touton giant cells with a variable degree of lipid deposition in the absence of a metabolic disorder.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A06.4 Xanthelasma of eyelid

Related terminology

Acceptable: xanthomatous lesions.

Not recommended: histiocytomas; non–Langerhans cell histiocytosis.

Subtype(s)

Xanthoma

Localization

Xanthelasma involves the eyelids.

Clinical features

Xanthelasma appears as flat or minimally elevated yellow lesions commonly in the medial aspect of the eyelids; the lesions are often bilateral and symmetrical {29296091}.

A tuberous xanthoma is an elevated and nodular xanthelasma. These appear as solitary or multiple, placoid or papular lesions of the eyelids, more probably associated with hyperlipidaemia types II and III {23882283}.

Epidemiology

Xanthelasma has a reported incidence of 0.56–1.5% and represents approximately 6% of eyelid lesions {19384066}. The age of onset varies from 15 to 75 years with a peak between the fourth and fifth decades of life {23105704}. It is unclear if there is a female predilection.

Xanthomas may occur in individuals of any age and show no sex predilection. In xanthoma disseminatum, there is a male predilection and the age of onset is < 25 years in as many as a third of cases {19384066}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Xanthelasma and xanthoma are not true neoplasms but are reactive developmental or inflammatory lesions {26794218}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown. The inflammatory milieu in xanthelasma is considered analogous to the early stages of cardiac atherosclerotic plaque formation {28481769}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Xanthelasma shows focal collections of lipid-laden histiocytes distributed mainly around blood vessels and adnexal structures in the eyelid dermis with chronic inflammatory cells and fibrosis.

Tuberous xanthomas are more deeply located in the dermis and display foamy histiocytes, Touton giant cells, extracellular cholesterol deposits, chronic inflammation, and fibrosis.

Immunohistochemistry

The foamy histiocytes typically express CD68, CD163, and factor XIIIa, but lack CD21, CD35, S100, CD1a, and Birbeck granules {33869889;19961259}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes necrobiotic xanthogranuloma, Erdheim–Chester disease and adult-onset xanthogranuloma with periocular xanthogranuloma {2043074;26966089}, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, Rosai–Dorfman disease, and crystal-storing histiocytosis (see related sections).

A clinicopathological correlation may be necessary for challenging cases.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: yellowish plaque or flat lesion around the eyelids; presence of lipid-laden histiocytes, giant cells, and chronic inflammatory cells.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The lesions are benign and are commonly observed. Surgical excision for larger lesions or cosmetic reasons may be considered.
