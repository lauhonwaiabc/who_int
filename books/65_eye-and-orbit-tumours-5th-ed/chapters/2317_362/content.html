2.3.1.7 Tubular adenoma

Definition

Tubular adenoma is a benign apocrine neoplasm comprising lobules of multiple irregularly shaped tubular structures that have a double- to several-layered cell lining (inner epithelial and outer myoepithelial), embedded in a fibrous stroma.

ICD-O coding

8211/0 Tubular adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH7SY6 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Tubular adenoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: tubulopapillary adenoma; papillary eccrine adenoma; tubular apocrine adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Equal numbers of cases are located on the upper and lower eyelid {31686976}.

Clinical features

The duration of symptoms is 6–12 months. The lesion size (where the data is available) is 6–8 mm. It presents as an asymptomatic, single, round, well-circumscribed nodule with a smooth surface {31686976}.

Epidemiology

There are very few reported cases of pure eyelid tubular adenoma. The age range is 38–63 years (mean: 47 years). The M:F ratio is 1:1. It mostly affects White people {31686976}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Activating mutations in the MAPK signalling pathway, affecting BRAF, KRAS, and HRAS, are present in syringocystadenoma papilliferum and tubular adenoma {25532942;29241739;35158743}. These increase cell growth, differentiation, and survival. Tubular adenoma can develop sporadically or, more frequently, on the genetic background of a cutaneous mosaic RASopathy, such as naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn {26360803}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Tubular adenomas are well-circumscribed dermal neoplasms that may extend into the subcutis. They have an overall lobular architecture and are typically encased by a fibrous stroma. The lobules consist of multiple irregularly shaped tubular structures that have a double- to several-layered epithelial lining. The peripheral epithelial layer consists of cuboidal to flattened (myoepithelial) cells, and the luminal layer consists of columnar cells that sometimes demonstrate decapitation secretion. In some tubules, papillary cellular extensions that are devoid of stroma project into the lumina. Cellular debris and eosinophilic granular material are found within some lumina {4113017}. The neoplasm lacks cytological atypia and mitotic figures. Overlying epidermal hyperplasia may be present. In cases that occur in conjunction with syringocystadenoma papilliferum {2551940;3036918}, the tubular adenoma component is typically present underlying the syringocystadenoma component.

Immunohistochemistry

BRAF p.V600E (antibody clone VE1) can be assessed by immunohistochemistry {29206289} and is seen in syringocystadenoma papilliferum and tubular adenoma. The luminal cells of syringocystadenoma papilliferum and tubular adenoma are positive for CEA and EMA, whereas the outer cell layer is typically reactive for myoepithelial markers including SMA, calponin, SOX10, and p63 [[Requena L, SangÃ¼eza O. Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRAF p.V600E mutations are present in 66% of tubular adenomas {35158743}. Naevus sebaceus–associated tubular adenomas consistently harbour HRAS or KRAS mutations {22683711}. In sporadic tumours, HRAS p.G13R mutations are found in a few cases; KRAS p.G12D mutations are less frequent {25532942}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobules consisting of multiple irregularly shaped tubular structures that have a double- to several-layered epithelial lining, embedded in a fibrous stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Tubular adenomas are benign neoplasms. Excision with clear margins is curative. Incompletely excised lesions can recur {31686976;29053036;26265653}. There are no cases of malignant transformation of tubular adenoma.
