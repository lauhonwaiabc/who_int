7.2.3.2 Melanoma involving the lacrimal drainage system

Definition

Melanoma of the lacrimal drainage system is a rare invasive melanocytic neoplasm.

ICD-O coding

8720/3 Melanoma involving the lacrimal drainage system

ICD-11 coding

2D03.Y & XH4846 Other specified malignant neoplasm of lacrimal apparatus & Malignant melanoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: lacrimal drainage system melanoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The lacrimal sac is the most common location, but tumours can invade other parts of the lacrimal drainage system or adjacent soft tissue and bone (e.g. the lacrimal crest) {23456510}. Rare cases arising in the nasolacrimal duct have also been reported {17123453}.

Clinical features

The most common presentations include unilateral epiphora, mass lesions in the medial canthal area, bloody discharge, epistaxis, and (rarely) pain {33843111}. Lesions are often misdiagnosed as dacryocystitis and repeated biopsies are sometimes necessary {24261959;25540768}. CT and MRI generally show a partially defined soft tissue mass in the lacrimal sac fossa, with possible extension into the nasolacrimal duct or anterior ethmoid sinus {32408870}.

Epidemiology

Melanomas of the lacrimal drainage system are extremely rare, accounting for 1–4% of tumours at this site {32408870;20958177;7947444}. The mean age of patients is 59 years.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown, but it is probably similar to that of melanomas occurring at other mucosal sites not exposed to the sun.

Pathogenesis

These melanomas probably originate from melanocytes associated with the epithelial mucosa lining the lacrimal drainage apparatus.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour may be seen as an irregular dark-brown mass adherent to the lacrimal sac wall {25540768}.

Histopathology

The mucosal lining of the lacrimal sac and surrounding soft tissues is infiltrated by variably pigmented spindled or epithelioid melanocytes with moderate to abundant pigmented granular cytoplasm, often showing large vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli. Nuclear pleomorphism and variable numbers of mitotic figures are present. Tumour cells are immunoreactive for HMB45, melan-A, and S100 {7222429}.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

One lacrimal sac melanoma has been reported to have BRAF p.V600E mutations {31747798}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstrated malignancy with melanocytic differentiation; relation to the mucosa of the lacrimal tract; no evidence of previous or synchronous melanoma outside the lacrimal tract.

Staging

Stage according to the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for sinonasal tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

The primary treatment modality is surgery (i.e. dacryocystectomy). Poor prognostic factors include positive surgical margins, large tumour size, and dissemination of the tumour {28215853}. Local recurrence is common. For advanced cases, treatment may range from wide en bloc resection to radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy in a variety of combinations {31747789;33240474}.
