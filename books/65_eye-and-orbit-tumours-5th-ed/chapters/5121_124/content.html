5.1.2.1 Medulloepithelioma of the optic disc and optic nerve

Definition

Medulloepithelioma of the optic disc and optic nerve is a rare primary intraocular embryonal tumour characterized by cords of multilayered neuroepithelial cells resembling the medullary epithelium of the optic cup.

Related terminology

Not recommended: diktyoma, teratoneuroma.

Subtype(s)

Benign non-teratoid medulloepithelioma; malignant non-teratoid medulloepithelioma; benign teratoid medulloepithelioma; malignant teratoid medulloepithelioma

Localization

Rare medulloepitheliomas involve the orbital optic nerve and optic disc. Most ocular medulloepitheliomas are located on the inner surface of the ciliary body.

Clinical features

Of reported cases, 86% involve the orbital optic nerve. Most are large orbital tumours that often extend to the orbital apex and can invade the CNS and cavernous sinus {20497085;21676432;10933227;22571440}. Optic nerve tumours typically present with symptoms of an orbital mass {20497085;20497433}. Misdiagnosis as optic nerve glioma is common. Fewer tumours form optic disc masses without significant optic nerve involvement {21676432;9338683}. These can cause leukocoria and often are misdiagnosed as retinoblastoma {9338683}. Ophthalmoscopically, the optic disc lesions appear as round or multilobular, well-circumscribed white tumours with a smooth convex surface that cover and obscure the optic nerve head {21676432;7231881}.

Epidemiology

About three quarters of optic nerve cases occur in boys. The mean age at presentation is 3.5 years (median: 3.5 years, range: 1–12 years) {20497085;22571440}.

Etiology

Most intraocular medulloepitheliomas occur sporadically and are not associated with known risk factors. Medulloepithelioma of the ciliary body (but not the optic nerve) has been reported in patients with the pleuropulmonary blastoma familial tumour predisposition syndrome caused by germline mutation of DICER1 {23796765}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Optic nerve medulloepithelioma appears as marked thickening of the optic nerve or a lobulated perineural mass {4827430;20497433}. Intraocular extension may be present.

Histopathology

Medulloepitheliomas are composed of bands or cords of polarized neuroepithelial cells, resembling embryonic medullary epithelium, that are surrounded by myxoid stroma rich in hyaluronidase-sensitive mucopolysaccharide. A net-like diktyomatous pattern of thinner interweaving neuroepithelium strands, sheets of poorly differentiated neuroblastic cells resembling retinoblastoma, multilayered rosettes, and cystoid spaces are additional features. Medulloepitheliomas are subclassified as benign and malignant, and teratoid and non-teratoid. Teratoid tumours contain heteroplastic elements including cartilage (most common), muscle, rhabdomyoblasts, and brain-like tissue. Criteria for malignancy include the sheets of poorly differentiated neuroblastic cells that can contain Flexner–Wintersteiner and Homer Wright rosettes, evidence of aggressive behaviour including invasion of ocular tissues and extraocular extension, sarcomatous change, atypia, and mitotic activity {655220;22288972}. Immunoreactivity for LIN28A, PAX8, and RB1 can help to distinguish poorly differentiated tumours from retinoblastoma {25748578;33002562}.

Cytology

Cytological diagnosis is limited to malignant round cell tumour in most reported cases of ciliary body medulloepithelioma {17300673;21062379;28217870;22288972;12713484}. Rosettes and focal epithelial-like fragments with cellular polarity and multilayering occasionally are observed {21062379}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour composed of bands of polarized neuroepithelial cells within a myxoid matrix located in the orbital portion of the optic nerve or on the optic disc.

Desirable: teratoid tumours contain heteroplastic elements, most often cartilage.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for optic nerve medulloepithelioma appears to be worse than for ciliary body tumours owing to direct access to the CNS {20497085}. Patients in several reported cases of optic nerve medulloepithelioma with direct intracranial extension invasion died within 18 months of tumour resection {10933227}, and another developed brain metastases {12045984}. The relationship between malignant histopathological features and clinical outcome has been questioned {27239464}.
