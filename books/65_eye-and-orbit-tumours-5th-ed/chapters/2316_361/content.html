2.3.1.6 Mixed tumour

Definition

Mixed tumour is a benign sweat gland neoplasm composed of epithelial, myoepithelial, and mesenchymal elements; it is the cutaneous analogue of pleomorphic adenoma.

ICD-O coding

8940/0 Mixed tumour

8940/0 Apocrine mixed tumour

8940/0 Eccrine mixed tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH2KC1 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Mixed tumour, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: chondroid syringoma.

Subtype(s)

Apocrine mixed tumour; eccrine mixed tumour

Localization

Mixed tumour is more common in the upper eyelid (upper eyelid 60% of cases, lower eyelid 40% of cases) and brow area {16644368}.

Clinical features

Mixed tumours are usually solitary, slow-growing, asymptomatic, well-circumscribed non-ulcerating nodules with a smooth surface. They are usually 5–30 mm {16644368} and not fixed to underlying structures {16644368}.

Epidemiology

Mixed tumour of the eyelid has a male predilection, and the average patient age is 50 years (range: 22–73 years) {15083078;8874433;16644368}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Pluripotent cells are probably the source of glandular and myoepithelial cells. PLAG1 {22038920} and EWSR1 {21725291} are rearranged in cutaneous mixed tumours. PLAG1 is a developmentally regulated zinc-finger proto-oncogene. Rearrangements of PLAG1 are detected by FISH in less than half of cutaneous mixed tumours {32345665}. PLAG1 activation through gene fusion typically occurs owing to promoter swapping from various fusion partner genes that are ubiquitously expressed, such as CTNNB1, LIFR, and CHCHD7.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Mixed tumour has two main subtypes: apocrine and eccrine.

Apocrine cases present as a well-circumscribed lesion with three components: epithelial, myoepithelial, and mesenchymal (i.e. mucinous, chondroid, osseous, adipocytic, or fibrotic). The epithelial component is composed of elongated branched tubules and cystic spaces. These epithelial structures are lined by two cell layers; the peripheral layer is composed of cuboidal myoepithelial cells, and the inner layer is formed by columnar cells. The luminal cell layer may exhibit secretion by decapitation. Cystic areas often contain homogeneous eosinophilic material. Solid aggregates of epithelial cells, as well as isolated polygonal and plasmacytoid epithelial cells, may be also seen. Tumours with a prominent myoepithelial component may show focal cytological atypia; such cases have been called hyaline cell–rich mixed tumour {17592277}. Follicular and/or sebaceous differentiation can be present {10408348}.

The eccrine subtype of mixed tumour is less common and lacks the PLAG1 gene rearrangement {30839344}. It is composed of numerous non-branching, round ductal elements lined by a single layer of cuboidal cells embedded in a predominantly myxoid or chondromyxoid stroma. Small solid epithelial aggregates may be seen. Unusual findings include cribriform areas, clear cell change, hyaline cells, pseudorosette-like structures, prominent osseous metaplasia, and striking intracytoplasmic vacuolization of the neoplastic cells resulting in physaliphorous-like cells {21697702}.

Immunohistochemistry

Mixed tumours express SOX10, p63, S100, calponin, SMA, EMA, KIT, CEA, various cytokeratins, and PLAG1, although the eccrine subtype does not express this marker.

Differential diagnosis

Myoepithelioma is a related entity distinguished by the lack of ductal structures. When the mesenchymal component is prominent, the differential diagnosis of mixed tumour includes chondroma, but chondromas do not contain epithelial cells.

Cytology

Although not described from the eyelid, FNAB of these tumours shows appearances reflecting their histology: fibrillary chondromyxoid material, with a prominent cellular component composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells {20391975;26800260;28772003}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed smooth-surfaced nodule; tubules and cords of benign glandular epithelium embedded in a myxohyalinized or a cartilaginous stroma; in eccrine mixed tumours, there are no signs of decapitation secretion or of sebaceous or follicular differentiation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Mixed tumours are benign neoplasms that grow slowly and can reach several centimetres. Complete surgical excision is curative. Incomplete excision can result in local recurrence {25177574;16644368}.
