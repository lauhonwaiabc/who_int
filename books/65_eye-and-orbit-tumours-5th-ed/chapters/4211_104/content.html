4.2.1.1 Congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium

Definition

Congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium (CHRPE) is a congenital patch of hypertrophic, benign retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells containing macromelanosomes.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa

Related terminology

Acceptable: solitary congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These lesions usually affect the peripheral or midperipheral fundus, but 2% of cases occur in the macular or peripapillary region {16568286;19604161}.

Clinical features

CHRPE presents as a unilateral, sharply demarcated, flat or minimally elevated plaque of abnormally tall RPE cells {1191102;14522773}. The lesions are typically asymptomatic and are found incidentally during ophthalmoscopy. Most lesions are round or oval, 13% are geographical with smooth margins marked by focal convexities, and 85% are jet black or dark brown. Rare non-pigmented cases in adults probably result from the confluence of characteristic depigmented intralesional lacunae, which develop in 43% of CHRPE lesions and have been documented to enlarge gradually in 32% of cases. The reported mean and median largest diameters are 4.7 mm and 4.5 mm, respectively (range: 1–13 mm). Most lesions are surrounded by a narrow pigmented or depigmented ring or halo. Of lesions followed up for > 3 years, 83% have shown subtle enlargement {23244302;7091284;14522773;7160628}. Nodules develop in about 1% of cases and have been confirmed to be RPE adenocarcinomas in several instances {19744732;11296028;10815155;15983818}. Optical coherence tomography reveals thinning and photoreceptor loss in the overlying retina {21117611;24126031;21817965;24366775;16581426}. Careful perimetry may disclose a focal scotoma corresponding to the lesion {1191102}. CHRPE appears dark on fundus autofluorescence imaging {18040252;25390583}.

CHRPE must be distinguished from choroidal naevus, small choroidal melanoma, congenital grouped pigmentation (so-called bear tracks), adenoma and adenocarcinoma of the RPE, solitary hamartoma of the RPE, torpedo maculopathy, and pigmented ocular fundus lesion (POFL) or hamartoma of the RPE in familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP). Unfortunately, the term “congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium” is often used erroneously in the non-ophthalmic literature to designate POFLs or hamartomas of the RPE that occur in some kindreds with FAP. Unlike CHRPE, hamartoma of the RPE tends to be multiple and bilateral, and it has less well defined margins {3821797;2845322}. In a clinicopathological study, three types of POFLs were identified: one type similar to CHRPE and two types consistent with hamartomatous RPE malformations in a patient with FAP {2173407}. In 1 patient with a POFL and FAP, optical coherence tomography disclosed retinal invasion {24266367}. CHRPE should not be used to determine the presence of hereditary FAP, though ophthalmoscopy may be helpful in addition to other investigations {31525261}.

Epidemiology

These lesions are believed to be congenital, but the mean patient age at diagnosis is 43 years (range: < 1 year to 83 years). There is no race predilection. A mild female predominance has been reported, with 64% of cases occurring in female patients {2717803;14522773;24750466}.

Etiology

CHRPE is believed to be a developmental lesion {2402417} and is associated with FAP, where it may be present in as many as 80% of patients {31525261}.

Pathogenesis

The lesion originates from the RPE. There is no genetic profile and no germline mutations have been found in the APC gene within the lesions.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Light microscopy reveals a monolayer of thickened RPE cells. The cytoplasm is packed with large round melanosomes, which often obscure the nucleus {1115190;2717803;2402417;15965180}. Lacunae show segmental loss of the RPE and focal chorioretinal adhesions. Fluorescence microscopy confirms the absence of lipofuscin in dysfunctional RPE cells. The sharply circumscribed patches of abnormal RPE cells are readily apparent on scanning electron microscopy {2717803;2402417}.

An increased amount of melanin pigment is also found focally in RPE cells in congenital grouped pigmentation (so-called bear tracks), but the granules retain their normal ellipsoidal configuration and do not fill the entire cytoplasm {8460012;1191104}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: unilateral, sharply demarcated, flat or minimally elevated plaque of abnormally tall RPE cells.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases are benign and asymptomatic and require no treatment. Rarely, RPE adenoma or low-grade RPE adenocarcinoma may develop from CHRPE {19744732;11296028;10815155;15983818}. Typical CHRPE does not appear to be associated with FAP or colorectal adenocarcinoma {8148147;12410463;1333580}.
