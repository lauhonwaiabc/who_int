2.2.1.1 Verruca vulgaris

Definition

Verruca vulgaris is a common benign HPV-driven epidermal squamoproliferative lesion.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

1E80 Common warts

Related terminology

Acceptable: common viral wart.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The common sites of occurrence of verruca vulgaris are the fingers, hands, elbows, knees, and face {22131115;9666831;23932731;29757860;1647902}.

Clinical features

Verruca may present as single or multiple papules, keratotic plaques, and nodules, most ranging between 1 and 20 mm {22131115;9666831}. Lesions may also occur on eyebrow tattoos {29757860;34646630}.

Epidemiology

The reported prevalence is as many as 3.5% of adults and 20–33% of adolescents {1647901;6162313;24976535;24019275}. Immunosuppression is a significant risk factor, with multiple verrucae often occurring per patient {22131115;1661712}.

Etiology

Low-risk HPV subtypes 1, 2, 4, 7, and 26–29 are well documented in common warts {22131115;2156916}. HPV subtypes 75–77 have been reported in lesions in immunocompromised patients {9146528}.

Recognized ways of contracting cutaneous HPV infection include via direct physical contact, tattooing, fomites, or autoinoculation {23932731;34646630}. For the latter, hand transfer helps explain the development of facial or eyelid lesions {23932731}.

Pathogenesis

After inoculation of the skin via a break in the epithelial barrier, keratinocytes are infected. HPV DNA production and viral assembly occur progressively in the superficial layers of the epidermis, with the eventual release of viral particles with desquamated cells {2156916}.

Macroscopic appearance

Verruca vulgaris presents as a skin-coloured elevated nodule with a keratotic and papillomatous surface {31241843}. Facial lesions may possess a filiform configuration {1647902}.

Histopathology

Verrucae are characterized by papillomatous epidermal projections tipped by columns of parakeratosis, alongside hyperkeratosis. Superficial keratinocytes feature coarse keratohyaline granules and the characteristic koilocytes are often present, frequently underneath the parakeratosis {22131115;9666831;23932731}. Elongated rete ridges are oriented centrally and dermal papillae show prominent blood vessels. A lymphocytic infiltrate with basal keratinocytic degeneration may be present, typically associated with regression {7430456;7430479}.

Seborrhoeic keratosis and trichilemmomas displaying hyperkeratosis and papillomatosis are differential diagnoses. The difficulty is compounded by koilocytes being often absent in older verrucae.

Cytology

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: papillomatosis; hypergranulosis; hyperkeratosis; parakeratosis.

Desirable: koilocytosis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Verrucae typically resolve spontaneously with time: in children, about 80% of lesions regress in 2 years {23932731;11167676}. In immunosuppressed individuals, verrucae may be more persistent {22131115}. Different treatment modalities in recalcitrant cases or in immune deficiency include curettage, cryotherapy, chemical agents, and laser {31241843}.
