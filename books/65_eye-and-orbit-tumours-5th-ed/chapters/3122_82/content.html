3.1.2.2 Lisch nodule

Definition

A Lisch nodule is a melanocytic hamartoma of the iris consisting of benign, predominantly spindle-shaped melanocytes, in the context of neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1).

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F36.1 Benign neoplasm of iris

Related terminology

Acceptable: iris hamartoma; melanocytic hamartoma; Sakurai–Lisch nodule.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lisch nodules are typically multiple and can occur anywhere on the iris, mainly its inferior half {14560838}.

Clinical features

The presence of ≥ 2 Lisch nodules constitutes one of the seven clinical diagnostic criteria for NF1 established by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) {3128965;28230061}. They are bilateral, small, minimally elevated multiple iris nodules, predominantly in the inferior iris {14560838}, and usually appear in early childhood, at about 2 years of age. Their colour can vary from tan to cream {27493794}. They can be mistaken for iris mammillations {8763309}.

Epidemiology

In NF1, the prevalence of Lisch nodules rises with age {1901624} and skin involvement {3081287}. They occur in 63% of patients of all ages, and in > 92% of postpubertal adults with NF1 {14560838}. In children with genetically confirmed NF1, the prevalence of Lisch nodules is 34% {35119474}.

Etiology

Haploinsufficiency of the NF1 gene located on chromosome arm 17q is a necessary event in most cases, according to germline testing {28436162;19516012}. A unilateral case without NF1 has been reported, though this may be a forme fruste of NF1 {12654389}. Other rare associations described are Cushing disease {8470969}, familial angiolipomatosis {10506451}, and neurofibromatosis type 2 {2510706}.

Pathogenesis

Mutations in NF1, leading to dysregulation of RAS signalling, underlie Lisch nodules {3107130;20027112}. DNA damage caused by ultraviolet (UV) radiation may play a role {19516012}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Lisch nodules are usually dome-shaped plaques of mostly spindle-shaped, cytologically bland melanocytes and are arranged haphazardly on the anterior face and anterior stroma of the iris. Nucleoli are not prominent, and mitotic figures are not identified. Ovoid to rounded melanocytes and melanophages can be present. The underlying iris stroma often contains similar melanocytes arranged with a poorly defined margin {6814406;1908964}. The melanocytes are positive by immunohistochemistry for S100. Transmission electron microscopy shows cells that contain immature and mature melanosomes. No pre-melanosomes are seen {6814406}.

Cytology

Not available

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Lisch nodules are not directly tested in clinical practice and detailed molecular genetic studies of the lesions are lacking.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland spindle or ovoid melanocytes, or both, without mitotic activity and with inconspicuous nucleoli, located on the iris surface.

Desirable: immunohistochemical positivity with a melanocytic marker.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Lisch nodules are not sight threatening and do not undergo malignant transformation.
