10.1.6.8 Lobular capillary haemangioma

Definition

Lobular capillary haemangioma (LCH) is a benign vascular cutaneous and mucosal neoplasm that consists of a proliferation of capillaries with a lobular architecture.

ICD-O coding

9131/0 Lobular capillary haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2F26 Lobular capillary haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: pyogenic granuloma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LCH occurs in the eyelid, the conjunctiva, and, less frequently, the sclera {26973052;31619900}.

Clinical features

LCH presents as a rapidly growing (weeks to months), circumscribed, lobular, red, occasionally ulcerated, vascular mass {31895731;31503170;27647119;31619900;26973052}.

Epidemiology

LCH of the periocular region is rare. This tumour occurs without appreciable sex predilection in adults at a median age of 40 years {26973052;31619900}.

Etiology

The etiology of LCH is not well understood. Conjunctival LCH has been occasionally associated with pregnancy {34550219}. Non-periocular LCH has been associated with vascular malformations and biological agents (retinoids and BRAF inhibitors) {22134277;6225396;27116335;16958814}.

Pathogenesis

LCH may be driven by FLT4 and the nitric oxide pathway {22955520}. BRAF, KRAS, HRAS, NRAS, GNA11, and GNA14 mutations have been reported in cutaneous LCH {31310691;26802240;27476652}, suggesting that MAPK signalling activation may be involved in the pathogenesis of some lesions.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

LCH consists of lobular arrangements of numerous capillary-sized vessels lined by proliferating endothelial cells {21788081}. Each vascular channel is surrounded by a layer of pericytes. The overlying epithelium/epidermis is often thinned and frequently ulcerated. There may be oedema and inflammation in the area of ulceration where, in addition, granulation tissue–like changes may be seen. The lobular arrangement of the lesion and relatively scant inflammatory infiltrate in the non-ulcerated region distinguish LCH from the exuberant granulation tissue in the conjunctiva (a lesion termed conjunctival pyogenic granuloma by clinicians) {26973052;31619900}.

Immunohistochemistry

Endothelial cells express CD31, ERG, and CD34, and are negative for GLUT1 and on staining with D2-40 {26973052}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobular proliferation of capillaries associated with oedematous stroma.

Desirable: endothelial cells are GLUT1-negative.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Periocular LCH is successfully treated with complete excision {26973052;31619900}.
