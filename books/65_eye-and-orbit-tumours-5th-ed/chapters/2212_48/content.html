2.2.1.2 Squamous papilloma of the eyelid

Definition

Squamous papilloma is a benign exophytic verrucous or broad-based skin tumour, with or without signs of HPV infection.

ICD-O coding

8052/0 Squamous papilloma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH50T2 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Squamous cell papilloma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: skin tag; squamous cell papilloma.

Not recommended: acrochordon.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lesions can be anywhere on the skin, including the eyelid.

Clinical features

Squamous papilloma is a sessile or pedunculated soft, smooth lesion with a similar colour to adjacent skin. The surface is more-or-less verrucous, with or without a keratin crust. Ulceration due to mechanical irritation may be present. Papillomas are often multiple {19384066}.

Epidemiology

Squamous papilloma is among the most common benign eyelid tumours and can occur at any age, with no sex predilection {30450316;26634208;34849127}.

Etiology

Some lesions are HPV-related and may represent burned-out HPV infections {32809572}.

Pathogenesis

Low-risk HPV types induce benign neoplastic epithelial proliferation.

Macroscopic appearance

Squamous papilloma is a sessile or pedunculated, small, skin-coloured lesion. The surface is more-or-less verrucous, with or without a keratin crust. It is sometimes ulcerated.

Histopathology

Squamous papilloma is a verrucous, broad-based or finger-like exophytic epithelial neoplasm with a fibrovascular core. There is an absence of dysplasia and of relevant mitotic activity, with no signs of dermal invasion. Hyperparakeratosis or ulceration due to mechanical irritation may be present. In contrast to verruca vulgaris, squamous cell papillomas have an exophytic, papillomatous, and less acanthomatous growth pattern. HPV cytopathic signs, such as koilocytes or double nuclei, may occur. However, some squamous cell papillomas may represent regressed verruca vulgaris.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: finger-like projections of vascularized connective tissue covered by hyperplastic epithelium; absence of dysplasia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrences occur in cases of incomplete removal.
