10.1.5.4 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a malignant spindle cell tumour often arising from a peripheral nerve or a pre-existing benign nerve sheath tumour, or in a patient with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) and often showing limited Schwannian differentiation.

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant schwannoma; neurofibrosarcoma; neurogenic sarcoma; malignant perineurioma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Localization

The most common locations are the trunk and extremities, followed by the head and neck area {27158754;3082508;25035100}.

Clinical features

MPNSTs typically present in people between 20 and 50 years of age, with younger ages for NF1-associated tumours. Patients present with proptosis and displacement of the globe. There may be associated neuropathic symptoms such as paraesthesia and pain {33777620}.

Epidemiology

Orbital MPNST is rare accounting for < 1 in 1000 orbital biopsies [[Verdijk RM, Pecorella I, Mooy CM. Chapter 12: The Orbit, Including the Lacrimal Gland and Lacrimal Drainage System. In: Heegaard S, Grossniklaus H, editors. Eye Pathology: An Illustrated Guide. Berlin (Germany): Springer-Verlag; 2015. pp. 547â€“731.]] and < 1 orbital case in 100 MPNSTs {20010306}.

Etiology

Some cases are associated with NF1 (~50%) or radiation (~10%) {7377756}.

Pathogenesis

MPNSTs have complex karyotypes and frequent, concurrent inactivating mutations involving three pathways: NF1, CDKN2A and/or CDKN2B, and PRC2 core components (EED or SUZ12) {25240281;25119042;25305755;30722027}. As many as 80% of high-grade MPNSTs exhibit complete loss of PRC2 activity and complete loss of H3 p.K27me3 on immunohistochemistry {26645727;25240281;26585554}. Epithelioid MPNSTs are molecularly distinct, characterized by frequent SMARCB1 gene inactivation {30864974}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most MPNSTs are > 50 mm. Intraneural tumours show fusiform enlargement of the involved nerve. MPNST shows a tan-white, fleshy cut surface, often with haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

MPNST shows interlacing fascicles of spindle-shaped cells, often with alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas. A haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern is characteristic, and denser perivascular growth is common. There are often areas of geographical necrosis and high mitotic activity. Nuclei are hyperchromatic and spindled to serpentine, while the cytoplasm is pale and eosinophilic. Occasional cases show prominent pleomorphism. Approximately 15% of MPNSTs show heterologous differentiation, which may be rhabdomyoblastic (triton tumour), osseous, cartilaginous, or vascular, as well as epithelial (glandular or squamous).

MPNSTs show limited staining for S100 (< 50% of tumours), SOX10 (< 70%), and GFAP (20–30%) {23929265}. Complete loss of staining for H3 p.K27me3 is seen in 50–80% of all MPNSTs, with loss being more frequent in high-grade and radiation-induced than NF1-associated MPNST {28776579;26645727;26585554;29482987}.

Differential diagnosis from melanoma (especially desmoplastic melanoma) can be challenging as there is overlap of histological and immunohistochemical features. Also, with melanoma being more common in the head and neck than MPNST, some of the reported MPNSTs in the past were, in fact, melanomas {27666764}.

Epithelioid MPNST is composed of epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, sometimes embedded in an abundant myxoid or hyalinized stroma {1746681}. It is not associated with NF1 and rarely arises ex schwannoma {25602794}. Unlike conventional MPNST, epithelioid MPNST shows strong and diffuse staining for S100 and SOX10, with retained H3 p.K27me3 and loss of SMARCB1 expression {30864974}.

Diagnostic criteria have been proposed to distinguish atypical neurofibroma, cellular neurofibroma, atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential, and low-grade and high-grade MPNST in NF1 patients {28551330}.

The main differential diagnosis at this site is with melanoma and solitary fibrous tumour.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In diagnostically challenging cases, DNA methylation profiling can identify a specific tumour signature of MPNST {33479225}. Other supporting molecular data include mutations in NF1 and SUZ12 or EED.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #38515Box 11.01.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

MPNST shows aggressive behaviour with poor prognosis. Adverse prognostic factors include a tumour size > 50 mm, local recurrence, positive surgical margins, and high-grade morphology (including triton tumour). Patients with NF1-associated MPNST appear to have a worse prognosis than patients with sporadic tumours {27158754}. Epithelioid MPNSTs are less aggressive {25602794}.

Essential:

Histopathologically consistent malignant spindle cell tumour in a patient with NF1a OR in a pre-existing neurofibroma

OR

Malignant spindle cell tumour associated with a peripheral nerve AND no more than focal/patchy S100/SOX10 expression AND no SS18::SSX (SSX1, SSX2, or SSX4) fusion gene present

OR

Malignant spindle cell tumour associated with a peripheral nerve AND evidence of PRC2 inactivation (molecularly or via loss of H3 p.K28me3 [K27me3] immunostaining)

OR

Tumour with features of ANNUBP in a patient with NF1, but with a mitotic count of ≥ 1.5–4.5 mitoses/mm2 (3–9 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.51 mm in diameter and 0.20 mm2 in area)

OR

Unresolved lesion with the methylation profile of MPNST

Desirable:

Loss of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3)

Loss of neurofibromin (NF1) expression

ANNUBP, atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential; HPF, high-power field; MPNST, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour; NF1, neurofibromatosis type 1.

aIn the setting of NF1, a diagnosis other than conventional MPNST demands strong molecular evidence (e.g. detection of a fusion gene pathognomonic of another tumour type).
