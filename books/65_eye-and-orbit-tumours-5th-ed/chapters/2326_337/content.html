2.3.2.6 Mucinous carcinoma

Definition

Mucinous carcinoma of the eyelid is a rare adnexal carcinoma with neoplastic cells floating in lakes of extracellular mucin.

ICD-O coding

8480/3 Mucinous carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH1S75 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Mucinous adenocarcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucinous adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The eyelid or brow is the most common location in the periorbital area, accounting for 30–45% of all cases of primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma {25627628;24452370;31702595}. Mucinous carcinoma affects the lower lid more than the upper {21158574} and can be bilateral in a small number of cases {10469115;26974419;21158574}.

Clinical features

Mucinous carcinoma is a slowly growing lesion and may be less aggressive in terms of its clinical behaviour compared with other subtypes of sweat gland carcinoma {26974419}. It has a variable clinical presentation. Lesions are often present for > 12 months before clinical presentation. Tumours can be firm or cystic; they can be tan, blue, red, or grey in colour; and they can assume a papillomatous, pedunculated, or fungating architecture, with a bumpy, smooth, or crusted surface {227576;21158574;34430175;20130716;}. Eyelid lesions are usually no more than 25 mm {} and can mimic a wide variety of benign and malignant eyelid tumours. These include chalazion, papilloma, myxoma, haemangioma, pyogenic granuloma, keratoacanthoma, basal cell carcinoma, squamous carcinoma, sebaceous carcinoma, melanoma, and Kaposi sarcoma {227576}.

Epidemiology

The age-adjusted incidence is 0.04 cases per 100 000 person-years. A systematic review of 159 published cases found that 122 (~77%) of the patients were White, 20 (~13%) were Asian, and 16 (~10%) were Black {24452370}. Mucinous carcinoma has a male predilection and presents in the fifth to seventh decades of life (mean: 61 years) {227576}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Mucinous carcinoma appears to result from progression from an in situ neoplasm. Inverse cell polarity, where tumour cells facing the stroma acquire apical secretory properties, is suggested to account for the accumulation of mucus. Although electron microscopic studies have suggested an eccrine lineage, about 50% of cases exhibit apocrine secretion, supporting apocrine differentiation. The gene ERBB2, encoding ERBB2 (HER2), was not found to be amplified in the few tumours in which it has been investigated {15897743}. Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma in situ is also a precursor lesion {9414195;16160476;32452870}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are soft nodules or masses, with a gelatinous or mucinous appearance. They are unencapsulated and often poorly circumscribed and are without lobulation.

Histopathology

Tumour cells are arranged in nests, strands, or individual units and float in lakes of extracellular mucin, sometimes separated by variably thin fibrous septa. The histopathological spectrum of primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma is wide, and analogous to that seen in its mammary counterparts, with at least two distinguishable patterns: a more-common pure tumour, in which extracellular mucin constitutes > 90%; and a rare mixed form, in which there is an invasive ductal component in addition to the mucinous element {15897743;1331212}. The floating neoplastic epithelial cells are usually relatively monomorphic, although some tumours may show substantial nuclear pleomorphism. Mitoses are generally scarce. A feature which should be looked for is an in situ component; it is detected in most cases (60–70%) and defines the neoplasm as primary cutaneous. In situ lesions usually constitute the minor moiety, and they include (using mammary pathology terminology) ductal hyperplasia, atypical ductal hyperplasia, and ductal carcinoma in situ (cribriform, micropapillary, papillary, and solid), occurring individually or (more rarely) in combination.

Immunohistochemistry

The in situ component can be highlighted by staining for myoepithelial markers (p63 and calponin). The epithelial cells express low-molecular-weight cytokeratin, EMA, CEA, E-cadherin, GATA3, and GCDFP-15. ER and PR are expressed in most cases. The typical CK7+/CK20− phenotype can sometimes be useful in the differential diagnosis with metastatic lesions. Neuroendocrine differentiation can be encountered in cutaneous mucinous carcinomas of both pure and mixed types, without a prominent solid component.

Differential diagnosis

Primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma must be differentiated from a metastatic mucinous carcinoma {18806501}. The presence of an in situ component qualifies the tumour as primary cutaneous; however, its absence does not necessarily imply an extracutaneous origin, because disrupted myoepithelial cells may no longer be identifiable during disease progression {15897743;15365364}. For such cases, a full clinical workup is needed to establish the origin.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a periorbital slow-growing lesion; nests and strands of epithelial cells floating in lakes of extracellular mucin.

Desirable: the presence of an in situ lesion, to confirm the lesion is a skin primary; in the absence of an in situ component, metastasis should be excluded by clinical investigation.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system for non-melanoma eyelid carcinoma applies.

Prognosis and prediction

Primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma has a propensity to recur at a rate of 26–40% {21158574;227576}. The recurrence rate is lower after surgery with intraoperative evaluation of surgical margins (i.e. either Mohs surgery or excision with frozen section control) {24452370;20837800}. Increased rates of recurrence and metastasis have been associated with White race, younger age at presentation, and large tumour size (> 15 mm) {24452370}. In a large study, 67% of patients had local disease, 10.5% had regional disease, and 5.8% had distant disease {31702595}. The mean overall survival is reported to be 11.4 years, with 5-year and 10-year overall survival at 85% and 78%, respectively {32890173}.
