10.1.8.2 Epithelioid sarcoma

Definition

Epithelioid sarcoma is a malignant soft tissue tumour with hybrid mesenchymal and epithelial morphological and immunophenotypic features.

ICD-O coding

8804/3 Epithelioid sarcoma

8804/3 Classic epithelioid sarcoma

8804/3 Proximal epithelioid sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH4F96 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Epithelioid sarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Classic epithelioid sarcoma (also known as conventional or distal epithelioid sarcoma); proximal epithelioid sarcoma

Localization

Epithelioid sarcomas have been reported in the extraconal soft tissues of the orbit.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a mass which may cause globe displacement depending on location.

Epidemiology

Epithelioid sarcomas account for < 1% of soft tissue sarcomas {19224301}, and < 1% of epithelioid sarcomas present in head and neck sites {33394376}. In the orbit, 6 primary cases and 1 metastatic case have been reported {7936567;21242638;30652038}. Patient age ranged from infancy to 87 years.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The fundamental oncogenic driver event in epithelioid sarcoma is loss of SMARCB1, leading to dysfunction of the BAF chromatin-remodelling complex, which alters cellular differentiation {32782366}. The background genome shows complex mutational changes without additional consistent driver events {26365879}.

Macroscopic appearance

Epithelioid sarcomas in sites deep to skin form multinodular masses tracking along fascial planes. Cut surfaces show solid grey areas with foci of necrosis and haemorrhage. Orbital cases have ranged from 20 to 45 mm.

Histopathology

Classic epithelioid sarcoma shows plump spindle cells with prominent eosinophilic cytoplasm blending into areas of larger more polygonal epithelioid cells, forming infiltrative, irregular sheets that frequently surround areas of necrosis in a pseudogranulomatous fashion {4014539}. Nuclei exhibit comparatively mild atypia, with vesicular chromatin and nucleoli. Mitotic activity is low. The proximal subtype displays sheet-like growth with only focal necrosis and is composed of more purely epithelioid carcinoma–like cells {11454997}. These have enlarged nuclei and prominent nucleoli, often with rhabdoid cytoplasm. Both subtypes have been reported in the orbit.

Immunohistochemistry

Epithelioid sarcoma cells coexpress epithelial (keratins, EMA) and mesenchymal (vimentin, CD34) markers. Most useful is loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) {19033866}, a feature relatively specific to this disease {33663878}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes granulomatous processes, carcinomas, and epithelioid vascular tumours.

Cytology

Cytology is not reported for ophthalmic cases.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Panel sequencing may detect mutations inactivating SMARCB1 {30652038}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid to spindled cytomorphology, with infiltrative growth; positivity for keratins, EMA, and vimentin; loss of expression of SMARCB1 (INI1).

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

Epithelioid sarcoma is an aggressive disease with a significant risk of local recurrence and nodal and systemic metastases. Five-year survival is about 70% but may be lower in orbital cases. Treatment generally requires exenteration supported by radiation therapy. Chemotherapy provides limited benefit but EZH2 inhibitors have shown activity in phase II studies {33035459;34278633}.
