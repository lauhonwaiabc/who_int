2.1.1.3 Epidermal inclusion cyst

Definition

Epidermal inclusion cyst (EIC) is a common, benign cyst lined by epidermis with a formed granular cell layer, containing flaky, basket-weave, or epidermal-type keratin.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

EK70.0Z Epidermoid cyst, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: epidermal cyst; epidermoid cyst.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cysts are most frequently found on the scalp and face, and they may occur along the eyelid margin in association with the cilia, along the brow, or elsewhere on the eyelid in association with the follicular units of vellus hairs.

Clinical features

EIC is typically a slow-growing, well-circumscribed, firm, and mobile dermal or subcutaneous mass. A central pore may be visible clinically {3341987}. Imaging has limited value in many eyelid lesions, but CT and MRI can confirm the diagnosis and delineate the extent of large subcutaneous epidermoid cysts {31516916}.

Epidemiology

EICs may occur in adolescents to elderly patients, without sex predilection. Occasional epidermoid cysts are congenital.

Etiology

EICs are believed to arise from the infundibular portion of the pilosebaceous unit or from traumatic implantation of surface epidermis into the dermis/subcutis {23330009}.

Pathogenesis

Pathogenesis is typically unknown but may result from follicular occlusion. Multiple epidermal cysts may occasionally be associated with Gardner or Muir–Torre syndromes {3341987}. Rarely, EICs have been associated with medications, including BRAF inhibitors (e.g. vemurafenib) {23406731}, cyclosporin A, and tacrolimus {32043723}.

Macroscopic appearance

EICs appears as well-circumscribed, white to yellow, oval to round masses filled with caseous or paste-like keratinous contents and occasionally hair shafts.

Histopathology

EICs are lined by stratified squamous epithelium identical to that of the surface epidermis and follicular infundibulum. A well-developed stratum granulosum is present, unlike the lining of pilar cyst. Contents consist of abundant flakes of laminated orthokeratin.

The differential diagnosis includes dilated pore of Winer, dermoid cyst, pilar (trichilemmal) cyst, vellus hair cyst, comedonal cyst, milium or hybrid cyst (mixed hidrocystoma and EIC; see section Ductal hidrocystoma).

Cytology

FNAB or impression cytology is not typically used to make the diagnosis of this entity at this site. When performed, aspirates show many nucleate and anucleate mature squamous cells and keratinaceous debris {18855902}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cyst in the dermis or subcutis; lined by epidermis with a granular cell layer; containing epidermal-type keratin; without an associated atypical squamous proliferation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision is typically curative. Rare cases of carcinoma (squamous or basal cell carcinoma) arising within an EIC have been reported {22460681;20736874}, and cysts may become secondarily infected {21158377}. Rupture without infection is common {23330009}.
