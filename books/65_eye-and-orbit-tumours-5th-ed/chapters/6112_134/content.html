6.1.1.2 Lacrimal gland oncocytoma

Definition

Oncocytoma of the lacrimal gland is a neoplasm composed of epithelial cells with a large amount of granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, rich in mitochondria.

ICD-O coding

8290/0 Oncocytoma

8290/3 Oncocytic carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH9Z86 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Oxyphilic adenoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: oncocytic adenoma; oxyphilic adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Periocular oncocytoma arises in the ducts of the main and the accessory lacrimal glands. Oncocytomas of accessory lacrimal glands can involve the caruncle and, less frequently, the palpebral and forniceal conjunctiva as well as the plica semilunaris {9088957}. In the lacrimal drainage apparatus, oncocytoma can involve the lacrimal sac, nasolacrimal duct, and lacrimal canaliculus (peripunctal) {17091220}.

Clinical features

Oncocytoma of the main lacrimal gland can cause fullness of the temporal aspect of the orbit, proptosis, nasal deviation of the eye, and eyelid ptosis. Oncocytomas are well-delineated masses causing compressive phenomena according to their location: enlargement of the caruncle, swelling of the conjunctiva or eyelid, and obstruction of the lacrimal drainage system.

Epidemiology

Oncocytomas are uncommon, accounting for < 1% of lacrimal gland neoplasms. They are tumours of adulthood with incidence peaking in the sixth to eighth decades of life. They tend to have slight female predominance {19843013}. Periocular oncocytic carcinomas are extremely rare; 1 case arising in the setting of neurofibromatosis has been described {20700068}, another 1 in plica semilunaris {3658316}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

While the precise mechanism of mitochondrial multiplication and its link to the proliferation of oncocytes remains unclear, a variety of deletions, SNPs, and gene rearrangements have been described in oncocytomas of the thyroid, kidney, salivary gland, and adrenal cortex. It appears that mutations in the mitochondrial rather than nuclear genes encoding for enzymes involved in oxidative phosphorylation are involved in the genesis of oncocytomas {24406625}. In a single lacrimal gland oncocytoma investigated, trisomy 8 and monosomy 22 were identified, together with an additional 33 sequence variants in mitochondrial DNA {28943925}. The large diversity of genetic changes found in oncocytomas in general precludes a unifying understanding of their pathogenesis.

Macroscopic appearance

Oncocytomas are well-circumscribed lobulated red-brown masses that are solid and/or cystic on the cut surface.

Histopathology

Oncocytomas are well-delineated neoplasms that display solid, papillary, or cystic architecture. They are composed of proliferating polygonal epithelial cells with a low N:C ratio, smoothly bordered nuclei, and large amounts of eosinophilic granular cytoplasm (highlighted by phosphotungstic acid–haematoxylin stain). The oncocytes are arranged in sheets, nests, or trabeculae, separated by thin fibrovascular bands. Their nuclei display vesicular chromatin, typically with a single conspicuous nucleolus. Ultrastructural analysis reveals a plethora of cytoplasmic mitochondria {16987774}.

Oncocytic carcinomas display nuclear atypia, mitoses, and invasion of neighbouring structures (soft tissue and bone) {23543280;21283034}.

Immunohistochemistry

Positivity with an antimitochondrial antibody (e.g. MU213-UC) may be helpful. Oncocytomas stain similarly to the lacrimal ducts: they are positive for CK4, CK5/6, CK7, CK8, CK17, CK18, S100, and EMA, but negative for CK20, CEA, and for markers of keratinizing epithelium {19843013}. Ki-67 shows a low nuclear labelling index (< 1%) and mitoses are exceptional {28943925}.

Cytology

Oncocytes are readily identified by their specific morphology in salivary gland FNA cytology preparations, but such a method has limited application in lacrimal neoplasms.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mass composed of oncocytic epithelial cells; eosinophilic granular cytoplasm.

Desirable: a well-delineated mass; variably solid or cystic growth; arising in lacrimal gland parenchyma.

Staging

Oncocytic carcinomas are staged according to Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM or the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Cancer staging manual, eighth edition, 2017.

Prognosis and prediction

No specific prognostic features have been delineated. Complete surgical excision is the treatment of choice for oncocytomas and oncocytic carcinomas of the lacrimal glands.
