10.1.6.3 Lymphangiectasia

Definition

Lymphangiectasia is a bulbar conjunctival dilation of developmentally normal lymphatic channels, produced as a result of degenerative, inflammatory, iatrogenic, or neoplastic etiologies, leading to either focal or diffuse lymphatic channel dilation.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A61.40 Vascular abnormalities of conjunctiva

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lymphangiectasia affects the bulbar conjunctiva.

Clinical features

Lymphangiectasia presents with ocular surface irritation {22019374}. Most lesions involve the temporal bulbar conjunctiva. A common comorbidity is pterygium {22019374}. If blood is present in the dilated lymphatics, it is termed lymphangiectasia haemorrhagica {9850253}.

Epidemiology

Lymphangiectasia accounts for 1% of all conjunctival lesions and 5–8% of all conjunctival cystic lesions {3301209;6629442;25892928}. In the largest series of conjunctival vascular lesions, lymphangiectasia accounts for 19% of 140 cases, with a male predilection {21788081;22019374}.

Etiology

Lymphangiectasia can be induced by IgE-mediated ocular surface allergies, degenerative lesions (especially pterygium and conjunctivochalasis), other inflammatory or local neoplastic diseases, organ transplant rejection, or scarring from ocular trauma, surgery, and radiotherapy {3301209;22019374;24590271;27994801}. There is also association with Fabry disease {28500230} and hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis {31074309;31949094}.

Pathogenesis

Lymphangiectasia is caused by the obstruction of normal lymphatic channels by local perilymphatic scarring or distal obstruction {22019374}. Lymphangiectasia haemorrhagica is caused by abnormal communications between conjunctival lymphatics and the deep conjunctival venous plexus {9850253}, with retrograde flow into the lymphatics.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Developmentally normal lymphatic channels are dilated and lined by flattened endothelial cells that stain positive with D2-40. The dilated channels may contain proteinaceous material and lymphocyte clusters. The surrounding stroma may be oedematous, fibrotic, and chronically inflamed {22019374;15810951}. The overlying conjunctival epithelium may show squamous metaplasia and/or keratinization {22019374}. In lymphangiectasia haemorrhagica conjunctivae, blood will be seen in the lumen {9850253}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dilation of normal lymphatic channels, lined by flattened endothelial cells.

Desirable: establishing a cause of the secondary lymphatic dilation (e.g. degenerative, inflammatory, iatrogenic, neoplastic); immunohistochemical expression of CD31 and CD34 and staining with D2-40 in the endothelial cells.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Asymptomatic lesions can be observed. Symptomatic cases can be locally excised {14516817;9333668;13422475;22019374} with a low risk of recurrence. Other modalities include cryotherapy, diathermy, and argon laser {20126499;5781037;9850253}.
