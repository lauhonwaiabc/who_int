2.4.0.1 Naevi of the eyelid

Definition

Junctional, compound, and dermal naevi are benign localized neoplastic proliferations of naevus melanocytic cells.

ICD-O coding

8740/0 Junctional naevus

8760/0 Compound naevus

8750/0 Dermal naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH1M79 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Junctional naevus, NOS

2F36.Y & XH27A6 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Compound naevus

2F36.Y & XH2MQ5 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Dermal naevus

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Junctional naevus; compound naevus; dermal naevus

Localization

Eyelid and periorbital skin

Clinical features

Depending on the subtype, most are well circumscribed, sharply demarcated, uniformly pigmented, light brown to dark brown or black in colour, and < 5 mm in diameter. The lesions are not ulcerated, unless they are traumatized.

Epidemiology

Most common naevi are acquired during childhood and reach maximum number in adolescence, declining in number thereafter owing to a process of senescence {16645597}. Junctional, compound, and dermal naevi are the most common in the eyelid.

Etiology

The number of naevi in various populations depends on genetic factors and exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation, in particular sunburn during childhood {20647408}.

Pathogenesis

Most melanocytic naevi are driven by activating the MAPK pathway. After initial clonal proliferation, melanocytes enter a stable cell-cycle arrest. BRAF oncogene mutation, commonly BRAF p.V600E, is found in > 60% of cases. A minority, especially congenital naevi, are caused by NRAS mutations whereas others have gene fusions {27125352}. BRAF and NRAS mutations are mutually exclusive {27307047;26300491;12447372}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

In a junctional naevus, nests are present by definition at the base of the epidermis, and often admixed with single cells. In a compound naevus, nests are also present in the dermis. In a dermal naevus, there is no junctional component. The lesions are sharply laterally demarcated and symmetrical. In compound and dermal naevi, melanocytes and nests usually decrease in size towards depth (type A cells with more cytoplasm and type B lymphocyte-like cells with maturation). Single, but not atypical, and upper dermal mitoses can be found.

Immunohistochemistry

As CDKN2A (P16) is often mutated in melanomas, but highly uncommon in naevi, p16 immunohistochemistry can be helpful to differentiate naevi from melanoma. In addition, positivity for PRAME is seen rarely in naevi {16331607;30045064}.

Differential diagnosis

Other types of naevi are seen more rarely and are covered in greater depth in the Skin tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Skin Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 12). https://publications.iarc.who.int/650.]]. Around the eye, congenital naevi, naevus of Ota, and Spitz naevi (see section Spitz naevus) may be encountered. Blue naevus is intradermal and shows similar features to naevi in the conjunctiva (see section Blue naevus of the conjunctiva), as do WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus) (see section WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus)) and combined naevus (see section Combined naevus).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Next-generation sequencing can be helpful in cases of uncertain malignancy. Identifying the driver mutation without additional genetic alterations underlines a benign diagnosis of naevus.

Molecular testing is recommended to rule out malignancy in the diagnosis of spitzoid neoplasms {34757982} (see section Spitz naevus). WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus) (see section WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus)) usually has BRAF mutation combined with CTNNB1 (β-catenin) mutation or loss of APC {28935960}, and naevus of Ota commonly has GNAQ mutation. BAP1-inactivated naevus usually has BRAF mutation combined with BAP1 loss or mutation, although combination with NRAS mutation can also occur.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a clinically small (< 5–6 mm), well-circumscribed, symmetrical, uniformly coloured lesion; histologically composed of naevus cells arranged in orderly junctional nests and/or in dermal nests, cords, and individual cell patterns with maturation and general absence of architectural and cytological abnormalities.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Incompletely excised naevi may recur. Owing to disorganization and pagetoid spread over the scar, they are melanoma simulators {19270643}. Individuals with an increased total number of naevi and large congenital naevi are at increased risk of melanoma. About one third of melanomas arise in compound naevi. However, the risk of progression of naevi is very low {15617989;10338338;26559571;27125352}.
