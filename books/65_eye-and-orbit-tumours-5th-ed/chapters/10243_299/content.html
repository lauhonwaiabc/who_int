10.2.4.3 Angiosarcoma of bone

Definition

Angiosarcoma of bone (AS-B) is a highly aggressive sarcoma with endothelial differentiation. It is extremely rare in the orbit.

ICD-O coding

9120/3 Angiosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B56.Y & XH6264 Angiosarcoma, other specified primary site & Haemangiosarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The orbit and adjacent cranial structures can be affected, with extension into the cranium, organs, and external soft tissue {9932897}. AS-B can be multifocal {34742485}.

Clinical features

AS-B usually presents as a rapidly growing mass with symptoms depending on the site, including visual disorders (e.g. diplopia), exophthalmia, chemosis, ptosis, and pain/headache {9932897}.

Radiographically, AS-B appears lytic and aggressive with poorly defined borders and frequent cortical destruction with extraosseous spread {34742485}.

Epidemiology

AS-B is rare (< 1% of malignant bone tumours) with the orbit one of the least frequent sites {34742485;9932897}. Primary AS from other sites exceptionally metastasizes to the orbit {21165236}. AS-B typically affects elderly people {34742485}.

Etiology

The etiology is mostly unknown, but radiation therapy can be causative {17998731;9932897}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is complex with karyotypic and genetic heterogeneity with overlap with AS at other sites. MYC amplification is not necessarily associated with previous radiation therapy {34742485;25231439}.

Macroscopic appearance

AS-B is typically haemorrhagic, friable, and destructive, with poorly defined borders and commonly extraosseous extension {34742485}.

Histopathology

Primary AS-Bs usually show epithelioid morphology with a diffuse and permeative growth pattern. Spindle cell tumours are less frequent, and in this case metastasis from elsewhere needs to be ruled out {34742485;21323951}.

Appearances range from relatively well to poorly formed vascular channels with variable solid growth and heterogeneity within a single neoplasm. Atypical (mainly epithelioid) endothelial cells show enlarged often hyperchromatic nuclei and frequently distinct nucleoli. Mitoses, haemorrhage, and necrosis are more frequent in less-differentiated tumours.

Immunohistochemistry

AS expresses CD31, ERG, and FLI1. There is often variable cytokeratin expression in tumours with epithelioid morphology, which can lead to confusion with metastatic carcinoma {34742485;31950476;21323951}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes metastatic carcinoma, epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, pseudomyogenic (epithelioid sarcoma–like) haemangioendothelioma, epithelioid haemangioma, Kaposi sarcoma, and melanoma {34742485}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone as primary site; microscopically, at least focally, vasoformative appearance and/or immunohistochemical expression of endothelial markers; prominent nuclear atypia and readily observed mitoses.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM8 staging system for soft tissue sarcoma of the trunk and extremities is used.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is very poor, and site and stage dependent {34742485}, with general 5-year overall survival for skeletal AS < 40%. Cases may be managed by surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or a combination thereof {34742485}.
