2.3.1.4 Pilomatricoma

Definition

Pilomatricoma is a benign cutaneous adnexal neoplasm that shows differentiation towards matrical cells and the hair cortex.

ICD-O coding

8110/0 Pilomatricoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH9E37 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Pilomatricoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: pilomatrixoma.

Not recommended: calcifying epithelioma of Malherbe.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pilomatricoma mostly involves the upper eyelids and medial eyebrow area {10355837;24801260;18929763;9704827;21769234;13700704}.

Clinical features

Pilomatricoma usually presents as a solitary, well-defined, painless, slowly growing, subcutaneous reddish-blue mass, which is firm and freely mobile, and has a gritty sensation due to calcification {7494166;18929763}. It can show rapid growth due to inflammation {18929763;31358136}. It is usually < 10 mm in diameter. Calcification may result in the tent sign due to angulation of the tumour {14663221;24838222}. Multiple lesions have been described {9704827;14663221;24838222}.

High-resolution MRI allows the identification of most eyelid structures and can assess the precise location of pilomatricomas inside the eyelid {32178233}. Furthermore, MRI aids in the differential diagnosis of eyelid masses, and perfusion-weighted imaging helps in differentiating a pilomatricoma from a malignant eyelid mass. Although it is a benign lesion, a low apparent diffusion coefficient can be seen, probably due to the presence of calcifications.

Epidemiology

The M:F ratio in White people is 2:3 {18929763}. It has a bimodal presentation, with one peak in children and young adults (40% in the first decade of life and 20% in the second decade of life) and another peak between 50 and 65 years of age {18929763;9704827;11829174}.

Etiology

Most are sporadic. Familial and multiple cases are linked to genetic conditions such as myotonic muscular dystrophy, Gardner syndrome, basal cell naevus syndrome, and xeroderma pigmentosum {14663221;5039107}. Associations with blunt trauma, hypercalcaemia, and sarcoidosis {29763996;18929763} are reported.

Pathogenesis

Pathogenesis has been linked to disruption of the hair growth anagen phase {18929763} and to the CTNNB1 gene mutation {11472567}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions appear as pink to tan-coloured well-circumscribed firm nodules that may be pseudoencapsulated {32304502;31358136}.

Histopathology

The tumour comprises one or several dermal nodules. The periphery of the nodules shows mitotically active basaloid cells, with scant cytoplasm. An intermediate zone shows smaller pyknotic basophilic nuclei with a paler cytoplasm and the central area shows shadow cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm and non-staining empty nuclei centrally {28457306;32304502}. Shadow cells may calcify over time {18929763}. Focal granulomatous inflammation, sheets of keratin-like material, and foreign body–type giant cells are also common {18929763;14048805;34119944}.

Cytology

FNAB produces smears with recognizable clusters of basaloid cells and foreign body–type multinucleated cells {29370510;30498290}. Ghost cells can be difficult to find {29370510}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: the presence of basaloid cells at the periphery, an intermediate zone with smaller pyknotic basophilic nuclei, and eosinophilic shadow (ghost) cells centrally.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence has been reported in 1.4% of cases in one series within an average time of 1.6 years {14663221}. Malignant transformation into pilomatrix carcinoma is rare {7995429}.
