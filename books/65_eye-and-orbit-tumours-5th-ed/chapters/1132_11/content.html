1.1.3.2 Epibulbar osseous choristoma

Definition

Epibulbar osseous choristoma is a choristoma composed of mature bone.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.06 Epibulbar choristoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: episcleral osseous choristoma.

Not recommended: osteoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Epibulbar osseous choristomas are typically unilateral and located in the superotemporal conjunctiva {22496017;29589231;22516954;13871021}. They can also occur temporally, and rarely they occur in the inferotemporal conjunctiva {13871021}.

Clinical features

Epibulbar osseous choristoma presents as a yellow, orange, pink, or white subconjunctival mass that may be multinodular and can be freely mobile or adherent to the sclera or muscles. The mass usually extends from the equator to the superotemporal fornix. Epibulbar osseous choristomas are firm, and calcifications can be seen using CT {22496017}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of epibulbar osseous choristoma is unknown. It is present from birth and frequently presents in patients aged < 20 years {22496017;12358295}, but it can also be discovered later in life {18569837}. There appears to be a slight female predilection {13871021;12358295}. Most of the reported cases have been in White people. However, a few cases have been reported in African-American patients {22516954;13871021}.

Etiology

Epibulbar osseous choristoma is by definition a benign congenital lesion of histologically mature bone in an abnormal location.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Firm whitish-yellowish mass

Histopathology

Epibulbar osseous choristomas are composed of compact lamellar bone with normal osteocytes. Haversian canals can be found. Haematopoietic bone marrow is usually absent but has been reported rarely {12358295}. The bone is surrounded by periosteum and an internal osteogenic layer {13871021}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: compact mature lamellar bone with normal osteocytes present at an episcleral location.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Epibulbar osseous choristomas are benign, and malignant transformation has not been described. In some exceptional cases, slight enlargement has been reported {22496017;12358295}.
