7.2.2.4 Other salivary gland-type carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system

Definition

Salivary gland–type carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system are a family of primary epithelial adenocarcinomas that morphologically resemble their salivary gland counterparts. They include adenoid cystic carcinoma (AdCC), duct adenocarcinoma (DA), oncocytic carcinoma, epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma (EMC), carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma (CEPA), and sebaceous carcinoma.

Related terminology

Not recommended: for ductal adenocarcinoma: salivary duct carcinoma; for epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma: adenomyoepithelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours arise from stromal seromucinous glands predominantly in the lacrimal sac {24040460}.

Clinical features

Most tumours present with unilateral tearing (74–100%) and a mass in the lacrimal fossa or swelling in the medial canthus {32304675;32506982;28399337}.

Features of concern for malignancy are a palpable, firm, non-compressible, immobile mass above the medial canthal tendon; chronic dacryocystitis which irrigates or partially irrigates; and sanguineous reflux on irrigation {28399337}.

Imaging modalities of the orbit and paranasal sinuses include contrast-enhanced thin-sectioned CT for tumour extent, MRI with contrast to differentiate between a solid mass and cystic or inflammatory disease, and dacryocystography to demonstrate a filling defect and delayed draining of contrast medium {32304675}.

Epidemiology

Salivary gland–type carcinomas affect middle-aged and elderly individuals, with a median age at diagnosis of 57.5 years {32304675}. There is a slight male predominance in all tumour types except for AdCC which is largely (80%) seen in women {32304675}.

Etiology

Chronic inflammation (as many patients present with or have been treated for presumed dacryocystitis) and previous probing or surgery of the lacrimal drainage system are thought to be risk factors for tumour development {32304675}. The occurrence of sebaceous carcinoma in the lacrimal sac is thought to arise from ectopic sebaceous glands within the stroma or sebaceous gland metaplasia of the lacrimal sac epithelium {30921057}.

Pathogenesis

Adenocarcinoma may arise from a pleomorphic adenoma and recurrent oncocytoma may undergo malignant transformation {32304675}. Activation of mTOR pathway proteins has been reported in DA {29503943;25699235}.

Macroscopic appearance

DA has been described as a polypoidal mass {18569829}, AdCC as a gelatinous mass {27982216}, and EMC as a grey-tan homogeneous mass {32341837}.

Histopathology

DA consists of polygonal tumour cells with granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, pleomorphic and vesicular nuclei arranged in ductular structures of varying patterns (glandular, cribriform, and solid), and frequent central comedonecrosis. Extracellular mucin may be present {31837385}. Tumour cells express cytokeratins, AR, GCDFP, and ERBB2 {31837385}.

AdCC, EMC, CEPA, and sebaceous carcinoma show the same morphological features as their salivary gland counterparts, which have been detailed elsewhere (see section Tumours of the lacrimal gland).

Oncocytic carcinoma displays large polyhedral cells with nuclear atypia and granular eosinophilic cytoplasm arranged in an infiltrative pseudoglandular pattern. Evidence of the increased mitochondrial content of the tumour cells is achieved with positive phosphotungstic acid–haematoxylin cytoplasmic staining and/or immunohistochemical expression of antimitochondrial antibodies {32304675;23543280;17237701}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of AdCC, EMC, CEPA, and sebaceous carcinoma is the same as for their salivary gland counterparts (see section Tumours of the lacrimal gland).

DA is distinguished from oncocytic carcinoma by the absence of excessive and abnormal mitochondria in the cytoplasm of the tumour cells, demonstrated by immunohistochemistry or electron microscopy.

Sebaceous carcinoma must be distinguished from direct extension from the conjunctiva or caruncle.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of MYB or MYBL1 rearrangements or gene fusions may be helpful for the diagnosis of AdCC.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See individual tumour types in section Tumours of the lacrimal gland.

Staging

While there is currently no separate TNM classification for carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system, the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system for lacrimal gland tumours can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Wide surgical excision (including exenteration) and/or radiotherapy are the preferred courses of management for these carcinomas {32304675;32506982}. AR and ERBB2 are potential therapeutic targets for salivary-type ductal adenocarcinomas {32304675;29503943}. Large tumour size and positive lymph node status correlate with a decreased overall and disease-free survival {32304675}. The 5-year overall survival is reported to range from 61% to 88% {32304675}.
