7.2.2.5 Lymphoepithelial carcinoma

Definition

Lymphoepithelial carcinoma of the lacrimal drainage system is a poorly differentiated / undifferentiated squamous cell carcinoma accompanied by reactive lymphoplasmacytic infiltration strongly associated with EBV.

ICD-O coding

8082/3 Lymphoepithelial carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D03.2 & XH1E40 Squamous cell carcinoma of the lacrimal apparatus & Lymphoepithelial carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma; non-keratinizing undifferentiated nasopharyngeal-type carcinoma; undifferentiated carcinoma with lymphocytic stroma; lymphoepithelioma

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

At this anatomical site, lymphoepithelial carcinomas present in the lacrimal sac and nasolacrimal duct.

Clinical features

The duration of symptoms is 1–24 months. Epiphora and swelling over the lacrimal sac are common, and a hard, non-tender, immobile mass above the medial tendon is characteristic {25902392;21957907}.

Epidemiology

Lymphoepithelial carcinoma is rare in the lacrimal drainage system, with only 12 reported cases {27588283}, in women (10 cases) and men (2 cases) aged 20–82 years; 10 of these patients were Asian, 1 was African-American, and 1 was White {27588283}.

Etiology

Nearly all lymphoepithelial carcinoma cases are strongly associated with EBV infection; 78% of cases in the lacrimal sac were found to be EBV-positive {25719371;27588283}.

Pathogenesis

It is presumed that EBV plays an oncogenic role in these tumours.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically, the tumour is polypoid, whitish, and firm.

Histopathology

Microscopically, the tumour is characterized by polygonal, undifferentiated malignant epithelial cells occurring in sheets, in clusters, and as isolated cells, mixed with an intense lymphoid infiltrate. The malignant cells have round vesicular nuclei and a single large central nucleolus. The background lymphoid infiltrate is composed of a mixture of B lymphocytes, T lymphocytes, plasma cells, and macrophages. Immunohistochemically, the epithelial tumour cells are positive for CK5/6, CK14, CK19, EMA, and p63 {25902392;27588283}. Cytokeratin staining can be focal {25719371}. The lymphoid infiltrate is polyclonal.

Cytology

There are no reports in lacrimal sac lymphoepithelial carcinoma, but the cytological findings of polygonal and spindled cells with prominent nucleoli and a heterogeneous background population of lymphoid cells are described in salivary gland tumours {34975253}. These findings are identical to those in metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma {34975253}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) may be helpful in establishing the presence of EBV infection.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pleomorphic epithelial cells with a heterogeneous background population of lymphocytes and plasma cells.

Desirable: EBV positivity.

Staging

While there is currently no separate TNM classification for carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system, the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for lacrimal gland tumours can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

The reported disease-free survival is 15–33 months. Three patients developed locoregional relapses or metastases despite treatment. Lymphoepithelial carcinoma can be life-threatening if diagnosed late, with a mortality rate of about 50% {27588283}.
