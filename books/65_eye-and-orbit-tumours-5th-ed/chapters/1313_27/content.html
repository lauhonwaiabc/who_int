1.3.1.3 Inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevus

Definition

Inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevus (IJCN) is a compound naevus of childhood and adolescence, associated with prominent inflammation.

ICD-O coding

8770/0 Inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH8WP0 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Inflamed juvenile conjunctival naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: inflamed conjunctival naevus of puberty and young adulthood; inflammatory juvenile compound conjunctival naevus.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most IJCNs occur in the juxtalimbal zone of the bulbar conjunctiva {11801498}.

Clinical features

IJCN presents with a well-circumscribed nodule, either amelanotic or pale flesh colour with areas of congested vessels. It may be pinkish or slightly and focally pigmented. The variation in the degree of pigmentation usually happens during growth of the IJCN and presents as an orange/red nodule secondary to both the vascularization and increase in pigmentation. One key finding is that the IJCN contains cystic structures and is freely movable, except when located in the limbus. A clinical characteristic of IJCN is the excessive concern it causes because of the rapid growth, vascularization, and congestion of the vessels {18985522;18300811;11801498;33603334;29250649}.

Epidemiology

The mean patient age at presentation with IJCN is 13 years. It is generally associated with puberty and arises most frequently in boys. Patients with IJCN may also have asthma, atopic dermatitis, bilateral papillary conjunctivitis, and vernal conjunctivitis {12036989;18985522;18300811;11801498;33603334;21128776;25171709}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. There is no known genetic susceptibility, including cutaneous dysplastic naevus or melanoma syndromes {18300811;11801498}. However, 75% of patients have a history of allergic disease with papillary conjunctivitis and many eosinophils in the naevus-accompanying inflammatory infiltrate, leading to the presumption that IJCN occurs in those susceptible to allergy {11801498}.

Pathogenesis

It is presumed that IJCN carries the same driver mutations as conventional conjunctival naevi, namely, mutually exclusive NRAS or BRAF mutations. This has been demonstrated in one of four studied IJCNs (BRAF c.1799T>A) {16123397}. Of 12 other juvenile conjunctival naevi, 4 showed the same canonical BRAF mutation, and the remainder displayed NRAS mutations; whether or not an associated inflammatory infiltrate was present in the latter group of juvenile naevi was not specified {29332123}.

IJCN is a compound naevus with predominance of the epithelial naevus component, and it is associated with eosinophilic and/or chronic inflammation. Expression of IL-17 in inflammatory cells of the naevus and VEGF in the vascular endothelium has been found in IJCN suggesting that these play a role in the pathogenesis and that they may have a potential role in treatment. The inflammatory reaction appears to be more stable; however, if the inflammation is composed predominantly of eosinophils, then the variation in size may be attributed to the inflammatory component. The eosinophilic infiltrate is more common in children with atopic and allergic conditions. Otherwise, a direct immune response induced by the naevus may be the cause of the inflammation {12036989;25171709;22139382;26615952}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, the lesions can be melanotic or amelanotic and flat or raised nodules {25171709}.

Histopathology

IJCN shares features of a conjunctival compound naevus, including the presence of cystic and solid epithelial nests in most of them. The cystic component often contains goblet cells, which give a positive PAS reaction {25171709}. Prominent lymphocytic inflammation is characteristic. Well-defined germinal centres may be present. Plasma cells and eosinophils are seen in about three quarters of IJCNs. Periods of rapid growth represent inflammatory infiltration and cystic enlargement by mucin production and accumulation {11801498;22139382;2657539;25171709}. IJCN shows apparent disordered growth such as prominent confluent junctional nests and lack of, or incomplete, maturation or paradoxical reverse maturation in which the nuclear and cytoplasmic size of naevus cells is greater in the subepithelial component than in the junctional region. This may lead to mistaken interpretation as a premalignant or malignant melanocytic lesion. The junctional component often extends horizontally beyond the subepithelial one {18985522;18300811;21128776;29250649}. Mitotic figures are rare, and the Ki-67 index is low. IJCN is positive for HMB45 in the superficial layer only. Tumoural cells can show immunopositivity for melan-A and S100 {29250649}. Immunohistochemistry for CD45 (LCA), CD3, and CD20 shows a mixed population of B cells and T cells {18985522;18300811;25171709}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: childhood or adolescent presentation; features of a conjunctival compound naevus mixed with inflammatory cells (lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils), and occasional lymphoid follicles.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

IJCN recurs exceptionally and is rarely the source of conjunctival melanoma {11801498;18300811}.
