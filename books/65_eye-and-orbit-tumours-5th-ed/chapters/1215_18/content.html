1.2.1.5 Hereditary benign intraepithelial dyskeratosis

Definition

Hereditary benign intraepithelial dyskeratosis (HBID) is a non-neoplastic hyperkeratotic and dyskeratotic epithelial disorder of the conjunctiva and oral mucosa.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

127600 Hereditary benign intraepithelial dyskeratosis; HBID

ICD-11 coding

DA02.0 Genetic or developmental disorders involving lips or oral mucosa

Related terminology

Not recommended: Witkop–von Sallmann syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

HBID presents with hyperkeratotic limbal conjunctival and/or oral mucosal lesions.

Clinical features

HBID appears as raised, leukoplakic, vascularized, and usually inflamed limbal conjunctival plaques that are frequently bilateral and change shape over time. The severity ranges from mild (erythematous) to moderate (leukoplakia limited to the conjunctiva) to severe (corneal involvement, which degrades vision because of surface irregularities and focal opacification) {24555743;21993456}. Seasonal variability exists, with exacerbation during spring and summer, followed by partial remission in autumn {18684035}.

Epidemiology

HBID is a rare condition that is almost exclusively encountered in the Indigenous Haliwa-Saponi tribe (short for Halifax and Warren counties, their ancestral origin), native to the US state of North Carolina. There have also been isolated reports of cases in families with no relation to this tribe {24555743}.

Etiology

HBID is an autosomal dominant disease with high penetrance.

Pathogenesis

The HBID gene has been mapped to chromosome 4 (4q35), where copy-number duplication has been described {11170897;26015711}. No protein-coding genes have been identified at this locus {24555743}. NLRP1 mutations have not been identified in 1 evaluated patient with HBID {24555743}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

The lesions are sharply demarcated from the surrounding conjunctiva. They show marked acanthosis with substantial multilayered parakeratosis displaying copious cytoplasmic dyskeratosis and pyknotic nuclei. Dyskeratotic cells characteristically are present within the squamous epithelium surrounded by the morphologically non-dysplastic squamous cells. No intervening granular layer is seen. Accompanying features include chronic inflammation and capillary telangiectasia in the substantia propria. The basement membrane remains intact {11568764}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes conjunctival squamous intraepithelial neoplasia and squamous cell carcinoma in situ. Bilateral presentation, lack of nuclear pleomorphism, retention of the N:C ratio, and lack of atypical mitoses distinguish HBID from squamous dysplastic lesions. Patients with multiple self-healing palmoplantar carcinoma may develop conjunctival and corneal epithelial lesions histopathologically similar to HBID {25050600;27662089}. The skin lesions (keratoacanthomas) and germline NLRP1 gene mutations help establish the diagnosis of multiple self-healing palmoplantar carcinoma {23349227}.

Cytology

Conjunctival brushings of patients with HBID exhibit rounded squamous epithelial cells with dense homogeneous orange cytoplasm and hyperchromatic, pyknotic, or crenated nuclei {18684035}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic analysis is not usually necessary as the clinical diagnosis is sufficient.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sharply demarcated acanthotic limbal conjunctival lesions with prominent intraepithelial dyskeratosis; prominent inflammation in substantia propria; absence of epithelial dysplasia.

Desirable: bilateral location; family history suggestive of autosomal dominant mode of inheritance; similar-appearing lesions in oral mucosa.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

While mostly self-limiting, some conjunctival lesions cause irritation and disrupt the tear film. Corneal involvement can substantially degrade vision {36219214}. There is limited experience in treating vision-compromising HBID {24555743;21993456}.
