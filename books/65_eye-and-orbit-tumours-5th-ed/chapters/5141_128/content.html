5.1.4.1 Melanocytoma of the optic disc and optic nerve

Definition

Melanocytoma is a benign, densely pigmented melanocytic tumour that is classically located in the optic disc and anterior portion of the optic nerve but can be found anywhere in the uveal tract.

ICD-O coding

8726/0 Melanocytoma of the optic disc and optic nerve

ICD-11 coding

2A02.3 & XH2RY7 Benign neoplasm of cranial nerves & Meningeal melanocytoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: magnocellular naevus; hyperpigmented magnocellular naevus.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Melanocytoma is usually unilateral and found on or adjacent to the optic disc. Bilateral involvement is more common in children {12834694;16500211}. Multiple tumours in the same eye are recognized {31755427}. Lesions average 1–2 disc diameters {24506313;16500211;15350331}. Rare giant examples may extend as far as the ora serrata {21624657;33123528}.

Clinical features

Melanocytoma is classically asymptomatic, with visual symptoms occurring in 24% of patients and pupillary defects in < 10%. The visual field is abnormal in 90% of patients, with enlargement of the blind spot ranging from minimal to marked. Vision loss can result from central retinal vein obstruction and/or spontaneous tumour necrosis and is not necessarily related to malignant transformation {1303415;16500211;15350331;31755427}. Fluorescein angiography shows blocked fluorescence rather than alternating hyperfluorescence and hypofluorescence as seen in melanoma {16500211}. Optical coherence tomography is characterized by a gradual transition from normal retina into nodular tumour. Optically empty internal details and a bright anterior border facilitate the distinction from melanoma {18327136;33463586}.

Epidemiology

The incidence is unknown and there is no race predilection. Optic nerve melanocytoma is proportionally more common than uveal melanoma in dark-skinned individuals. There is a slight female predilection, with an M:F ratio of approximately 1:1.5. The mean age at diagnosis is 50 years, with a range from infancy to 91 years {15350331;17621182}.

Etiology

Melanocytoma is considered to be of congenital origin.

Pathogenesis

Melanocytomas of the optic nerve may be related to other melanocytic lesions of the uveal tract and CNS. They are hypothesized to originate from extraepithelial melanocytes derived from the cranial neural crest {24460190}. It should be noted that in the Skin tumours volume, the term “melanocytoma” is used to delineate a completely different type of lesions at that site.

Few intraocular melanocytomas have been subject to genetic analysis. In a targeted sequencing study that included 5 melanocytomas of the optic nerve, a mutation of GNAQ was present in 1 case {27239460}. GNAQ and GNA11 encode alpha subunits of the Gq class and are frequently mutated in uveal melanoma and melanocytomas of the CNS. Interestingly, 2 cases that had a BAP1 sequence variant plus monosomy 3 (one of which was a case with GNAQ mutation) progressed to melanoma. In another study, gene expression profiling of three optic nerve melanocytomas revealed that all 3 cases showed a class 1A genetic signature, which is associated with a low risk of metastatic disease in uveal melanoma {25984377}.

Macroscopic appearance

Typical features include a well-demarcated, flat or elevated, dark-brown to black lesion.

Histopathology

Since the tumours are densely pigmented, bleaching is required in order to identify the round to oval or polyhedral cells with small nuclei, abundant cytoplasm, and generally inconspicuous nucleoli {10768345;16500211;17621182;14271285}. Mitoses are exceptional; however, necrosis is frequent, often accompanied by pigment-laden macrophages and lymphocytic inflammation. Extensive spontaneous tumour necrosis may occur in some cases. Melanocytic markers such as HMB45, melan-A (MART1), and S100 are variably positive {21624657;16500211;15350331;14271285}.

Cytology

Smear preparations reveal cohesive nests of elongated to polygonal, intensely pigmented cells with small, uniform nuclei and inconspicuous nucleoli. Macrophages containing melanin may be present {18323281}. FNAB can assist with documentation of malignant transformation to melanoma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: circumscribed, highly pigmented melanocytic tumour of the optic disc/nerve; limited cytological atypia and (almost) no mitoses; dense pigmentation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Progressive growth with local invasion is common, with minor enlargement seen in as many as one third of all patients by 10 years after diagnosis. Malignant transformation is reported in 1–2% of cases, usually within 5 years of the initial diagnosis {16500211;15350331}; however, malignant transformation has been documented as late as 33 years after diagnosis {6525581;23044959}.
