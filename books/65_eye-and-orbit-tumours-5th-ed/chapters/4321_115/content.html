4.3.2.1 Adenoma and adenocarcinoma of the ciliary body

Definition

Ciliary body adenoma and adenocarcinoma are benign and malignant neoplasms, respectively, that arise from the pigmented or non-pigmented ciliary epithelium.

ICD-O coding

8140/0 Ciliary body adenoma

8140/3 Ciliary body adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.2 & XH3DV3 Benign neoplasm of ciliary body & Adenoma, NOS

2D06.0 Adenocarcinoma of ciliary epithelium

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adenoma and adenocarcinoma both arise from the ciliary body epithelium, which has a non-pigmented inner and a pigmented outer layer. The adenocarcinomas can infiltrate the iris and anterior chamber angle and, eventually, the sclera anteriorly to form an extrascleral epibulbar mass {27085283;2197590;5661889;9917789;2461084}, or posteriorly through scleral channels.

Clinical features

Adenomas and adenocarcinomas of the ciliary body are often asymptomatic, but they may be associated with visual disturbances because of local inflammation, secondary glaucoma from angle invasion, or lens subluxation and sectoral cataract from lens touch. They can protrude into the posterior chamber and anteriorly to involve the iris and anterior chamber {9003334;11735805}.

Non-pigmented adenomas and adenocarcinomas are greyish-white, yellowish, or pinkish, vascularized, multilobular masses that transmit light on transillumination. Pigmented adenomas and adenocarcinomas appear dark grey to black-brown, and they block light on transillumination.

Ultrasound biomicroscopy of both non-pigmented and pigmented adenomas and adenocarcinomas reveals a solid mass with medium to high internal reflectivity and abrupt borders. The median diameter of adenomas is about 5 mm. On MRI, non-pigmented adenoma and adenocarcinoma is T1 isointense and T2 isointense to hyperintense relative to the lacrimal gland tissue, whereas pigmented adenomas and adenocarcinomas are T1 hyperintense and T2 hypointense relative to brain tissue {27130243;20044507}.

Epidemiology

Adenoma and adenocarcinoma are rare tumours of the ciliary body. Adenomas generally occur in middle-aged individuals, with no sex predilection {27130243}. The incidence of adenocarcinomas is highest in the fifth decade of life, with no sex predilection. The age at diagnosis tends to be slightly younger than that of choroidal melanoma. Pleomorphic adenocarcinoma usually affects patients in their eighth decade of life. Most patients are White. Few adenomas and adenocarcinomas have been reported in African-American {2197590;1919972} or Asian {18038890;25015166;33606097} patients or in children {6703952}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Several cases are reported in phthisical eyes several years after blindness from injury or chronic inflammation {25015166;6995386;5661889;9917789;30976584}.

Pathogenesis

Adenomas of the ciliary body derive from the ciliary epithelium that is a SOX10-negative region of the optic cup {25015166;29059311}. Adenocarcinomas are presumed to arise de novo, from longstanding reactive hyperplasia, or from a prior adenoma {25545484}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions appear as a pigmented, greyish-white, or yellowish mass behind the lens in the ciliary body.

Histopathology

Non-pigmented and pigmented adenomas: These are composed of nests, cords, and tubules of polygonal epithelial cells that have round to oval nuclei and small nucleoli, with minimal cytological atypia and very rare mitotic figures {25605545;9003334;27130243;10326955}. Some adenomas can show spindle-cell morphology {9003334}. These tumours can exhibit solid and papillary patterns, with delicate fibrous septa and basement membrane. Non-pigmented adenomas show variable acid mucopolysaccharide matrix {25605545;9003334}. Pigmented adenomas contain large spherical melanin granules, and the neoplastic cells are often vacuolated, the latter containing a hyaluronidase-resistant acid mucopolysaccharide {27130243;10326955}. Pigmented adenomas can show pigment dispersion into the anterior chamber and infiltration of the trabecular meshwork {12642323}. This is not an indication of malignancy as long as the cytology remains bland.

Both non-pigmented and pigmented adenomas show consistent strong nuclear expression of PAX8 {33002562}, vimentin, and S100, and variable expression of cytokeratins, most consistently CK8 and CK18 {19346082;25605545;9003334;27130243;10326955;1701528}. In pigmented adenomas, variable immunoreactivity for the melanocytic marker melan-A has been documented {19346082}.

Non-pigmented and pigmented adenocarcinomas: These are composed of strands, cords, and duct-like structures of cuboidal to epithelioid cells that are cytologically atypical with enlarged and irregular nuclei containing prominent nucleoli. The cells are frequently surrounded by a meshwork of basement membrane material that is PAS-positive. Papillary, solid, and pleomorphic growth patterns have been described {5525160;9917789}. The term “pleomorphic” has been used to describe the variation in differentiation across the tumour, with extraocular extension often showing poorly differentiated tumour compared with a better differentiated intraocular tumour component {9917789}. Extracellular mucopolysaccharide deposits can be present in their stroma {2825094}. Pigmented adenocarcinomas contain heavily pigmented cells, commonly associated with vacuoles that are thought to be extracellular {4115451}. Features of malignancy include widespread cytological atypia, conspicuous mitotic figures that may include abnormal forms, destructive local invasion, and necrosis.

Adenocarcinomas are immunoreactive with uniform nuclear staining for PAX8 {33002562}, vimentin, S100 {9917789}, and pancytokeratin (AE1/AE3), and often CK8, CK18, and CK5/6 {25015166;9917789}. In pigmented adenocarcinomas, variable immunoreactivity for the melanocytic markers melan-A, MITF, and HMB45 has been documented {18581131;25015166}.

Differential diagnosis

Adenomas: The differential diagnosis for non-pigmented adenomas includes adenomatous hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium, amelanotic melanoma and adenocarcinoma of the ciliary body, and medulloepithelioma of the ciliary body. For pigmented adenomas, it includes melanocytoma, pigmented melanoma, and pigmented adenocarcinoma of the ciliary body.

Adenocarcinoma: Non-pigmented ciliary adenocarcinoma includes amelanotic melanoma and metastatic adenocarcinoma in the ciliary body. For pigmented adenocarcinoma, the main differential diagnosis is pigmented ciliary body melanoma. In small biopsy specimens, the differential diagnosis with a ciliary body adenoma can be difficult if the atypia is not striking.

Cytology

Adenoma of the ciliary body: FNAB shows polygonal epithelial cells with bland round to oval nuclei and small nucleoli, with or without melanin granules. The presence of basement membrane material is helpful in differentiating this tumour from melanocytic and mesenchymal tumours {1808809;1882925;33002562;10326955}. The melanosomes of an adenoma are larger and more spherical than those of uveal melanoma. PAX8 immunohistochemistry is useful in distinguishing adenomas from melanocytic lesions of the ciliary body {33002562}.

Adenocarcinoma of the ciliary body: FNAB shows cohesive clusters of atypical cuboidal to large epithelioid epithelial cells with hyperchromatic, enlarged nuclei, prominent nucleoli, vacuolation, and basement membrane material. Similarly to adenomas, the cells may be pigmented or non-pigmented. The presence of basement membrane material differentiates these tumours from melanoma and metastatic adenocarcinoma in the ciliary body. PAX8 immunohistochemistry is useful in distinguishing an adenocarcinoma from melanocytic lesions {33002562}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRAF p.V600E mutations are commonly found in pigmented ciliary body adenomas and adenocarcinomas, whereas mutations in GNAQ or GNA11 are absent from them, in contrast to uveal melanoma {29059311}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Adenoma

Essential: bland polygonal epithelial cells arranged as nests, cords, and tubules with delicate fibrous septa and basement membrane; pigmented adenomas additionally show large cytoplasmic spherical melanin granules.

Desirable: hyaluronidase-resistant acid mucopolysaccharide matrix; immunohistochemical positivity for cytokeratins and PAX8; pigmented adenomas additionally show vacuolation and frequent immunohistochemical positivity for BRAF p.V600E.

Adenocarcinoma

Essential: cytologically atypical, mitotically active, cuboidal to epithelioid cells with enlarged and irregular nuclei containing prominent nucleoli, arranged in strands, cords, sheets, or duct-like structures; pigmented adenocarcinomas additionally contain large spherical melanin granules and are commonly associated with vacuoles.

Desirable: a meshwork of basement membrane around tumour that is PAS-positive indicates a primary ciliary body malignancy because metastases do not elaborate prominent basement membrane; hyaluronidase-resistant acid mucopolysaccharide matrix; immunohistochemical positivity for cytokeratins and PAX8; pigmented adenocarcinomas show frequent immunohistochemical positivity for BRAF p.V600E.

Note: Adenomas of the ciliary body with bland cytological features can be locally invasive with iris infiltration, angle closure, ciliary body invasion, and even extraocular extension, which do not necessarily indicate a malignant nature. Cytological atypia, mitotic activity, necrosis, and destructive local tissue invasion are the most reliable criteria to distinguish an adenoma from an adenocarcinoma of the ciliary body.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Adenoma of the ciliary body: Local resection is usually curative. Enucleation may be necessary when an adenoma is large and associated with loss of vision from tumour-related complications {9003334}.

Adenocarcinoma of the ciliary body: The prognosis is usually favourable, although a few patients have died after extraocular extension associated with orbital recurrence {30976584}, intracranial invasion {6995386;9917789;33529584}, or metastasis {9917789;30976584}.
