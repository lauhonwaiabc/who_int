1.3.1.5 Spitz naevus

Definition

Spitz naevi are melanocytic neoplasms composed of large epithelioid and/or spindled melanocytes with a regular architecture, and usually HRAS mutations or receptor kinase fusions.

Related terminology

Not recommended: spindle and/or epithelioid cell naevus; naevus of large spindle and/or epithelioid cells.

Subtype(s)

See #52542Table 3.02.

Localization

Spitz naevus has rarely been described in the eyelid {9822246;14300797;26498574;26270402}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation is nonspecific: a rapidly growing, pinkish-red, vascularized, solitary lesion seen mostly in young patients {28125759}. Most Spitz naevi in children are located in the head and neck {19318795;880553}.

Epidemiology

Because of its extreme rarity (< 10 cases reported), the incidence of Spitz naevus is unknown {26498574;9822246;26270402;14300797}. Since published cases to date have not been molecularly confirmed, the diagnosis should currently be defined as provisional.

Etiology

The etiology of eyelid Spitz naevus is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Spitz naevus may evolve from junctional proliferations of melanocytes, which then extend into the stroma. Some Spitz naevi lack an intraepithelial component.

Over 50% of Spitz naevi of the skin are the result of activating structural rearrangements such as chimeric fusions and truncations, whereas about 20% of Spitz naevi harbour activating HRAS mutations. Cases classified as atypical Spitz tumour may have additional mutations in other genes such as CDKN2A as well as some chromosomal copy-number aberrations {24451276}. Macroscopic appearance

Spitz naevi are typically < 6 mm but may exceed 10 mm in diameter and may be variable in colour, ranging from red/pink to tan or black {880553}. They may present as macules, papules, plaques, or nodules with a smooth or verrucous epidermal surface. Some Spitz naevi are polypoid. Compound Spitz naevi typically extend into the dermis and less commonly into the subcutis.

Histopathology

Spitz naevi are well circumscribed, with symmetrical borders. A benign lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate is present around the tumour, with multiple epithelial invaginations or cysts with goblet cells. The neoplastic cells are distributed in circumscribed nests and fascicles, oriented perpendicular to the surface. The eosinophilic cells are large and epithelioid or spindle shaped, with medium to large nuclei. Mitoses can be found in the superficial layers. The cytomorphology of the cells is uniform throughout the entire lesion. Pagetoid migration of lesional cells into the epidermis may be quite marked, especially in young children {23607499}. High-grade atypia must be absent. Maturation of the tumour cells can be observed. The agminated subtype has been described to occur in the eyelid {2748471;7659643}; see the Skin tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Skin Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 12). https://publications.iarc.who.int/650.]] for further details.

Immunohistochemistry

The cells stain for S100, SOX10, and melan-A. Junctional cells are HMB45-positive. Ki-67 staining can be positive in the superficial layers, and p16 is preserved {23607499;17894290}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed cutaneous neoplasm usually < 5 mm in diameter; enlarged epithelioid and/or spindled melanocytes with limited or no architectural aberration, cytological atypia, or mitoses (0–2 per mm2).

Desirable: histological features including symmetry, clefting about junctional nests, epidermal hyperplasia with a vertical raining down appearance of melanocytes, maturation of dermal melanocytes, wedge- or V-shaped dermal configuration, and absence of deep/marginal mitoses; confirmation of the melanocytic lineage with melanocytic markers such as melan-A (MART1) or SOX10 is useful for confirming the melanocytic lineage; retention of p16 expression; a single genetic alteration such as an HRAS-activating mutation or gene fusion involving genes such as ALK, NTRK1, NTRK2, or NTRK3 confirms the Spitz lineage and the exclusion of other entities; absence of additional pathogenic genetic alterations, or copy-number aberrations other than 11p gain or those associated with kinase fusions, particularly in adults.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Spitz naevi are benign lesions. They have a low risk of recurrence and are rarely precursors to melanoma.
