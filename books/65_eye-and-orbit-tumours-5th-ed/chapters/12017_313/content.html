12.0.1.7 Muir-Torre syndrome

Definition

Muir–Torre syndrome (MTS) is a rare autosomal dominant disorder defined by the association of cutaneous lesions (sebaceous neoplasms or keratoacanthoma) and Lynch syndrome (LS)-associated visceral malignancies. MTS results from constitutional (germline) pathogenic alterations affecting the DNA mismatch repair genes MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2.

Related terminology

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lesions are skin, eyelid, and visceral malignancies (see below).

Clinical features

MTS patients present with cutaneous manifestations which can appear before (22%), concurrent with (6%), or after (56%) the diagnosis of visceral malignancy {10534628}. The cutaneous manifestations include well-differentiated sebaceous neoplasms, such as sebaceous adenoma and sebaceoma and, less frequently, sebaceous carcinoma and keratoacanthoma, generally in non-periocular skin {3728878;26892655;18551751;26826402}. Many patients have numerous lesions and/or a family history {7601953}.

Visceral malignancies associated with LS (MIM number 120435) include tumours of the colorectum, endometrium, stomach, small bowel, ovary, gallbladder, hepatobiliary tract, pancreas, urinary tract, kidney, brain, and prostate {26657901;27261338;18398828}. Neoplasms can develop at any age, but often arise in young people. Some develop multiple tumours.

Epidemiology

The population incidence of MTS has not yet been determined, but the syndrome has been identified in approximately 5–9% of individuals from families with LS {26826402;16826164;18270343}.

Etiology

The primary cause of MTS is a germline mutation affecting a DNA mismatch repair (MMR) gene: MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, or PMS2. Some individuals have deletions involving EPCAM (TACSTD1) that can silence the neighbouring MSH2 gene. Based on immunohistochemistry, abnormalities of MSH2 and MSH6 expression are strongly associated with the MTS phenotype, and with MSH2 mutations in 90% of cases. Most of the remaining cases harbour MLH1 or PMS2 abnormalities. An isolated deficiency of MSH6 or PMS2 has been documented {26826402}. This strongly contrasts with LS, where germline deficits of MLH1 and MSH2 have roughly equal incidence. The phenotype of sebaceous neoplasia and visceral malignancy can also be seen in patients with autosomal recessive MUTYH-associated adenomatous polyposis (familial adenomatous polyposis 2; MIM number 608456).

A number of environmental and lifestyle factors have been identified as modifiers of LS. Cigarette smoking, increased body mass index, and alcohol consumption are associated with increased risk, whereas acetylsalicylic acid, ibuprofen, multivitamin and calcium supplements, and hormone replacement therapy are associated with a reduction of cancer risk {20145170;21559014;26282643;26109217}.

Pathogenesis

The four MMR genes encode proteins that detect and repair errors in base pairing during DNA replication by forming complexes {33005611}. MLH1 dimerizes with PMS2, while MSH2 dimerizes with MSH6 {25595178}. A germline pathogenic sequence alteration in one of the MMR genes followed by a second somatic hit to the remaining wildtype allele {18551751} leads to cells becoming mismatch repair deficient. This deficiency has several important consequences. Firstly, mismatch repair–deficient cells escape from the normal control of apoptosis and gain a relative growth advantage {20102395}. Secondly, mismatch repair deficiency leads to an increase in the point mutation rate, especially within repetitive stretches of DNA called microsatellites; this manifests as microsatellite instability (MSI). Thirdly, mismatch repair deficiency may also lead to abnormal mismatch repair protein expression, which is identifiable by immunohistochemistry.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross appearance is related to the tumour type and is not distinctive.

Histopathology

The histopathological appearance is related to the tumour type and is not distinctive.

Immunohistochemically, approximately one half to two thirds of unselected sebaceous neoplasms are deficient in MMR proteins {25006859;26826402;18551751}. The rates of loss vary: in the head and neck region, adenomas show 30–40% loss, whereas loss in carcinomas in this region is rare. Outside the head and neck region, both adenomas and carcinomas commonly show loss (in > 80% of cases).

MMR immunohistochemistry was found to be comparable to PCR-based MSI testing in LS-associated tumours {23556052;18556767}. Nevertheless, the sensitivity and specificity of MMR immunohistochemistry in predicting the underlying gene defect is 85% and 48%, respectively, in sebaceous neoplasms, compared with the sensitivity and specificity values of 92–94% and 88–100%, respectively, in colorectal tumours {23212176;24603434}. The value of MMR immunohistochemistry and MSI testing is low in periocular sebaceous neoplasms, specifically in sebaceous carcinoma. It is considerably enhanced by testing more than one tumour from the same family and/or individual, especially if the tumours are rare.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular genetic studies on tumour and blood DNA can directly evaluate the MMR gene status {25043945}. Since it is established that somatic MMR gene mutations are seen in sebaceous tumours, germline testing for confirmation is preferred {27287813}.

Although routine MTS and LS screening is not recommended for periocular sebaceous carcinoma, the Mayo MTS risk score guidelines may be applicable to periocular sebaceous neoplasms {31797796;24603434;33005611}. Patients with a score ≥ 2 should be evaluated by MMR immunohistochemistry on colon or endometrial cancer or, when available, by molecular genetic testing {24603434}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: at least one visceral cancer; neoplasms with evidence of mismatch repair deficiency (by MMR immunohistochemistry or MSI testing); definitive MTS/LS diagnosis involves identification of a germline MMR gene pathogenic mutation.

Desirable: multiple skin sebaceous neoplasms often present.

Staging

Tumours are staged according to Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM8, depending on the tumour type.

Prognosis and prediction

The visceral malignancies of MTS and LS are generally found to have less-aggressive behaviour than their sporadic counterparts {7601953}. The periocular cutaneous manifestation is usually of benign well-differentiated sebaceous neoplasms.
