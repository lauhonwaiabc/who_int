1.1.2.1 Reactive epithelial hyperplasia of the conjunctiva

Definition

Reactive epithelial hyperplasia is a reactive, benign, non-neoplastic epithelial proliferation, in response to an irritating stimulus.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A6Y Other specified disorders of conjunctiva

Related terminology

Acceptable: pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia; pseudoadenomatous hyperplasia.

Not recommended: pseudocarcinomatous hyperplasia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia tends to affect (limbal) bulbar conjunctiva. Pseudoglandular hyperplasia preferentially occurs in the fornix and tarsal conjunctiva.

Clinical features

Pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia may appear as a fleshy to white mass, minimally raised or thickened {17181623}. Pseudoglandular hyperplasia appears as a fleshy, poorly defined mass {11150278} and shares clinical features with ocular surface neoplasia.

Epidemiology

Lesions are uncommon; they have been reported in children and adults of both sexes.

Etiology

In many cases there is no known association. These lesions have been associated with granular cell tumour, blastomycosis, thermal burns, epithelial (limbal) cell transplantation, contact lens use, chemical exposure (e.g. iododerma), and previous surgery {11150278;3992475;6258438;17181623;7322470;6888855}.

Pathogenesis

Lesions arise as a result of nonspecific reactive changes in response to diverse presumed causal associations.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia consists of acanthotic epithelium with normal keratinocyte maturation. Squamous metaplasia may be present with surface keratinization, and the superficial epithelial cells can contain keratohyaline granules. Pseudoglandular hyperplasia is composed of prominent pseudoglands of Henle, lined with stratified squamous epithelium with goblet cells. Mucin often fills the lumina of the pseudoglands, and lamellar concretions are sometimes noted.

The histological differential diagnosis for pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia includes papilloma, inverted papilloma, and conjunctival squamous intraepithelial neoplasia. Normal maturation of keratinocytes is lost in epithelial dysplasia, and keratinocytes display pleomorphic nuclei with variable amounts of cytoplasm and mitotic activity.

The histological differential diagnosis for pseudoglandular hyperplasia is squamous cell carcinoma with mucinous differentiation (previously known as mucoepidermoid carcinoma). This will be more cellular and the keratinocytes will display nuclear atypia with focal intracytoplasmic mucin and mitotic activity.

Cytology

Smears show benign squamous epithelium.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no known genetic abnormalities.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: hyperplastic epithelium with benign cytology.

Desirable: identification of an underlying causal association.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign/reactive processes with a good prognosis. There are no reports about recurrences after incomplete removal or eradication of the underlying cause.
