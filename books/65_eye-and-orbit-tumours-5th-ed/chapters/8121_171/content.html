8.1.2.1 Combined lymphatic-venous malformation of the orbit

Definition

Combined lymphatic-venous malformation (LVM) is a congenital low-flow vascular malformation consisting of both malformed veins and lymphatic channels often interspersed with lymphoid follicles, areas of fibrosis, and old haemorrhage.

ICD-O coding

9170/0 Combined lymphatic-venous malformation of the orbit

ICD-11 coding

LA90.2Y Other specified peripheral venous malformations

LA90.12 Lymphatic malformations of certain specified sites

Related terminology

Acceptable: lymphatic malformation.

Not recommended: lymphangioma; venous anomaly.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Orbital LVM can be superficial in location or deep within the orbit, or have combined superficial and deep components {3808616}. Complex lesions involve other head and neck structures including the infratemporal, intracranial, and temporalis fossae {22828166}. Most cases of conjunctival involvement constitute the superficial component of an orbital lymphatic malformation {21816261;22666617;21788081}.

Superficially these lesions tend to be more microcystic, whereas orbital lesions tend towards being macrocystic. Deeper components tend to be more predominantly venous {34054491}.

Clinical features

LVMs can present in childhood or later in life, depending on the size and activity of the lesion. Superficial components tend to present as a subcutaneous or subconjunctival mass, whereas orbital components may present with unilateral proptosis, with or without extraocular motility disturbance, ptosis, and visual loss {3808616;27879620;10209440;12455141;28534344}. They can expand in times of viral illness and/or present with intralesional haemorrhage and/or thrombosis, the latter being characterized by the subacute onset of pain, proptosis, and/or ecchymosis. They can lead to vision impairment / optic neuropathy and amblyopia {18034192;34054491}.

Orbital LVMs can be misdiagnosed as rhabdomyosarcoma, haemangioma, or lymphoma on imaging studies {23828510}.

Epidemiology

There is a female preponderance with most lesions presenting in infancy and childhood {18034192;34054491}. Orbital LVM accounts for 0.3–4% of orbital lesions {3808616;23459274;34054491}.

Etiology

Systemic vascular malformations arise during embryogenesis and can be due to mosaic mutations or, more rarely, germline mutations leading to developmental errors in the vascular system {26839221;31950476;34166070}. Orbital LVMs have not been studied in this regard.

Pathogenesis

Some systemic LVMs have been found to be due to somatic mosaic mutations in PIK3CA which lead to activation of the AKT/mTOR signalling pathway with consequent increased cell growth, proliferation, and migration {29985963;30922748;25292196;25424831;33069286}.

Macroscopic appearance

LVM is tan to reddish or brownish in colour and unencapsulated, with multiple compartments and cysts filled with blood, and irregular margins due to its infiltrative nature {18635637}.

Histopathology

LVM forms an unencapsulated irregular mass of malformed endothelial-lined channels of various sizes separated by fibroconnective tissue septa that lack smooth muscle. The connective tissue may show aggregates of small mature lymphocytes, which may form lymphoid follicles. Haemosiderin-laden macrophages may be present, reflecting prior haemorrhage. The lymphatic channels often contain proteinaceous fluid, blood, or both. Lymphatic endothelial cells express CD31 and stain with D2-40 {23782346;9610694;16758665;24355066;19218988;18034192}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of somatic mutations can support the use of specific treatments in select cases (mTOR or PI3K inhibitors).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic clinical, radiological (MRI: vascular mass) findings; proliferation of dilated, variably sized lymphatic and venous channels.

Desirable: immunohistochemical expression of CD31 and CD34, and staining with D2-40 in the endothelial cells.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

LVMs typically progress slowly. The clinical course is variable, ranging from spontaneous regression to gradual progression. Treatment is reserved for vision-threatening lesions or those causing severe disfigurement, while less severe lesions can be observed. Treatment options include sclerotherapy, medical treatment with sildenafil (PDE5 inhibitor) or sirolimus (mTOR inhibitor), anti-VEGF medications, and surgical debulking {26077629;31232717;10837387;28534344}. Lymphatic and venous components can be treated separately {30601461}.
