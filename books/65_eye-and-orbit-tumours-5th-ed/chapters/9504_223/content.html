9.5.0.4 Rosai-Dorfman disease

Definition

Rosai–Dorfman disease (RDD) is a histiocytic disorder characterized by nodal or extranodal accumulation of large S100-positive macrophage-like histiocytes commonly displaying emperipolesis.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Rosai–Dorfman disease

ICD-11 coding

EK92 Histiocytoses of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Acceptable: Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease.

Not recommended: sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RDD most frequently involves lymph nodes, particularly in the cervical region. Extranodal disease occurs in 43% of cases. Ophthalmic manifestations occur in 10–30% of cases {434097;29428455;29720485;35236371}, and include unilateral or bilateral soft tissue masses in the orbit, eyelids, lacrimal glands, extraocular muscles, conjunctiva, or cornea {29720485;35236371;34637661}. Intraocular RDD can present as choroidal masses {434097;16714922;7795403}. Isolated ophthalmic RDD is extremely rare {434097;2180012;32775797;34814862}.

Clinical features

The typical presentation is that of painless, slow-growing lymphadenopathy, with accompanying B symptoms in one third of patients, or symptoms related to involved extranodal sites {2180012;29720485}. A painless soft tissue mass and/or proptosis are the commonest clinical manifestations of orbital RDD. Other symptoms include reduced vision, restricted motility, ptosis, pain, compressive optic neuropathy, and diplopia {434097;34814862}. The presentation of cutaneous RDD is variable, typically that of painless, multiple, reddish-brown papules, nodules, or plaques, often reaching a large size of 300 mm {25310213;17325475}, though single nodules are also common. This cutaneous RDD has its own unique clinical and epidemiological characteristics {32591351}.

Clinical associations are recognized: familial RDD, classic RDD, extranodal RDD, neoplasia-associated RDD; and immune disease–associated RDD {26966089}.

Imaging may show destruction of surrounding bones including the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses {34814862;29720485}. RDD presenting in the sella region is often accompanied by visual changes and diabetes insipidus {33330575}. Epibulbar masses, uveitis, and papilloedema have also been reported {434097;16714922;7795403}.

Epidemiology

The global incidence of RDD is unknown; the annual prevalence in the USA is 1:200 000 {29720485}. It affects children and young adults (mean age: 20 years) with a slight male predilection (58% of cases) {2180012}. In other series, the median age was 40–42 years {29428455;34814862}. RDD is more common among individuals of African descent {23863552}. Asian people are more likely to present with cutaneous eyelid lesions {16433797}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. An infectious etiology has been suggested with a possible role of parvovirus B19 {17142576} and retroviruses like HIV {24564426}. Neoplasia-associated RDD (including RDD mixed with malignant histiocytosis, Langerhans cell histiocytosis [LCH], or Erdheim–Chester disease [ECD]) can also occur {26966089}. Rare inherited conditions predispose to familial RDD, including germline mutations in SLC29A3 (H syndrome / Faisalabad histiocytosis) or FAS (TNFRSF6) (autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome) {20140240;24172204;15958855}.

Pathogenesis

In contrast to LCH {32750121} and ECD, the cell of origin that gives rise to histiocytes accumulating in RDD lesions is unknown {28566492}. Monocytes derived from a myeloid-committed progenitor cell have been suggested as an immediate precursor {29526544}. Approximately 50% of cases carry gain-of-function mutations in the MAPK/ERK pathway, including KRAS, NRAS, MAPK21, ARAF, CSF1R, and rarely BRAF p.V600E, suggesting that at least a proportion of cases of RDD represent a neoplastic disorder {26566875;26922062;28597077;28664935;29236635;32601132}. Their differentiation trajectory and susceptibility to inflammatory factors seems critically dependent on this MAPK signalling {28614806;30867592}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, lesions are rubbery with a lobulated surface, which is usually covered by a smooth capsule {15561688;18588698}.

Histopathology

RDD typically shows a mixed infiltrate of histiocytes displaying abundant or vacuolated cytoplasm, large nuclei but no xanthomatous features, lymphocytes, and variable numbers of plasma cells with or without Russell bodies {18588698;2180012}. While emperipolesis (lymphocytophagocytosis) with lymphocytes being typically phagocytized {15561688} is classic for RDD, Touton giant cells should be absent {32187362}.

Immunohistochemistry

Histiocytes classically express S100, which highlights the emperipolesis, CD14, CD163, CD68, factor XIIIa, fascin, and OCT2 {33177341}, but not CD1a or CD207 (langerin) {23895540;34814862}. While cyclin D1 and phosphorylated ERK are often positive {33177341}, mitotic figures are rare and the proliferation index is low {15561688;2180012}. Cytoplasmic BRAF (VE1) is rarely positive; however, ALK and NTRK should be negative {32187362}.

Differential diagnosis

Orbital mass: differential diagnosis includes ECD, LCH, adult-onset xanthogranuloma, juvenile xanthogranuloma, necrobiotic xanthogranuloma, and malignant histiocytosis {29720485;33710041}.

Eyelid swelling: differential diagnosis includes LCH, ECD, adult-onset xanthogranuloma, and adult-onset asthma and periocular xanthogranuloma {34637661}.

Lesions in iris, lacrimal glands, cornea, conjunctiva, extraocular muscles, choroid: differential diagnosis includes adult-onset xanthogranuloma, juvenile xanthogranuloma, ECD, malignant histiocytosis {34637661}.

Cytology

Demonstration of sheets of large histiocytes preferably displaying emperipolesis {31942098} is seen with FNAC.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Histiocytes expressing MAPK pathway–activating molecular alterations, in particular KRAS and MAP2K1 mutations, are found in a significant proportion of patients {29526544}. Mutations in BRAF (including BRAF p.V600E), ARAF, and CSF1R are rare {29720485;32591351;32187362}. For suspected familial RDD, workup should include SLC29A3 and FAS germline mutation analysis {24172204;20140240;15958855}. However, these are not readily available as routinely performed analyses on a common basis. At the same time, these are highly relevant to the etiology and pathogenesis and thus prove to be helpful in the differential diagnosis {32591351}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large histiocytes with round nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant pale cytoplasm, often with emperipolesis; abundant plasma cells in the background; positive immunostaining for S100, which also highlights the emperipolesis.

Desirable: coinfiltrating lymphocytes, and plasma cells; in diagnostically challenging cases, expression of OCT2 and cyclin D1, in the absence of CD1a, CD207, and ALK expression; MAPK pathway mutations.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Diagnostic evaluation should include assessment of disease extent by PET-CT or other anatomical imaging, as well as evaluation for conditions known to be associated with RDD. Orbital involvement should be investigated by gadolinium-enhanced MRI {29720485}. Published case series are too limited to describe the prognosis for ophthalmic RDD. Treatment should be tailored to the individual clinical situation. Extranodal RDD, including orbital manifestation, responds poorly to steroids alone {29720485}. Recurrence and/or progression of orbital RDD after surgery may necessitate chemotherapy {16714922;34814862;29720485} or combined chemotherapy and radiation therapy {23738161}. Some refractory extranodal RDD cases have been successfully treated with a MEK inhibitor {29236635;30867592}.
