3.1.2.1 Ocular melanocytosis

Definition

Congenital ocular melanocytosis is a benign, usually unilateral condition defined as a higher-than-normal population of non-proliferating hyperpigmented melanocytes in the uvea, sclera, and orbit.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA1Y Other specified structural developmental anomalies of the eye, eyelid or lacrimal apparatus

Related terminology

Acceptable: ocular melanocytosis; melanosis oculi; congenital melanosis bulbi; congenital oculodermal melanocytosis (naevus of Ota).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

When associated with adjacent cutaneous melanocytosis, usually at the distributions of the ophthalmic and maxillary divisions of the trigeminal nerves, the condition is known as congenital oculodermal melanocytosis, naevus of Ota, or naevus fuscocaeruleus ophthalmomaxillaris.

In phakomatosis pigmentovascularis, a group of neural-crest disorders found mostly in Asian people, ocular or oculodermal melanocytosis can occur in combination with a cutaneous vascular malformation such as an adjacent port-wine stain {33058925;32387657}.

Congenital ocular melanocytosis is usually unilateral, but 10% of patients have bilateral involvement (see #35343Fig. 4.XXX).

The extent of congenital ocular melanocytosis can be classified on the basis of the involved ocular tissue (sclera, iris, ciliary body, choroid) {32229516}.

Clinical features

The most typical presenting ocular sign of congenital ocular melanocytosis is iris heterochromia. Iris hyperpigmentation may be associated with iris nodules (iris mammillations), which are dome-shaped small protuberances of the iris surface. The mammillations are composed of aggregates of plump, lightly pigmented naevoid cells interwoven with mature, densely pigmented spindle-shaped uveal melanocytes {7891979}. Another clinical feature is loss of iris crypts (see #35297Fig. 4.XXX).

Patchy hyperpigmentation of the sclera is another very typical and the most common feature. The patches of pigmentation often appear as slate grey or bluish grey areas in the visible anterior sclera. The uvea is involved in a diffuse or sectoral pattern, either anteroposteriorly (subcategories 2–4) or circumferentially (subcategories A–D). The posterior sclera and orbit can also be involved, although this will not be clinically apparent.

In phakomatosis pigmentovascularis, an adjacent port-wine stain is present {33058925}.

Congenital ocular melanocytosis and phakomatosis pigmentovascularis can be associated with glaucoma {2383200;29607552;9022120}.

Epidemiology

Ocular and oculodermal melanocytosis combined occur in approximately 0.04% of White people, in 1.4–2.7% of patients with uveal melanoma, and in 3.1–4.3% of young patients with uveal melanoma {26854035;22020174;7133640;24227986}. These disorders occur in Asian populations, with a prevalence of 0.014–0.034%, and with an M:F ratio of about 1:5 {39723871}.

Etiology

Congenital ocular melanocytosis is a hamartoma-like disorder.

Pathogenesis

The histogenesis of congenital ocular melanocytosis is similar to that of cellular blue naevus and Mongolian spot. It involves abnormal migration of neural crest–derived melanocytes to the orbit, eye, and dermis, rather than to the dermoepidermal junction {13130904}. Somatic activating mutations of GNA11 or GNAQ have been found in some patients in association with cutaneous melanocytosis as well as in phakomatosis pigmentovascularis {26778290}.

Macroscopic appearance

Striking hyperpigmentation of the periocular skin is seen, along with patchy hyperpigmentation of the conjunctiva, episclera, and sclera, and diffuse or sectoral brown hyperpigmentation of the iris and choroid.

Histopathology

The characteristic histopathology of congenital ocular melanocytosis is overpopulation with spindle-shaped, bipolar, or dendritic melanocytes that may occupy the skin, soft tissues, sclera, uvea, orbit, and even intracranial structures, usually in the distribution of the ophthalmic and maxillary divisions of the trigeminal nerve. The iris and choroid are heavily pigmented and slightly thickened because of increased numbers of heavily pigmented melanocytes; this bears some resemblance to melanocytoma {6019552}. Similar melanocytes are abundant, but not clinically visible, in the ciliary body and between scleral lamellae. Mitoses are not seen. Thickened areas should be carefully examined to exclude an embedded early transformation to uveal melanoma.

The differential diagnosis includes diffuse uveal melanoma, bilateral diffuse uveal melanocytic proliferation, and conjunctival melanosis. The differential diagnosis of naevus of Ota includes Hori naevus and naevus of Sun, which are acquired Ota-like facial skin maculae.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: iris heterochromia and/or scleral pigmentation.

Desirable: abundant spindle-shaped, bipolar, or dendritic melanocytes that may occupy the periocular skin, soft tissues, sclera, uvea, orbit, and intracranial structures, usually in the distribution of the ophthalmic and maxillary divisions of the trigeminal nerve.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Although this condition is congenital, benign, and non-progressive, it may be associated with two major complications.

Glaucoma is a common ocular complication, caused by angle abnormalities or mechanical occlusion by pigment and melanocytes in an open iridocorneal angle anatomy {29607552}.

Uveal melanoma and cutaneous melanoma in oculodermal melanocytosis are important but relatively rare complications with a lifetime risk of 1 in 400 {9442799}. Uveal melanoma in a patient with congenital ocular melanocytosis is associated with a higher risk of metastasis {26854035;23522968;23681424}.

In atypical cases of uveal melanoma, such as multifocal, bilateral, and paediatric cases, as well as in orbital and leptomeningeal melanomas, underlying congenital melanocytosis should be suspected {11801268;30643768;16829813}. The association with uveal melanoma may be due to a shared predisposing mutation in GNAQ or GNA11 {19078957}.
