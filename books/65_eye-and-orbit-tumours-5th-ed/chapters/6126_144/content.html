6.1.2.6 Adenocarcinoma NOS of the lacrimal gland

Definition

Adenocarcinoma NOS of the lacrimal gland is a malignant epithelial neoplasm with a solid and glandular growth pattern that lacks the histopathological features characteristic of other specific carcinoma types.

ICD-O coding

8140/3 Adenocarcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2D0Y & XH74S1 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Adenocarcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: unclassified adenocarcinoma; primary ductal adenocarcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adenocarcinoma NOS arises from the orbital lobe of the lacrimal gland and rarely in accessory lacrimal glands {30320083;33663254}.

Clinical features

Patients present with similar clinical features to those of other lacrimal gland tumours. These include upper eyelid swelling, ptosis, non-axial proptosis, restriction of ocular movement, diplopia, and loss of vision secondary to the mass effects of the enlarged lacrimal gland {22471335;30320083}. Symptoms are usually present for < 1 year before presentation {22471335}. Pain is often a feature although is not as common as in adenoid cystic carcinoma of the lacrimal gland {22471335}. Imaging is non-diagnostic and shows typical features of a malignant lacrimal mass with irregular margins, bony erosion or invasion, and calcification {22471335;28399336}. Adenocarcinoma NOS has a propensity to spread by lymphatic and haematogenous routes, and may metastasize to regional lymph nodes as well as distant sites including the lung, brain, and mediastinum {34352903;30028745}.

Epidemiology

Adenocarcinoma NOS is the third most frequent malignant epithelial tumour of the lacrimal gland occurring in 5–10% of cases {18772674;28399336;34352903}. It is reported to be more common in men {22471335} and occurs in the older population with a mean age at presentation of 50–52 years {22471335;30028745}.

Etiology

These tumours may occur de novo or within a pleomorphic adenoma {34352903}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours may present as unencapsulated irregular masses {30320083}.

Histopathology

Adenocarcinoma NOS lacks the distinctive histological features that characterize other specific carcinoma types. It shows variable gland formation with duct-like configurations, and nests of clear cells {22471335;19667339}. Mitoses reported in these tumours vary from less than occasional to many {19667339}. These tumours can also show a higher grade of anaplastic cell proliferation with prominent glandular formation and extensive necrosis {19667339}.

Immunohistochemistry

Although nonspecific for diagnosis, the tumour cells have been reported to stain for cytokeratins {32282640}, CK7 {32282640;30320083}, GATA3 {32282640}, p63 {32282640}, E-cadherin {32282640}, GCDFP-15 {30320083}, and AR {30320083;30159157}, and focally for CEA {30320083}.

Differential diagnosis

As this is a diagnosis of exclusion, differential diagnoses of all other specific primary lacrimal gland carcinomas must be considered and excluded {22471335}. Unlike primary ductal carcinoma, the majority of adenocarcinoma NOS reported show absence of ERBB2 expression {30320083;32282640;30159157}.

Cytology

Cytological diagnosis has not been described.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is nonspecific without alterations that would categorize these tumours elsewhere.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

The diagnosis of adenocarcinoma NOS is made strictly by exclusion of other known primary lacrimal gland carcinomas {22471335}.

Staging

Staging is according to the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis is poor with an average survival of 1.5 years and mortality ranging from 50% to 80%. Earlier treatment intervention has been reported to decrease metastases and increase long-term survival {8259287}. A 10-year overall survival rate of 27.7% and 10-year disease-specific survival rate of 51.4% has been reported with early treatment intervention {25385802}. Tumour histology has been suggested to correlate with disease-specific survival {25385802}. Treatment consists of complete surgical excision by exenteration with lymph node dissection accompanied by adjuvant radiotherapy and rarely chemotherapy. Immunotherapy with checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab {30320083} and nivolumab {30159157} has also been used.
