9.2.1.2 IgG4-related disease

Definition

IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic, fibroinflammatory process that can affect multiple organs, histologically characterized by storiform fibrosis and lymphoplasmacytic infiltration, with numerous IgG4-positive plasma cells and an increased ratio of IgG4:IgG positive cells (> 40%) and often with an elevated serum IgG4 titre.

ICD-O coding

9760/1 IgG4-related disease

ICD-11 coding

4A43.0 IgG4-related disease

Related terminology

Acceptable: IgG4-related orbital disease.

Not recommended: IgG4-related autoimmune disease; IgG4-related sclerosing disease; Küttner tumour; pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

IgG4-RD can affect almost any organ, and may affect single or multiple organs simultaneously or metachronously. The most common sites of ophthalmic involvement are the lacrimal glands {22596100;34725471;28858963} and orbital soft tissues, mainly involving infraorbital nerves {24577252;22855022;32501880} and extraocular muscles {31261163;28272212;29970061}. More recently, tarsal, scleral, choroidal, and orbital bone involvement has been reported {29926366;28229544;24513111;28346259;33795286;31669684}.

Clinical features

Patients may present with periocular swelling, painless proptosis, decreased vision, or other symptoms depending on the ocular tissue involved {21482868;28858963;24513111;25564258}. Bilateral involvement is common but may not be symmetrical {21482868;32348524}. IgG4 serum levels may be elevated (> 135 mg/dL), although 30–40% of patients have normal levels {22596100;21124093;24651618}.

Orbital CT and MRI may show enlargement of the lacrimal gland, trigeminal nerve branches, and/or extraocular muscle, and whole-body 18F-FDG PET imaging is useful for evaluating systemic involvement {22700747;23645355;24627453;25564258}.

Epidemiology

Patients with IgG4-RD have a median age range of 58–67 years, with slight male predominance {25988916;34146222;34961492}. The disease is rare in children {23480695;24993327;27012661;29204759}. IgG4-RD disease may occur in all ethnic groups. A potential prevalence in Asian populations might be the result of numerous reports in the literature {22316447;26106212;26398142}. Overall frequency of ophthalmic involvement in systemic IgG4-RD ranges from 4% to 34% {24513111;28272212}.

Etiology

IgG4-RD appears to be immune-mediated, and may be linked to genetic predisposition, autoimmunity, and perhaps environmental factors {21045701;31852351;32708432;31612628}.

Pathogenesis

Recent studies have proposed that T-helper 2 and regulatory T lymphocytes lead to activation of B cells including IgG4-expressing B cells in a modified Th2 response {21045701;28830675;29781221}. Increased cytokine secretion of IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, IL-13 (Th2 cells), IL-1, TGF-β, and interferon-γ by CD4+ cytotoxic T cells and TGF-β by T regulatory cells is implicated in the pathogenesis of this disease {22786718;30422824;32023137;32708432}.

Macroscopic appearance

IgG4-RD varies according to the proportion of inflammation and fibrosis, resulting in soft tan-coloured to firm grey tissue fragments.

Histopathology

Characteristic histological findings include dense, polyclonal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, with increased IgG4+ cells, and fibrosis of collagenous and focally storiform type with a radiating pattern of closely packed fibroblasts. Obliterative phlebitis is rarely present in ocular IgG4-RD. It is characterized by transmural inflammatory infiltrate and fibrous occlusion of venous lumen, which can be demonstrated by elastic stain. However, it is seldom observed in ocular tissue biopsies. Eosinophilia and non-obliterative phlebitis can be found {22596100}. The histopathology is a spectrum evolving from an early lymphoid-predominant tissue infiltration to paucicellular fibrosis {24911364}. The presence of necrosis, vasculitis, granulomas, and acute inflammation generally excludes IgG4-RD {22596100}. The diagnostic IgG4+ plasma cell count is different according to sites. Elevation of both the absolute IgG4+ cell count and the IgG4:IgG ratio (> 40%), nevertheless, is required for the diagnosis (see #38043Table 10.02) {22596100}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical staining is essential to demonstrate increased IgG4+ plasma cells, a hallmark of this disease. While the count of IgG4+ cells may vary from 40/mm2 (in orbital myositis) to > 400/mm2 (as required for the lacrimal gland lesions), the IgG4:IgG ratio should be > 40% {33274670;22596100}. These numbers are determined as the mean of three separate hotspot counts (often less than a square millimetre in total).

Differential diagnosis

See #38043Table 10.02. Differential diagnoses include follicular hyperplasia, lymphoma, sarcoidosis, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA) positive granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Wegener granulomatosis), thyroid-related orbitopathy, idiopathic orbital inflammation, Sjögren syndrome, and infectious processes {29755273;28968906;30124607}. In cases of sclerouveitis, collagen-vascular disorders should also be considered. IgG4+ cells can be seen in several inflammatory conditions that do not meet diagnostic criteria for IgG4-RD {21233087;24144223} and xanthogranulomas.

Immunostaining or in situ hybridization for κ and λ light chains is recommended in select cases of IgG4-RD to demonstrate polyclonality and exclude an IgG4-expressing lymphoma with plasmacytic differentiation or plasma cell neoplasm {29285637;24030741;21102421}. Rarely, oligoclonal expansions of IgG4+ plasma cells may be encountered in nodal or extranodal sites, which may raise the possibility of lymphoma or a plasma cell neoplasm {28821195}.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In select cases immunoglobulin gene rearrangement studies may be performed in order to exclude IgG4-expressing lymphoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate; fibrosis, at least focally storiform; increased IgG4+ plasma cells with an IgG4:IgG ratio > 40%; clinicopathological correlation required; exclusion of well-defined entities that mimic IgG4-RD.

Desirable: phlebitis (that may rarely be obliterative); 40–400 IgG4+ cells per mm2 (mean within three high-power field hotspots per mm2); elevated serum IgG4 levels; response to steroids/rituximab.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Overall prognosis is favourable with a good response to steroid treatment. Some patients experience relapse and others might require long-term steroids and/or immunosuppressive therapy. Rituximab has been shown to be very effective as a steroid-sparing drug in recurrent disease {25564258;30734497}. An association between amyloidosis, carcinoma, and lymphoma has been suggested in patients with IgG4-RD {25155229;28177871;29444790;18580683;21584726;22211288;31872350;26472416;32188869;28544157}, but a causal relationship is still unsettled {24492683;25881845;32552502}.

> 400/mm2

Retroperitoneum, mediastinum, and aorta

IgG4-related retroperitoneal fibrosis (Ormond disease)

> 120/mm2

Asymptomatic or nonspecific constitutional symptoms (fatigue, anorexia, fever, weight loss), low back or flank pain, leg oedema, abnormal renal function test

Imaging shows soft tissue mass over paravertebral area and pelvis, frequently involving abdominal aorta and iliac arteries (IgG4-related periaortitis), and entrapping the ureter causing hydronephrosis {31942203;30754935}

IgG4-related mediastinal fibrosis

> 120/mm2

Thoracic counterpart of retroperitoneal fibrosis and often manifest simultaneously; frequently involves thoracic aorta (IgG4-related aortitis/periaortitis); asymptomatic or dyspnoea, haemoptysis, dysphagia, and chest pain {29851832}

IgG4-related aortitis / periaortitis / periarteritis

> 200/mm2

Most commonly involves infrarenal abdominal aorta and iliac arteries in the context of retroperitoneal fibrosis or thoracic aorta, less commonly other medium-sized arteries (coronary and mesenteric arteries); asymptomatic or low back pain; some may present with aortic aneurysm or dissection

Imaging shows vessel wall thickening (media and adventitia), mass-like lesion with contrast enhancement with or without luminal dilatation (aneurysm) or reduction; PET-CT useful to assess disease activity and detect asymptomatic lesions {33121641;31942203}

Lymph node

IgG4-related lymphadenopathy

> 400/mm2

Incidental lymphadenopathy on imaging, painless non-tender lymph nodes, often adjacent to organs affected by IgG4-RD, local mass effects (hydronephrosis, lower limb swelling for pelvic lymph nodes)

Lymph nodes usually mildly enlarged (mean: 20 mm) with homogeneous enhancement and no calcification or necrosis {18344866;30558692}
