2.1.1.6 Molluscum contagiosum

Definition

Molluscum contagiosum is a benign and self-limited viral skin infection that presents as multiple epidermal nodular, umbilicated lesions on the skin.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

1E76 Molluscum contagiosum

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Skin lesions of molluscum contagiosum may occur anywhere on the body but most commonly involve the head and neck, trunk, flexural areas of the extremities, axillae, and groin. Periocular location is evenly distributed on the upper and lower eyelids {29855150}.

Clinical features

Solitary or (more commonly) multiple skin lesions usually present as discrete 2–5 mm umbilicated, waxy, flesh-coloured to erythematous papules. Giant lesions (≥ 10 mm) occasionally occur, especially in individuals with HIV, but they also may occur in immunocompetent individuals {34409645}. Around the eye they can provoke a follicular conjunctivitis {28845328}.

Epidemiology

Molluscum contagiosum occurs worldwide and is more frequent in children, although adolescents and adults may also be affected. The overall prevalence in children is 8.3%. The prevalence among people with HIV is estimated to approach 20%. There is no sex predilection {31239742}.

Etiology

Molluscum contagiosum is caused by the molluscum contagiosum virus belonging to the Poxviridae family.

Pathogenesis

The virus is transmitted by direct skin contact, autoinoculation, or contaminated fomites in children, immunocompromised individuals, or sexually active adults {28884917;31239742}. The virus then replicates within the cytoplasm of keratinocytes of the epithelium.

Macroscopic appearance

The individual papule is crateriform and contains a thick, whitish-yellow, cheesy, or curd-like material.

Histopathology

The typical elevated crateriform lesion is formed by endophytic lobules of acanthotic epidermis containing large, eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies, known as molluscum bodies or Henderson–Paterson bodies, that compress the nucleus to the periphery of the cell. Ruptured lesions incite an inflammatory response, which sometimes demonstrates lymphocyte atypia resembling lymphoma, including the presence of CD30-positive large cells {18616762}. The surrounding stroma is often oedematous, mucinous, and/or fibrous {25891863}.

Cytology

Molluscum bodies can be identified in a Tzanck smear {20553359}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: crateriform, acanthotic epidermis; molluscum bodies.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Spontaneous regression typically occurs within 12–18 months. A variety of treatment modalities, such as cryotherapy, curettage, topical chemical agents, immunomodulators, or pulsed dye laser, can be used for extensive disease, secondary complications such as superinfection, or aesthetic purposes {31239742;28884917}. Immunocompromised individuals need more vigorous treatment to eradicate the disease {27136625}.
