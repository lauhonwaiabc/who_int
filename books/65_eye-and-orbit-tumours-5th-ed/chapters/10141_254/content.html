10.1.4.1 Leiomyoma

Definition

Leiomyoma is a benign tumour of smooth muscle.

ICD-O coding

8890/0 Leiomyoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH4CY6 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Leiomyoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Leiomyomas have been reported in the orbit, eyelid {8963938;20966798;23929515}, conjunctiva {27994813;27993264;30454719;14516824}, and caruncle {9881563}, and intraocularly in the iris, ciliary body, and choroid (see section Mesectodermal leiomyoma). Orbital leiomyomas may be located in any area of the orbit, including the orbital apex with intracranial extension, or inferior orbit with extension into the paranasal sinuses {14189707;15677948;16033356;1894345;4361898;14998695;11136988;10202288}.

Clinical features

Orbital leiomyomas may cause proptosis, diplopia, deviation of the globe, and pain {15677948;14189707;11136988;28151824}. There may be swelling of the eyelids, conjunctiva {27994813}, and caruncle {9881563} with limited ocular motility.

Epidemiology

There are < 25 cases of documented orbital leiomyoma {15677948;15921744;16033356;16286632;19273932;22384155;28151824;29276593}. The mean age is 38 years (range: 3–74 years) and there is a slight male predominance. There are 4 cases of conjunctival leiomyomas {27994813;27993264;30454719;14516824}, 1 of caruncular leiomyoma {9881563}, and 3 of eyelid leiomyomas {8963938;20966798;23929515}.

Etiology

Not known

Pathogenesis

Orbital leiomyoma is thought to arise from the smooth muscle cells of orbital vessels or from Müller muscle cells {1200096}.

Macroscopic appearance

Orbital leiomyomas are usually firm, well circumscribed but unencapsulated, and lobulated; cut sections typically show a firm, whorled, white surface with solid areas, sometimes with calcification {15677948;14998695}.

Histopathology

Ocular leiomyomas are composed of intersecting fascicles of spindled cells with blunt-ended cigar-shaped nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm. There is no atypia or necrosis.

Immunohistochemistry

Spindle cells are positive for SMA, desmin, and h-caldesmon, and negative for S100 {15677948;14998695;19273932}.

Differential diagnosis

Other benign spindle cell tumours that occur in the orbit should be considered, such as neurofibroma, schwannoma, and solitary fibrous tumour {14998695}.

Cytology

No information has been reported on orbital leiomyoma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: morphology of intersecting fascicles of spindled cells with blunt-ended cigar-shaped nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm; no atypia or necrosis.

Desirable: expression of SMA, h-caldesmon, and desmin; no staining for HMB45, cytokeratin, or S100.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Orbital leiomyomas are benign, but there can be destruction of the orbital bone {22384155} and local recurrence due to incomplete removal {434098;15677948;1200096;11136988}. Two reported cases developed intracranial extension {11136988;1200096}, and one case transformed to a malignant sarcoma possibly due to radiotherapy {1200096}.
