6.1.1.1 Pleomorphic adenoma of the lacrimal gland

Definition

Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is a benign primary lacrimal gland neoplasm consisting of a mixture of epithelial, myoepithelial, and mesenchymal-appearing components.

ICD-O coding

8940/0 Pleomorphic adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH2KC1 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Pleomorphic adenoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: benign mixed tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PA arises in the lacrimal gland, most commonly from the orbital lobe (90%), occasionally from the palpebral lobe {8874433;15632926;17613864;17275570;2158030}, and rarely from accessory lacrimal gland tissue {16530838;29855174}. It may also arise from sweat glands in the eyelid skin {23294914;33292052;34969791}.

Clinical features

PA usually presents as a slow-growing mass causing painless enlargement of the lateral upper eyelid, progressing to proptosis and globe displacement. Some patients experience diplopia and visual impairment {29680804;29755265}.

Imaging

CT and MRI are important to visualize surrounding bone and possible extension to adjacent structures, assisting with the differential diagnosis with malignant lacrimal neoplasms. On MRI, PAs are usually well-circumscribed, round to oval isointense lesions with regular margins, located in the lacrimal fossa {29755265}. Cystic areas, foci of calcification, and bony excavation may be seen {27184391}. There is often bony expansion (remodelling) of the lacrimal fossa without destruction owing to the slowly enlarging mass. Significant differences are observed between primary and recurrent PA in imaging studies. Recurrent PA tends to present with uneven density, unclear boundaries, adjacent bone destruction, and adherence to surrounding tissues {21684609;29680804;34926199}.

Epidemiology

Although PA accounts for only 0.6–2% of all orbital lesions, it is the most common epithelial neoplasm of the lacrimal gland and represents approximately 50–70% of lacrimal gland tumours in most series {19369032;19667339;24145905;24526859}. The mean patient age at presentation is 40 years, with a broad range from childhood {22102506} to the ninth decade of life {35298320;35115759;34926199}. No race, sex, or geographical location predilection has been identified {35115759;34926199;35298320}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Recurrent translocations of PLAG1, an adenoma-associated gene located at chromosome 8q12, result in the overexpression of the PLAG1 oncoprotein in PAs {29437290;29755265}. PLAG1 gene translocation t(5;8)(p13;q12) is highly specific for PA {29755265}. Translocations of HMGA2, located at chromosome 12q14, have also been reported in PAs although to a lesser extent {29755265}.

Macroscopic appearance

PAs are usually well-circumscribed, pseudoencapsulated, round to bosselated masses with a firm, whitish surface with occasional cysts, calcification, or cartilaginous areas {15498065}. Protrusions outside the pseudocapsule and satellite nodules may be observed infrequently. Recurrent PAs tend to be multinodular and multifocal.

Histopathology

The histological hallmark of PA is its morphological diversity because of the relative proportions of each component in each tumour {22471335;33737504;19667339}. The epithelial component is characterized by bilayered ductal structures, and the epithelial cells may show a squamous, clear, round, oncocytic, and/or plasmacytoid appearance. The stromal component, derived from myoepithelial cells, may be fibrous, myxoid, hyaline, chondroid, or osseous, or occasionally show adipose differentiation. The epithelial component predominates in the so-called cellular pattern, and the stromal component predominates in the stroma-rich pattern. The classic pattern has a balanced representation of epithelial and stromal components. Cyst formation and crystals may be present. Extensive squamous and focal mucinous metaplasia may be observed in some tumours. Although a few mitoses are usually present, overt cytological atypia and areas of carcinoma should raise the possibility of malignant transformation (carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma). There is a pseudocapsule, with protrusions of the tumour within it, explaining the tendency of these tumours to recur if not completely excised with surrounding tissue.

Immunohistochemistry

The luminal epithelial cells stain with cytokeratins (CK7 and CAM5.2), while the myoepithelial cells are positive for p40, p63, SMA, and calponin. KIT (CD117) is negative and the proliferative index with Ki-67 immunostain should be very low. Positive staining for PLAG1 (nuclear) is consider specific {23972314;27567603} along with HMGA2 {29437290}, and the tumours may also stain with GFAP {17824792;20614277}.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnoses include basal cell and canalicular adenoma, carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, myoepithelioma, and epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma. Tumours with extensive squamous or mucous metaplasia should be differentiated from squamous cell carcinoma and mucoepidermoid carcinoma.

Cytology

Cytological preparations are characterized by a mixture of small cuboidal epithelial cells in clusters or small sheets, and myoepithelial cells (usually numerous, discohesive, bland, elongated, or plasmacytoid in appearance). Variable amounts of fibrillary and chondromyxoid stroma, metachromatic on Giemsa staining or greyish blue on Pap staining, are observed in the background {12024925;22102506}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant in most cases

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lacrimal fossa mass; round or bosselated tumour with an admixture of bilayered ducts, myoepithelial cells, and chondromyxoid/fibrous stroma in the absence of invasion and malignant cytomorphological features.

Desirable: PLAG1 or HMGA2 alteration in select cases.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Two major factors determining prognosis in lacrimal gland PA are the probability of recurrence as a result of incomplete resection, and evidence of malignant transformation {19667339;34926199}. Biological behaviour is not affected by histological pattern. Multifocal benign recurrences as well as malignant transformation can be observed in lacrimal gland PA after previous incomplete excision and can be challenging to treat surgically. Spontaneous malignant transformation has also been reported {32282452}. Multiple recurrences and longstanding tumours seem to be associated with increased risk of malignant transformation {21684609;29755265;32187769}. Although cases of histologically benign-appearing PA demonstrating metastatic behaviour (benign metastasizing PA) have been described in salivary glands {25958295;31094927}, to date, such cases have not been reported in the lacrimal gland.
