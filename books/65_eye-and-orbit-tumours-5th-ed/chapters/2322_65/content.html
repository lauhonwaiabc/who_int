2.3.2.2 Adenoid cystic carcinoma of the eyelid

Definition

Adenoid cystic carcinoma (AdCC) is a malignant basaloid epithelial/myoepithelial neoplasm arising in the accessory lacrimal glands or sweat glands of the periocular skin with striking propensity for perineural invasion and local infiltration.

ICD-O coding

8200/3 Adenoid cystic carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH4302 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Adenoid cystic carcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Eyelid AdCCs arise in the accessory and ectopic lacrimal glands or sweat glands. They can involve both the upper and the lower eyelids and the periocular skin, with extension into the anterior orbital soft tissue {16897224;19397655;20305522;21629138}.

Clinical features

Eyelid AdCC presents as a slow-growing, non-tender, firm, well-circumscribed nodule with or without distortion or loss of eyelashes {22228272;20305522}. It can masquerade as chalazion {31332148}, sebaceous gland carcinoma {12488275}, or basal cell carcinoma {32118847}.

Epidemiology

The exact incidence of eyelid AdCC is unknown but it is a rare entity. It most commonly occurs in middle-aged or elderly patients with an average age of 60 years. There is a male predilection (M:F ratio: 2.7:1) {22228272;26466235;32118847}. Eyelid AdCC can also arise from secondary spread or metastasis from the lacrimal gland or adjacent skin {22366665}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

About 60% of AdCCs harbour MYB abnormalities, either mutations or structural rearrangements such as MYB::NFIB t(6;9)(q22-q23;p24-p23) {27859477}. This fusion product activates the transcription of genes involved in apoptosis, cell-cycle control, and DNA repair {26076064}. Perineural invasion is suggested to be due to activation of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway by nerve growth factor {31782565}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

AdCC is typically an infiltrating neoplasm composed of variably sized nests of basaloid cells showing a solid, cribriform, and/or tubular growth pattern. The solid areas are composed of small basaloid cells with hyperchromatic, slightly angulated nuclei and scant cytoplasm. The cribriform nests and pseudocystic structures are formed by decohesion of cells forming nests and contain basophilic mucinous material that stains with Alcian blue and/or hyalinized eosinophilic material that stains with PAS. Basaloid material may encapsulate nests and form lumina of pseudoglandular or pseudoductal arrangements. The true ducts are usually sparse. They have small round lumina and are composed of inner epithelial cells with uniform round nuclei with or without small nucleoli surrounded by an outer layer of basal/myoepithelial cells. Intraluminal secretion can be seen. Mitotic activity is usually low {24025525}. Perineural invasion is common {32118847}.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic cells show diffuse cytoplasmic positivity for broad-spectrum keratins, CAM5.2, and CK7. Staining for CK5/6 and with D2-40 is variable. The basaloid and myoepithelial cells display nuclear staining for SOX10. The luminal cells surrounding the true ducts often stain positive for EMA, CEA, and CK15. The myoepithelial component is highlighted by staining for SMA, p63, and calponin. Most tumours express MYB and are positive for KIT (CD117) {28098399;26076064}.

Differential diagnosis

In the eyelid, the major differential diagnosis is with other cribriform tumours. The solid pattern can be difficult to distinguish from basal cell carcinoma in small biopsies. Cytology

None

Diagnostic molecular pathology

MYB alterations or overexpression can help to distinguish AdCC from other basaloid neoplasms with similar appearance {33165092}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: hyperchromatic, angulated nuclei; mixture of tubular, cribriform, and solid patterns associated with basophilic matrix and reduplicated basement membrane material.

Desirable: perineural invasion; immunohistochemical expression of MYB and KIT (CD117).

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system for non-melanoma eyelid carcinoma applies.

Prognosis and prediction

Local control of AdCC is very difficult. AdCC has a propensity for local recurrence, which can occur many years after the initial presentation. Metastases are rare. Curative therapy may require wide excision and adjuvant radiotherapy if perineural invasion is identified. Radiation therapy may be associated with significant morbidity in the periocular region, but with careful planning it can be done in the lower eyelid {23446295}. In the upper eyelid, high dose radiation therapy is associated with significant toxicity to the ocular structures, particularly the cornea {23446295;22228272;19033839}.
