10.1.4.3 Leiomyosarcoma

Definition

Leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm of smooth muscle origin.

ICD-O coding

8890/3 Leiomyosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B58.Y & XH7ED4 Leiomyosarcoma, other specified primary site & Leiomyosarcoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ocular LMS is rare, with few cases described arising from the soft tissue of the conjunctival stroma {1961645;21574810;26214581;28573999;31559244} or the orbit {1200096;6626011;10202288;12614772;16304526;19300170;30326763;30601462}.

Clinical features

Conjunctival lesions present as a raised, fleshy, pale to hyperaemic growth that may extend from fornix to limbus. Orbital lesions present as painless mass lesions, occasionally with proptosis {19300170}.

Epidemiology

Ocular LMS is very rare, has a male predilection, and arises across a wide age range with a peak incidence in the seventh decade of life {1961645;21574810;21718409;24093170;26214581;28573999;28835829;31559244}.

Etiology

Risk factors include hereditary retinoblastoma, radiation exposure, and Li–Fraumeni syndrome {12610106;21837677;29165710;29220301;6626009}.

Pathogenesis

Soft tissue LMSs display extensive genomic instability, including chromothripsis and whole-genome duplication in subsets of cases {29321523}, with a modest burden of somatic mutations and substantial mutation heterogeneity {29100075;29321523}. Recurrent DNA copy-number alterations include losses involving the tumour suppressors PTEN (10q23.31), RB1 (13q14.2), and TP53 (17p13.1), as well as amplification of MYOCD at 17p12, which encodes a smooth muscle–specific transcriptional coactivator {9736047;10616536;11284036;15934046;18649996;21125665;29100075;29321523}. Ocular LMSs have yet to be investigated.

Macroscopic appearance

LMSs appear soft, fleshy, and red to pink in colour. Areas of necrosis may be seen.

Histopathology

LMSs are cellular tumours containing spindle to cigar-shaped cells with moderate eosinophilic cytoplasm arranged in fascicles, sheets, and bundles. Nuclear pleomorphism is typically found easily, although it may be absent or focal in some cases. Mitotic figures are generally abundant, including atypical forms. Giant cells may be seen.

For further details, see the related section in the Soft tissue and bone tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft Tissue and Bone Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]].

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells are positive for at least one smooth muscle marker (i.e. SMA, MSA, desmin, or caldesmon) in all cases, and most cases are positive for two or more. LMS may be positive, often focally, for cytokeratins, EMA, and CD34. LMSs are negative for S100 and MYOD1.

Cytology

Cytological features have not been described for ocular leiomyosarcomas.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fascicles of spindle cells with variable pleomorphism; positivity for SMA, MSA, desmin, or caldesmon by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Conjunctival LMSs have a favourable prognosis perhaps due to their small size and early stage at presentation {31559244}. A minority of orbital LMSs show local recurrence and rare cases develop metastasis {30601462}.
