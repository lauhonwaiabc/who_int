4.3.1.3 Reactive epithelial hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium

Definition

Reactive epithelial hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium is an increase in the number and volume of the ciliary epithelial cells, usually in response to injury or inflammation.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A90.Y Other specified degeneration of iris or ciliary body

Related terminology

Not recommended: pseudoadenomatous hyperplasia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Reactive epithelial hyperplasia occurs over the pars plana or pars plicata of the ciliary body, often with extension into the adjacent posterior chamber or vitreous base.

Clinical features

Reactive epithelial hyperplasia is typically an incidental finding in eyes enucleated because of other substantial pathology. It is almost always clinically obscured by opaque media of the diseased eye.

Epidemiology

The incidence is unknown. Reactive epithelial hyperplasia is seen mostly in eyes that have been subject to significant injury.

Etiology

Reactive epithelial hyperplasia of the ciliary body occurs in reaction to physical injury, surgery including cryotherapy, inflammation including pars planitis {31997032}, and longstanding retinal detachment. It occurs fairly often in end-stage, blind, phthisical eyes {29902388}.

Pathogenesis

Reactive hyperplasia may be induced by growth factors released during and after the inciting insult.

Macroscopic appearance

Although generally obscured by opaque refractive media of the diseased eye, reactive epithelial hyperplasia, typically with accompanying fibrous metaplasia, may form a cyclitic membrane: a variably pigmented fibrous sheet that extends from the ciliary body behind and around the lens.

Histopathology

The hyperplastic ciliary epithelium either proliferates as cords and strands or forms pseudoacinar structures. The proliferating cells derive predominantly from the inner, non-pigmented layer of the ciliary epithelium, but cells from the outer, pigmented epithelial layer may also contribute. The cells are entrapped within a variable amount of fibrous stroma, induced by the underlying insult, and they may appear stretched within this fibrous tissue. Together, the reactively proliferating epithelial cells and fibrous stroma may form a tumour-like mass or, more commonly, a diffuse cyclitic membrane over the ciliary body, extending towards the lens [[Schachat AP, Wilkinson CP, Hinton DR, et al., editors. Cytology

The proliferating cells vary in shape but show bland cytological features, minimal mitotic activity, and no invasive features.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not available

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: predominantly non-pigmented ciliary epithelial cells with bland cytological features that proliferate as cords and strands or form pseudoacinar structures, forming a membrane together with associated fibrous metaplasia.

Desired: a phthisical eye after past injury or inflammation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for vision depends on the inciting pathology and its effect on the eye. These proliferations generally become increasingly fibrous with time after the inciting pathology has subsided. It is suspected that a longstanding reactive proliferation of the ciliary epithelium in a phthisical eye may occasionally give rise to an adenocarcinoma of the ciliary body {25545484;25015166;6995386;5661889;9917789;30976584}.
