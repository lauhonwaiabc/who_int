2.3.2.1 Sebaceous carcinoma

Definition

Sebaceous carcinoma is a malignant neoplasm demonstrating sebaceous differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8410/3 Sebaceous carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH4VR2 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Sebaceous carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: Meibomian gland carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Sebaceous carcinoma occurs most commonly in and around the eyelids {7076199}. Periocular sebaceous carcinoma accounts for 75% of all cases {15749305;24227981;7076199;11346408;14663642;17728556}.

Clinical features

Periocular sebaceous carcinoma presents as a tan-pink-yellow immobile nodular lesion (10–30 mm). It may be mistaken clinically for benign inflammatory or neoplastic conditions, leading to higher morbidity and mortality {8958530;10218701;6465419;24227981;32788569}. Pagetoid sebaceous carcinoma may resemble chronic blepharoconjunctivitis {24227981}.

Epidemiology

Sebaceous carcinoma develops in older adults, with no sex predilection. The median age at diagnosis is 73 years. The estimated incidence (per 1 million person-years) is approximately 2 cases in White populations, 1 case in Asian and Pacific Islander populations, and 0.5 cases in Black populations {18988294}.

Etiology

Risk factors include prior radiotherapy, sun exposure, TP53 mutations, viral infections (HIV and HPV), and Muir–Torre syndrome {24227981;9708943}.

Pathogenesis

Periocular sebaceous carcinoma can harbour mutations in TP53, RB1, the NOTCH gene family, and ZNF750, as well as gain of the MYC locus {27287813;34445161}. A small subset are related to high-risk HPV infection {30420449;33745190;29760388;34023105}. While some extraocular sebaceous carcinomas are related to Muir–Torre syndrome, with most showing defective mismatch repair {27287813;31148199}, periocular sebaceous carcinomas rarely show defective mismatch repair {27287813;31148199;19515040;24321472;25427047;28323777;20055911}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Well-differentiated sebaceous carcinoma consists of polygonal tumour cells with atypical oval vesicular nuclei and abundant intracytoplasmic vacuoles that impart a foamy appearance and impinge on the nucleus creating scalloping. More poorly differentiated tumours show a higher N:C ratio, nuclear pleomorphism, prominent nucleoli, and basophilic cytoplasm, with fewer intracellular vacuoles {28323778} and brisk mitotic activity. Sebaceous carcinoma may show basaloid, squamoid (often with keratin pearl formation), spindle-cell/sarcomatoid, or neuroendocrine (rippled or organoid growth) features {6465419;15900121;25521100}.

Invasive tumours can adopt various architectural patterns, including well-circumscribed lobules with pushing borders and variable comedonecrosis, papillary, and highly infiltrative cords. Combinations of these patterns are often observed.

Approximately 60% of cases carry an intraepithelial component (pagetoid spread) with extension along and partial replacement of the pre-existing pilosebaceous units in the skin, and the conjunctival epithelium. Notably, intraepithelial skip lesions may also occur {16008694} and sebaceous carcinoma in situ (restricted to the epithelium) may be encountered {24666211;20966738}.

Immunohistochemistry

Sebaceous carcinoma shows immunoreactivity for pancytokeratin (and also CK7). Staining is variably positive for adipophilin, EMA, AR, p40, and p63, and with BerEP4 {29087003;28256768;20118912}. EMA labelling may enhance the cytoplasmic vacuolization of the tumour cells, but it is often only positive in well-differentiated tumours and lacks specificity. Adipophilin is a more sensitive and specific marker of sebaceous differentiation {20118912;29087003}. Positivity for CD10, DOG1, KIT (CD117), p16, p53, perforin, and factor XIIIa has also been reported in sebaceous carcinoma {25864700;28394798;26843360;27171611}.

Differential diagnosis

Sebaceous carcinoma should be excluded when examining basaloid, squamoid, and clear cell epithelial tumours in a periocular location. The differential diagnosis includes sebaceoma, balloon cell melanoma, clear cell squamous cell carcinoma, basal cell carcinoma, Merkel cell carcinoma, and metastatic clear cell carcinomas from visceral organs.

Cytology

None

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an invasive or intraepithelial tumour showing sebaceous differentiation, manifesting as multivesicular cytoplasmic clearing with nuclear scalloping.

Desirable: immunohistochemical confirmation of sebaceous differentiation is critical in cases lacking obvious histopathological evidence of sebaceous differentiation.

Staging

The eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system forms the basis for staging. The application of the eighth edition of the AJCC staging system was found useful in predicting nodal metastasis; however, local recurrence and distant metastasis were not significantly correlated to the T classification {32567368}.

Prognosis and prediction

Eyelid sebaceous carcinoma has a 5–25% local tumour recurrence rate after surgery {30865067}. According to the eighth edition of the UICC/AJCC TNM system, risk factors for local recurrence include stage T3b (> 20 mm) or worse disease, pagetoid spread, diffuse growth pattern, and multicentric origin. The literature review shows that surgical excision with frozen section control is the optimal modality, and any pagetoid spread is treated with topical chemotherapy and cryotherapy {30865067}.

According to the eighth edition of the UICC/AJCC TNM system, the 5-year Kaplan–Meier estimate rate for regional lymph node metastasis varies between 9% (T1) and 42% (T4), the rate for systemic metastasis varies between 0% (T1) and 34% (T4), and the rate for metastasis-related death varies between 0% (T1) and 43% (T4) {35347292}.
