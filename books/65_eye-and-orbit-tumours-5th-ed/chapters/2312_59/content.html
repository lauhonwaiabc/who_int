2.3.1.2 Syringoma

Definition

Syringoma is a benign adnexal tumour of eccrine sweat gland origin.

ICD-O coding

8407/0 Syringoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH6325 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Syringoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most syringomas arise in the lower eyelids and periorbital area {8599451}.

Clinical features

Syringoma presents as skin-coloured or slightly yellow smooth papules. Multiple syringomas may resemble milia cysts. When the lesions are pigmented, they may simulate urticaria pigmentosa or confluent and reticulated papillomatosis of Gougerot–Carteaud.

Epidemiology

Syringomas most commonly affect the lower eyelids of middle-aged to older women, but the eruptive pattern is most common in young women {11683289}. Clear cell syringoma is more common in people with diabetes.

Etiology

Syringomas are common in patients with Down syndrome {7971561}, and they have also been described in patients with hyperthyroidism, Ehlers–Danlos syndromes, Marfan syndrome, and Nicolau–Balus syndrome {7262384}. In familial forms, multiple syringomas affect several family members.

Pathogenesis

Syringomas resemble the ductal portion of the eccrine gland.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

Syringomas are well-circumscribed lesions typically affecting the upper half of the dermis; occasionally they can extend into the deep dermis or even the subcutis. Syringoma has two components: epithelial cells forming ducts, cords, tubules, and cysts, and a homogeneous and sclerotic stroma. The lumina of the ductal and tubular structures are lined by a double layer of cuboidal cells, sometimes containing a granular basophilic material. The ducts often show characteristic extensions resembling a tadpole tail. Solid collections of epithelial cells are also observed, often containing multiple confluent cytoplasmic vacuoles. Small cysts are often present, filled with basket-weave, basophilic, and orthokeratotic keratin and lined by squamous epithelium. The stroma consists of thick bundles of compact and sclerotic collagen. In eruptive syringoma, the stroma is less prominent.

Differential diagnosis

In superficial biopsies, the differential diagnosis includes microcystic adnexal carcinoma, sclerosing/morphoeic basal cell carcinoma, and desmoplastic trichoepithelioma / columnar trichoblastoma, from which distinction may be impossible without complete removal and clinicopathological correlation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: skin-coloured or slightly yellow smooth papules; tubular and cystic structures with circumscribed architecture and benign cytology, embedded in a sclerotic stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Syringomas are benign lesions. Low-energy radio frequency ablation and carbon dioxide laser have been used with good outcomes and are less invasive and associated with better aesthetic outcomes {22777235}.
