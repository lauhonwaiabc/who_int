10.1.7.2 Myopericytoma, including myofibroma

Definition

Myopericytoma is a perivascular myoid neoplasm on a continuum with myofibroma.

ICD-O coding

8824/0 Myofibroma

8824/1 Multicentric myofibromatosis

8824/0 Myopericytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH2HE9 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Myopericytoma

2F36.Y & XH0953 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Myofibroma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Myofibroma; myofibromatosis; infantile myofibromatosis

Localization

Ophthalmic myopericytoma usually arises in the dermis or subcutis of the eyelid {33089011}, and rarely the orbit {25931311;33089011;33632050}. Paediatric myofibroma may involve orbital or periorbital sites, with frequent involvement of periorbital bones and the skull base, particularly in the setting of infantile myofibromatosis {3538883;3207313;8573030;9307643;9823360;16855503;21512784}.

Clinical features

Tumours usually occur as a painless mass, sometimes with ptosis or eye displacement {21512784}.

Epidemiology

Solitary cases predominate in adults, with a male predominance {16330949}; multifocal myofibromatosis arises in paediatric patients, most often at birth or in the first 2 years of life, and has a female predominance {7284977;8573030}.

Etiology

Infantile myofibromatosis is familial, with reports of both autosomal dominant {12894106;3236168;21834820} and recessive {11260217} inheritance.

Pathogenesis

PDGFRB mutations are common {28505006;27776010}, with germline mutations seen in familial cases {23731537;23731542}. Cellular myofibromas may demonstrate recurrent SRF rearrangement {28248815;31478943;33021523}.

Macroscopic appearance

Myopericytomas are usually circumscribed, firm, and homogeneous. Orbital cases of infantile myofibroma average 30 mm (range: 2–100 mm) with frequent bone destruction {21512784}.

Histopathology

Myopericytomas are unencapsulated, circumscribed, variably cellular, nodular lesions composed of myoid spindle cells concentrically arranged around prominent vasculature {16330949}. Myofibromas are similar but often demonstrate a biphasic pattern, with central hypercellularity showing plump immature-appearing spindle cells, and peripheral nodular growth showing variable hyalinization {21512784}. “Cellular myofibroma/myopericytoma” refers to hypercellular and hyperproliferative lesions with infiltrative growth but lacking frankly malignant features including pleomorphism or necrosis {28248815}; these have not been described in ophthalmic cases.

Myopericytomas/myofibromas express SMA and are often negative for desmin (except SRF-rearranged lesions). H-caldesmon may be expressed by both, although less consistently in myofibromas.

Cytology

Cytology is not reported for ophthalmic cases.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PDGFRB point mutations or SRF rearrangement can be diagnostically supportive in the appropriate context.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for myopericytoma: bland myoid spindle cells with perivascular concentric growth; for myofibroma: biphasic pattern with immature hypercellular areas surrounded by a less cellular myoid population.

Desirable (in select cases): SMA immunopositivity; PDGFRB point mutations or SRF rearrangements.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Myopericytomas are clinically indolent, although the rare SRF-rearranged tumours can behave aggressively {31478943}. Solitary myofibromas likewise have an excellent prognosis, with frequent spontaneous regression; occasionally they recur locally after incomplete excision. Cases of multicentric myofibromatosis, particularly those with visceral involvement, have a significantly worse outcome {21512784}.
