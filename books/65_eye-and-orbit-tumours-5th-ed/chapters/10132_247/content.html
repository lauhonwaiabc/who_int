10.1.3.2 Rhabdomyosarcomas

Definition

Rhabdomyosarcoma is a group of malignant mesenchymal tumours with skeletal muscle differentiation, which consists of four types: embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS), alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS), spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma, and pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma. Ectomesenchymoma consists of rhabdomyosarcoma intermixed with ganglionic/neuroblastic components.

Related terminology

Not recommended: sarcoma botryoides.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ocular rhabdomyosarcoma can develop within the orbit, conjunctiva, eyelid, or uveal tract (iris, ciliary body). The most common location is the orbit, with a predilection for the superior nasal quadrant {12559326;15377992}.

Clinical features

Proptosis of rapid onset, eyelid swelling, and blepharoptosis are the main presenting features of orbital rhabdomyosarcoma {11733272}. A rapidly growing conjunctival mass is the characteristic presentation of conjunctival rhabdomyosarcoma {12559326}.

Epidemiology

Orbital rhabdomyosarcoma makes up approximately 10% of all rhabdomyosarcoma cases {11733272}. ERMS and ARMS are the predominant types, and ERMS accounts for the vast majority. Patients with ERMS are typically children < 10 years in age, whereas those with ARMS are most often adolescents and young adults {9142207;12559326;15377992}. Etiology

Most cases of rhabdomyosarcoma are sporadic, but certain genetic conditions can increase the risk, particularly for ERMS, such as Li–Fraumeni syndrome (germline TP53 mutation) or RASopathies (e.g. neurofibromatosis–Noonan syndrome) {17420382;11984810;12679651}.

Pathogenesis

ERMS commonly harbours whole-chromosome gains (of 2, 8, 11, 12, 13, and/or 20) and losses (of 10 and 15), and loss of heterozygosity at 11p15.5. Mutations in the RAS pathway (NRAS, KRAS, HRAS, NF1, FGFR4), the PI3K pathway (PTEN, PIK3CA), or the cell-cycle regulatory genes (FBXW7, CTNNB1) are frequent in ERMS {24436047;26349418;30617281;34755412}.

In ARMS, t(2;13) or t(1;13) rearrangement produces PAX3::FOXO1 or PAX7::FOXO1 fusion genes, encoding transcription factors that activate the PAX3 and/or PAX7 target genes {8098985;8187070;8275086;30617281}.

Ectomesenchymoma shares molecular similarities with ERMS and frequently carries HRAS mutations {26872011}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most lesions present as poorly circumscribed, soft, fleshy, gelatinous masses with a pale-tan cut surface. Botryoid ERMS has a grape-like polypoid appearance.

Histopathology

ERMS consists of atypical primitive round and spindle-shaped tumour cells set in a variably myxoid background. Large differentiated rhabdomyoblasts with bright eosinophilic cytoplasm can be observed. Mitotic figures are often numerous, and necrosis can be seen. Botryoid ERMS grows as a submucosal polypoid mass with a cambium layer (cellular aggregates beneath the surface). Anaplastic ERMS is recognized by pronounced nuclear pleomorphism and/or enlarged atypical mitoses {18985676}.

ARMS is composed of monomorphic small round to ovoid cells with scant cytoplasm organized into an alveolar architectural pattern or arranged in solid sheets and nests.

Ectomesenchymoma has features of ERMS intermixed with ganglioneuroblastomatous elements.

Immunohistochemistry

All types of rhabdomyosarcomas express desmin, myogenin, and MYOD1 {16861966}. Myogenin expression tends to be more diffuse in ARMS than in ERMS.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of PAX::FOXO1 fusion is particularly useful for classification of rhabdomyosarcoma with a round cell phenotype.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma

Essential: mitotically active, primitive spindle to round cells; alternating cellular and myxoid areas; heterogeneous myogenin expression.

Desirable (in select cases): absence of PAX3, PAX7, or FOXO1 rearrangement in tumours morphologically reminiscent of ARMS.

Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma

Essential: malignant small blue round cell neoplasm; homogeneous myogenin expression; PAX3, PAX7, or FOXO1 rearrangement.

Ectomesenchymoma

Essential: composite ERMS with neuroblastic elements; immunolabelling for desmin or myogenin and synaptophysin.

Staging

Staging for paediatric rhabdomyosarcoma is based on the Intergroup Rhabdomyosarcoma Study Group (IRSG) system. The orbit is a favourable site, and tumours confined to the orbit are considered stage I {22378628;30617281}.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with ocular ERMS have excellent outcomes {9142207;27473389;11733272}. The prognosis is worse for patients with anaplastic ERMS and FOXO1 fusion–positive ARMS {8470759;30617281;26756883;31146616;32800827}.
