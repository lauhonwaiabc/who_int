9.4.0.1 Myeloid sarcoma

Definition

Myeloid sarcoma (MS) is a tumour mass involving any anatomical site other than bone marrow (i.e. extramedullary) that effaces tissue architecture and is composed of myeloid blasts, with or without maturation.

ICD-O coding

9930/3 Myeloid sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2A60.39 Myeloid sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: chloroma; granulocytic/monocytic sarcoma; myeloblastoma; myelocytoma; chloroleukaemia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ocular adnexal MS (OA-MS) mainly arises in the orbital soft tissue where it moulds around the orbital structures. It may rarely involve the eyelid skin, conjunctiva, and lacrimal drainage system {24556444;26455987;28892915;29503920;30144455;32775766}.

Clinical features

OA-MS is reported in 2–9% of patients with acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) at diagnosis or relapse. It can rarely precede AML onset. MS may also be a manifestation of secondary AML transformation from myelodysplastic neoplasms, myeloproliferative neoplasms, or myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms {17170724;28892915;31467130;32577912}. De novo isolated MS with morphologically normal bone marrow is rare, occurring in about 1% of AML patients {28892915;34346084;34380367;34625114}.

OA-MS usually presents with unilateral proptosis and diplopia with/without pain, eyelid swelling, chemosis, and/or exposure keratopathy {12523908;24556444;26455987;29503920;30144455;32775766}.

Epidemiology

MS is more common in children and young adults (median age 7–12 years) but can occur at any age, with a slight male preponderance {12523908;24556444;26455987;28892915;29503920;30144455;32775766;34625114}.

Etiology

The etiology is similar to that of AML. The reasons for homing and clustering outside the haematopoietic system as an extramedullary mass are still unknown. OA-MS may originate from an involved area of adjacent bone/periosteum {16450777;26455987;28892915}. Adhesion molecules (e.g. ITGA7), chemokine receptor/ligand interactions, and extracellular matrix interactions are suggested as being involved {20582977;22388727;32033262}.

Pathogenesis

MS shares similar molecular abnormalities with AML, including t(8;21) (RUNX1::RUNX1T1), inv(16) (CBFB::MYH11), trisomy 4 and 8, monosomy 7, and deletions of 5q and 20q {17170724;23530613}. Some of these aberrations may be associated with particular involved sites, with t(8;21) often involving the orbital region in children {17170724;31671434;23530613}.

Other molecular abnormalities include gene mutations in NPM1, KMT2A, and FLT3-ITD, and in RAS pathway (~50%) and epigenetic pathway genes, and signalling, transcription, and tumour suppressor genes {27659839;28633614;29789584;33232718;34380367}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour presents as a diffuse infiltration and enlargement of the involved site with a fish-flesh grey/white cut surface, or as a green mass due to myeloperoxidase expressed by granulocytic cells.

Histopathology

MS diffusely effaces tissue architecture and comprises neoplastic cells as islands or cords or in a single-file pattern, with intervening delicate fibrous stroma. Brisk mitoses, spotty necrosis, and crush artefacts are frequently noted {26455987}. Tumour cells are intermediate-sized to large, with scant to moderate amounts of pale or eosinophilic cytoplasm, round-oval nuclei with irregular membranes, fine (blastic) chromatin, and usually small nucleoli {17170724;23530613;26185307}. Tumours may exhibit granulocytic (eosinophilic myelocytes and metamyelocytes), monocytic (cells with folded nuclei promonocytes), or myelomonocytic differentiation.

Immunophenotype

Tumour cells express CD13, CD33, CD43, CD68, and myeloperoxidase; CD45 is positive in 60% of cases. Tumour cells are CD34+ and KIT+ in tumours with an immature granulocytic profile, whereas they are CD34− in tumours with a mature profile. CD11b, CD11c, CD15, CD64, KIT (~30%), and lysozyme are expressed in tumours with immature monocytic cells / monoblasts, whereas expression of CD14, CD68, and CD163 is variable in those with a mature monocytic profile {7668936;11698000;17170724;12523908;24556444;26185307;28574302;29503920;32775766}. CD61 and glycophorin A are expressed in tumours with megakaryocytic and erythroid differentiation, respectively. MS may express B-cell, T-cell, or NK-cell markers, such as CD19, CD7, and CD56, respectively. CD123, CD99, and TdT (weak) can be expressed in subsets {17170724;26185307}.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis includes lymphoblastic lymphomas, small blue round cell tumours, histiocytic/dendritic neoplasms, orbital cellulitis {12523908;24390220;26185307;31261288;32591154;24556444;26455987;28892915;29503920;30144455;32775766}, and extramedullary haematopoiesis {9760159;17170724;17334344}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MS is equivalent to a diagnosis of AML {26185307;31682773}. Comprehensive evaluation is needed for correct classification, prognostication, and therapy planning.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: effacement of tissue architecture by a mass comprising myeloblasts of varying maturation; positive expression of granulocytic and/or monocytic markers by tumour cells.

Desirable: complete workup, including cytogenetic and molecular analyses, is suggested in patients with de novo MS.

Staging

Evaluation of peripheral blood and bone marrow for AML or other myeloid neoplasms (myelodysplastic neoplasms, myeloproliferative neoplasms, or myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms) is essential.

Prognosis and prediction

Recommended therapy for MS is AML-type chemotherapy, guided by targets identified by cytogenetic and molecular analyses, as per the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN). Identification of specific genetic aberrations improves diagnosis and prognostication, and provides therapeutic targets. Radiotherapy can be used in select cases.

The prognosis for patients with MS may be similar {25213180;28556258;29043233}, better {18623376;29043233;34346084;34380367}, or worse {17170724;31866570;33232718} when compared with that for patients with AML and their mutation profiles. Patients with isolated MS appear to have a better prognosis and longer remission.
