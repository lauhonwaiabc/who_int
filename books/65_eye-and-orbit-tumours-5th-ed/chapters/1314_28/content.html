1.3.1.4 Blue naevus of the conjunctiva

Definition

Blue naevus is a stromal melanocytic tumour composed of dendritic, spindle, and/or ovoid cells associated with melanin pigment and stromal sclerosis; it has a blue tinctorial appearance clinically.

Subtype(s)

Dendritic blue naevus; cellular blue naevus

Localization

The distribution is relatively even throughout the bulbar, forniceal, and palpebral conjunctiva and the caruncle {20622349;18191656;20940655;14769591}.

Clinical features

Blue naevi present as dark-brown to black, sharply demarcated, unifocal or multifocal, slightly elevated conjunctival masses, with a thickness of approximately 2 mm {18191656}. Unlike common acquired conjunctival naevi, blue naevi lack epithelial cysts and infrequently contain intrinsic or feeder vessels {18191656;14769591}.

Epidemiology

Blue naevi represent < 4% of conjunctival and caruncular naevi, occur with a slightly increased predilection in women, and are more common in White people {20622349;8883545;14769591;29551421}. Conjunctival blue naevi are thought to be congenital, although most of them are diagnosed in the third to fifth decades of life {20622349;18191656;8883545;14769591}.

Etiology

Blue naevi are considered neural-crest lesions derived from primitive Schwann cell and melanocyte precursors. In contrast to common acquired naevi and cutaneous melanomas, ultraviolet (UV) sun light irradiation is not an important etiological factor {24460190}. Familial/syndromic aggregation exists, such as in LAMB syndrome (lentigines, atrial myxomas, mucocutaneous myxomas, and blue naevi) or NAME syndrome (naevi, atrial myxomas, myxoid neurofibromas, and ephelides) of the Carney complex {21366456;19851128}.

Pathogenesis

Blue naevi and intradermal melanocytic proliferations (melanocytic neoplasms of the uvea and leptomeninges) probably arise from a distinct type of melanocyte that originates from the neural crest through the ventromedial developmental pathway arresting in the conjunctival substantia propria {24460190;26300491}. Although less well described than in cutaneous blue naevi, driver mutations in G protein–coupled receptors (especially those in GNAQ) have also been reported in conjunctival blue naevi {29332123;35258412}.

Histopathology

Dendritic (common) blue naevi comprise heavily pigmented bland spindle/dendritic/stellate melanocytic cells in the conjunctival substantia propria {29551421;18191656;6677846;2657539;19667333}. The melanocytic cells show slender, branching networks of dendritic processes, frequently associated with melanophages and stromal fibrosis {29551421;18191656;6677846;2657539}. No cellular atypia is present.

Cellular blue naevi contain dense aggregates of cytologically bland, pigmented spindle cells and/or ovoid, variably pigmented melanocytic cells, located in the substantia propria, arranged as sheets or fascicles, surrounded by collagen and melanophages, frequently set in a background of common blue naevus {29551421;1454347;11074819;2657539}.

In both benign blue naevus subtypes, mitotic figures are absent {2657539}. There is no associated junctional melanocytic proliferation or entrapped epithelial cysts, except in combined or collision lesions such as combined melanocytic naevus. These show features of both conventional melanocytic and blue naevi {10703123;20683369}. The histological differential diagnosis is covered in #32672Table 2.02.

Immunohistochemistry

The spindle/dendritic/stellate melanocytic cells are positive for S100, melan-A, and SOX10, and they can be positive for HMB45 antigen {29551421}. The melanophages are CD68 positive {29551421}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology may be helpful in occasional difficult cases (see Pathogenesis).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Dendritic blue naevus

Essential: heavily pigmented, loosely arranged, non-atypical spindle/stellate/dendritic cells within the conjunctival substantia propria; the melanocytic cells do not show pleomorphism, nuclear atypia, or hyperchromasia.

Desirable: sclerotic stroma with melanophages; immunohistochemical positivity with the melanocytic markers melan-A, SOX10, and S100; CD68 positivity of melanophages.

Cellular blue naevus

Essential: sheets or fascicles of variably pigmented spindle to ovoid cells surrounded by sclerotic stroma and numerous melanophages; the melanocytic cells do not show pleomorphism, nuclear atypia, or hyperchromasia.

Desirable: presence of dendritic blue naevus features surrounding cellular blue naevus; immunohistochemical positivity with the melanocytic markers melan-A, SOX10, S100; CD68 positivity of melanophages.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for blue naevi is excellent. Blue naevi (particularly the cellular subtype) of the conjunctiva have very rarely been described to give rise to conjunctival melanoma, which has been described in just 1 case after acquisition of BAP1 loss {11074819;35258412}.
