10.1.7.1 Glomus tumour

Definition

Glomus tumour is a mesenchymal neoplasm showing differentiation analogous to perivascular cells of the glomus body.

ICD-O coding

8711/0 Glomus tumour, NOS

8711/1 Glomus tumour of uncertain malignant potential

8711/3 Glomus tumour, malignant

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH47J2 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Glomus tumour, NOS

2D0Y & XH21E6 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Glomus tumour, malignant

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Glomangioma; symplastic glomus tumour; malignant glomus tumour

Localization

Tumours may arise in the soft tissue or bone {17036559;3019113;32604167}. The orbit and eyelid are rarely involved {32962404;14270138}.

Clinical features

Pain is a common manifestation {17027805}. Eye tumours may present with blindness and/or proptosis {18447675;23216695}.

Epidemiology

Glomus tumours rarely involve the eye; they have been reported in children and adults, with no clear sex predilection {14270138;18447675;22642677}.

Etiology

Most tumours are sporadic. A minority result from a germline GLMN {11845407} or NF1 mutation {19991403;19738042}.

Pathogenesis

Sporadic non-ophthalmic tumours frequently harbour gene fusions involving NOTCH1, NOTCH2, or NOTCH3, with multiple potential partners (e.g. MIR143, CARMN) {23999936;32604167;34245196}; fusions are less common in malignant tumours {32604167}. A minority have mutations of BRAF {22317887;28834810} or KRAS {22317887}. The molecular pathogenesis appears to vary based on anatomical location. These molecular features are based on non-ophthalmic tumours; however, there is no reason to suspect a difference from those arising elsewhere in the body.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are typically small and circumscribed. They may appear purple-blue, and solid or cystic {21371130}.

Histopathology

Morphologically tumours are composed of sheets of round to polygonal cells. The cytoplasm is eosinophilic to amphophilic with well-defined membranes. The nuclei are centrally located, round to ovoid, and monomorphic; mitotic activity is inconspicuous. Tumours have a delicate capillary-sized vasculature. The intervening stroma may be myxoid to collagenous; glomangioma is additionally characterized by ectatic and branching vascular spaces. Symplastic tumours contain marked nuclear pleomorphism, but no mitotic activity. Malignant tumours are classified based on: (1) atypical mitotic figures, (2) nuclear pleomorphism and high mitotic activity, or (3) size > 20 mm and deep anatomical location {11145243}.

Immunohistochemistry is typically positive for SMA and h-caldesmon, with variable staining for desmin and CD34.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

NOTCH family rearrangement or BRAF or KRAS mutation, in the appropriate clinical and histomorphological context, may be diagnostically helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: sheets of round-polygonal cells with well-defined cell borders, and monomorphic round central nuclei.

Desirable: immunoreactivity for SMA or h-caldesmon; NOTCH family rearrangement or mutations involving BRAF or KRAS in malignant tumours.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Conventional glomus tumours are benign. Malignant tumours are locally aggressive with the potential for metastasis {11145243;33133513}.
