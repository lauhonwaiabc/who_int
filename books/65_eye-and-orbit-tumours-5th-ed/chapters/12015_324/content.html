12.0.1.5 Neurofibromatosis type 1

Definition

Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by a pathogenic germline variant of NF1.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

162200 Neurofibromatosis type 1; NF1

ICD-11 coding

LD2D.10 Neurofibromatosis type 1

Related terminology

Not recommended: Von Recklinghausen disease; peripheral neurofibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

Mosaic neurofibromatosis type 1, including segmental neurofibromatosis type 1; spinal neurofibromatosis; neurofibromatosis–Noonan syndrome; 17q11.2 microdeletion syndrome; orbitofacial neurofibromatosis type 1

Localization

NF1 affects many different cell types and tissues in the body, predominantly localizing to central and peripheral nervous system sites.

Clinical features

Multiple café-au-lait macules are usually present at birth, and increase in number during the first 2 years of life. Skinfold freckling in the axillary, inguinal, and submammary regions occurs in > 80% of adults with NF1. Lisch nodules and skinfold freckling are usually detectable before puberty. Cutaneous neurofibromas typically develop during puberty and are identified in > 85% of adults {28230061}. In contrast, plexiform neurofibromas are considered congenital, developing in 30–50% of children with NF1 {2823006}. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours (MPNSTs) are extremely uncommon in the periocular tissues {28235631}. Lisch nodules are the most common ocular manifestations, usually detected before puberty. Other ocular/orbital manifestations include optic pathway gliomas and sphenoid wing dysplasia {19410195}. Orbitofacial neurofibromatosis type 1 (OFNF) has been historically studied as a clinical form of NF1 {22193879}. OFNF is often characterized by progressive, large disfiguring neurofibromas involving the orbit and periorbital tissues, with associated remodelling of adjacent skull bones. Recurrence after excision is relatively high, but tumour growth may stabilize as the individual ages. Progression to MPNST is extremely rare {28235631}.

Epidemiology

NF1 has a birth incidence of approximately 1 per 3000 {25354145}.

Etiology

NF1 is a highly penetrant disorder caused by heterozygous pathogenic loss-of-function variants in the tumour suppressor gene NF1, located at chromosome 17q11.2 and encoding neurofibromin (NF1) {2134734;2114220;1694727}. More than 3000 different disease-causing variants, including deletions and duplications, have been described in NF1, most of which are novel and truncate the gene, often through alteration of mRNA splicing {20301288}. Half of the individuals with NF1 have unaffected parents and represent de novo mutations.

Pathogenesis

Neurofibromin (NF1) primarily functions as a GAP for RAS proto-oncogenes {8499945}. Most NF1 clinical features in germline mutation carriers, including café-au-lait macules, bony abnormalities, and benign tumours, result from complete loss of neurofibromin (NF1) function due to subsequent somatic mutation of the wildtype allele (biallelic NF1 inactivation), leading to increased RAS and RAS effector (MEK/ERK or AKT/mTOR) signalling. In both neurofibromas and optic pathway gliomas / pilocytic astrocytomas, murine studies have revealed that non-neoplastic (stromal) cells with a heterozygous NF1 mutation, including mast cells, macrophages, neurons, T cells, and microglia, are critical for tumorigenesis and continued growth.

Novel NF1 mutations associated with OFNF have been detected in some Chinese families, as well as more severe ocular phenotypes in successive generations (i.e. anticipation) {31533651}. Differential promoter methylation and expression of the HOX family genes has also been reported in OFNF-associated tumours compared with neurofibromas developing at other sites {32366326}.

Histopathology

Lisch nodules develop on the anterior face of the iris, where there is usually a dome-shaped plaque of mostly spindle-shaped, cytologically bland melanocytes, arranged haphazardly. Nucleoli are not prominent and mitotic figures are not identified. Nerve sheath tumours in NF1 are predominantly neurofibromas. Recently proposed criteria for atypical neurofibromatous neoplasms of undetermined biological potential are used to describe premalignant or potentially early malignant changes in neurofibromas {28551330}. Most MPNSTs in individuals with NF1 are high-grade malignant spindle cell neoplasms with brisk mitotic activity and necrosis, which either develop in a pre-existing plexiform neurofibroma or arise de novo. Optic pathway gliomas are almost always pilocytic astrocytomas.

Cytology

Similar to sporadic counterparts

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Although no mutation hotspots have been identified, a pathogenic NF1 variant is detected in 95% of people with NF1 {10862084}. Nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site mutations, insertions, small deletions or duplications, and larger deletions including whole gene loss all result in NF1 inactivation. In approximately 5–11% of NF1 cases, a large 17q11.2 microdeletion is identified, in which multiple genes are codeleted along with the NF1 gene {34535841}. Therefore, sequencing and deletion/duplication analysis are used to identify the NF1 mutation spectrum. Owing to the frequency of mutations in NF1 affecting splicing, a subset of which are not demonstrable by standard genomic DNA sequencing, cDNA analysis results in a higher mutation detection rate {27322474}. Karyotype analysis may be considered for mutation-negative cases that have a strong NF1 clinical suspicion as (rarely) NF1 is disrupted by a translocation or complex cytogenetic abnormality.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a clinical diagnosis of NF1 requires the presence of at least two of the following features:

six or more café-au-lait macules (> 5 mm diameter in children, > 15 mm in adults) axillary/inguinal freckling two or more neurofibromas of any type or one plexiform neurofibroma optic pathway glioma two or more iris hamartomas (Lisch nodules) or two or more choroidal abnormalities distinctive bony abnormality (anterolateral bowing of the tibia, pseudarthrosis of a long bone, sphenoid dysplasia) heterozygous pathogenic NF1 variant with a variant allele fraction of 50% in apparently normal tissue parent with NF1 (by the above criteria).

These criteria were revised in 2021 {34012067}. Of note, there are other conditions with features that overlap with NF1, including Legius syndrome (MIM number 611431), Noonan syndrome {26337637}, and constitutive mismatch repair deficiency {30415209}.

Staging

Staging for NF1-associated MPNSTs is identical to that utilized for their sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

Individuals often have a shortened lifespan, mainly due to malignant tumours and cerebrovascular accidents {25354145;21694737;11283797}. The standard mortality ratio for many complications is higher in women < 50 years {25354145}.
