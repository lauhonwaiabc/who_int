6.1.2.3 Secretory carcinoma

Definition

Secretory carcinoma (SC) is a monophasic carcinoma characterized by a rearrangement of the ETV6 gene resulting in ETV6::NTRK3 fusion in most cases.

ICD-O coding

8502/3 Secretory carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D0Y & XH44J4 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Secretory carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: mammary analogue secretory carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Two cases of SC have been described in the main lacrimal gland {29963997;30059394}, and cases have been described in the eyelid, arising in the accessory lacrimal gland and in the sweat gland {29251639;35753863}.

Clinical features

SC presents with painless diplopia and proptosis, and as a circumscribed subcutaneous nodule {29963997;30059394;35753863}. CT and MRI show a heterogeneous circumscribed mass in the lacrimal fossa causing inferomedial displacement of the globe {29963997}.

Epidemiology

Reported lacrimal SC occurred in a 28-year-old man and a 52-year-old man. One tumour was identified in a retrospective review of 350 lacrimal tumours, suggesting low incidence in lacrimal glands {29963997}. Eyelid-skin SC occurred in a 55-year-old woman.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ETV6 gene rearrangement was present in reported cases {29963997;30059394}. RNA sequencing demonstrated a fusion transcript of exon 5 of the ETV6 gene and exon 14 of the NTRK3 gene in 1 case {29963997}. In salivary glands, alternative fusions include ETV6::RET {25651470;26492182;29076873}, ETV6::MET {29683815}, ETV6::MAML3 {30130630}, and VIM::RET {32675658}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lacrimal gland tumours are encapsulated and partially cystic {29963997;30059394}.

Histopathology

SC demonstrates papillary-cystic, solid, and microfollicular patterns {29963997;30059394;29251639}. A dense collagenous capsule with capsular invasion was present in both cases of main lacrimal gland SC. Tumour cells are round to ovoid, with mildly pleomorphic nuclei and abundant pale, eosinophilic, focally vacuolated cytoplasm. Clear cytoplasm can be present focally {29963997}. Goblet cells and intracytoplasmic zymogen granules are absent. Haemosiderin deposition can be focally prominent. No necrosis or perineural or lymphovascular invasion is identified. PAS staining highlights only luminal secretions.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells are positive for S100, mammaglobin, CK18, CAM5.2, and NTRK proteins. They lack expression of p63, CK5, DOG1, and AR. The Ki-67 proliferation index is below 5% {29963997;30059394;29251639;35753863}. SOX10, GCDFP-15, and vimentin positivity has been demonstrated in salivary cases.

Cytology

There are no reports of lacrimal SC cytology to date. Salivary SC shows loosely cohesive sheets, papillary or cribriform tissue fragments composed of cells with abundant finely granular or vacuolated cytoplasm, and uniform round to oval nuclei with fine chromatin, single nucleoli, and mild atypia {23042752;23225548;24585770;28738326;30942913}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

ETV6 gene rearrangement can be demonstrated by break-apart FISH. Detection of NTRK3 fusion by RT-PCR or RNA sequencing may be predictive of response to NTRK inhibitors for unresectable or metastatic cases {26884591;32223935}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a monophasic tumour with vacuolated secretory material, lacking cytoplasmic granules; immunohistochemical positivity for S100, mammaglobin, and NTRK, and negativity for p40/p63 and CK5.

Desirable: ETV6 and/or NTRK3 gene rearrangement.

Staging

The eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for lacrimal gland tumours applies.

Prognosis and prediction

SC is an indolent carcinoma which may be cured with a complete surgical resection. One reported lacrimal case showed no recurrence 12 years after resection {26884591}.
