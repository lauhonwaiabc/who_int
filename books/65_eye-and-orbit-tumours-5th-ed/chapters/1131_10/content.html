1.1.3.1 Epibulbar choristoma

Definition

Epibulbar choristoma is a benign congenital developmental lesion of histologically mature tissue located at conjunctival and/or corneal ectopic sites.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LA14.06 Epibulbar choristoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: for epibulbar dermoid: limbal dermoid; dermoid tumour; for dermolipoma: lipodermoid; for complex choristoma: mixed dermoid.

Subtype(s)

Epibulbar dermoid; dermolipoma; complex choristoma

Localization

Epibulbar dermoids are typically located close to the inferotemporal limbus beneath the conjunctiva {27725148}. If extensive, they can occupy the entire temporal quadrant. Dermoids are usually unilateral but can occur bilaterally in syndromic cases {27405313}. Dermolipomas are typically located in the conjunctival superotemporal quadrant {17183873}. If extensive, they can occupy the entire temporal quadrant, including the upper and lower fornices. Both types may involve the cornea.

Complex choristomas commonly extend from the superior temporal conjunctiva to the cornea. They may involve the entire temporal conjunctiva or, more rarely, the inferotemporal {9709758} or superior nasal conjunctiva {9082288}. Occurrences in the caruncle {22213061} and bilaterality {9709758} are exceptional. Occasionally, they are buried under pterygia {29443763}.

Clinical features

Epibulbar dermoids are whitish, firm lesions with hair {25971163}. They are non-mobile because of their attachment to underlying sclera.

Dermolipomas are yellowish, soft, non-mobile lesions that may contain hair {25971163}. The main differential diagnosis for dermolipoma is orbital fat prolapse. In dermolipoma, the conjunctiva is tethered to the underlying lesion, whereas in orbital fat prolapse, the conjunctiva is mobile over the lesion.

Complex choristomas are usually yellowish if they contain adipose tissue and pinkish with gelatinous elevations if they contain lacrimal gland {3684203}. The mass can also appear whitish, harbour hair, and display fine superficial vasculature.

Epidemiology

Epibulbar choristomas are rare. As congenital lesions, they are predominantly (in 60–70% of cases) diagnosed during childhood. Together, these choristomatous lesions account for approximately 8% of all epibulbar tumours in children {27725148;28125759}; in adults, this relative proportion is substantially lower (< 1%) {27725148}.

All three types of epibulbar choristoma are frequently observed in patients with Goldenhar syndrome and linear naevus sebaceus syndrome (MIM number 163200), in which as many as 50% of patients are affected by ocular anomalies, most commonly epibulbar choristomas {25772967;23683920;3200959;3684203}. Epibulbar choristomas may be part of other syndromes as well {19005989}.

Etiology

Epibulbar choristoma is a developmental abnormality. The occurrence of complex choristoma has been partially attributed to early fetal altered migration of the palpebral portion of the lacrimal gland {7294093}.

Pathogenesis

Epibulbar choristoma arises from tissues of dermal and mesenchymal origin.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Dermoids are composed of interweaving collagen bundles, covered by squamous epithelium (keratinized or non-keratinized, with or without goblet cells). Dermal appendages, in particular pilosebaceous units and sweat glands, may be present. Variable amounts of fat can be observed.

Dermolipomas in addition contain a large amount of adipose tissue. Skin appendages, in particular pilosebaceous units and sweat glands, are found less often and are less numerous than epibulbar dermoids.

In complex choristomas, various heterotopic tissues are found. These commonly include lacrimal gland and cartilage. Further components that can be present include smooth muscle {9709758;2655139}, peripheral nerve {9709758}, bone {3681614}, striated muscle {2655139}, immature teeth, and brain {166560}. The differential diagnosis for the presence of lacrimal gland components includes conjunctival lacrimal gland hamartoma {32360334}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: histologically mature tissue present at conjunctival and/or corneal ectopic sites.

Staging

Prognosis and prediction

Malignant transformation has not been described. Documented growth, possibly due to local reactive changes, has exceptionally been reported {3684203;21885965}. Debulking or excision is sometimes necessary to mitigate the risk of amblyopia associated with corneal lesions, inadequate lid closure, or dellen, or for cosmetic reasons {19512907;7933633}. Debulking may be favoured over excision to avoid complications {7933633}.
