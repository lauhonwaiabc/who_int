1.3.1.6 WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus)

Definition

WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus) (DPMN) is a melanocytic tumour composed of relatively large, finely pigmented ovoid or epithelioid melanocytes and genetically characterized by activation of the MAPK and WNT/β-catenin pathways. On the conjunctiva, almost all such lesions present as combined naevi.

ICD-O coding

8720/1 WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus)

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH81Y1 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Deep penetrating naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: deep penetrating melanocytoma; plexiform spindle cell melanocytoma; deep penetrating naevus; plexiform spindle cell naevus.

Not recommended: epithelioid naevus; inverted type A naevus; clonal naevus.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

WNT-activated DPMNs are most frequent on the bulbar conjunctiva, followed by the plica and caruncle {31558492;19667333;33005620}.

Clinical features

Most WNT-activated DPMNs are darkly pigmented. Changes in size and colour are noted in three quarters of cases, sometimes raising the clinical suspicion of a melanoma. An appearance of dark pigmentation within a pre-existent naevus may point to a WNT-activated DPMN {31558492;19667333;33005620;31367593}.

Epidemiology

WNT-activated DPMN of the conjunctiva shows a slight female predilection, with a wide age range (7–56 years). Combined lesions are relatively common, representing 9% of all excised conjunctival naevi in one series {31558492}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

WNT-activated DPMN develops through combined activation of the MAPK pathway and WNT/β-catenin signalling, which is usually mediated by a CTNNB1 gene mutation {28935960}. In WNT-activated DPMN of the conjunctiva, the MAPK pathway activation seems to be exclusively mediated by the BRAF p.V600E mutations {31558492;33005620}.

Macroscopic appearance

WNT-activated DPMN is usually a dark-brown lesion, measuring from < 1 to 4 mm, sometimes in the background of a less intensively pigmented conventional naevus {31558492}.

Histopathology

WNT-activated DPMN is characterized by nested or plexiform growth of relatively large ovoid or epithelioid melanocytes with a large amount of finely pigmented (dusty) cytoplasm. Maturation is absent. Mitoses are usually absent. Nests of melanocytes are practically always accompanied by melanophages {31558492;33005620;19667333;31367593;18300811}. Almost all WNT-activated DPMNs of the conjunctiva are part of a combined naevus (with either a common or an inflamed juvenile naevus) and in most cases represent its minor component. Most WNT-activated DPMNs are purely subepithelial, but they may be compound (in one third of cases) or rarely purely intraepithelial {31558492}.

WNT-activated DPMNs of the conjunctiva are positive for BRAF p.V600E by immunohistochemistry and also show nuclear positivity for β-catenin and cyclin D1 {31558492;33005620;34818422}. Retained p16 immunoreactivity and absent or only focal expression of PRAME can assist in differentiating WNT-activated melanocytoma from melanoma {35184152;33094849;34268800}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In diagnostically challenging cases, molecular analysis can be performed to confirm the presence of a CTNNB1 or APC mutation and to exclude additional mutations (such as TERT promoter mutations) and DNA copy-number changes indicative of melanoma {33005620;35184152}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nested or plexiform growth of large ovoid or epithelioid melanocytes with finely pigmented cytoplasm, typically accompanied by melanophages.

Desirable: nuclear positivity for β-catenin or a proven mutation in the WNT pathway.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

In diagnostically challenging cases, molecular analysis can be performed to confirm the presence of a CTNNB1 or APC mutation and to exclude additional mutations (such as TERT promoter mutations) and DNA copy-number changes indicative of melanoma {33005620;35184152}. This is a benign lesion with a very low risk of local recurrence {31558492;33005620;34818422}. While these lesions are histologically and molecularly similar to the skin equivalent, no further treatment is needed in most cases {31558492;33005620}.
