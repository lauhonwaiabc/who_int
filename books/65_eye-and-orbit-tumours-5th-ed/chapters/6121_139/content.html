6.1.2.1 Adenoid cystic carcinoma of the lacrimal gland

Definition

Adenoid cystic carcinoma (AdCC) is a malignant epithelial neoplasm composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells that are arranged in tubular, cribriform, and solid patterns associated with a basophilic matrix and reduplicated basement membrane material, frequently accompanied by MYB::NFIB gene rearrangements.

ICD-O coding

8200/3 Adenoid cystic carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D0Y & XH4302 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Adenoid cystic carcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

AdCCs arise most commonly from the orbital lobe of the lacrimal gland {14752304}, occasionally from the palpebral lobe {33749533}, and rarely from accessory lacrimal gland tissue in the conjunctiva and orbit {18434856;32912017;23034685}.

Clinical features

Clinically, AdCC may present initially with lacrimal gland enlargement causing ptosis, but the most common presentation is inferomedial globe displacement followed by non-axial proptosis {35298320;22471335;30028745;24526859}. The duration of symptoms is short, with a median of 7 months {24526859}. Pain and paraesthesia, secondary to perineural, muscle, or bone invasion, are cardinal signs distinguishing AdCC from benign lacrimal gland lesions {22471335;35298320;30028745}. Decreased motility, diplopia, and loss of vision have also been reported in more-advanced disease {35298320}. AdCCs most frequently metastasize to distant sites including lung, liver, and bone. Metastases to regional lymph nodes including preauricular, submandibular, and cervical lymph nodes occur less often {30028745}.

On CT or MRI, AdCCs may present initially as circumscribed masses but more often will appear as nodular lesions with irregular margins, infiltration of adjacent tissue, bony destruction, and calcification {22471335;30028745}. MRI may be useful for detecting small tumours, infiltration, and perineural invasion {34619831;22471335}.

Epidemiology

Although they are rare, accounting for approximately 1.6–4.8% of all orbital lesions {35298320;32912017;34619831}, AdCCs are the most common primary malignant epithelial tumour of the lacrimal gland, accounting for as many as 60% of cases {14752304;34619831}. AdCC has a bimodal age distribution, with the first peak in middle age (majority in their forties) and a smaller peak in the first decade of life {33749533;22471335}. There is a slight female predominance with a reported M:F ratio of 4:7 {24526859;15121380}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Similarly to salivary gland AdCCs, lacrimal gland AdCCs frequently (as many as 50%) carry a t(6;9)(q22-23;p24-23) chromosomal translocation that results in MYB::NFIB gene fusion, and activation of the MYB oncogene and associated target proteins such as KIT and BCL2, which are involved in cell growth, apoptosis, transcription, and cell-cycle regulation {23725736;28085142}. Other than losses in chromosome 6q, AdCCs in the lacrimal gland also show losses in chromosome 12q and 17p, and gains in chromosomes 19q, 8q, and 11q {23725736}. Whole-exome sequencing has implicated aberrant NOTCH pathway signalling in lacrimal gland AdCCs {28820917}. Other oncogenic mutations that may occur include those of KRAS, NRAS, and MET {25899808}.

Macroscopic appearance

AdCCs present as unencapsulated firm, white nodular lesions with irregular borders.

Histopathology

AdCCs are biphasic tumours composed of proliferating modified myoepithelial cells and luminal ductal cells {22829347;22471335}. There are three histological growth patterns that may be present in variable combinations: cribriform, tubular, and solid/basaloid {22471335;25325875;23972314}. The most common histological pattern is the cribriform followed by the solid then the tubular {24526859}. The cribriform pattern, classically described as a Swiss-cheese pattern, is most commonly seen and is composed of cystic structures where the connective tissue stroma is filled with basophilic amorphous glycosaminoglycans and/or eosinophilic hyalinized basal lamina. The tubular pattern is composed predominantly of small glandular structures {22471335;23972314}. The solid/basaloid pattern carries the worst prognosis. It is composed predominantly of compact sheet-like proliferating basaloid myoepithelial cells that lack the cribriform or tubular architecture {25325875;22471335}. Mitotic figures are infrequent but more commonly seen in the solid/basaloid pattern. Perineural invasion is commonly seen {22471335}.

High-grade transformation/dedifferentiation can occur with comedonecrosis, increased nuclear atypia, mitoses {11310924}, and rarely sarcomatoid or myoepithelial components {22829347;21283029}. Differential diagnosis includes adenocarcinoma, myoepithelial carcinoma, epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma, polymorphous adenocarcinoma, and basal cell adenocarcinoma {22471335;25325875}.

Immunohistochemistry is not usually required for diagnosis. However, immunostaining for MYB and its downstream target KIT, although less sensitive and specific, may be used as a surrogate for MYB rearrangement molecular studies {23972314}. The mean Ki-67 index is 30% and p53 may be expressed in 39% of cases {23972314}.

Cytology

Tumour cells are basaloid with small, angulated hyperchromatic nuclei and scant cytoplasm. These basaloid cells may encircle large well-demarcated spherules of acellular hyaline globules {32809258}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FISH for MYB rearrangement is not usually required, but may be helpful in difficult cases {28085142;33749533}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a malignant biphasic tumour composed of proliferating myoepithelial cells and luminal ductal cells.

Desirable: perineural invasion; positive MYB and/or KIT immunohistochemistry; FISH for MYB rearrangement may be useful in challenging cases.

Staging

Staging is according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM, eighth edition, for lacrimal gland carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

The mainstay of treatment is surgical excision such as orbital exenteration or eye-sparing surgery together with adjuvant radiotherapy or chemoradiation, both having similar recurrence outcomes {32680839}. The prognosis for AdCC has improved owing to earlier diagnosis and improved treatment. Poor outcomes such as higher rates of local/regional recurrence, distant metastasis, and death are associated with basaloid patterns of growth, perineural invasion, and larger tumours (> 25 mm) {19395039;30028745}. Several immunohistochemistry markers, i.e. NOTCH1 and activated NICD1 overexpression, and nuclear expression of BIRC5 (survivin), have been shown to be associated with a worse prognosis {33675181;23611357}. NOTCH1-directed targeted clinical trials are in progress and have yet to be reported.
