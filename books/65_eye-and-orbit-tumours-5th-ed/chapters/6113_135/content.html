6.1.1.3 Myoepithelioma

Definition

Myoepithelioma is a benign epithelial neoplasm of the lacrimal gland with myoepithelial differentiation. Its malignant counterpart is myoepithelial carcinoma.

ICD-O coding

8982/0 Myoepithelioma

8982/3 Myoepithelial carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH3CQ8 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Myoepithelioma

2D0Y & XH43E6 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Myoepithelial carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: myoepithelial cell tumour; myoepithelial adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Myoepitheliomas of the main lacrimal gland have been reported in > 10 published papers {25603534;15138773;15568156}. Myoepitheliomas have also been reported in the orbital apex without a connection to the lacrimal gland; such lesions are thought to arise from glandular embryonic remnants along the Rathke pouch {27928268}.

Clinical features

Myoepitheliomas of the main lacrimal gland are usually non-tender, slow-growing, well-circumscribed superotemporal orbital masses causing proptosis, internal deviation of the eye, or eyelid ptosis. Myoepithelial carcinomas can arise de novo or as clonal expansions in other tumours, such as pleomorphic adenomas {20408086}.

Epidemiology

Myoepithelial tumours account for < 1% of lacrimal gland neoplasms {25603534}. Patients’ age at presentation varies widely and both sexes are equally affected.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Salivary gland myoepitheliomas show chromosomal alterations (12q), a feature shared with pleomorphic adenomas {14994730}. In addition, myoepithelial carcinomas of the salivary gland, especially the clear cell pattern, show EWSR1 gene rearrangement {25581728}. Other studies on salivary gland tumours found that malignant transformation in myoepithelial cells requires, in addition to p16 pathway deregulation, inactivation of p53 {19076682}. These findings have not yet been validated in lacrimal gland myoepithelial neoplasms.

Macroscopic appearance

Myoepitheliomas are solid well-delineated masses with a tan to yellow appearance and glistening cut surfaces.

Histopathology

Myoepitheliomas are usually well circumscribed and sometimes surrounded by a variably thick fibrous capsule. They are densely cellular and display spindle, plasmacytoid, epithelioid, or clear cells proliferating in trabeculae or sheets. Occasional rudiments of ducts or microcysts as well as mucinous foci can be encountered. Interlacing fibrous fascicles with hyalinized areas can be present. The nuclei are normochromatic, smoothly bordered, and round to oval, and lack mitoses. Light-pink to clear cytoplasm is seen in variable amounts and is more abundant in the plasmacytoid pattern. The neoplastic cells are consistently positive for cytokeratins (CK7 and CK14) and variably immunoreactive for myoepithelial markers (S100, SMA, MSA, p63, calponin, and less for GFAP) {9400797}.

Myoepithelial carcinomas show invasion into neighbouring structures (lacrimal gland, orbital soft tissue, bone, or perineural spaces). They can have cytologically bland nuclei or display nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic activity.

Cytology

Myoepithelial cells can be recognized based on their spindle morphology with limited cytoplasm on cytology from FNAB of salivary glands {24107383;25431027}, but this method is of limited use for lacrimal gland neoplasms.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: monotonous epithelial cells coexpressing myoepithelial markers.

Desirable: tumours are encapsulated.

Staging

Myoepithelial carcinomas are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Cancer staging manual, eighth edition, 2017.

Prognosis and prediction

Myoepitheliomas are benign and treated by complete local excision. There are no specific prognostic criteria for myoepithelial carcinomas, which have a recurrence rate of > 20% after surgical resection and late metastases {24456911}.
