6.1.2.0 Carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma

Definition

Carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma is a malignant lacrimal gland epithelial neoplasm that shows the presence of an epithelial and/or myoepithelial malignancy arising in association with primary or recurrent pleomorphic adenoma (PA).

ICD-O coding

8941/3 Carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2D0Y & XH42V2 Other specified malignant neoplasms of eye or ocular adnexa & Carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: carcinoma ex benign mixed tumour; malignant mixed tumour; carcinoma arising in mixed tumour; carcinoma arising in pleomorphic adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lesions arise in the orbital and palpebral lobes of the main lacrimal gland {32282452;33710040;34320898} and accessory and ectopic lacrimal gland tissue.

Clinical features

Patients may present with a previously slow-growing mass in the lacrimal fossa, causing proptosis or inferior globe displacement, or with recurrent PA that abruptly shows rapid growth and/or becomes symptomatic with pain, skin ulceration, and/or fixation {2821976;33710040}.

Imaging

Although simple X-rays can show an orbital wall lytic lesion, CT and MRI are able to demonstrate the infiltrative pattern of an aggressive malignancy {29755265;32282452}.

Epidemiology

Carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma (CEPA) is the second most common lacrimal gland malignancy, although rare. Review of the literature of 40 cases showed no sex predilection and a median age at diagnosis of 56 years (range: 17–76 years) {18772674;19667339;34926199;32282452;29437290;31456912}.

Etiology

CEPA may arise de novo {32282452} or secondary to malignant change after incomplete excision of PA {29755265}.

Pathogenesis

Recurrent translocations of PLAG1 (chromosome 8q) and HMGA2 (chromosome 12q), involved in PA pathogenesis, are also seen in CEPA {29755265;29437290;32282452;32850170}. CEPA development follows progressive sequential loss of heterozygosity of 8q, 12q, and finally 17p {29755265;32282452;32850170}. Malignant transformation has been associated with alterations in 12q genes (HMGA2 and MDM2), 17p loss leading to loss of p53 {29437290;29755265;32282452;32850170}, and copy-number alterations involving 9p and 22q, resulting in activation of NFIB and PDGFB {29755265}. Overexpression of the IL-6/JAK/STAT3 pathway may also play a role in the malignant transformation {27286739}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions appear as a heterogeneous mass, often with areas resembling residual PA, or possible invasion of surrounding tissue.

Histopathology

Microscopically, transition between the malignant component and residual PA is observed. The carcinoma component may be of any type: adenocarcinoma of salivary duct appearance (most common) {32089922}, mucoepidermoid carcinoma {25005213;34939520}, myoepithelial carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma {33172316}, or adenoid cystic carcinoma {22471335}. Extensive sampling may be required for identification of a prior PA component, which may be represented by densely hyalinized, sclerotic, or hypocellular stroma. Intravascular, perineural, and/or bone invasion may be present.

Based on cases arising in the salivary glands, by convention, CEPA is considered intracapsular (non-invasive) when confined within the capsule, minimally invasive if there is minimal breach of the capsule (< 4–6 mm), and widely invasive if extension beyond the capsule is > 6 mm {26638158}. The terms “intracapsular” and “minimally invasive” should only be applied to uninodular tumours with a well-delineated interface after the entire lesion border has been microscopically evaluated. These terms should not be used for carcinomas arising in multinodular recurrent PA.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry is usually not necessary for diagnosis. Carcinoma areas may show increased expression of p53, MYC, and Ki-67. PLAG1 and HMGA2 expression may assist identification of the PA component {27567603;28796899;31456912}, and can be useful in separating CEPA from de novo lacrimal gland carcinomas {29437290}. AR positivity may indicate lacrimal gland ductal adenocarcinoma differentiation.

Differential diagnosis

This includes pleomorphic adenoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, carcinosarcoma, myoepithelial and epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma, and adenoid cystic carcinoma.

Cytology

Smears can show variable morphology depending on sampling. Features of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, mucoepidermoid or adenosquamous carcinoma, carcinosarcoma, or another lacrimal gland malignancy, in addition to classic PA features, may be seen {9723598;10450099;31675185;33528074}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PLAG1 or HMGA2 rearrangements, detected by FISH, serve as markers of pre-existing PA. These rearrangements in CEPA can assist in differentiating CEPA from de novo lacrimal gland carcinomas {29437290}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mass arising from the lacrimal gland; presence of an epithelial and/or myoepithelial malignant component; histological areas compatible with PA or history of previous PA in the same site.

Staging

Lesions are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Cancer staging manual, eighth edition.

Prognosis and prediction

Based on a small series, distant metastases were seen in 11% of cases. Treatment included complete excision, orbital bone resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy {32282452}. No current biomarkers are available to predict the risk of malignant transformation in PA.
