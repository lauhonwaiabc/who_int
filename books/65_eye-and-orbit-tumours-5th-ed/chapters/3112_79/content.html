3.1.1.2 Implantation cyst

Definition

An implantation cyst is a squamous epithelial–lined cyst of the iris that either arises developmentally or is secondary to penetrating ocular injury.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A91 Cyst of iris, ciliary body or anterior chamber

9A82 Cyst in the anterior chamber of the eye

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Implantation cysts arise most commonly in the inferior and superonasal sectors in the midperipheral iris.

Clinical features

An implantation cyst appears as a translucent cyst in the iris stroma, containing clear or slightly opaque fluid, which can be associated with secondary glaucoma, corneal oedema, and amblyopia {8352310;9663240;20490997}. Secondary posttraumatic implantation cysts are situated adjacent to the former corneal perforation site.

The clinical differential diagnosis includes uveitis-associated cysts that arise from interepithelial fluid accumulation in the iris and ciliary body {8976700}, cystic benign naevi, cystic uveal melanoma {6888862}, metastases {28882598}, intraocular foreign body {19702716}, and intraocular parasites {28882598}.

Epidemiology

Congenital primary cysts are incidental findings in patients, without sex predilection. Posttraumatic secondary cysts occur months or years after trauma or surgery at any age {28882598;31725833}.

Etiology

Primary cysts are idiopathic. Secondary cysts follow injury or intraocular surgery.

Pathogenesis

Congenital cysts originate from entrapped epithelium entering the eye during invagination of the surface ectoderm when the lens vesicle forms in the fourth week of embryogenesis {1739690;4050928}. Secondary posttraumatic and postsurgical cysts originate from surface epithelium that enters the anterior chamber during injury or surgery.

Macroscopic appearance

Cysts are whitish with clear fluid in the lumen.

Histopathology

Primary and secondary cysts are lined by concentric layers of stratified squamous epithelium with or without goblet cells, with or without keratinization {28882598}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cyst lined by surface epithelium consisting of non-atypical non-keratinizing or keratinizing squamous epithelium of varying thickness, with or without goblet cells.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

An enlarging cyst might cause visual deterioration and precipitate angle-closure glaucoma. Complete excision is curative.
