2.2.1.3 Seborrhoeic keratosis

Definition

Seborrhoeic keratoses are benign intraepithelial neoplastic squamous proliferations encountered on ageing skin.

ICD-O coding

8052/0 Seborrhoeic keratosis

8052/0 Irritated seborrhoeic keratosis

8052/0 Stucco keratosis

ICD-11 coding

2F21.0 Seborrhoeic keratosis

Related terminology

Not recommended: senile wart; seborrhoeic wart; basal cell papilloma.

Subtype(s)

Irritated seborrhoeic keratosis; stucco keratosis

Localization

Seborrhoeic keratoses are found on periocular skin, including the lid margin.

Clinical features

Patients with seborrhoeic keratosis present with a papillomatous, most often sessile tumour with a stuck-on appearance. The papules or plaques are tan to brown and keratotic; owing to the sometimes heavy pigmentation, they can clinically impress as naevi. An acute onset of multiple seborrhoeic keratosis can occur in the setting of internal malignancies, known as the Leser–Trélat sign {8682971}.

Epidemiology

Seborrhoeic keratosis is most often diagnosed at an older age (> 50 years). There is no known sex predisposition.

Etiology

Age is a widely acknowledged risk factor for seborrhoeic keratosis. A proposed association with HPV is contested, especially for non-genital lesions {2543229;35066969}.

Pathogenesis

Seborrhoeic keratosis is associated with ultraviolet (UV) radiation with a typical UV radiation signature, with the majority of alterations being C>T and CC>TT base changes at dipyrimidinic sites {28410231}. Somatic mutations have been reported in FGFR3, which may result in increased keratinocyte proliferation and reduced apoptosis {15772091;16338952;16778799;20420824}. Other mutated genes include PIK3CA, KRAS, HRAS, EGFR, and AKT1 {19845664;23739246;28410231}.

Macroscopic appearance

Seborrhoeic keratosis is a papillomatous, sometimes pigmented tumour with a broad base.

Histopathology

A well-demarcated, thickened epidermal layer due to an increase in basaloid keratinocytes with a flat or reticulated base is seen. Mitoses are rare. The basal epithelial cell layer often contains intracellular pigment. Horn cysts can be found between the basal cells and at the tumour surface hyperkeratosis. There may be lymphoplasmacellular inflammation at the tumour base {33958512}. Different growth patterns and subtypes may be encountered, and they are described in greater detail in the Skin tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Skin Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 12). https://publications.iarc.who.int/650.]].

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed intraepidermal squamous proliferation with a flat base, lacking squamous dysplasia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision is curative.
