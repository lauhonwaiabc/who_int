9.3.1.1 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma

Definition

Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (EMZL) is an indolent B-cell lymphoma with features that recapitulate normal mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue. EMZL can arise primarily in ocular adnexal structures, or, less often, as secondary involvement at other sites.

ICD-O coding

9699/3 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A85.3 & XH1V99 Extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma, primary site excluding stomach or skin & Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma of the conjunctiva, lacrimal gland, and lacrimal sac.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ocular adnexal EMZL most frequently involves the orbit, followed by the conjunctiva and lacrimal gland. Lacrimal sac and eyelid involvement are uncommon {31177189;17255761}. More than one ocular adnexal site can be involved. Involvement is usually unilateral (85% and 89%, respectively, in two series) {31177189;17255761}.

Clinical features

Clinical presentation depends on the ocular adnexal structure involved. Orbital and lacrimal gland EMZLs often present with a mass causing proptosis and displacement of the eyeball {30419193;32574779;32906630;31177189}. Conjunctival EMZL typically presents with a pink-coloured mass, which is often described as a salmon patch {26003619;31372366}, whereas EMZL of the eyelid often presents with a palpable mass of the eyelid {28209503}. Systemic symptoms are rare.

Epidemiology

About 8% of all extranodal lymphomas arise in the ocular adnexa {5007387}, and EMZL is the most common subtype of ocular adnexal lymphoma {30419193;32906630;34556080;32863278;32994547;31894706;9442797;8168043}. The incidence of ocular adnexal EMZL is low although reported to be increasing {32732342}. There is a female predominance reported, ranging from 52% to 75% of cases {17255761;31177189;28814410}. EMZL often affects patients in their fifties or sixties {17255761;31177189;28814410;32994547}. Children are rarely affected.

Etiology

EMZL is thought to arise from acquired MALT, which develops because of an underlying chronic inflammatory stimulus. In most cases that stimulus is unknown, but a small number of patients have an underlying autoimmune disease {17255761} or IgG4-related disease {29285637} and a subset have infection with Chlamydia psittaci. Some of the latter have shown regression of lymphoma with antibiotics; however, there is substantial geographical variation with respect to the proportion of patients with underlying Chlamydia infection {18040143;15100336;15968003;28288714}.

Pathogenesis

Trisomy of chromosomes 3 and 18 is common in EMZL of various sites; other genetic changes occur at highly variable frequencies. There are several different MALT lymphoma–associated translocations associated with NF-κB activation {27452667;15682443}; among them, t(14;18)(q32;q21) (IGH::MALT1) occurs in approximately one quarter of ocular adnexal EMZLs while the others are very uncommon {15356642}. EMZL of the ocular adnexa shows biased usage of certain immunoglobulin genes, resulting in expression of autoreactive B-cell receptor {22216179;22024723;22382892}. Autoantigens then trigger chronic B-cell activation, with resulting complex molecular events including the canonical NF-κB pathway activation, promoting lymphomagenesis. TNFAIP3 is a negative regulator of the canonical NF-κB pathway; it is frequently inactivated by mutation and/or deletion in ocular adnexal EMZL. Alterations of TNFAIP3 are often associated with IGHV4-34 B-cell receptor, possibly leading to cooperation in NF-κB activation {19608751;19006194;28682481}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Sections show a vaguely nodular and diffuse proliferation of small lymphocytes and marginal zone B cells which have oval to slightly irregular nuclei, dark chromatin, absent nucleoli, and a moderate amount of pale cytoplasm. Plasma cells, ranging from few to abundant, are commonly seen and in some cases contain Dutcher bodies. A few large lymphoid cells may be admixed. Among cases involving the lacrimal gland, lymphoepithelial lesions are uncommon. Sclerosis is usually absent. In rare cases there is amyloid deposition {17255761}. Remnants of reactive lymphoid follicles are often identifiable.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunophenotyping typically shows CD20+, CD19+, CD5−, CD10−, BCL6−, BCL2+, CD43−/+, and cyclin D1− B cells. IgM expression is much more common than IgG or IgA expression. Monotypic immunoglobulin light chain expression is demonstrated by flow cytometry. Paraffin section immunohistochemistry commonly demonstrates plasma cells expressing monotypic cytoplasmic immunoglobulin, particularly in cases with numerous plasma cells. Fragmented, CD21+ or CD23+ follicular dendritic cell meshworks consistent with remnants of reactive follicles are usually present {17255761}.

Differential diagnosis

EMZL can be distinguished from reactive hyperplasia by a sheet-like or permeative infiltration of CD20+ B cells, effacement of architecture, monotypic plasma cells, and monoclonal immunoglobulin gene rearrangements. Other mature B-cell lymphomas (such as follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia) can be excluded by lack of CD10, BCL6, CD5, cyclin D1, and LEF1 expression. EMZL with prominent follicular colonization may mimic follicular lymphoma; the presence of an appreciable extrafollicular component of B cells, lack of extensive expression of follicular centre markers (particularly CD10) within and outside follicles {33283568}, and absence of BCL2 gene translocation may support the former. Distinction between EMZL and lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma can be very difficult. Bone marrow and lymph node involvement at presentation, high titre IgM paraproteinaemia, frequent Dutcher bodies, and NP_002459.2 MYD88 (p.L265P) mutation would favour the latter. Rare cases of EMZL show extensive plasma cell differentiation, mimicking plasmacytoma, but the presence of a component of CD20+ B cells as well as presentation in a typical MALT site would favour the former.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Gene rearrangement studies may be helpful to detect clonal B-cell populations in cases in which light chain restriction is not demonstrable.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: architectural effacement by a small to medium-sized proliferation of CD5− and CD10− B lymphoid cells; exclusion of other small B-cell lymphomas.

Desirable: demonstration of light chain restriction in lymphoid cells or plasma cells, or clonal immunoglobulin gene rearrangement; remnants of underlying reactive lymphoid follicles.

Staging

The TNM staging system is used to stage primary ocular adnexal EMZL [[Amin MB, Edge SB, Greene FL, et al., editors. AJCC Cancer Staging Manual. 8th ed. New York (NY): Springer; 2017.]].

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for ocular adnexal EMZL is favourable, with a reported 10-year disease-specific survival of primary ocular adnexal EMZL of 93% in a large multicentre study of 689 patients {31177189}. Older age, secondary disease, relapse, and higher TNM stage are predictors of a worse prognosis {31177189}. Ocular adnexal EMZL can be treated effectively with local radiation therapy, with excellent local control {31177189;12910532;24417911;28588773;29134184}. Relapse is common, however, with 5- and 10-year relapse-free survival of 67% and 36% in one series. Most relapses occur in other extranodal sites and less often in lymph nodes. Large cell transformation is uncommon [[Ferry JA, Fung CY, Lucarelli MJ, et al. Ocular Adnexal Lymphoma: Long-Term Outcome, Patterns of Failure and Prognostic Factors in 174 Patients.
