7.2.2.1 Squamous cell carcinoma of the lacrimal drainage system

Definition

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the lacrimal drainage system is a malignant epithelial neoplasm originating from surface epithelium. Keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma (KSCC) shows squamous differentiation and keratin production, whereas non-keratinizing SCC (NKSCC) shows histological and/or immunohistochemical evidence of squamous differentiation, but with no or minimal keratinization.

Related terminology

Not recommended: squamous cell carcinoma NOS; epidermoid carcinoma; Schneiderian carcinoma; transitional cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

Keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma (KSCC); non-keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma (NKSCC)

Localization

SCC arises predominantly in the lacrimal sac but may involve the canaliculi and rarely the nasolacrimal duct {7947444;3147447;18488243;20958177;28582372;28399337;24911210;23960982;26649215}.

Clinical features

The majority of SCCs arising from the lacrimal drainage system are unilateral and present with persistent/chronic epiphora, recurrent dacryocystitis, and/or a non-tender, incompressible mass above the medial tendon. Serosanguineous punctal discharge, epistaxis, pain, nasal obstruction, and medial canthal skin ulceration/telangiectasia may occur. Some cases have been discovered incidentally during dacryocystorhinostomy or dacryocystectomy {12855966;4352147;16550051;23960982;28399337;28582372;24195987;20958177;21264538;3147447}.

Direct extension into the orbit, paranasal sinuses with bony destruction, and cranium, and rarely regional lymphadenopathy or metastases have been described {6646653;24195987;12868021;3147447}. CT and MRI are helpful to delineate tumour extension through bone and soft tissues.

Epidemiology

SCC of the lacrimal sac is rare and accounts for about 14% of all lacrimal sac neoplasms {6277817;7677021;4352147}. They typically present in the fifth to eighth decades of life, with no sex predilection {26735858;6646653;7677021;3147447;23960982;6277817}.

Etiology

High-risk HPV genotypes (HPV16, HPV18, or HPV33) have been reported in about 60% of lacrimal sac SCCs with predominantly non-keratinizing histomorphology {8386822;9215061;17362365;26735858;28399337;34452388}.

SCC in the lacrimal sac may arise as a de novo lesion or in a pre-existing inverted papilloma as an invasive carcinoma or carcinoma in situ ex inverted papilloma in about 10–15% of cases {12753996}.

Increased risk with cigarette smoking has also been shown in small studies {32501879;25879369}. Lesions may originate from pre-existing squamous metaplasia and chronic inflammation has been implicated in inducing malignant transformation {12753996}.

Pathogenesis

The genomic landscape in lacrimal sac SCCs is poorly defined. Gene mutations in EGFR, PTEN, CDKN2A, and KMT2D, and p53 expression dysregulation have been reported in sinonasal SCCs. SCCs arising from inverted papillomas have been shown to harbour the EGFR mutation {29145573;32868526}.

HPV16-associated SCC exhibits mutations of PIK3CA, FGFR3, AKT1, and/or PIK3R1; wildtype TP53; p16 overexpression; and deregulated high-risk E6/E7 transcription {35626161}. HPV testing by in situ hybridization or PCR identifies those tumours with associated HPV infection. In sinonasal SCC, HPV-associated p16 overexpression reflects oncogenic HPV in SCC pathogenesis {23095507;24030745}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions appear as an endophytic or exophytic mass with a friable white-tan surface with or without haemorrhage and/or necrosis.

Histopathology

KSCC comprises infiltrating nests and strands of polygonal squamous cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm, large hyperchromatic nuclei, and a moderately high mitotic count; extracellular or intracellular keratinization with intercellular bridging and squamous eddies; comedonecrosis; desmoplastic stromal reaction; and a dense inflammatory background. There may also be koilocytosis {26735858;6646653;7294095}.

NKSCC demonstrates large expansile nests, lobules, and ribbons of malignant epithelial cells with variable eosinophilia of their cytoplasm. Squamous differentiation may not always be obvious, especially in poorly differentiated carcinomas, and keratinization is usually minimal or absent. The carcinoma comprises atypical transitional-like epithelium with a lack of maturation and cell polarity, and an invasive acanthotic inverted/endophytic growth pattern or a mixed papillary and inverted growth pattern with fibrovascular cores. Occasional goblet cells may be present {28582372;8129653;4352147}.

Numerous abnormal mitoses and lymphovascular and perineural invasion can be seen in both subtypes. The SCC can be well, moderately, or poorly differentiated.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry is usually not required for KSCC, but it may be helpful in cases without keratinization or small biopsies. The carcinoma cells are positive for pancytokeratin, CK5/6, CK7, p40, p63, and EMA. They are negative for CK20, adipophilin, and other soft tissue, neuroendocrine, or melanoma markers, and with BerEP4.

Differential diagnosis

Poorly differentiated KSCCs and NKSCCs need differentiation from basaloid SCC, sinonasal malignancies, sebaceous cell carcinoma, amelanotic melanoma, and neuroendocrine carcinomas.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

HPV detection is not currently used in this setting.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for KSCC: malignant epithelial cells with morphological evidence of squamous differentiation and keratinization; for NKSCC: malignant epithelial cells with morphological and/or immunohistochemical evidence of squamous differentiation, and with no or minimal keratinization.

Staging

While there is currently no separate TNM classification for carcinomas of the lacrimal drainage system, the eighth-edition Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for lacrimal gland tumours can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

In a recent large series of lacrimal sac SCCs, the reported 5-year overall survival and progression-free survival were 88% and 76%, respectively {30629760}. Long-term follow-up is recommended owing to high rates of recurrence and metastases. NKSCCs, poorly differentiated KSCCs, and those with orbital/and or sinonasal invasion are associated with a higher recurrence rate and poor prognosis {16681067;7947444;24195987;28399337;21036121;30629760}. There are as yet no data on PDL1 expression and response to immunotherapy in this setting.
