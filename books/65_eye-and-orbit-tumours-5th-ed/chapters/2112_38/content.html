2.1.1.2 Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma

Definition

Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma (EMPSGC) is a low-grade, mucin-producing, adnexal neoplasm of sweat gland origin that shows neuroendocrine differentiation, with a predilection for the eyelid and periorbital skin.

ICD-O coding

8509/3 Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH4EK4 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma

2C33 & XH8010 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma in situ

Related terminology

Not recommended: cutaneous analogue of solid papillary carcinoma of the breast.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

EMPSGC has a predilection for the eyelid/periorbital skin, with rare extrafacial occurrence {24673415}.

Clinical features

Lesions present as slow-growing, skin-coloured or bluish nodules, papules, or cysts. They may be multifocal {16160476}.

Epidemiology

The true incidence is unknown. EMPSGC typically occurs in the sixth and seventh decades of life. Women are affected twice as often as men {32452870;16160476}.

Etiology

No known causes or risk factors

Pathogenesis

EMPSGC is a cutaneous analogue of solid papillary carcinoma of the breast {9414195;32452870}. In some cases it is associated with adjacent mucinous carcinoma and most probably represents a precursor lesion {9414195;16160476;32452870}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features.

Histopathology

EMPSGC grows as well-demarcated expansile nodules with cystic, solid, papillary, or cribriform architecture. Tumour cells are polygonal to round, small to intermediate-sized ductal cells with amphophilic or bluish cytoplasm and round to oval nuclei with finely stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. Small amounts of intracellular and extracellular mucin can be demonstrated by mucicarmine or PAS staining {9414195;16160476;32452870}. EMPSGC is frequently seen within dilated eccrine ducts, undermining benign ductal epithelium with a complete myoepithelial layer. At this stage the tumour represents carcinoma in situ. As it grows and the nests become large expansile nodules, the myoepithelial rim becomes incomplete or lost entirely, consistent with invasive carcinoma with pushing invasion {16160476;25925290}. Mitotic activity is low, and the Ki-67 proliferation index is seldom greater than 5–10% {25219513}. The presence of extracellular pools of stromal mucin and/or infiltrating tumour glands and nests indicates progression to mucinous adenocarcinoma. Such tumours should be diagnosed as invasive mucinous carcinoma {32452870}.

Immunohistochemistry

INSM1 is the most sensitive neuroendocrine marker {32745280}; staining with synaptophysin or chromogranin can be focal or absent {32452870}. EMPSGC typically strongly expresses ER, PR, and CK7. Markers such as p63 or calponin can highlight the myoepithelial layer. EMPSGC has also been shown to express CK8, CK18, AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, EMA, GATA3, GCDFP-15, WT1, and RCCm {16160476;25219513;25925290;27533071;31335409}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cystic, solid, papillary, or cribriform architecture; polygonal cells with bluish cytoplasm; neuroendocrine nuclei and intracellular or extracellular mucin.

Desirable: immunohistochemical expression of neuroendocrine markers; INSM1 is currently the most sensitive neuroendocrine marker for EMPSGC.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system for non-melanoma eyelid carcinoma applies.

Prognosis and prediction

EMPSGC has an excellent prognosis after excision with clear margins, recurring in up to 10% of cases {32452870;34057875}. The recurrence rate of EMPSGC-associated mucinous sweat gland carcinoma is about 20% {32452870}. Rarely, metastases may arise many years after surgery {31977506;33660318;34184156}.
