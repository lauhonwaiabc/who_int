10.2.1.3 Osteoblastoma

Definition

Osteoblastoma is a locally aggressive bone-forming tumour, generally > 20 mm.

ICD-O coding

9200/1 Osteoblastoma

ICD-11 coding

2E83.Z & XH4316 Benign osteogenic tumour of unspecified site & Osteoblastoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: epithelioid osteoblastoma.

Not recommended: pseudomalignant osteoblastoma; aggressive osteoblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In more than one third of cases the spine is affected. The craniofacial bones, including the orbit, are affected in < 5% of cases {33049284;17052641;24256752}.

Clinical features

The presenting symptom is frequently pain and/or swelling {8119712;33049284}. In the orbit, this may include exophthalmos, hearing or visual loss, diplopia, and nasal obstruction {24256752;33049284}.

On radiographs and CT, osteoblastomas present as lytic lesions > 20 mm, frequently with surrounding reactive sclerosis {27476846}.

Epidemiology

Osteoblastomas represent < 1% of primary bone tumours {18561158;8119712}. The peak incidence is between the second and third decades of life. In general, the M:F ratio is 2:1 {18561158;8119712}; however, in the orbit the reported sex distribution is equal {33049284}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Rearrangement of FOS is present in 87% of osteoblastomas {29858576;30582658}, and in a smaller percentage of cases FOSB rearrangement has been detected {29858576}. FOS rearrangements can be detected using an antibody against the FOS N-terminus, which results in nuclear immunoreactivity in the osteoblastic cells {30582658;31490237;31768625}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not clinically relevant

Histopathology

Osteoblastomas are characterized by interconnecting, delicate, woven bone trabeculae, rimmed by a single layer of polygonal osteoblasts {8119712}. The stroma is loose and usually richly vascularized, with dilated capillary-type blood vessels. Destructive host bone permeation and atypical mitotic figures should not be seen. The differential diagnosis includes sino-orbital osteoma with osteoblastoma-like features.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FOS gene rearrangement or rarely FOSB gene rearrangement is found in the majority of osteoblastomas {29858576}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone-forming tumour composed of trabeculae of woven bone rimmed by plump osteoblasts in a vascularized stroma; absence of host bone permeation; compatible radiological features of a > 20 mm lesion with well-defined tumour borders.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is good, but recurrences are described in as many as 23% of cases {18561158}, more frequently in patients in which complete excision has not been achieved, and this can be challenging at this location {18561158}.
