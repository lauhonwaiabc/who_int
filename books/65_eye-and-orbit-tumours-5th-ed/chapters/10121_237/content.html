10.1.2.1 Benign lipomatous tumours

Definition

Benign lipomatous tumours are a family of benign tumours composed of mature adipocytes comprising lipoma, angiolipoma, and spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma (SCL/PL).

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lipomas have been described in the eyelid, orbital fat, and extraocular muscles {32875157;27992661;19454946;17519657;20700070;25029341}. SCL/PLs have been reported in the orbital fat and conjunctiva {24819207;23328779;33154330;30567236}. Angiolipomas have been described in the subcutis and anterior orbit {24927028;30908468;3401137}.

Clinical features

Lipomas are slow growing and often asymptomatic. Progressive enlargement may cause proptosis, globe displacement, or diplopia. They are unilateral in > 60% of cases {12511353} in contrast to prolapsed orbital fat, which is frequently bilateral.

Imaging

CT demonstrates a well-circumscribed, non-enhancing mass with homogeneous low attenuation displacing but not infiltrating adjacent structures. On MRI, T1-weighted images show a mass isointense to fat and hypointense to muscle, whereas T2-weighted images show lipomas to be hyperintense to muscle.

Epidemiology

The true incidence of these tumours is unknown, because of incomplete immunohistochemical and molecular characterization of nearly all previously reported cases. Most previously reported orbital lipomas and SCL/PLs may in fact have been misdiagnosed herniated orbital fat (especially when bilateral) and atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma {12511353;32426822;20639786;30908464;24819207}. Some reported cases of conjunctival SCL/PL may in fact have been misdiagnosed conjunctival stromal tumour {33154330;30567236}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic abnormalities are found in as many as 75% of lipomas, most often affecting the 12q13-q15 region. Chromosomal rearrangements of 12q target the HMGA2 gene. The pathogenesis of lipomas is related to expression of the HMGA2 protein, which plays a role in the development of the mesodermal lineage during embryogenesis {15593017}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lipomas are soft, well-circumscribed, encapsulated, lobulated, yellow-tan lesions, while angiolipomas are usually encapsulated and yellow to reddish. The cut surface of SCL/PL may be yellow or grey-white, or myxoid. Extensively myxoid tumours can be gelatinous.

Histopathology

Lipoma: Lipomas are composed of lobules of mature adipocytes often separated by thin bands of fibrous tissue. The adipocytes are uniform but may demonstrate slight variation in size and shape compared with normal fat.

Angiolipoma: Angiolipomas contain mature adipose tissue with a proliferation of small-calibre vessels most prominent at the periphery and may have variable amounts of intravascular thrombi.

SCL/PL: SCL is composed of variable amounts of mature adipocytes, bland spindle cells, and ropy collagen. PL has similar features to SCL but also contains pleomorphic and multinucleated floret-like giant cells. Adipocytes with intranuclear vacuoles (Lochkern cells) and lipoblasts may occasionally be observed.

Immunohistochemistry

Lesional adipocytes are HMGA2-positive, which helps the distinction from normal adipose tissue {20834238}.

Neoplastic SCL/PL spindle cells express CD34 with loss of nuclear RB1 expression {22790852;15593017;17255763}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis mainly includes herniated orbital fat and atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma.

Cytology

Lipomas show adipocytes with a single lipid droplet and flattened nucleus on aspiration. SCL/PLs also show uniform spindle cells and eosinophilic collagen fibres, while PL may also contain multinucleated giant cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Lipoma: The presence of cytogenetic abnormalities in chromosome 12 {19454946} distinguishes these lesions from prolapsed orbital fat, which has no copy-number alterations {24588545}, and the absence of MDM2 amplification may be tested when lesions are deep and recurrent, or have equivocal atypia or concerning clinical or radiological features {26146760}.

SCL/PL: Deletion of 13q14 with loss of the RB1 gene and the absence of MDM2 amplification can be helpful in select cases (see above).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for lipoma: circumscribed mass; relatively uniform proliferation of mature adipocytes; absence of hyperchromatic stromal cells within thickened fibrous septa; for angiolipoma: mature fat with clusters of capillaries that have variable amounts of intravascular thrombi; for SCL/PL: mature adipose tissue, spindle cells, and ropy collagen; PLs also contain floret-like multinucleated cells.

Desirable: for lipoma and SCL/PL: absence of MDM2 amplification in challenging cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

While the definitive treatment for benign lipomatous tumours is surgical excision, observation may be a reasonable alternative for these indolent lesions. The prognosis is excellent. Recurrence is rare even after subtotal resection {23328779;17519657;24819207}.
