1.1.2.2 Papillary and follicular conjunctivitis

Definition

Papillary conjunctivitis (PC) is an inflammatory reaction of the conjunctiva characterized by the presence of papillae, composed of oedematous, inflamed fibrovascular stromal cores, covered in non-dysplastic epithelium of variable thickness, manifesting clinically as dome-shaped or flat-topped papillae with a vascular core.

Follicular conjunctivitis (FC) is an inflammatory reaction of the conjunctiva characterized histologically by reactive lymphoid aggregates in the conjunctival substantia propria, manifesting clinically as multiple discrete, rounded elevations of the conjunctiva, with an avascular core.

Localization

PC usually affects the upper tarsal conjunctiva but also the limbal conjunctiva in vernal keratoconjunctivitis {33631915;32339665;32864068;32210533;30366797}.

FC usually involves the palpebral, forniceal, and bulbar conjunctiva and rarely the limbal conjunctiva {32864068;32210533;30366797}.

Clinical features

PC consists of numerous dome-shaped or flat-topped papillae with a vascular core, mainly on the upper tarsal conjunctiva. The diameter of the papillae is variable. Subtypes are based on clinical criteria {28846256} and comprise acute papillary conjunctivitis, chronic papillary conjunctivitis, and giant papillary conjunctivitis (GPC, which is a subtype of chronic papillary conjunctivitis). In GPC, papillae can be ≥ 1 mm. Associated discharge can be purulent, mucopurulent, watery, or stringy, depending on the etiology. Associated clinical features in GPC include corneal shield ulcers, perilimbal Horner–Trantas dots (consisting of degenerate epithelial cells and eosinophils), and conjunctival scarring {23497516;32339665;32864068;30366797}.

In FC, follicles are discrete hemispherical elevations of the conjunctiva. They are pink or white/yellow and have no vascular core. They are usually 0.5–2 mm in diameter and are conspicuous on the superior tarsal and inferior forniceal conjunctiva. Associated exudates can be watery or mucopurulent and can be associated with hyperaemia, photophobia, and a foreign body sensation. Depending on etiology, associated clinical features include corneal subepithelial infiltrates and corneal/conjunctival scarring {32864068;30366797}. A clinical differential diagnosis of FC includes sarcoid granulomas that can present with multiple elevated nodules on the conjunctival surface {17098671}. Clinically defined subtypes are acute follicular conjunctivitis and chronic follicular conjunctivitis.

Epidemiology

PC in association with allergy occurs most often in children, with a male predominance, in warm and dry regions. The prevalence in Europe is estimated to be as high as 1.2–12.6 cases per 10 000 persons, in contrast to 2–37% in Africa {30998721}. Within the group of contact lens wearers, the reported prevalence of GPC varies widely between 1.5% and 47.5%, with frequency associated with several main variables. The prevalence of GPC tends to decrease with the use of rigid versus soft lenses and the availability of single-use versus non-disposable lenses {32339665}.

For FC, the epidemiology varies according to etiology (e.g. chlamydia, virus, toxin, etc.) {32210533}.

Etiology

Acute PC: The main cause is bacteria (Staphylococcus aureus, Haemophilus influenzae, streptococci, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Neisseria meningitidis) {32864068;35027140}.

Chronic PC: This can be caused by lacrimal sac infection, superior limbic keratoconjunctivitis {6830490}, floppy eyelid syndrome {35187400;31593763}, foreign bodies {6837695;6709289;3362510}, mucus-fishing syndrome {4058886}, keratoconjunctivitis sicca {7448154}, and blepharoconjunctivitis {32644328}. GPC {32339665} is usually a component of vernal and atopic keratoconjunctivitis {35015458}, or a complication of exposure to contact lenses {33775382;28540012}, ocular prostheses, or periocular devices (e.g. suture, scleral buckle, glaucoma exoplants) {32339665}.

Acute FC: The main causes are viruses. These include the adenovirus {31891773}, herpesvirus {10966946}, picornavirus {18957336;20724969}, orthomyxovirus {24096642;29321303}, and togavirus families {35456087}; SARS-CoV-2 {32265202;35394858;33595463;32709820}; and the poxvirus family {17026608}. It can also be caused by a few bacteria, for example Chlamydia (inclusion conjunctivitis) {32864068;30366797}.

Chronic FC: The main causes include Chlamydia trachomatis (manifesting as trachoma or inclusion conjunctivitis based on serovars) {35618795;33034349;32864068;30366797}, Moraxella {31167433}, molluscum contagiosum {17026608}, Borrelia (Lyme disease) {12194235}, and toxic causes (eye make-up and therapeutic eye drops [antiviral, antiglaucoma, and cycloplegic agents]) {30366797}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is related to the etiology and involves cellular and humoral responses to infectious agents and exposure to foreign material. Inflammatory responses are also subject to variables within the host’s immune apparatus {30366797;32864068;32210533}.

Histopathology

PC: The conjunctiva forms exophytic papillae that can be dome shaped or flat topped. A papilla is made up of oedematous conjunctival stroma containing capillary-sized vessels perpendicular to the papillae, covered in conjunctival epithelium that can exhibit erosion. The stroma usually contains a variety of inflammatory cell types that can include lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils, and mast cells {8044334}.

FC: The conjunctival substantia propria contains reactive lymphoid aggregates with lymphoid follicles causing dome-like elevation of the overlying epithelial surface. The main histological differential diagnosis for FC includes reactive lymphoid hyperplasia (which clinically presents as a mass lesion rather than multiple follicles) and various types of lymphoma that can clinically present with a follicular conjunctivitis picture. These include follicular lymphoma {26606298;26560164;30653223}, extranodal marginal zone lymphoma {30709807;10201599;14701961}, and mantle cell lymphoma {15162290}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Papillary conjunctivitis

Essential: papillae that can be dome shaped or flat topped; oedematous conjunctival stroma containing capillary-sized vessels perpendicular to the papillae, covered in hyperplastic conjunctival epithelium and containing inflammatory cells.

Desirable: knowledge of the clinical setting to permit an accurate clinicopathological categorization.

Follicular conjunctivitis

Essential: reactive lymphoid aggregates with lymphoid follicles in the substantia propria causing dome-like elevation of the overlying conjunctival surface.

Desirable: knowledge of the clinical setting to permit an accurate clinicopathological categorization.

Staging

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is dependent on the elimination of the inciting agent. The outcomes in PC and FC are etiology dependent {30366797;32864068;32210533}. Keratoconjunctivitis sicca occurs after resolution of conjunctivitis in some cases, possibly owing to damage of goblet cells. Accompanying keratitis with subsequent subepithelial scars in adenovirus-associated conjunctivitis might cause longstanding visual deterioration {18365204}.
