1.2.1.3 Conjunctival oncocytoma

Definition

Conjunctival oncocytoma is a benign neoplasm composed of columnar eosinophilic cells with numerous mitochondria (oncocytes).

ICD-O coding

8290/0 Conjunctival oncocytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.Y & XH9Z86 Other specified benign neoplasm of eye or ocular adnexa & Oxyphilic adenoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: oncocytic adenoma; oxyphilic adenoma.

Not recommended: oxyphilic granular cell adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Periocular oncocytomas occur mostly at the caruncle, and rarely at the bulbar conjunctiva or plica semilunaris. They may also occur in the lacrimal gland (see section Lacrimal gland oncocytoma), lacrimal sac, or lid margin, or peripunctally {19843013;21956543}.

Clinical features

Oncocytoma appears as a smooth or slightly papillomatous, circumscribed, sometimes vascularized, reddish, bluish, brownish, or (rarely) greyish or whitish nodule.

Epidemiology

Oncocytoma is diagnosed in elderly patients and represents 2–7% of benign caruncle tumours {27725148;18927596}. The incidence is estimated to be as high as 0.3 cases per 1 million person-years. Differing results are found in the literature regarding sex predisposition: there was none in a study of 15 US cases {21956543}, while in Denmark an M:F ratio of 1:2 was found in an investigation of 34 cases {19843013}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Oncocytomas arise as a benign proliferation of oncocytes, the cause of which is unknown. Caruncular oncocytomas most probably originate from accessory lacrimal glands of Popoff {19843013} or sweat glands {19032382}. Lacrimal sac oncocytomas arise in seromucinous glands. Accessory lacrimal gland oncocytoma was found to harbour mitochondrial DNA mutations {21555623}.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopically, oncocytoma is an ovoid, solid, sometimes cystic red-brown tumour {21956543}.

Histopathology

Oncocytomas are subepithelial circumscribed neoplasms composed of oncocytes, which are columnar epithelial cells with abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm and monomorphic, ovoid, centrally placed nuclei with a single nucleolus. Neoplastic cells are typically arranged in sheets, nests, and microcystic patterns containing lumina with PAS-positive proteinaceous fluid. Some tumours demonstrate a cystadenomatous architecture with papillary projections into the lumen containing granular, eosinophilic, PAS-positive material. Scattered goblet cells may be present {3025880}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells stain positively for antimitochondrial MU213-UC antibodies {19843013}. The immunohistochemical (cytokeratin) pattern is similar to that of lacrimal and accessory lacrimal gland duct elements {19843013} and also sweat glands {19032382}.

Differential diagnosis

The histopathological differential diagnosis includes oncocytic mucoepidermoid carcinoma, secretory carcinoma, acinic cell carcinoma, apocrine cystadenocarcinoma/carcinoma. Characteristic oncocytic cytomorphological features combined with the absence of nuclear pleomorphism, mitotic figures, and infiltrative margin favour oncocytoma (see also section Apocrine carcinoma; section Secretory carcinoma; section Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of the lacrimal gland).

The clinical differential diagnosis includes melanoma {29344489}, sebaceous hyperplasia {23629804}, papilloma, and conjunctival cyst.

Cytology

The cytology of caruncular/conjunctival oncocytoma has not been documented.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed solid/cystic tumour consisting of columnar oncocytic cells.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Oncocytoma rarely recurs despite positive tumour margins {19843013}. There are no well-documented cases of malignant transformation {19843013;21956543}.
