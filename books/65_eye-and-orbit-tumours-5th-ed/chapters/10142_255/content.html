10.1.4.2 Mesectodermal leiomyoma

Definition

Mesectodermal leiomyoma is a benign tumour arising from the neural crest–derived ciliary body muscle, with hybrid smooth muscle and neural features.

ICD-O coding

8890/0 Mesectodermal leiomyoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.2 & XH4CY6 Benign neoplasm of ciliary body & Leiomyoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Mesectodermal leiomyoma occurs in the suprauveal space and is probably unique to the ciliary body, whereas mesodermal leiomyomas have been described in the iris and choroid {31923479}.

Clinical features

The tumour mass is characteristically dome shaped and yellow to orange in colour with intrinsic vascularity, but can be darkly pigmented and may block transillumination {33748534;25136234}. It may also be asymptomatic and found incidentally, cause anterior staphyloma, or present differently if ring shaped {23519275;27258538;29344475}. Acute presentation due to rapid growth is possible with loss of vision, lens opacification and/or displacement, glaucoma, and retinal detachment {29344486;33304581}.

Epidemiology

There is no known race predilection. Mesectodermal leiomyoma typically affects women in their reproductive age (second to fourth decades of life) but has been reported in postmenopausal women as well {23519275;29344486}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Mesectodermal leiomyomas are thought to arise from the ciliary body muscle, which is of neural-crest origin {870167;4062640}, explaining the characteristic microscopic appearance of both muscular and neural features.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is usually a well-demarcated, round to oval solid mass. The cut surface is typically non-pigmented and grey to white or yellow, with a soft to firm consistency. The tumours reportedly range from 4 to 22 mm in greatest diameter, with most cases not exceeding 10 mm {18930601}.

Histopathology

Mesectodermal leiomyoma is composed of spindle- to epithelioid-shaped cells with abundant fibrillar eosinophilic cytoplasm containing round to oval, finely granular, slightly pleomorphic nuclei {15419251;870167;2920150}. Perinuclear cytoplasmic vacuoles are often seen {4062640}. Scattered melanocytes can be present {4062640}.

Immunohistochemically, this tumour is positive for SMA, MSA, caldesmon, calponin, and desmin {2696714;18930601;12490985;8155052}, and can express CD56 and NSE {15086838;17070579;18930601;34424080}. It is negative for GFAP, S100, HMB45, and melan-A {15086838;17070579;18930601}. Some cases express nuclear sex hormone receptors {23519275;33304581}.

Transmission electron microscopy shows myofilaments with fusiform densities, characteristic of smooth muscle, and fibrillary cytoplasmic processes containing myofilaments with fusiform densities arranged in a neurogenic pattern {870167;4062640}. Some cases show skeinoid fibres {9355238}.

The differential diagnosis includes ciliary body melanocytic naevus, ciliary body melanoma, neurofibroma, and schwannoma. These can be distinguished from mesectodermal leiomyoma by attention to cytological features and a judicious immunohistochemical panel.

Cytology

Cell blocks from FNAs demonstrate uniform cells with oval nuclei and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm {29071270}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no studies on the molecular pathology of this tumour.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: morphology of spindle-shaped cells with abundant fibrillar eosinophilic cytoplasm containing round to oval nuclei; positive SMA, MSA, caldesmon, calponin, and desmin; negative melanocytic markers.

Desirable: expression of neural markers such as CD56 and NSE.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The tumour is usually slowly progressive and complete local resection is generally successful in treating accessible tumours. However, enucleation may be necessary in advanced cases {32448235;33304581;11567967;29344486;29344475}. There is a low risk of recurrence but follow-up is recommended {12923346;32448235}.
