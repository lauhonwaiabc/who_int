10.1.5.5 Solitary circumscribed neuroma

Definition

Solitary circumscribed neuroma (SCN) is a benign nodular proliferation of nerve fibres with Schwann cells predominating over axons, showing a strong predilection for the face.

ICD-O coding

9570/0 Solitary circumscribed neuroma

9570/0 Plexiform solitary circumscribed neuroma

ICD-11 coding

2F24 & XH90Y8 Benign cutaneous neoplasms of neural or nerve sheath origin & Solitary circumscribed neuroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: palisaded neuroma; palisaded encapsulated neuroma; palisaded and encapsulated neuroma.

Subtype(s)

Plexiform solitary circumscribed neuroma

Localization

The skin of the face hosts approximately 90% of SCNs with many lesions arising near mucocutaneous junctions or in oral mucosa {20237984}. They may involve the upper or lower eyelids {11466253;20683368;26425742;35175297} but involvement of the palpebral conjunctiva is very rare {27522960}.

Clinical features

SCN usually presents as a slow-growing, firm, painless, circumscribed dermal-based nodule. In the eyelid the main clinical differential diagnosis is basal cell carcinoma. SCN is mostly solitary, but rare examples of multiple SCNs have been reported with no evidence of any associated systemic disease {26460241}.

Epidemiology

SCN is most common in the fifth to seventh decades of life, but it can occur in patients of any age without a sex predilection. In the periocular region, SCN accounts for only about 2% of nerve sheath tumours {28235631} but represented half (5 of 10 cases) of non-syndromic peripheral nerve sheath tumours developing in the eyelid in one study {25794028}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

SCN is a benign tumour of nerve fibres with slow growth over many years and is unrelated to trauma {2660609}.

Macroscopic appearance

SCN usually presents as a circumscribed dermal-based nodule with a firm, pale-grey cut surface, < 10 mm in diameter.

Histopathology

SCN is a circumscribed dermal-based tumour composed of bland spindle-shaped cells with wavy nuclei in a background of hyalinized collagen. The cells often form well-developed fascicles, and some palisading of nuclei within the tumour is characteristically present. A thin perineurial-derived fibrous capsule may surround the lesion, but it is often incomplete at the superficial aspect. Artefactual clefting is a frequent finding, and extension into the subcutis may be present on occasion {4639250}. No cytological atypia is present and mitoses are inconspicuous. The spindle cells are strongly and diffusely positive for S100. Neurofilament protein staining typically identifies the presence of small axons within the tumour {1705947}. Perineurial cells in the capsule stain for EMA, claudin-1, and GLUT1 {20237984}. Occasionally, SCNs may have a multinodular or plexiform growth pattern {19614730;8468415}.

Cytology

FNA of SCN shows a nonspecific paucicellular pattern of bland spindle cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No diagnostic molecular findings have been described.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: circumscribed cutaneous lesion of Schwann cell–rich fascicles of nerve fibres with or without partial encapsulation.

Desirable: S100 positivity with focal neurofilament protein staining; the peripheral capsule is highlighted by perineurial markers (EMA, GLUT1).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

SCN is a benign neoplasm with no metastatic potential.
