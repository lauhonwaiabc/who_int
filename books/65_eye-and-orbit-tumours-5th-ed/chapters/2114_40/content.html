2.1.1.4 Intratarsal cyst

Definition

Intratarsal cysts are benign keratin-containing cysts derived from Meibomian gland ducts.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F36.4 Cysts of eyelid

Related terminology

Acceptable: intratarsal keratinous cyst; tarsal epithelial cyst; Meibomian gland ductal cyst.

Not recommended: intratarsal inclusion cyst; keratocyst; keratic cyst.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intratarsal keratinous cysts arise within the tarsus of the eyelids, with a predilection for the upper eyelids.

Clinical features

Intratarsal keratinous cysts present as painless, well-defined firm nodules fixed to the tarsus, with no signs of inflammation or attachment to the skin. Eyelid eversion reveals a cream-coloured, yellowish, or sometimes blue-grey well-defined intratarsal nodule without associated inflammation {32826823;19875094;29287708;32367161}.

Epidemiology

Sporadic cysts are most common and are usually isolated lesions in the upper eyelids of older individuals (aged in their fifties to seventies). Multiple cysts, cysts in the lower eyelids, or cysts in a young patient can be associated with naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (Gorlin syndrome) {29287708}.

Etiology

The etiology of intratarsal keratinous cysts is not clear. Some cysts are associated with Meibomian gland blockage, trauma, and naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome {29287708;32367161;24403898}.

Pathogenesis

Blockage of a Meibomian gland duct with secondary dilation is one of the postulated mechanisms {32367161;24403898}. The rare association with naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome raises speculation that some cysts may arise from alterations in the sonic hedgehog signalling pathway genes {29287708}, analogous to odontogenic keratocysts {19362041}.

Macroscopic appearance

The lesions are circumscribed and grey to yellow or bluish with a milky or keratinous luminal material.

Histopathology

The cysts have a characteristic stratified squamous epithelial lining that is 4–6 cells thick, with the basal layer consisting of hyperchromatic cells with stringy eosinophilic cuticle in the lumen. The lining cells lack keratohyaline granules and display a corrugated, brightly eosinophilic cuticle similar to that in odontogenic keratocyst and steatocystoma.

Immunohistochemistry is not required for diagnosis. The lining cells and keratin are strongly positive for CK17 while the luminal portion of the lining and the contents may be positive for CEA and EMA {30439722;19875094}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: intratarsal location with a characteristic lining featuring corrugated eosinophilic cuticle without keratohyaline granules.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Treatment consists of complete excision of the cyst lining; partial excision may lead to recurrence.
