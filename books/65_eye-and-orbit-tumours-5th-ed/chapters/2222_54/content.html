2.2.2.2 Squamous cell carcinoma of the eyelid

Definition

Eyelid squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is a malignancy of epidermal keratocytes that exhibit a variety of grades, from dysplasia to invasive carcinoma in situ.

ICD-O coding

8070/3 Squamous cell carcinoma

8070/2 Squamous cell carcinoma in situ (Bowen disease)

8071/1 Keratoacanthoma

ICD-11 coding

2C31.Z & XH0945 Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma & Squamous cell carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: squamous cell epithelioma; epidermoid carcinoma; epithelioma spinocellulare; prickle cell epithelioma; spinalioma.

Subtype(s)

Squamous cell carcinoma in situ (Bowen disease)

Localization

The lower eyelid is most commonly involved, followed by the medial canthus, upper eyelid, and lateral canthus {27146927;20348855}.

Clinical features

SCC is usually painless and presents as an indurated eyelid plaque or nodule. Lesions commonly show keratinization, central ulceration, and rolled-out irregular borders. Subtle tumours can mimic chronic blepharitis wherein eyelid architecture and integrity of eyelashes has to be carefully assessed with a high index of suspicion. A biopsy confirms the diagnosis in suspicious early lesions. Keratoacanthoma-like lesions have also been described. Other presentations include cystic or papillomatous lesions and cutaneous horns {27146927;20348855}. The presence of pain, paraesthesia, hypoesthesia, or extraocular motility disturbances is suggestive of perineural invasion {17020898}.

Epidemiology

Eyelid SCC is the second most common malignancy of the eyelids after basal cell carcinoma {20348855}. In Asian Indians, it is the third, with sebaceous carcinoma being the most common followed by basal cell carcinoma {31049330}. It is seen in elderly fair-skinned individuals with chronic sun exposure. Children and young adults with xeroderma pigmentosum can develop SCC at a younger age {27146927}. Men are 2–3 times more commonly affected than women {20348855}.

Etiology

The most significant risk factor is exposure to ultraviolet A (UVA) light and actinic damage. Other factors include radiation, hydrocarbons, arsenic, and immunosuppression. Individuals with fair skin, immunosuppression, albinism, xeroderma pigmentosum, and epidermodysplasia verruciformis are prone to developing SCC {20348855}. SCC can arise de novo or from precursor lesions such as actinic keratosis or Bowen disease {27146927}. Anecdotal reports of the development of SCC in eyelid scar from thermal burns {28221294} and seborrhoeic keratosis {23259202} exist.

Pathogenesis

Laboratory evidence supports a stepwise fashion of evolution of SCC after exposure to UVA light. UVA-induced mutations in TP53 lead to altered apoptosis and proliferation of keratinocytes, which either progress to actinic keratosis or papilloma or regress, subject to the presence or absence of continued UVA exposure and other risk factors. In the event of continued exposure, further mutations in RAS, loss of FAS ligand, and loss of apoptosis ensue, culminating in a malignant neoplasm {20348855}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Many SCCs progress from in situ lesions to progressively more deeply invasive tumours. Classic SCC in situ shows full-thickness keratinocyte atypia with absence of maturation, mitoses at all levels, and dyskeratotic cells. Invasive SCC varies from well to poorly differentiated, with most cases classified as well to moderately differentiated. Some well-differentiated forms histologically resemble keratoacanthoma and are now termed keratoacanthoma-like SCC (see the Skin tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Skin Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2025. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 12). https://publications.iarc.who.int/650.]]). The main histological features distinguishing keratoacanthoma and keratoacanthoma-like SCC are outlined in #56553Table 3.01. Invasive tumour shows polygonal cellular profiles, pleomorphism, and varying amounts of cytoplasmic and extracytoplasmic keratin. Immunohistochemistry is generally not required, except for some poorly differentiated SCCs.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells express p63, p40, CK5/6, MNF116 keratin, and 34βE12 keratin, although immunostains are frequently not needed.

Differential diagnosis

Well-differentiated SCC may be difficult to distinguish from keratoacanthoma. Although there is still controversy, cutaneous keratoacanthoma is probably an entity separate from SCC, with different clinical behaviour, in particular its spontaneous regression {27323251;37573161;39449378}. Poorly differentiated SCC can resemble sebaceous or basal cell carcinoma.

Cytology

FNAC may be used to evaluate enlarged regional lymph nodes.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Overexpression of EGFR and PDL1 has been reported and could be of clinical relevance {34198863}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a solitary plaque, often progressing to a hyperkeratotic or ulcerated nodule; infiltration of the dermis by islands of atypical cells with variable keratinization.

Desirable: an in situ component may be present; expression of p63, p40, CK5/6, MNF116 keratin, and 34βE12 keratin in morphologically challenging cases.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system for non-melanoma eyelid carcinoma applies and has been validated in several studies {30869769}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for localized lesions amenable to surgical excision and with no locoregional extension or metastasis is excellent. Management of tumours that involve the lacrimal system, orbit, and intracranial cavity can be challenging {27146927}. Although advanced tumours have conventionally warranted exenteration and metastatic lesions have uniformly been fatal, targeted therapy with EGFR inhibitors and immunotherapy with PD1/PDL1 inhibitors have shown promise for both local/regional control and eye preservation in cases of locally advanced orbital and periocular squamous carcinoma {23229707;34625433;34198863}.
