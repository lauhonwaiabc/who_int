4.3.1.2 Nodular hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium

Definition

Nodular hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium is a well-circumscribed proliferation of the inner non-pigmented epithelium of the pars plicata of the ciliary body not associated with injury.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A90.Y Other specified degeneration of iris or ciliary body

Related terminology

Acceptable: adenomatous hyperplasia of the ciliary body; Fuchs adenoma.

Not recommended: coronal adenoma; age-related hyperplasia of the non-pigmented ciliary body epithelium; pseudoadenomatous hyperplasia of the ciliary body epithelium.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium is located in the pars plicata of the ciliary body and is usually confined to a single ciliary process.

Clinical features

Hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium is typically an incidental finding in an enucleated eye, because it is generally small and asymptomatic. It can be imaged by ultrasound biomicroscopy {32830591}. It can occasionally cause a sectoral cataract or grow to sufficient size to involve the iris root. Rarely, a larger hyperplasia may mimic a malignant tumour of the ciliary body {32434227}. These lesions can be solitary or multiple and unilateral or bilateral.

Epidemiology

Hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium is associated with age. At autopsy, these lesions are found in only about 5% of eyes from individuals < 50 years of age, but in about 60% of eyes from individuals > 80 years {25545484}.

Etiology

Hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium is an age-related degenerative lesion.

Pathogenesis

This type of hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium results in a nodular growth pattern. It appears to be associated with ageing. No genetic profile has been published.

Macroscopic appearance

Hyperplasias of the ciliary epithelium appear as small, yellowish-white or slightly pigmented nodules in a single ciliary process in the pars plicata of the ciliary body.

Histopathology

Hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium forms a small nodule that is composed of interlacing cords of uniform non-pigmented ciliary epithelial cells, surrounded by amorphous extracellular hyaline material that stains with PAS. The nodules may be pedunculated, countersunk, or embedded in the stroma of the ciliary body. In some cases, the extracellular material may be the predominant feature, with only occasional strands of epithelial cells. The extracellular material is immunoreactive for collagen IV and laminin {25545484}. Immunoreactivity for vimentin is reported {12180792}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nodule of interlacing cords of uniform non-pigmented ciliary epithelial cells surrounded by PAS-positive amorphous extracellular hyaline material.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Hyperplasia of the ciliary epithelium is a benign reactive proliferation and usually an incidental finding in an enucleated eye. The prognosis for vision is excellent. Occasionally, it can cause sectoral cataract or grow to sufficient size to involve the iris root and simulate a malignant neoplasm of the ciliary body, necessitating removal {32434227}.
