1.1.1.1 Epithelial conjunctival inclusion cyst

Definition

Epithelial conjunctival inclusion cysts, lined by conjunctival or caruncle-type epithelium, occur within the substantia propria of the conjunctiva and caruncle.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

9A61.6 Conjunctival cysts or argyrosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: conjunctival epithelial cyst.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Epithelial inclusion cysts reside within the substantia propria of mostly non-limbal bulbar conjunctiva or caruncle.

Clinical features

Cysts are transparent to cloudy, mostly compressible, and mobile or tethered to surrounding structures.

Epidemiology

Epithelial inclusion cysts are diagnosed at any age. There is no known sex predilection.

Etiology

Congenital inclusion cysts arise from heterotopic rests of epithelium. Acquired cysts originate from epithelial implantation after either accidental trauma or surgery {9577816}. Symptoms or signs can arise years after the initiating event {23557018}.

Pathogenesis

Epithelial conjunctival inclusion cysts may be congenital, arise spontaneously, or arise after surgical or accidental trauma {31755426}.

Macroscopic appearance

Thin-walled whitish cystic structure

Histopathology

Conjunctival inclusion cysts are lined by conjunctival-type, non-keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium with or without goblet cells. The cavity can contain proteinaceous material and be surrounded by a band of stromal fibrosis and inflammation. A traumatic implantation cyst will reflect the type of surface epithelium implanted in the stroma. The caruncle is a transition zone of mucous membrane and skin and therefore can give rise to epidermal inclusion cysts, lined by skin-type epidermis or conjunctival-type epithelium {216128;3789057}.

Differential diagnosis

For the conjunctiva, the differential diagnosis includes cystic crypts of Henle, dilated lymphatics (lymphangiectasia), and non–epithelial-lined lesions that can form from ocular fistulae (e.g. filtration blebs). For the caruncle, the differential diagnosis includes infundibular cyst, steatocystoma, and cystic oncocytoma {216128;14711437}.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not applicable

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cyst in the substantia propria, lined by benign conjunctival-type epithelium or benign caruncle-type epithelium.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision is curative.
