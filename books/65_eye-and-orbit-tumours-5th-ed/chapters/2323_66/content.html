2.3.2.3 Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma

Definition

Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma is an aggressive adnexal neoplasm composed of neoplastic cells with signet-ring or histiocytoid morphology that diffusely involves the dermis and subcutis of the eyelid.

ICD-O coding

8490/3 Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH0XE5 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Signet ring cell/histiocytoid carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: monocle tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Dermis and subcutis in the eyelid and periocular skin

Clinical features

Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma is most common in elderly men, where it presents insidiously as painless swelling, often affecting both upper and lower eyelids, and sometimes simulating periorbital oedema {30976586;33054772;33315842;34923671}, and can lead to a monocle appearance. The nonspecific nature of the clinical features can frequently lead to delayed diagnosis.

Epidemiology

The tumour has an M:F ratio of about 4:1 {21317710}. The reported age range is 42–87 years, with a median patient age of 66 years {21317710}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Next-generation sequencing has shown mutations in CDH1, the gene encoding E-cadherin {32358805;33617508;34804623}. Mutations in this gene are known to confer signet-ring morphology in other carcinomas. Mutations in NTRK3, p27 (CDKN1B), ERBB2, TP53, and PIK3CA have also been detected {33617508}. It is suggested that mutations particularly in CDH1 induce E-cadherin dysfunction which may play a role in tumour progression.

Macroscopic appearance

In exenteration specimens there may be a poorly defined periocular mass often involving both upper and lower eyelids. Complete surgical resection may be difficult to accomplish given the single-file nature of tumour cells and thus difficulty in achieving negative margins with conservative surgery.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of signet-ring or histiocytoid epithelial cells (exhibit eosinophilic granular cytoplasm) which diffusely infiltrate the collagen bundles of the dermis with sparing of the epidermis. The tumour cells may show a single-file pattern. Immunohistochemical staining is positive for cytokeratins (pancytokeratin and CK7). Staining is positive for GATA3, EMA (MUC1), MUC5AC, and AR, and with BerEP4 {34156717}. GCDFP-15 and E-cadherin stain positive in the majority of cases {33054772}. ER and PR are variably expressed {30976586}.

Differential diagnosis

Metastatic breast lobular carcinoma and gastrointestinal adenocarcinomas with a signet-ring cell morphology are the main differential diagnoses. Immunohistochemistry (negative staining for CDX2 and CK20) is helpful to confirm a skin primary. However, a full clinical assessment is essential to rule out breast and gastrointestinal primaries.

Cytology

There are no reports of cytology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CDH1 mutations are not in routine use to assist diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffuse infiltrate of signet-ring or histiocytoid cells; exclusion of metastatic signet ring carcinoma from other sites.

Desirable: immunohistochemical expression of cytokeratins and AR.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system for non-melanoma eyelid carcinoma applies.

Prognosis and prediction

There are limited data, owing to the small number of cases. Treatment usually requires exenteration with or without adjuvant radiotherapy. About of one third of cases are reported to develop metastasis over a 10-year follow-up period {33054772}.
