8.1.1.1 Orbital dermoid cyst and other orbital cysts

Definition

Dermoid cyst is a developmental lesion that is considered a choristoma with ectodermal and mesodermal components that are not normally present at the involved site but are histopathologically normal.

Localization

The incidence of periocular dermoid cysts is approximately 1 in 650 live births {30260262}. About one third of the epidermoid and dermoid cysts in the head and neck region occur in the orbit {32796308}. Orbital dermoid cysts have been classified as superficial (simple) or deep (complicated) based on their location with respect to the orbital septum {542255}. Deep cysts can be medial, lateral, superolateral, retrobulbar, or inferior, with the commonest (60–80%) being superotemporal in relation to the frontozygomatic suture {8541260;9430304;10619299;21267629;27599687}. In a large series, superficial dermoid cysts accounted for 71%, while 29% were deep in location {31712044}. Dumbbell dermoid cysts with connection to the temporalis fossa may cause visual disturbance and were observed in 5.7% in one series {9076785;29780914;31712044}.

Clinical features

The clinical features of dermoid cysts depend on their location. Superficial dermoid cysts present earlier, before the age of 1 year, as asymptomatic, slowly progressive, painless, palpable subcutaneous masses, and are usually fixed to the underlying bone {27599687;31712044}. Ocular sequelae such as amblyopia in these cases are rare {30260262}. Deep cysts are usually detected later in life, are clinically partially palpable or non-palpable, and may present with globe dystopia {8541260;27599687;31712044}. These deep dermoid cysts are associated with possible bone destruction and invasion especially if they have a subperiosteal component or undergo spontaneous rupture of the wall. Thus, they are better evaluated by radiological imaging {10619299;28089745;28991502;30466230}.

Dermoid cysts may have a characteristic dumbbell appearance with two cystic components: one involving either the superficial or deep orbit and the other involving the temporalis fossa or nearby spaces such as the frontal sinus, or the cranial fossa, with a narrow intraosseous communication through the bone resulting in a bony defect or even destruction of the lateral orbital wall depending on the location of the two interconnected cystic components {19016659;22801126}.

Epidemiology

Dermoid cysts account for 46% of orbital lesions in children {15110666} and are the commonest cystic lesion of the orbit in the paediatric age group accounting for 62% of all cysts, followed by developmental cysts in association with microphthalmos in 25.5% {2487190;30932383;32959608}. The incidence of dermoid cysts is 1 in 638 live births {30260262}. Age at presentation ranges from one month to 27 years for superficial dermoid cysts, while deep cysts tend to present later at 3–45 years {31712044}. A male predominance has been found {31712044}, but not consistently reported in the literature {30260262}.

Etiology

Dermoid cysts result from entrapment of surface ectoderm near sites of embryological bony suture lines {21267629;31712044}.

Pathogenesis

The entrapped surface ectoderm continues to slowly grow producing a cystic mass.

Macroscopic appearance

A dermoid cyst is usually oval or round in shape. The cystic mass is firm in consistency with a thin wall often showing protruding hair. Upon sectioning of the cyst, keratin-like cheesy material fills the cavity of the cyst with possible protruding hairs inside the cyst.

Histopathology

The wall of a dermoid cyst is typically keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium with skin appendages {9430304}. In a large clinicopathological study, hair shafts were observed in the wall and/or the cavity of the cyst in 99% of specimens, sebaceous glands in 75%, and (eccrine) sweat glands in 20% {9430304}. Chronic inflammation with collections of foreign body–type giant cells has been variably reported in nearly two thirds of cases in relation to the spontaneous rupture of the cyst wall and leakage of the contents {9430304;12045954;31712044;28991502}. The inflammatory response may completely replace the epidermal wall without collapse of the cyst (in 16%) {12045954}. This rupture of the dermoid cyst wall with granulomatous reaction has been reported to be significantly associated with a higher median age of patients {12045954;28991502}.

The differential diagnosis includes a wide array of other cystic lesions which may occur in the orbit. These may be lined by various types of epithelia or may be non-epithelial. The latter include both developmental abnormalities of the eye, such as microphthalmos with cyst, and infectious entities, such as those due to Echinococcus. The features of some other orbital cysts are described in #34801Table 9.02.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cystic structure lined by keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium with epidermal appendages within the wall of the cyst.

Desirable: eccrine sweat glands and possible granulomatous reaction in the case of spontaneous rupture of the cyst wall.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Dermoid cysts require complete surgical excision. There is a risk of intraoperative rupture of the cyst in about 10% of the cases as a whole reaching 56.3% in exophytic cysts that are removed via skin crease incision {27599687;30260262}. Cysts may require decompression before surgical excision {31712044}. Location of the cyst (superotemporal versus superomedial) does not seem to affect the morbidity or risk of cyst rupture {28089745}. Intraoperative cyst rupture or incomplete surgical excision increases the chance of recurrence, postoperative inflammation, and fistula formation {1115198;20654053}. Recurrence is reported to range from 4.2% to 6.3% {8195964;27599687;30260262}.

Other characteristics

Conjunctival cyst

{678180;9267597;9782472}

Non-keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium and goblet cells but without appendages

Tumour tends to present later in life, is usually soft, results in minor symptoms (eyelid fullness with no proptosis), and is mostly anterior and superomedial in location without bony defect

Conjunctival dermoid cyst

{678180}

Non-keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium with goblet cells and adnexal structures in the wall

Symptoms usually develop over years; usually involves the superomedial quadrant of the orbit, lacks osseous defect, and may cause proptosis {12045960}

Epidermoid cyst

{25276416}

Keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium with absent adnexal structures

Can be either primary (congenital) or secondary (implantation);a the primary cysts are usually slowly growing and deep in the orbit, and present at a relatively older age

Respiratory epithelial choristomatous cysta

{4088630;11766031;25314310;27097398}

Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells, filled with mucoid material; believed to arise from sequestered respiratory epithelium from paranasal sinuses during development

Located in orbital sites that are atypical for dermoids but may be associated with bony defect {12213161}; has been reported also in the eyelid {33063330}; usually follow a benign course but may result in strabismus and amblyopia in young children {25314310}; can be intraconal in location causing difficulty in the management {27097398}

Choristomatous lacrimal gland cyst

{9267597;7270633}

Thin ductal epithelial lining with or without fibrous capsule and adjacent nest of ectopic lacrimal gland tissue

Develops in relation to ectopic lacrimal gland tissue with ductal-like cyst formation

Ossifying cystic odontogenic and Schneiderian choristoma

{24171664}

Lined by Schneiderian cystic epithelium with adjacent mucinous oral glands, tooth, and lamellar bone

A single reported case of a 12-year-old girl with infraorbital-located recurrent mass {24171664}

Phakomatous choristoma

{10406159;31640459}

Islands of lenticular-like material surrounded by thick basement membrane

Rare, benign congenital tumour of lenticular anlage that can be partially cystic; almost always located in the medial lower eyelid and anterior orbit
