12.0.1.4 Von Hippel-Lindau syndrome

Definition

Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome (VHL) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by pathogenic germline variants of the VHL tumour suppressor gene (located at chromosome 3p25.3) and characterized by the development of haemangioblastoma of the CNS and retina, clear cell renal cell carcinoma (RCC), phaeochromocytoma, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours, and endolymphatic sac tumours.

Subtype(s)

Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome types 1, 2A, 2B, and 2C

Localization

Haemangioblastomas most often involve the retina, cerebellum, and spinal cord (especially paraspinal nerve roots), but they can occur anywhere along the craniospinal axis, including peripheral nerves and even tissues outside the nervous system {15122713;16981244;17219830;24145646;29189208;29941223}. Endolymphatic sac tumours arise from the vestibular aqueduct and may invade through the temporal bone and into the cerebellopontine angle {16322231;25944396}.

Clinical features

Retinal haemangioblastomas manifest at a mean age of 25 years (earlier than RCC) and thus offer the possibility of an early diagnosis {28650583}. CNS haemangioblastomas develop mainly in young adults (mean age: 29 years). They are predominantly located in the cerebellum and next most commonly in the brainstem or spinal cord {29379961}. Approximately 25% of all cases of CNS haemangioblastoma are associated with hereditary VHL. Key clinical characteristics of VHL-associated versus sporadic haemangioblastoma are presented in #56525Table 13.03.

Renal cysts and clear cell RCCs are typically multifocal and bilateral. Age of onset is 16–67 years with a mean of 37 years (compared with 61 years for sporadic clear cell RCC). Patients with VHL have a 70% chance of developing clear cell RCC by the age of 70 years. Metastatic RCC is the leading cause of death from VHL.

Adrenal phaeochromocytomas arise in 20% of patients with VHL; the mean age at onset is 30 years.

Pancreatic manifestations are predominantly multiple cysts but also neuroendocrine tumours {29748190}.

Other VHL-associated tumours include endolymphatic sac tumours (associated with hearing loss, tinnitus, and vertigo) and epididymal and broad ligament cystadenomas.

VHL type 1 is characterized by haemangioblastomas and RCCs (phaeochromocytomas are rare or absent), and it is typically caused by VHL deletions, truncations, or missense mutations that are predicted to highly disrupt VHL protein expression or function {20301636}. VHL type 2 is mainly associated with missense mutations that are less severe in impact on VHL protein expression and function. Type 2A is characterized by haemangioblastomas and phaeochromocytomas (RCCs are rare); Type 2B by haemangioblastomas, RCCs, and phaeochromocytomas; and Type 2C by phaeochromocytomas (haemangioblastomas and RCCs are absent).

Epidemiology

VHL is estimated to have an annual incidence rate of 2.8 cases per 100 000 population.

Etiology

VHL is caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic sequence variants or deletion of VHL at chromosome 3p25.3; more than 1000 variants have been identified spread over the three exons and are highly penetrant. Missense mutations are the most common, but nonsense mutations, in-frame deletions/insertions, splice-site mutations, and large/complex deletions also occur {20151405}. The heterogeneous clinical manifestations of VHL are a reflection of the diversity of germline mutations.

Pathogenesis

Mutational inactivation of the VHL tumour suppressor gene in affected family members is responsible for their genetic susceptibility to tumour development at various organ sites. However, the mechanisms by which the inactivation or loss of VHL protein causes neoplastic transformation are only partly understood {25533676}. The cell of origin (haemangioblast, stromal cell) is not well defined, but current evidence points to a developmentally arrested haemangioblast precursor {14612494}. Mutations are also common in sporadic haemangioblastomas (occurring in as many as 78% of cases) and ubiquitous in clear cell RCCs.

VHL protein has many different functions, and it is critically involved in protein degradation. A complex of the VHL protein α domain, elongin B, and other proteins has ubiquitin ligase activity, thereby targeting cellular proteins for ubiquitination and proteasome-mediated degradation. The α domain binding site for elongin B is frequently mutated in neoplasms associated with VHL.

VHL protein plays a key role in cellular oxygen sensing by polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of hypoxia-inducible factors (HIF1α and HIF2α) {10861440;9665461}. In the absence of functional VHL protein, HIF1α accumulates and activates the transcription of several hypoxia-inducible genes such as VEGFA and EPO, leading to a pseudohypoxia state characterized by altered expression of genes that drive vascularization, cyst formation, lipid storage, metabolic adaptation, and extramedullary erythropoiesis. Constitutive overexpression of VEGFA {7533661;9370235} explains the extraordinary vascularization of neoplasms associated with VHL due to increased angiogenesis/vasculogenesis, as well as the formation of cysts due to increased vascular permeability {11245271}. Increased erythropoietin expression is common in haemangioblastomas {9787178} and is responsible for intratumoural (extramedullary) haematopoiesis and for the paraneoplastic erythrocytosis syndrome that can occur in patients with VHL. HIF-dependent downregulation of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A leads to enhanced lipid storage, a characteristic of VHL-dependent tumours {5701259}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of VHL-associated tumours is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

The histopathology of VHL-associated tumours is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts. The three major types of cells of the retinal capillary haemangioblastoma are endothelial cells, pericytes, and stromal cells. The stromal cells are frequently foamy in appearance.

Cytology

The cytology of VHL-associated tumours is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

VHL sequencing and deletion/duplication analysis performed on peripheral blood to identify a germline heterozygous pathogenic mutation confirms the diagnosis of VHL.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of pathogenic germline variant in VHL and manifestation of any of the associated neoplasm(s).

Desirable: manifestation of haemangioblastomas of retina and/or CNS and any other associated neoplasm in the absence of a pathogenic germline sequence variant in VHL.

Staging

Stage as appropriate for the tumour type.

Prognosis and prediction

The median life expectancy of patients with VHL is 49 years. Outcome is improved by screening that allows earlier detection of malignancies. Clinical surveillance guidance has been published {24355456;28620007}.
