9.3.2.1 T-cell lymphomas

Definition

Ocular adnexal T-cell lymphomas (OATCL) are a family of extranodal mature non-Hodgkin lymphomas. They can be primary but are usually secondary to systemic involvement.

ICD-O coding

Code according to lymphoma type.

ICD-11 coding

Code according to lymphoma type.

Related terminology

Acceptable: mature T-cell lymphoma; peripheral T-cell lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

Not applicable

Localization

OATCL may arise in the eyelid, conjunctiva/caruncle, orbital soft tissues, and lacrimal system. The eyelid skin particularly and orbit are the most frequently involved sites {17255761;10571346;9709754;27894880}.

Clinical features

Depending on subtype and site, OATCLs usually present with unilateral erythema and/or ulceration and swelling (eyelid), masses with/without foreign body sensation or tearing, proptosis and/or diplopia, and reduced vision {17255761;9709754;32732342;27894880;30144455}.

Epidemiology

OATCLs account for < 1–3% of all ocular adnexal lymphomas. The relative frequencies of the various T-cell lymphoma types in the eyelid are different than in the rest of the ocular adnexa, where T-cell lymphomas are extremely rare. Mycosis fungoides, peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) NOS, anaplastic large cell lymphoma, γ/δ T-cell lymphoma, and adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma have been described in the orbit. Mycosis fungoides is the most common type, followed by PTCL-NOS. They generally occur in patients aged > 50 years and show male predominance. Only lymphomatoid papulosis has female preponderance. However, some mycosis fungoides, cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphomas, and acute T-cell lymphoblastic leukaemias/lymphomas of the eyelid have been reported in much younger patients {17846377;10571346;9709754;28399341;27894880;30144455}.

Etiology

The majority of OATCLs are secondary to systemic disease or correspond to a direct extension {10571346}. Primary OATCLs constitute much less than 1% of ocular adnexal lymphomas overall {20574170;9709754;17255761;28399341}. Causes remain unclear.

Pathogenesis

EBV has been associated with PTCL and anaplastic large cell lymphoma, whereas human T-cell lymphotropic virus type 1 has been linked with adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma {16496268;31564712;27894880;30144455}.

Macroscopic appearance

OATCLs present as ulcerated or papular lesions on eyelid skin and elsewhere in the orbit as a smooth, pinkish mass.

Histopathology

Depending on subtype, OATCLs comprise diffuse infiltration of lymphocytes that are small to medium sized with atypical, irregular nuclei, and scattered plasma cells, eosinophils, histiocytes, and occasional large cells (centroblasts, immunoblasts, or Reed–Sternberg–like cells). Remnants of reactive follicles, small blood vessels, and/or fibrous bands may be seen.

Immunohistochemistry

T-cell–associated antigens (CD3, CD5, CD4, CD8) are positive, with a CD4 predominance, and other markers including CD30, CD56, ALK-1, TIA1, and granzyme B may be positive depending on subtype. Ki-67 is high (60–70%). Tumour cells are negative for B-cell–associated antigens. EBV / EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) in situ hybridization may be positive, depending on the lymphoma type.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnosis includes reactive processes, or any B-cell lymphoma in which infiltrates may be enriched in reactive T cells.

Cytology

FNAB is not recommended for the diagnosis of lymphomas or inflammatory processes of the ocular adnexa.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of clonal TR gene rearrangements may help in the diagnosis. Refer to the Haematolymphoid tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid Tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO Classification of Tumours Series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]. It is likely that OATCLs, especially in eyelid skin, share similar genetic aberrations to their counterparts in other locations but these are difficult to confirm owing to their rarity.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: T-cell lymphoma (confirmatory immunohistochemistry); exclusion of B-cell lymphoma; exclusion of reactive process.

Desirable: demonstration of clonal T-cell population; cell-of-origin subtyping; cytogenetics; EBV-associated markers (depending on type).

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and eighth-edition TNM staging systems better predict outcome and are preferred over the Ann Arbor staging system for primary ocular adnexal lymphoma {31768360;28399341;27894880;24180616}.

Prognosis and prediction

Tumour type is the most important prognostic indicator. Primary OATCLs generally have a good disease-specific survival, especially those of the eyelid, whereas conjunctival T-cell lymphomas have a poorer prognosis. Secondary and high-stage disease are predictors of poorer prognosis. Most OATCLs are secondary manifestations of systemic disease and thus carry a poor prognosis with higher rates of recurrence and relapse {32732342;28209503;10571346;27894880}.
