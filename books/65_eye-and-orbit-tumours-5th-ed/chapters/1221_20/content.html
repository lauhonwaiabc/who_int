1.2.2.1 Conjunctival squamous intraepithelial neoplasia

Definition

Conjunctival squamous intraepithelial neoplasia (CSIN), also known as conjunctival intraepithelial neoplasia, is a preinvasive lesion manifesting as a range of aberrant cytological, maturational, and architectural changes, leading to varying degrees of intraepithelial squamous dysplasia, ranging from mild to severe dysplasia, and progressing ultimately to in situ squamous carcinoma.

Related terminology

Acceptable: conjunctival intraepithelial neoplasia; intraepithelial neoplasia of the conjunctiva; conjunctival squamous dysplasia.

Not recommended: ocular surface squamous neoplasia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CSIN generally occurs on the conjunctival surface that is sun exposed. The most affected sites are the limbus and the nasal and temporal bulbar conjunctiva {30593587;21071282;26378883}. The lesions frequently show corneal invasion and occasionally involvement of the forniceal or palpebral conjunctiva {30593587;21071282;26378883;14711437}.

Clinical features

CSIN has variable clinical appearances: it can have papilliform, gelatinous, or leukoplakic (secondary to overlying hyperkeratosis) forms. The commonest type is gelatinous with feeder blood vessels and a pearly appearance, but lesions may be opaque or show loss of lustre with white plaques on the surface (leukoplakia). Most CSIN lesions are slowly growing, minimally elevated, and near the limbus, and arise within the interpalpebral fissure causing redness, irritation, and foreign body sensation. As many as 30% of cases can be asymptomatic {11045245;14711437;21071282}. While most lesions are typically non-pigmented, some may be melanotic in patients with more pigmented skin (Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI) {3377516} and misinterpreted clinically as conjunctival melanoma {11438062}. CSIN can be difficult to differentiate from invasive squamous neoplasia clinically; however, an intraepithelial lesion is usually freely mobile, in contrast to an invasive lesion, which can be fixed to the underlying episclera {21071282}.

“Ocular surface squamous neoplasia” is a clinical term and should not be used to describe the histopathology of CSIN {24807632}.

Epidemiology

CSIN is the most common preinvasive malignancy of the ocular surface. Its incidence varies geographically: in the United Kingdom, it is estimated at 0.4 cases per 1 million person-years, whereas Australia shows an incidence of 19 cases per 1 million person-years {30593587;1562262}.

Etiology

Chronic exposure to ultraviolet B (UVB) radiation and immunosuppression are the main risk factors for CSIN, supported by ecological and mechanistic evidence {32461624;32717381;8676629;24030075}. A subgroup of tumours is strongly associated with HPV {33191633}, exhibiting histopathological and genomic features pathognomonic of HPV-induced disease {31485010;34779821}. Additional proposed risk factors include cigarette smoking, atopy, exposure to petroleum products or chemicals, DNA-repair deficiencies, chronic inflammation, and vitamin A deficiency {24237784;28509683}.

Pathogenesis

CSIN arises from the conjunctival epithelium {17460260}, and the preferred limbal location suggests that many arise from limbal stem cells. Somatic TP53 mutations have been reported {10746446}, along with chromosomal alterations involving loci encoding DNA-repair genes {25491297}. They have been linked to overexpression of telomerase due to TERT promoter mutations consistent with UV radiation–induced damage {26348634}. HPV infection is present in some cases {33191633;31141610;31485010}. However, none of these changes are pathognomonic, as they have also been reported in other conjunctival epithelial neoplasms.

Macroscopic appearance

Lesions may be flat or raised. Flat lesions are usually gelatinous cream/white epithelial thickenings. Raised lesions can be cream/white and papillomatous or verrucous. Surface keratin may be present. Some lesions can exhibit brown pigmentation, particularly in patients with more pigmented skin (Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI).

Histopathology

Aberrant cytological, architectural, and maturation changes constitute dysplasia. At low magnification, CSIN usually shows a sharp demarcation with the background normal conjunctival epithelium, and characteristically with loss of goblet cells. At higher magnification, cytological changes comprise nuclear enlargement, hyperchromatic chromatin, nuclear pleomorphism, and an increased N:C ratio, and nuclei may show prominent nucleoli. Dyskeratosis and apoptosis may be present with mitotic figures at varying levels of the epithelium. The epithelium shows abnormal stratification and loss of polarity and can be covered in keratin and parakeratin. The epithelium can adopt a papillomatous or verrucous configuration {7660300;3951824;3301209;8574978;22771047;33619216;35074957}.

The degrees of dysplasia can be graded as mild, moderate, or severe. Mild dysplasia is confined to the lower third of the epithelium, moderate dysplasia extends into the middle third, and severe dysplasia spreads to the upper third but retains some surface differentiation. Squamous carcinoma in situ shows full-thickness epithelial dysplasia {7660300;3951824;3301209;8574978;22771047;33619216;35074957;19125065;11045245}.

Cases associated with high-risk HPV usually lack surface keratin or parakeratin, tend to exhibit full-thickness epithelial dysplasia (squamous carcinoma in situ) with hyperchromatic crowded oval nuclei without nucleoli, and have scant basophilic cytoplasm with poorly defined cell boundaries, imparting a basaloid appearance associated with apoptosis and numerous mitotic figures {7660300;31485010;31649051}. UV radiation–associated cases can resemble skin actinic keratoses with rete-like ridges, larger eosinophilic polyhedral keratinocytes with larger hyperchromatic and pleomorphic nuclei often with prominent nucleoli, plentiful eosinophilic cytoplasm (that can contain glycogen), intercellular bridges, keratohyaline granules, squamous eddies, and variable surface keratin and parakeratin {7660300;31485010;31649051}. Areas of elastotic degeneration are often present in the stroma, indicating chronic sun exposure, and can contain eosinophils in patients with atopy.

Pigmented CSIN pattern: Squamous intraepithelial neoplasia in patients with more pigmented skin (Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI) can show dendritic melanocytic hyperplasia among the dysplastic squamous epithelium leading to pigmented dysplasia {11438062;6677848;1390513;974995}.

Conjunctival intraepithelial neoplasia with mucinous differentiation pattern: This features dysplastic squamous cells and atypical cells with intracytoplasmic mucin, morphologically resembling dysplastic goblet cells and signet ring–like cells. It may be in some cases a precursor to squamous cell carcinoma with mucinous differentiation, or adenosquamous carcinoma, of the conjunctiva (see section Adenosquamous carcinoma).

Immunohistochemistry

p53 is expressed in atypical cells; CK17 is positive and CK7 can be lost {23786782}. Cases associated with high-risk HPV can be p16-positive, but this finding is not specific in all studies {26735858;29533279;31485010;31649051}.

Differential diagnosis

CSIN can develop in pingueculae, pterygia, and viral squamous papillomas {23856952;26034543;19065760}. See #32566Table 2.01.

Cytology

Exfoliative cytology {6990906;835082;7660300} and impression cytology {11241267;16299150;7867466;11159477;18362650;11423469;21498806;7660300} have been employed to sample the conjunctiva in suspected CSIN. Dysplastic cells show nuclear enlargement, an increased N:C ratio, irregular nuclear outlines, coarsely granular chromatin, nuclear pleomorphism, and nucleoli, with or without keratinization. Features suggestive of carcinoma in situ include syncytial sheets with poorly defined cytoplasmic borders. Impression cytology has a reported sensitivity of 70–82% for squamous dysplasia {7867466;11159477;18362650}. A keratotic surface or deeply located intraepithelial dysplastic cells account for some false negative results {21498806}. Importantly, invasive carcinoma cannot be diagnosed with these techniques.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dysplasia within the conjunctival epithelium characterized by atypical squamous cells with enlarged, often hyperchromatic nuclei and increased mitotic activity extending upward from the epithelial base.

Desirable: immunohistochemical proof of squamous phenotype in morphologically challenging cases; exclusion of sebaceous intraepithelial neoplasia by use of a discriminatory antibody panel, in cases where there is histological uncertainty (e.g. basaloid phenotype).

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system is applied for carcinoma in situ (Tis).

Prognosis and prediction

CSIN is usually an indolent tumour but may progress to invasive squamous cell carcinoma {15763187}. The underlying mechanisms for disease progression are not understood. The tumour has a high risk of recurrence, especially in cases with positive surgical margins. Immunosuppression has been documented to be a risk factor for more aggressive disease {21762990}. Observational studies indicate a reduced recurrence rate with perioperative adjuvant cryotherapy of the conjunctival margins and adjuvant chemotherapy in the case of positive surgical margins {26890663}.
