12.0.1.3 BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome

Definition

BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by pathogenic germline variants in the BAP1 tumour suppressor gene. The syndrome is characterized by a predisposition to various tumours, most frequently including uveal melanoma, mesothelioma, cutaneous melanoma, and renal cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Multiple sites and organ systems are involved, including the eyes {21051595;21941004;32273508}, pleura, peritoneum {21874000}, skin {21874003;25225168}, kidney {23684012}, liver {24243779}, and meninges {21941004;28170043}.

Clinical features

The median age of tumour onset is younger in people with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome than in the general population. Affected individuals can have multiple types of primary cancers {30517737}. Mesotheliomas have substantially different clinical features when arising in BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome: they arise 20 years earlier, lack male predominance, and are frequently peritoneal rather than pleural {25380601;26719535;30376426}. Histologically distinctive BAP1-inactivated naevi/melanocytomas occur in as many as three quarters of germline carriers {28793149;31206729}, yet they are not specific for the syndrome {28560743}. Patients are diagnosed through genetic testing owing to personal or family history of cancer or through identification of cutaneous lesions associated with the syndrome.

Criteria for genetic counselling and testing include (1) a medical history of at least two tumours associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome, (2) one tumour associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome and a first- or second-degree relative with at least one tumour associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome, and/or (3) young age at tumour onset {26096145;30517737;31382694}. In the context of these criteria, loss of BAP1 on immunohistochemistry or detection of BAP1 variants in relevant tumours further supports a need for germline testing {28793149;30517737}. For uveal melanoma, the youngest age reported is 15 years {32509949}. To identify tumours at early stages, screening (including eye examinations, skin examinations, and abdominal MRI) starting 5 years before the earliest reported cancer has been recommended {26096145;29413689;30517737;31382694}. BAP1-inactivated naevi can arise in children, and thus the BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome can be diagnosed at a young age.

Epidemiology

The literature reports 141 families with pathogenic null variants (104 unique), and 85% of mutation carriers in these families presented with one or more cancers {30517737}. Rare missense variants in the catalytic UCH are associated with a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder {35051358} or BAP1-associated cancers. The carrier frequency of germline pathogenic variants is 1 in 19 698 in people without cancer {32461654}, and about 1 in 1299 {29625052} among patients with cancer, suggesting that the syndrome prevalence is underestimated. Two common founder variants, NM_004656.4(BAP1):c.1780_1781insT (p.G594Vfs*49) (in Finland) and NM_004656.4(BAP1):c.1717del (p.L573Wfs*3) (in the USA), have been identified, with the latter traced back to Swiss origins in the 16th century {26683624;30517737}. The lifetime risk of developing cancer is reported to be as high as 80–100%, with many carriers developing multiple cancers; most families have at least two types of tumours in first- or second-degree relatives {26096145;30517737}.

BAP1 is the most frequently mutated gene in sporadic mesothelioma {26928227} and in metastatic uveal melanoma {21051595;21941004;32273508}, but only 1–2% of these BAP1-mutant tumours arise in germline carriers {21874000;25468148;25974357;31058963}. Although the frequency of BAP1 germline mutations is low in the overall population of patients with uveal melanoma, the frequency increases to about 20% in patients with familial occurrence {27718540}, which is defined as the occurrence of uveal melanoma in two or more members of the same blood line {32273508}. Etiology

BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is caused by heterozygous pathogenic germline sequence variants, or more rarely deletions, involving the BAP1 tumour suppressor gene, located at chromosome 3p21.1 {21874000}. The mutations are most commonly deleterious nonsense, frameshift, or splice-site alterations that result in a truncated, non-functional protein (null variants), or deleterious missense mutations that interfere with the ubiquitin hydrolase function of BAP1 {23277170}. More data are required to evaluate the effect of environmental mutagens (e.g. asbestos, ultraviolet [UV] radiation) on modulating the penetrance of germline mutations. De novo BAP1 germline mutations are uncommon {30517737;31382694}. Germline homozygous deletion of BAP1 in mice is lethal in early embryogenesis whereas mice with heterozygous BAP1 deletion are viable with no obvious anatomical abnormalities, but these mice have a higher frequency of spontaneous tumours and increased risk of mesotheliomas upon asbestos exposure {22878500;24928783;26896281}.

Pathogenesis

The BAP1 gene at chromosome 3p21.1 comprises 17 exons encoding a protein with ubiquitin carboxyl hydrolase activity first identified as interacting with BRCA1 {9528852}. BAP1 interactors include ASXL1, ASXL2, FOXK1, FOXK2, HCFC1, and YY1, consistent with roles in DNA damage response, transcriptional regulation, cell-cycle regulation, metabolism, inflammatory responses, and lineage differentiation {28482042;28713672;30517737;31555735}. Nuclear localization and deubiquitination activity are both necessary for BAP1-mediated tumour suppression {18757409}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of tumours in patients with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is similar to that of sporadic tumours.

Histopathology

The histopathology of tumours associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Cytology

The cytology of tumours associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome is similar to that of sporadic tumours.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BAP1 sequencing and deletion/duplication analysis performed on peripheral blood to identify a germline heterozygous pathogenic mutation confirms the diagnosis of BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome. Loss of BAP1 immunoreactivity in tumour nuclei readily identifies deficient tumours; however, concordance between immunohistochemistry and genotyping is high but incomplete {21642991;24633195;25658628;27015033}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of germline pathogenic variant in BAP1.

Note: No phenotypic diagnostic criteria are associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome. Although there are no formal criteria, testing for BAP1 mutations should be considered in patients with (1) two or more confirmed tumours associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome or (2) one confirmed tumour associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome and a first- or second-degree relative with a confirmed tumour associated with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome {29413689;30517737;26096145;27748099}. Loss of BAP1 by immunohistochemistry in these tumours can be used to further screen for individuals who need germline testing {23709298;30517737}.

Staging

Stage as appropriate for the tumour type.

Prognosis and prediction

Sporadic BAP1-mutant tumours are often more aggressive than corresponding tumours lacking such mutations {23620406;30660076}. The prognosis for germline carriers with tumours is incompletely characterized because most studies of single tumour histology contain few patients with BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome. Uveal melanomas arising in BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome have increased risk of metastasis {22545102;25974357}. BAP1-mutant meningiomas display aggressive behaviour with frequent recurrences {28170043;28482042}, although it is unclear whether meningiomas arising in BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome have a different prognosis than sporadic BAP1-mutant meningiomas. By contrast, patients with germline BAP1-related mesothelioma have a better 5-year survival rate (47%) than those with sporadic mesotheliomas (6.7%) {25380601;26719535;30376426}.
