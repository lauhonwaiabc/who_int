10.2.6.1 Ewing sarcoma

Definition

Ewing sarcoma of the orbital bone is a small round cell sarcoma containing a fusion between one FET family gene (usually EWSR1) and one ETS family gene (most often FLI1).

ICD-O coding

9364/3 Ewing sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B52.Y Ewing sarcoma of bone or articular cartilage of other specified sites

Related terminology

Not recommended: (peripheral) primitive neuroectodermal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ewing sarcoma of the orbital area is more likely to arise from orbital bone (83%) than soft tissue (17%) {29219959}. Extraorbital extension occurs in most patients and is mainly intracranial.

Clinical features

The most common presenting complaints were proptosis and upper eyelid swelling in the largest reported series {29219959}.

Epidemiology

Ewing sarcoma of the orbit occurs most commonly in children and young adults {29219959}.

Etiology

The majority of cases are sporadic. Although germline mutations (in BRCA1, for example) have been detected {29977059}, their pathogenetic roles remain unclear.

Pathogenesis

Most tumours harbour a t(11;22)(q24;q12) translocation {6713356;6713357}, resulting in EWSR1::FLI1 fusion {29977059}. The second most common translocation is t(21;22)(q22;q12), which results in EWSR1::ERG fusion {29977059}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface of untreated Ewing sarcoma specimens is greyish-white and soft, and it frequently includes areas of haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

Tumours consist of a monotonous population of small round cells arranged in sheets or solid aggregates, separated by fibrous septa. Focal rosettes are sometimes present. Cells have round nuclei, coarse chromatin, small nucleoli, and scant eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm. Mitotic activity is brisk and necrosis is common.

Immunohistochemistry

There is diffuse, strong, membranous CD99 expression and nuclear FLI1 expression. NKX2-2 has a higher specificity than CD99 {29661713}. Focal positivity with broad-spectrum cytokeratins is common. Desmin, myogenin, and MYOD1 are negative.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

All cases of Ewing sarcoma harbour a FET::ETS fusion. Genetic confirmation is often required for Ewing sarcoma diagnosis. The most common Ewing sarcoma fusion (present in ~85% of cases) is EWSR1::FLI1. The second most common is EWSR1::ERG in about 10% of cases. The remaining cases have alternative translocations that join either EWSR1 or FUS (which, along with TAF15, form the FET family) to other ETS family members.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: small round cell morphology; strong CD99 membranous expression.

Desirable: strong and diffuse NKX2-2 expression; detection of a FET::ETS fusion.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM systems can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for Ewing sarcoma has improved considerably with current multimodal therapy, with a 65–70% cure rate for localized disease. However, metastatic and early relapsing tumours have a poor prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate of < 30%. The presence of metastases appears to be the main prognostic factor. Other prognostic factors include the anatomical location of the tumour, tumour volume, and the patient’s age {29977059}. Complete pathological response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a favourable prognostic factor {27482030}. There are currently no other prognostic markers in widespread use.
