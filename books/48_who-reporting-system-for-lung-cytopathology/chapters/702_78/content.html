7.0.2 Sample reports: Suspicious for malignancy

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format will depend on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and information on the macroscopic features and rapid onsite evaluation (ROSE) are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as "Diagnostic summary" (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Type of specimen:

Sputum sample.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

30 mm mass in left upper lobe. No definite hilar lymphadenopathy. Smoker.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: Suspicious for malignancy

Diagnosis: Atypical glandular cells. See report below.

 

Microscopic description:

Very occasional small, crowded tissue fragments of epithelial cells with large, mildly pleomorphic, occasionally indented nuclei with prominent single round nucleoli and eccentric pale, occasionally vacuolated cytoplasm are present. These epithelial cells lack cilia, and they are mixed with small tissue fragments and sheets of bronchial cells, along with siderophages, histiocytes, neutrophils, and squamous cells.

Comment: These scattered glandular epithelial tissue fragments are suspicious for adenocarcinoma. Correlation with imaging and clinical history is required. Further workup with bronchial brush and FNAB, as clinically indicated, is recommended.

Sample report 2

Type of specimen:

Bronchial brush.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

Previous squamous cell carcinoma of lung. Chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Review. Bronchial brush right middle lobe bronchus.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Bronchial brush right middle lobe bronchus

Category: Suspicious for malignancy

Diagnosis: Atypical squamous cells suspicious for residual squamous cell carcinoma

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show plentiful bronchial epithelial tissue fragments, neutrophils, and histiocytes, with some carbon-laden macrophages. In addition, there are a small number of squamous cells with variable (occasionally high) N:C ratios and moderately enlarged, pleomorphic nuclei with coarse chromatin and prominent nucleoli. There are also large cells with large, sometimes vacuolated nuclei, some of which have blurred chromatin. There is a background of debris but no definite keratinous material or necrosis.

Comment: The features are suspicious for residual squamous cell carcinoma, although therapy-related changes are also present. Correlation with imaging is required, and FNAB should be considered to provide material for typing, staging, and ancillary tests.

Editorial note:

The diagnostic summary includes the specific information regarding the site of the lesion from the request form and specimen container of any needle-rinsing material.

Sample report 3

Type of specimen:

Endobronchial ultrasound–guided FNAB right upper lobe.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

FNAB right upper lobe. Poorly defined 30 mm mass. Immunocompromised. Adjacent hilar lymphadenopathy.

 

Diagnostic summary:

FNAB right upper lobe

Category: Suspicious for malignancy

Diagnosis: Necrosis with scant atypical squamous epithelium suspicious for keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma. See report below.

 

Microscopic description:

There is considerable necrotic material and keratinous debris, as well as two minute tissue fragments of keratinized cells with small pyknotic, hyperchromatic nuclei and a low to moderate N:C ratio. Occasional macrophages are also present. No definite fungi are seen on routine stains.

Comment: The features are suspicious for keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma. Special stains are underway. Correlation with clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings is required.

 

Supplementary report 1:

The cell block shows similar necrotic and keratinous debris, with no viable epithelium present. The methenamine silver stain for fungi is negative, as are the auramine stain for mycobacteria and the Gram stain. Correlation with clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings is required. Consideration should be given to repeat FNAB.

Editorial note:

The diagnostic summary includes the specific information regarding the site of the lesion from the request form and specimen container of any needle-rinsing material.

There has been a supplementary report on special stains for infectious agents for this case performed subsequently on a cell block, and this is included in the single integrated report.
