5.0.0.3 Discussion and background

The “Benign” category includes inflammatory processes and benign neoplasms found in all types of respiratory cytopathology specimens. The category includes those cases where the material is diagnostic of a specific process (such as acute or granulomatous inflammation) or a specific tumour (such as a pulmonary hamartoma), as well as those cases where the normal components of lung tissue (including bronchial cells, alveolar macrophages, alveolar septa and type 2 pneumocytes) are found. Correlation with imaging is required wherever available. The “Benign” categorization should be followed by as specific a diagnosis as possible. If the cytopathological findings are benign but do not correlate with the imaging when it is indeterminate or suspicious, then the case can be categorized as “Benign”, the cytopathological findings should be described, and in the conclusion a caveat should be stated that “the cytopathological material may not represent the lesion seen on imaging”. Recommendations for further diagnostic workup should be given {23020728; 33145073; 25640352}.

Inflammatory lesions that are categorized as “Benign” are presented in this chapter, using a pattern-based approach, in three sections, covering acute inflammation/suppuration, histiocytic/lymphocytic/eosinophilic inflammation, and granulomatous disorders. If possible, the specific type of inflammation should be diagnosed, including wherever possible a specific infectious agent (e.g. Aspergillus spp., Pneumocystis jirovecii, or CMV), or another specific inflammatory process should be diagnosed or suggested (e.g. lipid pneumonia or pneumonia caused by foreign body aspiration). This routinely involves special stains for organisms, such as the Ziehl–Neelsen or related stain for mycobacterial infection, Gram stain for bacteria, Grocott methenamine silver for fungi, and ICC as appropriate. Wherever possible, correlation with microbiological studies is required. If an exact diagnosis is not possible, description and assignment to one of the morphological patterns is helpful for the differential diagnosis of the lesion. A recommendation for further diagnostic workup is appropriate {30568132; 28794143; 30156776; 30889574}.

The presentation of benign neoplastic tumours follows the presentation in the fifth-edition WHO classification of thoracic tumours and includes pulmonary hamartoma, sclerosing pneumocytoma, bronchial papillomas, salivary gland tumours, pulmonary chondroma, paraganglioma, perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa), schwannoma, meningioma, and granular cell tumour. Not every entity described in histopathology has also been described in cytopathology, so not every entity listed in the WHO classification is presented in this volume {30473462; 7781378}.
