4.0.0.6 Sample reports: Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format will depend on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and information on the macroscopic features and rapid onsite evaluation (ROSE) are not presented here.

For consistency, the term “Insufficient” has been selected for these three sample reports. Cytopathologists and their institutions should decide whether their laboratory will use “Insufficient”, “Inadequate”, or “Non-diagnostic” for this category and should not use these terms on an ad hoc or interchangeable basis. See the introduction at the beginning of this chapter for a discussion regarding the use of the term “Non-diagnostic”.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as "Diagnostic summary" (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Type of specimen:

Sputum sample (first of three).

 

Clinical and imaging information:

30 mm solid mass left upper lobe of lung. Presented with haemoptysis. Prior history of colorectal carcinoma. ? metastasis.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: Insufficient

Diagnosis: Insufficient material for diagnostic assessment

 

Macroscopic description:

3 mL of thick, mucoid, grey frothy material.

 

Microscopic description:

Only upper aerodigestive tract components are present, with no bronchial macrophages. The material is insufficient for diagnostic assessment.

Comment: Repeat study or consideration of bronchial wash/brush or FNAB is recommended.

Sample report 2

Type of specimen:

Bronchial wash and brush left upper lobe bronchus.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

30 mm solid mass left upper lobe of lung. Presented with haemoptysis. Prior history of colorectal carcinoma. ? metastasis.

 

Diagnostic summary:

 

Specimen 1:

Bronchial wash

Category: Benign

Diagnosis: Benign bronchial material. However, there is a mass lesion on imaging and this sample may not represent the target lesion.

 

Specimen 2:

Bronchial brush

Category: Insufficient

Diagnosis: Severe air-drying artefact prevents diagnostic assessment.

 

Macroscopic description:

Specimen 1: Bronchial wash left upper lobe bronchus: 4 mL pink frothy saline rinse.

Specimen 2: Bronchial brush left upper lobe bronchus: 2 alcohol-fixed direct smears.

 

Microscopic description:

 

Specimen 1: These highly cellular smears show bronchial epithelium in tissue fragments and as single dissociated cells, with a small number of macrophages and lymphocytes.

Comment: There is benign material. However, there is a mass lesion on imaging and this sample may not represent the target lesion. Repeat study or FNAB should be considered if clinically indicated.

 

Specimen 2: There is a small amount of material showing severe air-drying artefact, preventing cytopathological assessment.

Comment: There is insufficient material for diagnostic purposes. Repeat study or FNAB should be considered if clinically indicated.

Sample report 3

Type of specimen:

FNAB of left upper lobe lung. Endobronchial ultrasound.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

30 mm solid mass left upper lobe of lung. Presented with haemoptysis. Prior history of colorectal carcinoma. ? metastasis.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: Insufficient

Diagnosis: Insufficient material for diagnostic assessment

 

Macroscopic description:

3 air-dried slides, 3 alcohol-fixed slides

3 mL pink normal saline needle rinsings

 

Microscopic description:

Blood only is present on the smears. There is no diagnostic material in the cell block.

Comment: There is insufficient material for diagnostic assessment. Repeat study should be considered if clinically indicated.
