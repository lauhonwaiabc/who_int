5.2.0.8 PEComa

Definition

Pulmonary perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa) is a benign neoplasm of lung that arises from epithelioid perivascular cells.

Clinical features and imaging

PEComas, which have been known as “clear cell tumour of lung”, are more common in elderly patients. They have a slight female predominance and occasionally are associated with tuberous sclerosis and lymphangioleiomyomatosis {9331299; 32690041}. They occur anywhere in the lungs, but most commonly in the lower lobes as a solitary peripheral lung nodule {9331299}. Peripheral nodules are asymptomatic, whereas central lesions may present with shortness of breath, high fever, cough, haemoptysis, or thrombocytosis {15037292; 19932282}.

PEComas are often incidentally discovered by CXR as peripheral coin-shaped, well-demarcated nodules, with no cavitation or calcifications {30363768}. The reported size ranges from 1 mm to 120 mm {17184693}. CT shows intense postcontrast enhancement due to the rich vascular stroma {9331299; 19119463}. PET shows mild FDG uptake {20479562}.

Histopathology

PEComa presents as a well-circumscribed, unencapsulated nodule with a red-tan cut surface. Microscopically it presents as sheets or vague nests of cells surrounded by thin blood vessels. The cells are round to oval and occasionally spindle, with abundant clear or slightly eosinophilic cytoplasm and well-defined cell borders. The nuclei are not mitotically active, and they vary in size and shape but are usually oval with finely granular chromatin and occasional small nucleoli {9383822}. Electron microscopy reveals that the cytoplasm is rich in free and membrane-bound glycogen and premelanosomes {1689555}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Moderately cellular smears
  • Small to large cohesive tissue fragments traversed by thin capillaries, and scattered spindle cells
  • Bland cells with round to oval nuclei with mild anisonucleosis, indistinct nucleoli, and no atypia or mitoses make up the tissue fragments
  • Abundant clear or finely vacuolated cytoplasm and distinct cell borders
  • Rare intranuclear pseudoinclusions
  • Background of blood, lymphocytes, macrophages, and numerous single round to oval bare nuclei

Reference(s): {18181192; 29276673}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes other primary or metastatic clear cell carcinomas, including primary lung adenocarcinoma and acinic cell carcinoma and metastatic renal cell carcinoma. Less frequently, it includes adrenal cortical carcinoma; glycogen-rich clear cell breast carcinoma; clear cell hepatocellular carcinoma; clear cell tumours from the ovary, uterus, and cervix; and melanoma {18181192; 29276673; 15108238; 15945088; 18323277; 16334034; 9250313; 10603192}.

Ancillary testing

PEComas give a positive PAS reaction, reflecting the abundant glycogen content. ICC is positive for HMB45, S100, melan-A (MART1), vimentin, MYOD1, desmin (focally), SMA, α1-antitrypsin, factor XIIIa, NSE, synaptophysin, cathepsin B, and CD57 (LEU7). One report showed a strong reaction for CD1a, but this was not reproduced in other studies {29516164; 1711793; 1689555; 16872440; 12778001; 9720495}. The cells are negative for pancytokeratin AE1/AE3, CK7, CK20, and EMA {29276673; 18181192}.
