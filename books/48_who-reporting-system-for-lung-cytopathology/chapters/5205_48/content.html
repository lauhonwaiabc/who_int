5.2.0.5 Salivary gland neoplasms

Definition

Benign salivary gland neoplasms of the respiratory tract include pleomorphic adenoma, myoepithelioma, and oncocytoma and closely resemble their salivary gland counterparts.

Clinical features and imaging

Clinical symptoms are related to the location and size of the tumours and include cough, wheeze, haemoptysis, and postobstructive changes such as pneumonia. On CXR and CT, the tumour typically appears as a central well-circumscribed polypoid mass arising within the bronchus.

Histopathology

Pleomorphic adenoma is a biphasic tumour consisting of nests, tubules, glands, and branching ducts, with a variable amount of fibromyxoid or chondromyxoid stroma. The epithelial cells are cuboidal, and the myoepithelial cells are large with clear cytoplasm, forming the outer layer of the ducts or tubules, and present in the stroma as spindle cells. Myoepithelioma shows solid, canalicular, or mixed patterns of myoepithelial cells, with no duct formation, and the cells can be epithelioid, plasmacytoid, spindle, clear, or hyaline. The matrix can be hyaline or myxoid. Oncocytoma is composed of large cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, large vesicular nuclei, and prominent nucleoli. The neoplasm may show papillary configurations, focal necrosis, cystic change, and haemorrhage with cholesterol clefts {17130314}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Pleomorphic adenoma
  • Epithelial cells are small, cuboidal with a high N:C ratio, occasionally basaloid, and seen singly and in tissue fragments forming stellate-shapes, strands, and tubules
  • Myoepithelial cells are plasmacytoid, spindle, or clear cells, and they are dispersed singly, in small tissue fragments or embedded in the matrix
  • Chondromyxoid or fibrillary matrix that is magenta (Giemsa) or pale green (Pap)
  • Low mitotic activity and no necrosis
Myoepithelioma
  • Myoepithelial cells with clear, spindle, epithelioid, or plasmacytoid features seen singly or in loose tissue fragments
  • Uniform round to oval nuclei and eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm
  • Nuclear inclusions can be seen in plasmacytoid cells
  • Variable amount of fibrillary or myxoid matrix
Oncocytoma
  • Loosely cohesive tissue fragments of large epithelioid cells in a clean background
  • Large cells with abundant granular cytoplasm; round nuclei; and small, prominent nucleoli

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The clinical behaviour of respiratory tract pleomorphic adenoma varies from benign to more aggressive. The differential diagnosis includes pulmonary hamartoma, myoepithelioma, epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, pulmonary blastoma, and carcinosarcoma. Tumours with a stromal component are in the differential diagnosis with myoepithelioma, including adenoid cystic carcinoma, pleomorphic adenoma, and epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma. Carcinoid tumour, metastatic oncocytic carcinoma of the thyroid, and metastatic oncocytic carcinoma of the kidney are in the differential diagnosis with oncocytoma. Overall, the prognosis of these tumours is favourable.

Ancillary testing

The epithelial cells in pleomorphic adenoma are positive for CK7, CAM5.2, AE1/AE3, and S100, and the myoepithelial cells are positive for p63, p40, S100, SMA, and GFAP in pleomorphic adenoma and in myoepithelioma {28484699}. Oncocytoma is positive for CAM5.2 and negative for chromogranin, PAX8, and S100. Surgical resection is the recommended treatment.
