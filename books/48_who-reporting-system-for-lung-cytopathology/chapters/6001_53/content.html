6.0.0.1 Introduction

In respiratory cytopathology specimens the use of the “Atypical” category is based on an assessment of the following: the pattern of dispersal of tissue fragments and individual cells, the architectural features of the tissue fragments, the nuclear features of the epithelial or lymphoid cells present, and background features. The majority of these features may appear benign or reactive, but some findings may suggest malignancy {20490328; 26990836; 32894657; 30294354} [[Layfield LJ, Baloch Z, editors. The Papanicolaou Society of Cytopathology System for Reporting Respiratory Cytology: definitions, criteria, explanatory notes, and recommendations for ancillary testing. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2019.]]. The atypical features should be specified, the differential diagnosis discussed, and the most likely diagnosis suggested in a report categorized as “Atypical” {29757490; 27221749; 30051969; 32222718}.

All cellular elements of the respiratory tract, including the oropharyngeal squamous cells, metaplastic squamous cells, respiratory bronchial epithelial cells, terminal bronchiolar or alveolar lining cells, and pulmonary macrophages, may demonstrate architectural and/or cellular atypia {8384547}. The majority of cytopathological cases categorized as “Atypical” will have histopathological correlates, which are benign lesions demonstrating reactive and reparative changes, including infectious etiologies, squamous metaplasia, and nuclear atypia secondary to iatrogenic effects, in particular radiation therapy and chemotherapy {27221749; 26990836; 20490328}.

It is recommended that clinical and imaging findings are reviewed and correlated before categorizing a specimen as “Atypical” {27221749; 33389149}. Clinical signs and symptoms of acute inflammatory conditions can be variable and overlap with many other pathologies, such as pulmonary embolism, interstitial lung disease, cardiac failure, acute respiratory distress syndrome, and malignant tumours {28941558}. Similarly, the imaging features of benign and neoplastic processes in the lung may overlap.
