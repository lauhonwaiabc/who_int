2.0.0.4 Risk of malignancy and management recommendations

The WHO Reporting System for Lung Cytopathology is designed not only to provide standardized terminology for categories and key diagnostic cytopathological criteria for cytomorphological diagnosis, but also to supply clinically relevant information on the estimated risk of malignancy (ROM) of each category. The ROM for each category is linked to a set of recommendations that then assist the clinician in managing patients and making clinical decisions. Each category has a distinct ROM and the ROM shows a progressive increase from the “Benign” category to the “Malignant” category.

A summary of the recommendations for each category provided in the category chapters is presented in section Chapter 8: Management recommendations for each diagnostic category, to assist clinicians.

The specific cytopathological diagnosis, or in some cases the differential diagnosis, remains paramount in clinical decision-making. The recommendations presented in this volume are suggestions to help standardize management, including further diagnostic procedures to make (or to make more specific) a particular diagnosis, and are not intended to impose strict management guidelines on clinicians using the WHO Reporting System. The recommendations are suggestions for diagnostic follow-up and not mandated guidelines, and they should be applied where clinically relevant. They are summarized in #29152Table 1.04 and #29153Table 1.05 .

The performance measures and ROM for each category are dependent on the cytopathology sampling method. The ROMs for the categories “Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic” and “Benign” are higher for sputum sample cytopathology than for FNAB, and the recommendations following these cytopathology report categorizations vary with the specimen type and ROM. In these situations, FNAB of any mass lesion is the usual key follow-up recommendation.

The FNAB ROM estimates are based on published experience at academic centres and may differ from those associated with other practice types. The ROMs represent historical published ranges and will be the subject of further research and subsequent modification in future editions of the WHO Reporting System {30051969; 32222718; 34319004}.

In the lung, the site of a lesion and the imaging findings (i.e. “benign”, “indeterminate”, or “suspicious”), the state of the patient and their comorbidities, and the availability of biopsy techniques (endobronchial ultrasound–directed transbronchial FNAB, endobronchial ultrasound–directed transbronchial core needle biopsy, and transthoracic FNAB) all form a relatively complex matrix of information along with the type of cytopathology specimen, the report category, and in many cases the specific diagnosis or differential diagnosis. This is quite different to other FNAB sites, such as thyroid and breast, where there is only one cytopathology specimen type – FNAB – and recommendations regarding further diagnostic management are relatively straightforward. The recommendations for further diagnostic management are presented in #29152Table 1.04 and #29153Table 1.05 , and comments regarding poorly resourced situations in low- and middle-income countries are provided, because the WHO Reporting System is designed to be applicable internationally.

Correlation with clinical and imaging findings is required in the management of all categories. Apart from "Benign" cytopathology cases that are found to correlate with the imaging findings, the other categories usually require further diagnostic investigation, ideally to attain further cytopathological or histopathological material to facilitate a specific pathological diagnosis.

The ROM estimates given in #29152Table 1.04 and #29153Table 1.05 are derived from those cited in studies utilizing the Papanicolaou Society of Cytopathology (PSC) system {26990836}, other classification systems {32222718}, and studies applying categories similar to those used in the present system to their own data {30051969; 26331778; 24127207; 18701418; 22246929; 19890836}.

Some clinical management suggestions are outlined above. In medically poorly resourced settings, an “Insufficient”, “Atypical”, or “Suspicious for malignancy” sputum sample report after correlation with imaging such as a CXR would usually be repeated and the slides reviewed if BB, BW, and endobronchial ultrasound–guided transbronchial FNAB are not available. Similarly, if BB and BW were “Insufficient”, “Atypical”, or “Suspicious for malignancy” a repeat BW and BB procedure would be recommended after correlation with imaging if endobronchial ultrasound–guided transbronchial FNAB is not available.

Recommendations:

1. When repeating a sputum sample, always provide clear instructions regarding the procedure or assist the patient with a physiotherapist or trained paramedic.

2. Whenever a sputum sample, BW/BB, or FNAB result is received, correlation with clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings is mandatory; in patients suspected of having a malignancy, this will ideally be carried out in a multidisciplinary meeting.

3. Whenever an “Atypical” or “Suspicious for malignancy” report is provided, or whenever there is discrepancy between the clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings and the cytopathology report, the reporting cytopathologist should, if possible, get a second expert opinion on the cytopathology.

4. Routine follow-up at 3–6 months for “Benign” and “Atypical” reports is determined by the patient’s lung cancer risk.

Recommendations:

1. Whenever an FNAB result is received, correlation with clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings is mandatory; in patients suspected of having a malignancy, this will be carried out in a multidisciplinary meeting.

2. Whenever an “Atypical” or “Suspicious for malignancy” report is provided, or whenever there is discrepancy between the clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings and the cytopathology report, the reporting cytopathologist should get a second expert opinion on the cytopathology.

3. Routine follow-up at 3–6 months for “Benign” and “Atypical” reports is determined by the patient’s lung cancer risk.

4. ROSE is recommended at transthoracic FNAB and endobronchial ultrasound–guided FNAB.

5. CT or ultrasound is recommended at transthoracic FNAB.
