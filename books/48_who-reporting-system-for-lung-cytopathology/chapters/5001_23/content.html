5.0.0.1 Introduction

Sputum samples, bronchial washings, bronchial brushings, and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) are used to investigate suspected infections and to rule out malignancy. Sputum sampling has a pooled sensitivity and specificity of 60% and 90% respectively, and the diagnostic yield approaches 95% when combined with bronchial samples {3123151; 31759097}. Currently, bronchial washing / BAL is the specimen of choice for the evaluation of suspected infectious and interstitial lung disease; these specimens are used for cytopathological assessment including cell counts, identifying lipid- or haemosiderin-laden macrophages, and testing for amiodarone toxicity, as well as to provide material for microbiological testing {27753244}. The bronchial washing procedure is routinely performed at the same time as bronchial brushing. A fresh sample is required for cultures. However, liquid-based fixatives are suitable for molecular testing, as is scraped material from direct smears {25881088}.

A “Benign” categorization can be issued in adequate exfoliative specimens that lack malignant cells, and there may be no diagnosis of a specific benign process or lesion. If an FNAB of a nodule yields benign lung components or an inflammatory process and imaging shows a lesion that suggests a benign neoplasm, a “Benign” report can be issued with routine follow-up. If an FNAB of a nodule that on imaging is regarded as suspicious for malignancy yields benign lung components, the case can be categorized as “Benign” and the report should describe the material present and then in the diagnostic summary or conclusion clearly state a caveat that “the material may not represent the lesion seen on imaging”. Some cytopathologists may categorize such cases as “Non-diagnostic”. The final diagnosis should be established in the context of the triple test, with correlation of the cytopathology findings with the clinical and imaging findings {23020728}. This is especially important in benign neoplasms such as hamartoma, where the material may resemble normal lung elements. If a diagnosis is not established by FNAB in a case where clinical or imaging findings are suspicious for malignancy, further evaluation such as repeat FNAB, core needle biopsy, or limited resection should be performed.
