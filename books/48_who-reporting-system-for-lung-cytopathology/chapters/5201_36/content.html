5.2.0.1 Pulmonary hamartoma

Definition

Pulmonary hamartoma is a benign neoplasm that consists of at least two mesenchymal elements, combined with entrapped respiratory epithelium.

Clinical features and imaging

Pulmonary hamartoma is the most common benign neoplasm of the lung, accounting for approximately 8% of solitary lung nodules, and is more common in men than in women {26291008; 3321538}. Pulmonary hamartomas are often peripherally located, although rarely they are endobronchial {12114359}.

On imaging, pulmonary hamartomas are solitary, well circumscribed, and heterogeneous. CT scans are in general more sensitive than CXR for identifying adipose tissue and calcifications. However, there is significant overlap between hamartomas and other benign and malignant neoplasms on imaging {24119266}.

Histopathology

Pulmonary hamartomas are composed of a disorganized proliferation of mesenchymal tissues (typically cartilage) and may also include adipose tissue, fibrous tissue, or smooth muscle. There are often entrapped adjacent airways lined by cuboidal to low columnar respiratory epithelium. Occasionally, metaplasia, hyperplasia, and reactive atypia can be observed in the epithelium {30473462}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Cellularity varies from low to high
  • A typical biphasic pattern of tissue fragments of fibromyxoid stroma and epithelium is seen at low power
  • The fibromyxoid stroma is typically wispy with irregular feathered outlines, basophilic (Pap), and intensely metachromatic magenta (Giemsa), and it contains bland fusiform and stellate stromal cells with small nuclei
  • The epithelial component can sometimes be prominent, with bronchial cells in sheets or metaplastic cells, and it can show reactive atypia
  • Mature cartilage tissue fragments are usually sparse and stain dark blue (Pap) and bright magenta (Giemsa); the lacunar chondrocytes can show slight nuclear variability but lack significant cytopathological atypia
  • Other mesenchymal elements, such as adipose tissue, bone, and muscle, can be present

Discussion and differential diagnosis

FNAB has a high diagnostic accuracy for pulmonary hamartoma when the typical cytopathological features are displayed. One study demonstrated that FNAB has 100% accuracy {17388933}. However, the College of American Pathologists (CAP) Interlaboratory Comparison Program has shown that pulmonary hamartoma is a common source of false positive results {15628903}, when it is mistaken for carcinoid tumour, pulmonary adenocarcinoma, and small cell carcinoma. Correctly recognizing the mesenchymal component of the FNAB smears is key. An uncommon variant, mucinous adenomyomatous pulmonary hamartoma, can mimic primary mucinous neoplasms of the lung (e.g. mucinous adenocarcinoma), owing to its irregular monotonous growth of non-ciliated mucinous cells in a smooth mucin-rich smear {18540306; 32715806}.

The differential diagnosis of pulmonary hamartoma also includes biphasic neoplasms, such as pulmonary salivary gland–type tumours, including pleomorphic adenoma (which is usually centrally rather than peripherally located, as is pulmonary hamartoma) and adenoid cystic carcinoma. Although both pulmonary hamartoma and pleomorphic adenoma can have fibromyxoid stroma, pleomorphic adenoma usually lacks other mesenchymal components {12708911}. The FNAB of pulmonary adenoid cystic carcinoma usually shows cohesive tissue fragments of basaloid cells with a high N:C ratio, and homogeneous hyaline globules of metachromatic basement membrane–like material, which contrasts with the fibrillary stroma and chondroid elements of pulmonary hamartoma {10578983; 14696146}.

Pulmonary chondroma is a rare tumour that often arises in the setting of Carney triad and is morphologically similar to pulmonary hamartoma but generally lacks adipocytes, muscle, and entrapped respiratory epithelium {18043038}. Loss of the SDHB subunit of succinate dehydrogenase has been shown to be specific to pulmonary chondroma, whereas pulmonary hamartoma usually retains expression {31236950}.

Ancillary testing

The fibromyxoid stroma of pulmonary hamartoma can express S100. The epithelial component of entrapped bronchial and alveolar cells can be positive for CK7 and thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) {7483993}. It has been demonstrated that clonal translocation at chromosome 6p21 occurs in the mesenchymal cells of pulmonary hamartoma, but the value of molecular tests for confirming the diagnosis is limited {1423265}.
