8.2.2.2 Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

Definition

Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC) is a high-grade non-small cell carcinoma with neuroendocrine morphology that expresses neuroendocrine immunocytochemical markers.

Clinical features and imaging

LCNEC has a peripheral location in > 75% of cases and rarely involves the main airways {26279139}. It has a strong association with smoking, and the clinical presentation is similar to that of other non-small cell lung carcinomas (NSCLCs). Half of the cases are inoperable at diagnosis, with distant metastases mostly to brain, liver, and bone {31601526; 26898325}.

The imaging features are similar to those of other NSCLCs: irregular tumour borders and expansive growth in peripheral lung fields by CT. Mediastinal lymphadenopathy is common, whereas obstructive pneumonia is rare {14684518; 26279139; 17996599}.

Histopathology

LCNECs vary in size from 10 to 100 mm and are tan-red and necrotic. Microscopically, an organoid growth pattern with nests, trabeculae, rosettes, and palisading is observed, with a variable desmoplastic reaction and generally extensive necrosis. Tumour cells are large (usually more than the diameter of three small lymphocytes), with nuclear atypia, coarsely granular chromatin, prominent nucleoli, and abundant weakly eosinophilic (Pap) cytoplasm. The diagnosis is confirmed by a mitotic count exceeding 10 mitoses/2 mm2, with a wide variation (median: 70 mitoses/2 mm2) {1709558; 25065926; 21043816; 29201447}. In some cases, combined tumours occur, with LCNEC components and small cell lung carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or adenocarcinoma components {25065926; 26291008; 1709558}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Moderately to highly cellular
  • Small, cohesive or loosely cohesive tissue fragments in a background of single cells and necrosis; rare cases present as predominantly single intact cells, mimicking lymphoma
  • Thin fibrovascular strands may traverse the tissue fragments
  • Cells are intermediate to large in size, i.e. usually greater than the diameter of three lymphocytes, with moderate to abundant cytoplasm
  • Nuclear moulding may be present but is subtler than in small cell lung carcinoma, and the chromatin is typically granular and fine but can be coarse or show perinucleolar clearing in the Pap-stained specimen
  • Nucleoli range from inconspicuous to large
  • Chromatinic smearing is common
  • Necrosis is prominent in most cases
  • Mitoses are frequent

Reference(s): {11135471; 11747218; 16138368; 18433011; 23166111; 29271588}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The definitive diagnosis of LCNEC is difficult in cytopathology because of morphological overlap with small cell lung carcinoma, NSCLC, and (rarely) lymphoma, which have to be considered in the differential diagnosis, along with neuroendocrine tumour (NET) (atypical carcinoid) (see #23265Table 7.05). However, the diagnosis may be suggested in cases with cellular cell blocks that allow for the assessment of neuroendocrine morphology (including nesting, palisading, and rosettes) and ICC.

Ancillary testing

LCNECs are positive for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), synaptophysin, chromogranin, CD56, and INSM1 in 75% of cases {21319315; 31345782; 23635388}. Neuroendocrine markers must be interpreted in the context of neuroendocrine cytomorphology and appropriate clinical history, because NSCLC and metastatic disease can be positive for these markers {31345782; 12925987; 30572031}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is primarily used to separate high-grade from low-grade neuroendocrine neoplasms. LCNEC typically demonstrates a Ki-67 proliferation index > 40% {33474631; 15644774}, and although it is not included in the diagnostic criteria, Ki-67 can be useful in cytopathology specimens and small biopsies to differentiate high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma from NET (atypical carcinoid tumour) {33474631; 15644774}. A subset of LCNECs harbour canonical and non-canonical non-small cell carcinoma mutations {33474631; 26960398}, including established and potential targets of therapy {27507618; 25700797; 29219616}. Some LCNECs may also express PDL1, providing a different therapeutic approach, if clinically appropriate {30885341}. Predictive ICC for ALK, ROS1, and PDL1, as well as single-gene or next-generation sequencing, may inform clinical management.
