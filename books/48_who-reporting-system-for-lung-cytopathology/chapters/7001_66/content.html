7.0.0.1 Introduction

The category “Suspicious for malignancy” is used in respiratory cytopathology for cases that the cytopathologist assesses as most likely malignant but where there is a degree of uncertainty as to a definitive diagnosis of malignancy {26990836; 32222718; 27221749}. This category is used when there is insufficient quality or quantity of cellular material in an adequate smear for a definite diagnosis of malignancy. The category remains subjective, with a high rate of interobserver disagreement {30294354}, because the threshold for the diagnosis of malignancy depends on the cytopathologist’s experience, the type of cytopathological preparation, and the degree of cellular atypia. The category is used particularly to avoid a false positive diagnosis of malignancy, which may lead to unnecessary intervention, and so maintain the high positive predictive value of a “Malignant” categorization. The category of “Suspicious for malignancy” should not be used as the sole basis for therapy {27221749; 32359109; 32222718}. The majority of cases categorized as “Suspicious for malignancy” will have histopathological correlates that are malignant lesions, and the risk of malignancy of the “Suspicious for malignancy” category is approximately 82% (range: 54.5–90%) {26990836; 30051969; 32222718; 26331778; 31051765}.
