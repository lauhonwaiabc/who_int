7.0.1.3 Ectopic thyroid and parathyroid tissues

Definition

Ectopic thyroid and parathyroid are developmental abnormalities due to abnormal migration of the thyroid anlage or parathyroid tissue during embryogenesis.

Clinical features and imaging

Ectopic thyroid has a reported incidence in the general population of 1 case per 100 000–300 000 person-years {32871503}. In the great majority (~90%) of cases, ectopic thyroid is seen along its migration pathway; however, in as many as 10% of cases it can be seen in other locations, such as the mediastinum, lung, oesophagus, aorta, heart, adrenal gland, and gallbladder {32871503; 30725888; 32119467; 30105609; 26721191}. Only rare cases of intrapulmonary ectopic thyroid tissue have been reported {21171513; 32775272; 26047938}.

On imaging, ectopic thyroid in either mediastinum or lung can mimic primary or metastatic tumours at these sites {21171513; 32775272; 26047938}.

Ectopic parathyroid is more commonly related to the inferior parathyroid gland than to the superior parathyroid gland {33394375; 31082016; 33579799; 10483753; 26721191}. The ectopic locations for superior parathyroid glands include the tracheoesophageal groove, posteromedial to the thyroid, the carotid sheath, within the thyroid, and the mediastinum. The common ectopic locations for the inferior glands are the intrathymic and perithymic soft tissues, the thyrothymic ligament, the carotid sheath, within the thyroid, and the anterior mediastinum. Rare cases of intratracheal parathyroid tissue have been reported {33394375; 10483753; 31082016; 33579799}. The majority of ectopic parathyroid glands are clinically silent; however, parathyroid neoplasms (usually adenomas) can develop, causing hyperparathyroidism {26263931; 23259300}.

Ultrasound usually fails to localize parathyroid neoplasms arising in ectopic locations, whereas technetium-99m sestamibi CT and PET-CT have shown high sensitivity and specificity for localizing these lesions {23259300; 23174995; 31145436}.

Histopathology

The histopathological features of ectopic thyroid and parathyroid tissue and their associated neoplasms are similar to those occurring in the thyroid and parathyroid in their usual anatomical location.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Ectopic thyroid
  • Substernal goitre or a parasitic goitrous nodule on FNAB shows cytomorphological features of thyroid tissue: abundant thin colloid, macrophages, and monolayer sheets of follicular cells without nuclear atypia
  • Follicular nodular disease nodules will show follicular sheets, tissue fragments of follicles of varying size including macrofollicles, and usually abundant thin colloid; cystic change with siderophages may also be present
  • Follicular adenoma arising in ectopic locations will show predominant microfollicles, thick luminal colloid, and a lack of nuclear features of papillary thyroid carcinoma, and will be diagnosed as follicular neoplasm
  • Rare cases of papillary carcinoma arising in ectopic thyroid tissue will show the diagnostic nuclear features of elongation, fine dusty chromatin (Pap), grooves, and pseudoinclusions, with or without papillary tissue fragments

Reference(s): {7486437; 15839636; 8458284; 32534865; 31464909; 28940588; 31790949}

Ectopic parathyroid
  • Parathyroid neoplasms, which are usually adenomas arising in ectopic locations, are usually hypercellular and consist of epithelial cells with round nuclei and clear to slightly dense cytoplasm arranged in monolayer sheets and acini
  • Fine blood vessels may traverse the tissue fragments, and papillary architecture may be seen

Reference(s): {32534865; 28658791; 2313181}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Ectopic thyroid tissue or its associated neoplasms arising in the mediastinum and lung can mimic primary and metastatic neoplasms at these locations. This diagnosis can only be rendered after excluding metastasis from a thyroid primary {31464909}. Parathyroid tissue in an ectopic location can mimic low-grade neuroendocrine neoplasms and thyroid tumours {33232572; 23174995}.

Ancillary testing

The cytomorphological diagnosis of thyroid tissue can be confirmed by positive ICC for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), thyroglobulin, and PAX8. In some cases, an aliquot of the FNAB rinse can be submitted for thyroglobulin assessment {29744727; 33125564; 32506193}. Parathyroid origin can be confirmed by demonstrating high PTH concentration in the FNAB rinse or by ICC positivity for PTH, GATA3, and chromogranin {29626276; 34863413; 33040850}.
