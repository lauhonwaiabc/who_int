8.0.0.3 Discussion and background

Malignant neoplasms involving the lungs include both primary and secondary tumours. These can occur confined to the pulmonary parenchyma, or as pleural-based lesions with minimal parenchymal involvement, or as small or imaging-occult pulmonary lesions with extensive mediastinal lymph node involvement giving rise to a hilar mass {27221749; 26291008; 33669241; 32965978}. It is important to be aware of the limitations of cytopathology samples due to qualitative and quantitative issues {33435733; 33145811; 33088028}. Therefore, a “Malignant” categorization and, wherever possible, a specific tumour type diagnosis should be rendered only in specimens with adequate cellularity demonstrating definitive key diagnostic cytopathological criteria for malignancy {33132332}.

On the basis of cytomorphology, good accuracy (> 70%) can be achieved in differentiating between non-small cell (lung) carcinoma, which is most commonly adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), and small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (SCNEC). A diagnosis of adenocarcinoma can be made without a need for special stains if there is distinct glandular morphology with or without intracellular or extracellular mucin production, and a diagnosis of SCC can be made if there is keratinization, although thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) staining may be required to confirm lung origin in adenocarcinomas. However, with the advent of targeted therapies, exact classification of the cancer as adenocarcinoma or SCC is essential and the current standard of care {30380531; 22328844}. This can be achieved using a limited ICC panel consisting of TTF1, p40, and napsin A {30380531; 22328844; 21258246; 28429425}. Dual stains such as napsin A / p40 are especially helpful in preserving tissue in very scant samples {27412420}. In cases of poorly differentiated carcinomas lacking overt glandular or squamous differentiation but displaying a clear immunophenotype of TTF1 positivity and p40 negativity, a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma can be rendered with confidence. The diagnostic designation of non-small cell carcinoma NOS is appropriate for poorly differentiated carcinomas lacking adenocarcinoma or SCC differentiation both by cytopathology and ICC (if available).

It is also important to be aware of the diagnostic pitfalls associated with ICC stains, especially in cases of common secondary malignancies of the lung; for example, TTF1 positivity can be seen in metastatic thyroid carcinoma, and further specific ICC may be required {30380531; 21258246; 31913583}.

The “Malignant” category also includes salivary gland–type carcinomas, adenosquamous carcinoma, pleomorphic carcinoma, pulmonary blastoma, carcinosarcoma, NUT carcinoma, thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumour, low-grade neuroendocrine tumour (NET) (previously known as “carcinoid” and “atypical carcinoid”), lymphoproliferative malignancies, mesenchymal tumours, mesothelioma, angiosarcoma, and metastatic malignancies. Once a case has been categorized as “Malignant”, the cytopathologist should attempt, wherever possible, to give as specific a tumour diagnosis as possible.

A “Malignant” cytopathology report of a lung lesion requires the WHO Reporting System category and a clear statement, if at all possible, of the specific diagnosis classified by the WHO classification of thoracic tumours, and if this is not possible, then the presentation of a brief differential diagnosis is required. Some cytopathologists may want to add a statement that the material received was adequate, whereas others may want to state the degree of cellularity and provide a clear and concise microscopic description of the key diagnostic cytopathological features present. In institutions routinely using molecular ancillary testing, the report should state, if possible, the tumour fraction of both the direct smears and the cell block. It is essential that any subsequent ancillary testing results (including ICC, molecular studies, and flow cytometry) are incorporated into a final integrated cytopathology report.
