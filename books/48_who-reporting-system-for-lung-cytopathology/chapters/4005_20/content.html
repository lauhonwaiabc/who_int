4.0.0.5 Risk of malignancy and management recommendations

Overall, the "Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic" category has a risk of malignancy of approximately 40–60%, depending on the mode of cytopathological sampling and the imaging characteristics of the lung lesion {26990836; 18195376; 28240562}. An “Inadequate” lung cytopathology report should lead to a review of the clinical and imaging findings, preferably in a multidisciplinary setting, and a decision should be made as to whether to repeat the study or proceed to a more invasive test.

Owing to a high risk of malignancy in patients with a mass lesion on imaging, for both exfoliative and FNAB specimens, the number of "Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic" samples should be kept to a minimum, and the reason for the use of this “Inadequate” category should be provided in each report. It is recommended that cytopathology services regularly audit their “Inadequate” rates to highlight problems with the procurement of the specimens and the reporting of these cases. The risk of malignancy is reported to be approximately 40% for endobronchial ultrasound–guided transbronchial FNAB (EBUS-TBFNAB), as high as 60% for transthoracic FNAB, and > 60% for exfoliative samples {26331778; 31455841; 30051969}.

Reporting the reasons for inadequacy in a specimen can contribute to improving the diagnostic yield of a repeat examination {32222718}. If a sputum sample lacks alveolar macrophages, the report can state this and recommend an adequate deep cough to produce sputum. If a cytopathology specimen is bloody in a patient on anticoagulant therapy, controlled cessation of the treatment can be recommended. In the case of a bronchial brushing (BB) or bronchial washing (BW) of an endobronchial mass that yields only normal bronchial cells owing to the submucosal localization or the fibrotic nature of the tumour, the approach can be changed and an EBUS-TBFNAB performed.

If a sputum sample is “Inadequate”, the clinical and imaging findings can be reviewed, and the sputum sampling repeated. This applies both in low- and middle-income countries and in better-resourced countries. If the imaging is suspicious and further biopsy is required, then BW and BB, EBUS-TBFNAB, or transthoracic FNAB or mediastinoscopy can be used to obtain material, depending on the site of the lesion. If these procedures are not available in a low- or middle-income country, then there may be a need to proceed to the biopsy of another site, such as a left supraclavicular lymph node if enlarged, or an open wedge resection of lung.

If a BW and BB are “Inadequate”, correlation with clinical and imaging findings is recommended, but in almost all cases repeat BW, BB, EBUS-TBFNAB, or transthoracic FNAB with or without a core needle biopsy will be required. Some clinical teams will have mediastinoscopy available.

If an EBUS-TBFNAB or transthoracic FNAB is “Inadequate” at rapid onsite evaluation (ROSE), as many as four or five repeat passes are recommended {29320774}. If the specimen is still “Inadequate”, or the diagnosis of inadequacy is reported without ROSE at a later date, repeat FNAB with or without core needle biopsy is recommended, and again correlation with the imaging findings is required. Mediastinoscopy may be available. Ancillary testing on the cell block may provide diagnostic material, which can allow the category of the initial cytopathology report to be changed to “Benign”, “Atypical”, “Suspicious for malignancy”, or “Malignant”. The multidisciplinary clinical team may decide to progress to lung wedge resection and/or lobectomy, with frozen section providing information to determine the extent of surgery (see #29152Table 1.04, and #29153Table 1.05, in section Chapter 1: Introduction to the WHO Reporting System for Lung Cytopathology).
