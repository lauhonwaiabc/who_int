8.4.0.1 Spindle cell tumours

Definition

Malignant spindle cell tumours encompass a wide range of malignant neoplasms composed almost exclusively of spindle cells or those in which spindle cells are a major component of the lesion.

Clinical features and imaging

The wide range of spindle cell tumours has no distinctive clinical presentations or imaging.

Histopathology

Spindle cell carcinoma is a subtype of pleomorphic carcinoma consisting solely of neoplastic spindle cells, with markedly atypical or deceptively bland nuclei {12604887; 7672783; 8199991}.

Spindle cell neuroendocrine (carcinoid) tumour (spindle cell NET) consists of small to intermediate-sized spindle cells with finely granular or pale cytoplasm and uniform round to elongated nuclei with fine to coarsely granular, evenly dispersed chromatin and inconspicuous to single round nucleoli. There may be a mix with other variants.

Pulmonary blastoma shows a biphasic pattern of primitive mesenchymal cells that are closely packed, primitive, oval to spindle, small blastema-like cells, which focally show bizarre nuclei, mixed with fibroblast-like cells, glandular structures resembling endometrioid carcinoma, and morules {1849449; 2301281}.

Pulmonary artery intimal sarcoma is a poorly differentiated mesenchymal tumour with fibroblastic and myofibroblastic differentiation, which occasionally shows leiomyosarcomatous, rhabdomyosarcomatous, or angiosarcomatous components {18779732; 16613346}.

Leiomyosarcomas are high-grade and hypercellular and consist of long, intersecting fascicles that are parallel and perpendicular to the plane of section and consist of spindle cells with moderate to abundant pale to eosinophilic cytoplasm and moderately pleomorphic plump nuclei with blunt ends (cigar-shaped). Mitoses (including atypical mitoses) are frequent {7272926}. Necrosis is found in larger tumours.

Monophasic synovial sarcoma consists of monomorphic, delicate, relatively uniform and small spindle cells with a high N:C ratio, sparse cytoplasm, and ovoid hyperchromatic nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli, arranged in dense cellular sheets or vague fascicles with scant collagen, occasional herringbone architecture, staghorn vascular pattern, and limited myxoid change. Biphasic synovial sarcoma has epithelial and spindle cell components in varying proportions.

Spindle cell melanoma variant is characterized by cells with elongated, narrow, tapering cytoplasmic processes and can have a mix of more typical epithelioid melanoma. Melanin pigment may or may not be present.

Sarcomatoid mesothelioma displays a proliferation of spindle cells arranged in fascicles or in haphazard patterns. The spindle cells are elongated and tapered, with nuclei that range from bland to pleomorphic.

Gastrointestinal stromal tumours consist of spindle cells in sheets or vague storiform arrangements, or epithelioid cells in sclerosing or hypercellular tumours or (on occasion) in mixed tumours. There is usually minimal nuclear pleomorphism, although some cases may show more marked nuclear atypia.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

In general, the malignant spindle cell tumours produce in FNAB smears greater cell dissociation, cellularity, necrosis, and mitotic activity than benign tumours, which usually show intact tissue fragments with few dispersed cells (see #28212Table 7.06). See also section Spindle cell tumours, in the chapter on the “Benign” diagnostic category.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes sarcomas of smooth muscle, nerve sheath tumours, myofibroblastic tumours, and other neoplasms of mesenchymal origin, as well as spindle cell carcinoma and spindle cell melanoma. Correlating the patient’s history and clinical and imaging features is essential. Metastatic soft tissue tumours are more common in the lung than primary spindle cell tumours, apart from spindle cell mesothelioma.

Spindle cell carcinoma cannot be diagnosed on the basis of cytopathology specimens, but if poorly differentiated spindle cells showing varying degrees of nuclei atypia are present, it enters the differential diagnosis. However, if cytopathology shows non-small cell carcinoma with a spindle cell carcinoma component, a comment should be made that its presence raises the possibility of a spindle cell carcinoma.

Spindle NETs are usually located peripherally, with bland, elongated nuclei and long, whip-like cytoplasm {29770932}.

So-called benign metastasizing leiomyoma should be considered if there are multiple lung nodules in women with a previous hysterectomy, and it is otherwise difficult to distinguish from primary low-grade leiomyosarcomas, which have cohesive tissue fragments of cells often arranged in a parallel pattern with oval, blunt-ended to spindle nuclei and spindle cytoplasm. High-grade leiomyosarcoma may have similar more-cellular tissue fragments with more plentiful dispersed single intact cells and may show bland to hyperchromatic pleomorphic nuclei, prominent nucleoli, mitoses, and necrosis. There are epithelioid subtypes.

Metastatic melanoma can enter the differential diagnosis of a spindle cell tumour in a thoracic FNAB. Tumour cells may be misdiagnosed as reactive fibroblasts if they are bland, or as centrocytes of lymph nodes if they are rounded and highly dispersed. If the cells are pleomorphic, the differential diagnosis includes sarcoma, and the presence of pigment in tumour cells (which can be confirmed in cell block material by melanin bleach stains and negative Perls iron stain) and rounded, often pleomorphic nuclei with macronucleoli and nuclear pseudoinclusions may be clues to the diagnosis {24107480}.

The differential diagnosis of sarcomatoid mesothelioma is primarily with soft tissue sarcomas and spindle cell carcinoma, and requires ICC {28782639}.

Gastrointestinal stromal tumour is the most common mesenchymal tumour in the gastrointestinal tract and most often metastasizes to the liver and peritoneum; it rarely metastasizes to lung {19838991; 32118771}.

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour and solitary fibrous tumour may recur or (rarely) metastasize and can be seen in FNAB of the thorax and in the differential diagnosis of low-grade sarcomas. The cytopathology of these tumours is relatively bland, and their diagnosis often relies on the cell block. Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour exhibits nonspecific features and can be hypocellular to hypercellular with bland, fibroblast-like cells varying to spindle to epithelioid cells with prominent atypia and prominent nucleoli admixed with inflammatory cells {29673102; 27087029; 20730898; 21472870}. Solitary fibrous tumour shows low to moderate cellularity of mixed oval, elongated, round, and stellate cells with pink (Pap) collagenous stroma and hypercellular tissue fragments with minimal atypia {28914981; 20209623}. Benign and malignant solitary fibrous tumours have common cytopathological features, and the ability to distinguish between them is limited.

Lymphangioleiomyomatosis, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours, and type A thymoma all occur in the thoracic cavity and have been rarely reported in FNAB material. FNAB of lymphangioleiomyomatosis reveals relatively large, monomorphic spindle cells with a moderate N:C ratio both in tissue fragments and as dissociated single cells, with oval nuclei with evenly distributed chromatin {9167718; 34000096}. Because the cytopathology of malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour overlaps with that of other sarcomas, the FNAB diagnosis of peripheral nerve sheath tumours is difficult {22434579}. However, elongated, slender, often wavy nuclei and (less commonly) a delicate, fibrillary, metachromatic stroma are observed in some cases {10097706}. FNAB of type A thymoma yields highly cellular smears with epithelial cells in tissue fragments and as single cells demonstrating elongated or elliptical cell nuclei with minimal irregularity and bland chromatin, as well as a relative lack of lymphocytes {18514125}.

Ancillary testing

An initial ICC panel for spindle cell tumours is recommended to include pancytokeratin, vimentin, and at least one marker each for NET, mesothelioma, and melanoma, such as synaptophysin, calretinin, and melan-A (MART1), respectively (see #28212Table 7.06) {9285187}. For further analysis, the panel might be expanded, especially if there is any history of a previous soft tissue tumour at another site. In spindle cell tumours, specific genetic changes may occur, and appropriate use of sequencing techniques and FISH may make a definite diagnosis possible in these cases (see #28212Table 7.06).

Pulmonary arterial intimal sarcoma should be differentiated from other spindle cell sarcomas and tumours that express MDM2 amplification (e.g. liposarcoma).

SS18::SSX fusion testing is recommended when ICC of cell block material is inconclusive in synovial sarcomas.

Cytoplasmic immunoreactivity for ALK is detectable in 50–60% of cases of inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour and correlates well with the presence of ALK gene rearrangement {17938159; 11684952; 11485898; 25723109}.

Nuclear STAT6 immunoreactivity is a valuable marker for solitary fibrous tumours.

The spindle cells of lymphangioleiomyomatosis are consistently positive for SMA and HMB45; ER, PR, and melan-A (MART1) are also often positive, whereas S100 is negative.
