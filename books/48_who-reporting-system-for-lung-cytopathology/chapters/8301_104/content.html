8.3.0.1 Lymphomas

Definition

Primary pulmonary extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (EMZL) is a low-grade B-cell lymphoma that is believed to arise from the marginal zone B cells of the bronchial mucosa–associated lymphoid tissue.

Primary pulmonary diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a B-cell lymphoma with the same morphological, immunophenotypic, and molecular features as systemic or nodal DLBCL.

Clinical features and imaging

EMZL is a mucosa-associated lymphoma (a MALT lymphoma) and the most common type of primary pulmonary lymphoma, representing 70–90% of all primary lymphomas involving the lung {33499258; 19541720; 26452211}. There is a slight female patient predominance, and it is associated with cigarette smoking and autoimmune diseases. A monoclonal gammopathy is detected in approximately 60% of patients {11474283}. The most common imaging finding is the presence of solitary or multiple nodules of < 50 mm in one lung, although other patterns, including poorly defined infiltrates with CT attenuation containing air bronchograms and a peripheral mass with pleural thickening, have also been described.

Primary pulmonary DLBCL represents approximately 10% of all primary lymphomas arising in the lung {33499258; 32444246}. The median age at presentation is approximately 60 years; however, paediatric cases have been described, particularly in immunodeficient patients. There is no sex preference. There is a spectrum of imaging findings, including areas of consolidation, solitary or multiple nodules, and an interstitial infiltrate or ground-glass appearance with or without mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Most lesions are bilateral. Cavitation or central necrosis is a common feature {25360736}.

Histopathology

EMZL presents in resected lung specimens as a tan-white, light-grey homogeneous unencapsulated mass with relatively well-defined borders and peripheral areas of consolidation. Microscopically, dense lymphoid infiltrates disrupt the lung parenchyma as discrete nodular lesions or as a lymphangitic distribution along the pleura or around bronchovascular bundles. Reactive lymphoid follicles and lymphoepithelial lesions are frequently seen {26452211}.

Pulmonary DLBCL shows a dense lymphoid infiltrate composed of medium-sized to large lymphoid cells with perinucleolar clearing of the chromatin and often single or multiple nucleoli. Mitotic figures and apoptotic cells are frequently seen, as well as infiltration of bronchial walls.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma
  • Relatively cellular smears
  • Mix of small lymphocytes, centrocytes, and monocytoid cells with clear cytoplasm, as well as a relative abundance of plasma cells or plasmacytoid cells, which are best observed with Giemsa staining
  • Lymphoid cell cytoplasmic fragments (lymphoglandular bodies) are seen in the background with Giemsa staining
  • Amyloid can be present

Reference(s): {24261323; 21166908}

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
  • Variable cellularity
  • Relatively monotonous intermediate and (predominantly) large lymphoid cells, mostly arranged as single cells, although pseudoaggregation may occur
  • Nuclei may show a prominent central single nucleolus and eccentric cytoplasm in the immunoblastic subtype, or one to three marginal nucleoli in the centroblastic subtype, with scant amphophilic to basophilic cytoplasm; pleomorphic variants can also occur
  • Cytoplasm can be eccentric or more circumferential and is well defined
  • Mitoses and apoptotic bodies are frequent
  • Lymphoid cell cytoplasmic fragments (lymphoglandular bodies) are seen in the background with Giemsa staining

Reference(s): {33499258}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The diagnosis of EMZL of the lung on FNAB, bronchial wash, or sputum samples can be difficult, and the differential diagnosis includes reactive lymphoid processes and other types of low-grade B-cell lymphomas, such as small lymphocytic lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, low-grade follicular lymphoma, and lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma. Flow cytometry is required.

Pulmonary DLBCL is thought to arise from a transformation from previous marginal zone lymphoma or to represent de novo disease. The differential diagnosis includes poorly differentiated non-small cell carcinomas of the lung and metastatic carcinomas to the lung, metastatic melanoma, and other metastatic diseases to the lung. The neoplastic cells in DLBCL can show pseudoaggregation, whereas some carcinomas and melanomas can present predominantly as single cells.

Ancillary testing

EMZL has admixed CD3+ and CD5+ T cells among the CD20+, CD79a+, and PAX5+ B cells, and it lacks a characteristic ICC phenotype marker, which makes ICC of limited use. Flow cytometry studies are critical in detecting the presence of a CD20 clonal B-cell population and demonstrate CD5− and CD10− B cells. Coexpression of CD43 is seen in 30–80% of cases. Molecular studies demonstrate the presence of a clonal B-cell population. FISH studies may demonstrate the presence of MALT1 rearrangement or trisomy 3 {24261323}.

DLBCL often requires ICC to exclude poorly differentiated carcinomas and melanoma and to demonstrate a lymphoid origin. Flow cytometry can demonstrate B-cell monoclonality and exclude other lymphomas to make a precise classification: the cells are typically positive for CD20, PAX5, and CD79a and negative for CD5, CD10, BCL2, BCL6, cyclin D1, and CD3. Flow cytometry, however, has a lower sensitivity for DLBCL (owing to cell fragility, which can yield false negative results) and should be interpreted with care. FISH studies for BCL2, BCL6, and MYC are used to rule out double-hit high-grade B-cell lymphomas, which have a similar cytomorphology.
