8.1.2.3 Pleomorphic carcinoma

Definition

Pleomorphic carcinoma is a poorly differentiated non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC), including adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and/or large cell carcinoma containing spindle and/or giant cells, or a carcinoma that consists of spindle and/or neoplastic giant cells.

Clinical features and imaging

The signs and symptoms of pleomorphic carcinoma are similar to those of other NSCLCs. Presenting symptoms include cough, pain, haemoptysis, and dyspnoea {29454534; 27993578}. Imaging shows a large central or peripheral mass, usually in the upper lobe, showing central necrosis, frequent pleural invasion, poorly defined borders with a ground-glass halo, and intense PET avidity {15972411; 15215543; 31165974; 27300448}.

Histopathology

Pleomorphic carcinoma is composed of > 10% spindle and/or giant cells admixed with components of adenocarcinoma (in 31–72% of cases), squamous cell carcinoma (in 12–26%), or large cell carcinoma (in as many as 43%) {12604887; 12960804; 19124452}. Some tumours are composed only of spindle cells and/or tumour giant cells. The epithelial and spindle/giant cells may be intimately admixed or sharply demarcated from one another. Sometimes, osteoclast-type giant cells are scattered throughout the tumour, and these may be negative for keratin and positive for macrophage markers {19124452; 9845743}. Giant cell carcinoma consists solely of highly pleomorphic giant cells with multiple irregular, sometimes multilobated nuclei and abundant eosinophilic to granular cytoplasm. Spindle cell carcinoma consists solely of neoplastic spindle cells {12604887; 7672783}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Discohesive, enlarged, and highly pleomorphic rounded or neoplastic spindle cells with prominent blue-grey (cyanophilic) (Pap) cytoplasm
  • Variable number of tumour giant cells containing two or more nuclei can be present
  • Large nucleoli
  • Background of necrotic debris, inflammatory infiltrate, and myxoid or collagen-rich stroma often present

Reference(s): {11391606; 23323109; 30908904}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Pleomorphic carcinoma is part of the spectrum of so-called sarcomatoid carcinomas that encompass pleomorphic carcinoma, carcinosarcoma, and pulmonary blastoma. Pleomorphic carcinoma cannot be definitively diagnosed on cytopathology, because it requires a resection specimen showing > 10% of spindle and/or giant cell components. However, if cytopathology shows NSCLC with spindle and/or giant cell carcinoma patterns, a comment should be made that their presence raises the possibility of a pleomorphic carcinoma. The differential diagnosis includes carcinosarcoma, metastatic sarcomatoid carcinoma from various sites, sarcoma, germ cell tumours, melanoma, dendritic cell sarcoma, and biphasic/sarcomatoid mesothelioma {31824199; 19124452}. If adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma components are present, pleomorphic carcinoma is favoured even if keratins are completely negative in the spindle and/or giant cell component. A diagnosis of carcinosarcoma would require malignant heterologous elements such as chondrosarcoma.

Ancillary testing

Genetic events mirror those in other NSCLCs, particularly pulmonary adenocarcinoma {27174587; 25969368; 28315738}, and the frequency of KRAS and EGFR mutations is similar to that in pulmonary adenocarcinoma {27174587; 22169481; 26215952; 25969368}. ALK and ROS1 rearrangements rarely occur {27174587; 33116594}. MET exon 14–skipping mutations are present in a subset {28285687; 26215952; 30587547}. There is a high incidence of PDL1 expression (60–90%), which is often stronger in the giant or spindle cell areas {31570483; 26329973; 31165974; 29110857; 28315738}.
