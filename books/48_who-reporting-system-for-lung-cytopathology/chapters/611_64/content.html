6.1.1 Sample reports: Atypical

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format will depend on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and information on the macroscopic features and rapid onsite evaluation (ROSE) are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as "Diagnostic summary" (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Type of specimen:

Sputum sample.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

30 mm mass in left upper lobe. No definite hilar lymphadenopathy. Smoker.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Scant atypical glandular cells. See report below.

 

Microscopic description:

Very occasional small, mildly crowded tissue fragments of epithelial cells with large mildly pleomorphic and minimally indented nuclei, prominent single round nucleoli, and eccentric pale, occasionally vacuolated cytoplasm are present. The nuclei within each tissue fragment show similar changes. These epithelial cells lack cilia and are mixed with plentiful tissue fragments of ciliated bronchial cells in small tissue fragments, along with siderophages, histiocytes, neutrophils, and squamous cells.

Comment: These occasional glandular epithelial tissue fragments are atypical. Correlation with imaging and clinical history is required. Further workup with bronchial brush and FNAB as clinically indicated is recommended.

Sample report 2

Type of specimen:

Bronchial brush.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

Previous squamous cell carcinoma of lung. Chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Review. Bronchial brush right middle lobe bronchus.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Bronchial brush right middle lobe bronchus

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Occasional atypical squamous cells. See report below.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show occasional tissue fragments of squamous cells with variable occasionally high N:C ratio, moderately enlarged pleomorphic nuclei with blurred chromatin, and prominent nucleoli in occasional cells. Occasional cells are very large associated with nucleomegaly. Plentiful bronchial epithelial tissue fragments, neutrophils, and histiocytes with some carbon-laden macrophages are also present.

Comment: the features are most likely related to therapy, but residual carcinoma cannot be excluded. Correlation with imaging is required, and FNAB should be considered to provide material for typing, staging, and ancillary tests.

Editorial note:

The diagnostic summary includes the specific information regarding the site of the lesion from the request form and specimen container of any needle-rinsing material.

Sample report 3

Type of specimen:

Endobronchial ultrasound–guided FNAB right upper lobe.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

FNAB right upper lobe. Poorly defined 30 mm mass. Immunocompromised. Adjacent hilar lymphadenopathy.

 

Diagnostic summary:

FNAB right upper lobe

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Abscess with atypical squamous epithelium. See report below.

 

Microscopic description:

These moderately cellular smears show considerable pus and debris, with plentiful neutrophils and macrophages, fragments of alveolar septa, occasional small tissue fragments of ciliated bronchial cells, and small tissue fragments of squamous cells with atypical nuclei. No definite fungi are seen on routine stains.

Comment: The features are consistent with an abscess. The squamous epithelium shows atypia, raising a differential diagnosis of metaplastic squamous epithelium or possibly squamous neoplasia. Special stains are underway. Correlation with clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings is required.

 

Supplementary report 1:

The cell block shows similar material. The methenamine silver stain for fungi is negative, as are the auramine stain for mycobacteria and the Gram stain. Correlation with clinical, imaging, and microbiological findings is required. Consideration should be given to repeat FNAB.

Editorial note:

The diagnostic summary includes the specific information regarding the site of the lesion from the request form and specimen container of any needle-rinsing material.

There has been a supplementary report on special stains for infectious agents for this case performed subsequently on a cell block, and this is included in the single integrated report.

Sample report 4

Type of specimen:

Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) right lower lobe; liquid-based cytopathology.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

74-year-old man with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and chest infection. Started on antibiotics. BAL right lower lobe to exclude malignancy.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Favour reactive inflammatory changes

 

Microscopic description:

This liquid-based cytopathology preparation shows numerous alveolar macrophages and occasional 3D tissue fragments of epithelial cells with nuclear enlargement and prominent nucleoli. The changes most likely represent reactive changes; however, well-differentiated adenocarcinoma cannot be excluded on this sample. Further workup with biopsy is recommended.

Sample report 5

Type of specimen:

Endobronchial ultrasound–guided transbronchial FNAB posterior lesion.

 

Clinical and imaging information:

48-year-old woman with an incidental finding of a poorly defined mass at the lung hilum. ? neoplastic. Endobronchial ultrasound–guided transbronchial FNAB performed.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Limited material raising the possibility of a schwannoma

 

Microscopic description:

These smears show limited cellular material with occasional small tissue fragments consisting of small spindle cells with wavy, elongated nuclei. Very occasional single similar spindle cells with small, elongated nuclei are seen in the background. There is no necrosis. Inflammatory cells are scant. Similar spindle cells are seen in the cell block.

Comment: The features may represent a spindle cell neoplasm or possibly a reactive inflammatory proliferation. Ancillary tests are in progress and a supplementary report will follow. Discussion at the lung multidisciplinary meeting recommended.

 

Supplementary report 1:

Spindle cells in the cell block are positive for S100 and negative for SMA, desmin, and CD34, raising the possibility of a schwannoma. The SS18::SSX fusion is negative, excluding synovial sarcoma. Correlation with history and imaging is required. Further biopsy is recommended.

Editorial note:

A preliminary report categorizing the lesion as “Atypical” can be issued and ancillary test results added at a later stage, or some cytopathologists may prefer to wait until the ancillary testing is available before issuing a report. In either situation, only a single integrated final report incorporating the ancillary testing should be made.
