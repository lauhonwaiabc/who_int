8.1.2.6 NUT carcinoma

Definition

NUT carcinoma is a poorly differentiated carcinoma genetically defined by the presence of the nuclear protein in testis (NUT) gene (NUTM1) rearrangement.

Clinical features and imaging

NUT carcinoma typically presents at an advanced stage, in a wide age group of patients ranging in age from 0 to 80 years. Symptoms at presentation often include pleuritic chest pain, dyspnoea, non-productive cough, and weight loss {8374886; 20395873; 21263084}. Local invasion, pleural invasion, and spread by lymphatic and haematogenous routes are common {22896655; 32328562}. Distant metastases are common and include early metastases to bone, as well as dissemination to other distant sites, such as ovaries, liver, and brain, which occur later in the course of disease {11571733; 20017639; 16542442; 24371650; 22301500; 24470009; 22033856}.

Features of NUT carcinoma seen on CT generally include a hypoattenuating, heterogeneously enhancing mass, often with necrosis and poorly defined infiltrative borders, with frequent invasion of adjacent structures {20395873; 22033856; 19550370; 21263084}. NUT carcinoma on PET-CT typically shows high FDG uptake {21825839; 21447744; 22057302}.

Histopathology

The characteristic morphology is that of an undifferentiated carcinoma. Focal abrupt keratinization is seen in approximately 33% of tumours {32328562}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Highly cellular smears with loosely cohesive tissue fragments and single isolated tumour cells
  • Cells are round to oval and 2–3 times as large as a lymphocyte, with a high N:C ratio
  • Cytoplasm is generally scant, delicate, and rarely vacuolated
  • Nuclei show finely granular chromatin, discrete prominent nucleoli, and plentiful mitoses
  • In cytospins, discohesive cells and tight cellular spherical tissue fragments are present and may show nuclear moulding resembling neuroendocrine tumours (NETs)
  • Neutrophilic infiltrate is common
  • Background shows necrosis, karyorrhectic and cellular debris, bare nuclei, and chromatinic smearing
  • Rare squamous differentiation can show spindle or polygonal cells with dense cytoplasm, and keratinized cells may be seen, particularly in cell blocks
  • Rarely reported in sputum samples

Reference(s): {32877005; 19795508; 27400194; 25685583; 21210877}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

NUT carcinoma has nonspecific cytomorphological features and resembles the spectrum of malignant small blue round cell tumours, germ cell tumours, pure and combined small cell carcinoma with squamous cell carcinoma, basaloid squamous cell carcinoma, any undifferentiated or poorly differentiated carcinoma/adenocarcinoma, and melanoma. Because primary pulmonary squamous cell carcinomas are very rare in young non-smokers, it is wise to consider doing NUT ICC in these cases with large mediastinal masses. Morphologically, small cell lung carcinoma demonstrates typical granular, dark and light (salt-and-pepper) chromatin and more frequent nuclear crushing and resultant chromatin smearing. Additionally, the tumour cells of small cell carcinoma do not show the prominent nucleoli seen in NUT carcinoma. Other tumours can be distinguished by a high index of suspicion for NUT carcinoma and the use of ICC and/or gene rearrangement studies.

Ancillary testing

NUT carcinomas are commonly (but not always) positive for pankeratins and for p63 (which is more often positive than p40) {32981612; 32502287; 33231320; 33009876}. They are usually negative for neuroendocrine markers and thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1), with few exceptions {32502287; 26001144; 33231320; 22301500}. Cell blocks are invaluable for the demonstration of the NUTM1 rearrangement, either by FISH or ICC {29520920}. Speckled nuclear expression in > 50% of tumour nuclei in ICC using clone C52B1 is required for establishing the diagnosis. The ICC has 100% specificity and 87% sensitivity {19363441}. Alternatively, demonstrating NUTM1 rearrangement can be done by molecular methods such as karyotyping, FISH, PCR, or next-generation sequencing–based methods {27590195; 23128391; 30723300}.
