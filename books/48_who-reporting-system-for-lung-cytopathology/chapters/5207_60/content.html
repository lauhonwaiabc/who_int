5.2.0.7 Paraganglioma

Definition

Paraganglioma is a rare neuroendocrine neoplasm arising from the pluripotent neural crest–derived chromaffin cells occurring in extra-adrenal sites.

Clinical features and imaging

Pulmonary paraganglioma is extremely rare. It usually forms a solitary pulmonary mass found close to blood vessels and nerves, and it is often located near the branching pulmonary artery. Paragangliomas are non-functioning or may produce catecholamines {32514340}.

Histopathology

Paraganglioma is a well-circumscribed, solid tumour with nests or anastomosing trabeculae of tumour cells surrounded by an arching thin fibrovascular network, producing an organoid architecture (Zellballen). The tumour cells are variably sized and shaped: round, polyhedral, or spindle, with moderate to abundant granular, eosinophilic, and pale cytoplasm, and with indistinct borders. The nuclei are round to ovoid with finely granular chromatin, common nuclear pseudoinclusions, and occasional hyperchromasia and binucleation {15166677; 30416779}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Cellularity ranges from scant to hypercellular
  • Loosely cohesive and often syncytial tissue fragments are 3D with follicular, acinar, and nested architecture and intervening thin fibrovascular strands; papillary-like structures are rare
  • Variable single cell dissociation and stripped nuclei are seen
  • Often bloody background due to hypervascularity
  • Medium-sized epithelioid and/or spindle cells vary in size with poorly defined cell margins when in the tissue fragments, whereas single cells show abundant granular, pale-grey (Pap) cytoplasm with magenta (Giemsa) cytoplasmic granules
  • Round to oval nuclei show evenly dispersed chromatin, micronucleoli, and occasional anisonucleosis, with scattered larger cells showing marked pleomorphism, nuclear grooves, intranuclear pseudoinclusions, binucleation, and multinucleation

Reference(s): {11393086; 28261927; 29575826; 16900997}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Paraganglioma shows nuclear features reminiscent of an endocrine neoplasm {11393086; 28261927; 29575826; 31367219}. The differential diagnosis includes cases of primary or metastatic neuroendocrine neoplasms, including neuroendocrine tumours (NETs, or carcinoids), metastatic adrenal cortical carcinomas, melanomas, metastatic renal cell carcinomas, and solid pseudopapillary carcinoma {28261927; 31367219}. In rare paragangliomas with papillary-like features, sclerosing haemangioma and adenocarcinoma with lepidic pattern are in the differential diagnosis {11393086}.

Ancillary testing

Paragangliomas in all locations including the lungs are positive in ICC for neuroendocrine markers such as synaptophysin, chromogranin, INSM1, NSE, and CD56, and negative for cytokeratins and thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) {11393086; 15166677; 28719469; 30358615}. In ICC, NETs (carcinoids) and other neuroendocrine carcinomas are positive for cytokeratin and TTF1 {27529763}, in contrast to paraganglioma.
