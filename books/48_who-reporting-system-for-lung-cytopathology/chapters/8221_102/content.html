8.2.2.1 Small cell lung carcinoma

Definition

Small cell lung carcinoma (SCLC) is a malignant epithelial tumour composed of small cells with scant cytoplasm, finely granular nuclear chromatin, absent or inconspicuous nucleoli, a high mitotic count, and frequent necrosis, and in most cases it expresses neuroendocrine markers. SCLC can be combined with non-small cell carcinoma.

Clinical features and imaging

SCLC accounts for approximately 15% of all lung carcinomas and has a strong association with smoking {22214967; 21043816; 33446664}. SCLC may also occur when non-small cell lung carcinoma acquires resistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy {21430269; 23470965; 32152485}.

Most SCLCs are at an advanced stage and metastatic on presentation. Common sites of metastasis include the brain, liver, bone, and adrenal glands {33446664}. Malignant pleural and pericardial effusions may occur. Patients may present with respiratory symptoms including cough, dyspnoea, or haemoptysis, and paraneoplastic syndromes are common, whereas distant metastases are associated with fatigue, weight loss, and neurological symptoms {21565397; 33446664}. Despite initial chemosensitivity, stage IV SCLC has a poor prognosis, with a median survival time of < 1 year {33446664}.

Histopathology

SCLC is characterized by small cells with nested or sheet-like growth {12218575}. Pseudorosettes can be present, usually focally. Cells are ovoid or elongated, with scant cytoplasm, nuclear moulding, and finely granular chromatin. Large areas of necrosis or smaller areas of pinpoint necrosis are common. The mitotic count is > 10 mitoses/2 mm2 (median: 80 mitoses/2 mm2). Crush artefact may be prominent in small biopsies, obscuring tumour morphology and mitotic figures.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

  • Crowded irregular tissue fragments in a background of discohesive cells forming doublets, triplets, and cell cords, as well as many single intact cells
  • Pseudorosettes may occasionally be present
  • Cell size < 3 times that of a small lymphocyte; occasionally larger cells
  • Scant cytoplasm, although a moderate amount of pale eosinophilic (Pap) cytoplasm can be seen in a subset of cases
  • Nuclei are angulated, and nuclear moulding is best appreciated on Giemsa-stained smears
  • Finely granular chromatin pattern, best appreciated on Pap-stained smears
  • Occasional small nucleoli may be seen, but no large or frequent nucleoli
  • Chromatin smearing representing nuclear crush artefact may limit cytomorphological assessment
  • Mitoses are usually plentiful but may be difficult to appreciate
  • Blue bodies, which are small, round, and blue (Giemsa), impinge the nuclear margin but are not entirely specific
  • Background of necrotic and apoptotic debris
  • In sputum samples, the neoplastic cells are usually degenerated with small dark nuclei and tend to be found along mucous strands
  • In liquid-based cytopathology, the neoplastic cells are more discohesive, with more conspicuous nucleoli and subtle moulding, and exhibit dark, even chromatin that stands out against the lighter chromatin of benign background cells; chromatin smearing (an artefact induced by manual spreading) is more subtle and appears as slightly elongated nuclei or tangles of thread-like material

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Cytopathology has a sensitivity of 79% and an accuracy of 94% in the diagnosis and staging of SCLC {27833073}. The differential diagnosis includes other neuroendocrine neoplasms, carcinomas, and small blue cell tumours (see #32364Table 7.04). It should be noted that although the cells are small in relation to non-small cell lung carcinoma, the nuclei are larger than those seen in most lymphoid cells and larger than those seen in bronchial cells.

Basaloid squamous cell carcinoma is characterized by nests of small to medium-sized cells with high N:C ratios, often with peripheral palisading, and by a high mitotic count. There may be stromal wrapping of tissue fragments.

Small round cell sarcoma, i.e. Ewing sarcoma / primitive neuroectodermal tumour, shares some features with SCLC including rosette or pseudorosette formation, a high N:C ratio, and a necrotic background, with poorer cellular cohesion and less crush artefact.

NUT carcinoma and SMARCA4-deficient thoracic tumours can have a high N:C ratio and mimic SCLC, although some will show focal abrupt keratinization in tissue fragments or in the background.

Extrapulmonary small cell carcinoma is cytopathologically and immunocytochemically indistinguishable from pulmonary SCLC, including a shared thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1) reactivity. Only the clinical history can assist in the correct diagnosis {11175640}.

Merkel cell carcinoma is indistinguishable from SCLC on cytopathology. Clinical history and ICC are required.

Pulmonary or mediastinal lymphomas, including diffuse large B-cell, mantle cell, follicular, MALT, Burkitt, and T-cell lymphomas, share many features with SCLC, including varying degrees of crush artefact. Lymphoma cells are usually very discohesive and predominantly present as single cells, with characteristic nuclear features in many cases.

Reserve/basal cell hyperplasia appears in exfoliated samples as tightly cohesive tissue fragments with rare isolated cells in a background of other reactive changes. The cells lack nuclear atypia and necrosis and are non-reactive to neuroendocrine markers {32348027; 19856422}.

Ancillary testing

ICC is routinely utilized to confirm the diagnosis of SCLC and exclude the possibility of mimics, particularly the basal cell variant of squamous cell carcinoma {27998793; 22214967}. The majority of SCLCs express neuroendocrine markers, including chromogranin A, synaptophysin, CD56 (NCAM), and INSM1 {22214967; 21043816; 28719469; 30154579}. Staining for chromogranin A is typically weak. Approximately 20% of SCLCs are negative for both synaptophysin and chromogranin A, although they are typically positive for CD56 and/or INSM1 {21043816; 28719469; 30154579}. Importantly, CD56 is the least specific of the neuroendocrine markers and can be expressed in a variety of non-neuroendocrine tumours. Occasional SCLCs with minimal or no expression of standard neuroendocrine markers do occur, and they can be diagnosed in the context of appropriate cytomorphology and exclusion of mimics {22214967; 21043816}. SCLC is usually positive for pancytokeratins, with a characteristic dot-like cytoplasmic staining, but it is negative in high-molecular-weight keratins such as CK5/6. TTF1 is positive in 90% of SCLCs, but it is not specific for lung origin {22214967; 21043816}. Napsin A is consistently negative in SCLC {28884744}. Diffuse expression of the squamous markers p63, p40, and CK5/6 excludes the diagnosis of SCLC, but a patchy expression or labelling of scattered cells may occur in SCLC {22214967; 21043816}. Nearly all SCLCs harbour inactivating RB1 and TP53 mutations, associated with the loss of RB1 and aberrant p53 expression by ICC {33446664}. The Ki-67 proliferative index is 65–100%.

ICC is required for the distinction of the following specific tumours:

  • Basaloid squamous cell carcinoma, which is non-reactive to neuroendocrine markers and strongly reactive to p40, p63, and high-molecular-weight cytokeratins such as CK5/6 {12558748; 32348027}
  • Ewing sarcoma / primitive neuroectodermal tumour, which is positive for synaptophysin, CD99, and FLI1 and negative for cytokeratin, and which has the EWSR1::FLI1 fusion {32348027; 28346326; 24801613; 27766786; 17487851}
  • SMARCA4-deficient thoracic tumours, which show a loss of SMARCA4 protein expression, and NUT carcinoma, which shows nuclear localization of NUT protein in tumour cells {33409598; 32854100; 31751681}
  • Merkel cell carcinoma, which shows CK20 perinuclear dot positivity; Merkel cell polyomavirus ICC confirms the diagnosis {11175640; 23225406}

ICC and flow cytometry assist the diagnosis of SCLC and lymphoma, respectively. Lymphomas express clonal lymphoid markers and are negative in neuroendocrine markers and cytokeratin {32348027; 24092372; 30156771}.
