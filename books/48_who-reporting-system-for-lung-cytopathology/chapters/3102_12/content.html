3.1.0.2 Bronchial wash and bronchial brush techniques and specimen management

Flexible fibre-optic bronchoscopy is the basic procedure for the diagnosis of diseases of the lung and mediastinum, and it enables a large number of investigations including bronchial washing (BW) and bronchial brushing (BB), which are simple methods for sampling mucosal surfaces of the bronchial tree and collecting samples to assist in the diagnoses of tumours and infections (see #23292Table 2.02). In BW, 3–5 mL of balanced salt solution is instilled through the working channel of the bronchoscope into the bronchial system and then aspirated back into a container. The BB technique is a targeted method in which the brush is guided under fluoroscopy to the endobronchial lesion or lesions beyond the visual segmental bronchi. BBs are obtained by the use of a small circular stiff-bristle brush, which is moved back and forth after positioning in the target lesion {22627632}. BB samples must be obtained before transbronchial biopsies to avoid an excessive and obscuring haemorrhagic background, whereas BW samples obtained after abrading the bronchial surface with a brush give optimum results {31974454}.

The diagnostic accuracy of BW and BB cytopathology is comparable to that of transbronchial biopsy. In central lesions, the sensitivities from BW and BB alone, which are 48% and 59% respectively, can be improved by combining them with FNAB and/or forceps biopsies. In peripheral lesions, transbronchial biopsies provide the highest sensitivity (57%), followed by BB (54%) and BW and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) (43%) {23649436}. Multiple brushings increase the sensitivity to 90% and decrease the need for repeated bronchoscopy {1394056}. Very peripheral lesions, pleural lesions, submucosal masses, and mediastinal masses cannot be directly sampled using these techniques.

Processing of samples

In BW the sediment of the recovered fluid subsequent to centrifugation is used for the preparation of smears, which can either be immediately fixed in alcohol for Pap staining, or rapidly air-dried using a warm plate or hairdryer for Giemsa staining. The remaining material should be used for cell block preparation and microbiological examination.

In the endoscopy suite, brushes can be rapidly and directly smeared onto the glass slides by rolling the end of the brush and immediately fixing in alcohol. If necessary for rapid onsite evaluation (ROSE), air-dried slides can be prepared. Alternatively, brushes can be rinsed in saline solution or alcohol-based fixatives and sent to the laboratory for further processing.

In general, a satisfactory specimen should contain cells or microorganisms diagnostic of a pathological process. Although adequacy criteria are poorly developed for BW and BB samples, specimens with few bronchial cells or macrophages and those heavily contaminated and obscured by blood, inflammation, or large numbers of oral squamous cells or oral saprophytes are considered unsatisfactory {10405813; 26990836}.

Complications

Flexible fibre-optic bronchoscopy is a safe procedure with rare complications {19146197; 17722491}.
