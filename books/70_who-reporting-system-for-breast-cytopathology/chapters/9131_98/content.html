9.1.3.1 Angiosarcoma of breast (including primary angiosarcoma and postirradiation angiosarcoma)

Definition

Primary angiosarcoma of the breast is a malignant endothelial neoplasm of breast parenchyma, not associated with radiation exposure, while postradiation angiosarcoma of the breast occurs secondary to irradiation in the skin or breast parenchyma.

Clinical features and imaging

Angiosarcomas account for 0.05% of all malignancies of the breast and can develop de novo or secondary to radiation or longstanding chronic lymphoedema. Patients with primary angiosarcoma have a median age of 40 years at diagnosis {18091049; 18813119}, compared with a median age of 67 years for secondary angiosarcoma {22466664}. Patients invariably present with a poorly defined, rapidly growing mass or swelling or asymmetry of the breast {17325063}. Two thirds of the neoplasms are large and > 50 mm {29205355}.

Radiation-induced angiosarcomas develop 4–12 years after radiation with a mean latency of 5.2 years after breast-conserving therapy {18091049; 19433291}. These may appear as multifocal cutaneous lesions, in the form of erythematous lesions, plaques, patches, and nodules, or as deep-seated masses {16006792; 23327731; 20187111}.

Mammography shows a non-calcified mass or focal asymmetry in most cases. Ultrasonography reveals either a hyperechoic mass or mixed hyperechogenicity and hypoechogenicity with architectural distortion. MRI shows nonspecific features and high signal intensity on T2-weighted images. Dynamic contrast material–enhanced MRI shows a rapid enhancement and washout that is characteristic of a malignant lesion {17325063}. Nearly 20% of patients present with regional disease or metastasis at the time of diagnosis {28449661}, with 3.5% having either clinical or pathological nodal involvement {29208319}. The most frequent sites of distant metastasis are the lungs, liver, bones, and CNS.

Histopathology

Angiosarcomas are mostly deep seated, with or without involvement of the skin. They are infiltrative and show various architectural patterns from vasoformative to solid. Primary angiosarcomas show well-formed, small to medium-sized anastomosing dilated, compressed, or angulated blood vessels dissecting through the fibroadipose tissue {24384851}, infiltrating the stroma and lobules, and lined by endothelial cells showing varying mild to severe atypia. The endothelial cells range from mildly raised to multilayered, hobnail, or tufted. Poorly differentiated angiosarcomas comprise markedly atypical spindle and epithelioid cells arranged in solid and vasoformative patterns with areas of haemorrhage, necrosis, and increased mitoses. Epithelioid angiosarcomas show a solid pattern of polygonal tumour cells {11474291; 17284115; 1718176}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Well-differentiated angiosarcoma can be difficult to diagnose on cytopathology owing to the mild nuclear atypia. FNAB is associated with a false negative rate as high as 40% and CNB is the preferred diagnostic technique. Epithelioid angiosarcoma can be mistaken for carcinoma, compounded by its positive aberrant expression of epithelial markers. High-grade tumours with predominantly spindle cells are easily categorized as malignant, and need to be distinguished from phyllodes tumour or other sarcomas. In such cases, ICC for CD31 and ERG is useful to support the diagnosis of angiosarcoma {21053536}. Secondary angiosarcomas arise after breast-conserving therapy with adjuvant radiation therapy and the clinical history and presentation are valuable in assisting the diagnosis {10679998; 17017445; 20187111}.

Ancillary testing

ICC for CD31 (cytoplasmic membranous) and ERG (intranuclear) is invariably positive. CD34, FLI1, and D2-40 show variable positivity. Epithelioid angiosarcoma may stain positively for epithelial markers {11474291; 17284115; 1718176}. Postradiation/secondary angiosarcomas show strong nuclear expression of MYC, as a result of underlying MYC amplifications {21909081; 22121953}. Most cases of radiation-induced angiosarcoma display loss of H3 p.K27me3 {28983701}.
