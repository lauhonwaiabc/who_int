5.0.0.3 Discussion and background

An adequate FNAB sample supplies sufficient cytopathological material for diagnostic purposes, and evaluation of the cytopathological material is done in conjunction with the clinical and imaging findings in the triple test. Sparsely cellular samples from a patient presenting with a palpable or well-defined solid mass on imaging may be categorized as “Insufficient”, “Inadequate”, or “Non-diagnostic”. A FNAB sample discordant with clinical and/or imaging findings, however, does not constitute an insufficient sample. If ample benign breast ductal epithelial cells are obtained from a patient with a mass of high clinical and imaging concern for malignancy, the sample should be categorized as “Benign” with a comment in the cytopathology report stating that “clinical–imaging correlation is recommended to ensure this sample represents the targeted lesion”. Repeat sampling by FNAB or CNB may be appropriate in such settings. This approach is favoured over calling the sample “Non-diagnostic” as the lesion may in fact be benign and reflect a discordant imaging interpretation; for example, on imaging a radial scar can mimic a stellate carcinoma.

The reasons that render a FNAB sample “Insufficient”, “Inadequate”, or “Non-diagnostic” should be stated in the report and include a paucicellular or acellular sample from a palpable mass or defined solid mass on imaging; preparation artefacts including air-dried cells in alcohol-fixed smears, slow-air-drying artefact in air-dried smears, crush artefact, and/or thick smear preparation; and obscuring elements, such as blood or ultrasound gel.

FNAB samples without or with only scant epithelial cells may be adequate and classified as “Benign” in the appropriate clinical and imaging setting, such as patients presenting with lesions that are thought to be an abscess displaying only acute inflammatory cells; or a cyst with only proteinaceous material with or without histiocytes; or fat necrosis with histiocytes, cellular debris, necrotic fat, and punctate necrotic material. In samples with limited epithelial cells, identification of any atypical features, including cytopathological or architectural atypia, necrosis, or acellular mucin, should render the specimen “Atypical”. For example, a FNAB demonstrating scant epithelial cells with minimal nuclear atypia, cytoplasmic vacuolization, and marked dispersal with only occasional minute tissue fragments, from a mass lesion with a clinical and imaging suspicion of invasive carcinoma, may be considered “Atypical” or “Suspicious for malignancy” particularly of invasive lobular carcinoma {25678304}. Likewise, FNAB material revealing acellular mucin from a mass lesion, which is clinically and on imaging suspected to be carcinoma, should be categorized as “Atypical” {31112942}.
