6.0.0.3 Discussion and background

The categorization of a FNAB as “Benign” can only be made when the material is adequate. In general, a benign FNAB is usually cellular showing characteristic patterns of different benign lesions and with no atypical or malignant features. However, in some cases an adequate FNAB may not require epithelial material to be present if the cytopathological findings correlate with the clinical and imaging findings in the triple test and are sufficient to allow a precise diagnosis to be made. Such situations include pus consistent with an abscess; a proteinaceous background with or without histiocytes consistent with cyst contents when the cyst has been drained under imaging or has no residual mass on palpation; fat tissue fragments consistent with a lipoma or fatty nodule; spindle cell lesions; fat necrosis; reactive lymphoid material consistent with an intramammary lymph node; or in some cases a hyalinized fibroadenoma which correlates with imaging. However, in the case of a mass lesion detected on palpation or imaging that does not drain or at least significantly decrease in size on FNAB, it is recommended that seven tissue fragments each consisting of ≥ 20 epithelial cells should be a measure of adequacy, thus allowing assessment of the architecture and the presence or absence of myoepithelial cells in the tissue fragment, in addition to assessment of the epithelial cells {31112942} [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]].

The vast majority of breast FNABs are benign and broadly include inflammatory lesions, fibrocystic changes, fibroadenomas, benign epithelial hyperplastic changes, and other lesions such as intraductal papillomas and rare lesions including phyllodes tumours, radial scars, and other benign tumours.

Among the inflammatory lesions, the disease entities include acute and chronic mastitis, abscess and duct ectasia, and granulomatous inflammation. The typical cytopathological features of most of the inflammatory lesions are nonspecific, consisting of mostly mixed inflammatory cells within a background with debris. The only possible exception is granulomatous inflammation, where granulomas composed of epithelioid histiocytes and multinucleated giant cells may be seen {12835297}. Fibrocystic changes very often are accompanied by epithelial hyperplasia, and thus may be considered proliferative. The frequent occurrence of cyst and apocrine metaplasia may assist in diagnosing fibrocystic changes. The cytopathological features of any superimposed epithelial hyperplasia may be less specific and may sometimes be confused with atypical epithelial hyperplasia [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]]. The cytopathological features of adenosis have been described but these are also nonspecific. The cytopathological diagnostic criteria for fibroadenomas have been well described {21660275}. Given that this is the most common lesion encountered in benign breast FNAB, familiarity in diagnosing fibroadenomas is crucial. One specific differential is with phyllodes tumour and there is significant cytopathological morphological overlap, sometimes rendering this differentiation challenging {12365219}. Most other benign tumours and lesions are relatively uncommon, including radial scars, hamartoma, pleomorphic adenoma, tubular adenoma, and adenomyoepithelioma.

The typical benign FNAB consists of benign epithelial cells showing bland nuclear morphology and arranged in cohesive tissue fragments of varying size and demonstrating a bimodal pattern of epithelial cells and myoepithelial cells. Hyperplastic branching sheets of ductal epithelial cells are seen in epithelial hyperplasia most typically in fibroadenomas. In the background there are stripped oval “bipolar” myoepithelial nuclei showing evenly distributed chromatin and smooth nuclear membranes, an absence of nucleoli, and strong positivity for p63 {12811858}. There is also a variable presence of metaplastic apocrine cells most commonly in cysts, where foamy histiocytes are present in a proteinaceous background. Other benign FNABs will have inflammatory cells reflective of inflammation or abscess formation, and stromal fragments of fibroadenomas or intraductal papillomas.
