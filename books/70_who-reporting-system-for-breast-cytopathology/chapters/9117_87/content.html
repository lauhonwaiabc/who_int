9.1.1.7 Mucinous carcinoma

Definition

Mucinous carcinoma of the breast is a special type of invasive breast carcinoma, characterized by epithelial tumour cells with low- to intermediate-grade nuclei suspended in extracellular mucin.

Clinical features and imaging

Mucinous carcinoma represents about 2% of all breast carcinomas, with an excellent prognosis, with a 10-year survival rate of 89%, occurring in women with a median age of 71 years {18026874; 18809061}. Clinically, it can present as a palpable soft lump. Mammographically, mucinous carcinoma appears as a well-circumscribed mass and may mimic a benign process with its rounded contours. On ultrasound, most of the cases are seen as a hypoechoic mass {20615642}. MRI reveals a persistent enhancement pattern and hyperintensity on T2-weighted images {21071383}.

Histopathology

Mucinous carcinoma is defined as a tumour composed of malignant epithelial cells floating within extracellular mucin lakes, characterized by a > 90% mucinous component. Low nuclear grade (i.e. grade 1 or occasionally grade 2) is required for the diagnosis, precluding grade 3 nuclear changes for the diagnosis of mucinous carcinoma. Tumours with high nuclear grade have been described (see below for discussion) {17873114}.

Two main forms of mucinous carcinoma have been described: type A and type B {6254868}. Type A, considered the classic variety, has large amounts of extracellular mucin, and is relatively hypocellular, producing paucicellular mucin-rich smears that can potentially mimic benign lesions, such as mucocoele-like lesions with extravasated mucin. Type B is the more cellular form, with endocrine differentiation and sometimes signet-ring cells. As mentioned, the diagnosis of pure mucinous carcinoma requires a mucinous component of > 90%, while mixed mucinous carcinoma has 10–90%. A mucinous component of < 10% should be mentioned. Pure mucinous carcinoma may have foci with a micropapillary pattern consisting of epithelial tissue fragments suspended in mucin pools resembling invasive micropapillary carcinoma {12378595; 20962055; 23517923; 25953688; 18387988}. These tumours have more marked usually high-grade nuclear atypia, may have hobnail cells and psammomatous calcifications, show more frequent lymph node metastasis {20962055; 23517923; 25953688}, and are frequently ER-negative and HER2-positive, and are best classified as invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (IBC-NST) with mucin [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]]. Carcinomas with signet-ring cells without extracellular mucin are not classified as mucinous carcinoma. Signet-ring cytomorphology is most common in invasive lobular carcinoma, but may also be present in IBC-NST and rarely in other special histopathological subtypes. These cases should be distinguished from metastases. Mucinous carcinomas are ER-positive and PR-positive, and HER2-negative.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The combination of bland epithelial tumour cells in tissue fragments and as dispersed cells within abundant background extracellular mucin is the distinctive feature of pure mucinous carcinoma. However, the presence of extracellular mucin is not unique to mucinous carcinoma and may be seen in other benign and malignant lesions of the breast. The main differential diagnoses include mucocoele-like lesions, myxoid fibroadenomas, papillary neoplasms, IBC-NST with mucin production, and micropapillary carcinoma with mucin production {9622685; 16329537; 12931549; 22994403; 26104545; 36891770}. The relatively low-grade cytopathological features of pure mucinous carcinoma can suggest a benign lesion. Mucocoele-like lesions are acellular to sparsely cellular with small flat sheets of cohesive uniform epithelial cells often with myoepithelial cells in a background of thin mucin, derived from cysts. Single isolated atypical epithelial cells are notably absent. Myxoid fibroadenomas have a granular mucinous background but lack the abundant and fibrillary mucin of mucinous carcinoma {16329537}, and are characterized by cohesive branching tissue fragments of ductal epithelial cells, stromal fragments, and bare, bipolar nuclei. Intraductal papillomas and papillary neoplasms may be associated with scant mucin, but intraductal papillomas have cohesive ductal epithelial tissue fragments with myoepithelial cells, fibroelastotic stellate papillary tissue fragments, and usually apocrine sheets and siderophages. Papillary ductal carcinoma in situ and related lesions have tissue fragments with fine branching fibrovascular cores and often columnar epithelium with usually mild to moderate nuclear atypia, and variable but often marked dispersal of single cells. IBC-NST with mucin production and micropapillary carcinoma with mucin production yield highly cellular smears with a small amount of mucin in the background, and the degree of cellularity and cytopathological nuclear atypia is generally greater than that of pure mucinous carcinoma. Mucinous carcinoma may have foci with a micropapillary pattern reminiscent of invasive micropapillary carcinoma and this can be associated with high nuclear grade, when the carcinoma is best regarded as an IBC-NST {12378595; 20962055; 23517923}. IBC-NST with prominent mucin and high nuclear grade, and invasive micropapillary carcinoma with mucin and high nuclear grade are not classified as mucinous carcinoma [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]]. Micropapillary carcinoma may have small papillary tissue fragments that can resemble florets {36891770}.

FNAB is a sampling technique as is CNB and the subtyping of a carcinoma as a mucinous carcinoma can be suggested on the FNAB features, but excision with assessment of the whole tumour is required to demonstrate the percentage of mucinous carcinoma within the tumour mass to make the diagnosis of a pure mucinous carcinoma.

Ancillary testing

Mucinous carcinoma is positive for ER and PR and usually negative for HER2 (ERBB2) overexpression and/or amplification {23771415; 18026874; 18809061}. Mucinous carcinoma with a prominent micropapillary pattern and high-grade nuclei is best considered as IBC-NST with mucin production and may be HER2-positive {23517923}. AR is positive in 80% of cases {21552212}, and WT1 {18469795} and GATA3 {25544392} are frequently expressed. At the molecular level, mucinous carcinomas are genetically heterogeneous and lack a pathognomonic fusion gene or somatic mutation {30649385}.
