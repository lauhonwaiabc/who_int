9.1.1.5 Tubular carcinoma

Definition

Tubular carcinoma (TC) is a low-grade invasive breast carcinoma composed of > 90% well-formed tubules with open lumina lined by a single layer of neoplastic cells.

Clinical features and imaging

There are no specific clinical features differentiating TC from other breast carcinomas {16896563; 10334529; 8001000}. On mammography TCs are small spiculated masses sometimes accompanied by scattered microcalcifications in the carcinoma or associated ductal carcinoma in situ. Ultrasound shows a low-echogenicity tumour centre with surrounding spiculations and tissue disruption. TC may be multifocal in 10–20% of cases {9199642; 9930775; 31112942}.

Histopathology

TCs are by definition grade 1 carcinomas consisting of > 90% round to ovoid or angular tubules with open lumina composed of a single layer of epithelial cells without surrounding myoepithelium. Many tubules may be more complex and cribriform. Nuclei are low-grade showing sparse mitoses. Epithelial cell apical snouts and luminal secretions are frequently present, and calcifications may be present in the tubules and the surrounding sclerotic desmoplastic stroma, and in the low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ, which is often present.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Usually high cellularity Predominance of cohesive, rigid, tubular, angled tissue fragments Variable number of monolayered or irregular 3D tissue fragments lacking myoepithelial cells Variable number of cribriform tissue fragments varying from small to large, with punched-out holes and lacking myoepithelial cells Monomorphic cells with small nuclei showing minimal nuclear atypia and inconspicuous nucleoli Single isolated intact cells with often eccentric cytoplasm, which may contain single large vacuoles Occasional stromal tufts No necrosis or mitoses Occasional stripped bare nuclei in the background may be present

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Diagnosis of TC may be challenging owing to low nuclear grade and the tightly cohesive tissue fragments with minimal nuclear atypia. The false negative rate for FNAB of TC has been reported to be as high as 11%.

TC should be differentiated from fibroadenomas and fibrocystic change with epithelial hyperplasia, especially radial scar. Fibroadenomas are composed of benign epithelial cells in cohesive tissue fragments that are irregular with secondary lumina and myoepithelial cells, stromal tissue fragments, and usually plentiful bare bipolar nuclei. Nuclear pleomorphism is usually mild with no or minimal atypia. Occasional tubular tissue fragments can be present but these have myoepithelial cells, and it is the overall pattern that assists in the differential diagnosis from TC {8160643}. Similarly, cohesive irregular epithelial tissue fragments lacking nuclear atypia with myoepithelial cells, apocrine sheets, and bare bipolar nuclei with histiocytes in a proteinaceous background favour radial scar / complex sclerosing lesions {8173027; 7992574}.

Ancillary testing

TCs are ER-positive and HER2-negative and belong to the luminal A molecular class of breast cancer {18720457; 16753605; 16753605; 8187589}, which provides a biological basis for the excellent clinical outcome.
