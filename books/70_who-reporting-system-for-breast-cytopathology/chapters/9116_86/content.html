9.1.1.6 Invasive cribriform carcinoma

Definition

Invasive cribriform carcinoma (InvCC) is a low- to intermediate-grade invasive carcinoma composed of islands of tumour cells with well-defined cribriform spaces.

Clinical features and imaging

InvCC is a rare type of primary breast carcinoma with an incidence of 0.3–3.5% {36684641}. It may present as a mass but is frequently clinically occult. Occasionally, InvCC presents with axillary lymph node involvement.

On imaging, InvCC, like other types of invasive carcinoma, forms a spiculated mass, associated frequently with microcalcifications {7955831}. Multifocality is present in as many as 20% of cases {8780923; 6884999}.

Histopathology

InvCC consists of invasive epithelial islands containing well-defined, rounded spaces similar in appearance to cribriform ductal carcinoma in situ {6884999; 7810003; 3343375}. Mitotic activity is sparse and there is no substantial nuclear atypia. InvCCs are by convention grade 1 tumours {20804570}. Stromal osteoclast-like giant cells may be present {11480724}. InvCC is frequently mixed with tubular carcinoma.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

InvCC is composed of ≥ 90% cribriform islands of epithelial cells with low-grade nuclei and rare if any mitoses. Frequently, tubular tissue fragments are present signifying a mixed tubular and cribriform carcinoma. Both these subtypes at the time of wide local excision when final classification occurs may show a mixed carcinoma with invasive carcinoma of no special type (NST).

Distinguishing reliably between InvCC and low-grade cribriform ductal carcinoma in situ cannot be performed on FNAB, and correlation with imaging is required. CNB may assist, but as with FNAB, both are sampling tests. Naked bipolar nuclei and myoepithelial cells are absent {11480724}. When plump spindle cells or stroma are seen in the background, fibroepithelial lesions may come into the differential diagnosis. Adenoid cystic carcinoma, which can have cribriform architecture with or without basement membrane or mucinous material in the cribriform spaces, may sometimes be in the differential diagnosis, but these are triple-negative tumours.

Ancillary testing

ICC for myoepithelial markers may be used to distinguish InvCC from cribriform ductal carcinoma in situ if there is sufficient material in the cell block. ICC is ER-positive and PR-positive {26252312; 2312110}, and HER2-negative (94%). InvCC is part of the low-grade breast neoplasia pathway {26252312; 20452298}.
