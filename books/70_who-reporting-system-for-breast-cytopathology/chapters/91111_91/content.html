9.1.1.11 Metaplastic carcinoma

Definition

Metaplastic carcinoma (MC) is a heterogeneous group of invasive breast carcinomas characterized by differentiation of the neoplastic epithelium towards squamous, spindle, and/or mesenchymal elements, including but not restricted to chondroid and osseous cells.

Clinical features and imaging

MC is rare and represents 0.2–1% of all invasive breast carcinomas {29330212; 25012264; 24838367}. MC includes low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma and fibromatosis-like carcinoma, which tend to present in older patients and have a good prognosis, and high-grade carcinomas that can be spindle cell, squamous cell, and MC with heterologous mesenchymal differentiation, all of which are more likely to present at an advanced stage {29330212} with a palpable mass. High-grade tumours are frequently mixed and may be ulcerated. MC is usually of a large size, with mixed forms of MC being larger than the low-grade forms such as low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma and fibromatosis-like carcinoma.

Most cases (85%) are detected as a mass lesion. Calcifications are uncommon, and when present they are often associated with ductal carcinoma in situ and/or osseous differentiation {27245135}.

Histopathology

The subtypes of metaplastic carcinoma include low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma, fibromatosis-like metaplastic carcinoma, spindle cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, metaplastic carcinoma with heterologous mesenchymal differentiation, and mixed metaplastic carcinoma.

Low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma is composed of glands and solid cords of epithelial cells, which may contain polygonal squamous cells sometimes with squamous pearls, or squamous cyst formation. Fibromatosis-like metaplastic carcinoma is composed of bland small spindle cells with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and slender nuclei with tapered edges. These cells are often arranged in wavy, interlacing fascicles. Stroma shows varying degrees of collagenization or hyalinization. Spindle cell carcinomas are characterized by dense, atypical spindle cells arranged in long fascicles in herringbone or interwoven patterns varying to short fascicles in a storiform pattern. Nuclear pleomorphism is usually moderate to high. Mitotic figures are seen. Squamous cell carcinoma is composed of polygonal, malignant cells showing variable degrees of squamous differentiation. Cells infiltrate the adjacent stroma in the form of sheets, cords, and nests. Metaplastic breast carcinomas with heterologous mesenchymal elements are composed of an admixture of mesenchymal heterologous chondroid, osseous or rhabdomyoid components intermingled with clearly carcinomatous areas in glandular or solid islands. Mixed metaplastic carcinomas display a mixture of different metaplastic and conventional adenocarcinomatous components.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The different patterns of metaplastic carcinomas may be mixed. The presence of one or more of squamous cells, osteoclast-like giant cells, spindle cells, and/or osseous/chondroid matrix are clues to the diagnosis of metaplastic carcinoma {21791899; 21244696}.

The existing literature on the cytopathological features of metaplastic carcinoma is limited, with a small number of case series and single cases reports {21244696; 16798932; 21791899}. In general, spindle cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, metaplastic carcinoma with heterologous mesenchymal differentiation, and mixed metaplastic carcinomas produce hypercellularity and nuclear features of malignancy. Recognition of the various tumour patterns may be challenging because FNAB and CNB sample the often mixed tumours. Low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma and fibromatosis-like metaplastic carcinoma often produce low cellularity with low-grade or bland nuclei. Low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma should be differentiated from microcystic adnexal or adenosquamous carcinoma of the skin or syringomatous adenoma of the nipple, and squamous cells are suggestive of metaplastic carcinoma {9884821; 25322776}. Fibromatosis-like metaplastic carcinoma may be misdiagnosed as fibroadenoma; the presence of tissue fragments of epithelial ovoid to plump spindle cells associated with occasional spindle cells in the background, and a lack of typical myxoid or sclerotic fibroadenoma stroma, and a lack of plentiful bare bipolar nuclei raise the possibility of metaplastic carcinoma {17324295; 20968161}.

High-grade metaplastic carcinoma must be differentiated from metastases to the breast or other rare primary malignancies whenever possible by reviewing the patient history and imaging scans and utilizing ICC. Spindle cell carcinoma should be differentiated from other spindle cell malignancies, such as metastatic melanoma or sarcoma, especially from spindle cell angiosarcoma in patients previously treated by radiotherapy {12951681}. Metaplastic carcinoma with heterologous mesenchymal differentiation should be differentiated from pleomorphic adenoma {26823890} or primary or metastatic osteosarcoma/chondrosarcoma especially in patients previously treated by radiotherapy {17427216}. Mixed metaplastic carcinomas, depending on their morphological composition, combine all of these differential diagnoses.

Ancillary testing

More than 90% of metaplastic carcinomas are triple negative on ICC for ER, PR, and HER2 (ERBB2) and frequently positive for CK5/6 or CK14 and are classified as basal-like carcinomas {37987613; 16842242; 29330212; 24838367}.
