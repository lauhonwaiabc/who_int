7.0.0.4 Risk of malignancy and management recommendations

Since the publication of the International Academy of Cytology Yokohama System for Reporting Breast Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy Cytopathology, studies have reported a wide range in the proportion of lesions categorized as “Atypical”, from 1.2% to 34.8% of cases (median: 9.3%, mean: 10.7%) {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}. The large variation in usage of this category reflects the inherent issues of an indeterminate category, in particular a lack of reproducibility and variable cytopathologist experience and confidence. Consequently, the variable use of “Atypical” has resulted in a similar wide range of reported ROM, from 13.0% to 40.0% (median: 24.1%, mean: 24.0%) {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}. The WHO Reporting System for Breast Cytopathology aims to differentiate “Atypical” lesions from lesions of greater concern (“Suspicious for malignancy”) with an overall lower ROM, ideally of 13–15%.

Management of a patient with a breast FNAB classified in the “Atypical” category is initially correlation with the clinical and imaging findings in the triple test. If the clinical and imaging findings are benign, representing a low clinical suspicion, repeat clinical and imaging examination with a repeat FNAB if there are any significant changes in 3–6 months is appropriate. If the clinical and imaging findings are indeterminate or are suspicious for malignancy, CNB is the preferred next step in management if this is available in the medical resource setting; repeat FNAB may be performed in settings where CNB is not available. If repeat sampling is also categorized as “Atypical”, a tissue biopsy is recommended.
