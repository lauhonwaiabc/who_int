10.1.2 Category: Benign

Definition: A specimen categorized as “Benign” demonstrates unequivocal benign cytopathological features which may or may not be diagnostic of a specific process or benign neoplasm.

The “Benign” category includes highly cellular neoplasms such as fibroadenoma, intraductal papilloma, and fibrocystic change with and without epithelial hyperplasia including radial scars, but in some benign cases an adequate FNAB may not require epithelial material to be present if the cytopathological findings correlate with the clinical and imaging findings in the triple test, including pus consistent with an abscess, a proteinaceous background with or without histiocytes consistent with cyst contents when the cyst has been drained under imaging or there is no residual mass to palpation, fat tissue fragments consistent with a lipoma or fatty nodule, spindle cell lesions, fat necrosis, reactive lymphoid material consistent with an intramammary lymph node, or in some cases a hyalinized fibroadenoma which correlates with imaging. However, in the case of a mass lesion on palpation or imaging that does not decrease in size and drain on FNAB, it is recommended that seven tissue fragments each consisting of 20 or more epithelial cells should be a measure of adequacy and allows for assessment of the architecture and the presence or absence of myoepithelial cells in addition to the assessment of the epithelial cells {31112942} [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]]. Lesions such as epithelial hyperplasia, lactational change, and columnar cell change can be diagnosed by FNAB, while other proliferative lesions, such as atypical ductal hyperplasia, flat epithelial atypia, and lobular neoplasia, can be suspected, but a specific diagnosis cannot be made because it requires assessment of size and histopathological features. The distinction of fibroadenoma, the commonest lesion sampled by FNAB {21660275}, from low-grade phyllodes tumour can be difficult owing to overlap of diagnostic features {12365219}.

The “Benign” category also includes mastitis, suppurative abscesses, and granulomatous mastitis. FNAB allows for rapid management of breast abscesses, making the diagnosis and then offering drainage as a therapeutic approach to assist antimicrobial therapy. In cases of granulomatous inflammation, FNAB can make the diagnosis and yield material for microbiological investigations and appropriate treatment {12835297}. FNAB of a cyst allows for both diagnosis and management in the form of cyst drainage. Even if a lesion appears benign clinically and on imaging, a final cytopathological diagnosis can be very reassuring to patient and clinician alike {32749785; 33755356; 34029451; 21660275; 34866246}.

Benign breast disease is reported as 10 times more common in most settings than breast malignancy {30990294; 27866091; 35168773}, and in several published series of breast FNAB, the majority of diagnoses are benign, constituting 24% to 77.5% of total cases {21660275; 33755356; 32749785; 34029451}. The ROM for a lesion categorized as “Benign” ranges from 0% to 11.7% in a series of articles utilizing the International Academy of Cytology Yokohama System for Reporting Breast Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy Cytopathology (IAC System), although some series have reported ranges from 0% to 5.8%, with variations depending on the patient cohorts, the role of FNAB in the diagnosis of lesions varying from diffuse and poorly defined to solid mass lesions to microcalcifications, the nature of the lesion, and the form of statistical analysis [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]] {31108486; 30783035; 34866246; 36370120; 33333512; 36370120; 34866246}. A recent meta-analysis pooling 27 studies over 10 years showed a 4.7% ROM for the “Benign” category based on the IAC System {31141809}. The negative predictive value of benign breast FNAB is > 97% [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]].

A recent meta-analysis of nipple discharge cytopathology reported a sensitivity of 75% and specificity of 87% for benign breast disease, bearing in mind that nipple discharge cytopathology is frequently acellular in both benign and malignant breast disease, limiting diagnostic utility. Other diagnostic modalities such as ultrasound, mammography, MRI, and galactography performed no better in patients with nipple discharge {34839426}.

The management of a lesion categorized as “Benign” on cytopathology is correlation with the clinical and imaging findings in the triple test, and although there is a long more than 50-year tradition of FNAB diagnosis of palpable lesions directed by palpation only, including abscesses, fibroadenomas, and cysts, imaging is required wherever available. If on review there are no clinical or imaging concerns, routine physical and imaging examination at 12-month intervals is appropriate depending on available resources, with an immediate repeat FNAB if there is any alteration in the lesion. If the clinical and/or imaging findings on review and correlation are indeterminate or atypical, consideration should be given to repeat FNAB or CNB when this is available [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]] {32044283}.
