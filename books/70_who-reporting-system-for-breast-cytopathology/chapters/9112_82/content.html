9.1.1.2 Lobular carcinoma

Definition

Lobular neoplasia, which includes atypical lobular hyperplasia (ALH) and lobular carcinoma in situ (LCIS), is a non-invasive neoplastic proliferation of discohesive small cells originating in the terminal duct lobular unit.

Invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC) is an invasive breast carcinoma most often composed of discohesive monotonous cells that are dispersed singly or arranged in minute tissue fragments.

Clinical features and imaging

ALH and LCIS are often multicentric and bilateral {209887; 1853802; 5915941; 218506; 5394401}. In most cases they lack clinical and mammographic findings and are usually incidental findings in breast CNB or surgical specimens performed for other indications {19970575}. They are not associated with any grossly recognizable findings {209887}.

Pleomorphic LCIS (PLCIS) and florid LCIS may present as a unifocal lesion, continuous in distribution and often colocalized with ILC {21287281; 30489319}. They are often associated with microcalcifications and detected on screening mammography {19701073; 28612228; 28929550; 25893410; 24372322; 23809857; 12379750; 20586632}.

ILC usually presents as a poorly defined palpable mass, and imaging findings are frequently, particularly in screening programmes, an architectural distortion that is often poorly defined or in some cases a spiculated mass. The macroscopic findings frequently show irregular and poorly defined tumours {8156495} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]].

Histopathology

LCIS is defined by loss of membranous E-cadherin expression {23759937; 7682767; 15696125; 12861063} and there are three forms: classic LCIS has > 50% expansion of the terminal duct lobular unit; ALH is defined by expansion of < 50% of the acini in a terminal duct lobular unit; and PLCIS is characterized by larger cells and nuclear pleomorphism and atypia similar to high-grade DCIS. In classic LCIS, two epithelial cell types are present in varying degrees: type A cells are small with hyperchromatic nuclei and smooth chromatin, and type B cells have larger nuclei that are more pleomorphic with vesicular chromatin and small nucleoli. There is loss of cohesion between both cell types in the expanded terminal ductules. With florid LCIS, the expansion of the terminal duct lobular unit or ducts is more pronounced with loss of intervening stroma. ILC can occur with all types of LCIS, although, there is a high prevalence of ILC, as much as 87%, with both florid LCIS and PLCIS [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int/581.]].

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The features of ALH, classic LCIS, and classic ILC overlap in cytopathology, and in many cases all three are present {2750704; 8143551; 12112810}. The findings are subtle and easily missed with low cellularity leading to a significant rate of false negative diagnoses where cases are misinterpreted as benign or insufficient. In histopathology, ILC frequently infiltrates around normal lobules and ducts that may show fibrocystic or hyperplastic changes, and these may be present in FNAB smears masking the invasive carcinoma. Correlation with clinical findings including breast imaging studies is critical. In cases where a suspicious lesion is noted on breast imaging and the FNAB is discordant, a CNB or excision should be recommended.

The main differential diagnosis of ALH, classic ILC, and LCIS is benign epithelial changes. Pleomorphic ILC has high-grade atypia and the cells are obviously malignant even when cellularity is low.

There is limited literature on PLCIS, but the main differential diagnoses are high-grade DCIS and classic lobular carcinoma. Three reports of a total of 15 PLCIS cases propose a moderate to marked increase in nuclear size and pleomorphism as the most distinguishing feature {9100538; 27489086; 18499996}. In one series of eight PLCIS cases, all were diagnosed as DCIS on FNAB and as PLCIS on the surgical specimens, and the authors suggested that only plasmacytoid features were a clue to distinguish PLCIS from DCIS. ICC for E-cadherin on cell block material will be negative in PLCIS.

Ancillary testing

Ancillary testing for lobular neoplasia on cell blocks shows negative staining for E-cadherin, and cytoplasmic staining for p120 in lobular neoplasia versus membrane staining in ductal carcinoma. Similarly, β-catenin is lost in lobular neoplasia {34359596; 25849106}.
