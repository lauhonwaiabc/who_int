3.1.0.6 Cell preparation methods

All breast cytopathology samples should be collected, transported, and stored following the standard operating procedure of the cytopathology laboratory. The effect of preanalytical problems should be minimized. There are multiple cell-preparation methods for different breast cytopathology samples.

Fine-needle aspiration biopsy

To make a smear, the FNAB sample is expressed onto the centre of a glass slide. Using a light touch to avoid crushing the sample, a smearing slide should be drawn in a perpendicular fashion across the drop of material to form a thin oval smear of even thickness. This skill takes practice and should not be performed by an inexperienced or unskilled person as it can result in loss of the sample. The smear slide can be left to air-dry for a Giemsa modified stain or it can be immediately fixed in alcohol by immersing the slide into 95% ethanol for a few seconds. If the slide for Giemsa stain is bloody or thick, or has a lot of fluid, then rapid air-drying with a hairdryer should be considered to avoid slow-air-drying artefact. The alcohol-fixed slides can then be stained with standard Pap. For ROSE, the Giemsa-stained slide can be examined without a coverslip. Alternatively, the alcohol-fixed slide can be stained with toluidine blue, coverslipped (without adhesive), reviewed for ROSE, and then returned to the alcohol container where the coverslip will come off and the slide will be decolorized {22923899}. The alcohol-fixed slide can then be stained with Pap. Other less commonly used staining methods include H&E, using the same methodology as for a surgical pathology frozen section, and ultrarapid Pap.

Cell block

FNAB cell blocks should be prepared by rinsing the FNAB material through the needle directly into formalin with the goal of preserving the cytomorphology and architecture of tissue fragments and creating CNB equivalents. Formalin-fixed FNAB samples can be processed using agar or collodion bag techniques {27105161}. Alternatively, the FNAB material can be rinsed in a transport medium and then centrifuged in the laboratory, the supernatant removed, and the pellet processed. Saline is not an ideal transport medium as it disrupts cells. The very commonly used plasma-thrombin cell block technique, where thrombin is added to plasma and the pellet, and the resultant clot is processed in formalin, is not ideal because of the risk of contamination with foreign DNA {31756042; 31756044}. Cell block systems that utilize alcohol as a fixative should also be avoided as alcohol fixation affects ICC staining for breast biomarkers including ER and PR {27557411}. The College of American Pathologists’ (CAP) guidelines for breast biomarkers specifies strict fixation guidelines for formalin fixation including length of time {29846104; 31928354}, and laboratory protocols should be validated.

Fluid samples

Cyst fluid and nipple discharge fluid can be processed as LBC, which also allows for transportation in stable collecting media {25427721}. Alternatively, cyst fluid samples can undergo cytospinning to create slides or be centrifuged and the pellet smeared onto slides. A few drops of any fluid sample can also be processed as direct smears which can be air-dried or alcohol fixed as described above.
