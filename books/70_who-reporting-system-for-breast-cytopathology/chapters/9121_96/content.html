9.1.2.1 Borderline and malignant phyllodes tumours

Definition

Phyllodes tumour (PT) is a generally circumscribed fibroepithelial neoplasm characterized by a prominent intracanalicular architecture with expansile leaf-like hypercellular stromal fronds covered by benign epithelium and myoepithelium.

Clinical features and imaging

PT present as unilateral, firm, painless, mobile masses with rapid growth and distortion and may ulcerate the skin, which does not automatically denote malignancy, although if the masses are polypoid and fleshy, they are malignant {25880837}. They often develop at an older age and are larger in size, with more rapid growth than benign PT {22049216}. Local recurrence rates increase with increasing malignancy. Malignant PTs may develop haematogenous distant metastases.

Imaging shows a round to bosselated, circumscribed mass with clefts, cysts, and coarse calcifications, and does not reliably distinguish benign from borderline or malignant PT {21353414; 29412020}, although irregular cyst walls, lower or equal signal intensity on T2-weighted imaging, and low apparent diffusion coefficient on MRI suggest malignancy {17032912}.

Histopathology

PTs show an intracanalicular architecture with leaf-like projections into variably dilated clefts and cysts lined by ductal epithelium which can be hyperplastic with myoepithelial cells, and are graded on a constellation of histopathological criteria including increased stromal cellularity; nuclear atypia of stromal cells; the number of stromal mitoses; tumour boundaries ranging from rounded/pushing to infiltrative; and the presence or absence of stromal overgrowth defined as an increase in stroma lacking an epithelial component, that is, no epithelium in a field utilizing a ×4 objective and ×10 eyepiece. Stromal features can vary from myxoid to sclerotic in different areas of PT, making assessment based on sampling techniques such as FNAB and CNB difficult. The margins of the tumour should be cleared at surgery {22049216}.

In malignant PT, at least four of the following five features must be present: stromal cellularity is diffusely and markedly increased; marked stromal nuclear atypia is present; mitoses exceed 5 per mm2; stromal overgrowth with a minimum of one ×400 field lacking epithelium is seen; and an infiltrative margin is present. If a malignant heterologous element is present, this diagnoses a malignant PT, although well-differentiated liposarcoma has less significance and lacks MDM2 and CDK4 amplifications {27040923; 26542423; 26843318}. For malignant PTs, positive surgical margins and breast-conserving surgery are risk factors for local recurrence, while large tumour size and malignant histopathology increase metastatic risk {29146885}.

Borderline PT can have as many as three of the features defining malignant PT, but generally shows moderately increased stromal cellularity, which can be diffuse or heterogeneous, mild to moderate nuclear atypia, and a mitotic count between 2 and 5 per mm2. There is usually no stromal overgrowth though some borderline tumours may possess focal stromal overgrowth. There are no malignant heterologous components, apart from the well-differentiated liposarcoma which can be observed in borderline tumours.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

PTs show a histopathological spectrum, and classification as benign, borderline, or malignant PT is based on the synthesis of multiple criteria, some of which, such as assessment for an infiltrating margin, require a surgical specimen. PTs also show intratumoural heterogeneity, making a specific diagnosis on FNAB and on CNB difficult, and resulting in a lower grade of malignancy on FNAB than in the final surgical diagnosis {16461806}. One study reported a higher proportion of spindle cells in the stroma (> 30%) in malignant PTs {28738381}, while another reported increased spindle and pleomorphic sarcomatous cells in the background. A triple test including the clinical picture, such as patient age, tumour growth rate, and imaging, is essential. The recent change in the sixth-edition Breast tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series regarding the requirements for four not five criteria for the diagnosis of malignant PT is not strictly applicable to FNAB cytopathology {40223225}. These five criteria include marked stromal nuclear pleomorphism, a marked increase in stromal cellularity usually diffusely, increased mitoses, stromal overgrowth defined by a ×40 magnification field, and an infiltrative margin, and only the first two and to an extent mitoses can be assessed by FNAB. The categorization of a malignant PT as malignant can usually be made on FNAB, but in many cases distinguishing borderline tumours from possible malignant tumours is difficult. Excision biopsy is required.

Metaplastic carcinoma is a key differential diagnosis of malignant PT owing to its mesenchymal elements, although the diagnosis can be straightforward if a metaplastic squamous cell carcinoma component is present.

PT may be associated with myxoid degeneration in the background and must be differentiated from mucinous carcinoma. The biphasic epithelial-stromal appearance in PTs and the abnormal epithelial tissue fragments in mucinous carcinoma are points of differentiation.

In angiosarcoma, spindle cells are present in tissue fragments and singly, and vasoformative differentiation with rudimentary atypical vessels amid atypical spindle cells helps in the differential diagnosis {31117069}. In tumours arising after irradiation, the clinical history is also helpful.

Primary breast sarcomas are rare, but if an epithelial component is not present, and particularly if heterologous elements are present, distinction is problematic for both FNAB and CNB and will rely on surgical excision.

Ancillary testing

Some studies have reported p53 mutations in malignant PTs {12118111; 10383692}. In general, however, although several biomarkers have been proposed (CD117, EGFR, VEGF, etc.) to confirm malignancy of PTs, there is no known distinct marker that can differentiate them on a case-by-case basis. Other studies, such as those on mRNA expression and chromosomal aberrations, are underway, although they are not fully conclusive {18937276}.
