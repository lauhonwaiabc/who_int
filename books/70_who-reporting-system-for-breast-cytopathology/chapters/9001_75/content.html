9.0.0.1 Introduction

A diagnosis of “Malignant” in breast FNAB cytopathology indicates malignancy and may be the basis for definitive treatment. Sufficient cellular contents are present on the cytopathology preparations in satisfactory preservation to categorize a case as “Malignant”: abnormal cells with nuclear enlargement, nuclear contour irregularity, and an increased N:C ratio, present also as single cells. In each case the malignant features should be specified, and diagnosis discussed. Clinical and imaging features must be concordant with malignant cytopathological features {31112942; 36412573}.

While a generic diagnosis of “malignant neoplasm” may sometimes be sufficient detail to decide on further management, indicating the type of malignancy is preferred, for example, “carcinoma, no special type”.

The positive predictive value of a “Malignant” cytopathological categorization is close to 100%; this high certainty is possible because the indeterminate categories “Atypical” or “Suspicious for malignancy” are used for those cases that do not fulfil all the criteria of cytomorphological malignancy or that have a discordant triple test {31141809}.

In contemporary practice of breast cancer treatment, ancillary studies are required to map a treatment plan for the patient. Practice patterns are variable and need to be adapted to locally available resources. Cell block material can be used to reliably determine ER/PR and HER2 (ERBB2) profiles to determine eligibility for hormonal and targeted treatment, respectively {27383478}.

A few limitations of FNAB samples have to be kept in mind: a comment on the nuclear grading is feasible but formal grading requires tissue biopsy. An unequivocal diagnosis of invasive carcinoma is usually not possible on FNAB material but can be made clinically actionable in the case of a mass lesion with established metastatic spread to local lymph nodes. Some special types of breast cancers, such as lobular carcinoma, may produce relatively scant smears with subtle atypia of tumour cells, precluding categorization as “Malignant” on FNAB material alone.

Further ancillary testing of the proliferation index with ICC markers such as Ki-67 or tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes may be estimated on FNAB material but at present this is subject to investigation {35367980; 35129867}.
