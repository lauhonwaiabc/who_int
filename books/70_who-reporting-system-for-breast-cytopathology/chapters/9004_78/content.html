9.0.0.4 Risk of malignancy and management recommendations

For many years, non-standardized descriptive reporting of breast FNAB has been practised, with variable correlation to histopathological outcomes, as well as potentially inconsistent management approaches. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama System for Reporting Breast Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy Cytopathology (the IAC System), initiated in 2016 and published in 2020, recommended a five-tiered categorization (Inadequate/Insufficient, Benign, Atypical, Suspicious of malignancy, Malignant) that provided clarity of terminology, incorporating a definition, ROM, and suggested management algorithm that allowed triaging and flexibility for geographical variations in clinical care settings {31112942}. There have been significant research and a large number of publications assessing the accuracy and usefulness of the IAC System, which are the source of the ROM presented in this first edition of the WHO Reporting System for Breast Cytopathology .

The type of malignancy should be provided as far as is possible [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]]. If the specific tumour type cannot be defined with certainty, the report should comment as to the differential diagnoses and which is the most likely diagnosis.

A “Malignant” categorization based on some 27 studies {30783035; 34876918; 34703093; 35573260; 34535580; 34866246; 33017520; 34133084; 32749785} utilizing and testing the IAC System has a ROM of 98.7%, with complete sensitivity and specificity of 96.3% and 98.8%, positive predictive value and negative predictive value of 98.7% and 95.3%, and false negative and false positive rates of 3.7% and 1%, respectively {31141809}.

When only “Malignant” categorizations are considered according to the IAC System, the sensitivity, specificity, and diagnostic accuracy are reported as 97.56%, 100%, and 99.11%, respectively {31141809}.

The triple approach that correlates FNAB with clinical and imaging findings is crucial, with clinical, imaging, and cytopathological concordance of malignancy as the ideal. If there is a cell block containing adequate material, prognostic and predictive markers can be performed for definitive treatment, including surgery and neoadjuvant chemotherapy.

Depending on local practices, some centres require confirmation using CNB on which the receptor status (ER, PR, and HER2) and Ki-67 proliferation fraction are established in the invasive carcinoma. A CNB is also needed if the FNAB is not concordant with the clinical and imaging findings.

Clinical examination of the axilla, complemented by ultrasound evaluation of axillary lymph nodes, can inform the need for preoperative sampling. If the lymph nodes are clinically suspicious and/or suspicious on imaging, initial FNAB, followed by CNB if the FNAB is unable to render a firm diagnosis, under imaging guidance, may obviate the necessity of an intraoperative sentinel node procedure if it demonstrates malignancy.

Discussion and consensus on the treatment approach for individual patients at a multidisciplinary meeting is the ideal management decision process.
