6.1.3.2 Epithelial-myoepithelial tumours (including pleomorphic adenoma and adenomyoepithelioma)

Definition

Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is a benign tumour with variable cytomorphological and architectural manifestations of epithelial and myoepithelial components set within a chondromyxoid stroma.

Classic adenomyoepithelioma (AME) is a biphasic epithelial-myoepithelial neoplasm characterized by small glandular spaces lined by inner luminal epithelial cells and an outer layer of variably enlarged myoepithelial cells. While classic AME is typically benign, malignant transformation may occur from either the epithelial or the myoepithelial component.

Clinical features and imaging

PA usually presents as a breast nodule in adult female patients {2467872; 28043647}. On imaging, it appears round and often resembles a fibroadenoma. Microcalcifications can be seen, raising suspicion for malignancy {25830053}. Some PAs may show intraductal growth and resemble a cystic lesion {30240069; 1660850}.

AME is rare, with an incidence reported in one series as 0.048% {18524618}. It predominantly affects postmenopausal women with a peak age > 60 years, but occurs over a wide age range, with rare cases reported in male patients {23627458; 8389319; 9275934}. A palpable nodule is the most common presenting symptom, with an average size of 20–25 mm, but larger presentations as much as 100 mm occur. Screening programmes have increased the detection of occult AME. On imaging, AME generally presents as a round or lobulated dense mass with indistinct margins. Calcifications and a cystic appearance are rare mammographic findings {23627458; 23325754; 33607537; 27494340}. On rare occasions, AME can be associated with genetic syndromes, such as neurofibromatosis type 1 {19561236} and Lynch syndrome {38297508; 35707990}.

Histopathology

The histopathological features of a PA are similar to those in the salivary glands. It is usually composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells, organized in glands and cords within a myxochondroid stroma. The glands show two cell layers, with inner epithelial and outer myoepithelial layers. The cords and strands are composed of polygonal, oval to stellate cells that are thought to be myoepithelial in origin. There is no nuclear atypia, necrosis, or atypical mitoses. The stroma may show cartilaginous or osseous metaplasia.

Classic AME is a biphasic tumour composed of tubules with a double cell layer of inner epithelial cells and outer myoepithelial cells which may be spindle, myoid, or plasmacytoid with clear glycogen-rich cytoplasm {6438900; 2844382; 1709559; 2824328; 27494340}. At low magnification, AME typically presents a multilobulated contour with central sclerotic areas {16160470; 17357195; 2831705; 2824328}. Foci of collagenous spherulosis may be present {14693844; 20518417}.

AME exhibits various often mixed architectural patterns {1709559; 2824328}: a tubular pattern consisting of discrete small tubules with prominent myoepithelial cells {1709559; 2824328}, which may have infiltrative margins and is referred to as adenomyoepithelial adenosis by some authors {21048915}; a spindle cell pattern in which spindle myoepithelial cells compress the tubules lined by epithelial cells, which may be hard to recognize {1709559; 23627458}; a lobulated pattern of predominantly myoepithelial cell nests surrounded by matrix which can be densely hyaline and sclerotic, or myxoid or chondroid {11284321}; and a papillary pattern in which the AME, often showing apocrine metaplasia, pushes into ducts merging with the features of intraductal papilloma {2824328; 23627458; 16373236; 1709559}. Atypical AME shows atypia in either the myoepithelial or the epithelial component and an increase in mitoses, noting that AME may have mitoses {33829532}. The sixth-edition Breast tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series has not accepted a recent proposal for a modified classification of AME {33829532}. Malignant AME is defined as carcinoma arising in a recognizable AME with transition to carcinoma, which shows cytopathological atypia, increased mitoses, and necrosis. The carcinoma may arise in the epithelial component as carcinoma of no special type (NST) or lobular carcinoma or other special types, or in the myoepithelial component as metaplastic spindle cell carcinoma, or in both presenting as a biphasic carcinoma, or it may show an aberrant immunophenotype with loss of clear distinction between the dual cell types {33829532}. The use of the term “epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma” is discouraged. ICC can be used to demonstrate the biphasic epithelial-myoepithelial cell types, and the protein encoded by HRAS p.Q61R is specific for AME {31887226}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of PA includes tumours with mucinous or myxoid stroma: mucinous carcinoma, which has finely fibrillary mucin with epithelial tissue fragments and intact epithelial cells floating in the mucin showing mild to moderate nuclear atypia, and dissected capillary tissue fragments, while the stroma in PA is myxofibrillary containing small ovoid to spindle myoepithelial cells {35100498}; and metaplastic carcinoma, in which the matrix can be myxoid, spindle, or chondroid and the nuclear grade of the epithelial fragments is high.

The differential diagnosis of AME includes tumours with biphasic patterns: AME lacks the chondromyxoid or basement membrane–like material seen in PA {12835294}; and fibroadenoma and benign phyllodes tumours have separate epithelial fragments and stromal fragments without the gradual transition or extension of the stroma to the epithelial fragments, bare bipolar myoepithelial nuclei are present in the background, and the epithelial fragments are cohesive and often large with a distinct bimodal pattern of ductal and myoepithelial cells {16544319; 17924402; 21548117}. AME lacks tissue fragments of pleomorphic epithelial cells and dispersed atypical single cells of carcinoma, and has stripped, naked bland nuclei and possible intranuclear inclusions in myoepithelial cells. Classic AME can show mitoses as can atypical AME, with necrosis possible in the latter, but atypical mitoses and necrosis should lead to consideration of malignant AME and a request for a biopsy. The specific diagnosis of malignant AME, which is a rare entity and requires recognition of residual classic AME components, with a further typical NST or other special type of carcinoma, or with a metaplastic spindle cell carcinoma, or mixed malignancy, is not possible on FNAB and requires assessment of the entire excised lesion.

Ancillary testing

ICC on cell block material can demonstrate the dual cell population of myoepithelial and epithelial cells in PA. In AME, p63, SMA, and calponin highlight the outer myoepithelial cell layer, although this is variable, and CK7 and paradoxically CK5/6 stain the inner epithelial cell layer of the tubules, which can also be focally positive for ER and PR.
