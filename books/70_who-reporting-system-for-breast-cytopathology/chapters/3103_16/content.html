3.1.0.3 The role of imaging guidance in breast FNAB

Ultrasound is the primary image guidance used for breast FNAB and CNB. The roles for mammographic and MRI guidance are limited. Ultrasound guidance can be used for breast FNAB of palpable breast lesions to assess the lesion, ensure adequate sampling, and ensure the needle stays within the targeted lesion without sampling adjacent tissue. Additionally, in settings where the targeted lesion is adjacent to a breast implant, ultrasound guidance can be used to help avoid puncturing the implant. In settings where radiologists perform all breast biopsies, ultrasound is generally used for all breast FNAB, including palpable and non-palpable lesions. For breast lesions that are non-palpable, owing to location either deep in the breast or in larger breasts, ultrasound guidance can be critical.

FNAB of a breast lesion can be performed with minimal discomfort without local anaesthetic injected into the skin when a 22 or 23 gauge or smaller needle is utilized. However, some cytopathologists prefer to use local anaesthetic particularly if it is planned to go on to CNB. In this latter scenario, lidocaine or a similar local anaesthetic can be injected into the skin and along the expected needle track, extending from the skin up to the targeted lesion, taking care not to inject into the lesion or in the area immediately adjacent to the lesion. It is helpful to raise a small wheal in the skin with lidocaine as the injection needle is withdrawn. The introducer needle, generally 18–20 gauge, should be gently inserted until the tip of the needle is visualized by ultrasound along the edge of the targeted lesion. The stylet should be carefully removed and set aside in a sterile field. Using a coaxial system, the biopsy needle, generally a 25 gauge spinal needle, is inserted into the introducer up to the edge of the lesion. When the biopsy needle is advanced, the tip should be visible beyond the tip of the introducer as it enters into the lesion. FNAB passes should be performed while visualizing the biopsy needle tip making excursions across the length of the targeted lesion. The biopsy can be performed with or without suction; however, for cystic or partially cystic lesions, it is very helpful to have a 10 mL syringe attached to withdraw the cyst fluid. Complete or partial collapse of a cystic lesion can be visualized in real time on the ultrasound and this information can be incorporated into the final diagnosis. Once an adequate number of excursions have been performed, often indicated by a “flash” of FNAB material into the hub of the needle or syringe, the vacuum pressure should be released and the needle should be withdrawn and used to prepare smears and cell blocks as appropriate {32997819}.

A similar approach should be used for image-guided FNAB of axillary lymph nodes. Once the introducer is in place, it can be helpful to use it with the stylet in place to puncture the capsule of the lymph node. Again using a coaxial system, the biopsy needle should then be passed through the introducer into the lymph node. Needle excursions should be varied in direction in order to sample different parts of the lymph node with attention to any areas identified as suspicious by the radiologist. Once FNAB material is visualized in the hub of the needle, the needle should be withdrawn and smears, cell blocks, and samples for other ancillary studies should be collected as appropriate {24353146}.

ROSE is very helpful in the setting of ultrasound-guided FNAB of the breast or axilla as it allows immediate imaging correlation with the FNAB material, ensuring adequate sampling in real time. In cases where the imaging suggests that the targeted breast lesion is an invasive carcinoma, and the ROSE supports the diagnosis, the radiologist may choose to proceed with an ultrasound-directed CNB to better characterize the invasive and in situ components of the breast cancer. For patients for whom neoadjuvant therapy is an option, a clip is placed in the breast lesion after the CNB, as well as in any lymph nodes that are suspicious or malignant by imaging and ROSE.
