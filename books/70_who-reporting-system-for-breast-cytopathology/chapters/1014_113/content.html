10.1.4 Category: Suspicious for malignancy

Definition: A specimen categorized as “Suspicious for malignancy” demonstrates some cytopathological features suggestive of malignancy but with insufficient features, either in number or in quality, to make an unequivocal diagnosis of malignancy.

The ability to distinguish and use the “Atypical”, “Suspicious for malignancy”, and “Malignant” categories shows considerable interobserver variability based on experience, the quality of the sample, and the nature of the lesion. The commonest neoplastic lesions leading to a “Suspicious for malignancy” categorization are invasive carcinoma of no special type (NST) {33017520; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34029453; 33629823; 34866246; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093}, ductal carcinoma in situ {34029453; 32749785; 31108486; 34703093; 33755356}, and lobular carcinoma {32749785; 31108486}, while cellular fibroadenoma, benign phyllodes tumours, intraductal papilloma, and sclerosing adenosis account for most of the benign lesions in this category {7992576; 34703093; 32749785; 33017520; 33629823; 34535580; 33755356; 10458501}.

Reasons for a “Suspicious for malignancy” rather than a “Malignant” categorization include low cellularity {7992576} intrinsic to the lesion as found with some invasive lobular carcinomas, low-grade invasive carcinoma NST, and metaplastic carcinomas {10934957; 9250310; 16798932}; ductal carcinoma in situ and papillary lesions that show a spectrum of changes; low-grade nuclear features; obscuring necrosis, debris, and blood; and poor FNAB and smearing technique, poor staining, and lack of experience in the reporting pathologist {36412573; 7992576; 24021880}.

Fibroadenoma and papilloma may show enlarged nuclei and prominent nucleoli {8291351; 11747216}, myxoid change in a fibroadenoma may be extensive enough to suggest mucinous carcinoma {7992576; 11747216}, phyllodes tumours may be associated with enlarged epithelial cells and ductal hyperplasia {15743740; 1311889; 33017520}, and high cellularity and exuberant ductal epithelial proliferations can be associated with radial scars / complex sclerosing lesions and intraductal papillomas {10458501}.

The ROM of the “Suspicious for malignancy” category in reported series is broad, ranging from 45.8% to 100% (median: 85.2%, mean: 85.2%) {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}.

The report should always state which features are suspicious, provide a differential diagnosis, and suggest the most likely lesion if at all possible, while recommending correlation with clinical and imaging findings {31112942; 31141809; 36412573}. But further biopsy is required, preferably by CNB or by excision biopsy, or in some situations repeat FNAB if CNB is not available. CNB provides assessment of invasion and material for prognostic and predictive markers for ER, PR, and HER2 (ERBB2). FNAB can provide material for marker testing utilizing ICC and FISH for HER2. If malignancy is confirmed on CNB, the patient is treated by the multidisciplinary team as having carcinoma. If the repeated sampling does not confirm malignancy, careful correlation with clinical and imaging findings and consideration of repeat sampling or excision biopsy is recommended.
