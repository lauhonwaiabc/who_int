8.0.0.4 Risk of malignancy and management recommendations

The ability to distinguish the “Atypical”, “Suspicious for malignancy”, and “Malignant” categories varies greatly among pathologists based on diagnostic certainty, experience, and the quality of the sample. The ROM of the “Suspicious for malignancy” category is broad ranging, from 45.8% to 100% (median: 85.2%, mean: 85.2%) {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}.

Additional tissue sampling is imperative for the patient with a breast FNAB categorized as “Suspicious for malignancy”. Repeat FNAB or, preferably where available, CNB is the preferred sampling modality in this setting, affording the ability to assess for invasion and perform breast biomarker analysis for ER, PR, and HER2 (ERBB2). If malignancy is confirmed on CNB, the patient is treated by the multidisciplinary team as having carcinoma. If the repeated sampling does not confirm malignancy, careful correlation with clinical and imaging findings and consideration of repeat sampling or excision biopsy is recommended.
