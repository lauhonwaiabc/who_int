8.0.0.1 Introduction

The “Suspicious for malignancy” category is used for breast FNAB when cytopathological features are present which suggest a malignant lesion, but the features are insufficient in number or specificity to permit a definitive diagnosis. There is likely to be a degree of interobserver variability in this category related to the cytopathologist’s experience and confidence, the actual material on the smears, and the nature of the lesion. Specimens in this category are generally from tumours that tend to yield hypocellular smears, such as invasive lobular carcinoma, or are from neoplasms with a continuum of low-risk to high-risk features, such as a phyllodes tumour or ductal carcinoma in situ. Atypical papillary lesions may show a spectrum of cytopathological appearances and some will fall into the suspicious category. Specific features of ductal and lobular carcinoma in situ will be addressed in section Chapter 7: Diagnostic category: Malignant, but often the features are insufficiently developed to allow a diagnosis of malignancy and will be most appropriately categorized as “Suspicious for malignancy”.

A suspicious categorization may also be rendered when the atypical cells show features suggestive of malignancy but the quality of the material for assessment is suboptimal, such as poorly preserved or stained smears, and precludes a confident diagnosis of malignancy. This may include samples in which there are scant atypical epithelial cells and a background of necrotic or mucinous material. Such specimens should not be categorized as “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic”. The category must be used in conjunction with the clinical history, physical examination, and imaging findings. The report should always state that “correlation with clinical and imaging findings is required” {31112942; 31141809; 36412573}.
