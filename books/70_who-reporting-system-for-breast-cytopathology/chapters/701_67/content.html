7.0.1 Sample reports: Atypical

Sample report 1

Specimen type:

FNAB one pass, 23 gauge needle

Clinical and imaging information

Female patient 47 years old, with history of right breast carcinoma. Discrete, palpable, round 15 mm right axillary tail mass.

Diagnostic summary

Site: Right axilla

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Atypical epithelial cells in a background of lymphoid cells. See comment.

Material received

One air-dried slide; no needle rinsings for cell block or flow cytometry

Microscopic description

Dispersed lymphoid population with scattered germinal-centre tissue fragments, and several small epithelial acinar tissue fragments lined by cells with bland, round nuclei and pale cytoplasm, with only very occasional stripped bare bipolar nuclei. A small amount of luminal secretion is identified. There is no dispersal of single epithelioid cells. No granulomas are seen and no material for a cell block is available.

Comment: A few small tissue fragments of bland epithelial cells are present in a lymphoid background, suggestive of a lymph node. The epithelial cells do not show cytopathological features of malignancy and are suggestive of epithelial inclusions. However, in the setting of a previous diagnosis of breast carcinoma, the findings are regarded as atypical, and correlation with repeat imaging is recommended and repeat FNAB or CNB is suggested.

Sample report 2

Specimen type

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information

Female patient 45 years old, 20 mm irregular mass right breast 11 o’clock, 40 mm from nipple, on mammography and ultrasound

Diagnostic summary

Site: Right, 11 o’clock, 40 mm from nipple

Diagnostic category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Atypical ductal epithelial cells. Please see comment.

Material received

LBC vial received

Microscopic description

The liquid-based preparation shows moderate cellularity consisting of predominantly cohesive tissue fragments of ductal epithelial cells with mild nuclear and architectural atypia. Some tissue fragments are large, crowded with mild nuclear enlargement and overlapping, and have foci suggestive of cribriform architecture, while others have associated myoepithelial cells. Occasional scattered single epithelial cells are present.

Comment: Low-grade atypia is present and the differential diagnosis includes usual epithelial hyperplasia, atypical ductal hyperplasia, and low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ. Correlation with the imaging features is required, and repeat FNAB or CNB is recommended.

Sample report 3

Specimen type

FNAB, two passes, 22 gauge needle

Clinical and imaging information

Female patient 70 years old, left breast 2 o’clock 50 mm from nipple, 7 mm mass, rounded margins on ultrasound

Diagnostic summary

Site: Left 2 o’clock 50 mm from nipple

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Rare atypical epithelial cells present in a background of abundant mucin. Please see comment.

Material received

Four air-dried smears, one alcohol-fixed smear

Microscopic description

Scattered epithelial cells with mild nuclear pleomorphism, arranged in small tissue fragments and dispersed singly in a background of abundant mucin.

Comment: The evaluation is limited by scant cellularity consisting of atypical epithelial cells in a mucinous background raising a differential diagnosis of a mucocoele-like lesion or possibly mucinous carcinoma. Correlation with imaging is required, and repeat FNAB or CNB is recommended.

Sample report 4

Type of specimen

FNAB

Clinical and imaging information

Female patient 65 years old, 25 mm palpable mass, right breast 7 o’clock, 30 mm from nipple, with vague features on mammography; ultrasound shows a rounded lesion

Diagnostic summary

Site: Right 7 o’clock 30 mm from nipple

Category: Atypical

Diagnosis: Scant mildly atypical spindle cell. Please see comment.

Material received

Two air-dried slides and 1 mL fluid for cell block

Microscopic description

Mildly cellular smears show a spindle cell proliferation in scattered stromal fragments. The spindle cells show mild nuclear pleomorphism. Neither necrosis nor increased mitotic activity are present. The background shows scattered lymphocytes and rare small tissue fragments of ductal epithelial cells. There is no material on the direct smears.

Comment: Evaluation is limited by scant cellularity and limited material in the cell block for immunocytochemistry. The differential diagnosis includes fibromatosis, myofibroblastoma, pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia, and fibroadenoma, and possibly benign phyllodes tumour. Correlation with imaging is required and repeat FNAB with cell block or CNB is recommended.
