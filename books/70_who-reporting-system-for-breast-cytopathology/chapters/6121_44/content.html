6.1.2.1 Hamartoma

Definition

A hamartoma is a circumscribed mass composed of normal breast tissue components.

Clinical features and imaging

Breast hamartoma arises most often in premenopausal women, with an average age of onset ranging between 33.5 and 66.5 years {25141109}. It occurs as a variably sized palpable mass, which is soft to firm in consistency, mobile, and well circumscribed. Some lesions are reported to grow rapidly, leading to breast deformity and asymmetry {34403159}. Patients with Cowden syndrome may present with multiple, bilateral hamartomas of the breast {30503250}.

Hamartoma appears as a distinct round or oval mass with circumscribed margins and variable opacity on mammography and is generally considered as probably benign. On ultrasound, it shows heterogeneous echogenicity in most cases and is sometimes hypoechoic and rarely isoechoic, and Doppler studies frequently show vascularity. The elasticity is similar to that of peripheral parenchyma on shear wave elastography {31436244; 34403159}.

Histopathology

Hamartoma is composed of normal breast tissue including a stromal component with a variable amount of mature adipose tissue, acellular collagen, and areas of pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia. The epithelial component is made up of ducts and lobules randomly scattered throughout the stromal components. Epithelial alterations such as columnar cell change, adenosis, epithelial hyperplasia, and cysts are frequently present {12461066; 34403159}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Low cellularity Sheets and tissue fragments of uniform ductal epithelial cells with small, uniform nuclei, no nucleoli, and evenly distributed fine chromatin, and myoepithelial cells on the sheets Small numbers of stripped oval myoepithelial nuclei (bare bipolar nuclei) in the background Hypocellular stromal fragments and adipose tissue Intact benign lobules

References: {12873312; 31343114}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The cytopathological features of hamartoma are the components of normal breast. There is considerable overlap with other benign lesions, and correlation with clinical and specific imaging features is essential to make the diagnosis {31343114}. Fibroadenoma has a very similar clinical and imaging presentation and cannot be distinguished reliably by FNAB alone, although intact lobular units and hypocellular sclerotic stroma have been suggested as diagnostic clues favouring a diagnosis of hamartoma over fibroadenoma {16604552}.

Ancillary testing

Ancillary testing has no role in the diagnosis of hamartomas.
