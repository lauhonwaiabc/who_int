9.1.1.9 Invasive micropapillary carcinoma

Definition

Invasive micropapillary carcinoma (IMPC) of the breast is an invasive carcinoma composed of small, hollow, or morula-like tissue fragments of malignant cells with an inside-out growth pattern lacking fibrovascular cores.

Clinical features and imaging

IMPC presents as a firm, palpable, commonly solitary mass with poorly defined margins, similar to other invasive breast carcinomas, ranging from 10 to 100 mm {11431712; 15198358}. Mammographically, it appears as a high-density, round to irregular mass with spiculated to indistinct margins with or without associated microcalcifications or as an architectural distortion {19542384; 12239039}. On ultrasound, it commonly appears as a homogeneously hypoechoic, irregular, or microlobulated mass {19542384; 12239039}. On MRI, it appears as irregular spiculated multifocal masses with washout patterns {22493629; 19542384}. Patients with IMPC are reported to show higher clinical stages and histopathological grades, higher incidences of lymphovascular invasion and axillary lymph node extracapsular extension, and a higher degree of lymph node involvement than patients with invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (NST) {28418916; 23763700; 35310681; 29228910}, with two thirds presenting with axillary nodal metastases at diagnosis {11431712; 15198358; 28418916; 11557778; 23763700; 35310681}.

Histopathology

IMPC is characterized by small papillary fragments and rounded nests that lack definite fibrovascular cores within clear spaces separated by dense fibrocollagenous or delicate fibrovascular stroma, giving a honeycomb or sponge impression {11127740; 15198358}. The papilliform epithelial fragments show inside-out or reverse polarity, in which the cells are aligned with the basally located nuclei in the centre and the apical cytoplasm towards the periphery of the fragments {11127740}. This is in contrast to the usual glandular architecture in which the apical cytoplasm points towards the central lumen. The clear space mimics lymphatic invasion or artefactual tissue retraction, but lacks an endothelial lining. The carcinoma cells are cuboidal to columnar, with finely granular or dense eosinophilic cytoplasm and often distinctive cell margins, and nuclei are intermediate- or usually high-grade and pleomorphic with prominent nucleoli {15198358}. Mitotic figures are frequent, apocrine features may be present, and most IMPCs are Nottingham grade 2 or usually 3 {15198358; 11431712}.

Pure IMPC is rare and accounts for only 0.7–3% of all breast carcinomas and must show > 90% micropapillary morphology [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int/581.]] {11557778; 15198358}. Mixed forms are more frequent with the dominant component usually being invasive breast carcinoma NST {11431712; 11557778; 19479727}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Mucinous carcinoma may have foci with a micropapillary pattern reminiscent of invasive micropapillary carcinoma and this can be associated with high nuclear grade, when the carcinoma is best regarded as invasive breast carcinoma NST {12378595; 20962055; 23517923}.

IMPC should be differentiated from other papillary neoplasms including solid papillary carcinoma, both in situ and infiltrative, and metastatic papillary carcinomas [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int/581.]] {15198358}.

Solid papillary carcinoma shows high cellularity and discohesive crowded tissue fragments varying from small to large, with many single intact cells, and round to oval nuclei, finely granular chromatin, and mild to moderate nuclear pleomorphism {17580353}. Tissue fragments of thin branching and anastomosing vessels with rounded bulbous capillary loop ends are usually present. The intermediate to high nuclear grade features of IMPC are not present. Solid papillary carcinomas may have endocrine differentiation and this is associated with discohesive epithelial tissue fragments and abundant single cells with nuclei showing fine granular chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli, and eccentric variably granular cytoplasm {17580353}.

Metastatic ovarian papillary serous carcinoma to the breast usually shows large crowded cells in papilliform tissue fragments with marked nuclear enlargement and atypia and prominent nucleoli {10527477; 25745286; 18306588}. Psammomatous microcalcifications from ovarian carcinoma may be similar to those seen in IMPC.

Ancillary testing

Most IMPCs are ER-positive and PR-positive {28418916; 23763700; 19479727; 18484683} with a triple-negative phenotype in 15–20% of cases {29228910; 24921921; 26200840}. ERBB2 (HER2) amplification may be detected in some cases {19479727; 20444748; 23763700}. Expression of basal antigens is uncommon {18484683}. Metastatic papillary carcinomas to the breast will often have distinctive ICC; for example, metastatic ovarian carcinomas will be PAX8-positive.
