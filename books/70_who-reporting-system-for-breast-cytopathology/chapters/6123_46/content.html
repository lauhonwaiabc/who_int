6.1.2.3 Columnar cell change and flat epithelial atypia

Definition

Columnar cell lesions (CCLs) are clonal alterations of the terminal duct lobular unit characterized by enlarged, variably dilated terminal ductules lined by columnar epithelial cells. Flat epithelial atypia (FEA) is a flat proliferation of at least two layers of epithelial cells with low-grade monomorphic cytopathological atypia and no other intraluminal architecture.

Clinical features and imaging

CCLs are most often detected because of the presence of clustered, luminal amorphous or fine pleomorphic microcalcifications on mammography {18180237}. Less frequently they are incidental microscopic findings adjacent to or intermingled with other lesions, both benign epithelial proliferations and the spectrum leading to low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ.

Histopathology

The histopathology presents a morphological continuum of changes from enlarged variably dilated terminal ductules lined by a single layer of columnar epithelial cells often with apical cytoplasmic snouting to FEA, where there is replacement of the native epithelial cells by at least two up to several layers of a single epithelial cell type showing low-grade monomorphic cytopathological atypia {12717115; 17182657}. There is a high frequency of coexistence with lobular neoplasia, low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ, and invasive low-grade carcinomas {17325484}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

CCLs and FEA present mammographically as grouped microcalcifications, and not as palpable lesions in the breast. They are usually diagnosed in stereotactic biopsies and are rarely the primary target of FNAB. However, CCLs and FEA are commonly found in association and concurrent with other lesions, including a spectrum of changes from epithelial hyperplasia through to atypical ductal hyperplasia, which is not a diagnosis made in cytopathology, low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ, and invasive carcinomas {17325484}. The main clinical risk is a concomitant low-grade invasive carcinoma or carcinoma in situ. The long-term risk of a later occurring invasive carcinoma or carcinoma in situ is very low [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]].

CCLs and FEA are rarely seen in cytopathological material as a specific diagnosis, and the features are often intermingled with those of epithelial hyperplasia, apocrine metaplasia, and cystic change {17230565; 15540173}. The features described mirror the histopathological findings of a spectrum of changes found in histopathology. The architectural and cellular as well as nuclear findings range from benign to low-grade atypia. The majority of cases are diagnosed as atypical, indicating that the cytopathological findings do not meet the criteria of epithelial hyperplasia {17230565; 15540173}, with some categorized as suspicious for malignancy and carcinoma, indicating that there is a risk of overdiagnosis.

The main benign differential diagnoses include proliferative lesions as fibrocystic changes, ductal epithelial hyperplasia, adenosis, and fibroadenoma {17230565; 15540173}, and the cytopathological findings of these benign lesions and CCLs and FEA may be mixed, reflecting the histopathology.

Ancillary testing

The ICC of CCLs is diffuse, strong ER and PR positivity, and positivity for low-molecular-weight cytokeratins including CK8, CK18, and CK19. BCL2 shows strong cytoplasmic positivity and E-cadherin is membrane positive. High-molecular-weight cytokeratins CK5/6 and 34βE12 are negative {10971699; 11114859; 11350726; 10526005}.
