9.1.3.2 Liposarcoma

Definition

Liposarcoma is a family of soft tissue neoplasms with lipogenic differentiation and varying biological behaviour, ranging from locally aggressive to metastasizing, with the subtypes atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma (ALT/WDLPS), dedifferentiated liposarcoma, myxoid liposarcoma (MLPS), pleomorphic liposarcoma, and myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma.

Clinical features and imaging

Primary breast liposarcoma presents across a wide age range in adults, in both women and men, including all subtypes {3019868}. Patients typically present with palpable, slowly enlarging masses. Otherwise, these tumours are detected by screening mammography {21931026}. Imaging features are nonspecific and mimic other common lesions such as a fibroadenoma {19292812; 21931026}.

Histopathology

ALT/WDLPS is composed of relatively mature adipocytes separated by irregular fibrous septa containing hyperchromatic stromal cells. Lipoblasts may be present but are not an essential criteria for the diagnosis. Some cases of ALT/WDLPS are predominantly composed of fat, whereas others are more fibrotic.

Dedifferentiated liposarcoma displays an abrupt or gradual transition from ALT/WDLPS to a wide range of morphological patterns, including undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. Additionally, there may be heterologous elements, including myogenic, osteosarcomatous, chondrosarcomatous {23032820; 29372852; 35074968}, and/or homologous pleomorphic liposarcomatous differentiation {20588177}.

MLPS is composed of small, round to ovoid cells with minimal atypia embedded in a myxoid matrix with distinctive capillary-sized arborizing vasculature, reminiscent of a chicken-wire appearance. Scattered univacuolated lipoblasts may be seen. A round cell component, indicative of a higher grade, consists of densely packed primitive cells with hyperchromatic nuclei, a high N:C ratio, and no myxoid stroma. When present, the round cell component should be carefully quantified, because MLPS with a > 5% round cell component is associated with aggressive biological behaviour and poor prognosis {16926564}. It is noteworthy that MLPS of the soft tissues has a proclivity for metastasis to unusual sites, including the breast. Therefore, metastasis should be carefully excluded before making a diagnosis of primary MLPS of the breast {23826427; 24994066}.

Pleomorphic liposarcoma is a high-grade pleomorphic sarcoma with varying admixtures of lipogenic and non-lipogenic areas, including pleomorphic sarcomatous, round, epithelioid, and spindle cell components. S100 expression is reported in pleomorphic liposarcoma {11979090; 15371941}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma
Tissue fragments of variably sized adipocytes Variable nuclear atypia within the adipocytes, ranging from slight hyperchromasia to pronounced pleomorphism and multinucleation Floret cells may be present, with a central circular arrangement of nuclei and outer cytoplasm
Dedifferentiated liposarcoma
Variable cellularity and cytomorphological patterns Tissue fragments of fat with variable nuclear atypia Tissue fragments of spindle, polygonal, and pleomorphic forms exhibiting a variable degree of nuclear pleomorphism Singly dispersed spindle cells against a myxoid background
Myxoid liposarcoma
Variable cellularity Tissue fragments of abundant myxoid stroma Delicate, branching, capillary-sized blood vessels organized in a plexiform pattern A variable number of lipoblasts with an eccentric well-defined cytoplasmic vacuole and a nucleus that may be impinged by the vacuole; multiple vacuoles may be present Small, relatively monomorphic oval to spindle cells with irregular nuclear membranes and prominent nucleoli A variable number of round cells with high N:C ratios, mostly singly dispersed In cases of round cell liposarcoma, which is indicative of high-grade morphological features, the myxoid stroma may be scant with fewer lipoblasts
Pleomorphic liposarcoma
Moderate cellularity Discohesive tissue fragments and scattered cells displaying marked pleomorphism Stripped nuclei A variable number of lipoblasts and cells with fibrillary cytoplasm and microvesicles Epithelioid and spindle cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and irregular nuclear membranes Inflammatory cells, mitotic figures, lipoproteinaceous background, and necrosis Rarely, myxoid background and branching blood vessels

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Primary liposarcomas of the breast are extremely rare and most cases of liposarcomatous differentiation constitute malignant phyllodes tumour with heterologous liposarcomatous foci {33378115; 11843559; 9568143; 10820530}. Liposarcoma of the breast can be mistaken for a lipoma or fibroadenoma, in view of the presence of adipocytic tissue fragments and benign ductal epithelial cells {18677756}. A careful assessment of cytomorphological features, which depend on the type of liposarcoma, and application of ancillary techniques is useful in differentiating a specific subtype of a liposarcoma from its diagnostic mimics.

Ancillary testing

In cases of ALT/WDLPS, MDM2 (IF2 clone) and CDK4 show diffuse, nuclear staining {35074968}. MDM2 positivity may be seen in macrophages, as seen in organizing fat necrosis, depending upon the availability of certain clones, which constitutes a diagnostic pitfall, and MDM2 amplification by FISH is ideal for a diagnostic confirmation {24227706}. Malignant phyllodes tumour of the breast with liposarcomatous dedifferentiation lacks MDM2 and CDK4 amplification, unlike a dedifferentiated liposarcoma {26542423}.

MLPS is characterized by a balanced translocation involving DDIT3 on chromosome 12 with either FUS on chromosome 16 or less frequently EWSR1 on chromosome 22. Detection of a fusion transcript involving the DDIT3 gene locus by FISH break-apart probe is a sensitive and specific test for MLPS {22089349}.

ICC is useful in distinguishing the round cell type of high-grade MLPS from other small round cell tumours. Additionally, ICC nuclear positivity for DDIT3 is a sensitive and specific marker for MLPS lesions, including those with a round cell component {32815829; 33731886}.
