10.1.1 Category: Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic

Definition: A specimen categorized as “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” lacks sufficient material in quantity or quality for a reliable diagnosis.

An “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” FNAB is unable to provide diagnostic information regarding the targeted lesion. The preferred terminology for such unsatisfactory samples may differ among institutions so three options are provided to allow a familiar term for the clinical team. Each laboratory should pick one term and use it consistently for all such reports, and liaise closely with their clinical team regarding this decision. Regardless of the term used, the reason for the classification of FNAB material as “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” should always be stated in the final report. If any atypical cytopathological features are identified, such as nuclear or architectural atypia, necrosis without evidence of fat necrosis, acellular mucin, or scant, dispersed small cells with mild nuclear atypia and eccentric cytoplasm and vacuoles, the biopsy material should be considered “Atypical”, rather than “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” {25678304; 31112942}.

Determination of the adequacy or inadequacy of a FNAB incorporates assessment of the quality and quantity of cellular material relative to the clinical and imaging impressions of the targeted lesion, in the triple test, which influences the criteria for adequacy: for a mass or suspicious lesion, a minimum number of seven well-seen tissue fragments of epithelial cells, each of at least 20 cells to allow assessment for the presence or absence of myoepithelial cells, is required for adequacy {9100536; 9477100; 9208973; 9915135; 31112942}. In the appropriate clinical and imaging setting, there is no minimum epithelial cellularity requirement for cysts with their histiocytes and multinucleated histiocytes in a proteinaceous background, for mastitis/abscess with often only pus, and for intramammary lymph nodes with their polymorphous lymphoid material in which small lymphocytes predominate.

Technical factors can lead to an “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” categorization, including poor cell preservation related to air-drying artefact of alcohol-fixed smears or slow-air-drying artefact of air-dried smears, poor smearing technique, obscuring blood or ultrasound gel, and the intrinsic properties of the targeted lesion, in particular the degree of fibrosis and sclerosis and the inherent cellularity. Contributing factors also relate to the experience of the FNAB operator and the FNAB smear-maker.

A FNAB showing features that do not correlate with the imaging impression does not constitute an insufficient sample, and if ample breast ductal epithelial cells with myoepithelial cells are obtained from a patient with a suspicious mass on clinical and/or imaging findings, the sample should be categorized as “Benign”, with a comment in the cytopathology report stating that “clinical and imaging correlation is recommended to ensure that this sample represents the targeted lesion”, rather than as “Non-diagnostic”. The lesion may in fact be benign and reflect a discordant imaging interpretation; for example, the imaging of a radial scar can mimic a stellate carcinoma.

Since the publication of the International Academy of Cytology Yokohama System for Reporting Breast Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy Cytopathology (IAC System) [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]], studies have reported a wide range in the use of the “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” category, from 0.3% to 22.6% of cases (median: 4.3%, mean: 5.9%), reflecting the variations in the surveyed practice, varying from a primary care facility to a specialist referral centre, and the variations in the patient cohort as symptomatic or screening, and the indications for FNAB {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}. Similarly the reported ROM has ranged widely from 0% to 60.9% (median: 14.8%, mean: 21.2%) {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}. The IAC System based its ROM on two studies, one in which cytopathologists performed the FNAB with ultrasound direction, and one where cytopathologists provided ROSE on specimens procured by experienced breast physicians using ultrasound or experienced radiologists using ultrasound provided the specimens. These two scenarios achieved a ROM for this category ranging from 2.6% to 4.8%, and represent examples of modern best practice in diagnostic breast clinics {31108486; 30783035}.

A breast FNAB categorized as “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” should prompt a review of the clinical and imaging findings to determine whether a repeat FNAB or CNB should be performed: if the clinical and imaging level of suspicion is indeterminate or high, repeat FNAB or CNB is considered mandatory ideally with ROSE, while if the clinical and imaging suspicion is low, repeat clinical and imaging evaluation in 3–6 months can be suggested, with repeat FNAB immediately if any changes are noted during this period.
