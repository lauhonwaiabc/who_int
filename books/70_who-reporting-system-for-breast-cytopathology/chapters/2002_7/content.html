2.0.0.2 The role of breast cytopathology

Determining the prevalence of breast disease, particularly breast masses, poses a challenge. However, symptoms such as palpable breast masses, mastalgia, and nipple discharge are frequently reported to health care professionals by individuals seeking medical attention. Benign breast disease is 10 times more common than breast malignancy in most settings {30990294; 27866091; 35168773}.

The breast is highly accessible to biopsy sampling, including FNAB, and this includes palpable masses, which may be sampled by palpation alone or utilizing imaging guidance, which is a huge advantage in low- and middle-income countries where image guidance may not be available. Non-palpable masses are readily sampled under ultrasound or stereotactic guidance. There is a longstanding history and widespread acceptance, by both patients and clinical staff, of breast FNAB as a diagnostic modality. FNAB allows a rapid and accurate diagnosis without the need for anaesthesia or hospitalization, and there is a very low rate of complications such as bleeding, infection, and scarring.

The sensitivity of breast FNAB is 90–99% with a positive predictive value approaching 100% and a very low false positive rate [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]]. Indications for breast FNAB include the diagnosis and drainage of cysts, the diagnosis of palpable and impalpable mass lesions, and the sampling and diagnosis of inflammatory conditions, with the ability to obtain material for microbiological evaluation. In patients who are pregnant or lactating, FNAB can be used so as to avoid sinus formation, and can be used similarly in patients with bleeding disorders. FNAB facilitates the performance of ROSE for immediate diagnosis and appropriate triage of material [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]].

Despite the utility of breast FNAB, in some countries CNB is the procedure of choice in women with a breast lesion, either palpable or non-palpable, particularly in established mammographic screening programmes where the target lesion is frequently calcifications or a small lesion. CNB is indicated for the workup of calcifications and more-diffuse lesions within the breast. CNB does offer better diagnostic ability in certain proliferative lesions and low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ. The specificity of FNAB and CNB is similar. Interestingly for non-palpable image-guided breast masses, FNAB is more specific than CNB {27866091} [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]]. CNB is more invasive and costly and has more complications, including significant and on occasion massive haemorrhage {28837268}. The choice between FNAB and CNB depends on the prevailing practice in a particular clinical milieu and on the characteristics of the breast lesion.

Another cytopathological modality in the evaluation of breast lesions is nipple discharge. Approximately 5–10% of patients at a breast clinic present with nipple discharge, which can be physiological, such as lactation, or pathological. Indeed, nipple discharge may be the only presenting symptom in a patient with breast cancer. Nipple cytopathology is a non-invasive diagnostic modality with easy collection of material for microscopic evaluation, and has a sensitivity of 75% and 62% and specificity of 87% and 71% for benign and malignant breast disease, respectively {34839426}.

Axillary lymph node FNAB is a useful diagnostic test especially in patients with breast cancer to exclude or confirm metastases and guide further management decisions. Ultrasound-guided axillary FNAB may spare patients sentinel node biopsy, allowing surgeons to move directly to axillary clearance if metastatic carcinoma in a lymph node has been demonstrated by FNAB. Meta-analyses of FNAB in this regard show a sensitivity of 63% and specificity of 99% [[Raymond WA, Kleinig P. The value of fine needle aspiration biopsy in the pre‑operative assessment of the axilla in breast cancer patients. J Mol Pathol. 2022 Oct 20;3(4):228–242. doi:10.3390/jmp3040020.]] {25496004}.

The cytopathological findings should always be correlated with the clinical and imaging features wherever possible in the triple test, which provides a definitive diagnosis in most patients with breast lesions and enables a coordinated management approach. ROSE performed at the time of FNAB will reduce the rate of inadequate diagnoses and facilitate triage of additional material and selection of ancillary procedures [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]].
