5.0.0.4 Risk of malignancy and management recommendations

Since the publication of the International Academy of Cytology Yokohama System for Reporting Breast Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy Cytopathology (IAC System) [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]], studies have reported a wide range in the use of the “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” category, from 0.3% to 22.6% of cases (median: 4.3%, mean: 5.9%) {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}. Cases may be rendered “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” owing to numerous factors, including the experience of the operator, skill of the slide-maker, and degree of sclerosis or fibrosis inherent in the targeted lesion. The rate reported will also reflect the surveyed practice, which can vary from primary care facility to specialist referral centre; the patient cohort as symptomatic or screened; and the indications for FNAB. Therefore, the reported ROM for this category shows a wide distribution, ranging from 0% to 60.9% (median: 14.8%, mean: 21.2%) {30783035; 31524134; 33017520; 30929288; 32749785; 31108486; 33755356; 34133084; 34029453; 33629823; 34321772; 34866246; 34876918; 34515039; 34535580; 34703093; 36516743; 36988122}. The IAC System based its ROM on two studies, one in which cytopathologists performed the FNAB with ultrasound guidance, and one in which cytopathologists provided ROSE on specimens procured by experienced breast physicians using ultrasound or experienced radiologists using ultrasound provided the specimens. These scenarios represent examples of modern best practice in diagnostic breast clinics and achieved a ROM of 2.6% to 4.8% {3078035; 31108486}.

A breast FNAB classified as “Insufficient / Inadequate / Non-diagnostic” should prompt a review of the clinical and imaging findings to determine whether a repeat FNAB or CNB should be performed. If the clinical and imaging suspicion for malignancy of the targeted lesion is indeterminate or high, additional sampling by FNAB or CNB is considered mandatory. If the clinical and imaging suspicion for malignancy is low, patients may return for repeat clinical and imaging evaluation in 3–6 months, with repeat FNAB if any changes are noted.

In determining management, consideration should be given as to the cause of the insufficient sample, for example, preparation artefact or poor technique for the FNAB or smear preparation.
