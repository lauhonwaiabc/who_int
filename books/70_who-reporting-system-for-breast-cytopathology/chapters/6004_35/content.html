6.0.0.4 Risk of malignancy and management recommendations

The ROM for a lesion categorized as “Benign” has been reported as ranging from 0% to 11.7% in a series of articles utilizing the International Academy of Cytology (IAC) Yokohama System for Reporting Breast Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy Cytopathology, although some series have reported narrower ranges from 0.6% to 5% [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]] {34866246; 36370120; 33333512; 36370120; 34866246}, and some even lower at 1.4–2.3% {31108486; 30783035}. A recent meta-analysis pooling 27 studies over 10 years showed a 4.7% ROM for the “Benign” category based on the IAC Yokohama system {31141809}. Another study reported a ROM as high as 15.3%, with no clear explanation, but the authors suggested incorporating ROSE as a way of reducing the ROM for benign lesions [[Nargund A, Mohan RH, Pai MM, et al. Demystifying breast FNACs based on the international academy of cytology yokohama breast cytopathology system: a retrospective study. J Clin Diagn Res. 2021;15(3):EC01–EC05. doi:10.7860/JCDR/2021/45366/14606.]]. The negative predictive value of benign breast FNAB is > 97% [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]].

A recent meta-analysis of nipple discharge cytopathology reported a sensitivity of 75% and specificity of 87% for benign breast disease, bearing in mind that nipple discharge cytopathology is frequently acellular in both benign and malignant breast disease, limiting diagnostic utility. Other diagnostic modalities such as ultrasound, mammography, MRI, and galactography performed no better in patients with nipple discharge {34839426}.

Lesions that appear benign on all three arms of the triple test may be followed up with sequential physical and imaging examination at intervals of 3–6 months or longer, depending on available resources. A repeat FNAB is undertaken immediately should any alteration in the clinical or imaging findings be noted. If the clinical and imaging findings are worrisome or indeterminate, atypical, or suspicious, and the cytopathological findings are benign, the patient should undergo further investigation, which can include repeat FNAB, CNB, or on rare occasions simple excision [[Field AS, Raymond WA, Schmitt F, editors. The International Academy of Cytology Yokohama system for reporting breast fine needle aspiration biopsy cytopathology. 1st ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2020.]] {32044283}.
