9.1.1.12 Salivary gland–type carcinomas (including acinic cell–like carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, and mucoepidermoid carcinoma)

Definition

Acinic cell–like carcinoma is an invasive carcinoma composed of clear and granular epithelial cells, some of which contain intracytoplasmic zymogen granules, with various architectural patterns ranging from microglandular to solid.

Adenoid cystic carcinoma (AdCC) is a triple-negative breast cancer, which has similar morphological, immunophenotypical, and molecular features to its salivary gland counterpart, and the commonest of its three subtypes, classic AdCC, is composed of neoplastic epithelial and myoepithelial cells arranged in tubular, cribriform, and solid patterns associated with a basophilic matrix and reduplicated basement membrane material, frequently with MYB::NFIB fusion. Solid-basaloid AdCC and AdCC with high-grade transformation also occur.

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) is an invasive carcinoma composed of mixed mucinous, intermediate/basaloid, and squamoid/epidermoid neoplastic cells arranged in solid and cystic patterns.

Clinical features and imaging

Acinic cell–like carcinoma, as described in the sixth-edition Breast tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series, is a very rare low-grade, triple-negative breast carcinoma, and its incidence awaits further elucidation. Acinic cell carcinoma, however, has 68 reported cases {28043647; 36332545} with a similar clinical presentation to invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (IBC-NST) {28043647; 36332545} with one associated with a BRCA1 mutation carrier {23374397}, and is presented here, including photomicrographs (see #39660Fig. 7.XXX, #39661Fig. 7.XXX, #39662Fig. 7.XXX, and #39667Fig. 7.XXX). The axillary lymph node metastasis rate is low, but local recurrences and distant metastases to bone, lungs, liver, and peritoneum have been reported {21163678; 15569060; 12101208; 35003741}.

AdCC is a rare special type of IBC accounting for 0.1–3.5% of all breast tumours {22154460; 23456318; 20653964; 30189404}. Tumour size ranges from 1 to 160 mm, with a mean of 21 mm {20653964}. The majority of the tumours are grossly well demarcated {9591727; 12095593} and unifocal {20653964; 30189404}, with multifocal cases reported {20190690; 36357304}. On mammogram, AdCC can appear as a smooth round to oval circumscribed mass, as an irregular spiculated mass, or as an asymmetrical density {9843312; 36357304}. Ultrasound shows an irregular, heterogeneous, or hypoechoic mass with minimal vascularity {20410430}. It can appear similar to a benign lesion on mammogram, ultrasound, or MRI {36357304}.

Breast MEC is a rare malignant breast neoplasm with only limited cases reported {33425121}. It affects adult women aged 27–86 years old, with tumours ranging from 5 to 110 mm (average: 34 mm) {22135628; 33425121}, and presents as a solid or cystic nodule that can be well circumscribed or poorly defined. When located in a retroareolar area, it can present with nipple discharge {28043647}. The tumour has no specific radiographic appearance. On mammography, some show a well-defined mass with a partly lobulated border, and ultrasound shows a complex septated cystic mass with a mural nodule with increased vascularity {35787204; 35169478; 35777033}. Axillary and distant metastases have been reported but in lesser frequency than those of IBC-NST {22135628; 33425121} and are mostly seen in patients with high-grade MEC {22135628; 33425121}.

Histopathology

Acinic cell–like carcinoma shows a variety of architectural patterns ranging from microglandular, similar to that in microglandular adenosis, to solid nests with necrosis, and the patterns are often mixed in the one tumour {10963383; 12101208; 28043647} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]]. A variable number of the polygonal tumour cells have abundant, eosinophilic to basophilic granular cytoplasm, which on DPAS has eosinophilic granules varying from fine to coarse; in some cases these cells resemble Paneth cells {21163678}. Other tumour cells may have clear cytoplasm. Round central nuclei are mildly atypical with prominent nucleoli, and mitoses can be seen in the solid tumour areas {10963383; 8865856; 27067778; 35003741; 21847414}. There can be colloid-like eosinophilic material in the lumen {27067778}. Acinic cell–like carcinoma is often mixed with IBC-NST and metaplastic carcinoma {26011570; 25688711} or high-grade ductal carcinoma in situ {21163678; 27067778}. ICC is positive for serous differentiation markers, such as lysozyme and α1-antichymotrypsin, as well as EMA, S100, CK7, and occasionally CK5/6, and GCDFP-15 and GATA3 focally. Acinic cell–like carcinoma is usually triple negative for ER, PR, and HER2 {27067778; 36332545; 20306833}.

There are three subtypes of AdCC based on architecture and cytopathological features: classic AdCC, solid-basaloid AdCC, and AdCC with high-grade transformation {28043647}. Classic AdCC shows a central cribriform area with surrounding predominantly tubular areas, with luminal-type epithelial cells lining the glandular spaces showing Alcian blue–positive mucins, surrounded by myoepithelial cells, which can also form pseudolumina filled with myxoid, mucinous, or hyalinized stromal matrix and basement membrane material {25516849; 9591727; 30189404}. The luminal cells have round nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm and the myoepithelial cells have central hyperchromatic oval nuclei and scant pale or clear cytoplasm {25516849; 37208239; 20306833}. Sebaceous differentiation, squamous differentiation, and focal necrosis can be seen {3022669; 9591727; 12095593}. The luminal epithelial cells are positive for CK7, CK8/18, EMA, and KIT (CD117) {20203221; 28043647}. The basal cells are positive for CK14, CK17, CK5/6, myoepithelial markers (p63, S100, calponin, actin), and EGFR {20203221; 15846389; 25516849}. The stromal cells are positive for collagen IV and laminin {28043647}. The differential diagnosis of classic AdCC includes collagenous spherulosis {16810311}, microglandular adenosis {12883237}, tubular carcinoma, and invasive cribriform carcinoma {15846389}.

The solid-basaloid subtype shows > 90% solid trabecular, “geographical”, and rounded nests of basaloid cells with marked nuclear atypia, a high mitotic count, necrosis, and frequent perineural invasion {11914618}. Well to poorly formed intercalated ducts are embedded in the solid areas, which are surrounded by typically myxoid or hyalinized stroma {11914618}. The differential diagnosis focuses on basal carcinomas. AdCC showing highly malignant transformation of any of its components is designated AdCC with high-grade transformation. The transformations reported include spindle cell carcinoma with melanoma {18283489}, small cell carcinoma {16325513}, IBC-NST {19233861}, and malignant adenomyoepithelioma {25056281}.

AdCC is often negative for ER, PR, and HER2 {20306833; 20653964} with some ER and PR positivity reported in some classic-type AdCC {20653964}. MYB expression is often observed in most cases, which is helpful in differentiating these tumours from other triple-negative breast carcinomas {34599282}.

Breast MEC is composed of an admixture of varying proportions of basaloid, intermediate, epidermoid, and mucinous cells arranged in cystic and solid configurations {28043647}. The oval basaloid cells have small round central nuclei with coarse chromatin {14634807}, while intermediate cells are larger with round central nuclei with small nucleoli and more abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm {14634807}. Epidermoid cells are polygonal and twice the size of intermediate cells, have central nuclei and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm with well-defined cytoplasmic borders, and are rarely keratinized {14634807; 4029947; 36916425}. Mucinous cells are relatively large cells with eccentric pale cytoplasm filled with sialomucin {22135628; 20306833}. Basaloid cells are mostly located at the periphery of neoplastic cysts and solid nests, gradually merging with the central solid area made up of intermediate and epidermoid cells {20306833}. Low-grade MEC mostly forms cystic spaces lined by mucinous cells, which may constitute > 50% of the tumour {20306833; 33425121}. High-grade MEC shows solid nests with fewer mucinous cells and predominantly epidermoid and intermediate cells {20306833}, with nuclear atypia, mitoses, necrosis, and lymphovascular or neural infiltration {24166608; 14634807}.

Polymorphous adenocarcinoma has been cut from the sixth-edition Breast tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Breast salivary gland–like tumours are often triple negative. They can have different compositions, such as having purely myoepithelial components, mixed epithelial-myoepithelial components, or purely epithelial components. Pure myoepithelial tumours include benign myoepithelioma and malignant myoepithelioma. The complex mixed epithelial-myoepithelial component tumours can be benign (myoepithelioma, pleomorphic adenoma), low-grade malignant (adenomyoepithelioma, AdCC), or high-grade malignant (malignant myoepithelioma). The purely epithelial component includes MEC, secretory carcinoma, and acinic cell–like carcinoma {12835294; 35862856; 28043647}. Unlike other triple-negative breast cancers, these tumours usually have an indolent course {28721389}.

Acinic cell–like carcinoma is described in the sixth-edition Breast tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series as a carcinoma related to microglandular adenosis, and is an extremely rare breast malignancy that has not yet been described and defined clearly in the cytopathology literature. A single case of acinic cell carcinoma has been described previously {22734799}. Possible differential diagnoses include carcinoma with apocrine differentiation, which is usually triple negative. Acinic cell–like carcinoma has a molecular profile similar to those of other triple-negative breast cancers and carcinomas arising in microglandular adenosis, and unlike salivary gland acinic-cell carcinoma, it does not show t(4;9)(q13;q31) rearrangement and NR4A3 protein overexpression. It is included in this section at this time of developing concepts.

AdCC should be differentiated from adenomyoepithelioma, collagenous spherulosis, invasive cribriform carcinoma, and IBC-NST. Adenomyoepithelioma shows large biphasic cellular tissue fragments with a dual population of epithelial and myoepithelial cells associated with numerous naked nuclei, but dense and balled-up magenta (Giemsa) stroma is not seen. Collagenous spherulosis shows fewer, smaller rounded magenta (Giemsa) spherules in ductal epithelial tissue fragments with myoepithelial cells and bare bipolar nuclei, in a background of epithelial hyperplasia or fibrocystic change.

High-grade primary MEC should be differentiated from high-grade IBC-NST, by the presence of squamoid cells with denser cytoplasm without actual keratinization. Low-grade or high-grade MEC with cellular or extracellular mucin secretion should be differentiated from mucinous carcinoma and metaplastic carcinoma with heterologous mesenchymal differentiation, and from lipid-rich carcinoma {19795489; 3378488}, glycogen-rich carcinoma {9100798}, sebaceous carcinoma {20432752}, and acinic cell–like carcinoma {22734799}.

Ancillary testing

In acinic cell–like carcinoma, granules are DPAS-positive and on ICC are positive for α1-antichymotrypsin, amylase, E-cadherin, EMA, GCDFP-15, and lysozyme, and negative for ER and PR {22734799}.

AdCC is negative for ER, PR, and HER2, and strongly positive for KIT (CD117), helping to differentiate it from IBC-NST and cribriform carcinoma.

MECs are triple negative and show positive staining for DPAS, pankeratin, EMA, and CEA. The presence of CRTC1::MAML2 fusion transcripts supports a diagnosis of MEC.
