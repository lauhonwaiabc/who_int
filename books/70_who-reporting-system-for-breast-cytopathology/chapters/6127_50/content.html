6.1.2.7 Radial scar / complex sclerosing lesions

Definition

Radial scar and complex sclerosing lesion are benign lesions with central fibroelastosis containing entrapped distorted tubules, with or without proliferative epithelial lesions. Radial scars are small lesions, typically with a stellate configuration, whereas complex sclerosing lesions are larger, often > 10 mm, and more disorganized.

Clinical features and imaging

Mammographically these lesions are seen as a spiculated mass or architectural distortion that may be indistinguishable from an invasive carcinoma. The centre of the lesion may be radiolucent or dense. Ultrasonographically there is an irregular hypoechoic mass with poorly defined borders and posterior acoustic shadowing. MRI enhancement characteristics are variable {20958939; 26412991; 24936496; 32520588}. They are usually not palpable.

Histopathology

Radial scar has a stellate configuration, whereas complex sclerosing lesions are larger and more disorganized. Central dense hyalinized collagen and elastosis are seen, containing entrapped small, irregular tubules that usually show a two-cell layer of ductal and myoepithelial cells, although this is not always visible on H&E staining. Within and extending out to the periphery of the lesion there are ductal dilatation, usual ductal hyperplasia, cysts and apocrine metaplasia, and sclerosing adenosis in a lobulocentric proliferative process. In larger complex sclerosing lesions, these changes are seen over a large area often with small, frequently sclerosed papillomas {30390667; 28813198; 25578683; 20306826} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]].

Discussion and differential diagnosis

One study comprising 88 cases of radial scar / complex sclerosing lesion {10458501} and four with a smaller number (11–22) have been reported {8173027; 9360048; 17580343; 9181322}. The original cytopathological classification is reported in four of these. About half of these cases were reported as benign, but all had some cases reported as atypical or suspicious (19/155 = 12.3%). The epithelial hyperplasia in complex sclerosing lesion can be florid and cause concern in some cases. In addition, these lesions are occasionally seen at histopathological follow-up together with mainly low-grade in situ and invasive carcinomas, causing mixed cytopathological findings {16518071; 24300733}. In conjunction with imaging findings, which may be suspicious, there is a risk of overdiagnosis.

Ancillary testing

ICC such as p63 staining for myoepithelial cells is positive in epithelial tissue fragments, which show a mosaic pattern in CK5/6 staining and a patchy ER positivity.
