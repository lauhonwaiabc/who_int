3.1.0.4 Rapid onsite evaluation

ROSE is a technique that enables both sample adequacy assessment and, in many cases, onsite provisional diagnosis during a FNAB procedure. Performing ROSE is beneficial for the clinical team as well as the patient, primarily to determine whether additional cytopathological material is required for ancillary testing, or a CNB is required for evaluation of histopathological parameters {32044283}. CNB may be performed immediately after FNAB and ROSE without the need for a return appointment {34816801; 36932831; 31108486; 27160231; 32044283}. Moreover, ROSE provides a provisional diagnosis, reducing patient anxiety and facilitating management through cost-effective immediate triage and patient selection for ancillary testing.

ROSE requires the participation of a cytopathologist or a well-trained cytologist and may be performed in 5–10 minutes. The cytopathological material is first smeared onto slides, which are then either air-dried or immediately fixed in alcohol. Air-dried slides are stained with the Giemsa stain, while alcohol-fixed smears may be stained with toluidine blue or rapid H&E. The stained smears are then evaluated microscopically to determine whether the sample is adequate for diagnosis. At this time, a provisional diagnosis may also be issued to the clinical team. Some stains, such as toluidine blue, may be subsequently washed out and the slide re-stained with a different stain such as Pap.

Some specific examples of the utility of ROSE are listed below.

ROSE may indicate when complementary CNB would be helpful. Suspicious or malignant diagnoses of breast lesions or regional lymph nodes by ROSE may prompt histopathological biopsy, which may be performed concomitantly. Benign ROSE diagnoses which are concordant with the clinical and imaging findings in the triple test reduce the need for a CNB, which in turn reduces burdens on patients and costs to health care systems {12112806; 25722099}. ROSE guides triage of samples for ancillary testing, particularly when specialized media or processing are needed. Inflammatory processes identified at the time of ROSE may indicate the need for additional sampling to generate direct smears for special stains or material for microbiological cultures or molecular techniques. Concern for a lymphoproliferative disorder at the time of ROSE will indicate that material should be reserved for flow cytometry. Tumours detected at ROSE that may require molecular testing for characterization and classification (e.g. sarcomas) will prompt additional sampling to ensure sufficient material is available. ROSE diagnosis can identify scenarios where additional sampling for cell block creation would be beneficial, especially if histopathological biopsy is not readily available. ROSE supports timely triage of patients for clinical care purposes in lower-resource settings {33493018}. ROSE can be utilized to improve diagnostic accuracy of breast FNAB and reduce non-diagnostic rates (e.g. additional samples may be obtained if the initial smears are of suboptimal quality), prevent missed diagnoses, and provide reliable estimates of ROM {34515039; 24353146; 31108486; 12112806; 32044283; 36932831}. ROSE can reduce patient anxiety, especially for benign lesions, as the findings may be immediately communicated to the patient by the clinical team {28703443; 27160231} [[Harcourt, D. Same-day diagnosis of symptomatic breast problems: Psychological impact and coping strategies. Psychology, Health & Medicine. 1999;4(1):57–71. doi:10.1080/135485099106405.]]. A well-organized multidisciplinary approach with experienced health care team members enables the advantages of ROSE to be maximized.
