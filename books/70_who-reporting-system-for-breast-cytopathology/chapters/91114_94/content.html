9.1.1.14 Neuroendocrine tumour and carcinoma

Definition

Breast neuroendocrine neoplasms (NEN) include the vanishingly rare malignant well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumour (NET) and neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC), which is composed of small cells with high mitotic and proliferation indices, occurring as pure or combined with other carcinomas, defined by the presence of neurosecretory granules and a diffuse uniform immunoreactivity for neuroendocrine markers.

Clinical features and imaging

The clinical presentation of breast NEN is similar to that of other invasive breast cancers and affects older women {11895486; 28118820}, with nonspecific imaging of a circumscribed round to oval high-density mass on mammography {25055297} and a hypoechoic solid microlobulated hypervascular mass on ultrasound, rarely accompanied by structural distortion and microcalcification {35007984; 25055297}. MRI is also nonspecific, showing a homogeneous solid mass with heterogeneous enhancement patterns {25052497}.

Histopathology

NETs are exceedingly rare low- to intermediate-grade tumours consisting of densely cellular solid sheets and trabeculae of neoplastic cells which may be spindle, plasmacytoid, or polygonal with eosinophilic and granular cytoplasm, or form nests separated by delicate fibrovascular stroma. Papillary and insular patterns have been reported in breast NET {33531618; 21668471}. NETs usually express ER and PR.

NECs are malignant epithelial, infiltrative high-grade neoplasms and are rare, accounting for about 0.1% of all breast cancers {25853028}, with densely packed, uniform, small cells with high N:C ratios, hyperchromatic round to pleomorphic nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli, frequent nuclear moulding, and scant and poorly defined cytoplasm. Plentiful mitoses, prominent apoptosis, areas of necrosis, and frequent lymphatic tumour emboli are seen. Small cell NEC can occur in a pure form or mixed with any other carcinoma subtype, while large cell NEC in the breast is invariably classified as invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (NST) with neuroendocrine differentiation. On ICC, 30–50% of small cell NECs express ER and PR {30306431; 10976697} and HER2 is negative {10976697; 33531618}.

About 10–20% of invasive breast cancers express neuroendocrine markers, without typical neuroendocrine morphology, and are not regarded as NECs.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma
High cellularity Irregular crowded sheets and tissue fragments Crowded small cells with poorly defined cytoplasm and high N:C ratio, and small hyperchromatic round to pleomorphic nuclei exhibiting moulding, with finely granular dark and light punctate (salt and pepper) chromatin, with inconspicuous nucleoli Chromatinic smearing, apoptosis, and necrosis

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The sixth-edition Breast tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series states that it is uncertain if primary NENs of the breast exist, apart from solid papillary carcinoma, hypercellular mucinous carcinoma, and the very rare small cell carcinoma, analogous to small cell NEC of the lung {35133666} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]]. Breast NETs are extremely rare, and if a lesion with features suggesting NET is found, the first consideration should be to exclude a rare NET metastasis.

NECs are also rare and may be mixed with invasive breast carcinoma NST or other subtypes. The current diagnostic terminology is based on the extent of neuroendocrine differentiation [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; forthcoming. (WHO classification of tumours series, 6th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int.]]. Invasive carcinomas with cytomorphological features suggestive of neuroendocrine differentiation, which are at most focally or weakly positive for neuroendocrine markers, are regarded as invasive carcinoma NST. Carcinomas with cytomorphological features suggestive for neuroendocrine differentiation and showing moderate to strong or definite diffuse weak neuroendocrine marker patterns are classified as “invasive breast carcinoma NST with neuroendocrine differentiation” and an indication of the proportion of neuroendocrine differentiation should be provided in the report. The management of these carcinomas is as for invasive breast carcinoma NST.

For the diagnosis of NET to be considered, solid papillary carcinoma, hypercellular mucinous carcinoma, low-grade invasive breast cancer with neuroendocrine differentiation, and metastatic NEN from the gastrointestinal tract and other sites must be excluded. The differential diagnosis of NEC is mainly metastatic small cell carcinoma from other sites and high-grade invasive breast carcinoma without neuroendocrine differentiation. Solid papillary carcinoma shows high cellularity with plentiful atypical epithelial cells and distinctive papillary tissue fragments, and no necrosis {31513348}. Mucinous carcinoma can show nuclear neuroendocrine differentiation {15001999}, with tissue fragments and occasional single tumour cells showing mildly atypical nuclei and a moderate amount of vacuolated or granular cytoplasm in a fibrillary mucinous background {21552409}.

Ancillary testing

The aim of ancillary testing for NET/NEC is to confirm neuroendocrine differentiation and breast origin. These tests are performed in cases that show neuroendocrine differentiation of the nuclei. ICC confirms neuroendocrine differentiation by detecting cytoplasmic granular staining of chromogranin A and synaptophysin in two thirds of cases, synaptophysin showing higher sensitivity {32472950; 26114478}. INSM1 has high sensitivity, showing nuclear staining and positive association with synaptophysin and chromogranin {32951906}. A breast primary NET is positive for pankeratin and GATA3 and variably positive for ER (30–50%) {27125358}.

A breast primary NEC is positive for pankeratin, mammaglobin, GCDFP-15, and GATA3 and variably positive for ER in 30–50% of cases {27125358}.
