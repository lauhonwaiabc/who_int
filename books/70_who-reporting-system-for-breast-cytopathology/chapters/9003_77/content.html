9.0.0.3 Discussion and background

Malignant breast FNAB diagnosis has a very low false positive rate with a positive predictive value bordering on 100% in many practices {9568124}. The IAC Yokohama System for reporting breast FNAB cytopathology provided a ROM of 99-100% for a "Malignant" categorization {31112942}. For epithelial tumours, the most common diagnosis is carcinoma of no specific type [[Field AS, Raymond W, Schmitt F. The IAC Yokohama reporting system for breast FNAB cytopathology. Springer 2020]]. A small percentage of cases will represent lobular carcinoma, although distinguishing between these two entities on breast FNAB may have limited clinical utility. Other less common subtypes of epithelial breast malignancies include the various subtypes diagnosed in surgical pathology. Accurate classification of these subtypes by breast FNAB typically requires well-prepared smears and a cell block for ICC or NGS if indicated. Other epithelial malignancies in the breast include metastasis to the breast with ovary, lung, gastrointestinal and urinary tract being the most common primary sites {35447141}. Non-epithelial malignant lesions involving the breast include lymphoma, melanoma, malignant phyllodes tumour and sarcoma {14965721}. Similarly, accurate classification of uncommon or rare malignancies in the breast requires well-prepared smears and a cell block.

 

The vast majority of breast lesions referred for FNAB are mass lesions of the breast, as opposed to imaging detected abnormalities such as calcifications, asymmetry or architectural distortions, which are best sampled by image guided CNB. Most malignant mass lesions of the breast diagnosed on FNAB (97.7%) will have at least some component of invasive carcinoma, regardless of whether ductal carcinoma in-situ (DCIS) is also present in the lesion {10794158}. For cases in which breast biomarkers, estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), HER2 and Ki-67/MIB-1 are needed to inform treatment decisions, a CNB should be considered to delineate the extent of invasive and in-situ carcinoma in the tumour and to accurately identify which component is staining in the ICC. 

False negative diagnosis of malignancy can occur {9568124}. This is particularly true for lesions that are difficult to sample due to desmoplasia or extensive necrosis or location in the breast. The diagnosis of malignancy in the setting of low cellularity or poorly preserved cells should be made with caution and consultation. To reduce errors or misdiagnosis, correlation with physical examination findings and any available imaging studies is recommended, both at the time of FNAB with ROSE, and at the time of final review when the report is generated. A low threshold for recommending CNB or excision of lesions for which the cytomorphology and clinical findings are discordant can help reduce the incidence of misdiagnosis {15569203}.
