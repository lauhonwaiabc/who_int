3.1.0.2 Nipple discharge

Nipple discharge

Gentle massage is applied to the retroareolar breast in the direction of the nipple to express duct contents to the tip of the papilla and the discharge may come from one or more ducts. Duct contents may be clear serous, opaque, or haemorrhagic. The expressed material is caught on a slide, smeared, fixed in alcohol or air-dried, and appropriately stained for cytopathological evaluation {30484956}. Haemorrhagic discharge is more likely to be associated with malignancy {3456180}.

Nipple scraping

Eczematous nipple changes may be evaluated by scraping the nipple with a stylet or scalpel and smearing the sample. Paget disease of the nipple may be successfully diagnosed by this non-invasive, simple, well-tolerated technique {8912355}. Positive diagnosis of malignancy with this method warrants careful clinical examination to exclude an intramammary malignancy. Skin-based lesions may benefit from punch or incisional/excisional biopsy for further evaluation.

Lidocaine or other topical anaesthetic can be applied to the nipple in either liquid or gel form before the nipple scraping and will reduce patient discomfort during the procedure. The lidocaine is placed on the nipple in the area to be scraped and should be absorbed in a few minutes. For more sensitive lesions a small volume of lidocaine should be injected, preferably 5 mm away from the site to be scraped.
