9.1.1.8 Secretory carcinoma

Definition

Secretory carcinoma (SC) is an invasive carcinoma composed of epithelial cells with intracytoplasmic secretory vacuoles and extracellular eosinophilic, bubbly secretions, arranged in a variable architecture and frequently associated with ETV6::NTRK3 fusion.

Clinical features and imaging

SCs are carcinomas with excellent prognosis that were initially reported in children and adolescent female patients {4285563}. Subsequent research demonstrated that SCs occur mainly in middle-aged and older patients {6445777; 22494666}. SC usually presents as a unilateral, well-defined, regular mass {31012486}. Roughly half of the cases have stage I disease. Clinically, axillary lymph nodes do not show metastases in more than half of the cases {22494666}.

The imaging features are often nonspecific on mammography {18499854; 14528441}. On ultrasound, SC may be isoechoic or hypoechoic, may have homogeneous as well as heterogeneous echotexture, and has well-circumscribed and lobulated margins {18499854}.

Histopathology

SCs show variable architectural patterns with tumour cells arranged in variably sized cysts and glands as well as solid and papillary areas {22157932}. The cells have moderate to abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm with DPAS-positive globules, and uniform, mostly low-grade nuclei. Extracellular DPAS-positive secretions are also present. Mitotic rate is low {6445777; 22157932}. The stroma infiltrated by the carcinoma is often sclerotic and an in situ component may be present {14691916}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The extensive vacuolization and secretions may be mistaken for lactational change {9684599}. Clinical information is therefore important for the diagnosis. SC lacks myoepithelial cells in the tissue fragments and bare bipolar nuclei in the background. Apocrine carcinoma and acinic cell–like carcinoma may also be considered in the differential diagnosis, but do not have the metachromatic secretory extracellular material or intracellular globules {8834076}.

Ancillary testing

The secretions are DPAS-positive. SC is considered to be mostly triple negative, but some studies have reported that half of the cases may actually be ER-positive in usually < 10% of cells {31012486}. The tumour cells characteristically express CEA (polyclonal), S100, mammaglobin, GATA3, SOX10, and MUC4, and have a basal-like immunophenotype including positivity for EGFR, CK5/6, and CK14 {22157932; 28548128}. Diffuse and/or focal strong nuclear pan-TRK positivity is a sensitive and specific marker for SC of the breast {31498178}. ETV6::NTRK3 fusion testing by break-apart FISH or NGS for targeted RNA fusion is confirmatory {31012486}.
