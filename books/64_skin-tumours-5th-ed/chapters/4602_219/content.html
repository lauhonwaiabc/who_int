4.6.0.2 Primary cutaneous CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder: Primary cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphoma

Definition

Primary cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphoma (C-ALCL) is a mature T-cell lymphoma characterized by anaplastic, pleomorphic, or immunoblastic cells, with CD30 expression in > 75% of tumour cells.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The lesions impact the skin with no specific predilection site {20026848; 19528422}. Mucosal sites can be involved (rarely with concurrently involved cutaneous sites) {15604547; 22388754}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with solitary or localized skin nodules or tumours, rarely papules, often with ulceration {10845893; 14639383}. Multifocal lesions are seen in about 20% of patients. Lesions may show partial or complete regression (~20–25%). Relapse in skin is common. Regional lymph nodes are involved in approximately 10% {10845893}.

Epidemiology

C-ALCL is the second most common form of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma {30635287}. The median age is 60 years; children can be affected. The M:F ratio is 2–3:1 {10845893}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Rearrangement of the DUSP22 locus at 6p25.3 occurs in 20–25% of C-ALCL {19812605; 21169992}. Rearrangements of the TP63 gene are rare in C-ALCL {27146432; 22855598}. Frequent chromosomal aberrations are gains of 7q31 and losses at 6q16-q21 and 13q34 {20130593; 19710685}.

NPM1::TYK2 fusion, resulting in constitutive STAT signalling, has been described in sporadic cases of C-ALCL {25349176}. TYK2 fusions were found in 15%, leading to upregulation of PI3K, MAPK, and G protein pathways {34382383}. Gene expression profiling revealed high expression of the skin-homing chemokine receptor genes CCR10 and CCR8, possibly explaining skin tropism and infrequent extracutaneous dissemination {19710685; 22855598; 27146432}.

The vast majority of C-ALCLs do not show translocations involving the ALK gene at chromosome 2 {8605362}. Rare cases of ALK-positive C-ALCL, including cases with the t(2;5) chromosomal translocation and cases with variant ALK fusion partners have been reported both in children and in adults, usually with an excellent prognosis {24128129; 18670345; 22773605; 23525618; 32412717}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The dermis is infiltrated by tumour cells with anaplastic morphology, with round / oval / irregularly shaped nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant cytoplasm {30635287}. Less commonly, the tumour cells show a pleomorphic or immunoblastic appearance {10845893; 18095994; 7751878}. Epidermotropism may be present, particularly in cases with DUSP22 rearrangement {25131361}. Ulcerated lesions may show a lymphomatoid papulosis–like histology, with abundant reactive T cells (mostly at lesion borders); admixed histiocytes, eosinophils, and neutrophils; and relatively few CD30-positive cells. In such cases, epidermal hyperplasia may be prominent. The inflammatory background is prominent in the rare neutrophil-rich (pyogenic) and eosinophil-rich patterns {12653754}. Cases with prominent angiocentric and angiodestructive growth have been described {24246691}. Rare cases of intralymphatic and intravascular C-ALCL occur {24805854; 20337769}. Mucosal lesions show a similar spectrum of features {15604547; 22388754}. A more recent sarcomatoid pattern has been reported {33234878}, which can be confused with mesenchymal skin tumours.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells show variable loss of CD2, CD3, CD5, and CD7, and frequent expression of cytotoxic proteins such as TIA1, perforin, granzyme M, and granzyme B {10845893; 10672058; 9347791}. Some may show a CD4+/CD8−, CD4−/CD8+, or CD4+/CD8+ T-cell phenotype {24394302}. CD43 expression is usually seen, and most cases are negative for EMA. CD30 is expressed by > 75% of tumour cells {30635287}. CD15 is expressed in approximately 40% of cases {19416236; 18234282}. Cases can be TCRβ+, TCRβ−/TCRδ−, and rarely TCRδ+. LEF1 expression is associated with DUSP22 rearrangement {33165091}. In contrast to systemic anaplastic large cell lymphoma, almost all cases of C-ALCL lack expression of ALK, except for exceedingly rare cases of ALK-positive C-ALCL {29057463; 32412717}.

Differential diagnosis

The distinction between C-ALCL and lymphomatoid papulosis may be challenging and is usually established on clinical grounds. The term “borderline lesions” may be used in those cases where, despite careful clinicopathological correlation, a definite distinction between C-ALCL and lymphomatoid papulosis cannot be made {10845893; 14710887; 30635287; 7751878}. CD30-positive large cell transformation of mycosis fungoides should also be excluded. Reactive CD30-positive disorders (viral or arthropod) may be included in the differential diagnosis, although it is often more relevant to consider these for lymphomatoid papulosis, and very rarely for C-ALCL {10218681; 16412214}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Clonal rearrangements of TR genes are detected in most cases {8604686}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: distribution limited to skin and/or mucosa; cells with anaplastic, pleomorphic, or immunoblastic morphology; CD30 expression in > 75% of tumour cells; no clinical history or evidence of mycosis fungoides (to exclude mycosis fungoides with CD30+ large cell transformation); no history of lesions waxing and waning (to exclude lymphomatoid papulosis).

Staging

The TNM classification system for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides is applied {17339420}. Staging includes extensive history (including history of lymphoid neoplasms and duration of skin lesions), laboratory studies, and imaging studies (contrast-enhanced CT with and without PET, or whole-body integrated PET) {21841159}. Bone marrow examination has limited value unless there are multifocal lesions or unexplained abnormal haematological values {18782320}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is favourable, with a 10-year disease-related survival rate of approximately 90% {10845893; 14639383}. Presentation with multifocal skin lesions or regional lymph node involvement does not influence the prognosis. Worse outcome has been associated with extensive skin lesions on the legs, or patient age > 60 years {20026848; 14639383; 25068227; 19528422; 34081802}.
