2.2.1.1 High-CSD melanoma (including lentigo maligna melanoma)

Definition

Melanoma in skin with a high degree of cumulative sun damage (high-CSD melanoma) is a melanoma that arises on skin. Lentigo maligna melanoma (LMM) is a type of high-CSD melanoma characterized by a lentiginous in situ component called lentigo maligna. Desmoplastic melanoma often occurs in high-CSD skin but is discussed in its own section.

ICD-O coding

8742/3 Lentigo maligna melanoma

8742/2 Lentigo maligna melanoma in situ

ICD-11 coding

2C30.2 Lentigo maligna melanoma, primary

Related terminology

Acceptable: lentigo maligna melanoma.

Not recommended: Hutchinson melanotic freckle.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lentigo maligna and LMM occur in chronically sun-exposed skin, most frequently that of the head and neck region.

Clinical features

Lentigo maligna presents as an expanding asymmetrical patch with irregular indistinct borders and varying colour {20534080}. Amelanotic lesions present as an erythematous patch. There may be patchy areas of regression, appearing as grey or flesh-coloured regions. The vertical growth phase presents as a nodule or plaque area. There may be ulceration and a history of bleeding {8483369}. Lentigo maligna can grow (over months to decades) to several centimetres in diameter without developing an invasive component (vertical growth phase) {15523371}. The dermoscopic criteria of lentigo maligna include grey dots, grey globules, asymmetrical follicular openings, rhomboidal structures, grey circles surrounding follicular openings, red rhomboid structures, and increased density of the vascular network {33314891; 32592885}.

Epidemiology

High-CSD melanoma and LMM occur on the chronically sun-exposed skin of older patients, who tend to have an increased number of other neoplasms associated with chronic sun exposure such as solar lentigines and actinic keratoses, and fewer naevi {16572428; 14679157; 23647022; 12783935}.

Etiology

Cumulative sun exposure results in a high mutation burden in light-skinned individuals with poor tanning ability {16572428}.

Pathogenesis

High-CSD melanoma has one of the highest mutation burdens of any cancer. The mutations carry an ultraviolet (UV) radiation signature {26214590}. Mutations in the MAPK pathway are common and consist of mutually exclusive activating mutations of the oncogenes NRAS, BRAF (non-p.V600E), or KIT {24460190; 26214590; 18532874} or inactivating mutations of NF1. TERT promoter mutations are common and can arise before the tumour becomes invasive {26559571}. Biallelic inactivation of CDKN2A is common in the invasive component {26343386}. In addition to lentigo maligna, lentiginous dysplastic naevi on high-CSD skin are possible precursor lesions {38371417}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

High-CSD melanoma and LMM are poorly circumscribed lentiginous proliferations of atypical melanocytes with epidermal thinning and loss of rete ridges, with a lesser degree of nesting and with less pigment than low-CSD melanomas {18532874}, on skin with severe solar elastosis (defined as the presence of amorphous aggregates of elastotic material) {16809487}. Associated naevi are rare {25239415}.

Two major patterns of the radial growth phase of high-CSD melanoma may be distinguished:

The first pattern is termed classic lentigo maligna and shows a poorly circumscribed, broad proliferation of atypical naevoid to epithelioid melanocytes along the dermal–epidermal junction. Irregularly distributed nests, which typically appear to hang down from the junction in a droplet-like pattern, may be present. A common feature is extension of the lentiginous proliferation to the follicular infundibula and, occasionally, to the acrosyringium {22286443}.

The second pattern, seen in naevoid lentigo maligna {10994474} and dysplastic naevus–like lentigo maligna {15953373}, shows more nests, which may bridge adjacent rete ridges. The distinction from dysplastic naevus can be difficult.

The invasive component is often composed of small to moderately enlarged, atypical, ovoid, somewhat naevoid melanocytes; sometimes they have a more spindled shape.

Immunohistochemistry

Melanocyte-lineage antigens such as melan-A and SOX10 can be useful in assessing the cellularity and degree of confluence of the junctional melanocytic proliferation {25774012; 25674009} and reveal occult invasion {27533878}. PRAME is typically positive and may aid in the assessment of surgical margins {30045064}.

Differential diagnosis

Normal skin at chronically sun-exposed sites can show increased numbers of basilar melanocytes, some of which can have enlarged nuclei. These changes should be interpreted with caution when evaluating excision margins or high-CSD melanoma. Pigmented actinic keratosis must be distinguished from a melanocytic neoplasm. Dysplastic naevi do not commonly occur in chronically sun-damaged skin of older individuals, especially the face, and this diagnosis should be made with caution and with serious consideration of the possibility of a naevoid lentigo maligna {10994474} or dysplastic naevus–like lentigo maligna {15953373}. Occasionally, lymphocytic involvement of the basal epidermis in lichenoid inflammatory processes occurring in sun-damaged skin can be confused with lentigo maligna. Melan-A–positive pseudomelanocytic nests may be seen in association with lichenoid inflammation {19384076}. Lentigo maligna may colonize basal cell carcinoma and suggest invasive melanoma {25692077}. Pigmented spindle cell naevus/tumour of Reed usually occurs in younger patients, on skin without severe solar elastosis, and lacks the effacement of the epidermis and the more significant aberrant growth and cytological atypia seen in high-CSD melanoma {1985078}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analyses are typically not necessary for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an in situ component consisting of a poorly circumscribed lentiginous proliferation of atypical melanocytes in the epidermis on skin with severe solar elastosis; an invasive component of similar atypical melanocytes may be seen.

Desirable: thinning and effacement of the epidermis; limited pagetoid spread; adnexal involvement.

Staging

High-CSD melanoma should be staged according to the current Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification for cutaneous melanoma.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of high-CSD melanoma and LMM worsens with increasing tumour thickness or mitotic count, or with the presence of ulceration, lymphovascular invasion, or microscopic satellites.
