8.0.1.1 Onychopapilloma

Definition

Onychopapilloma is a benign epithelial proliferation originating in the distal nail matrix.

ICD-O coding

8052/0 Onychopapilloma

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms

Related terminology

Not recommended: localized multinucleate distal subungual keratosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These are most common on fingernails. They originate at the distal nail matrix and involve the nail bed and hyponychium {26518173; 29729116}.

Clinical features

Onychopapilloma usually occurs in adults, with only rare paediatric cases reported. These lesions typically present with longitudinal erythronychia, a reddish nail streak and/or yellowish discolouration, sometimes beginning in a wedge-shaped contour in the distal matrix. Splinter haemorrhage is also common, as is distal subungual hyperkeratosis, often forming a spicule. These are as large as 5–6 mm in width and are often more centrally placed. Distal nail plate splitting, fissuring or notching, and onycholysis can occur. Punctate haemorrhage has also been described. Onychopapilloma can show different patterns, including pigmented (often due to melanocytic activation) and those presenting with leukonychia {26518173; 29729116; 34396563}.

Epidemiology

Onychopapilloma is a relatively common and probably underreported benign epithelial proliferation of the nail unit; its incidence has not been formally studied {26518173; 29729116}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The best method of sampling to observe diagnostic features is a full-thickness longitudinal nail unit excision encompassing the lesion, which is also sectioned or oriented in this plane for processing {29729116}. Diagnostic features may remain attached to the nail plate during nail plate avulsion or reflection, which also often avulses the superficial nail unit epithelium. Proximally, there may be slight hyperplasia of nail matrix epithelium, and so-called matrical metaplasia of the nail bed, consisting of a pattern of maturation similar to matrix, with retained keratinocyte nuclei over eosinophilic superficial keratinocytes. More distally, there is nail bed hyperplasia (which is often papillated most distally) with hypergranulosis, mimicking verruca vulgaris, which is the main entity in the differential diagnosis. Some cases demonstrate multinucleate keratinocytes in the mid to distal nail bed as well, giving rise to the original name for this entity {7669645; 10886147; 26518173}. Subungual haemorrhage may be present. Distal subungual parakeratosis and hyperkeratosis is characteristic, and it may indent the overlying nail plate undersurface. These features, as well as haemorrhage, can be seen in nail plate clippings {26518173}. A single case of malignant onychopapilloma has been described, with histological features that overlap with those of squamous cell carcinoma in situ {33067854}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nail bed hyperplasia and matrical metaplasia, with clinicopathological correlation.

Desirable: subungual haemorrhage and distal hyperkeratosis; multinucleate nail bed keratinocytes.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

This is in general a benign entity, which does not tend to recur upon complete longitudinal excision {29729116}. A single malignant case has been described {33067854}.
