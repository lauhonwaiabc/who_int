4.3.2.2 Burkitt lymphoma

Definition

Burkitt lymphoma (BL) is a mature, aggressive B-cell neoplasm composed of monomorphic, medium-sized cells with basophilic cytoplasm, multiple small nucleoli, a germinal-centre B-cell phenotype, a high proliferation index, and IG::MYC rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

ICD-11 coding

2A85.6 & XH4KA9 Burkitt lymphoma, including Burkitt leukaemia & Burkitt lymphoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: Burkitt cell leukaemia; atypical Burkitt lymphoma; Burkitt-like lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

Traditional subtypes: endemic Burkitt lymphoma; sporadic Burkitt lymphoma; immunodeficiency-associated Burkitt lymphoma.

Recommended subtypes: EBV-associated Burkitt lymphoma; EBV-negative Burkitt lymphoma.

Localization

The disease is often widespread {26114450; 23426001; 16713488; 24562050}. It tends to involve the trunk and/or head {26114450; 20428660}.

Clinical features

Cutaneous involvement can be seen at presentation {23426001; 24562050; 20428660} or relapse {23426001; 9034303}, with subcutaneous nodules {26114450; 23426001; 24562050; 9034303} or (less often) macules, papules {26114450}, or large plaques {16713488}, typically along with extracutaneous disease. The lesions are usually multiple and widespread, suggesting haematogenous dissemination {26114450; 23426001}. The skin can be involved via extension from underlying lymphoma {26114450; 9034303} and postsurgical procedures such as excisions of lymph nodes involved by BL {16713488}. Although rare, BL of the skin may mimic herpes zoster infection clinically {32489872}.

Epidemiology

Cutaneous BL occurs in the setting of HIV infection {24562050; 20428660}, as well as in individuals who are otherwise healthy. Only 2% of HIV-positive patients with BL have cutaneous involvement {26114450}. Nearly all patients with cutaneous BL are male, and adults are affected more commonly than children {23426001; 24562050; 20428660; 9034303}.

Etiology

Risk factors for endemic BL are Plasmodium falciparum (malaria) and EBV infections. Factors causing BL in low incidence areas, such as Europe, Asia, and South America, are not well understood. The presence or absence of EBV better correlates with distinct biological subtypes of BL {30617194; 34617271; 34725813; 34888589}. EBV is detected in, at most, 20% of patients with sporadic BL {7059918}. A subset of cases are related to immunodeficiency {22260300; 32735838}. HIV infection is the most common cause of immunodeficiency in patients with BL {32735838}.

Pathogenesis

All subtypes of BL carry MYC translocations that probably occur as mistakes in immunoglobulin heavy chain switching or IGV somatic hypermutation {26712879}. EBV may be essential early in BL pathogenesis by allowing B cells with genetic defects to evade apoptosis. The presence of ID3 and TCF4 mutations in BL results in tonic B-cell receptor signalling {24492847}. These mutations are nearly mutually exclusive with EBV infection, suggesting a dual mechanism of BL pathogenesis: mutational versus virus-driven {26468873}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

A diffuse infiltrate of medium-sized, monotonous lymphoid cells with round nuclei, finely clumped chromatin, multiple medium-sized basophilic nucleoli, and deeply basophilic cytoplasm involves the dermis and/or subcutis, sparing the epidermis. Mitoses are numerous. Interspersed apoptotic debris is often abundant. Tingible-body macrophages typically impart a starry-sky appearance {26114450; 23426001; 9034303; 582573}.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic cells are positive for CD20, CD10, BCL6, and MYC and negative for BCL2 and TdT. EBV, as demonstrated by in situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER), can be positive or negative, and the Ki-67 proliferation index is nearly 100%.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes other aggressive B-lineage lymphomas, such as diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, high-grade B-cell lymphoma with concurrent MYC and BCL2 rearrangements, the blastoid subtype of mantle cell lymphoma, plasmablastic lymphoma, and lymphoblastic lymphoma. Strict adherence to diagnostic criteria is required to exclude other entities in the differential diagnosis.

Cytology

If amenable to FNA, skin lesions consist of medium-sized lymphoid cells with deeply basophilic cytoplasm, often containing some fine lipid vacuoles. Nuclei are round, and multiple basophilic nucleoli are noted {6546890; 2998986; 7833470}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Translocation involving MYC and IGH, or less often IGK or IGL, is characteristic. Translocations of BCL2 and BCL6 are absent.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: medium-sized, monomorphic lymphoma cells with basophilic cytoplasm and multiple small nucleoli; CD20 and CD10 positivity; absence or (rarely) weak expression of BCL2; Ki-67 index > 95%; usually strong expression of MYC (in > 80% of cells) and/or demonstration of MYC breakage or IG::MYC translocation.

Desirable: starry-sky pattern, cohesive growth pattern; BCL6 positivity, TdT negativity, CD38 positivity; exclusion of BCL2 and BCL6 rearrangements (mainly required in adult BL).

Staging

The predominantly extranodal distribution of BL makes Ann Arbor staging suboptimal. The system proposed by Murphy in 1980 and revised in 2015 is used routinely for cases in children {25940716}.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients usually present with advanced-stage disease. With appropriate therapy, the prognosis is good. Disseminated cutaneous involvement appears to be an unfavourable prognostic factor {26114450}.
