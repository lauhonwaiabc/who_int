5.2.1.8 Dermatomyofibroma

Definition

Dermatomyofibroma (DM) is a rare benign cutaneous mesenchymal neoplasm of fibroblastic/myofibroblastic differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8824/0 Dermatomyofibroma

ICD-11 coding

2F23.Y & XH18K3 Other specified benign dermal fibrous or fibrohistiocytic neoplasms & Dermatomyofibroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: plaque-like dermal fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Predilection sites are the upper trunk and upper arm, shoulder, axillary folds, and neck, followed by the abdominal wall and thigh {8468425; 1597573; 19155724}.

Clinical features

DM manifests as an asymptomatic, erythematous, reddish-brown or hypopigmented, longstanding or slowly growing, slightly indurated, flat, plaque-like lesion, or (less often) a nodule {33059951; 19155724}. Linear and annular presentations have also been reported. Rarely, the tumour may be congenital, multicentric, or of considerable size {34152037; 18801097}.

Epidemiology

DM most frequently occurs in young females {8468425; 19155724}. DM in young children is rare {21752049; 28318057}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

DM is a myofibroblastic tumour {1597573; 8872155}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Throughout the dermis, there is a horizontally oriented plaque-like arrangement (except in the deep dermis / subcutis where the arrangement is usually perpendicular to the epidermis) of densely packed slender bundles and long fascicles of cytologically bland fusiform spindle cells showing fibroblastic and myofibroblastic differentiation. The epidermis is generally unaffected. The papillary dermis is typically spared. The adnexal structures are surrounded by the tumour but are usually not disrupted. Elastic fibres are preserved or even slightly increased. DM extension into the superficial subcutis is rare.

Immunohistochemistry

DM usually expresses vimentin and calponin, while α-SMA and CD34 show variable positivity. Desmin, h-caldesmon, and S100 are negative {33059951; 19155724}.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis includes dermatofibroma and plaque-stage dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans {31908825}. Unlike in dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, there is no COL1A1::PDGFB rearrangement {31090131}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The absence of COL1A1::PDGFB rearrangement may help in challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a benign myofibroblastic proliferation limited to the reticular dermis; groups of spindle cells arranged in fascicles and parallel to the epidermis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

DM is a benign tumour. Recurrences are rare even after incomplete excision {19155724}.
