5.2.2.2 Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma

Definition

Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma (MIFS) is an infiltrative, locally aggressive fibroblastic neoplasm with a predilection for acral soft tissue, characterized by prominent inflammation, a myxohyaline stroma, and pleomorphic fibroblastic cells with macronucleoli.

ICD-O coding

8811/1 Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH2D15 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: inflammatory myxohyaline tumour of the distal extremities with virocyte-like or Reed–Sternberg–like cells; inflammatory myxoid tumour of the soft parts with bizarre giant cells.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MIFS usually affects the acral dorsal extremities, particularly the hands {9578090; 9706971}; proximal examples amount to 5% of cases {12376919; 24121178}. The hand, finger, and wrist account for about 60% of cases, and the foot and ankle for 20% {24121178}.

Clinical features

MIFS usually manifests as a painless mass. On imaging, it is centred in the subcutis (in about two thirds of cases) or deeper and typically displays infiltration into adjacent tissues, often mimicking infection, tenosynovial giant cell tumour, or ganglion cysts {27560024; 17449774; 15908525}.

Epidemiology

MIFS is rare. It has a peak incidence in the fourth to sixth decades of life and no sex predilection {24121178; 18190444; 9706971; 9578090; 9779486}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Amplification of VGLL3 on 3p11-p12, resulting in a supernumerary ring chromosome, is reported in > 50% of cases {31898851}. A t(1;10) with breakpoints around OGA on 10q24 and TGFBR3 on 1p22 is documented in 13% of cases {31898851; 32514165} and is also found in pure haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumours, in examples with mixed MIFS morphology (where the translocation appears to be more prevalent) {21717526; 26980036}, and in pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour {24705316}. BRAF fusions or amplifications are also found {28692601; 32514165; 31898851}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are lobulated and variably gelatinous, fleshy, or firm, typically attaining a size of about 30 mm.

Histopathology

MIFS is infiltrative and multinodular, being centred in the subcutaneous tissue in most cases. Especially in acral sites, these lesions tend to be superficial and to involve the dermis. They are characterized by dense inflammation merging with stroma varying from myxoid to hyalinized and containing sheets and small foci of epithelioid and spindled cells. Some lesions contain foamy histiocytes, giant cells, and haemosiderin. Within the inflammatory background, scattered bizarre cells with large vesicular nuclei and macronucleoli reminiscent of Reed–Sternberg cells are present. Many cells show degenerated (smudged) chromatin. Pseudolipoblasts with cytoplasmic mucopolysaccharide matrix may be seen. The inflammatory infiltrate is mixed, consisting of lymphocytes, plasma cells, histiocytes, and eosinophils in varying proportions. Emperipolesis, often subtle, is a common feature {9779486; 32514165; 25886867}. Mitotic activity is minimal.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry is unhelpful. The majority of cases exhibit expression of cyclin D1 (BCL1), factor XIIIa, D2-40, and CD10 {9706971; 24121178; 25886867; 32514165}. Variable expression of CD34, CD163, and PRAME is also observed, as well as focal expression of keratin, KIT (CD117), CD68, and actin {18190444; 24121178; 9706971; 25886867}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: typical distal extremity location; atypical fibroblastic cells with macronucleoli; variably myxoid and hyalinized matrix with a mixed inflammatory infiltrate.

Desirable: pseudolipoblasts.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrences are common and often repeated. Metastases are rare, occurring in < 1% of cases after multiple recurrences. Initial complete excision is the best predictor of a favourable outcome {24121178}. No specific histological criteria predict aggressive behaviour, but one study associated high-grade histological progression with metastatic potential {2588687}.
