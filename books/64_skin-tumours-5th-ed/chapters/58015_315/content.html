5.8.0.15 Hybrid nerve sheath tumours

Definition

Hybrid nerve sheath tumours are benign peripheral nerve sheath tumours with combined features of more than one conventional type (neurofibroma, schwannoma, perineurioma).

ICD-O coding

9563/0 Hybrid nerve sheath tumours

9563/0 Perineurioma/schwannoma

9563/0 Schwannoma/neurofibroma

9563/0 Perineurioma/neurofibroma

ICD-11 coding

2F24 & XH01G0 Benign cutaneous neoplasms of neural or nerve sheath origin & Hybrid nerve sheath tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign nerve sheath tumour NOS.

Subtype(s)

Perineurioma/schwannoma; schwannoma/neurofibroma; perineurioma/neurofibroma

Localization

Tumours show a wide anatomical distribution, most commonly occurring in the dermis or subcutaneous tissue {19623031; 9639114}. The most commonly reported site for hybrid schwannoma/perineurioma is on the fingers {15322875}.

Clinical features

Hybrid nerve sheath tumours manifest most often as painless masses in subcutaneous tissue or dermis.

Epidemiology

Hybrid nerve sheath tumours are rare. The most common examples are tumours showing hybrid features of schwannoma and perineurioma, followed by hybrid neurofibroma/schwannoma. These tumours may occur at any age, with a peak in young adults and an equal sex distribution {19623031}.

Etiology

Hybrid schwannoma/perineurioma occurs sporadically {19623031}, whereas hybrid neurofibroma/schwannoma is strongly associated with the neurofibromatoses {22446939}. A high prevalence of hybrid schwannoma/neurofibroma morphology was found in tumours from patients with schwannomatosis (71%), often leading to misdiagnosis as neurofibroma {22446939; 15955931}. Hybrid neurofibroma/perineurioma has been described in association with neurofibromatosis type 1 {24751814; 23676318}.

Pathogenesis

VGLL3 gene rearrangement was found in most cases of hybrid perineurioma/schwannoma in one cohort, suggesting these tumours represent a distinct pathological entity {33649458}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, the tumours are well circumscribed, with a firm cut surface.

Histopathology

Hybrid schwannoma/perineurioma shows a storiform or fascicular growth and is usually composed of an intimate admixture of alternating Schwann cells with plump nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm and perineurial cells with slender nuclei and delicate elongated cytoplasmic processes. The Schwann cell component is often prominent. The tumours may show degenerative nuclear atypia (ancient change). Mitoses are rare. The two components can be highlighted by immunohistochemistry: Schwann cells are positive for S100, and perineurial cells for EMA, claudin-1, and GLUT1 {19623031}.

Hybrid neurofibroma/schwannoma is composed of schwannomatous nodules or Schwann cell bundles within an otherwise typical neurofibroma. Immunostaining highlights the mixed population of cells in the neurofibromatous component, including fibroblasts (CD34), Schwann cells (S100 and SOX10), and perineurial cells (EMA, claudin-1, and GLUT1), and the monomorphic Schwann cell populations in the Schwann cell solid nodules (highlighted by diffuse expression of S100 and SOX10) {9639114; 22446939}.

Hybrid neurofibroma/perineurioma contains a plexiform neurofibroma with areas of perineurial differentiation. These areas are often only recognizable with the aid of immunohistochemistry. The perineuriomatous areas are immunopositive for EMA, GLUT1, and claudin-1, and the neurofibroma areas show a mixed population of cells including fibroblasts (CD34), Schwann cells (S100 and SOX10), and perineurial cells (EMA, claudin-1, and GLUT1) {23676318; 24461704}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: intermingled features of two types of benign nerve sheath tumours; appropriate immunohistochemical staining for each component.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The tumours are benign and rarely recur locally {19623031}.
