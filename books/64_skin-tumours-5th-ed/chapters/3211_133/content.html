3.2.1.1 Trichoblastoma

Definition

Trichoblastoma is a benign biphasic neoplasm with dual differentiation towards follicular germinative epithelium and follicular papillae.

ICD-O coding

8100/0 Trichoblastoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH2K97 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Trichoblastoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: trichoblastic fibroma; trichoepithelioma.

Not recommended: cutaneous lymphadenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The head and neck area are predilection sites for all trichoblastoma patterns. Large lesions seem to have a predilection for the pelvic girdle.

Clinical features

Sporadic lesions are typically solitary, asymptomatic, skin-coloured or reddish, and 5–30 mm in size. Large nodular trichoblastoma is often associated with a sebaceous naevus and appears pigmented. Syndromic tumours appear as bilateral, small (2–10 mm), discrete and/or confluent skin-coloured papules and nodules on the face, preferentially involving the nasolabial folds.

Epidemiology

Sporadic tumours typically occur in the fifth or sixth decade of life, whereas syndromic lesions usually develop around puberty.

Etiology

Most cases are sporadic; multiple lesions are usually associated with Brooke–Spiegler syndrome or its phenotypic subtype, multiple familial trichoepithelioma, characterized by the occurrence of only trichoepitheliomas {10835629; 15677973; 21389835; 11533075}. Syndromic cases (occurring in the settings of Brooke–Spiegler syndrome and multiple familial trichoepithelioma) are associated with germline mutations in CYLD. Somatic mutations identified in the syndromic tumours are either loss of heterozygosity or sequence change {15854031; 23249834; 21389835; 20502185}.

Pathogenesis

Trichoblastomas recapitulate events seen in the embryo and in postnatal anagen hair follicles. Mutations (and polymorphisms) in CTNNB1 (encoding β-catenin), HRAS, and PTCH1 have been found in a minority of sporadic tumours {17597182; 19384065; 25532942}.

Recurrent FOXK1::GRHL and GPS2::GRHL fusions have been detected in trichogerminoma, which suggests that GRHL1/GRHL2/GRHL3 gene rearrangements might represent the oncogenic driver in follicular germinative neoplasms {35049062}. Pigmented trichoblastomas arising in a naevus sebaceus show HRAS mutations as a common molecular driver {28554764}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

On the basis of their architectural features, trichoblastomas can be grouped in large nodular, small nodular, adamantinoid (lymphadenoma), retiform, and racemiform patterns. Conventional and desmoplastic trichoepitheliomas have also been referred to as cribriform and columnar trichoblastomas, respectively {5476873; 10835629; 15677973; 21389835; 11533075}. A combination of the above patterns is not uncommon {34797788}. Trichogerminoma is also considered a pattern of trichoblastoma by most authors {1282133}, although its distinctive molecular profile argues in favour of a peculiar subtype {35049062}.

All trichoblastomas are usually well-circumscribed, non-ulcerated neoplasms confined to the dermis, rarely involving the subcutis. Occasionally, trichoblastoma can have a shelled-out appearance or a multinodular arrangement, with the formation of distinct fibroepithelial units, usually separated by clefts. Tumours are composed of uniform basaloid cells with scant cytoplasm associated with a stromal component that resembles follicular papillae and the perifollicular sheath. This type of stroma is less conspicuous in adamantinoid and large nodular trichoblastomas, as well as in desmoplastic trichoepithelioma (also called columnar trichoblastoma).

In conventional trichoepithelioma (also called cribriform trichoblastoma), the predominant arrangement of the epithelial elements is cribriform (sieve-like), although the racemiform pattern (cellular aggregations resembling clusters of grapes) and retiform structures (cords and columns of neoplastic cells in a net-like pattern) are also often seen, suggesting that all of these may represent various stages of growth or that they are seen in different sectioning planes. Several discrete or confluent large nodules, often with melanin deposits, are seen in large nodular trichoblastoma. In the small nodular pattern, the stroma may dominate over small epithelial aggregates of follicular germinative cells.

Adamantinoid trichoblastoma is composed of epithelial cell nodules with peripherally located palisaded basaloid cells and centrally located pale larger cells with vesicular nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant faintly eosinophilic or amphophilic cytoplasm admixed with numerous small lymphocytes and occasional large multinucleated cells resembling Reed–Sternberg cells. Desmoplastic trichoepithelioma (columnar trichoblastoma) is usually located in the upper reticular dermis and is composed of basaloid cells arranged in short cords, thin columns, and small nests. It is enveloped by bundles of collagen; rudimentary follicular papillae are rarely seen {17461815; 9504675; 1989457}.

Histopathological features variably encountered in trichoblastomas include infundibulocystic structures, clear cell change (probably recapitulating the outer root sheath at the bulb/stem), focal sebaceous differentiation, focal ductal differentiation, melanocytic colonization, melanocytic hyperplasia overlying the tumours or an association with a melanocytic naevus (seen in 10–15% of columnar trichoblastomas, formerly known as desmoplastic trichoepithelioma), epithelial multinucleated cells, and various stroma alterations (e.g. amyloid deposits, lipomatous metaplasia, and prominent myxoid change) {7692756; 3186547; 16778484}.

Immunohistochemistry

The epithelial cells are positive for BerEP4 {11801790; 10235376; 10446774; 10446773}. Data on the presence of CK20-positive Merkel cells in trichoblastoma are somewhat conflicting: although some studies have found a consistent presence of Merkel cells {9808429; 10839616; 35049062}, there are also cases completely devoid of Merkel cells {17519622}. The follicular stem cell marker PHLDA1 (TDAG51) has been proposed as the best marker for histopathological differential diagnosis between trichoblastoma and basal cell carcinoma, because it is usually positive in the former and negative in the latter {21352265; 22958125; 23398472; 23719479}. The negative results for PHLDA1 in basaloid neoplasms developed in naevus sebaceus support the notion that they are basal cell carcinomas rather than trichoblastomas {23489134}. When present, ductal structures stain positively for EMA and CEA {8594073; 9331893; 10235376; 9027628}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed neoplasm composed of epithelial aggregates of germinative follicular cells and abundant, densely cellular fibrotic stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Trichoblastomas are benign neoplasms. Malignant transformation is rare and is seen rarely in syndromic cases, in which basal cell carcinoma can develop within the background of pre-existing benign lesions {19730223}. Malignant lesions with epithelial and stromal components resembling those of trichoblastoma have been reported as trichoblastic carcinomas {33993349} and trichoblastic carcinosarcomas {18212548}. Biphasic tumours composed of melanomas arising from the proliferation of dendritic melanocytes colonizing trichoblastomas have been described {23503317; 24335517}. Merkel cells are present in trichoblastomas {9808429; 10839616; 35049062}, and Merkel cell carcinoma has been described arising in trichoblastoma, due to virus integration {31759946}.
