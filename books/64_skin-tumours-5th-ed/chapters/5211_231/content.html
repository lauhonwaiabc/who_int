5.2.1.1 Fibroma of tendon sheath

Definition

Fibroma of tendon sheath is a rare benign tumour, presumably of (myo)fibroblastic origin, that arises in the tenosynovial soft tissue, usually attached to a tendon, and occurs predominantly in the upper extremities.

ICD-O coding

8813/0 Fibroma of tendon sheath

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH0WB3 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Fibroma of tendon sheath

Related terminology

Not recommended: tenosynovial fibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common sites are the tendons of the fingers, hands, and wrists. Less common sites include the forearm, foot, and knee {27125357; 91424}.

Clinical features

Most tumours present as an asymptomatic to mildly painful, small (< 20 mm), slow-growing, and solitary mass, although multiple nodules have also been reported {23212985; 91424; 22028571}.

Epidemiology

Fibroma of tendon sheath is rare, with a predilection to occur in middle-aged men {23212985; 27125357; 91424}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

These tumours have been found to have gene fusions involving USP6 and a variety of partners, including PKM, RCC1, ASPN, COL1A1, COL3A1, MYH9, MIR22HG, CTNNB1, SPARC, CAP1, EMP1, CYTOR (LINC00152), NR1D1, RAB1A, and TNC {34088995; 34520696}. They may be a tenosynovial form of nodular fasciitis {32661296}.

Macroscopic appearance

Fibroma of tendon sheath is well circumscribed, with a lobulated mass, a uniform grey to white cut surface, and occasional myxoid to cystic change.

Histopathology

Fibroma of tendon sheath is composed of small to medium-sized bland fibroblastic spindle and stellate cells arranged in fascicles or a storiform pattern, set within a dense fibrocollagenous hyalinized matrix with slit-like spaces and dilated vascular channels. Some myxoid change, extravasated red blood cells, mononuclear inflammatory cells, and (rarely) giant cells can be seen {26729987}. Cellular areas may also be present {91424}. Tumours with unusual histological findings such as a palisading growth pattern, epithelioid cells, and osteoclast-like multinucleated giant cells may have PKM or ASPN as a gene fusion partner {32661296}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes nodular fasciitis, myofibroma, and benign fibrous histiocytoma.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

USP6 rearrangement may support diagnosis in challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed lobulated nodule on tendon; usually hypocellular, with spindle cells in a collagenous background and slit-like vascular spaces.

Desirable: USP6 rearrangement (in selected or challenging cases).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Fibroma of tendon sheath is benign but recurs locally in about 25% of cases {91424}.
