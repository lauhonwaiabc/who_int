4.4.2.2 Hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder

Definition

Hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder (HV-LPD) is an EBV-positive T-cell (or occasionally NK-cell) lymphoproliferative disorder that spans a range of behaviours, from the classic form with self-limited photodermatosis to the systemic form with extensive skin lesions and multiorgan involvement.

ICD-O coding

9725/1 Hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder

9725/1 Classic hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder

9725/1 Systemic hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder

ICD-11 coding

2B0Y & XH0AK5 Other specified primary cutaneous mature T-cell or NK-cell lymphomas and lymphoproliferative disorders & Hydroa vacciniforme–like lymphoproliferative disorder

Related terminology

Not recommended: severe hydroa vacciniforme; hydroa vacciniforme–like lymphoma; hydroa vacciniforme–like lymphoproliferative disorder.

Subtype(s)

Classic hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder; systemic hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder

Localization

The classic form involves sun-exposed skin. In the systemic form, non–sun-exposed skin is often additionally affected, combined with multiorgan involvement (of the liver, spleen, lymph nodes, and lungs) {30998212}.

Clinical features

Classic HV-LPD is characterized by recurring papulovesicular eruptions on sun-exposed skin that usually evolve to blisters and ulcers, leaving varioliform atrophic scars. The skin lesions often exhibit seasonal variations, recurring in spring and summer. While some cases resolve during adolescence, others exhibit a more protracted course. The lesions of the systemic HV-LPD are not clearly induced or exacerbated by sun exposure {26368647}. As the disease progresses, severe and extensive skin lesions develop in sun-exposed and non–sun-exposed areas, accompanied by fever, hepatosplenomegaly, and lymphadenopathy. Rare cases show an atypical clinical presentation, including prominent periorbital or lip oedema associated with systemic symptoms {26368647; 25162933}. Rare patients have concomitant severe mosquito bite allergy {22297643}.

Epidemiology

HV-LPD is uncommon. It is encountered principally in Asian children and adolescents, as well as in Indigenous populations of South and Central America and Mexico. It is rare in White populations. The median patient age at onset is 16 years (range: 4–60 years) {30654082; 23982171; 23541598; 11893040; 17976208}.

Etiology

EBV infection is the key factor, and genetic/ethnic predispositions may play a role {31064750}. In the classic form, expansion of EBV-infected γδ T cells is apparent in peripheral blood and blisters {19698076; 22886572; 22297643}. αβ T cells are more common in the systemic form {23982171}.

Pathogenesis

Most cases show monoclonal rearrangements of the TR genes and EBV clonality. Genetic and environmental factors appear to account for differences in the clinical behaviour, which seems to be more indolent in White populations {31064750}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The skin shows epidermal reticular degeneration evolving to intraepidermal vesiculation and sometimes ulceration. There is a perivascular and periadnexal infiltrate of atypical small to medium-sized lymphoid cells in the dermis, occasionally extending into the subcutis. Angiodestruction and necrosis are often present {30654082; 23982171; 23541598; 9580256; 31889602}.

Immunohistochemistry and in situ hybridization

Many cases comprise CD8-positive cytotoxic T-cells expressing TIA1, granzyme B, or perforin. Less commonly, the lymphoid cells have an NK-cell phenotype (positive for CD56, negative for CD5 and surface T-cell receptor); such cases often show prominent subcutis involvement, morphologically mimicking subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma {23982171; 30998212}. T-cell receptor surface expression is variable. Cases with the NK-cell phenotype lack T-cell receptor expression. Variable numbers of lymphoid cells stain positively in in situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER).

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes other lymphoproliferative processes with angiocentric or subcutaneous infiltrates or necrosis, such as cutaneous gamma-delta T-cell lymphoma or sCD30-positive lymphoproliferative disorders (e.g. lymphomatoid papulosis subtype E), as well as infectious processes such as leishmaniasis or syphilis.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In situ hybridization for EBER is required to demonstrate the presence of EBV. Clonal TR gene rearrangements are supportive.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for classic HV-LPD: no persistent systemic symptoms, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, hepatitis, haemophagocytic syndrome, or NK-cell lymphocytosis; for systemic HV-LPD: one or more of these persistent symptoms or signs of extracutaneous disease; papulovesicular skin eruptions with or without photoexacerbation that heal with varioliform scarring; perivascular and periadnexal atypical lymphoid infiltrates of cytotoxic T cells or NK cells; EBV positivity.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical course is variable. Classic HV-LPD often pursues a relapsing course, with complete resolution of the disease by adolescence or adulthood. Currently, no guidelines for the treatment of HV-LPD are available. For early-stage HV-LPD affecting children and young adults, conservative treatment (photoprotection and topical steroids) may be used. Recent clinical studies suggest that systemic HV-LPD can be temporarily controlled with immunomodulators (thalidomide, antivirals, interferon), although haematopoietic stem cell transplantation can apparently lead to remission in patients with systemic disease or lymphoma {23982171; 32516422; 33982815}. T-cell clonality, the number of EBV-positive cells, and the density of the lymphoid infiltrate are not correlated with the clinical course {23982171; 25234411}. Late-onset disease (> 9 years of age) and BZLF1 expression correlate with a poor prognosis {25234411}.
