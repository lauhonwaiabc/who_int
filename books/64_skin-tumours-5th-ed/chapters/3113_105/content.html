3.1.1.3 Poroma

Definition

Poromas are benign neoplasms with differentiation towards the intradermal portion of the sweat gland.

ICD-O coding

8409/0 Poroma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH8N28 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Poroma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: acrospiroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intraepidermal poroma commonly involves the extremities, but it can affect other sites. About 65% of classic poromas involve the palms or soles. Intradermal poroma commonly involves the head and neck region.

Clinical features

The classic pattern of poroma typically arises as a solitary sessile or pedunculated papule or an asymptomatic nodule ranging from 2 to 20 mm in size, but they can also be cup-shaped, papillomatous, or wart-like. The colour of the lesion varies from red to the normal skin colour. Some neoplasms have an angiomatous, fleshy appearance resembling pyogenic granuloma. Pigmentation has also been described {5938222}. Multiple poromas have been described after radiotherapy or allogeneic bone marrow transplantation and immunosuppression {6098189}. The hidroacanthoma simplex pattern of poroma usually appears as a verrucous or flat keratotic plaque or erythematous patch with well-defined borders.

Epidemiology

The age at presentation is 60–80 years.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Poromas are thought to derive from the basal keratinocytes of the sweat duct ridge and the lower acrosyringium {20571345}. Some poromas show loss of heterozygosity in the APC gene, but the significance of this genetic anomaly is uncertain {10674823}. Most poromas harbour recurrent YAP1::MAML2 or YAP1::NUTM1 gene fusions {35665951; 35167714; 33739783; 31145701}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

All poromas are composed of poroid and cuticular cells. Poroid cells have a rounded or oval nucleus and scant cytoplasm. Cuticular cells have ample eosinophilic cytoplasm and a central nucleus, sometimes with moderate atypia. Ductal differentiation is seen, in the form of small intracytoplasmic vacuoles or the formation of authentic intracytoplasmic ductal structures. Additional findings include areas of necrosis, cystic areas, foci of keratinization, pale cells, dendritic melanocytes, melanin pigment, and keratohyalin granules. Superficially traumatized lesions may show focal cytological atypia and increased mitotic activity.

The hidroacanthoma simplex pattern is mainly intraepidermal.

The classic pattern includes cords and aggregates of poroid cells that connect with the epidermis in the superficial dermis. Poroma with a dermal duct tumour pattern is composed of small nodules of similar cells scattered within the upper dermis, without connection with the epidermis. Poroma with a poroid hidradenoma pattern has one or several nodules with solid and cystic areas {3301956}. Areas of necrosis en masse within neoplastic aggregates may be observed. The stroma surrounding poromas is often richly vascular and may resemble granulation tissue.

Apocrine- and eccrine-pattern poromas are similar. However, apocrine poromas may connect to follicular structures {2843062}. Focal decapitation secretion, sebocytes, and focal follicular germinative differentiation may also be seen {9747272}. CEA staining highlights the luminal border of ductal structures; EMA decorates the neoplastic cells {26269431}. NUT is expressed in poromas driven by YAP1::NUTM1 gene fusion {33739783}.

Differential diagnosis

Clonal-type seborrhoeic keratosis can be confused with the hidroacanthoma simplex pattern, but the ductal structures and two populations of cells in the hidroacanthoma simplex pattern facilitate correct diagnosis. Hidradenomas are composed mainly of cells with pale cytoplasm, whereas poromas include both poroid and cuticular cells.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a neoplasm composed of two cellular populations: poroid (smaller and darker) and cuticular (larger and with an eosinophilic cytoplasm) with duct differentiation.

Desirable: richly vascular stroma resembling granulation tissue; areas of necrosis en masse within neoplastic aggregations (classic poromas and dermal duct tumours).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Poromas are benign neoplasms.
