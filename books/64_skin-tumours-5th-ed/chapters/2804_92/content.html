2.8.0.4 Uveal melanocytoma

Definition

Uveal melanocytoma is a subtype of uveal melanocytic naevus, comprising plump polygonal cells with dense intracytoplasmic melanin.

ICD-O coding

8726/0 Uveal melanocytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F36.0 Benign neoplasm of choroid

2F36.1 Benign neoplasm of iris

2F36.2 Benign neoplasm of ciliary body

Related terminology

Acceptable: magnocellular naevus of the uvea.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Melanocytomas occur more frequently in the iris {15767055; 20829742} and ciliary body {10768345} than in the choroid; they are also found adjacent to the optic nerve head {20829742; 16500211}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with an asymptomatic discoid or dome-shaped dark-grey to black mass that elevates the retina, that can have a mossy or irregular surface that may cause iris heterochromia. Both ciliary body and iris melanocytomas can cause glaucoma. In a few cases, overlying orange pigment and subretinal fluid might be seen, which makes the distinction from a choroidal melanoma or other deeply pigmented choroidal naevi difficult {19125075; 11786063; 17621182; 32273508}.

Imaging

Imaging techniques of value include high-frequency anterior segment ultrasound biomicroscopy, ultrasound biomicroscopy, and B-scan ultrasonography. In uncertain cases, FNAB may be required {19125075}.

Epidemiology

Uveal melanocytomas are rare. The age at presentation ranges from infancy to 90 years {902779; 17621182}. The distribution between the sexes and different ethnic groups is nearly equal {19125075}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Uveal melanocytomas harbour GNAQ mutations at exon 5, GNAQ p.Q209L {23685997; 27239460}, with no other aberrations detected {23685997; 27239460; 35835335; 15767481}.

Macroscopic appearance

On gross examination, melanocytomas appear dark brown to jet back and generally measure 1–5 mm in thickness.

Histopathology

Melanocytomas are composed of large polygonal pigmented melanocytes. Bleaching is usually required to adequately evaluate the tumour cells, which show a low N:C ratio and oval to round bland nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli. Although necrosis can occur in large tumours, mitoses are frequently absent.

Immunohistochemistry

Melanocytomas are positive for melan-A, HMB45, SOX10, MITF, and S100. Nuclear BAP1 immunoreactivity is retained. The Ki-67 growth fraction is very low or absent (0–2%) {11942551}.

Differential diagnosis

The major differential diagnoses include uveal naevi and melanoma. Although uncommon, adenomas of the pigment epithelium of the ciliary body, iris, and/or retinal pigment epithelium should also be considered.

Cytology

Uveal melanocytoma has large, ovoid cells with small, centrally located nuclei and pigmented cytoplasm {18323281}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a deeply pigmented uveal lesion with ovoid cells with abundant cytoplasm, small and centrally located nuclei, and few indistinct nucleoli.

Desirable: immunohistochemical confirmation of melanocytic lineage.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Instances of malignant transformation to melanoma are rare {1078332; 8010703; 23685997; 15965181}.
