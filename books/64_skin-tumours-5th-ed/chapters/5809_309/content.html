5.8.0.9 Smooth muscle hamartoma

Definition

Smooth muscle hamartoma (SMH) represents an anomalous proliferation of haphazardly arranged smooth muscle bundles in the dermis.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LC02 Complex epidermal hamartoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Congenital smooth muscle hamartoma; acquired smooth muscle hamartoma

Localization

The majority of cases occur on the trunk or extremities; the head and neck region and external genitalia are less common sites of involvement {32804407}.

Clinical features

There is a slightly indurated and pigmented or flesh-coloured plaque, ranging from millimetres to several centimetres in size, with or without perifollicular papules and hypertrichosis. A positive pseudo–Darier sign is present in 80% of congenital SMHs (CSMHs). Most CSMHs are solitary; however, rare multiple, familial, and non-familial cases have been documented {22630577}. Diffuse CSMH has been described as a component of Michelin tyre baby syndrome {8436660}, and cases of combined SMH with congenital or blue naevi have also been reported {18544054; 8436660}.

Epidemiology

SMH has a slight male predominance. CSMH is uncommon, affecting approximately 1 in 2600 births {2356798}. Acquired SMH is extremely rare, with < 20 cases reported to date. Age at presentation ranges from 12 to 64 years {29774573; 34637157}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Abnormal migration and proliferation of arrector pili muscle during fetal development may play a role.

Pathogenesis

A subset of CSMHs result from postzygotic ACTB mutations. These mutations have been previously reported in Becker naevi, suggesting that CSMHs and Becker naevi are biologically related {32170967}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

SMH demonstrates haphazardly arranged, mature smooth muscle bundles in the dermis, often in close proximity to the pilosebaceous units {2658846}. This is in contrast to pilosebaceous leiomyoma, which demonstrates more orderly smooth muscle bundles arranged in ramifying fascicles. Atypia/pleomorphism and mitoses are absent. Focal retraction artefact may be observed around the smooth muscle bundles. Expression of smooth muscle markers is seen. Overlying epidermal hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, and basal hyperpigmentation, reminiscent of Becker naevus, may be encountered.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: haphazardly arranged muscle bundles within the dermis; absent atypia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

SMH is benign. Treatment may not be necessary, and excision is typically curative. Rare cases of recurrence/persistence following incomplete excision have been reported {32804407}.
