3.1.1.2 Syringoma

Definition

Syringoma is a small, benign sweat gland tumour, thought to arise from eccrine ducts.

ICD-O coding

8407/0 Syringoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH6325 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Syringoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most syringomas arise in the lower eyelids and periorbital area {8599451}. Eruptive lesions tend to occur on the neck, chest, axillae, pubic area, umbilicus, and genital region.

Clinical features

Syringoma manifests as skin-coloured or slightly yellow smooth papules. Eruptive syringoma manifests as several scattered small papules (mostly on the anterior chest wall and flexural areas of the limbs), which may coalesce to form plaques {1918498}. They can have a linear and one-sided distribution. Vulvar syringomas are usually pruritic {8832956}.

Multiple syringomas may resemble milia cysts. When the lesions are pigmented, they may simulate urticaria pigmentosa or confluent and reticulated papillomatosis of Gougerot–Carteaud.

Dermoscopy of syringoma is said to show reticular brown lines on a light-brown background, with reticular vessels {30201149; 33088820; 34604332}.

Epidemiology

Syringomas most commonly affect elderly women, but the eruptive pattern is most common in young women {11683289}. Clear cell syringoma, which is more common in people with diabetes, is histopathologically characterized by ducts and solid aggregates of epithelial cells with optically clear cytoplasm.

Etiology

Syringomas of the eyelids may be sporadic, but they are common in patients with Down syndrome {7971561}. They have also been described in patients with hyperthyroidism, Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, Marfan syndrome, and Nicolau–Balus syndrome {7262384}. In familial forms, multiple syringomas affect several family members.

Pathogenesis

Syringomas resemble the ductal portion of the eccrine gland, from which they are thought to arise. The hedgehog pathway may be involved, and high expression of sonic hedgehog has been reported {33714217}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Syringomas are well-circumscribed lesions more frequently located in the upper half of the dermis. Syringoma has two components: epithelial cells forming ducts, cords, tubules, and cysts; and a homogeneous and sclerotic stroma. The lumina of the ductal and tubular structures are lined by a double layer of cuboidal cells, sometimes containing granular basophilic material. The ducts often show characteristic extensions resembling a tadpole tail. Solid collections of epithelial cells are also observed, often containing multiple confluent cytoplasmic vacuoles. Small cysts are often present, filled with basket-weave, basophilic, and orthokeratotic keratin and lined by squamous epithelium. The stroma consists of thick bundles of compact and sclerotic collagen. In eruptive syringoma, the stroma is less prominent.

Differential diagnosis

In superficial biopsies, the differential diagnosis includes microcystic adnexal carcinoma, sclerosing/morphoeic basal cell carcinoma, and columnar trichoblastoma (formerly desmoplastic trichoepithelioma), from which distinction may be impossible without complete removal and clinicopathological correlation. Ductal proliferations in cicatricial alopecia, alopecia areata, and prurigo nodularis may resemble syringoma; however, in such cases, the stroma is different. Eruptive hamartomas of clear cells of the eccrine duct (or so-called clear cell metaplasia of the eccrine duct) resemble clear cell syringomas {8776379}, but these lesions affect only the excretory ducts of pre-existing eccrine glands.
