3.1.1.9 Syringocystadenoma papilliferum

Definition

Syringocystadenoma papilliferum (SCAP) is a benign apocrine neoplasm that arises either as an isolated lesion or in association with naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn.

ICD-O coding

8406/0 Syringocystadenoma papilliferum

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH1PY0 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Syringocystadenoma papilliferum

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

About 90% of cases are located on the head and neck, including the eyelids {7977619} and external auditory canal {7888022}. Less commonly, they may involve the mammary region {9700386} and lower extremities.

Clinical features

Most lesions appear as a solitary papule or nodule. Less frequently, they present as multiple grouped, oozing, crusted papules or nodules forming a plaque or horn. Many cases are associated with naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn.

Epidemiology

The tumours have a female predilection and occur across a wide age range.

Etiology

None

Pathogenesis

Pathogenesis is due to activating mutations in the MAPK signalling pathway, affecting BRAF, KRAS, and HRAS. Syringocystadenoma papilliferum can develop sporadically or (more frequently) in the genetic setting of a cutaneous mosaic RASopathy, such as naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn {26360803}. In sporadic tumours, HRAS p.G13R mutations are found in a few cases. KRAS p.G12D mutations are less frequent {25532942}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

SCAP is an endophytic crateriform lesion, connected focally to the epidermis/hair follicle and composed of two layers of cells forming dilated papillary and cystic structures. The luminal cells are columnar, with rounded or oval nuclei near the basal pole of the cell and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm with decapitation secretion. Mucinous metaplasia may be observed. The outer layer consists of basal/myoepithelial cells. The overlying squamous epithelium often displays prominent hyperplasia. The epidermis surrounding SCAP is often papillomatous or wart-like, particularly in cases associated with naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn. The stroma contains numerous plasma cells.

Immunohistochemistry

BRAF p.V600E mutation can be shown, with excellent sensitivity and specificity, using the anti–BRAF p.V600E antibody (clone VE1). The luminal cells of the tubules are positive for CEA and EMA, whereas the outer cell layer is typically reactive for myoepithelial markers (SMA, calponin, SOX10, and p63) [[Requena L, Sangüeza O. Cutaneous adnexal neoplasms. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2017.]]. A focal lack of myoepithelial cells can be seen in areas with prominent squamous metaplasia or intraluminal secretions.

Differential diagnosis

The histopathological differential diagnosis of SCAP includes hidradenoma papilliferum and nipple adenoma {26863059}. Hidradenoma papilliferum has papillary formations similar to those of SCAP, and the epithelial cells lining these structures also show evidence of secretion by decapitation; however, hidradenoma papilliferum is not connected to the epidermis, lacks plasma cells, and is localized in the vulva {18752136}. In adenoma of the nipple, proliferation of tubular structures is more prominent, connections with follicular infundibula are less obvious, and there are no plasma cells.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Syringocystadenoma papilliferum is associated with BRAF p.V600E and HRAS mutations {25532942; 30398985}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an intradermally located tumour with micropapillary cystic architecture; connection with the epidermis and/or pre-existing hair follicles.

Desirable: plasma cell–rich stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

SCAP is a benign neoplasm.
