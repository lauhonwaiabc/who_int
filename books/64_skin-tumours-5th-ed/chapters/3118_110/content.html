3.1.1.8 Tubular adenoma

Definition

Tubular adenoma is a benign dermal neoplasm composed of tubules and glandular structures with or without papillae.

ICD-O coding

8211/0 Tubular adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH7SY6 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Tubular adenoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: tubular papillary adenoma; tubular apocrine adenoma.

Not recommended: papillary eccrine adenoma; tubular syringoadenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

It is most common in the skin of the lower limbs but can also affect the face and scalp.

Clinical features

The lesions appear as asymptomatic, single, well-circumscribed nodules with a smooth surface. In some cases, they show a papillated surface and can be ulcerated. Their size ranges from < 10 mm to 70 mm, although most cases are < 20 mm.

Epidemiology

Tubular adenoma most frequently occurs in middle-aged women {8599457}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Similar to syringocystadenoma papilliferum, tubular adenoma can develop sporadically or (more frequently) in the genetic setting of a cutaneous mosaic RASopathy, such as naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Tubular adenomas are well-circumscribed dermal neoplasms that may extend into the subcutis. They have an overall lobular architecture and are typically encased by a fibrous stroma. The lobules consist of multiple irregularly shaped tubular structures that have a double- to several-layered epithelial lining. The peripheral epithelial layer consists of cuboidal to flattened (myoepithelial) cells, and the luminal layer consists of columnar cells that sometimes demonstrate decapitation secretion. In some tubules, papillary cellular extensions that are devoid of stroma project into the lumina. Cellular debris and eosinophilic granular material are found within some lumina {4113017}. The neoplasm lacks cytological atypia and mitotic activity. Overlying epidermal hyperplasia may be present. In cases that occur in conjunction with syringocystadenoma papilliferum {2551940; 3036918}, the tubular adenoma component is typically present, underlying the syringocystadenoma component.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry with the anti–BRAF p.V600E antibody (clone VE1) can be used successfully to detect cases with BRAF p.V600E mutation {29206289}. The luminal cells of the tubules are positive for CEA and EMA, whereas the outer cell layer is typically reactive for myoepithelial markers including SMA, calponin, SOX10, and p63 [[Requena L, Sangüeza O. Cutaneous adnexal neoplasms. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2017.]]. A focal lack of myoepithelial cells can be seen in areas with prominent squamous metaplasia or intraluminal secretions.

Differential diagnosis

Tubular adenoma and syringocystadenoma papilliferum show overlapping morphological features {17519623}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Some cases of tubular adenoma carry mutations in the MAPK signalling pathway, affecting BRAF, KRAS {29241739}, and HRAS {22683711}. Tubular adenomas associated with naevus sebaceus almost always harbour HRAS or KRAS mutations {22683711}. Activating mutations of BRAF, HRAS, and KRAS increase cell growth, differentiation, and survival. In sporadic tumours, HRAS p.G13R mutations are found in a few cases. KRAS p.G12D mutations are less frequent {25532942}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobules of irregularly shaped tubules with a dual or multilayered epithelium embedded in fibrous stroma.

Desirable: intraluminal micropapillae lacking a fibrovascular core.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Tubular adenoma is a benign neoplasm.
