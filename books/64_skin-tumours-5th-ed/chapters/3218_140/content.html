3.2.1.8 Trichodiscoma and fibrofolliculoma

Definition

Trichodiscoma and fibrofolliculoma are hamartomatous lesions that differentiate towards the epithelium and mesenchyme of the hair follicle mantle.

ICD-O coding

8391/0 Trichodiscoma

8391/0 Fibrofolliculoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH4YU8 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Follicular fibroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: mantleoma; neurofollicular hamartoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most lesions arise on the head and neck, especially on the nose and perinasal skin.

Clinical features

Trichodiscoma and fibrofolliculoma arise as solitary or multiple white or skin-coloured papules with a smooth surface, measuring 1–10 mm, with no symptoms {24786577}. The presence of multiple lesions strongly suggests Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome, in which they are associated with angiofibromas, oral papules, cutaneous collagenomas, renal neoplasms, and pulmonary cysts {14512919} [[Sattler EC, Steinlein OK. Birt-Hogg-Dubé syndrome. In: Adam MP, Mirzaa GM, Pagon RA, et al., editors. GeneReviews. Seattle (WA): University of Washington, Seattle; 2006 Feb 27 [updated 2020 Jan 30].]].

Spindle cell–predominant trichodiscoma (SCPT) is a pattern presenting as a solitary, dome-shaped, skin-coloured papule with a smooth surface, measuring 5–10 mm. Clinically, it may be mistaken for a melanocytic naevus, basal cell carcinoma, or cyst {16456317}.

Epidemiology

Trichodiscoma, fibrofolliculoma, and SCPT are rare. Solitary lesions tend to occur in the fifth or sixth decade of life, with no sex predilection {24786577; 16456317}. Multiple, syndromic lesions are seen earlier in life {14512919}.

Etiology

The etiology of sporadic lesions is unknown. Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome is an inherited autosomal dominant syndrome associated with a pathogenic variant in the folliculin gene (FLCN) [[Sattler EC, Steinlein OK. Birt-Hogg-Dubé syndrome. In: Adam MP, Mirzaa GM, Pagon RA, et al., editors. GeneReviews. Seattle (WA): University of Washington, Seattle; 2006 Feb 27 [updated 2020 Jan 30].]]. Outside the setting of Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome, multiple familial trichodiscomas with no systemic organ involvement and no FLCN mutation have also been reported {21794948}.

Pathogenesis

Trichodiscoma, fibrofolliculoma, and SCPT are thought to be hamartomatous tumour lesions derived from the hair mantle.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Fibrofolliculoma and trichodiscoma represent stages of a single pathological process forming a morphological continuum. Fibrofolliculoma is a symmetrical dermal infundibulocentric proliferation, with a central dilated infundibulum from which thin epithelial strands and sebaceous glands emanate, sometimes with a fenestrated pattern. The characteristic stroma is composed of fibrillary ribbon-like collagen, mucin, and fibrocytes in varying proportions {24786577}. The fibrocytes are spindle-shaped, with elongated, slightly wavy nuclei and tapering cytoplasm {16456317}. Collagen bundles are perpendicular to the epithelial strands. In trichodiscoma, the predominant component is stroma, with peripheral clusters of sebaceous lobules described as mitts or “hands of banana”. Depending on the section plane, morphological variation may occur {10189247}.

SCPT is a pattern with abundant mucinous stroma and a cellular haphazard proliferation of fibrocytes. Discrete lipomatous metaplasia is a common feature, and histopathological differential diagnosis with spindle cell lipoma may be challenging {30908293}. Uncommon histopathological findings include a focal palisaded, schwannoma-like arrangement of fibrocytes {20644461; 29621024} and lesions with hyperchromatic pleomorphic nuclei (symplastic SCPT) {21915028}.

Fibrocytes in fibrofolliculoma, trichodiscoma, and SCPT are positive for CD34 and negative for S100. A morphological pattern of SCPT with few entrapped S100-positive nerves was previously described as neurofollicular hamartoma {1659245; 8030767}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes dermal spindle cell lipoma and neurofibroma. In contrast with spindle cell lipoma, most spindle cells may show loss of RB1 staining, but FISH analysis does not demonstrate heterozygous deletion of RB1 {30908293}. The spindle cells of neurofibroma are S100-positive.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

No genetic data are needed for the diagnosis. Patients with multiple lesions should be referred for oncogenetic consultation and testing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a dome-shaped papule with infundibulocentric proliferation of delicate epithelial strands and surrounding stroma (fibrofolliculoma), or predominant central stroma with lateral collarettes of mature sebaceous lobules (trichodiscoma); stroma composed of fibrillary collagen bundles, mucin, and CD34-positive fibrocytes.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Trichodiscoma, fibrofolliculoma, and SCPT are benign.
