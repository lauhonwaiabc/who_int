8.0.1.2 Onychomatricoma

Definition

Onychomatricoma is a benign fibroepithelial proliferation, usually of the nail matrix.

ICD-O coding

8810/0 Onychomatricoma

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms

Related terminology

Acceptable: onychomatrixoma.

Not recommended: unguioblastic fibroma; subungual pleomorphic fibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Onychomatricomas are most frequently located on fingers and less commonly on toes {20098079}.

Clinical features

Patients with onychomatricoma typically present with a nodular area under the proximal nail fold. The corresponding nail plate is thickened, in a sharply demarcated manner, and is often yellowish with transverse overcurvature and splinter haemorrhage {1610693; 12140434; 28611999}. The width of the altered nail plate may vary, with giant lesions rarely reported {27386467}. Dorsal pterygium may occur {18842322}. Pigmented patterns display melanonychia {25060607; 28332321}. Cavities can be seen when viewing the distal nail plate end-on, and are seen on the proximal nail plate as wormwood-like or honeycomb-like holes when avulsed.

Epidemiology

This is a relatively uncommon nail tumour (< 200 cases, with underreporting likely) largely of middle-aged adults, with a female predominance and a rare paediatric case reported {25970230; 17300649}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

A gross specimen may consist of a nodular portion of tissue in the area of the nail matrix, with an interdigitating, markedly thickened nail plate with overcurvature. When the nail plate is detached and examined, many cavities can be seen proximally {20098079; 9777762}.

Histopathology

The proximal portion originates in the nail matrix and rarely the proximal nail fold, and consists of a variably fibrocellular and rarely myxoid stroma {31503009; 25830718} with CD34-positive spindle cells, surmounted by reduplicated matrical epithelium. This interdigitates in a finger-and-glove pattern with the nail plate, forming many V-shaped keratogenous zones. The nail plate is thickened, with loculated serum, haemorrhage, and some papillated epithelium, often containing dilated blood vessels {1610693; 12140434; 20098079}. The proliferative pattern with exaggerated epithelium may represent the same entity as onychoblastoma {32271204; 15888182}. The pleomorphic pattern contains multinucleate to pleomorphic nuclei {24965861; 34694416}. Some such cases have been reported as subungual pleomorphic fibroma or unguioblastic fibroma {14507406; 20514682}. Pigmented patterns may display epithelial and nail-plate melanin {25060607; 28332321}. An origin from the nail bed has also been reported {25039855; 31049340}, although whether such cases are the same entity is unclear. Onychomycosis may occur secondarily {34604337}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of the fibrocellular portion includes other CD34-positive entities such as superficial acral fibromyxoma (also variably expressing EMA and CD99) {11486169; 22367301}, angiofibroma, neurofibroma, and perineurioma. Nail clippings can be used to make the diagnosis, with a thick nail plate, loculated serum, haemorrhage, and sometimes an epithelial lining {12140434; 21931060; 23278722}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a matrical fibroepithelial proliferation, interdigitating with thickened nail plate (when intact).

Desirable: submitted nail plate with thickening, loculated serum, and haemorrhage, with attached interdigitating epithelium.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

This is benign tumour with a low rate of recurrence upon complete excision {20098079}.
