2.8.0.1 Conjunctival naevus

Definition

Conjunctival naevi are benign melanocytic neoplasms of the conjunctival epithelium and its invaginations, the epithelial–subepithelial junction and stroma, or the stroma alone.

Related terminology

Acceptable: melanocytic naevus.

Subtype(s)

Junctional naevus; compound naevus; subepithelial (stromal) naevus; balloon cell naevus

Localization

Junctional melanocytic naevi are usually located at the juxtalimbal bulbar conjunctiva {14769591}. Most compound and subepithelial melanocytic naevi arise in the bulbar (commonly juxtalimbal) conjunctiva, caruncle, and plica semilunaris; they are rarely located in the palpebral and forniceal conjunctiva {2657539; 8883545; 20683369; 14769591}.

Clinical features

Conjunctival naevi are well-defined, variably pigmented (usually tan to brown) or amelanotic, mobile macules, papules, or plaques {14769591; 21128776}. Compound naevi often show multiple small epithelial inclusion microcysts by slit-lamp examination {2657539; 34424954}.

Epidemiology

Junctional, compound, and subepithelial melanocytic naevi account for 3–6%, 70–78%, and 15–24% of biopsied conjunctival naevi, respectively {20622349; 8883545; 14769591; 21128776}. Junctional melanocytic naevi have an M:F ratio of 2:1 and occur in childhood or adolescence. Compound and subepithelial melanocytic naevi are diagnosed at a median patient age of 22 years (range: 5–74 years) and 46 years (range: 12–87 years), respectively {20622349; 2657539; 8883545; 14769591}. Compound and subepithelial melanocytic naevi have an M:F ratio of 1:1 and are more common in White populations.

Etiology

The prevalence of naevi in various populations depends on phenotypic factors (in particular sun susceptibility), sun exposure patterns, and genetic susceptibility {20647408}.

Conventional conjunctival naevi are acquired melanocytic naevi that arise from neural crest–derived melanocytes {29332123} through somatic mutations.

Pathogenesis

Like other melanocytic naevi, conjunctival naevi are clonal proliferations caused by oncogenic mutation. Most conjunctival naevi have either BRAF or NRAS mutations {29332123; 27009410; 27307047; 26300491; 12447372}. Conjunctival naevi with NRAS mutations appear to be larger and occur at a younger age than those with BRAF mutations {29332123}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Like common acquired naevi of the skin, the three main histological types of melanocytic naevi are junctional, compound, and subepithelial.

Junctional melanocytic naevi exhibit an intraepithelial proliferation of round to oval epithelioid cells and/or oval to cuboidal, intermediate, or lymphocyte-like naevus cells {2657539; 21128776}. The nucleoli are usually basophilic without atypia. Mitotic figures are rare {2657539; 21128776}. The naevus cells can be arranged as a lentiginous proliferation or as individual, confluent, or irregular nests {2657539; 21128776}, without pagetoid spread.

Compound melanocytic naevi contain naevus cells within the epithelium and the substantia propria, exhibiting maturation with depth. Solid and cystic surface epithelial invaginations of conjunctival epithelium (with goblet cells) are common. Mitotic activity, if present, appears in the superficial layer {2657539; 21128776}.

Subepithelial melanocytic naevi arise in the substantia propria, with melanocytes arranged as nests. Subepithelial naevi are more likely to be amelanotic or minimally pigmented {20142539}. The superficial naevus cells tend to be larger and more polygonal, whereas the deep naevus cells may display neuroid differentiation {2657539; 20142539}. Sporadic pleomorphism can be seen. Mitotic figures are generally absent {20142539}. Solid and cystic conjunctival epithelial invaginations are seen in about half of the cases {20142539; 14769591}.

Uncommonly, junctional and compound naevi may show disordered architectural alterations intermediate between those of conventional naevi and melanoma, such as increased diameter > 5 mm, peripheral extension of the junctional component, asymmetry, poor circumscription, irregular and confluent junctional nesting, and focal pagetoid spread. Aberrant alterations in the subepithelial component may include diminished or even reversed maturation, nodule formation, and occasional mitotic activity. On occasion, naevi may be inflamed with prominent lymphocytic infiltrates, resembling halo naevi in the skin.

Immunohistochemistry

Most conjunctival naevi are positive for HMB45 in the superficial areas only {11683945}, with homogeneous deeper staining for melan-A, SOX10, and MITF. They are usually negative for cyclin D1 and PRAME, whereas most conjunctival melanomas are positive for these markers {34268800; 34089198; 35356600}.

Differential diagnosis

For junctional melanocytic naevi, the main differential is the conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesion (see section Conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions). Junctional melanocytic lesions in an adult should be assumed to be conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions until proved otherwise {19128387; 2657539; 21128776; 33130046}. Conjunctival melanoma usually exhibits a larger size, aberrant architecture such as confluent and hypercellular nesting, cytological atypia, and greater mitotic activity. Melanoma cells are larger than naevus cells and show conspicuous nuclear atypia {2657539; 30672666}. Adjoining melanoma in situ may be present. Extraocular extension of a uveal melanoma will show widespread broad involvement of the biopsy base by melanoma cells with characteristic cytology and genetics {23222563; 32273508} (see the section on choroidal and ciliary body melanomas in the Eye and orbit tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Eye and orbit tumours [Internet; beta version ahead of print]. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2023. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 13). https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/65.]]).

Cytology

Conjunctival naevi comprise epithelioid cells (type A), oval to cuboidal cells (type B), and spindled cells (type C) {21128776}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign melanocytic neoplasms of naevus cells within the conjunctival epithelium, at the epithelial–subepithelial junction, and/or in the conjunctival stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is excellent. Progression to conjunctival melanoma is rare {2657539; 15763188; 14769591}.
