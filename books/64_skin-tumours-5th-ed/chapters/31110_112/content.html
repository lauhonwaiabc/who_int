3.1.1.10 Mixed tumour

Definition

Mixed tumour is a benign sweat gland neoplasm composed of epithelial, myoepithelial, and mesenchymal elements; it is the cutaneous analogue of pleomorphic adenoma.

ICD-O coding

8940/0 Mixed tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH70N8 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Mixed tumour

Related terminology

Not recommended: chondroid syringoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours often involve the scalp and face but can also affect the external ear canal, eyelids, hands and arms, and acral sites. The trunk and genital region are rarely involved.

Clinical features

Mixed tumours are solitary (rarely multiple), well-circumscribed nodules with a smooth surface. They are typically 5–30 mm, but can be as large as 120 mm. Tumours with an osteocartilaginous component may be firm {21697702; 17707152}.

Epidemiology

Mixed tumour is most common in middle-aged men (median age: 53.5 years); the reported age range is 10–96 years {17707152}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Pluripotent cells are probably the source of glandular and myoepithelial cells. PLAG1 {22038920} and EWSR1 {21725291} are rearranged in cutaneous mixed tumours. PLAG1 is a developmentally regulated zinc-finger proto-oncogene; rearrangements of PLAG1 are detected by FISH in fewer than half of cutaneous mixed tumours {32345665}. PLAG1 activation through gene fusion typically occurs due to promoter swapping from various fusion partner genes that are ubiquitously expressed, such as CTNNB1, LIFR, and CHCHD7.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Mixed tumour has two main patterns: eccrine and apocrine.

The apocrine pattern presents as a well-circumscribed lesion with three components: epithelial, myoepithelial, and mesenchymal (i.e. mucinous, chondroid, osseous, adipocytic, or fibrotic). The epithelial component is composed of elongated, branched tubules and cystic spaces. These epithelial structures are lined by two cell layers; the peripheral layer is composed of cuboidal myoepithelial cells, and the inner layer is formed by columnar cells. The luminal cell layer may exhibit secretion by decapitation. Cystic areas often contain homogeneous eosinophilic material. Solid aggregates of epithelial cells, as well as isolated polygonal and plasmacytoid epithelial cells, may be also seen. Tumours with a prominent myoepithelial component may show focal cytological atypia; such cases have been called hyaline cell–rich mixed tumour {17592277}. Follicular and/or sebaceous differentiation can be present {10408348}.

The eccrine pattern of mixed tumour is less common and lacks the PLAG1 gene rearrangement {30839344}. It is composed of numerous non-branching, round ductal elements lined by a single layer of cuboidal cells embedded in a predominantly myxoid or chondromyxoid stroma. Small solid epithelial aggregates may be seen. Unusual findings include cribriform areas, clear cell change, hyaline cells, pseudorosette-like structures, prominent osseous metaplasia, and striking intracytoplasmic vacuolization of the neoplastic cells, resulting in physaliphorous-like cells {21697702}.

Acral mixed tumours are nodular and characterized by sheet-like growth of plasmacytoid myoepithelial cells in a myxoid or angiomatous stroma. Duct differentiation is often a focal finding only {35354162; 23630011}.

Immunohistochemistry

Mixed tumours express SOX10, p63, S100, calponin, SMA, EMA, KIT, CEA, various cytokeratins, and PLAG1 (although the eccrine pattern does not express PLAG1).

Differential diagnosis

Myoepithelioma is a related entity, distinguished by a lack of ductal structures. When the mesenchymal component is prominent, the differential diagnosis of mixed tumour includes chondroma, but chondromas do not contain epithelial cells.

Cytology

FNAB smears show fibrillary chondromyxoid material, with a prominent cellular component composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells {20391975; 26800260; 28772003}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed, smooth-surfaced nodule; tubules and cords of glandular epithelium embedded in a myxohyalinized or cartilaginous stroma; for eccrine mixed tumours: no signs of decapitation secretion or of sebaceous or follicular differentiation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign neoplasms that grow slowly, but over the course of years they can reach several centimetres in size.
