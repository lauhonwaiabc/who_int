1.2.1.7 Basal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid differentiation

Definition

Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) with sarcomatoid differentiation has an epithelial BCC component and malignant stroma that can show varying differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8092/3 Basal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid differentiation

ICD-11 coding

2C32.Y & XH1JH6 Other specified basal cell carcinoma of skin & Basal cell carcinoma, sarcomatoid

Related terminology

Not recommended: metaplastic carcinoma; carcinosarcomatous basal cell carcinoma; sarcomatoid basal cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This BCC type occurs on sun-exposed skin of elderly White men, most commonly on the head, followed by the upper extremities and abdomen.

Clinical features

Patients present with a rapidly growing new lesion or rapid enlargement of a longer-standing lesion. It is typically large at diagnosis (average: 28 mm). The morphology is usually that of an exophytic nodule, and ulceration is frequent {26268472}.

Epidemiology

See the family description (section Basal cell carcinomas).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The epithelial component is believed to undergo divergent mesenchymal differentiation, leading to the development of the stromal component. While the sarcomatous stroma is typically negative for keratins and p63, positive p63 staining in 1 case bolsters this hypothesis {26268472}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Nodules of BCC are separated by a malignant mesenchymal stroma that resembles pleomorphic undifferentiated sarcoma, osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and (rarely) myoepithelial carcinoma {23221485; 31278765}. Sarcomatous stroma will have focal or absent staining for keratins or p63 {17001162}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a rapidly growing new exophytic lesion or rapid enlargement of a longer-standing lesion; nodules of BCC separated by malignant mesenchymal stroma (sarcomatous).

Desirable: stroma lacks staining for epithelial markers.

Staging

Staging is according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

This type of BCC has a similar recurrence rate and metastatic potential to other BCCs of similar size, despite its aggressive histology. This is in contradistinction to the more aggressive behaviour of sarcomatoid squamous cell carcinoma and adnexal carcinomas with sarcomatous differentiation.
