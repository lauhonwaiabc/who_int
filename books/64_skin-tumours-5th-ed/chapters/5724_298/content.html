5.7.2.4 Dermal clear cell sarcoma

Definition

Dermal clear cell sarcoma (CCS) is a malignant neoplasm of uncertain histogenesis displaying melanocytic differentiation and associated with a gene fusion involving EWSR1 and ATF1 or CREB1.

ICD-O coding

9044/3 Dermal clear cell sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2C35 & XH77N6 Cutaneous sarcoma & Clear cell sarcoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: melanoma of soft parts.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Dermal CCS predominantly affects the extremities, especially the distal extremities {20087159; 31688004}, but it can also occur elsewhere, including in the head and neck region.

Clinical features

Most superficial cutaneous (i.e. primary dermal) tumours manifest as small amelanotic nodules. They may be confused with melanocytic neoplasms, such as Spitz naevi {23228147}.

Epidemiology

CCS tends to affect young adults, most often during the third to fourth decade of life {14332545; 6614306; 20087159}. Very rarely the tumour may also occur in children {32324602} or older individuals.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The majority (70–90%) of CCSs harbour an EWSR1::ATF1 fusion secondary to the reciprocal translocation t(12;22)(q13;q12). The oncogenic fusion constitutively activates the promoter of MITF (microphthalmia-associated transcription factor), resulting in melanocytic differentiation and tumour growth. A variant translocation, t(2;22)(q32.3;q12), which results in an EWSR1::CREB1 fusion, is rare, accounting for approximately 5% of tumours {19561568; 17227118}. CCS may be associated with additional chromosomal aberrations, such as extra copies of chromosomes.

Macroscopic appearance

Gross examination typically reveals a firm, amelanotic, often white or cream-coloured nodule.

Histopathology

The tumour is characterized by fascicles and nests of usually amelanotic spindle and epithelioid cells. A few cells may display clear cytoplasm. Multinucleate wreath-type giant cells are often seen. The degree of nuclear atypia and number of mitoses are variable. The tumour often displays collagenous septa separating tumour cell aggregates. Most tumours have a grenz zone {20087159}. On rare occasion a tumour may involve both the dermis and the epidermis (compound CCS), often as junctional nests, and is therefore more likely to be confused with a primary cutaneous melanoma or a melanocytic naevus {20087159; 23228147; 23980901; 31688004}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells express S100, SOX10, and melanocyte differentiation antigens such as PMEL (gp100), TYRP1 (gp75), melan-A, and MITF {23228147}. In contrast to cutaneous melanomas, PRAME expression is usually absent or only focal {32681554; 34991102}.

Differential diagnosis

Dermal CCS may be confused with spitzoid melanocytic tumours, cellular blue naevus, primary nodular or metastatic melanoma, epithelioid sarcoma, or other sarcomas.

Cytology

Aspiration specimens show hypercellular aggregates of monomorphic amelanotic spindle and epithelioid cells, often with multinucleated cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of EWSR1 gene rearrangement and/or documentation of an EWSR1::ATF1 or EWSR1::CREB1 gene fusion may be helpful, in particular to differentiate CCS from malignant melanoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic nested or fascicular architecture; plump spindle or ovoid cells with palely eosinophilic cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli; spindle and epithelioid neoplasm with melanocytic differentiation confirmed by immunohistochemistry.

Desirable: documentation of an EWSR1 gene rearrangement and/or EWSR1::ATF1 or EWSR1::CREB1 fusion.

Staging

The eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system applies.

Prognosis and prediction

The tumour is clinically characterized by a propensity for local recurrences with late metastases. Patients with cutaneous CCS may have a more favourable outcome than those with deep soft tissue tumours. An analysis of 23 cutaneous CCSs showed an association with local recurrence in 46% (median relapse-free survival time: 15 months), while 2 patients died of disease, one at 38 months and the other at 60 months {32157275}. Small tumour size and negative sentinel lymph node status have been associated with longer disease-free survival {32157275}.
