5.3.2.6 Hobnail haemangioendotheliomas

Definition

Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma (PILA) and retiform haemangioendothelioma (RHE) are uncommon, rarely metastasizing vascular neoplasms composed at least in part of hobnail endothelial proliferations with a lymphatic endothelial phenotype.

ICD-O coding

9135/1 Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma

9136/1 Retiform haemangioendothelioma

ICD-11 coding

2F72.Y & XH4SY7 Other specified neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of skin & Endovascular papillary angioendothelioma

2B56.1 & XH64U8 Angiosarcoma of skin & Retiform haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: hobnail haemangioendothelioma (for RHE).

Not recommended: Dabska tumour; endovascular papillary angioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma; retiform haemangioendothelioma

Localization

PILA most often involves the proximal extremities, especially the buttock or thigh {10971050; 10478659; 10197456; 24063649; 29423171}. RHE usually occurs in the skin and subcutaneous tissue of the distal extremities, particularly the leg {21915027; 8291650; 8764941; 9432675; 16176302}.

Clinical features

PILA manifests as a slow-growing asymptomatic cutaneous induration, plaque, or nodule. RHE manifests as a red/bluish slow-growing plaque or nodule, usually < 30 mm.

Epidemiology

All are rare, with < 50 reported cases of PILA {5343389; 10478659} and RHE {21915027; 8291650; 8764941; 9432675; 16176302}. PILA typically occurs in infants, with a slight female predominance (M:F ratio: ~0.7:1). RHE usually affects young adults or children, without a sex predilection.

Etiology

The etiology is mostly unknown. Exceptional RHE cases occur in the setting of radiotherapy {8291650}, lymphoedema {8291650; 27730656}, or cystic lymphangioma {21915027}.

Pathogenesis

PILA pathogenesis is unknown. Subsets of RHE demonstrate YAP1 rearrangements, most often YAP1::MAML2 fusions {32991341}.

Macroscopic appearance

PILAs are poorly defined and usually involve the dermis and/or subcutaneous tissue {10478659}. Greyish-white to pink streaks or cystic change in otherwise normal skin may be present. Tumour size ranges from 10 to > 400 mm (mean size: 70 mm) {10478659}. RHEs present as a diffuse, sometimes discoloured induration of the dermis, with frequent subcutis involvement.

Histopathology

Cytologically, PILA and RHE are identical, being composed of darkly staining columnar or hobnail endothelial cells. PILA appears as a poorly delineated proliferation of slit-like or dilated lymphatic channels, sometimes with an associated cavernous lymphatic malformation, whereas RHE is typified by an infiltrative growth of elongated, branching vascular channels, resembling the rete testis. Associated hyaline fibrosis is often present. Both PILA and RHE may contain intraluminal papillary tufts with a fibrotic core, lined by hobnailed endothelial cells, which are more prominent in PILA {21915027}. A lymphocytic infiltrate is often present in both.

Immunohistochemistry

The hobnail endothelial cells of PILA and RHE express pan-endothelial markers (e.g. CD31, ERG, FLI1; less often CD34) and lymphatic endothelial markers (e.g. podoplanin, VEGFR3, and PROX1 {10478659; 10971050; 19674202; 22067331; 18212541}. Loss of expression of YAP1 C-terminus is seen in the tumours with YAP1 rearrangements {34148063}. Limited synaptophysin expression may also be seen in PILA and RHE, a finding which in combination with shared YAP1::MAML2 fusions supports a relationship between these hobnailed haemangioendotheliomas and composite haemangioendothelioma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: darkly staining hobnail endothelial cells, typically expressing lymphatic markers; slit-like and dilated lymphatic spaces in PILA; branching vascular structures with surrounding fibrosis in RHE.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

PILA and RHE frequently recur (as many as 60% of cases), but there is only a very low risk of lymph node or distant metastasis {5343389; 8291650; 19172034; 9432675}. Most patients have an excellent prognosis after complete excision; mortality is extremely rare {10478659; 10716148; 17895759}.
