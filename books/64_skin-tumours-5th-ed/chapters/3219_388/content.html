3.2.1.9 Trichoadenoma

Definition

Trichoadenoma is a benign neoplasm with infundibular and trichilemmal follicular differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8391/0 Trichoadenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In most cases the lesion is located on the face or the scalp {626013; 915052; 3551747; 6526647}, although examples have also been described on the back, buttock, thigh, and vulva {3562410; 28273976}.

Clinical features

Trichoadenoma usually appears as a small, slowly growing, solitary nodular lesion with a telangiectatic surface. Multiple milia-like cystic structures surrounded by thin linear vessels on an erythematous-yellowish background of poorly defined borders have been described as characteristic dermoscopic features of trichoadenoma {32671822}.

Epidemiology

Trichoadenoma may appear at any age from birth {6526647} to 80 years {9860019}. In 1 reported case the lesion was congenital {17939947}. The incidence seems similar in both sexes.

Etiology

The term “trichoadenoma” is misnomer, because it is not a glandular neoplasm but a neoplasm with mostly infundibular follicular differentiation. LRIG1-positive stem cells have been proposed as the cells of origin for trichoadenoma {26771241}.

Pathogenesis

One example of trichoadenoma has been studied by DNA flow cytophotometry, and a normal diploid DNA content was found in the tumour cells {8471097}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with a well-circumscribed dermal nodule.

Histopathology

Trichoadenoma appears as a well-circumscribed dermal nodule composed of epithelial and mesenchymal components. The epithelial component consists of numerous infundibulocystic structures, which may be connected to each other, but without connections to the epidermis. These cystic structures are small infundibular cysts, lined by squamous epithelium with a basal layer, an eosinophilic squamous layer of variable thickness, and a well-developed granular layer. The content of the cysts is basophilic and orthokeratotic keratin, similar to the keratin present in the infundibulum of hair follicles. Sometimes the epithelial lining of these cysts is similar to the epithelium of the sebaceous duct, resembling steatocystoma. Finally, some cystic structures of trichoadenoma may be lined by an eosinophilic epithelium with peripheral palisading on a basement membrane; the cyst wall cells show pale pink cytoplasm and keratinization without a previous granular layer resulting in eosinophilic compact orthokeratotic keratin, resembling tiny trichilemmal cysts. Some solid epithelial aggregates of infundibular epithelium are also present in most lesions. Foreign body granulomas are frequently observed in the vicinity of ruptured cystic structures. The stroma of trichoadenoma is much less abundant than the epithelial component and consists of a densely vascularized fibrohistiocytic component and fibrillary collagen. Stains for elastic tissue demonstrate abundant elastic fibres arranged concentrically around both the solid epithelial and the cystic components {915052}. Warty trichoadenoma shows a hyperkeratotic and papillomatous surface {2768595; 23984252}.

Immunohistochemistry

Scattered CK20-positive Merkel cells are detected in the epithelial aggregates of trichoadenoma {20115950}. Immunoprofile studies for cytokeratins support the hypothesis that trichoadenoma differentiates mainly towards the follicular infundibulum and follicular bulge of the outer root sheath {16225641}. Trichoadenoma in association with intradermal melanocytic naevus supports the notion that trichoadenoma and columnar trichoblastoma (formerly desmoplastic trichoepithelioma) represents the two ends of a spectrum of lesions {17284971}.

Differential diagnosis

With an inadequate and too-superficial biopsy, differential diagnosis with syringoma, columnar trichoblastoma (formerly desmoplastic trichoepithelioma), and microcystic adnexal carcinoma may be impossible, and a larger and deeper sample should be requested. Syringoma is composed of small ductal structures lined by two layers of epithelial cells embedded in a sclerotic stroma. Infundibular cysts are less numerous in syringoma than in trichoadenoma, and in difficult cases, immunostains for EMA and CEA demonstrate that ductal structures of syringoma express these two markers in the luminal border of the ducts, whereas infundibular cysts of trichoadenoma are negative. Columnar trichoblastoma (formerly desmoplastic trichoepithelioma) with abundant infundibular cysts may resemble trichoadenoma, but it is mostly composed of elongated thin cords of basaloid follicular germinative cells expressing PHLDA1 and a prominent desmoplastic stroma {22958125}. Microcystic adnexal carcinoma is a malignant and poorly circumscribed neoplasm that often extends to subcutaneous tissue. In superficial areas, the histopathological picture may be similar to that of trichoadenoma, because there are numerous small infundibular cysts, but microcystic adnexal carcinoma is also composed of small round solid aggregates of pale cells and elongated tubular structures in deeper areas. Perineural involvement is also frequently seen in these deeper areas of microcystic adnexal carcinoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed dermal nodule composed of tiny interconnected infundibular cysts.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Trichoadenoma shows a quite benign biological behaviour, and simple excision is curative.
