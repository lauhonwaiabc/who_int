1.1.1.2 Arsenical keratosis

Definition

Arsenical keratoses present as multiple hyperkeratotic lesions with a predilection for the hands and feet, following chronic exposure to arsenic.

ICD-O coding

8070/0 Arsenical keratosis

ICD-11 coding

EK90.Y & XH5NG4 Other discrete epidermal dysplasias & Arsenical keratosis

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Although the anatomical distribution is broad, the hands and feet (particularly the palms and soles) are most commonly affected.

Clinical features

Patients present with diffuse or localized hyperpigmentation and raindrop pigmentation affecting mainly the trunk, as well as yellowish hyperkeratotic papules and confluent verrucous plaques with a predilection for palmoplantar skin, measuring 2–10 mm and developing with a latency of decades after arsenic exposure {8125698; 23370535; 9169318; 29387754}.

Epidemiology

The incidence in high-income countries is very low; the disease occurs predominantly in poorer nations, due to contamination of the groundwater {29387754}.

Etiology

The main reasons for chronic arsenic exposure include environmental, medicinal, and occupational exposure.

Pathogenesis

Chronic arsenic exposure promotes oxidative stress, which leads to antioxidant depletion, immune dysfunction, genotoxicity, impaired DNA repair, and disrupted signal transduction {25173797}. A pathogenetic role of HPV has also been postulated {11028865}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Arsenical keratoses show a spectrum of morphology ranging from acanthosis, papillomatosis, and hyperkeratosis to squamous dysplasia, often with the dysplastic keratinocytes showing vacuolization, multinucleated forms, and atypical mitotic activity {21050621; 12426152; 23370535; 10397578; 29387754}. The squamous dysplasia does not affect the full-thickness epidermis.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: squamoproliferative skin lesions with or without squamous dysplasia in the setting of chronic arsenic exposure.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Arsenical keratoses continue to grow with time and may progress to squamous cell carcinoma, with potential for metastatic disease.
