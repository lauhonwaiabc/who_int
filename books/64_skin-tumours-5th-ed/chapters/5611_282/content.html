5.6.1.1 Neurofibroma

Definition

Neurofibroma is a benign tumour of axons, Schwann cells, fibroblasts, and perineurial cells in a collagenous matrix containing mast cells and mucin.

ICD-O coding

9540/0 Neurofibroma

9541/0 Neurofibroma, melanotic

9550/0 Neurofibroma, plexiform

9550/0 Diffuse neurofibroma

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Solitary neurofibroma; diffuse neurofibroma; plexiform neurofibroma; melanotic neurofibroma

Localization

Sporadic neurofibromas occur characteristically on the trunk; diffuse neurofibromas localize on the head and neck {6638406}.

Clinical features

Sporadic neurofibromas are solitary, slow-growing, painless, soft, skin-coloured papules or nodules. Subcutaneous (including diffuse) neurofibromas form nodules or deep plaques. Plexiform neurofibromas are congenital, irregular, tortuous, multinodular masses. Diffuse and plexiform neurofibromas usually affect children or young adults {6638406; 12789188; 3068997}.

Epidemiology

All demographic groups are affected, with no sex predilection and with peak incidence in the third decade of life [[Antonescu CR, Scheithauer BW, Woodruff JM. Tumors of the peripheral nervous system. Washington, DC: Armed Forces Institute of Pathology; 2013. (AFIP atlas of tumor pathology, series 4; fascicle 19).]].

Etiology

Neurofibromas are the most common peripheral nerve sheath tumours, with the majority occurring sporadically as solitary lesions. Less often, they occur as multiple or numerous tumours in individuals with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1). The diffuse cutaneous and plexiform tumours are presumably of congenital origin and, in NF1, the localized cutaneous and localized intraneural neurofibromas begin to appear in the second half of the first decade of life.

Pathogenesis

The originating cell and nosological classification are controversial {5354040; 7528474; 6798835; 9504678; 20233971}.

Most neurofibromas are sporadic and solitary. Multiple tumours or the plexiform and diffuse subtypes suggest neurofibromatoses (NF1 and NF2), autosomal dominant disorders caused by genetic alterations in the neurofibromin gene (NF1) on chromosome 17 or in NF2 (encoding merlin [NF2, schwannomin]) on chromosome 22. Many affected patients’ disease reflects new mutations. NF1 encodes neurofibromin (NF1), a negative regulator of the RAS/MAPK pathway. NF1 mutations cause loss of neurofibromin function, resulting in RAS signalling and MAPK activation. Germline plus somatic mutations (NF1−/−) cause loss of functional neurofibromin {22155606}. Detailed genotype–phenotype correlations have been documented {32771543}. Subsequent loss of CDKN2A on chromosome 9p21.3 eventuates in atypical neurofibromatous tumour of uncertain biological potential, which may progress to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) {31091306}.

Macroscopic appearance

Neurofibroma appears tan-white, homogeneous, and firm. Diffuse neurofibroma shows subcutaneous thickening. Plexiform neurofibroma expands large peripheral nerve segments. Diffuse and plexiform neurofibromas may be pigmented (melanotic neurofibroma) {10716146}.

Histopathology

Neurofibromas are unencapsulated tumours composed of spindle cells, collagen fibres, lymphocytes, and mast cells {2479359}; skin appendages are usually preserved. Neurofibromas are composed of spindle cells with thin, wavy, hyperchromatic nuclei and delicate, pale, eosinophilic cytoplasm. Several patterns have been described: interstitial mucin is prominent in myxoid neurofibroma {11558882; 10564384; 7528474}. Sclerotic neurofibroma has scant spindle cells in a collagenous matrix {16441412; 18496433}. Lipomatous neurofibroma has admixed adipocytes {12142613; 15769276; 12140442}.

Diffuse neurofibroma infiltrates dermis and subcutis, entrapping adnexa or adipocytes {7528474; 7695017; 12789188}.

Plexiform neurofibroma consists of tortuous nerves distended by axons, Schwann cells, and fibroblasts {3068997}; multivacuolated, mucin-filled cells express CD34 and GLUT1 {27816720}.

Melanotic neurofibroma contains variable melanin {3049734; 10716146}.

Immunohistochemistry

Some cells are positive for S100 and SOX10 {1693425; 7695017}, and for peripherin in thin axons {9414188}. EMA is negative {3169685} except in plexiform neurofibroma. A characteristic “fingerprint” pattern consists of CD34-positive perineurial fibroblasts anastomosing around Schwann cells {20849453; 21457155; 24033860}. Even non-pigmented diffuse neurofibromas may express melanocytic markers {23715161; 9414187}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes schwannoma, perineurioma, dermatofibroma, sclerotic fibroma, neurotized and sclerotic/desmoplastic melanocytic naevus, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, fibromatosis, and desmoplastic melanoma.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analyses do not have an established role in the diagnosis of neurofibroma. However, chromosomal copy-number profiling (showing CDKN2A/CDKN2B deletion) may be helpful for the evaluation of atypical neurofibroma / atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential (see the relevant section in the WHO classification of tumours of soft tissue and bone [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]]) and its differentiation from MPNST (which shows a complex, highly rearranged genome). Of note, conventional dermal and plexiform neurofibromas, atypical neurofibroma / atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential, and MPNST all exhibit distinct DNA methylation profiles {26857854}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an infiltrative, low-cellularity spindle cell neoplasm associated with a variably myxoid to collagenous stroma.

Desirable: S100 positivity in the Schwannian cell population and CD34 positivity in the stromal component.

Note: For diagnosis of NF1 and NF2, see the related sections in the Paediatric tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Paediatric tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 7). https://publications.iarc.who.int/608.]].

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Depending on location, solitary sporadic neurofibromas are usually asymptomatic; neurofibromas in neurofibromatosis may cause appreciable morbidity, owing to their number or location. An additional significant concern (especially in NF1) is transformation to MPNST. Although exceedingly rare in sporadic neurofibromas, MPNSTs reportedly arise in 8–29% of patients with NF1. NF1-associated MPNSTs have an earlier onset and reduced survival than sporadic MPNSTs {4621893; 12011145}.
