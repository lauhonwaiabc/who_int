2.8.0.3 Conjunctival melanoma

Definition

Conjunctival melanoma is an invasive malignant melanocytic neoplasm arising in the conjunctiva.

ICD-O coding

8720/3 Conjunctival melanoma

ICD-11 coding

2D00.0 Melanoma of conjunctiva

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Any part of the conjunctiva can be involved. The bulbar surface, near the limbus, is the most common site of occurrence {11074806}. Invasion of the cornea, eyelid, sclera, or orbit may occur in advanced lesions {20723990}.

Clinical features

Conjunctival melanomas vary in appearance, coloration, and size. Some lesions are amelanotic (pale or pink) {29777046}. Characteristics that favour a melanoma over a conjunctival naevus include tumour haemorrhage, large tumour base, tortuous feeder vessels, adherence to sclera, corneal invasion, multifocal lesions, and a lack of cysts {28125759}.

Epidemiology

Conjunctival melanoma accounts for 5% of ocular melanomas. The incidence is rising and is estimated at 0.3–0.8 cases per 1 million person-years {19628487; 16033743; 27009410}. Conjunctival melanoma is most common in older individuals (in the sixth or seventh decade of life) with northern European ancestry, and less common in Asian and Black populations {12407149; 31079055; 32215588; 18191091}. There is no sex predilection {19128387; 16226513; 12407149}.

Etiology

The increased occurrence in sun-exposed areas and the genetic profile of conjunctival melanoma suggest that ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure and cumulative sun damage (CSD) may play a role {33383577; 31647501; 33431815; 26657935}.

Pathogenesis

Most invasive melanomas (60–70%) have adjacent conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions. The remaining tumours arise de novo or from a naevus {3972395; 26425792; 20723990}.

Conjunctival melanomas are genetically diverse. Most show a high mutation burden {33383577; 31647501; 33431815; 26657935}. BRAF, NRAS, or NF1 mutations are present in most {16123397; 28938534; 31247083; 29332123; 33383577; 27009410; 24170549; 29559732; 34359736; 34071371}. TERT mutations are frequent {18980976; 23799844; 23633454; 21693616; 26425792; 34071371}. Other genes associated with conjunctival melanoma progression include CTNNB1, ACSS3, RET, TP53, KIT, TET2, CDKN2A, MAP2K1, RAC1, MET, and SF3B1 {34359736; 33383577; 35085662}. Most tumours show copy-number changes involving multiple chromosomes {30672666; 33383577}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The histological features are similar to those of invasive melanoma in the skin or mucosa {21128776}. The intraepithelial component can be nested, pagetoid, lentiginous, or (rarely) absent. It can extend well beyond the invasive component and can involve the skin and/or minor lacrimal glands and invade the cornea. The subepithelial component shows stromal invasion by nests or sheets of atypical melanocytes. Melanoma cells can show the entire spectrum of cytomorphology encountered in melanoma elsewhere, ranging from small naevoid cells to highly atypical pleomorphic melanocytes with spindle or epithelioid cytomorphology. Nuclei can be hyperchromatic with inconspicuous nucleoli or vesicular with prominent nucleoli. Ulceration, mitotic activity, angiotropism, satellite metastases, in-transit metastases, and neurotropism may be seen. A residual naevus may be present.

Immunohistochemistry

Conjunctival melanoma expresses melanocytic markers such as S100, SOX10, melan-A, MITF, and HMB45. Nuclear expression of PRAME and loss of p16 on immunohistochemistry are more common in conjunctival melanoma than in naevi {34268800; 31247083}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FISH, cytogenetics, and sequencing can be used to identify genetic aberrations consistent with melanoma. GNAQ or GNA11 mutations are indicative of blue naevus–like or uveal melanomas rather than conjunctival melanoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: severe cytological and architectural atypia, exceeding that expected in a naevus.

Desirable: mitoses, invasion of the cornea, extension into the skin, ulceration, tumour necrosis, pagetoid growth pattern; molecular features consistent with melanoma (PRAME positivity and loss of p16 on immunohistochemistry; chromosomal instability confirmed by FISH, TERT promoter mutations, etc.).

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM tumour staging applies.

Prognosis and prediction

The 10-year disease-specific mortality rate is 25–35% {8199108; 20723990}. The 5-year local recurrence rate is 29–45% {20723990; 12407149; 9122819}. Unfavourable prognostic indicators include location on the palpebral conjunctiva, multiple tumours, tumour size > 2 mm, epithelioid cytology, ulceration, mitoses {23818735}, lymphatic invasion {20723990; 12407149; 35245602}, angiotropic satellite or in-transit metastases {27020389}, and sentinel lymph node positivity {31425167}.
