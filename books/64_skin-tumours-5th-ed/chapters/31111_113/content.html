3.1.1.11 Myoepithelial tumour

Definition

Myoepithelial tumours are neoplasms composed exclusively of myoepithelial cells, similar to their soft tissue and salivary gland counterparts. Syncytial myoepithelioma is a distinctive cutaneous subtype. (Although considered soft tissue tumours, myoepitheliomas are morphologically similar to mixed tumours and are therefore discussed with appendageal tumours.)

ICD-O coding

8982/0 Myoepithelial tumour

8982/0 Syncytial myoepithelioma

8982/3 Myoepithelial carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH3CQ8 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Myoepithelioma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Syncytial myoepithelioma; myoepithelial carcinoma

Localization

Cutaneous myoepithelioma shows a wide anatomical distribution, with a predilection for the extremities {12753168; 14745720; 23588365}.

Clinical features

Patients with cutaneous myoepithelioma present with a painless, slow-growing papule or nodule {12753168; 14745720; 23588365; 33340147}.

Epidemiology

Myoepithelioma usually arises in adults, with a peak incidence in the third through fifth decades and an M:F ratio of 3:1 {10389622; 11224605; 14745720; 23588365}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

More than 80% of syncytial myoepitheliomas harbour an EWSR1::PBX3 fusion {23588365; 30834570; 31135487; 34854109}. EWSR1 rearrangements are less common in other cutaneous myoepitheliomas {21725291}. Cutaneous mixed tumours harbour PLAG1 rearrangements, as do a small subset of myoepitheliomas lacking ductal differentiation {22038920; 23630011}.

Macroscopic appearance

Cutaneous myoepitheliomas are well circumscribed and range from 3 to 27 mm (mean: 10 mm) {14745720; 23588365}.

Histopathology

Cutaneous myoepitheliomas are well circumscribed, occasionally with an epidermal collarette. Some tumours are lobulated and composed of a trabecular or nested arrangement of epithelioid, spindled, plasmacytoid, or clear cells, usually within a myxoid or hyalinized stroma, with areas of solid, sheet-like growth {12753168; 14745720}. Extension into the subcutis may be seen. Cutaneous syncytial myoepitheliomas (accounting for 50% of cases) are composed of sheets of histiocytoid to short spindle cells with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm; one third show adipocytic metaplasia, whereas chondroid differentiation is rare {23588365}. Myoepithelial carcinomas are exceptionally rare and often show marked nuclear atypia and a high mitotic rate; the minimal criteria for malignancy have not been established in the skin {12753168; 14745720; 23992447}. Cutaneous myoepitheliomas are consistently positive for EMA and S100. Expression of keratins, GFAP, SOX10, p63, and SMA is variable {10389622; 12753168; 14745720}. Syncytial myoepitheliomas are negative for keratins {23588365}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Rearrangements of EWSR1 can be detected.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobulated, trabecular, or nested architecture with variably epithelioid, spindled, plasmacytoid, or clear cells within a myxoid or hyalinized stroma; for syncytial myoepithelioma: sheets of histiocytoid or short spindle cells with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm; EMA and S100 positivity.

Desirable: positivity for EMA/keratins and for S100, SOX10, or GFAP.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Cutaneous myoepitheliomas are benign and rarely recur {10389622; 14745720; 23588365}. Myoepithelial carcinomas may metastasize and follow an aggressive course {23992447}.
