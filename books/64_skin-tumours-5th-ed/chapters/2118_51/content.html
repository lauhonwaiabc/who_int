2.1.1.8 WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus)

Definition

WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus) (DPMN) is a neoplasm of spindled and/or epithelioid melanocytes and melanophages, arranged in a wedge-shaped, fascicular or plexiform pattern that frequently extends into the deep reticular dermis. It is caused by the combined activation of the MAPK and WNT signalling pathways.

ICD-O coding

8720/1 WNT-activated deep penetrating / plexiform melanocytoma (naevus)

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH81Y1 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Deep penetrating naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: deep penetrating melanocytoma; plexiform spindle cell melanocytoma; deep penetrating naevus; plexiform spindle cell naevus.

Subtype(s)

Deep penetrating melanocytoma (naevus); plexiform spindle cell melanocytoma (naevus)

Localization

WNT-activated DPMNs most commonly involve the head and neck area, followed by the extremities and trunk {32509588; 2909196; 25175710}; they can also occur in the conjunctiva {32509588; 31558492; 1985078}.

Clinical features

WNT-activated DPMNs typically present as symmetrical, well-circumscribed, darkly pigmented papules {1985078; 2045075; 25300464} < 10 mm in size {2909196; 35184152}, or as pigmented papules within a pigmented macule.

Epidemiology

WNT-activated DPMNs are infrequent. They affect all age groups (mean age: 30 years) {32509588; 30756182; 25175710} and show a slight female predilection {32509588}.

Etiology

Mutations in melanocytes are associated with constitutional factors such as tanning ability, DNA repair capacity, and genetic susceptibility, as well as environmental factors such as sun exposure {20016485}.

Pathogenesis

Most WNT-activated DPMNs arise from common acquired naevi, through an additional mutation that activates the WNT pathway either through gain-of-function mutations in the gene encoding β-catenin (CTNNB1) or through loss-of-function mutations in APC {28935960}. However, a residual common naevus is not always found {35184152}. CTNNB1 mutations are most common (> 98%) and localized in exon 3 {28935960; 35184152}. The concomitant MAPK pathway mutations mostly affect BRAF (p.V600E) or MAP2K1, with some cases harbouring HRAS mutation or BRAF fusions {23599145; 28935960; 35184152}.

Melanomas arising in WNT-activated DPMNs often harbour additional genetic alterations, including TERT promoter mutations {28935960; 35184152; 34205480; 34013383} and inactivation of CDKN2A by mutations or deletions. IDH1 p.R132C mutations have been reported in some cases {34205480; 35184152; 32526042}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

WNT-activated DPMNs are dermal-based melanocytic tumours consisting of loose nests of slightly enlarged epithelioid to spindle-shaped, pigmented melanocytes with intercalated aggregates of melanophages {1985078; 2045075; 25300464}. The melanocytes are approximately twice the size of basal keratinocytes. The mitotic rate is ≤ 2 mitoses/mm2. The cells have greyish-brown, finely granular pigment {2909196; 21366455}. A junctional component is present in 60–85% of lesions. In the deep penetrating subtype, the architecture is wedge-shaped with frequently bulbous extension into the deep reticular dermis or subcutis. Fascicles often extend along adnexal and neurovascular structures {2909196; 21366455}. In the plexiform spindle cell subtype, the proliferation of neoplastic melanocytes is situated more superficially, with a fascicular–plexiform (candelabra-like) architecture {1985078; 2045075; 25300464}. A conventional naevus component is often present.

Immunohistochemistry

β-catenin, LEF1, and HMB45 expression is usually continuous from top to bottom (as opposed to the gradual decrease of immunoreactivity with increasing distance from the epidermis or adnexal structures in conventional naevi), with some neoplastic melanocytes showing nuclear expression {32520758; 35184152}. p16 immunoreactivity is preserved and PRAME is negative {35184152; 33094849}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes melanoma (both primary and metastatic), blue naevus, pigmented epithelioid melanocytoma, pigmented Spitz tumour, and combined naevi. Melanomas tend to be larger, and they lack symmetry and regular architecture, with more pleomorphism and > 2 mitoses/mm2. Molecular analysis may be needed for ambiguous lesions.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analysis can be performed to confirm the presence of a CTNNB1 or APC mutation and rule out the presence of additional mutations such as TERT promoter mutations or CDKN2A inactivation, or DNA copy-number changes typical of melanoma. The presence of more than two copy-number variations has been proposed to be indicative of melanoma {35184152}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an intradermal component composed of a nested or plexiform proliferation of large spindled or epithelioid melanocytes; intercalated melanophages; no evidence of malignancy; evidence of WNT pathway activation.

Desirable: absence of a gradient of β-catenin, LEF1, or HMB45 expression; a proven mutation in the WNT pathway.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

WNT-activated DPMNs are stable lesions that rarely progress {32509588}.
