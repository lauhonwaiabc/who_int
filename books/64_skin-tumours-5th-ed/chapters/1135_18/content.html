1.1.3.5 Large cell acanthoma

Definition

Large cell acanthomas are benign intraepidermal keratinocytic proliferations on a morphological spectrum with solar lentigines and early seborrhoeic keratoses.

ICD-O coding

8072/0 Large cell acanthoma

ICD-11 coding

2F21.Y & XH7AQ2 Other specified benign keratinocytic acanthomas & Large cell acanthoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Chronically sun-exposed skin is typically affected, with a predilection for the face and the arms.

Clinical features

Large cell acanthomas appear as well-circumscribed tan to brown macules, typically measuring < 10 mm {24917472; 12581141}.

Epidemiology

Fair-skinned, middle-aged to elderly adults are mainly affected {24917472; 12581141}.

Etiology

Large cell acanthomas are linked to chronic ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure.

Pathogenesis

There is considerable overlap with solar lentigo with respect to the expression of keratin and of antiapoptotic and proliferative markers {24917472}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Large cell acanthomas are sharply delineated from the adjacent normal epidermis and characterized by epidermal flattening and overlying basket-weave hyperkeratosis. They are composed of enlarged keratinocytes with nuclei up to twice the size of those of adjacent normal keratinocytes. The lesional keratinocytes contain abundant cytoplasm and the nuclear contours are smooth and regular. There is no nuclear pleomorphism, and mitotic figures are rare. Hyperpigmentation of the basal cell layer is frequently observed. There are often admixed or adjacent changes of solar lentigo.

Differential diagnosis

Actinic keratoses show squamous dysplasia affecting the lower and mid epidermal layers, often associated with overlying parakeratosis. Lentigo maligna is characterized by confluent growth of single junctional melanocytes, which may be highlighted by immunohistochemistry for melan-A or SOX10.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sharply demarcated intraepidermal keratinocytic proliferation showing mild acanthosis and containing enlarged keratinocytes, in the absence of squamous dysplasia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Large cell acanthomas are benign.
