5.8.0.11 PEComa

Definition

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumours (PEComas) are mesenchymal neoplasms composed of perivascular epithelioid cells (PECs) – distinctive epithelioid cells that are often closely associated with blood vessel walls and that express both melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

ICD-O coding

8714/1 PEComa

8714/3 PEComa, malignant

Related terminology

Not recommended: clear cell myomelanocytic tumour; clear cell sugar tumour.

Subtype(s)

PEComa, malignant

Localization

PEComas most often arise in somatic soft tissue or visceral sites. In skin, by far the most common site is the extremities (especially lower); uncommon locations include the trunk, head, or neck {15953380; 18277881; 15842631; 28556973; 17593087; 24124856}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with painless cutaneous nodules, papules, or plaques.

Epidemiology

Primary cutaneous PEComas are rare. Tumours most often arise in female patients and occur over a wide age range (15–81 years), with a peak in middle age {15953380; 18277881; 15842631; 28556973; 17593087}.

Etiology

Virtually all cutaneous PEComas are sporadic, in contrast to their soft tissue or visceral counterparts, of which a small subset are associated with tuberous sclerosis {11406657; 16327428; 18277881}. However, cutaneous cases of a recently described pattern (fibroma-like PEComa) associated with tuberous sclerosis have been described {31943330}.

Pathogenesis

Deletion of 16p, the location of the TSC2 gene, is a frequent finding in PEComa; loss of heterozygosity at the TSC2 locus occurs in both sporadic and syndromic tumours {18085521}. TP53 mutations have been identified in 63% of TSC2-mutated PEComas {25651471}. A small subset of PEComas harbour TFE3 gene fusions, which correlate with strong nuclear immunoreactivity for TFE3 {20871214}, but in skin, TFE3 rearrangements have not been described {23711163}.

Macroscopic appearance

Cutaneous PEComas are usually smaller than those arising at deep locations (range: 3–40 mm; most are < 20 mm) {18277881; 15842631; 28556973}. The tumours are well circumscribed, with a firm yellow or tan cut surface {15953380; 28556973}.

Histopathology

Tumours are dermal-based but often extend into the subcutis {18277881; 15842631; 28556973}. The edges are typically irregular and may show entrapment of dermal collagen. Most PEComas show a nested or trabecular architecture with a distinctive perivascular pattern of growth, with tumour cells radially arranged around vessels, replacing the vessel wall and approaching the endothelium, and are composed of epithelioid cells with granular, palely eosinophilic, or clear cytoplasm and round nuclei with small nucleoli. A mixed epithelioid and spindle cell morphology is less common {18277881}. Scattered multinucleated cells may be seen, and occasionally mild pleomorphism, but mitotic figures are usually scarce or absent {18277881; 28556973}. Almost all reported cases of cutaneous PEComa lack histological features of malignancy (mitotic activity, necrosis, and pleomorphism) {16327428; 24061520; 30001237}, with only rare case reports of malignant forms {24124856; 33908075}. The presence of “malignant” features should raise the possibility of a cutaneous metastasis rather than a true primary tumour. So-called angiomyolipomas of skin probably represent hamartomatous or lipomatous neoplasms with a smooth muscle component, rather than being part of the PEComa family of tumours.

Immunohistochemistry

PEComas characteristically express both melanocytic markers, such as HMB45 (the most sensitive immunomarker), melan-A, and MITF, and muscle markers, such as SMA, desmin, and caldesmon {19604538; 18223480; 24061520; 30001237; 11800647}. However, expression of smooth muscle markers is less common in primary cutaneous PEComas than in soft tissue counterparts, occurring in just over 50% of cases {18277881; 15842631}. Nuclear expression of S100 is uncommon and usually focal {16327428; 18277881}. Tumour cells lack expression of keratins.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis may include metastatic melanoma or metastatic renal cell carcinoma (there is often a strong and diffuse expression of CD10 by the tumour cells {28556973}).

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid cells with granular eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm; nested, trabecular, or sheet-like architecture with frequent perivascular orientation; variable coexpression of melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Virtually all cutaneous PEComas are benign and cured by simple excision. Given the rarity of primary cutaneous malignant PEComas, there are no data for prognostication, but at other sites malignant forms are typically large (> 50 mm) and pursue an aggressive clinical course {16327428; 24061520; 30001237}.
