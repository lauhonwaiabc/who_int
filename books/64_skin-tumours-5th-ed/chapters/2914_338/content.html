2.9.1.4 Dermal melanoma

Definition

Dermal melanoma is a primary melanoma limited to the dermis and subcutis, without intraepidermal involvement, regression, or ulceration.

ICD-O coding

8750/3 Dermal melanoma

ICD-11 coding

2C30.Y Other specified melanoma of skin

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is no specific localization {11074704}.

Clinical features

Dermal melanoma manifests as a solitary firm nodule of variable size, most often < 10 mm, with variable colour, ranging from flesh-coloured to red to blue {14732666}.

Epidemiology

Dermal melanomas are rare tumours, accounting for < 1% of all melanomas {11074704}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is not fully understood. Dermal melanomas with BRAF and NRAS driver mutations have a similar oncogenic progression to that of other melanomas with these anomalies {27846954; 32946880}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Dermal melanoma is a circumscribed dermal, or dermal to subcutaneous nodule composed of atypical melanocytes {18209168; 11074704}. Tumours may display areas of necrosis and haemorrhage {14732666}. Cytomorphology may be epithelioid, spindled, rhabdoid, or pleomorphic, and mitoses are common.

By definition, ulceration or significant regression, having possibly obliterated an intraepidermal component, should not be present {14732666; 18209168}. A precursor naevus excludes this diagnosis.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic melanocytes express melanocytic markers, including melan-A, SOX10, HMB45, and S100 {32946880}.

Differential diagnosis

Metastatic melanoma is typically associated with a history of melanoma, clinically suspected melanoma elsewhere, or multiple lesions of rapid growth. Local recurrence of melanoma typically shows an adjacent scar. A regressed intraepidermal component of a conventional melanoma is indicated by papillary dermal fibrosis, often with melanophages.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Mutations in GNAQ or GNA11 suggest melanoma arising in blue naevus. Fusions in relevant genes suggest mesenchymal tumours such as cutaneous clear cell sarcoma {20087159} or a cutaneous tumour with CRTC1::TRIM11 rearrangement.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a melanoma confined to the dermis and subcutis without an intraepidermal component, ulceration, or regression; absence of prior history of melanoma and exclusion of melanoma at another site.

Staging

Dermal melanoma is staged according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM criteria like other cutaneous melanomas {29028110}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is better than for metastatic melanoma and better than for other melanomas of similar thickness. However, locoregional recurrences, distant metastases, and deaths have been reported {11074704; 14732666; 18209168}.
