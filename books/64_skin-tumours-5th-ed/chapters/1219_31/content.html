1.2.1.9 Fibroepithelial basal cell carcinoma

Definition

Fibroepithelial BCC is a BCC composed of thin, interanastomosing strands of basaloid cells surrounded by abundant fibroblastic stroma.

ICD-O coding

8093/3 Fibroepithelial basal cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C32.Y & XH45F3 Other specified basal cell carcinoma of skin & Basal cell carcinoma, fibroepithelial

Related terminology

Not recommended: fibroepithelioma of Pinkus; Pinkus tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fibroepithelial BCC is most common on the trunk, especially the lateral hip and back, and may rarely occur multiply {27329375}.

Clinical features

Fibroepithelial BCC appears as an exophytic, flesh-coloured nodule, often resembling a fibroepithelial polyp, and it may be pigmented {27329375}. Dermoscopy shows fine arborizing vessels and white septal lines or streaks. Grey-brown structureless pigmentation and grey-blue dots may be seen in pigmented lesions {17043187}.

Epidemiology

Fibroepithelial BCC is rare, accounting for 0.2–1.4% of all BCC cases {7751091}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Controversy exists over whether this tumour may represent a trichoblastoma, because it occurs in locations uncommon for BCC, and it features horn cysts and fibroblast-rich stroma that are unusual in BCC. However, fibroepithelial BCC is uncommon on the head and neck but can be found on the foot, in contradistinction to trichoblastoma {27329375}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Fibroepithelial BCC shows multiple strands of basaloid epithelium with peripheral palisades emanating from the epidermis. The strands are surrounded by abundant fibroblastic, eosinophilic stroma.

Differential diagnosis

Eccrine syringofibroadenoma shows a similar growth pattern, but the tumour strands show ductal differentiation, and it is more often located on extremities {11495523}. Fibroepithelial BCC frequently shows retraction artefact and mucinous stroma, and stains for AR, distinguishing it from trichoblastoma {17284955}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multiple strands of basaloid epithelium with peripheral palisading arising from the epidermis, surrounded by fibroblastic stroma.

Staging

These tumours should be staged according to Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) guidance, depending on site.

Prognosis and prediction

This is considered to be a low-risk type of BCC for which standard excision is recommended.
