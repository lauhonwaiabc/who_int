4.3.1.3 Primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type

Definition

Primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type (PCLBCL-LT), is a primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) composed exclusively of centroblasts and immunoblasts, most commonly arising in the leg.

ICD-O coding

9680/3 Primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type

ICD-11 coding

2A81.A Primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type

Related terminology

Acceptable: primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These neoplasms preferentially affect the lower legs, but 10–25% of cases arise at other sites like the trunk or arms; presentation at the head is exceptional {15947086; 17353548; 16492713; 28479318}.

Clinical features

PCLBCL-LT manifests with red or bluish-red, often rapidly growing and ulcerating, localized or multiple skin tumours, most frequently affecting one or both (lower) legs {11504742; 15947086; 17353548; 16492713}. These lymphomas frequently disseminate to extracutaneous sites such as lymph nodes, CNS, lung, and other visceral organs.

Epidemiology

PCLBCL-LT accounts for 3–4% of all primary cutaneous lymphomas and 10–20% of primary cutaneous B-cell lymphomas {15692063; 30635287; 33131055}. It typically occurs in elderly patients, with a median age of about 75 years, and it is more common in women, with an M:F ratio of between 1:2 and 1:4 {8915307; 33131055}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

PCLBCL-LT is thought to arise from a germinal centre–experienced B cell with a high level of IGHV somatic hypermutation. Molecular features of the switch µ region indicate a class-switch recombination defect {28838616}. Chromosomal translocations involving IGH in 50%, MYC in 5–43%, and BCL6 in 23–50% are reported, and double-hit lymphomas with MYC and BCL6 translocations are observed in 4–19%; translocations of BCL2 are exceptional {15191563; 24030746; 28479318; 30113335; 31764222; 33548250}. The characteristic strong BCL2 expression of PCLBCL-LT despite it lacking BCL2 translocations might be explained by high-level amplifications of 18q21.31-q21.33, reported in 67% of cases, which includes BCL2 and MALT1 loci {16330669; 28479318}. Recurrent genetic abnormalities include loss of PRDM1 (BLIMP1), described in 15% of PCLBCL-LTs; loss of TNFAIP3 (A20), in 25%; and loss of CDKN2A and CDKN2B in chromosome 9p21, in 67–75%, this last of which correlates with adverse prognosis {16330669; 19020554; 28479318}.

Highly recurrent hotspot mutations in the adaptor molecule of the Toll-like receptor MYD88 (p.L265P) are found in approximately 70–75% of cases. PCLBCL-LT also exhibits recurrent mutations in CD79B, TNFAIP3 (A20), and CARD11 affecting the NF-κB and B-cell receptor signalling pathways, as well as frequent mutations in PIM1 (reported in 70%) {23863863; 22495176; 24030746; 25055137; 28479318; 29857068; 31150604}. Genetic alterations in immune evasion pathways, such as deletions of the MHC class I/II loci and the MHC regulator CIITA and translocations of the CD274 (PDL1) and PDCD1LG2 (PDL2) loci, have been described {28479318; 29857068}. The gene expression profile of PCLBCL-LT resembles activated B-cell–like DLBCL {15308563}. The molecular features of PCLBCL-LT share similarities with the genetic signatures recently described in C5/MCD activated B cell–like DLBCL {29641966; 29955182; 32289277} and resemble those of lymphomas arising in immunoprivileged sites such as primary DLBCL of the CNS and testis {24030746; 28479318; 29857068}. In contrast, gene expression profiles and the landscape of genetic alterations are completely different from those of primary cutaneous follicle centre lymphoma (PCFCL), which may also be largely composed of large cells {15308563; 27189828; 28684330; 35028710}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

PCLBCL-LT consists of diffuse monotonous, non-epidermotropic infiltrates and confluent sheets of centroblasts and immunoblasts {11504742; 8915307}. Mitotic figures are frequent.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic B cells are positive for pan–B-cell markers, including CD20 and CD79a. PCLBCL-LTs strongly express BCL2, IRF4 (MUM1), FOXP1, MYC, and cytoplasmic IgM with monotypic light chains. BCL6 is frequently expressed (at variable strengths), whereas CD10 remains negative in most cases {21700336; 14657713; 14726400; 14674895; 15947086; 20551823}. The proliferation index is high (Ki-67 > 40%).

Approximately 10% of cases deviate from this typical immunophenotype (e.g. showing BCL2 or IRF4 [MUM1] negativity and weak CD10 positivity) but can still be diagnosed as PCLBCL-LT if the other diagnostic features of the entity are present (see #32967Table 6.09) {15947086; 17353548; 30715765}. Small B cells and CD21+/CD35+ follicular dendritic cell meshworks are absent. Reactive T cells are relatively sparse and are often confined to perivascular areas.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis, especially in lymphomas at non-leg sites, is PCFCL with a diffuse growth pattern rich in large and/or spindled cells {30635287}. #32967Table 6.09 summarizes the main differing features between both entities. Very rare cases that do not meet the diagnostic criteria for either PCLBCL-LT or PCFCL might be diagnosed as PCLBCL-NOS.

Secondary cutaneous infiltration by systemic large B-cell lymphomas and Richter transformation must be excluded by staging in all cases. Rarely, the skin might be the first presentation of the blastoid subtype of mantle cell lymphoma mimicking PCLBCL-LT and should be excluded with cyclin D1 staining {26034867; 31219818}.

In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is recommended to exclude EBV-associated lymphoproliferations and lymphomas, including EBV-positive mucocutaneous ulcers and plasmablastic lymphomas, especially in patients with acquired, iatrogenic, or posttransplant immunodeficiencies {15166665; 14616390; 23718915; 24805861}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Most cases can be diagnosed without additional molecular tools. In cases with a difficult differential diagnosis with PCFCL, the presence of MYD88 and/or CD79B mutations favours PCLBCL-LT.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dermal and/or subcutaneous infiltration by sheets of large cells (centroblasts, immunoblasts, blastoid cells); mature B-cell phenotype; diffuse growth with absence of follicular dendritic cell meshworks; skin-confined disease.

Desirable: strong BCL2 expression; expression of IgM and IRF4 (MUM1); non–germinal-centre B-cell phenotype.

Staging

Staging should be performed according to the International Society for Cutaneous Lymphomas (ISCL) / European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) guidelines for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides {17339420}.

Prognosis and prediction

Some studies have reported a 5-year overall survival rate of approximately 50% {11504742; 17875875; 8915307}; however, more recent studies report a significantly better clinical outcome for patients when rituximab is added to multiagent (CHOP or CHOP-like) chemotherapy regimens {24647650; 24001373}. Involved field radiation might be added for localized initial disease or relapses {30039522}. Multiple skin lesions at diagnosis, inactivation of CDKN2A (by either deletion or promoter hypermethylation), and the presence of MYC translocations have been reported to be associated with an inferior prognosis {11504742; 16330669; 17353548; 17875875; 19020554; 30113335}.
