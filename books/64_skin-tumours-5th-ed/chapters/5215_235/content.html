5.2.1.5 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Definition

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour (PFHT) is a plexiform tumour within the dermis and subcutis, showing a biphasic morphology characterized by a nodular growth of histiocytoid cells and fascicles of myofibroblastic spindle cells.

ICD-O coding

8835/1 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH4GL1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is a marked predilection for the arms, particularly the forearms, followed by the lower limbs, trunk, and head and neck area {17870015; 10366148; 1723956; 2847569}.

Clinical features

The tumours present as slowly enlarging nodules and plaques, typically measuring about 20–30 mm {2847569}.

Epidemiology

Children, adolescents, and young adults are most commonly affected, with a reported median age ranging from 14.5 to 20 years (overall age range: 1–77 years). There may be a female predilection {17870015; 10366148; 1723956; 2847569}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

There are no known consistent, reproducible molecular signatures. Single case reports have documented cytogenetic findings that include 46,X,del(X)(q13)[3]/46,XX[23], 46,XY,t(4;15)(q21;q15), and 46,XY,-6,-8, del(4)(q25q31),del(20)(q11.2) with additional der (8) and (p22) {9973942; 2164878; 21261704}.

Macroscopic appearance

PFHT is a tan-white, firm, small to medium-sized cannonball-like or plaque-like tumour at the dermal to subcutaneous interface that appears to be entirely subcutaneous and rarely involves superficial skeletal muscle.

Histopathology

PFHT is a fibroblastic-fibrohistiocytic, dermal–subcutaneous interface tumour composed of small to medium-sized, cannonball-like nodules of whorling, histiocyte-rich foci with osteoclast-like giant cells and/or fascicular spindled myofibroblastic fascicles in the subcutis. PFHT has a plexiform to poorly circumscribed architecture. Three histological patterns are based on the predominant cell type: myofibroblastic, histiocytoid, and mixed {17870015}. Features of haemorrhage (to which the osteoclast-like giant cells are attracted), myxoid change, chronic inflammation (mostly in the pure myofibroblastic pattern), fat or adnexal trapping with perineural or peri-Pacinian corpuscle growth, stromal hyalinization, rare metaplastic bone, and pseudogranulomatous patterns may be observed. Mitotic activity is low, and cytological atypia, vascular invasion, and atypical mitoses are exceedingly rare {2847569; 1723956; 17870015; 17617005; 10366148}.

Immunohistochemistry

There is expression of SMA in the spindled myofibroblastic foci and CD68 in the histiocytic cannonballs with osteoclast-type giant cells. The tumours are generally negative for CD34, desmin, EMA, keratin, and S100.

Differential diagnosis

This tumour can be confused with other dermal or subcutaneous reactive or neoplastic processes, including granulomata, fibromatosis, lipofibromatosis, myofibromatosis, fibrous hamartoma of infancy, nodular fasciitis, giant cell tumour of soft parts, so-called plexiform myofibroblastoma (which may be identical to pure myofibroblastic PFHT) {34449590; 32618598}, and cellular neurothekeoma {17325474; 19342943; 17870015}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a fibrohistiocytic tumour in dermis and subcutis; plexiform growth pattern; fascicular fibroblastic and/or cannonball-like histiocytic proliferation; osteoclast-like giant cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

PFHT is a low-grade malignant tumour with indolent biological behaviour in most cases. Local recurrences are frequent (12–37.5%) {2847569; 10366148; 17870015}, but metastatic spread is distinctly uncommon, with locoregional lymph node metastases and distant metastases to the lungs observed in 2.4% and 1.9% of patients, respectively {30859648}. Death from disease is exceptional.
