4.4.1.6 Primary cutaneous CD8-positive aggressive epidermotropic cytotoxic T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Primary cutaneous CD8-positive aggressive epidermotropic cytotoxic T-cell lymphoma (PCAETL) is a neoplastic proliferation of T lymphocytes usually expressing CD8 along with other cytotoxic molecules, characterized by striking epidermal involvement, with epidermal necrosis, a high proliferative index, and aggressive clinical behaviour.

ICD-O coding

9709/3 Primary cutaneous CD8-positive aggressive epidermotropic cytotoxic T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

Not recommended: Berti lymphoma; Ketron–Goodman/disseminated pagetoid reticulosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Patients present with generalized or localized skin lesions (acral sites are more frequently involved). Mucosae may be involved {28128277; 35001425}.

Clinical features

PCAETL is characterized by roundish, localized, well-defined plaques and tumours or, more commonly, by generalized papules and nodules, showing central–peripheral erosive or necrotic evolution {10433941; 24438036; 28128277}. Localized cases rapidly develop more widespread disease. Ulcerated tumours appear at advanced stages, and the presence of a large ulcer may mimic pyoderma gangrenosum {19438529; 30259963; 34951938; 34477215}. Typically, PCAETL is not associated with immunosuppression. Differential diagnosis from cytotoxic primary cutaneous peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PCPTCL) NOS may be considered in cases with localized plaques and tumours. In some patients, lesions may be preceded by chronic poorly defined patches similar to those of mycosis fungoides {28128277}. Cases of CD8-positive PCAETL in transformed mycosis fungoides are also reported {10433941; 35253522}. PCAETL may disseminate to visceral sites (lung, CNS); superficial lymph nodes are usually spared {28128277}. Blood involvement is exceedingly rare.

Epidemiology

PCAETL is extremely rare (< 1% of cutaneous T-cell lymphomas [CTCLs]) and can occur at any age, but it occurs more commonly in adults and in males {10433941; 30635287; 24438036; 28128277}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Overactivation of JAK-2 through gene fusion events is observed in virtually all cases expressing CD8 (not detected in other cytotoxic CTCLs). In fusion-negative cases, gain-of-function mutations in JAK2, STAT3, and STAT5B and loss of negative regulators of the JAK/STAT pathway, particularly SH2B3, can be observed. Immunohistochemistry can be used to detect activation of the JAK/STAT pathway {30307677; 33792220; 34432866; 34882212}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Infiltrates are monomorphic to pleomorphic and are formed by atypical T lymphocytes. Tumour cells are medium-sized, with large pleomorphic lymphocytes often seen in tumoural lesions. Nuclei are often indented, with scant cytoplasm. In some cases, the infiltrate is composed of immunoblastic cells {10433941; 28128277}. Cytotoxic T cells show striking pagetoid epidermotropism {15692063; 10433941; 24438036; 28128277}, especially in cases with diffuse generalized lesions, and marked adnexotropism may be seen {24438036; 28391645; 33759218}. Spongiosis can lead to blister formation. In tumours, the infiltrate involves the whole dermis with a patchy or diffuse pattern. Histological signs of cytotoxicity (epidermal, adnexal, and dermal necrosis; haemorrhage; karyorrhexis) are common, while angiocentricity is rare {10433941; 22226429; 24438036; 28128277}. Rimming of subcutaneous fat spaces has been reported {22226429}.

Immunophenotype

Most cases show a CD3+, CD8+, CD4− phenotype, but CD8 may be very weak and occasionally negative {25792011}. βF1+ (TCRβ) expression with cytotoxic markers (TIA1, granzyme B, perforin) and a high Ki-67 proliferation index (> 75%) are expected. A significant subset of cases express CD45RA and lack CD2 and CD5, suggesting a naïve T-cell phenotype. TCRδ and EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) are negative; CD7 and CD56 are variably expressed. These cases tend to be CCR4− (~90%). Immunohistochemistry can also be used to detect activation of the JAK/STAT pathway with phosphorylated STAT3 and phosphorylated STAT5 {30307677; 33792220; 34432866}.

Differential diagnosis

Clinical correlation and extensive immunophenotypic studies (see #34538Table 6.17, and #38297Table 6.18) are necessary to differentiate this entity from other epidermotropic CD8-positive CTCL, such as mycosis fungoides, lymphomatoid papulosis type D, and the cytotoxic pattern of PCPTCL-NOS {24438036; 22226429}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The presence of clonal TR gene rearrangement and the detection of JAK2 gene fusions or mutations activating the JAK/STAT pathway may help in the diagnosis of PCAETL.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an epidermotropic-adnexotropic cutaneous diffuse infiltrate consisting of pleomorphic cytotoxic EBV− αβ T lymphocytes; ulcerated or erosive plaques and tumours without spontaneous resolution.

Desirable: CD8− or CD4−/CD8−; CD7+; CCR4−; demonstration of activating mutations or gene fusions of the JAK-2 pathway.

Staging

PCAETL is staged according to the TNM classification system for CTCL other than mycosis fungoides and Sézary syndrome {17540844}.

Prognosis and prediction

PCAETL has an aggressive course (median survival time: 12 months). No significant prognostic differences have been found between cases with small or large cell morphology and localized or diffuse lesions {28128277}.
