2.1.1.7 Recurrent naevus

Definition

Recurrent naevus is a benign melanocytic naevus that recurs after incomplete removal.

ICD-O coding

Code as type of naevus from initial biopsy.

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y Other specific types of melanocytic naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: persistent naevus; traumatized naevus.

Not recommended: pseudomelanoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Recurrent naevus may occur at any anatomical site. The most common location is the back, followed by the abdomen and extremities {21362017; 19270643}.

Clinical features

Recurrent naevus manifests as an area of pigmentation within a scar that can be uniformly pigmented or variegated causing clinical concern for melanoma {21732772; 1200664}. There is usually a history of a prior biopsy.

Epidemiology

Recurrent naevus predominantly affects women aged 20–30 years {3624565; 21362017; 21732772}.

Etiology

Recurrent naevus develops after incomplete removal of the original naevus. It may also occur after local trauma or cryotherapy. Most lesions recur within the first 6 months after biopsy, and recurrence after 24 months is seen only rarely {27774100; 21362017; 22176022}.

Pathogenesis

Recurrent naevus results from a proliferation of residual naevus cells {27774100; 21362017}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

There is an atypical junctional and oftentimes superficial dermal melanocytic proliferation overlying a scar, beneath which there often are morphologically bland naevus cells {21732772}. There is effacement of the rete ridge pattern and epidermal atrophy. The junctional component can be lentiginous with occasional pagetoid scatter and can have irregularly distributed nests. Melanocytes are epithelioid with mild to moderate cytological atypia. Occasionally the atypia is moderate to severe. Any dermal component is within the superficial portion of the scar and cytologically resembles the junctional component {3624565; 21362017; 22176022}. Mitoses are rare. The atypical melanocytic proliferation should be strictly confined to the area of scar, an important distinction from melanoma {19270643}. Review of the prior biopsy in diagnostically challenging cases is important to avoid misdiagnosis {1200664}.

Immunohistochemistry

The proliferation rate in recurrent naevi is low. Immunohistochemistry for tyrosinase or HMB45 antigen {11493377} shows decreased immunoreactivity in the deeper cells.

Differential diagnosis

The histological differential diagnosis includes melanoma {22176022}, dysplastic naevus, sclerosing naevus {18537860}, and dysplastic naevus with florid fibroplasia {21238828}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a benign melanocytic proliferation confined to a scar; history of prior biopsy or trauma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrent naevi are benign lesions.
