4.3.1.1 Primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma

Definition

Primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma (PCMZL) is an indolent cutaneous B-cell lymphoma composed of neoplastic small B cells, plasma cells, and a variable number of reactive T cells.

ICD-O coding

9699/3 Primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma

9699/3 Heavy chain class-switched subtype of primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma

9699/3 IgM-positive non–class-switched subtype of primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A85.2 Extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma, primary site skin

Related terminology

Acceptable: primary cutaneous marginal zone (MALT) lymphoma; primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoproliferative disorder.

Not recommended: immunocytoma; cutaneous follicular hyperplasia with monotypic plasma cells; primary cutaneous plasmacytoma.

Subtype(s)

Heavy chain class-switched subtype of primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma; IgM-positive non–class-switched subtype of primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma; immunoglobulin light chain amyloidoma (AL amyloidoma)

Localization

PCMZL is preferentially located on the trunk and upper arms {18569636; 16172311; 17353548}.

Clinical features

Patients with PCMZL present most frequently with non-ulcerated multifocal and sometimes solitary red or violaceous plaque(s) or nodule(s) {21107089; 18486274; 18569636; 16172311; 23796549}. The lesions may wax and wane and can undergo spontaneous regression {21219395; 27986434}. The development of anetoderma in spontaneously regressing lesions has been reported {26945704; 20157029; 27986434}. One study showed a high incidence of gastrointestinal disorders and autoimmune diseases in patients with PCMZL {24500411}.

Cases previously referred to as immunocytoma, cutaneous follicular hyperplasia with monotypic plasma cells, and primary cutaneous plasmacytoma are considered PCMZL {8406383; 15692063; 12028158; 7802133}.

Epidemiology

PCMZL accounts for 30–40% of all primary cutaneous B-cell lymphomas. PCMZL mostly affects adults in the fourth and fifth decades of life, with a male preponderance {16172311; 20462658}. It is the most common form of cutaneous B-cell lymphoma in children and adolescents {17353548; 18569636; 27501236; 24698939}.

Etiology

PCMZL is thought to develop from chronic antigenic stimulation by intradermally applied antigens such as tick bites (Borrelia sp.), tattoo pigments, and vaccines {16872479}. The association with Borrelia burgdorferi infection is found in endemic areas in Europe, but not in the USA or Asia {9331890; 10976703; 11737518; 18820662}. Furthermore, RNA sequences of HCV and antibodies against HCV were detected in patients with PCMZL {10354158; 12834485; 12358827}. Patients appear to have an increased frequency of various gastrointestinal disorders (reflux, gastric ulcers, positive Helicobacter serology, irritable bowel disease) and of several different autoimmune diseases {24500411} of uncertain significance.

Pathogenesis

Post–germinal-centre B lymphocytes of the marginal zone are considered to be the cells of origin. The t(14;18)(q32;q21)/IGH::MALT1 rearrangement has been identified in as many as one third of patients with PCMZL {12406890; 15356642; 16630178; 17519619}. IGH::BCL2 translocation or other translocations involving IGH, BCL10, MALT1, BCL2, and BIRC3 are absent or very rarely associated with PCMZL {16784987; 15682432}. Methylation of DAPK1 and CDKN2A (p16INK4a) is frequently observed {18820662}. Most cases of PCMZL lack MYD88 p.L265P mutations, but they can be observed in some of the non–class-switched (IgM-positive) cases {26099629; 27861728}. Recurrent FAS alterations were found in 63% of cases {29481902}. Somatic mutations have also recurrently been identified in SLAMF1, SPEN, and NCOR2 {29481902}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

PCMZL shows a dense dermal multinodular infiltrate of small lymphocytes, plasma cells, and follicles with reactive germinal centres {18569636; 20462658; 9888708; 27986434}. The plasma cells are typically located at the periphery of the infiltrates and are often arranged in clusters. The small B cells in most cases show a lymphoplasmacytoid morphology. Rare cases of PCMZL show extensive plasma cell differentiation, mimicking plasmacytoma. A predominance of monocytoid B cells and IgM+ should raise suspicion of a secondary cutaneous MALT lymphoma / marginal zone lymphoma {21107089}. Transformation to large B-cell lymphoma with sheets of large cells occurs very rarely {23190506}.

Two subtypes can be distinguished: (1) the more common heavy chain class-switched subtype, which is characterized by the expression of IgG, IgA, or IgE and a high number of T cells; and (2) the much less frequent IgM-positive, non–class-switched subtype of PCMZL, which has large sheets of B cells and usually has fewer T cells {21107089; 18687986; 31464711}.

The class-switched subtype is regarded by some authors as a clonal chronic cutaneous lymphoproliferative disorder {21107089; 27986434; 31464711}. The non–class-switched, IgM-positive subtype, which is much more similar to marginal zone lymphomas arising at other sites {32808967}.

It has been suggested that immunoglobulin light chain amyloidoma is also a subtype of PCMZL, characterized by prominent circumscribed (nodular) amyloid deposits accompanied by a moderate infiltrate of small T and B lymphocytes and monotypic plasma cells {28505007; 30839349; 30801660}.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic B cells express B-cell markers, including CD19, CD20 (except for plasma cells), and CD79a. They are positive for BCL2 but negative for CD5, CD10, BCL6, and cyclin D1 / SOX11. The reactive germinal centres contain BCL6-positive and BCL2-negative cells supported by networks of CD21-positive follicular dendritic cells. The plasma cells show monotypic expression of immunoglobulin light chains in most cases. Reactive T cells may be prominent. Clusters of CD123-positive plasmacytoid dendritic cells and a convergence of CD1a-positive dendritic cells around lymphoid nodules are typically found in the periphery of the infiltrates {19718787; 25384543}. A variably reported proportion (13–39%) of PCMZL cases with plasmacytic differentiation are IgG4-positive; this finding is not associated with signs of a systemic IgG4-related disorder {23765244; 28151793; 31464711}. There is expression of IgM and CXCR3; IRTA1 is more frequently positive in the non–class-switched subtype {34309899}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes other primary and secondary cutaneous B-cell lymphomas as well as B-cell leukaemias (see #33471Table 6.08). Differentiation of the common class-switched form of PCMZL from cutaneous B-cell–rich lymphoid proliferations (see section Reactive B-cell–rich lymphoid proliferations) is challenging because of overlapping clinical and pathological features. Reactive B-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation typically contains polytypic plasma cells, usually presents as a solitary nodule, and lacks recurrence (see section Reactive B-cell–rich lymphoid proliferations).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Gene rearrangement studies may be helpful to detect a B-cell clone that is found in 50–80% of PCMZL cases {18806482}. Occasionally, different clones can be detected at different sites and during disease evolution {21219395; 22588547}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infiltrates of small B cells (CD5−, CD10−, BCL2+, BCL6−) with lymphoplasmacytoid (or monocytoid) morphology; demonstration of monotypic plasma cells and/or clonal IG gene rearrangement; no evidence of extracutaneous disease at the time of diagnosis.

Desirable: lesions on trunk or arms; reactive lymphoid follicles in lesion; class switch: evaluation of IgG and IgM.

Staging

For PCMZL, TNM classification for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides and Sézary syndrome is applied {17339420}. In patients with the typical clinical presentation and no indication of extracutaneous disease on physical examination or blood tests, there is no need for further staging {21841159; 20026848}.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is favourable, with a 5-year disease-specific survival rate of > 98%. Recurrences are common. Extracutaneous spread rarely occurs (4% of all patients) and is more frequently observed in patients with longstanding multifocal disease, in the non–class-switched form and in PCMZL with transformation {21107089; 18486274; 23796549}.
