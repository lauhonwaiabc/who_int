5.6.1.6 Schwannoma

Definition

Schwannoma is a benign neoplasm composed entirely or nearly entirely of mature neoplastic Schwann cells.

2F24 & XH98Z3 Benign cutaneous neoplasms of neural or nerve sheath origin & Schwannoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: neurilemoma.

Subtype(s)

Plexiform schwannoma; epithelioid schwannoma; ancient schwannoma; cellular schwannoma; microcystic/reticular schwannoma; neuroblastoma-like schwannoma

Localization

Schwannomas occur in the soft tissues of the extremities, head and neck, and trunk. Subcutaneous localization is more common than dermal. Schwannomas of the skin are thought to arise from small peripheral nerves in subcutaneous soft tissue or, less commonly, in nerve twigs of the dermis.

Clinical features

Schwannomas are slow-growing and occasionally painful {5796779}. They are typically solitary, except in patients with neurofibromatosis type 2 (NF2) or schwannomatosis {19545378}.

Epidemiology

Sporadic schwannomas typically arise in the fourth to sixth decades of life {5796779}.

Etiology

The etiology of most schwannomas is unknown, although there are established associations with prior irradiation, NF2, and schwannomatosis {3173432; 12381708; 33588442}.

Pathogenesis

Conventional schwannomas typically harbour somatic loss-of-function mutations in NF2 and complete or partial loss of chromosome 22, including the wildtype NF2 allele {8889506}. Other recurrently mutated tumour suppressors include ARID1A/ARID1B (mutated in 29% of cases) and TSC1/TSC2 (in 15%). SH3PXD2A::HTRA1 fusions (found in 10% of cases) activate the MAPK pathway and are mutually exclusive with mutations in the receptor tyrosine kinase gene DDR1 (found in 11% of cases) {27723760}. Germline pathogenic mutations in NF2, LZTR1, SMARCB1, and DGCR8, all located on chromosome 22q, are associated with schwannoma susceptibility. Vestibular schwannomas are the hallmark of NF2. Schwannomatosis 1 and schwannomatosis 2 are due to germline mutations of SMARCB1 (INI1) and LZTR1, respectively {17357086; 24362817}. Schwannomas in these patients typically demonstrate loss of two tumour suppressors (NF2 and either LZTR1 or SMARCB1) {27921248}. DGCR8 point mutations are associated with familial multinodular goitre with schwannomatosis {31805011}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours vary in size from a few millimetres to > 80 mm in diameter. Smaller ones are usually white, fusiform, circumscribed, and encapsulated. Larger tumours are lobulated, with greyish-yellowish areas on sectioning, and may rarely contain cystic areas or haemorrhage {5796779}.

Histopathology

Antoni A areas containing spindle cells with wavy nuclei alternate with hypocellular Antoni B areas, which contain a myxoid matrix and a variable lymphohistiocytic infiltrate. Within the Antoni A areas, parallel rows of nuclei (Verocay bodies) may be seen. Mitoses are rare. In ancient schwannoma, there is degenerative nuclear atypia. Rare subtypes include plexiform, epithelioid, cellular, neuroblastoma-like, and microcystic/reticular schwannoma {3557324; 3096870; 28368924; 18520439; 8116794; 2493641; 26350054; 9508344}.

Immunohistochemistry

Schwannomas are strongly positive for S100 and SOX10 and have an EMA-positive perineurial capsule.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes neurofibroma, although this often has a lattice-like CD34 staining pattern not seen in schwannomas, as well as a smaller proportion of S100/SOX10-positive cells. It also includes solitary circumscribed neuroma. Epithelioid schwannoma should be distinguished from melanoma, myoepithelioma, and ossifying fibromyxoid tumour; roughly 40% of epithelioid schwannomas show loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression {28368924}. Cellular schwannomas with increased atypia and/or mitotic activity may raise concern for malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST). However, schwannomas typically show diffuse S100/SOX10 staining (unlike MPNST) and express p16 and H3 p.K27me3, which are lost in most MPNSTs {26990975}.

Cytology

Schwannomas often yield paucicellular material on cytological preparations. Aspirate smears show cohesive clusters of bland spindle cells with uniform spindled or wavy nuclei and thin cytoplasmic processes.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Chromosome 22q deletion and/or NF2 mutation are frequent but nonspecific alterations. Schwannomas have a distinct DNA methylation pattern, although in the vast majority of cases, molecular testing is not needed {33479225}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed spindle cell tumour with classic Antoni A and/or Antoni B regions.

Desirable: Verocay bodies, encapsulation, hyalinized blood vessels; diffuse S100 immunoreactivity; extensive SOX10 positivity; lack of a lattice-like CD34 staining pattern.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Schwannoma recurs infrequently; malignant transformation is extraordinarily rare {8067509; 11145248; 22082606}.
