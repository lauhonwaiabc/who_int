3.1.2.16 Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma

Definition

Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma is a rare, aggressive adnexal neoplasm preferentially affecting the eyelid and histopathologically resembling a metastatic lobular carcinoma of the breast and/or some adenocarcinomas arising in the gastrointestinal tract.

ICD-O coding

8490/3 Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH0XE5 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most tumours involve the eyelids. Rarely, identical neoplasms have been reported in the axilla {21317710}.

Clinical features

Patients with eyelid lesions present with a diffuse thickening of the involved lid, which is covered by normal or erythematous skin. Cases are often misinterpreted as inflammatory disorders, which can delay the correct diagnosis and worsen the prognosis. Extension into the adjacent eyelid results in a monocle appearance. A case with involvement of both eyes (a binocle presentation) has also been described {27391452}. The reported age range is 42–87 years, with a median patient age of 66 years {21317710}.

Epidemiology

Signet-ring cell / histiocytoid carcinoma is a rare neoplasm, with < 60 cases reported. The tumour mainly affects men, with an M:F ratio of about 4:1 {21317710}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Apocrine differentiation and an origin from the glands of Moll have been proposed, on the basis of the location and immunophenotype {22935888; 21317710}. Somatic mutations in CDH1, PIK3CA, NTRK3, CDKN1B, and TP53 were detected in several studied cases, but their diagnostic significance is to be confirmed in larger series {34804623; 32358805; 34156717}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not reported in detail

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of sheets or cords of single rows of cells arranged interstitially between collagen bundles. The cells have eccentric nuclei displaced by vacuolated cytoplasm (in the signet-ring cell pattern) or exhibit eosinophilic granular cytoplasm (in the histiocytoid pattern). Both cell types can be found in a single lesion, but one type usually predominates. Immunohistochemically, the neoplastic cells express high- and low-molecular-weight pancytokeratins, CK7, E-cadherin, CEA, EMA, GCDFP-15, and human milk fat globule {21317710}.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnoses are metastatic lobular carcinoma of the breast and adenocarcinomas with a signet-ring cell morphology originating from the gastrointestinal tract. Immunohistochemistry (i.e. lack of CK5/6, CDX2, and ERBB2 [HER2] expression) can be helpful in some cases, but careful clinicopathological correlation with a full clinical workup is mandatory to rule out a primary internal malignancy {21317710}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not clinically relevant unless tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy is contemplated.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: single rows of cells with eccentric nuclei displaced by vacuolated cytoplasm and/or with eosinophilic granular cytoplasm; exclusion of metastatic lobular carcinoma of the breast and exclusion of adenocarcinomas with a signet-ring cell morphology arising in the gastrointestinal tract.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

This is an aggressive neoplasm (often persisting after incomplete surgical excision) with the potential to involve the adjacent eyelid and orbit or for regional lymph node or distant metastasis. Axillary lesions appear to be less aggressive, with reports of metastasis only to the adjacent lymph nodes {9505264; 10027525; 21317710}.
