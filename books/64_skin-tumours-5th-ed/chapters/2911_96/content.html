2.9.1.1 Nodular melanoma

Definition

Nodular melanomas are primary melanomas in vertical growth phase (VGP) that lack an appreciable radial growth phase (RGP).

ICD-O coding

8721/3 Nodular melanoma

ICD-11 coding

2C30.1 Nodular melanoma, primary

Related terminology

Acceptable: rapidly growing melanoma (subset); primary melanoma without a radial growth phase.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The distribution in the skin is widespread and parallels that of other melanomas.

Clinical features

Nodular melanomas manifest as pigmented or unpigmented nodules. They often grow rapidly, can ulcerate and bleed, and can be mistaken for vascular or other neoplasms {26563931; 19207640}.

Epidemiology

Nodular melanoma may not be a distinct entity but rather a manifestation of an accelerated progression of melanomas of multiple pathogenetic pathways (see #33781Table 3.05, in section Genomic landscape of melanoma), including melanomas in skin with a low or high degree of cumulative sun damage (low- and high-CSD melanomas), acral melanoma, and mucosal melanoma {12622643}. Their epidemiology is therefore similar to that of melanomas in these pathways. An increased incidence in older men has been described {25142970}.

Etiology

Nodular melanomas have etiological factors similar to those of the melanomas in the respective pathway (see #33781Table 3.05, in section Genomic landscape of melanoma). Ultraviolet (UV) radiation–mediated mutagenesis is the major etiological factor in tumours on sun-exposed skin.

Pathogenesis

The concept of nodular melanomas propounds that these tumours progress to the VGP without an appreciable RGP; however, it is possible that any antecedent RGP could have been replaced or obliterated by the expanding nodule of VGP. A possible explanation is that nodular melanomas acquire the necessary genetic alterations in rapid succession or in altered sequential order, so that the barriers that prevent partially transformed tumours from developing a VGP are overrun before the proliferation-inducing alterations are acquired {27125352}.

Genetic profile

No specific genetic alterations, patterns of oncogenic mutations, or DNA copy-number changes distinguishing nodular melanomas from melanomas with an adjacent RGP of the same pathogenetic pathway (see #33781Table 3.05, in section Genomic landscape of melanoma) have been identified {16291983; 18532874}. The genetic landscapes of melanomas in these pathways are discussed in other sections of this volume.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Nodular melanoma may arise de novo or from a pre-existing naevus. Histologically, nodular melanomas appear as cohesive aggregates of often epithelioid or spindled neoplastic melanocytes in the dermis {6500548}. Any overlying epidermal involvement is by definition coterminous with the dermal component, and the flanking skin does not show regression {18209168}, as evidenced by a zone of fibrosis with pigment-containing macrophages and free melanin. An epidermal collarette often flanks the nodule {28881040}.

Immunohistochemistry

Markers of melanocytic lineage, such as melan-A, HMB45, tyrosinase, MITF, and SOX10, are typically expressed, as well as S100.

Differential diagnosis

Unpigmented tumours must be distinguished from squamous cell carcinomas, appendageal tumours, sarcomas, and cutaneous lymphomas by immunohistochemistry.

Melanoma metastases tend to have more mitoses or intravascular deposits of neoplastic melanocytes at the base.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The diagnostic molecular pathology considerations are similar to those of the primary categories of low- and high-CSD, acral, and mucosal melanomas. To differentiate primary melanomas from cutaneous metastases, molecular analyses can help assess the clonal relationship to any other melanomas of the patient.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a melanoma in VGP without a present or regressed RGP; no evidence of melanoma metastasis.

Desirable: immunohistochemical detection of melanocyte markers.

Staging

Nodular melanoma is staged according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM criteria like other cutaneous melanomas {29028110}.

Prognosis and prediction

Standard prognosticators, in particular ulceration, tumour thickness, Clark level of invasion, and mitotic count, have the same value as in other melanomas {2593166; 24258986} (see section Melanocytic neoplasms: Introduction).
