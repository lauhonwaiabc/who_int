5.7.2.2 Myxofibrosarcoma

Definition

Myxofibrosarcoma is a nodular spindle-to-pleomorphic sarcoma with wide variations in cellularity, nuclear atypia, and a myxoid stroma with curvilinear vasculature.

ICD-O coding

8811/3 Myxofibrosarcoma

8811/3 Epithelioid myxofibrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B53.0 Myxofibrosarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: myxoid malignant fibrous histiocytoma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid myxofibrosarcoma

Localization

Myxofibrosarcomas usually involve the extremities, especially the thigh. The trunk, head and neck, and acral sites are rare locations. Approximately half of the cases occur in dermal/subcutaneous tissues; the remainder involve deeper compartments {8604805; 15138937; 26759307; 29065228}.

Clinical features

Myxofibrosarcoma is an often slow-growing and painless tumour with frequent local recurrence. Low-grade cases may develop histological progression to higher grades upon recurrence, thus acquiring an increased risk of metastasis.

Epidemiology

Myxofibrosarcoma, with a slight male predominance, is a rather common sarcoma of elderly patients, occurring usually in the sixth to eighth decades of life; it is rare in patients aged < 30 years {8604805; 20878245; 26759307}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Myxofibrosarcomas harbour frequent chromosomal structure rearrangements and copy-number alterations, even in low-grade cases, and they exhibit increasing genomic complexity in recurrent lesions with histological progression {16415793; 20601955; 29100075; 30018380}. Myxofibrosarcomas are predominated by somatic copy-number alterations, where several oncogenes (TRIO, RICTOR, SKP2, and AMACR) on frequently gained 5p are variably coamplified and associated with increased grade {22322669; 25384383; 27577794}. In half of myxofibrosarcomas, the pathogenetic aberrations converge on p53 signalling and the G1/S cell-cycle checkpoint. Tumour suppressor mutations that inactivate TP53 (46%, most frequent), RB1 (18%), and/or CDKN2A/CDKN2B (16%) occur more frequently than oncogene amplifications of CDK6, CCND1, or MDM2 {30018380}. RB1-inactivated and TP53-inactivated myxofibrosarcomas critically rely on SKP2 for survival {32161142}, while amplifications of TRIO and RICTOR activate the RAC/PAK and AKT/mTOR pathways, respectively {27577794}.

Macroscopic appearance

Superficial myxofibrosarcomas are typically multinodular, variably gelatinous masses involving the subcutis and dermis. High-grade lesions may show necrosis.

Histopathology

As the histological grade increases, myxofibrosarcoma exhibits increased cellularity, pleomorphism, and proliferative activity but decreased myxoid stroma {8604805}; a minimum of myxoid stroma (optimally 5%) is required to distinguish undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas {26759307}. Subcutaneous lesions often have extensive infiltration beyond what can be clinically detected {15138937}. Low-grade myxofibrosarcoma is hypocellular, prominently myxoid, and composed of mildly atypical spindle-to-stellate cells featuring nuclear hyperchromasia and perivascular cellular condensation with some mucin-containing pseudolipoblasts {27591498}. The intermediate-grade lesions become more cellular and pleomorphic but lack the solid areas characteristic of high-grade cases. The latter often show necrosis, atypical mitoses, and overlap with undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. The rare epithelioid subtype typically shows cohesive clusters and/or sheets of epithelioid cells with abundant cytoplasm and vesicular nuclei, simulating carcinoma or melanoma {17197925}.

Immunohistochemistry

SMA and/or CD34 reactivity is occasionally seen in myxofibrosarcoma {23600956; 34326362}, whereas desmin and S100 are negative.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes superficial angiomyxoma, myxoid spindle cell lipoma, nerve sheath myxoma, myxoid dermatofibrosarcoma, low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, and other higher-grade sarcomas with a myxoid matrix.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a multinodular tumour with infiltrative margins; myxoid stroma; variably pleomorphic cells; curvilinear vessels; hypercellular areas in higher-grade lesions.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system is applicable.

Prognosis and prediction

The 5-year overall survival rate is about 77% {20878245}. Repeated local recurrences, occurring in 30–40%, are unrelated to histological grade, are usually associated with inadequate surgery, and are less common in the lower extremities {26759307}. Low-grade myxofibrosarcoma has a favourable prognosis, with minimal metastatic potential.

Intermediate-grade and high-grade myxofibrosarcomas metastasize in 15–23% of cases, usually to the lung, bone, or lymph nodes {20878245; 26759307}. Per cent myxoid component (inversely related to grade), size, and (potentially) CD34 expression are significant predictors of survival, while clear surgical margins mitigate local recurrences {20878245; 26759307; 34326362}. Epithelioid myxofibrosarcomas behave aggressively, with an increased metastatic risk (> 50%) {17197925}.
