4.4.3.2 Nodal T follicular helper cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type

Definition

Nodal T follicular helper cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type (nTFHL-AI), the prototypical and well-studied type of nodal T follicular cell lymphoma, is a neoplasm with the phenotype and gene expression signature of T follicular helper (TFH) cells, which commonly involves the skin. There is limited evidence regarding skin involvement in the less common follicular and NOS types of nodal TFH lymphoma.

ICD-O coding

9705/3 Nodal T follicular helper cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type

ICD-11 coding

2A90.9 Angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma; follicular helper T-cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type.

Not recommended: peripheral T-cell lymphoma; angioimmunoblastic lymphadenopathy with dysproteinaemia; immunoblastic lymphadenopathy; lymphogranulomatosis X.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

nTFHL-AI is primarily a lymph node disease, but it frequently involves the liver, spleen, and bone marrow {22869878}, with cutaneous manifestations in about 50% of cases {28945625}.

Clinical features

At presentation, patients have advanced disease, frequent B symptoms, and laboratory abnormalities suggesting immune dysregulation (e.g. polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia) {22869878}. Cutaneous manifestations include pruritic erythematous, a maculopapular rash involving the trunk and extremities, and (less commonly) nodules with or without necrosis/ulceration and petechial/purpuric papules {28945625; 30980412}, and may rarely be the initial presentation.

Epidemiology

nTFHL-AI affects middle-aged and elderly individuals, accounting for 36% of non-cutaneous T-cell lymphomas {26045291}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The lymphomagenesis is largely driven by stepwise acquisition of genetic changes: early TET2 and DNMT3A mutations in haematopoietic stem cells and late events such as RHOA p.G17V and IDH2 p.R172 specifically in neoplastic TFH cells {24413737; 26268241}. These mutations dysregulate the function of affected lymphocytes and their biological interactions, consequently promoting their expansion.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

There is a variably dense perivascular and/or periadnexal dermal infiltrate of small to medium-sized mildly atypical neoplastic T cells, which can be difficult to identify, being obscured by a reactive infiltrate {17592274}. There may be admixed reactive lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils and histiocytes, and/or granulomas. Vascular proliferation occurs infrequently and epidermotropism is rare {28945625; 30980412; 23046157}.

Immunophenotype

The CD4+, CD8− neoplastic T cells often express two or three TFH markers (PD1, ICOS > CXCL13, BCL6, CD10) {28945625; 30980412}.

In a minority, there may be scattered EBV+ large B cells, clonal large B cells (often EBV+), or plasmacytic (often EBV−) proliferations {28945625; 30980412; 25839892; 30240776; 22918164}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes inflammatory dermatoses. When neoplastic T-cell infiltrates are subtle, strong expression of PD1 favours lymphoma {28945625}.

Other cutaneous T-cell lymphomas / lymphoproliferative disorders may variably express TFH markers but usually are clinically distinguishable: primary cutaneous small/medium CD4-positive lymphoproliferative disorder; Sézary syndrome, which shows strong PD1 positivity; and mycosis fungoides, which may be TFH marker–positive, but epidermotropism is rare in nTFHL-AI compared to mycosis fungoides {22918164; 30635287; 33095875}. Adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma is frequently positive for PD1 and ICOS {17721918}. Another diagnostic consideration will include cases of primary cutaneous peripheral T-cell lymphoma with TFH phenotype {35895386}.

The differential diagnosis also includes B-cell lymphomas. Cutaneous secondary EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma {31599749; 29956714} and clonal plasma cell proliferations, which occur rarely in nTFHL-AI, may mimic primary diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma, respectively {28945625; 25839892; 31077877}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identical clonal TR gene rearrangement in skin and diagnostic lymph node biopsies confirms skin involvement, but in isolation does not exclude other cutaneous T-cell lymphomas or inflammatory dermatoses {25794369}. A lack of demonstrable T-cell clone does not exclude involvement.

Detection of RHOA p.G17V or IDH2 p.R172, if also present in the diagnostic lymph node biopsy, may be useful to confirm skin involvement {28945625}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: systemic disease and involvement of lymph nodes (see the related section in the Haematolymphoid tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]); mature T cells with a TFH phenotype; lymph node biopsy is recommended for primary diagnosis, because diagnostic criteria for lymph nodes may not be applicable to skin biopsies.

Desirable: detection of RHOA p.G17V or IDH2 p.R172 in lymph node and skin.

Staging

Staging is performed according to the Lugano modification of the Ann Arbor system {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

The 3-year overall survival rate is about 50% {34292324; 33496747} (for further details, see the related section in the Haematolymphoid tumours volume [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]).
