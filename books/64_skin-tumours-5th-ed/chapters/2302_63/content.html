2.3.0.2 Spitz naevus

Definition

Spitz naevi are neoplasms of large epithelioid and/or spindled melanocytes with a regular architecture. They usually harbour HRAS mutations or kinase fusions.

Localization

Spitz naevi can occur at any cutaneous site but are most common on the lower extremities {25308882}, face, and trunk {19318795; 880553}.

Clinical features

Spitz naevi typically arise as pink papules or nodules. They usually undergo a period of rapid growth lasting 3–6 months and then stabilize. Rarely, multiple or agminated (grouped) Spitz naevi can occur, sometimes within a pigmented patch as part of a naevus spilus {22300833}. Rarely, multiple Spitz naevi can develop in an eruptive fashion over the course of weeks or months {9666837}.

Epidemiology

Spitz naevi occur in people of all ethnic groups and constitute about 1% of all naevi excised from children, and 1–2% of melanocytic lesions removed from patients of any age {13160941}. Spitz naevi can occur at any age but have a predilection for younger individuals. In one study of 349 patients, the mean and median age was 21 years, with 40% of the patients aged < 15 years and 77% aged < 30 years {19318795}. There appears to be a female predominance {25308882; 19318795}.

Etiology

Spitz naevi are clonal proliferations of melanocytes, caused by somatic mutations.

Pathogenesis

Spitz naevi harbour initiating oncogenic mutations that activate the MAPK pathway. These include HRAS activating mutations, activating fusions of receptor tyrosine kinases (ROS1, ALK, NTRK1, NTRK2, NTRK3, MET, MERTK, RET), and activating fusions of kinases of BRAF, RAF1, or MAP3K8 {10980135; 23890088; 24445538; 26013381; 27477320; 30833747; 31719662}. In patients with eruptive Spitz naevi, the clonal relationship between the individual lesions indicates that the neoplastic melanocytes can disseminate without indication of malignant transformation {33004920}. Spitz naevi within a naevus spilus result from copy-number gains of the mutant HRAS allele in a background café-au-lait macule with only the HRAS mutation {23884457}.

Histopathology

Spitz naevi are usually symmetrical and sharply circumscribed {20179431}. They contain nests of large epithelioid cells, spindle cells, or both, extending from the epidermis into the reticular dermis, often in a wedge-shaped configuration {19318795; 880553; 367281}. The cells often have polyangular contours, abundant opaque or ground-glass cytoplasm with variable amounts of melanin, nuclei with delicately dispersed chromatin, occasional nuclear pleomorphism, and uniform nucleoli {880553; 367281}. The junctional nests of melanocytes often are separated from the surrounding epidermis or adjacent melanocytes by clefts. A hyperplastic epidermis and vertical orientation of junctional nests of spindled melanocytes contribute to a raining-down appearance. Any upward scatter of melanocytes in the epidermis is usually focal, sparsely cellular, and limited to the centre of the lesion and the lower half of the epidermis {7661280; 15370113}. Kamino bodies (eosinophilic hyaline globules) are often present {19318795}. There are multiple histological patterns of Spitz naevi with distinct features (angiomatous, desmoplastic, hyalinized, plexiform/fascicular, neuroid, pagetoid, pigmented spindle cell, recurrent/persistent, combined, tubular, myxoid) {24736667}. Histopathological features correlate, to some extent, with the initiating driver mutation {24698967; 27776007; 34761304; 33428338} (see #38596Table 3.08).

Melanocytes organized in junctional and/or superficial dermal nests often give way to smaller nests and eventually to single melanocytes deeper in the dermis, with a corresponding transition from larger to smaller cells with single-cell dispersion at the base of the lesion {19318795; 880553; 367281}. The mitotic count in Spitz naevus is low (usually ≤ 2 mitoses/mm2), but a higher mitotic count can be seen in the superficial dermis. Some Spitz naevi demonstrate rare mitoses in the deep dermis or at the deep edge (advancing front), but atypical mitoses are uncommon {19318795; 880553; 367281; 11902448}.

Immunohistochemistry

Spitz naevi express S100 and melanocyte markers such as SOX10, melan-A, tyrosinase, and MITF. A gradient of diminishing HMB45 and Ki-67 immunoreactivity from top to bottom and a low Ki-67 proliferation index (< 5–10%) are typical. Most Spitz naevi strongly express p16 and other senescence markers {23005921}, and coherent areas with complete loss of p16 expression suggest a diagnosis of Spitz melanocytoma (atypical Spitz tumour) or Spitz melanoma, rather than Spitz naevus {880553}.

Differential diagnosis

The most important differential diagnosis is melanoma. Suspicion for melanoma should be higher with patients aged > 40 years, lesions > 10 mm in diameter, the presence of ulceration, > 2 mitoses/mm2 {10086449}, and involvement of subcutaneous fat. Other important criteria are asymmetry, poor circumscription, increased cellularity, diffuse pagetoid spread throughout the entire height and/or width of the epidermis, diminished or absent maturation of the dermal component, angiotropism, lymphovascular invasion, and marked cytological atypia {21389834; 10333219; 26061100; 10086449; 880553}. BAP1-inactivated melanocytic melanocytomas lack epidermal hyperplasia, are usually epithelioid rather than spindled, show loss of BAP1 expression, and often include a common naevus component {22367297; 21874003}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analyses are usually not required to establish the diagnosis. Identification of a characteristic initiating mutation supports a Spitz lineage. Spitz naevi typically retain p16 expression in a diffuse or mosaic pattern. Homozygous CDKN2A deletion or pathogenic TERT alterations are absent. Most Spitz naevi do not demonstrate DNA copy-number aberrations. Exceptions are isolated copy-number gains of chromosome 11p in HRAS-mutated Spitz naevi {10980135} and focal copy-number aberrations on the chromosomes that harbour the genes participating in the kinase fusion of a given lesion.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed cutaneous or mucosal neoplasm, usually < 5–6 mm in diameter; enlarged epithelioid and/or spindled melanocytes with limited or no architectural aberration, cytological atypia, or mitoses (0–2 mitoses/mm2).

Desirable: histological features including symmetry, clefting around junctional nests, epidermal hyperplasia with a vertical (raining-down) appearance of melanocytes, maturation of dermal melanocytes, wedge-shaped silhouette, and absence of mitoses in the deeper component; confirmation of the melanocytic lineage with melanocytic markers such as melan-A or SOX10; retention of p16 expression.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Spitz naevi are benign tumours with a low risk of malignant transformation {367281; 30467833}.
