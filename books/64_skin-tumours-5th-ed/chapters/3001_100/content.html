3.0.0.1 Appendageal tumours: Introduction

Cutaneous adnexal tumours are a diverse and heterogeneous group of neoplasms that differentiate towards one or more of the skin appendages or recapitulate events occurring during embryo development. They include tumours with predominant apocrine, eccrine, follicular, sebaceous, and multilineage differentiation. Here, we consider entities that are site-specific and derive from specific anatomical structures, such as the glands of Moll and anogenital mammary-like glands. Despite the remarkable variability in histological appearance, there is considerable morphological overlap between entities, reflecting the marked plasticity of these neoplasms. Some adnexal tumours have extracutaneous counterparts, typically in the salivary glands and breast, but most lesions are unique to the skin. Benign tumours are far more common and can occur at any age {27914683}. Adnexal carcinomas, although rare, show increasing incidence, mainly because of ageing populations {26951748; 22119735; 23505050}. They are more frequent in men than in women, and in non-Hispanic White populations than in other ethnic groups {20566926}.

Because adnexal neoplasms may constitute cutaneous markers of an array of hereditary syndromes, their recognition is essential to alert the clinician to these conditions. Apart from the syndromic setting, genetic alterations have also been identified in sporadic lesions (#36565Table 4.01), but much is still unknown about the etiopathogenesis of adnexal tumours {35158743; 34064849; 34197659}. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure and immunosuppression are possible triggering factors {27067779}, and some neoplasms express putative follicular stem cell markers {20700038; 26914519}, suggesting development from pluripotent stem cells.

Cutaneous adnexal tumours occur as solitary lesions or with multiple papules, nodules, and plaques {26114606; 28256760}. Syndromic cases are usually multiple. The most common localization is the head and neck region. Some lesions have a distinctive clinical appearance (e.g. syringoma), making clinical diagnosis straightforward, but in most cases diagnosis is possible only on the basis of microscopic investigation. Carcinomas commonly arise de novo, but some have a benign component indicating progression from a pre-existing benign lesion. Certain lesions may be associated with skin adnexal tumours; for example, naevus sebaceus is a hamartoma in which trichoblastoma is the most common neoplasm to develop {24268309}. The diagnosis of cutaneous adnexal tumours is sometimes complicated by their ability to produce cysts or because of pigmented patterns. The growth rate and resulting symptoms can vary greatly, with lesions ranging from completely asymptomatic to very painful. Features such as irregular borders, colour variation, induration, and ulceration may suggest a malignant tumour {25154603}, but some benign neoplasms also exhibit these traits. Dermoscopy does not reveal pathognomonic features; the most commonly described presentation is an atypical or polymorphous vascular pattern {24080919}. Immunohistochemistry has limited value in the diagnosis but may be useful in certain situations. Because of these considerations, it is common to experience uncertainty in deciding whether a particular tumour is benign or malignant; in such cases, it is appropriate to give a descriptive diagnosis, such as “atypical skin adnexal tumour” or “skin adnexal tumour of uncertain malignant potential”, along with a differential diagnosis and a recommendation for complete excision and consideration of follow-up. For adnexal carcinomas, extensive clinical evaluation is necessary, to exclude metastasis from elsewhere.

Malignant skin appendageal tumours can be locally aggressive and can affect deep extradermal structures. They spread by local dissemination. Once lymphatic and/or blood vessels have been invaded, there may be involvement of regional lymph nodes or (in a minority of cases) primary haematogenous dissemination {23677677; 21931043}. A treatment algorithm for invasive cutaneous adnexal carcinomas has not yet been developed {24666868}. In most cases, adnexal carcinomas diagnosed at a localized stage and treated by wide surgical excision with clear margins have an indolent course, with an excellent prognosis. Conversely, patients with metastatic disease have a poor prognosis {20566926; 23505050}. The role of lymph node staging remains to be determined.
