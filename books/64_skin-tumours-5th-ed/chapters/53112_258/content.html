5.3.1.12 Lobular capillary haemangioma

Definition

Lobular capillary haemangioma is a common vascular cutaneous and mucosal neoplasm that consists of a proliferation of capillaries with a lobular architecture.

ICD-O coding

9131/0 Lobular capillary haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2F26 Lobular capillary haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: pyogenic granuloma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lesions have a predilection for the fingers and the head and neck, in particular the nasal and oral mucosae. Gingival lesions occur in pregnant women (granuloma gravidarum). Involvement of internal organs is rare {17922196}.

Clinical features

The lesion is a rapidly growing, polypoid, haemorrhagic, and frequently ulcerated blue or red nodule. Multiple lesions occasionally develop; in children, recurrences may be associated with multiple satellites {12423439; 3777375; 5647967}. Lesions can occur in association with port-wine stain and other vascular malformations. They are also associated with many medications and biological agents, including retinoids and BRAF inhibitors {22134277; 6225396; 27116335; 16958814}. Rare tumours arise in deep dermal / subcutaneous or intravenous locations {575269}. A congenital disseminated aggressive lobular capillary haemangioma with a predilection for the skin, brain, viscera, and musculoskeletal system has been described {32622671}.

Epidemiology

This haemangioma (including rare congenital cases) occurs over a wide age range, with no sex predilection {5220911; 10827405; 1792196}.

Etiology

Because of the prominent secondary inflammatory changes present in the tumour and the frequent association with trauma, lobular capillary haemangioma was previously considered to be a reactive inflammatory process. However, the notion that it is a neoplastic vascular proliferation is now favoured by many {7535775}.

Pathogenesis

Sporadic lobular capillary haemangiomas and those occurring within port-wine stain may be associated with RAS-family and BRAF mutations (mainly c.1799T>A p.V600E) {26802240}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The tumours are polypoid and have an epidermal collarette, and show frequent ulceration with prominent inflammation consisting of neutrophils, usually restricted to the superficial aspect of the lesion. Variably dilated capillaries lined by plump endothelial cells rimmed by a pericyte layer are arranged in lobules within oedematous stroma. Mitotic activity is common, and focal cytological atypia may be noted, in particular in mucosal lesions. Older lesions show variable fibrosis. Deep-seated and intravenous lesions are identical but lack inflammation {1792196; 5220911}.

Differential diagnosis

Nodular Kaposi sarcoma shows a similar architecture but consists of bundles of spindle-shaped cells with clefting, intracytoplasmic eosinophilic globules, and KSHV/HHV8 positivity. Bacillary angiomatosis (an infectious vascular proliferation caused mainly by Bartonella henselae, formerly called Rochalimaea henselae) is histologically similar, but the vascular channels are lined by pale epithelioid endothelial cells and aggregates of neutrophils, and nuclear dust and amorphous basophilic or amphophilic material consisting of Warthin–Starry stain–positive bacteria are identified.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a polypoid tumour with an epithelial collarette and lobules of capillaries associated with an oedematous stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Complete regression is not typical, and local recurrence occurs in approximately 10% of cases.
