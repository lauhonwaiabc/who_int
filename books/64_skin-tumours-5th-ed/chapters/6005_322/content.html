6.0.0.5 Carney complex

Definition

Carney complex (CNC) is an autosomal dominant tumour predisposition syndrome whose cardinal features include spotty skin pigmentation, naevi, myxomatosis, secretory endocrine tumours, and pigmented schwannomas.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

160980 Carney complex, type 1; CNC1

ICD-11 coding

Not available

Related terminology

Not recommended: NAME syndrome (naevi, atrial myxoma, myxoid neurofibromas, and ephelides); LAMB syndrome (lentigines, atrial myxoma, mucocutaneous myoma, and blue naevus).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CNC affects the skin, mucosa, endocrine glands (e.g. pituitary, thyroid, and adrenal), heart, gonads, peripheral nerves, and bone.

Clinical features

CNC is an indolent disease with symptoms and tumours accumulating over time. CNC is typically diagnosed in the patients’ early 20s {11549623; 27943004; 30428497}. Skin pigmentation and heart problems caused by myxomas are usually the first signs of the condition {4010501}. The characteristic spotty skin pigmentation develops on the lips, inner and outer corners of the eyes, and conjunctiva (membrane lining) of the eye, as well as around the genital area. Other common features of CNC are ACTH-independent Cushing syndrome (due to primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease) {6329005}, acromegaly, and multiple thyroid nodules. With increasing age, patients may develop CNC-associated tumours, including primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease, thyroid carcinomas (follicular or papillary) {18948764; 29239041}, gonadal tumours (large cell calcifying Sertoli cell tumour, ovarian cystadenoma or cystadenocarcinoma) {11095480; 11832717}, cutaneous myxomas {8116795}, melanocytic naevi {8772778}, psammomatous melanotic schwannomas {9711672}, osteochondromyxomas of the bone {11176065}, tubular adenomas, myxomas and myxoid fibroadenomas of the breast, intraductal mucinous tumours of the pancreas, and fibrolamellar hepatocellular carcinomas {29222914}.

Epidemiology

Fewer than 1000 cases have been described in the literature. There is an M:F ratio of 1:2.

Etiology

Germline inactivating genomic aberrations of PRKAR1A are identified in 62% of cases. Mutations in PDE8B or PDE11A and germline amplification of PRKACA are less common {25905341; 26130139}.

Pathogenesis

Inactivation of PRKAR1A is observed in the majority of CNC-related tumours {34359735; 34532875; 34780072}. It leads to increased activity of catalytic subunits of PKA (PKA Cα or PKA Cβ), key components of the cAMP signalling pathway, which regulates the proliferation of tissues susceptible to developing CNC-associated tumours {12424709}. Activation of PKA Cα due to PRKACA fusions or microinsertions was shown to have a similar effect in fibrolamellar carcinoma of the liver and cardiac myxoma {29222914; 28369983} and suggests that aberrations involving components of the cAMP signalling pathway other than PRKAR1A may be responsible PRKAR1A-wildtype CNC-associated tumours. PRKAR1A inactivation is usually superimposed on other background genetic abnormalities specific for the individual tumours {34359735}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours in CNC have a macroscopic appearance similar to those of their sporadic counterparts.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CNC can be diagnosed by the detection of pathogenic germline mutation of PRKAR1A or (less commonly) of PDE8B or PDE11A. Molecular diagnosis also includes gene copy testing for PRKACA or PRKACB {26130139}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

At least two major criteria, confirmed by laboratory testing, histopathology, or imaging, must be present to establish the diagnosis of CNC. In the presence of one supplemental criterion, a single clinical manifestation is sufficient to establish the diagnosis.

Major diagnostic criteria:
Typical skin pigmentation (lentiginosis, or multiple blue or epithelioid blue naevi) Myxomas (cutaneous, mucosal, breast, or cardiac) Specific endocrine tumours or endocrine hyperactivity (primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease, pituitary neuroendocrine tumour / pituitary adenoma leading to acromegaly or evidence of acromegaly, large cell calcifying Sertoli cell tumour or characteristic calcification on ultrasound, thyroid cancer or multiple thyroid nodules in young patients) Any of the other rare tumours, which include pigmented melanotic schwannoma, osteochondromyxoma, and breast adenomas
Supplemental criteria:
An affected first-degree relative Pathogenic inactivating germline PRKAR1A mutation
Desirable (in patients with clinicopathological diagnosis of CNC):
Germline pathogenic PRKAR1A sequence variants Germline pathogenic PDE8B or PDE11A sequence variants in PRKAR1A-wildtype cases Gene copy testing for PRKACA or PRKACB in PRKAR1A-wildtype cases

Staging

Individual malignant tumours are staged according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

With the exception of pigmented melanotic schwannoma, which often metastasizes and can be lethal, the tumours associated with CNC are usually benign and slow-growing. Cardiac myxomas, although histologically benign, can cause catastrophic cardiac problems due to their location and/or complications from the open heart surgery necessary to treat them.
