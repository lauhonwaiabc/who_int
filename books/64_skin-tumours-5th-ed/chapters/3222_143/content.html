3.2.2.2 Proliferating trichilemmal tumour

Definition

Proliferating trichilemmal tumour (PTT) is a solid-cystic neoplasm with differentiation towards the isthmic part of the outer root sheath. PTT encompasses a morphological spectrum including benign, atypical (intermediate), and rare malignant lesions {15487455}.

ICD-O coding

8103/1 Proliferating trichilemmal tumour

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH7WJ7 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Proliferating trichilemmal cyst

Related terminology

Acceptable: proliferating tricholemmal tumour; proliferating pilar tumour.

Not recommended: proliferating trichilemmal cyst; proliferating trichilemmal cystic carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

More than 90% of cases occur on the scalp. Rare cases have been described in the perianal region {30408740}.

Clinical features

PTT usually manifests as a solitary, slow-growing, exophytic lesion, 20–250 mm in size. Some lesions grow rapidly and may develop ulceration. In 1 reported case, a tumour developed in a pre-existent naevus sebaceus {28419495}.

Epidemiology

PTT most commonly occurs in women aged > 40 years.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Some cases develop from a pre-existing trichilemmal cyst. Malignant PTT can develop in patients with multiple trichilemmal cysts {35310136}.

Pathogenesis

Aneuploidy has been demonstrated in some cases by flow cytometry {7817671}. TP53 mutations and loss of heterozygosity at 17p have been described in the malignant areas of malignant PTT {9490283}. CTNNB1 mutations do not seem to play a role in the histogenesis of the neoplasm {19384065}.

Macroscopic appearance

PTT is a large, often multinodular, solid-cystic tumour.

Histopathology

PTT displays a morphological continuum {15487455}. At one end of the spectrum, it appears as a well-circumscribed dermal solid and cystic neoplasm, sometimes involving the subcutaneous tissue. In addition to the typical features of a trichilemmal cyst, the tumour is characterized by prominent epithelial infoldings into the cystic cavity. Small, basaloid cells displaying peripheral palisading are arranged along a thick vitreous membrane, and inner layers of the neoplasm show differentiation towards large monomorphic keratinocytes with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, lacking significant atypia, with low mitotic activity and abrupt keratinization in the absence of a granular layer {3688371; 10564395}. Areas of calcification and abundant cholesterol crystals are frequently seen within the compact eosinophilic keratin. The other end of the morphological spectrum is represented by tumours with malignant features including infiltrative growth extending beyond the confines of the cyst wall, nuclear pleomorphism, and high mitotic activity. These areas may be indistinguishable from squamous cell carcinoma (SCC).

Differential diagnosis

The histopathological differential diagnosis of PTT includes ruptured trichilemmal cyst, giant trichilemmal horn, SCC, and the proliferating pattern of epidermal (infundibular) cyst. Ruptured trichilemmal cysts may show irregular aggregates of keratinocytes with trichilemmal keratinization, but they lack the solid multilobular structure of PTT. Giant cutaneous trichilemmal horn consists of a mixture of squamous cells and trichilemmal keratinized debris and in some cases is connected at its base to a trichilemmal cyst. Mitotic activity is frequent, and cytological atypia is lacking {12023585}. Uncommonly, SCC may show a cystic configuration, but nuclear atypia is more marked than in PTT. SCC shows no evidence of trichilemmal differentiation. The proliferating pattern of epidermal (infundibular) cyst {8594071} consists of cystic structures lined by infundibular epithelium associated with basket-weave, basophilic, and orthokeratotic keratin.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a solid-cystic neoplasm with prominent epithelial infoldings into the cystic cavity, composed of irregular aggregates of keratinocytes with trichilemmal keratinization.

Staging

The eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM staging system applies for cases with metastases.

Prognosis and prediction

In most reported cases, the behaviour is benign. In the rare instances of malignant PTT, distant metastases have been reported {12142616; 11474259; 9335249; 7722047; 34797787}.
