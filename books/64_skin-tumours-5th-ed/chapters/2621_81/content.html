2.6.2.1 Melanoma arising in blue naevus

Definition

Melanoma arising in blue naevus (MBN) is a melanoma occurring in a pre-existing blue naevus, usually a cellular blue naevus.

ICD-O coding

8780/3 Melanoma arising in blue naevus

ICD-11 coding

2C30.Y & XH1G74 Other specified melanoma of skin & Blue naevus, malignant

Related terminology

Acceptable: blue naevus–like melanoma; melanoma ex blue naevus; melanoma mimicking cellular blue naevus.

Not recommended: malignant blue naevus.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The scalp is the most common site, followed by the face, buttocks, back, and chest {22790865}.

Clinical features

MBN is a fast-growing, large nodule, usually in a longstanding blue naevus {22790865}.

Epidemiology

MBN is rare. It can occur across a wide age range (5–73 years), most often in adults aged > 45 years, and shows no sex predilection {19472395; 24743221; 33053549}.

Etiology

Although sun exposure is a well-known factor in the development of other types of melanocytic naevi, its role in the development of blue naevi and neoplasms evolving therein is less clear.

Pathogenesis

MBNs evolve through genetic progression from blue naevi and related intradermal melanocytic proliferations, caused by somatic mutations of the Gqα signalling pathway typically involving GNAQ, GNA11, PLCB4, or CYSLTR2 {21083380; 26403583}. Malignant progression is associated with mutations involving SF3B1, EIF1AX, or BAP1 {32118628}, as well as chromosomal aberrations also seen in uveal melanoma, with gains in 6p or 8q, monosomy 3, and loss of 1p {26743478; 28409567}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

MBN is an intradermal proliferation composed of dense dermal sheets of large spindled and epithelioid melanocytes with severe atypia and high mitotic activity, often effacing adnexal structures. Pigmentation is variable, sometimes only found within a subclone. Central areas of tumoural necrosis are frequent in large tumours. A residuum of blue naevus can usually be found at the periphery {19472395}.

Immunohistochemistry

The Ki-67 proliferation index is often > 20%. When nuclear BAP1 staining is lost in the melanoma, the loss typically affects large contiguous populations of neoplastic cells but is retained in any naevus remnant {26645730}.

Differential diagnosis

Cellular blue naevi have less pleomorphism, lack necrosis en masse, and retain expression of BAP1. Immunohistochemistry for BRAF p.V600E oncoprotein is often positive in metastatic melanoma and local recurrences. WNT-activated and pigmented epithelioid melanocytomas should be excluded, and clear cell sarcoma in unpigmented cases must be considered in less typical presentations.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

MBNs harbour GNAQ, GNA11, PLCB4, or CYSLTR2 mutations, rather than BRAF or other mutations associated with other melanoma types {21083380; 26403583}. Mutations of SF3B1, EIF1AX, or BAP1 can help support the diagnosis of malignant transformation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an intradermal melanoma; exclusion of metastases or local recurrences of other melanoma types.

Desirable: a pre-existing blue naevus in the adjacent skin; loss of BAP1 expression in the melanoma cells; GNAQ, GNA11, PLCB4, or CYSLTR2 mutation.

Staging

MBN is staged according to the current Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification for cutaneous melanoma.

Prognosis and prediction

MBNs are often aggressive, with frequent metastasis to the regional lymph nodes and liver; other common metastatic sites can also be affected. Monosomy 3 and BAP1 inactivation have been proposed as indicators of a less favourable outcome {26645730}.
