3.2.1.3 Trichilemmoma

Definition

Trichilemmoma is a benign epithelial proliferation associated with the hair follicle infundibulum and showing trichilemmal differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8102/0 Trichilemmoma

8102/0 Desmoplastic trichilemmoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH5AU2 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Trichilemmoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Desmoplastic trichilemmoma

Localization

Lesions occur mostly on the head and neck, with a predilection for the centrofacial area {4711118}.

Clinical features

Patients with trichilemmoma present with a (usually) solitary, small (3–8 mm in diameter), hemispherical papule with a smooth or verrucous surface. Trichilemmoma frequently develops in a pre-existing naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn {9790113; 10770429; 19652586}. Multiple trichilemmomas may be associated with Cowden syndrome {6512057; 6512062}.

Epidemiology

Most trichilemmomas arise in middle-aged to elderly adults {4711118}, with a slight male predilection. Multiple lesions, especially if there is involvement of the oral mucosa, can be a cutaneous marker of Cowden syndrome {465314; 657103; 24136893}.

Etiology

Some PCR studies have demonstrated HPV DNA in both solitary and multiple lesions in patients with Cowden syndrome {9021542; 12920361}. Cowden syndrome is caused by germline mutations in PTEN, a tumour suppression gene located on chromosome 10q23 {465314; 24136893}.

Pathogenesis

Solitary trichilemmomas have no genetic associations. Multiple trichilemmomas are a major diagnostic criterion for Cowden syndrome, which is part of the PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome spectrum {465314; 24136893}. The high frequency of HRAS activating mutations in trichilemmomas, including the HRAS c.182A>G substitution, suggests that these lesions are true neoplasms {24890286}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with a small papular lesion with a smooth or verrucous surface.

Histopathology

Most lesions are vertically oriented, show an exophytic and endophytic component, and are usually associated with one or more pre-existing hair follicles. Prominent digitated to bulbous thickening of the follicular infundibulum is seen, with superficial hypergranulosis and hyperkeratosis. A collarette of adnexal epithelium often surrounds the lesion {4711118}. The main bulk of the lesion is composed of lobules of monomorphic epithelial cells with pale eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm (the latter due to abundant glycogen) and round, vesicular, and eccentrically located nuclei, reminiscent of cells of the outer root sheath (trichilemma) of the lower segment of hair follicle. Frequently, the cells in the periphery display palisading and rest on a thick basement membrane similar to the vitreous membrane of normal hair follicles. Central foci of infundibular keratinization, squamous eddies, and droplets of basement membrane material are occasionally found.

In desmoplastic trichilemmoma there is sclerotic collagen and an increased deposition of basement membrane–like material, in a desmoplastic-looking stroma associated with small, irregular epithelial strands of pale cells that may be mistaken for invasive carcinoma {2319039; 1373583}.

Immunohistochemistry

PTEN expression may be lost, more commonly in lesions from patients with Cowden syndrome {22486434}. Both conventional and desmoplastic trichilemmomas commonly express p16 and CD34 {19461237; 18976402}.

Differential diagnosis

The histopathological differential diagnosis of trichilemmoma includes other clear cell proliferations, especially the clear cell subtype of apocrine hidradenoma. In contrast with trichilemmoma, clear cell hidradenoma is a mostly solid neoplasm, which often occupies the entire thickness of the dermis with little or no contact with the epidermis. Inside the aggregates of clear cells of clear cell hidradenoma, there is evidence of ductal differentiation. Often, mucinous cells are observed in some of these ductal structures, forming part of the layer of luminal cells. In general, in clear cell hidradenoma there is no peripheral palisading, and the peripheral cells are not apposed on a thick basement membrane as in trichilemmomas.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant in most cases

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a solitary, vertically oriented benign tumour with an exophytic and an endophytic component; hair follicle–associated lobules of clear cells with peripheral palisading surrounded by a thick basement membrane.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Trichilemmoma is a benign lesion.
