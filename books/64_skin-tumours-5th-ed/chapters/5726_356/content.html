5.7.2.6 Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma is a rarely metastasizing endothelial neoplasm that often presents as multiple nodules in different tissue planes and histologically mimics a myoid tumour or epithelioid sarcoma.

ICD-O coding

9138/1 Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.OZ & XH26F6 Neoplastic haemangioma, unspecified & Pseudomyogenic (epithelioid sarcoma–like) haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: epithelioid sarcoma–like haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma most commonly involves the lower extremities (55%); the upper extremities and trunk are less commonly affected (20% each), and the head and neck are rarely involved (5%) {12502927; 1371711; 23381465; 21263239}.

Clinical features

Patients present with painless or painful nodules; ulceration may be present. In about 60% of cases the disease is multifocal, involving multiple tissue planes, most commonly the skin and subcutis, but it may also involve skeletal muscle and bone {12502927; 21263239; 1371711; 23381465}.

Epidemiology

Young men (mean age: 30 years) are most commonly affected (M:F ratio: 3.5:1) {21263239}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma has rearrangements of FOSB, including SERPINE1::FOSB, ACTB::FOSB, WWTR1::FOSB, CLTC::FOSB, and EGFL7::FOSB {24374978; 30459475; 30256258; 31243110; 32749039; 33550637}. This results in upregulation, with FOSB acting as an active transcription factor capable of its own regulation {24374978; 29511030}. Tumours with ACTB::FOSB fusions are more likely to be solitary {30256258}. Tumours with EGFL7::FOSB may be more aggressive {33550637}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, the tumour is poorly defined, with a grey to white firm cut surface. Most are < 30 mm in size {21263239}.

Histopathology

Pseudomyogenic haemangioendotheliomas are variably composed of infiltrative sheets and fascicles of spindled (most commonly) to epithelioid cells with dense, eosinophilic cytoplasm {21263239; 12502927}. Atypia is usually mild, and tumour nuclei are vesicular with small nucleoli. Significant nuclear atypia may be seen in about 10% of cases {21263239}. Admixed neutrophils are seen in approximately 50%.

By immunohistochemistry, they are positive for keratins (most often with AE1/AE3 clones, less often MNF116), FLI1, ERG, and FOSB, but negative for CD34 {21263239; 12502927; 27515856; 28009608}. About 50% are positive for CD31, and focal expression of SMAs is seen in as many as one third of cases. SMARCB1 expression is retained {21263239}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of FOSB rearrangement may be helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fascicles or sheets of spindled to epithelioid cells with dense eosinophilic cytoplasm, vesicular nuclei, and expression of ERG and AE1/AE3.

Desirable: demonstration of FOSB expression or FOSB rearrangement.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence or development of new nodules in the same region is frequent (~60%) {21263239}. Lymph node and distant metastases are uncommon (< 5%) {21263239}. Histopathological features are not predictive.
