5.2.1.18 Desmoplastic fibroblastoma

Definition

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma is a benign soft tissue tumour characterized by stellate fibroblasts in a fibromyxoid background.

ICD-O coding

8810/0 Desmoplastic fibroblastoma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH2ZF3 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Desmoplastic fibroblastoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: collagenous fibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma has a predilection for the upper extremities, although it may occur anywhere {7661281; 9670823; 8832562}.

Clinical features

This tumour is a painless, solitary, slow-growing mass and predominantly subcutaneous, although it may involve skeletal muscle {9670823}.

Epidemiology

Desmoplastic fibroblastomas are rare. They predominantly affect adults (median age: 50–60 years) and show a male predominance {7661281; 9593348; 9670823; 8832562}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Desmoplastic fibroblastomas have been reported to have translocations involving 11q12, including t(2;11)(q31;q12) and t(11;17)(q12;p11.2) {15036892; 19596257}. The FOSL1 gene is located nearby (at 11q13.1), and the FOSL1 protein is overexpressed in these tumours {27442992; 22411068}.

Macroscopic appearance

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma manifests as a circumscribed, firm mass with a white-grey cut surface. It can be as large as 200 mm.

Histopathology

Desmoplastic fibroblastomas are hypocellular tumours with bland, spindled to stellate fibroblasts and a fibromyxoid to delicate collagenous background {7661281; 9670823; 8832562}. Immunohistochemistry is nonspecific; desmin, keratin, and CD34 are negative {8832562}. The differential diagnosis includes desmoid-type fibromatosis.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a hypocellular tumour with a fibromyxoid collagenous background; bland spindled to stellate fibroblasts.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The behaviour of this tumour is benign, with no local recurrence after excision {7661281; 9670823; 8832562}.
