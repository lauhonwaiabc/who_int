6.0.0.4 Naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (Gorlin syndrome)

Definition

Naevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (NBCCS) is an autosomal dominant disease associated with developmental disorders and predisposition to benign and malignant tumours.

ICD-O coding

MIM numbering

109400 Basal cell naevus syndrome 1; BCNS1

ICD-11 coding

LD2D.4 Gorlin syndrome

Related terminology

Acceptable: Gorlin syndrome; Gorlin–Goltz syndrome.

Not recommended: basal cell naevus syndrome; fifth phakomatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Sites of disease include the following: skin – basal cell carcinomas (BCCs), sebaceous cysts / facial milia, palmoplantar pits, epidermal inclusion cysts, multiple naevi {36297395}; jaw – odontogenic keratocysts (OKCs), cleft palate/lip {26297395}; bone – congenital anomalies (bifid or splayed ribs, frontal bossing, hypertelorism, scoliosis, flame-shaped lucencies in hands/feet, digit syndactyly, long bone cysts, mandibular prognathism); and CNS – macrocephaly, falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli and/or sella turcica calcification, medulloblastoma, meningioma, craniopharyngioma {12879481}. Other manifestations include intratarsal eyelid cysts, fibromas (ovarian and cardiac {35735206}), and lymphomesenteric cysts {17221269}.

Clinical features

The most frequent manifestations of NBCCS are multiple BCCs (in sun-exposed and non-exposed skin), as well as jaw OKCs, found together in > 90% of affected individuals by the age of 40 years {8326488}. OKCs most commonly occur in the posterior mandibular ramus, and they may be multifocal when syndrome-associated {1065842; 21159911}. The overall prevalence of OKCs in NBCCS is 66–86% {8326488; 8042673; 9096761; 22246785}, but OKCs have an age-dependent onset, and by the age of 40 years the prevalence is reported to be > 90% {8326488}. Cysts commonly arise relatively early, at a mean age of 15.5 years {8042673}, but they may also occur much later in life {8326488}. Other frequent manifestations include calcification of the falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli, and/or sella turcica; palmar and plantar pits; and bifid or fused ribs {9096761; 8042673}. These findings are considered major diagnostic criteria (see #38440Box 9.03). Minor criteria include medulloblastoma {12879481}, ovarian fibroma, macrocephaly, congenital facial abnormalities (e.g. cleft lip or palate, frontal bossing, and hypertelorism), skeletal abnormalities (e.g. digit syndactyly), and radiological bone abnormalities (e.g. bridging of the sella turcica, flame cysts in digits, long bone cysts) {12879481; 9096761}. The clinical features manifest at different ages: macrocephaly and rib anomalies can be detected at birth; medulloblastoma typically develops within the first 3 years of life; OKCs usually manifest after 8 years; and BCCs are usually found after 10 years of age {25131638}. Radiation treatment of medulloblastoma may induce multiple BCCs, as well as various other tumour types (e.g. meningioma) within the radiation field {6837723; 9010431; 11878577}.

NBCCS-associated medulloblastoma usually develops in children aged < 5 years, with the majority identified in patients aged < 2 years {12879481}, nearly always showing desmoplastic/nodular histopathology {12879481; 19276247; 7856756; 25403219}. Therefore, it is proposed that desmoplastic/nodular medulloblastoma in young children should serve as a major criterion for the diagnosis of NBCCS {12879481; 19276247}. About 1–2% of patients with NBCCS with germline PTCH1 pathogenic sequence variants develop medulloblastoma, compared with about 20% of patients with germline SUFU pathogenic sequence variants {25403219}.

Epidemiology

Disease prevalence varies by reporting country. In the United Kingdom, it is 1 case per 18 976 population {20082463}; in Australia, 1 case per 164 000 {8042673}; in Japan, 1 case per 235 800 {25131638}; and in Italy, 1 case per 256 000 {30129523}. Of 131 children with medulloblastomas in one study, 6% had germline SUFU mutation {22508808}.

Etiology

NBCCS results from inactivating constitutional sequence variants in the human homologue of the Drosophila segment polarity patched gene (PTCH1) located on chromosome 9q22 {8681379; 8658145}, SUFU on 10q24 {25403219}, and (in exceptional cases) GPR161 on 1q24.2 {31609649}. Numerous different PTCH1 constitutional pathogenic sequence variants associated with NBCCS have been reported {25131638; 16419085}. The detection rate of specific pathogenic variants has increased significantly in recent years, owing to the development of improved methods {25131638}. The mostly truncating mutations are distributed over the entire PTCH1 coding region, with no mutation hotspots {8981943}. Missense mutations cluster in a highly conserved region (the sterol-sensing domain), and particularly in transmembrane domain 4. The rate of new PTCH1 pathogenic variants has not been precisely determined, but it is estimated that a high proportion (14–81%) of cases are the result of de novo alterations {8326488; 3547011; 8042673; 9415689}. SUFU pathogenic sequence variants are detected in only about 5–6% of tested probands, and they are mostly truncating {25403219}, with most cases inherited from an unaffected, healthy parent; the remaining cases show a de novo variant {25403219}. Constitutional pathogenic variants in GPR161 have been detected in sonic hedgehog (SHH) subgroup medulloblastomas, suggesting possible NBCCS association {31609649}.

Pathogenesis

PTCH1 encodes a 12-pass transmembrane protein expressed on many progenitor cell types that functions as a receptor for members of the secreted hedgehog protein family of signalling molecules (SHH, IHH, and DHH) and acts through downstream factors to repress transcription of genes encoding members of pathways active in poorly differentiated cells, such as TGF-β and WNT {8906794; 8906787}. PTCH1 controls another transmembrane protein, SMO {9278137; 8906787}. In the absence of ligand, PTCH1 inhibits the activity of SMO {9278137; 8906787}. Hedgehog signalling takes place in the primary cilium {25172843}. Binding of hedgehog proteins to PTCH1 can relieve its inhibition of SMO, allowing its translocation to the tip of the primary cilium, which results in the activation and translocation of GLI transcription factors into the cell nucleus and transcription of a set of specific target genes controlling the survival, differentiation, and proliferation of progenitor cells. In vertebrates, this pathway is critically involved in the development of various tissues and organ systems, such as the limbs, gonads, bone, and CNS {9883719; 9529611}. SUFU is located downstream in the hedgehog pathway; it has been found to directly interact with GLI proteins and is a negative regulator of hedgehog signalling {10564661}. PTCH1 and SUFU are classic tumour suppressor genes; the second allele of the mutant gene is lost in most NBCCS-related tumours. The inactivation of PTCH1 or SUFU leads to pathological activation of the SHH signalling pathway.

Macroscopic appearance

Syndromic tumours appear grossly similar to their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Practically all medulloblastomas associated with NBCCS have the morphological features of nodular tumours, including desmoplastic/nodular medulloblastoma and medulloblastoma with extensive nodularity {7856756; 12879481; 19276247; 22508808; 25403219}. BCCs do not show any unique histological features, with all subtypes represented, including pigmented subtypes. Basaloid neoplastic cells with peripheral palisading, epithelial–stromal clefting, stromal mucin deposition, and infundibulocystic differentiation are characteristic {33063358}. NBCCS-associated OKCs are typically multifocal and develop in younger patients than non–syndrome-associated cases {1065842; 21159911; 2442330}. The thin epithelium typically lacks rete ridges and shows a surface with corrugated parakeratin, and the basal cells are palisaded and have hyperchromatic nuclei. Basal budding and microcysts in the wall are more likely in syndromic cysts {2441019}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Comprehensive mutation analysis of the PTCH1 and SUFU genes by DNA sequencing and a search for deletions/duplications by appropriate methods (e.g. multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification [MLPA], targeted array-based analysis) identifies most NBCCS-related constitutional pathogenic variants {20301330}. To detect more complex alterations, additional analysis at the transcript level may be necessary in some cases. Despite improved detection rates {25131638}, in 15–27% of index cases, neither PTCH1 nor SUFU pathogenic variants can be identified {28596197}. Because constitutional pathogenic variants are frequent in children younger than 5 years with extensive nodular or desmoplastic/nodular medulloblastomas, genetic counselling should be offered to the families, and early pathogenic variant analysis of PTCH1 and SUFU should be performed. The detection of a constitutional condition is important for appropriate treatment planning to avoid unnecessary radiation therapy {19276247; 22508808; 25403219; 29186568; 29753700}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #38440Box 9.03 .

Staging

Malignancies are staged in accordance with the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

The life expectancy of patients with NBCCS is 73.4 years, only slightly reduced from unaffected populations (in contrast to some other genetic tumour syndromes); however, there is considerable impact on quality of life {22362873}. Surveillance guidelines are well established {28620006; 20301330; 33860896}; for SUFU pathogenic sequence variant carriers, they include a recommendation for repeated brain MRI screening for medulloblastoma development within the first 5 years of life {28620006}.

The prognosis of NBCCS-associated medulloblastomas seems to be better than that of sporadic cases, and it has been suggested that therapy protocols be adjusted in patients aged < 5 years with NBCCS, to prevent radiation-induced secondary tumours {12879481; 11878577}. Medulloblastoma patients with SUFU germline pathogenic sequence variants are said to have a poor prognosis {29186568}, although no difference in survival was found between patients with medulloblastoma carrying a PTCH1 versus a SUFU pathogenic variant in other studies {29753700}. The cumulative BCC burden (> 100 BCCs in about a third of children), requires careful treatment optimization in terms of methods and timing of tumour removal to achieve a good cosmetic result and the best quality-of-life outcomes {34816134; 34375441}. Statistical modelling suggests earlier syndrome diagnosis if certain cardinal features are identified and result in genetic testing (i.e. OKCs, BCCs, palmoplantar pits) {34611197}.
