4.3.1.4 Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma (IVLBCL) is an aggressive extranodal B-cell lymphoma characterized by the proliferation of large neoplastic B cells virtually restricted to the lumina of blood vessels. The skin is most commonly affected, with diverse dermatological manifestations.

ICD-O coding

9712/3 Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma

9712/3 Cutaneous intravascular large B-cell lymphoma

9712/3 Classic intravascular large B-cell lymphoma

9712/3 Haemophagocytic intravascular large B-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A81.1 Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiotropic lymphoma; intravascular lymphomatosis.

Subtype(s)

Cutaneous intravascular large B-cell lymphoma; classic intravascular large B-cell lymphoma; haemophagocytic intravascular large B-cell lymphoma

Localization

IVLBCL may involve virtually any organ, including the skin. Lymph nodes are usually spared {30111607}.

Clinical features

Cutaneous subtype

The cutaneous subtype is by definition restricted to the skin at presentation. A wide range of appearances are described, including “peau d’orange”–like lesions, palpable blue-red discolouration, solitary or clustered plaques, and ulcerating nodules and tumours. In the cutaneous subtype, as many as 30% of patients present with systemic symptoms {35621627}.

Classic subtype

Most patients present with fever of unknown origin (50–70%), pain, local organ-specific symptoms, or multiorgan failure. The most common site of presentation is the skin (30–40%), but by definition this is associated with the involvement of other organs {31044423}. A random deep skin biopsy (with the inclusion of subcutaneous fat tissue), even in the absence of clinically overt cutaneous lesions, can be helpful in the diagnosis {30609011; 30647028}.

Haemophagocytic subtype

Presenting symptoms are dominated by those related to haemophagocytic syndrome, hepatosplenomegaly, and thrombocytopenia (> 75%), and the onset and clinical course are generally rapid. Skin involvement often spares the dermis but may be present in the subcutaneous tissue, even when there is no skin eruption {11122144}.

Epidemiology

The cutaneous subtype occurs in patients with a median age of 59 years, is virtually restricted to women, and is more frequent in countries in the Western Hemisphere. The classic subtype of IVLBCL occurs in adults with a median age of 70 years and has no sex predilection. The haemophagocytic subtype is predominantly reported in Asian countries and has a similar mean age at presentation and age distribution to the classic subtype {30111607; 33122951; 17488659}. Overall, the incidence of the disease is 0.095 cases per 1 million person-years {28278725; 21481222}, without important geographical differences, in contrast to the geographical distribution seen in the subtypes {17488659}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Viral associations are not described.

Pathogenesis

Tumour cells are confined to the intravascular space by various mechanisms that allow them to adhere to endothelial cells but render them unable to migrate across the blood vessel wall {10685637; 21376176; 22381519; 18987832; 27001523}. MYD88 and CD79B hotspot mutations are reported in 50% and almost 70% of cases, respectively. SETD1B (57%) and HLA-B (57%) mutations are also common {29514783; 29472348; 33512416}. Structural variants affecting CD274 (encoding PDL1) and PDCD1LG2 (encoding PDL2) are reported to be recurrent {33512416}. Together, the genetic landscape suggests that constitutive NF-κB activation and immune evasion are dominant pathogenetic mechanisms in IVLBCL.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Large lymphoid cells with vesicular nuclei and single or multiple prominent nucleoli, often showing mitotic figures, are observed within the lumina of small vessels, especially capillaries. A free-floating (discohesive), cohesive, or marginating/adherent growth pattern may be observed. Rarely, the lymphoid cells can be anaplastic. In the haemophagocytic subtype, a varying infiltrate of non-neoplastic histiocytes with signs of haemophagocytosis is present.

Immunophenotype

Pan–B-cell markers are expressed (CD20, CD79a), often with coexpression of CD5 and PDL1 (CD274; 20–40%) {30938862; 30688388}. Most cases express IRF4 (MUM1) (75–80%) and have a non–germinal-centre B-cell immunophenotype; however, approximately 13% are CD10-positive {16985183}.

Differential diagnosis

Various systemic B- and T-cell lymphomas, such as KSHV/HHV8-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, may show a dominant intravascular presentation {19287457}. Specifically in the skin, IVLBCL should be differentiated from intravascular T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation, in which large heterogeneously CD30-positive blastoid cells are present in and around lymphatic vessels (see section Reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferations) {25319987; 26844617} and from intralymphatic ALK-positive and ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphomas that may present with isolated skin localizations {24805854}.

Cytology

Not applicable

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In equivocal cases restricted to the skin, DNA analysis on cutaneous biopsy samples may be interrogated for genes recurrently mutated or for clonality. In cases with systemic presentation, similar analyses using cell-free DNA by liquid biopsy may be considered {29472348; 33512416}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large lymphoid cells with centroblastic, immunoblastic, or (rarely) anaplastic morphology; restricted to intravascular spaces, especially capillaries (a minimal extravascular component is acceptable); pan–B-cell markers positive.

Desirable: KSHV/HHV8 negative by latency-associated nuclear antigen (LANA) immunohistochemistry; EBV negative by EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) in situ hybridization.

Staging

After exclusion of systemic involvement, staging should be performed according to the International Society for Cutaneous Lymphomas (ISCL) / European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) guidelines for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides {17339420}.

Prognosis and prediction

IVLBCL is an aggressive lymphoma that is often diagnosed late during the disease course, contributing to a poor prognosis. The cutaneous subtype has a better outcome, possibly related to earlier detection {15461623; 19717091}. Chemotherapy with rituximab improves the outcome, with a 3-year overall survival rate of 60–81% {18699850; 18838697; 19717091; 18506023; 32171071; 32367674}.
