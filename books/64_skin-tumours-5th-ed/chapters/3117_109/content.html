3.1.1.7 Cylindroma

Definition

Cylindroma is a benign adnexal neoplasm with a mosaic microscopic pattern. Cylindroma commonly occurs as a hybrid with spiradenoma, a phenomenon that has been referred to as cylindrospiradenoma or spiradenocylindroma.

Subtype(s)

Cylindrospiradenoma/spiradenocylindroma

Localization

Cylindroma most commonly affects the head and neck, but it is also found on the trunk and extremities.

Clinical features

Cylindromas are typically smooth, dome-shaped, hairless, reddish-brown papules or nodules. Multiple cylindromas located on the scalp have historically been referred to as “turban tumours”.

Epidemiology

Cylindromas can be solitary or multiple. They can arise sporadically or as part of Brooke–Spiegler syndrome. There is no sex predilection.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Multiple cylindromas are seen in patients with Brooke–Spiegler syndrome, an inherited autosomal dominant disease characterized by the development of multiple adnexal cutaneous neoplasms, most commonly spiradenoma, cylindroma, spiradenocylindroma, and trichoepithelioma {26971504}. The gene implicated in the pathogenesis of Brooke–Spiegler syndrome is CYLD, a tumour suppressor gene located at 16q12.1 {15854031}. Germline CYLD mutations are found in about 80–85% of patients with the classic Brooke–Spiegler syndrome phenotype. Loss of heterozygosity at 16q has also been found in sporadic cylindromas and spiradenomas.

Pathogenesis

Several genetic studies have identified mutations of the tumour suppressor gene CYLD in patients with Brooke–Spiegler syndrome {7493027; 23249834}. Like CYLD, DNMT3A has been described as a driver gene in sporadic cylindroma {31101826}. Sporadic cases can also harbour a MYB::NFIB fusion {21618541; 31101826}.

Spiradenoma and cylindroma are related to oncogenic activation of the NF-κB pathway, triggered by mutually exclusive mutations of ALPK1 and CYLD {31101826}. A wide range of CYLD mutations affect coding and splicing, resulting in CYLD inactivation {10835629}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Cylindroma is a symmetrical, well-circumscribed lesion. It consists of multiple aggregates of basaloid cells in a jigsaw-puzzle arrangement and surrounded by a prominent basement membrane that gives a positive PAS reaction. Two types of cells form the epithelial collections: small basaloid cells and larger pale cells. There are also ductal structures. Many neoplasms show findings of both spiradenoma and cylindroma in the same lesion (spiradenocylindroma) {11756779}. Malignant transformation is rare.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells usually express KIT (CD117) and SOX10 {28098399}. MYB overexpression has also been reported and does not attest to the presence of a fusion gene {21618541; 31101826; 25857824; 28098399}.

Differential diagnosis

Adenoid cystic carcinoma with a prominent jigsaw-puzzle arrangement can enter the differential diagnosis, as can rare examples of basal cell carcinoma in which a thick basement membrane surrounds the aggregates of basaloid cells {9415612; 24025525}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CYLD mutation analysis may be helpful, particularly if a germline genetic disorder is suspected.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a symmetrical, well-circumscribed lesion, histologically composed of multiple aggregates of basaloid cells in a jigsaw-puzzle arrangement and surrounded by a prominent basement membrane.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Cylindroma is a benign neoplasm.
