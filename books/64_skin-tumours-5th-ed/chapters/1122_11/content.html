1.1.2.2 Verruca plantaris and verruca palmaris

Definition

Verruca plantaris is an HPV-induced benign epidermal squamoproliferative lesion affecting the soles or palms.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

1E80.1 Plantar warts

Related terminology

Acceptable: plantar wart; palmoplantar wart; plantar verruca.

Not recommended: myrmecia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The soles and palms are the principal sites of involvement.

Clinical features

The lesions can be single or multiple, and they are often painful. The myrmecial form (associated with HPV1) presents as a hard, slightly elevated keratotic papule or nodule on the plantar or palmar surface. The more superficial mosaic form (associated with HPV4 and HPV2) exhibits confluent growth and is resistant to therapy {22131115; 6299203}. Black dots reflect intralesional vascular thrombosis. Dermoscopy is a useful diagnostic adjunct and tool for assessing response to treatment {34538428}.

Epidemiology

Children are affected more frequently than adults, with a reported prevalence of 1.8–20% {10770027; 19438464}.

Etiology

HPV1, HPV2, and HPV4 usually predominate {22131115; 6299203}. HPV27 and HPV57 have been recorded in cases from Spain and California {27736001; 24725033}. HPV60 has been linked to plantar warts and epidermal cysts {7979443}. Primary hyperhidrosis and visits to swimming pools and communal showers are risk factors {19395123; 23932731; 7852935}. Patients with HIV may have larger and more numerous lesions {9770936}.

Pathogenesis

See the Pathogenesis subsection of the section Verruca vulgaris section .

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The lesions are characterized by marked hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, hypergranulosis, and koilocytosis {22131115}. Papillomatosis is usually mild. The myrmecial (ant-hill) form shows endophytic growth and vacuolated keratinocytes containing coarse eosinophilic keratohyalin granules. Regressing lesions exhibit haemorrhage, necrosis, thrombosis, and a mixed inflammatory infiltrate {7059201}.

Differential diagnosis

Callosities occur on pressure points and are also endophytic and hyperkeratotic, but they lack koilocytes and coarse keratohyalin granules {27023088}. Molluscum contagiosum also displays endophytic growth but has basophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions rather than heterogeneous eosinophilic keratohyalin granules {20398595}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Routine HPV subtyping is not indicated.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: papillomatosis; parakeratotic columns; coarse eosinophilic keratohyalin granules; koilocytosis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Verruca plantaris often regresses spontaneously. Lesions in children typically resolve more rapidly {22131115}. Verrucous carcinoma is a very rare complication {6168555}.
