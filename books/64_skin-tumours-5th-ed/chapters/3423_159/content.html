3.4.2.3 Adenocarcinoma of mammary gland type

Definition

Adenocarcinoma of mammary gland type comprises a group of primary vulval tumours showing histopathological features identical to breast carcinomas.

ICD-O coding

8500/3 Adenocarcinoma of mammary gland type

ICD-11 coding

2C70.Z & XH9FX2 Malignant neoplasms of vulva, unspecified & Adenocarcinoma of mammary gland type

Related terminology

Acceptable: adenocarcinoma of anogenital mammary-like glands.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common location is the labia majora {28418164} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Female genital tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 4). https://publications.iarc.who.int/592.]].

Clinical features

Most patients are postmenopausal and present with a vulval mass.

Epidemiology

These are rare vulval neoplasms.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Adenocarcinoma of mammary gland type is proposed to arise from anogenital mammary-like glands located in the anal and vulval area {28418164; 27759697}. Mutations in the genes of the PI3K/AKT cascade have been found in a few studied cases {28291131}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is that of a solitary nodule with a fibrous cut surface, similar to breast carcinomas.

Histopathology

The most common type is adenocarcinoma, which is identical to mammary invasive ductal carcinoma and may (uncommonly) have an identifiable in situ component in the adjacent glands. Rare types include lobular, tubulolobular, mixed ductal and lobular, and mammary analogue secretory carcinoma. There is commonly expression of ER, PR, GATA3, and ERBB2 {27655126;16625074; 20505503; 16931966; 29672325}. Based on the immunoprofile, they can be classified like their breast counterparts into luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched, and basal-like subtypes {28418164}.

Cytology

No data available

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not clinically relevant in most cases. Secretory carcinoma harbours the characteristic ETV6::NTRK3 fusion.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a solitary nodule with a fibrous cut surface, similar to breast carcinoma; histology consistent with that of invasive breast carcinoma.

Desirable: location usually the labia majora; association with lobular structures consistent with mammary differentiation; immunohistochemical expression of ER, PR, and ERBB2.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

Adenocarcinomas of mammary gland type are locally aggressive tumours that often metastasize to regional lymph nodes. Distant metastases are uncommon. Hormonal and anti-HER2 therapy may be helpful {21132401; 23030494}.
