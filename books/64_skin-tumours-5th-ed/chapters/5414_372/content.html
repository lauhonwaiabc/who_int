5.4.1.4 Myofibroma and myofibromatosis

Definition

Myofibroma and myofibromatosis are benign mesenchymal neoplasms of presumed myofibroblastic histogenetic origin/phenotype.

ICD-O coding

8824/0 Myofibroma

8824/1 Multicentric myofibromatosis

8824/1 Generalized myofibromatosis (with visceral involvement)

ICD-11 coding

2E84.Y & XH0953 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of other specified sites & Myofibroma

2E84.Y & XH1N00 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of other specified sites & Myofibromatosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: infantile myofibroma; infantile myofibromatosis; adult myofibroma.

Subtype(s)

Myofibroma; multicentric myofibromatosis; generalized myofibromatosis (with visceral involvement)

Localization

Solitary lesions originate in the skin or subcutis of the head and neck, upper extremities, or trunk. Deep sites (muscle and bones) are uncommonly affected {22486319}. Multicentric disease has a similar site distribution to solitary myofibroma. The generalized form of the disease shows concomitant visceral involvement {22486319}.

Clinical features

Myofibroma occurs in three clinicopathological settings: solitary, multicentric, and generalized. The solitary form of the disease has a male predilection, whereas the multicentric form predominantly affects females. Clinically, patients with myofibroma usually present with a well-circumscribed but unencapsulated violaceous superficial dermal or subcutaneous nodule measuring a few millimetres to several centimetres in maximum diameter, with a firm consistency {22486319}.

Epidemiology

Myofibroma represents 12% of paediatric soft tissue lesions. The age range is wide, spanning from the neonatal period (including congenital lesions) to 70 years; however, 90% of cases are paediatric, and 65% occur by the age of 2 years {22486319}. Multicentric lesions are exclusively paediatric.

Etiology

Germline PDGFRB mutations predispose to myofibromatosis in affected families.

Pathogenesis

Although of presumptive myofibroblastic origin {8067513}, myofibroma shares with other perivascular myoid cell neoplasms features of modified myopericytes {16330949}. The presence of vascular balls and myointimal proliferations argues for an analogy to other perivascular myoid cell neoplasms and may explain the morphological overlap between these lesions {9591720}.

Germline mutations in PDGFRB (89%) and NOTCH3 (11%) have been detected in the familial disease. Sporadic solitary lesions have frequent somatic PDGFRB mutations {23731537; 23731542; 27776010}. PDGFRB mutation types differ between familial and non-familial (sporadic) myofibromatosis cases. SRF rearrangements have been documented in tumours with high cellularity {32529351}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with small, firm, cutaneous or subcutaneous nodules with irregular margins.

Histopathology

Myofibroma is characterized by a biphasic growth, with alternating bundles of primitive, darker-staining, small rounded cells and plump spindled mesenchymal cells. These are associated with numerous thin-walled haemangiopericytoma-like vessels and mature eosinophilic plump spindled myoid cells with frequent myointimal-like nodular proliferations (vascular balls). Foci of calcification may be present. Mitotic activity is usually low but may be increased. Foci of ischaemic-type coagulation necrosis can be seen. The neoplastic cells stain diffusely for α-SMA, but h-caldesmon is absent or only focally positive. Myofibroma is usually desmin-negative.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: biphasic pattern with myoid cells forming fascicles and more primitive cells associated with haemangiopericytoma-like vasculature; absent atypia.

Desirable: identification of PDGFRB mutation in challenging cases, and SRF rearrangement in cellular cases.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Myofibroma is benign and does not recur after excision. The generalized form, however, is frequently fatal due to extensive visceral involvement and limited options for surgical intervention. Atypical histological features (significant cellularity, inconspicuous or absent myoid nodules, infiltrating borders, intravascular growth, perineural invasion, entrapment of nerves, and increased mitotic rate) do not affect prognosis {24921644}.
