5.2.1.15 Gardner fibroma

Definition

Gardner fibroma is a benign fibroblastic tumour composed of thick, haphazardly arranged collagen bundles, usually occurring in children and often associated with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP).

ICD-O coding

8810/0 Gardner fibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH7GT0 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Gardner fibroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: Gardner-associated fibroma.

Not recommended: desmoid precursor lesion.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The strongest predilection is for the back and the head and heck region, followed by the extremities and chest wall.

Clinical features

Gardner fibromas are typically solitary but rarely multiple.

Epidemiology

Most cases occur in children (especially boys); Gardner fibroma is uncommon in adults {17325483}.

Etiology

There is an association with Gardner syndrome {11342777}. They are often associated with desmoid-type fibromatosis {11342777; 17325483}.

Pathogenesis

FAP-associated tumours have germline mutations in APC {11342777}.

Macroscopic appearance

These are poorly defined tumours with a plaque-like growth pattern, rubbery consistency, and white cut surface. If the lesion contains an apparent fat component, the cut surface may show yellow soft areas. Tumours as large as 120 mm can be seen.

Histopathology

These tumours are composed of dense collagen with clefts and cracks and bland spindle cells. Entrapped fat and mast cells can be seen. A helpful diagnostic feature is entrapment of adjacent adipose tissue and neurovascular bundles at the edge of the lesion.

Immunohistochemistry

β-catenin is positive {17325483}, and nuclear accumulation of β-catenin is often observed, particularly in cases associated with FAP {17325483; 26840078}. CD34 is almost always positive, whereas SMA is negative {11342777; 17325483}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes nuchal-type fibroma and desmoid-type fibromatosis. Some authors have proposed that a subset of nuchal-type fibromas are in fact Gardner fibromas {11342777}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a plaque-like tumour, commonly arising on the back of the head and neck of young children; dense collagen with clefts and cracks and spindle cells; positive nuclear staining for β-catenin.

Desirable: entrapment of adjacent adipose tissue and neurovascular bundles at the edge of the lesion.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Gardner fibroma is a benign tumour with a rate of recurrence as high as 50%. Some patients may develop desmoid-type fibromatosis {11315790; 3760506}.

Given the close association between Gardner fibroma and FAP, a diagnosis of Gardner fibroma should prompt screening of the family for FAP.
