2.1.1.10 BAP1-inactivated melanocytoma

Definition

BRCA1-associated deubiquitinase 1 (BAP1)-inactivated melanocytoma is a compound or intradermal proliferation of epithelioid melanocytes caused by inactivation of the BAP1 gene. It can occur sporadically or in association with BAP1-related tumour predisposition syndrome (BAP1-TPDS).

ICD-O coding

8720/1 BAP1-inactivated melanocytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F20.1 Atypical melanocytic naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: BAP1-inactivated melanocytic tumour; BAP1-inactivated naevus.

Not recommended: BAPoma; BAP1-inactivated spitzoid naevus; melanocytic BAP1-mutated atypical intradermal tumour (MBAIT); Wiesner naevus; BAP1-deficient tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas can occur at any anatomical site of the skin but are most commonly observed on sun-exposed skin, such as the head, neck, trunk, and upper extremities {21874003}.

Clinical features

BAP1-inactivated melanocytoma usually arises as inconspicuous, skin-coloured to reddish-brown, dome-shaped papules or nodules 4–12 mm in size, sometimes within a conventional naevus. By dermoscopy, they usually appear as structureless, pink to tan lesions, sometimes with irregular eccentric dots/globules or peripheral vessels, or as a network with raised structureless pink to tan areas or with a globular pattern {30244062}.

Epidemiology

BAP1-inactivated melanocytoma is rare and mostly occurs sporadically as a solitary lesion. Multiple BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas arise in patients with the autosomal dominant syndrome BAP1-TPDS (MIM number: 614327), caused by BAP1 germline mutations (see section BAP1-related tumour predisposition syndrome).

Etiology

Mutations in melanocytes are associated with constitutional factors such as tanning ability, DNA repair capacity, and genetic susceptibility, as well as environmental factors such as sun exposure {20016485}.

Pathogenesis

BAP1-inactivated melanocytoma typically arises from a conventional naevus with BRAF p.V600E or (less frequently) with NRAS mutations or RAF1 fusions {21874003; 22367297; 34857909}, or through biallelic inactivation of the BAP1 tumour suppressor gene caused by loss-of-function mutations resulting in a non-functional or absent protein, deletions of the BAP1 gene, or a combination of both.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas are predominantly intradermal proliferations of large, unpigmented, epithelioid melanocytes with well-defined cytoplasmic borders, large amounts of eosinophilic cytoplasm, and vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli, often accompanied by a lymphocytic infiltrate {23277170; 22367297; 34857909}. There are no or few mitoses. A conventional naevus remnant is often present.

Immunohistochemistry

Biallelic BAP1 inactivation caused by deletions or nonsense mutations leads to complete loss of nuclear immunoreactivity in the epithelioid cells, whereas immunoreactivity can be retained in the less common scenario in which inactivating missense mutations corrupt the catalytic domain of BAP1 {29416875}. In approximately 5–10% of BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas, there is retained nuclear expression but a pathogenic variant in BAP1 can be identified by sequencing studies.

Differential diagnosis

BAP1-inactivated melanocytoma may resemble a Spitz naevus, but Kamino bodies are absent and the lesions typically lack the wedge-shaped silhouette of Spitz naevi. Combined BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas may be confused with melanoma arising in a naevus. Helpful clues in the differential diagnosis are the recognition of the typical epithelioid melanocytes, and the loss of BAP1 expression by immunohistochemistry in the absence of malignant features.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of biallelic inactivation of the BAP1 gene can help support the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nodular proliferation of large epithelioid, unpigmented melanocytes, often adjacent to a conventional naevus; demonstration of BAP1 inactivation by loss of nuclear BAP1 expression by immunohistochemistry or molecular pathology.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas typically have an indolent clinical course {23277170}. Rare BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas can seed lymph node metastases without any overt features of malignancy {34857909}. The increased incidence of BAP1-inactivated cutaneous melanomas in patients with multiple BAP1-inactivated melanocytomas indicates an increased risk of progression to melanoma {21874003; 26154183; 26517632}. Histopathological features, patient age, and size of residuum should be considered when making recommendations for a re-excision. See section BAP1-related tumour predisposition syndrome for carriers of BAP1 germline pathogenic variants.
