4.4.1.8 Primary cutaneous peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS

Definition

Primary cutaneous peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PCPTCL-NOS) is a very rare and still poorly characterized group of T-cell lymphomas, which does not correspond to any of the specifically defined entities of primary cutaneous T-cell lymphomas (CTCLs) (i.e. it is a diagnosis of exclusion).

ICD-O coding

9709/3 Primary cutaneous peripheral T-cell lymphoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B0Z Primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma of undetermined or unspecified type

Related terminology

Acceptable: primary cutaneous peripheral T-cell lymphoma, unspecified.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Disseminated skin lesions are predominantly located on the trunk and limbs {29573477; 32997839}, whereas solitary tumours are more commonly found on the legs and arms, but the head and neck can also be affected {29573477; 32997839}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with disseminated plaques, papules, or tumours {32997839; 29573477; 12750155}. About one third show (initially) solitary lesions {32997839; 29573477}.

Ulceration can be found (~15%) {32997839}. PCPTCL-NOS is skin-limited at the time of diagnosis, but systemic involvement is common during the later disease course.

Epidemiology

PCPTCL-NOS is exceedingly rare. Most cases of PCPTCL-NOS occur in adults in their sixth decade of life, but it can also occur in children {32997839; 29573477; 12750155}. There is a male predominance (M:F ratio: 3:1) {12750155; 29573477; 32997839}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is largely unknown, although gain of chromosomes 7q, 8, and 17q, and loss of chromosome 9p21 have been reported {19710685}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Since PCPTCL-NOS is a diagnosis of exclusion, distinct and characteristic histopathological and immunophenotypic features are lacking; however, any such features of other cutaneous T-cell lymphoma entities can be observed, albeit outside their typical context. There is a diffuse or nodular dermal infiltrate consisting of cohesive sheets of mostly medium-sized to large tumour cells, although cases consisting of small to medium-sized cells are also reported {12750155; 27498959; 30296539; 32997839}. The subcutis is commonly involved {32997839}. Limited and focal epidermotropism of lymphocytes is found in approximately one third {32997839} of cases. Angiocentric/angiodestructive features and folliculotropism are rare {12750155; 30296539; 32997839}.

Immunophenotype

The tumour cells are positive for CD3 and CD2 {32997839}. The most common phenotype is CD4+/CD8− {12750155; 32997839}. About one third of cases show a CD4−/CD8− phenotype, and approximately 10% are CD4+/CD8+ {32997839}. EBV is negative {32997839}. Cytotoxic markers (TIA1 and granzyme B) are mostly negative (75%) {32997839}. Aberrant expression of CD20 can occur {32997839}.

The proliferative activity is moderate to high (30–90%). PD1 expression (> 40%) can be found {32997839}.

Very rare cases of primary CTCLs with a T follicular helper (TFH) cell phenotype have been described; further data are needed to clarify whether these should be recognized as a separate pattern, subtype, or entity in the future. Therefore, primary CTCLs with a TFH cell phenotype (defined by the expression of at least two to three TFH cell markers: PD1, ICOS, CD10, BCL6, CXCL13) should currently be mentioned in this group, i.e. as PCPTCL-NOS with TFH phenotype.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not generally relevant. TR gene rearrangements can be informative but are not disease-specific.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffuse or nodular dermal infiltrate of atypical T lymphocytes; no extracutaneous involvement of the lymphoma at time of diagnosis; diagnosis of exclusion (does not conform to one of the specifically defined entities of primary CTCL).

Desirable: molecular studies to exclude other specific entities in selected cases.

Staging

PCPTCL-NOS is staged according to the International Society for Cutaneous Lymphomas (ISCL) and Cutaneous Lymphoma Task Force of the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) guidelines {17339420}.

Prognosis and prediction

In general, the prognosis of PCPTCL-NOS is poor, with mean overall survival rates between 27 months {29573477} and 5.6 years {27498959}. Some studies reported a better prognosis for solitary lesions {29573477}. This might provide a rationale for radiotherapy in such cases {12750155; 29573477; 32997839}. B symptoms, multifocal lesions, and systemic involvement are unfavourable prognostic criteria {27498959}. In disseminated lesions, multiagent chemotherapy is used, even if not very effective {12750155; 32997839}.
