5.7.2.5 Ewing sarcoma

Definition

Ewing sarcoma is a small round cell sarcoma showing gene fusions involving one member of the FET family of genes (usually EWSR1) and a member of the ETS family of transcription factors. Superficial / primary cutaneous lesions are rare but well described.

ICD-O coding

9364/3 Ewing sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B52.3 Ewing sarcoma of soft tissue

2B52.Y Ewing sarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of other specific sites

Related terminology

Not recommended: Askin tumour (Ewing sarcoma arising in the chest wall); primitive neuroectodermal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ewing sarcoma most commonly involves the lower limbs (38% of cases), followed by the upper extremities (26%), head (20%), and trunk (16%). Extraskeletal Ewing sarcoma occurs in approximately 12% of patients and has a wide anatomical distribution, including superficial / primary cutaneous sites {22098102; 33635948; 29977059; 9363452; 21649689; 19783720; 18820660; 9500772}.

Clinical features

Ewing sarcoma manifests as dermal or subcutaneous nodules that may be painful, polypoid, and/or ulcerated. The median tumour size is 23 mm (range: 5–120 mm) {9363452; 9500772; 19783720; 18820660}.

Epidemiology

Cutaneous Ewing sarcoma tends to occur in children and young adults, but it has been reported in older individuals. The median age at diagnosis is 17 years (range: 2–77 years); 67% of patients are female, and 33% are male {22098102; 33635948; 29977059; 9363452; 21649689; 19783720; 18820660; 9500772}.

Etiology

The majority of cases appear to be sporadic, but germline mutations in genes predisposing to cancer have been described {26580448}.

Pathogenesis

As with their deeper counterpart, the pathogenesis of cutaneous Ewing sarcomas is driven by transcription factors encoded by chimeric FET::ETS gene fusions, most commonly EWSR1::FLI1 and EWSR1::ERG {24680181; 30709443; 9500772; 7493136; 21649689}. Chimeric fusion genes encode transcription factors that serve as master regulators that activate and repress thousands of genes. Expression of these transcription factors in the correct tumour microenvironment is ostensibly required for the development of Ewing sarcoma. Proteins bind both GGAA microsatellites and canonical ETS binding sites in the genome and recruit chromatin regulators. Binding of the fusion proteins to GGAA microsatellites results in transition from a closed to open chromatin state that establishes de novo enhancers which activate genes. Conversely, binding to canonical ETS binding sites displaces wildtype ETS factors and represses gene expression. In combination, these events establish an oncogenic gene expression programme that underlies tumour initiation {10623711; 29977059}.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface of untreated Ewing sarcoma specimens is grey to white and soft. Foci of haemorrhage and necrosis are common.

Histopathology

Tumours are composed of sheets of mitotically active, uniform small blue round cells with indistinct cell borders and round nuclei with fine stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. Constituent cells have a high N:C ratio and scant, lightly eosinophilic to clear glycogenated cytoplasm that gives a positive PAS reaction {9363452; 9500772; 19841938}.

Immunohistochemistry

Membranous CD99 immunoreactivity is a highly sensitive but nonspecific finding in nearly all cases. Neural markers (e.g. NSE, S100, PGP9.5, and CD57) {2163356} and epithelial markers (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2) may occasionally be expressed, but desmin positivity is rare and typically only very focal {11176079; 16006796; 10716155; 19841938}. FLI1 and ERG are often expressed in cases with the corresponding translocation; some cross-reactivity has been reported {22766791}. NKX2-2 is sensitive but imperfectly specific {26847175; 22446943; 29661713}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes a very rare subset of superficially located Ewing-like undifferentiated small blue round cell sarcomas such as CIC-rearranged and BCOR-rearranged sarcomas, representing distinct entities.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular confirmation is often required for a diagnosis of Ewing sarcoma, as round cell morphology is common to other sarcomas, carcinomas, and lymphomas. All cases of Ewing sarcoma harbour FET::ETS fusions. The EWSR1::FLI1 fusion is seen in > 95% of cutaneous and superficial Ewing sarcomas {8022439; 9500772; 8834175; 9121764}. Rarely, an EWSR1::ERG fusion is present {7700648; 27526022; 8162068; 18820660}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: small blue round cell cytomorphology; membranous CD99 immunoreactivity.

Desirable: detection of a FET::ETS fusion.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM systems may be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Cutaneous and superficial Ewing sarcomas have a better prognosis and outcome than their deep-seated soft tissue and bony counterparts {22098102; 27186040; 21464495; 18820660; 9363452}. Long-term survival has been reported both with and without adjuvant therapy {27297500; 10661351; 25894676; 11186429}. The overall survival rate is 93% and the 10-year probability of survival is estimated at approximately 90% {22098102; 26304893}.
