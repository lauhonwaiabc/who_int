2.1.1.11 Combined naevus

Definition

Combined naevus is a benign melanocytic neoplasm with two or more phenotypically distinct tumour cell populations that is not a WNT-activated, BAP1-inactivated, or pigmented epithelioid melanocytoma; a proliferating nodule; or a collision tumour.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Combined naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH0DU8 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Combined naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: biphenotypic naevus; clonal naevus; naevus with phenotypic heterogeneity; naevus with dermal epithelioid component.

Not recommended: inverted type A naevus.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Combined naevus can occur on any anatomical skin site.

Clinical features

Patients with combined naevus commonly present with a papule or nodule in a patch or plaque with different pigmentation. There can be a history of recent change.

Epidemiology

Combined naevi account for < 1% of melanocytic naevus biopsies. They can develop at any age but typically arise in young adults (mean age: ~30 years). There is no sex predilection {15370111}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Combined naevi are probably a heterogeneous group of lesions representing forms of stepwise tumour progression similar to melanocytomas, in which a phenotypically distinct subclone arises as a consequence of an additional pathogenic mutation.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Typically, there is a compound or dermal naevus of the superficial congenital type with a subpopulation of morphologically distinct cells, usually situated in the superficial dermis. The clone can have varying morphology, but unpigmented epithelioid or hyperpigmented melanocytes are most common. Cytological atypia and mitotic activity are limited {21881487; 15370111}.

Immunohistochemistry

The melanocyte population of the background naevus and the subclone may express different immunophenotypes.

Differential diagnosis

Immunohistochemistry or molecular studies can assist in ruling out BAP1-inactivated, WNT-activated, and pigmented epithelioid melanocytoma, as well as proliferative nodules in congenital naevi.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The small size of the clone often limits the potential to study its molecular features. By definition, alterations defining any specific melanocytomas should be absent.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: two or more cytologically distinct populations of melanocytes in a benign melanocytic lesion that is not a specifically defined melanocytoma.

Desirable: special immunostains may be used to rule out specific melanocytomas (β-catenin, BAP1, PRKAR1A).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Combined naevi are benign. Recurrences are rare.
