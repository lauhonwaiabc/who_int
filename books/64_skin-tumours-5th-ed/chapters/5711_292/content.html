5.7.1.1 Cellular neurothekeoma

Definition

Cellular neurothekeoma is a benign cutaneous neoplasm of uncertain histogenesis. This tumour type is not related to nerve sheath myxoma (formerly “myxoid neurothekeoma”).

ICD-O coding

9562/0 Cellular neurothekeoma

ICD-11 coding

2F24 & XH1UZ6 Benign cutaneous neoplasms of neural or nerve sheath origin & Cellular neurothekeoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: neurothekeoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cellular neurothekeoma has a predilection for the face (in particular the nasomalar and nasolabial areas), shoulders, and upper limbs {16327434; 7446487; 17325474; 3297114}. It is usually solitary, although rare examples of multiple lesions have been reported {17592278; 16441413}.

Clinical features

Cellular neurothekeoma manifests as a solitary firm papule or nodule with no distinctive clinical features.

Epidemiology

Cellular neurothekeoma occurs most commonly in children and young adults, with a female predominance {16327434; 7446487; 17325474; 3297114}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The histogenesis is uncertain, but the gene expression profile is similar to that of cellular fibrous histiocytoma, suggesting myofibroblastic differentiation {21297585}.

Macroscopic appearance

Cellular neurothekeoma appears as a well-circumscribed dermal nodule.

Histopathology

Cellular neurothekeoma is a lobulated or micronodular neoplasm composed of nests of usually epithelioid but occasionally spindled cells with a vesicular nucleus, small nucleolus, and pale eosinophilic cytoplasm, often separated by dense collagenous stroma {17325474}. Some tumours contain prominent myxoid stroma, and rare cases may show an infiltrative growth pattern {29744902}. Occasional neoplastic cells may be multinucleated or may show hyperchromatic nuclei, and mitotic figures are common {1880258; 2154139; 24186141}. In rare cases, areas of calcification {3745622}, ossification {8030774}, perineural invasion {24186141}, and a plexiform pattern with hybrid features of both cellular neurothekeoma and perineurioma can be seen {23598966}.

Immunohistochemistry

The most consistent markers expressed in cellular neurothekeoma are CD63 (NKI/C3) {8188941; 33264109}, NSE {7693776}, PGP9.5 {10555009}, and S100A6 {12641790}, as well as KBA.62 and PRAME (these last two are also expressed in melanoma) {24151815; 34761425}. Expression of MITF, SMA, and podoplanin (D2-40) is more variable {19318798; 14685254; 24186141; 33264109}. Cellular neurothekeoma does not express S100, GFAP, CD57, SOX10, or EMA {1880258; 2154139; 3297114; 16327434; 24475873}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes nerve sheath myxoma, Spitz naevus, cutaneous leiomyoma, and plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour. Nerve sheath myxomas and Spitz naevi are positive for S100. Cutaneous leiomyoma has an intersecting fascicular pattern and is desmin-positive. Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour has a biphasic appearance and a plexiform growth pattern.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a multilobulated neoplasm; nests of epithelioid cells with a vesicular nucleus, small nucleolus, and pale eosinophilic cytoplasm, often separated by dense collagenous or myxoid stroma.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Cellular neurothekeoma is benign, and complete surgical removal is curative.
