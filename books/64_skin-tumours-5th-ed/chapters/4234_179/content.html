4.2.3.4 ALK-positive histiocytosis

Definition

ALK-positive histiocytosis is a histiocytic neoplasm lacking high-grade cytological atypia and characterized by anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) immunoreactivity, usually due to ALK gene rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

9750/3 ALK-positive histiocytosis

ICD-11 coding

EK92 Histiocytoses of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Acceptable: ALK-related histiocytosis; ALK-rearranged histiocytosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

See #35151Table 6.06 .

Clinical features

The skin lesions take the form of solitary or multiple nodules or papules, representing sole lesions or part of a multisystem disease {34727172; 30573850}.

Epidemiology

ALK-positive histiocytosis is rare. The multisystem systemic form occurs in infancy, while the multisystem (others) and single-system forms can occur in any age group, including children and adults {30573850; 34482333; 31119374; 32826530; 31371408; 32721959; 29224419; 34727172}. Patients are more commonly female than male {34727172}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ALK-positive histiocytosis is characterized by ALK gene translocation, with the commonest partner gene being KIF5B {30573850; 34727172}, and uncommon ones being CLTC, TPM3, TFG, EML4, DCTN1, COL1A2, and TRIM33 {34727172; 30573850; 33243034}. ALK rearrangement can lead to the activation of many different signalling pathways, including the MAPK pathway {34727172}.

Macroscopic appearance

Skin lesions are macules, papules, nodules, or plaques, with whitish-yellow cut surfaces {30573850; 34727172}.

Histopathology

The skin shows a non-circumscribed, non-epidermotropic infiltrate comprising sheets of histiocytes, which may be accompanied by a variable degree of fibrosis. The histiocytes range in appearance from large oval (epithelioid), foamy, spindle cells to multinucleated cells, which frequently show irregular nuclear contours but no significant pleomorphism. They have abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm that may show fine vacuolation, haemosiderin deposition, and emperipolesis. There can be admixed Touton giant cells {30573850; 34727172}. The morphology can be indistinguishable from that of juvenile xanthogranuloma.

Immunohistochemistry

The histiocytes are ALK-positive {30573850; 32826530; 34727172} and show variable positivity for histiocytic markers, such as CD68, CD163, CD14, CD4, and lysozyme. Some cases may show staining for S100, cyclin D1, or OCT2 {34727172}. Fascin and factor XIIIa are commonly positive, while CD30, CD1a, and CD207 (langerin) are negative. The Ki-67 proliferation index is low.

Differential diagnosis

See #36272Table 6.05 in section Rosai–Dorfman disease for differential diagnosis.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of ALK translocation is helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tissue infiltration by aggregates and sheets of histiocytes lacking high-grade atypia; positive immunostaining for ALK (albeit focal and weak sometimes) and two or more histiocytic markers (CD163, CD68, CD14, CD4, lysozyme).

Desirable: histiocytes showing irregular nuclear foldings; ALK gene translocation (except in limited specimens).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The outcome is variable depending on the pattern of disease involvement {18660380; 30573850; 34727172}. Patients with multisystem disease may benefit from systemic chemotherapies or ALK inhibitors {30573850; 32826530; 34727172}. Single-system involvement can be cured by surgical ablation where complete removal is possible {30573850; 31119374; 34482333; 34727172}. Skin lesions may regress spontaneously {30573850}.
