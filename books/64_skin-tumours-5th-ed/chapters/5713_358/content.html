5.7.1.3 Non-neural granular cell tumour

Definition

Non-neural granular cell tumour (NNGCT) is a rare, low-grade neoplasm of mesenchymal cells of unknown lineage with prominent cytoplasmic granularity.

ICD-O coding

8990/1 Non-neural granular cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2E89.1 & XH5LL8 Benign tumours of uncertain differentiation, soft tissue & Primitive non-neural granular cell tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: primitive polypoid granular cell tumour; primitive non-neural granular cell tumour; dermal non-neural granular cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The back was the preferred site in one series; in another, the lesions were widely distributed on the torso, limbs, and face {16045779; 15958858}.

Clinical features

The lesions are most commonly non-ulcerated papules or nodules.

Epidemiology

NNGCT occurs in children and adults {1985501; 16045779; 15958858}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

In a small series, 3 of 5 NNGCTs (60%) harboured ALK rearrangements {30001233}. Other pathogenic alterations have not been identified.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

NNGCTs can be polypoid. Some lesions are gently domed. The lesional cells often extend to the base of the dermis or into the subcutis. The overlying epidermis is usually thin, with epithelial collarettes defining the lateral edges. The granular cells form sheets and are large and oval, with coarse eosinophilic granules. The cellular granularity is due to the accumulation of lysosomes in the cytoplasm of the affected cells. The largest granules are surrounded by clear haloes (pustulo-ovoid bodies of Milian). The nuclei can vary from moderately sized and monomorphic to large, pleomorphic, and heterochromatic. Mitoses can range from few to many (as many as 6 mitoses/mm2), and atypical mitoses can occur. While typically circumscribed, some cases show poorly demarcated deep margins {16045779}. A few cases have shown lymphatic invasion {1985501}.

Immunohistochemistry

The cells of NNGCTs fail to stain for S100 and other neural or melanocytic markers {1985501; 16045779; 15958858}. They usually stain positively for CD68 and CD63 (NKI/C3), probably because of reactivity with lysosomes in the cytoplasm, but such positivity is not lineage-specific; it can also be seen in the cells of many other neoplasms. They are negative for desmin, SMA, and cytokeratin.

Differential diagnosis

The diagnosis of NNGCT is one of exclusion. While the tumours often resemble conventional granular cell tumour of Schwannian origin, a polypoid configuration, epithelial collarettes, nuclear pleomorphism (including prominent nucleoli), and easily detectable mitoses favour NNGCT. Unlike NNGCTs, conventional granular cell tumours are positive for S100 and SOX10. Granular cell fibrous papules are usually found on the nose, are smaller dome-surfaced lesions, and have nearly clear cytoplasm with paler granules. For NNGCT with cytological atypia and mitotic activity, granular cell patterns of basal cell carcinoma, dermatofibroma, leiomyosarcoma, and dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans are considerations and can usually be distinguished by the expression of lineage-specific markers or by areas in which the conventional aspects of these neoplasms are apparent.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dermal tumour of granular cells; no expression of lineage-specific markers; nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic activity are acceptable.

Desirable: ALK-positive staining or ALK rearrangement in selected cases.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases are indolent, despite worrisome histopathological features. Few cases with regional lymph node metastasis have been reported, without further dissemination or recurrence {19734486; 15958858}.
