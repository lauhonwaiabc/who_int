1.2.1.3 Micronodular basal cell carcinoma

Definition

Micronodular basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is an aggressive type of BCC characterized by small nests with an irregular, tentacular, and infiltrative deep or peripheral edge. It typically extends deeply into the dermis or beyond.

ICD-O coding

Localization

Micronodular BCC is most commonly situated on the head and neck.

Clinical features

Patients with micronodular BCC usually present with a pink papule or nodule that is often ulcerated and cannot be distinguished from nodular BCC on naked-eye examination or dermoscopy.

Histopathology

Micronodular BCC is composed predominantly of rounded, ovoid, or slender groups of basaloid cells that are often scattered throughout the dermis and subcutis {34931722; 35885545}. These may appear as separate satellites outlined by a thin rim of stroma and separated by normal dermis. The tumours have an irregular, tentacular, and infiltrative deep or peripheral edge composed predominantly (i.e. > 50%) of rounded, small, discrete groups of cells (< 0.15 mm in diameter). Single nodules may be seen distant from the bulk of the main tumour. The tumours can reach a large size and may demonstrate perineural involvement. This type of BCC is frequently mixed with other types, including nodular and infiltrative BCC. Punch biopsies have a significant false negative rate for detecting micronodular BCC and mixed tumours. Section misorientation and the lack of strict definitions in terms of the size and number of micronodules may contribute to poor interobserver reproducibility in the diagnosis of this BCC type {27311865; 22989368; 34931722}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes nodular BCC with focal micronodular architecture, but such tumours are encased by a common stroma and lack the satellite pattern seen in micronodular BCC.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an infiltrative, permeative, and tentacular growth pattern; tumour composed predominantly of rounded small nests.

Desirable: extension into the deep dermis or beyond.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic data for micronodular BCC are limited by inconsistent histological classification and poor interobserver reproducibility. Nevertheless, this tumour type appears to be strongly associated with an increased risk of local recurrence {26050215; 27311865; 34931722}.
