3.1.2.13 Syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum

Definition

Syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum is a rare adnexal neoplasm evolving (in most cases) from a pre-existing syringocystadenoma papilliferum, usually in the setting of naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn.

ICD-O coding

8406/3 Syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH0BE5 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Because syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum usually evolves from a pre-existing syringocystadenoma papilliferum (a common secondary tumour in naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn), the most common location is the head and neck area, particularly the scalp {20216201}.

Clinical features

The lesion appears clinically as a nodule, inflammatory plaque, or tumour (sometimes ulcerated), 20–60 mm in size. It is sometimes associated with a sebaceous naevus {26317389}.

Epidemiology

Syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum is extremely rare, with < 100 reported cases {8735727; 17519621; 20216201}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

In a benign precursor, syringocystadenoma papilliferum, mutation of BRAF and HRAS has been found {25532942; 30398985; 31294472}. A case of syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum with in-frame alteration in MAP2K1 has been reported {29578881}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with a solitary nodule, mass, or plaque with a papillary surface. Ulceration and exudates can be present.

Histopathology

In most cases, the tumour shows the overall papillary architecture of syringocystadenoma papilliferum (or direct transition from syringocystadenoma papilliferum can be seen), with areas exhibiting nuclear and cellular atypia, abnormal mitotic figures, loss of polarity, and a focal disorderly proliferation of the neoplastic cells, resulting in solid areas and/or cribriform structures. In situ tumours are associated with an intact myoepithelial cell layer. The invasive adenocarcinomatous component usually appears as a well-differentiated or moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma; rarely, an intradermal proliferation of small ductal structures composed of a single cell layer involving the full thickness of the dermis or sarcomatoid (metaplastic) carcinoma is seen {14631190} [[Kazakov DV, Michal M, Kacerovska D, et al., editors. Cutaneous adnexal tumors. Philadelphia (PA): Lippincott Williams & Wilkins; 2012.]] {26317389; 17997741}. Intravascular invasion is extremely rare {26317389}. Other rare appearances of the invasive component include well-differentiated to moderately differentiated squamous cell carcinoma, such as verrucous carcinoma, lesions resembling lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma, and tumours with a basaloid component {20216201; 22343110; 31449665}. Other findings include various ductal changes in the intradermal component, pagetoid spread of the neoplastic cells within the adjacent/overlying epidermis or hair follicles (or both), and atypical stroma {27533073; 20216201; 16722938; 31237708}. Features of a pre-existing sebaceous naevus or other secondary neoplasm occurring in the naevus can sometimes be recognized {17519621; 20216201; 26317389}.

Immunohistochemistry

The in situ component can be highlighted by staining for myoepithelial cell markers. The epithelial cells, in both the pre-existing benign lesion and the carcinoma (including areas with pagetoid spread), stain for CK7 and CAM5.2. BRAF p.V600E can be detected by VE1 immunohistochemisty in both the precursor lesion and the carcinoma component {16722938; 31449665}.

Differential diagnosis

Areas of hyperplasia of luminal cells with mild architectural disorder, nuclear crowding, and formation of solid or cribriform areas may sometimes be seen in otherwise benign syringocystadenoma papilliferum; however, unless unequivocal cytological atypia and abnormal mitotic figures are present, such lesions should not be diagnosed as syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum (in situ) {24394301}. Rare cases of syringocystadenoma papilliferum associated with florid pseudocarcinomatous hyperplasia in the overlying or adjacent epidermis or adnexal epithelium should be distinguished from syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum in which an invasive component is represented by a well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma {20670144}. Pagetoid spread of the neoplastic cells in syringocystadenocarcinoma papilliferum should not be misinterpreted as extramammary Paget disease, especially in small specimens. Clinicopathological correlation is essential in such cases {26863065}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour with papillary architecture, with nuclear and cellular atypia, abnormal mitotic figures, loss of polarity, and a focal disorderly proliferation of the neoplastic cells; an intact myoepithelial cell layer in in situ lesions.

Desirable: remnants of benign syringocystadenoma papilliferum and/or naevus sebaceus; presence of BRAF mutation.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

Surgical excision appears to be sufficient for preventing recurrence and metastasis. Metastatic disease has rarely been reported, but one study documented a high rate of lymph node metastasis. Large tumours and those with a short disease course may behave more aggressively {23348331; 26317389; 28337777}.
