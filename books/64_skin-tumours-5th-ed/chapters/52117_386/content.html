5.2.1.17 Elastofibroma

Definition

Elastofibroma is a benign soft tissue tumour characterized by abnormal elastic fibres, bland fibroblasts, and myxoid stroma.

ICD-O coding

8820/0 Elastofibroma

ICD-11 coding

FB51.Y & XH3BQ8 Other specified fibroblastic disorders & Elastofibroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: elastofibroma dorsi.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Elastofibroma is most often subscapular. It sometimes involves the chest wall but can occur anywhere.

Clinical features

These tumours are slow-growing, typically solitary, and usually asymptomatic, but they can cause pain, limitation of movement, or scapular snapping/impingement.

Epidemiology

Elastofibroma most commonly affects elderly adults, with a female predominance.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Some cases run in families {9696981}; in one series, one third of the patients reported a familial history {7116305}.

Pathogenesis

Elastofibromas have a variety of chromosomal abnormalities. Copy gains (including gains of Xq) {12165800}, alterations to chromosome 1, and t(8;12)(q22;q24.3) {11343783} and/or t(2;19) and (X;1) rearrangements {11458949} have been found in some of the cases.

Macroscopic appearance

Elastofibromas are poorly defined firm masses ranging in size from 50 to 100 mm. The cut surface is variegated grey and white, with small areas of adipose tissue.

Histopathology

Elastofibromas are large and have poorly defined borders. They are often hypocellular with degenerative collagen fibres that are fragmented, thickened, and globular; these can be highlighted with Verhoeff elastic staining. Variable proportions of bland uniform small spindle cells, aggregates of mature adipocytes, and myxoid stroma are present {7116305}.

Cytology

There are bland spindle cells, fragmented degenerative collagen fibres (braid-like and globular) and mature adipocytes {23554149}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fragmented, thickened, and globular collagenous fibres; bland spindle cells; admixed mature adipocytes and myxoid stroma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Elastofibroma is benign, and simple excision is curative {7116305; 20130320}.
