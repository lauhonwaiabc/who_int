5.1.0.2 Lipoma

Definition

Lipoma is a benign mesenchymal tumour composed of mature adipocytes.

ICD-O coding

8850/0 Lipoma

8862/0 Osteolipoma

8862/0 Chondrolipoma

ICD-11 coding

2E80.0Z Lipoma, unspecified site

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Osteolipoma; chondrolipoma

Localization

Superficial lipomas usually occur in the subcutaneous tissue of the neck, upper trunk, and proximal limbs. Facial lesions usually only occur in a subfascial location on the forehead. Distal extremity involvement is infrequent.

Clinical features

Lipomas present as a slow-growing, mobile and painless mass. Multifocality may be sporadic or syndromic. In familial multiple lipomatosis, subcutaneous lipomas develop, usually in the third decade, on the forearms, trunk and thigh {3745530}. Multiple lipomas may also be seen in Proteus syndrome and Bannayan–Zonana syndrome.

Epidemiology

Lipoma is the most common mesenchymal neoplasm in adults, typically presenting in the fifth or sixth decade, and is slightly more common in males. The diagnosis is uncommon in children.

Etiology

The majority of lipomas are idiopathic. Familial multiple lipomatosis is usually autosomal dominant and the involved gene has been mapped to 12q14.3. Germline mutations in the PTEN tumour suppressor gene are common in patients with Proteus syndrome and Bannayan–Zonana syndrome.

Pathogenesis

The protein encoded by HMGA2 contains structural DNA-binding domains that play an important role in embryological adipogenesis {26966068; 15593017}. Structural aberrations resulting in deregulation of the HMGA2 gene are frequent in lipomas, with the most common being translocations involving 12q13-q15 {7606786; 23508853}. Rearrangements involving 6p21 (HMGA1) and interstitial deletions of 13q have also been reported {1988102; 11576996}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lipomas are well-circumscribed, with a uniform glistening, yellow to cream cut surface. Some cases show macroscopic bone and/or cartilage.

Histopathology

Lipomas display a thin capsule and are composed of lobules of mature adipocytes, dissected by thin, incomplete fibrous septa. Additional features include fat necrosis with foamy macrophages, fibrosis and myxoid change. In some lesions, metaplastic bone and/or cartilage may be seen {21822651}. Adipocytes sometimes show intranuclear inclusions (Lochkern cells).

Immunohistochemistry

Lesional adipocytes are positive for HMGA2, which helps the distinction with normal adipose tissue {20834238}. However immunohistochemistry is rarely necessary for the diagnosis.

Differential diagnosis

Distinction from atypical lipomatous tumour (ALT) is based on the presence of nuclear enlargement and hyperchromatism, and stromal atypical cells in the latter.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular studies are usually not necessary for diagnosis. In challenging cases, studies for MDM2 amplification may be helpful to exclude ALT {34997060}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobular proliferation of mature adipocytes; absence of cytological atypia (different from degenerate changes).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lipomas are benign but may rarely recur (in < 5% of cases) in a non-destructive fashion.
