3.2.1.6 Tumour of the follicular infundibulum

Definition

Tumour of the follicular infundibulum is a rare benign adnexal neoplasm showing differentiation towards the epithelium of the follicular isthmus.

ICD-O coding

8104/0 Tumour of the follicular infundibulum

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH0489 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Tumour of follicular infundibulum

Related terminology

Not recommended: infundibular tumour; tumour of the follicular isthmus; infundibuloma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Single lesions typically occur on the face, and multiple lesions favour the face and neck.

Clinical features

Tumours of the follicular infundibulum occur as solitary or (more often) multiple smooth or slightly hyperkeratotic and hypopigmented papules arranged in plaques {2069339; 7593824; 8786188}. Multiple lesions are more common on photoexposed areas and appear as grouped hypopigmented macules with a reticulated pattern, or they may present as lesions mimicking hypopigmented plane warts {19389540; 20137756; 24685851; 26143173}. In some patients, eruptive lesions have been described {6824362; 8786188; 29693061}. Tumour of the follicular infundibulum may develop in a pre-existing naevus sebaceus of Jadassohn {9082365; 19652586; 25761672}.

Epidemiology

The relative frequency reported is 3–17 cases per 100 000 skin biopsies {19633534} or 12 cases per 121 500 biopsies {7490369}. There is no sex predilection {19633534}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with hypopigmented papules arranged in plaques.

Histopathology

Tumour of the follicular infundibulum is composed of anastomosing horizontally arranged cords of pale epithelial cells, connecting at intervals with the epidermis and creating a fenestrated pattern {6392372}. Most cells within the cords are large, with pale cytoplasm (as a result of abundant intracytoplasmic glycogen) and a small round or oval nucleus. Peripheral cells are basaloid, with basophilic, poorly defined cytoplasm and palisading of the nuclei. At the periphery of neoplastic cords, epithelial prominences consisting of small infundibular cysts are seen. Small ductal structures with irregular luminal borders, similar to sebaceous ducts, and small clusters of mature sebocytes may be also seen {11401679; 21747619}. The stroma of is composed of loose fibrovascular connective tissue with numerous elastic fibres arranged in a reticular pattern.

Differential diagnosis

Superficial basal cell carcinoma shows peripheral clefting and cytological atypia.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: anastomosing epithelial cords, similar to the epithelium of the follicular isthmus, connected to the epidermis and involving the superficial dermis with a reticulated and fenestrated pattern.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Tumours of the follicular infundibulum are benign lesions.
