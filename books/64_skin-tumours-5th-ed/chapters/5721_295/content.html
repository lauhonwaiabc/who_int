5.7.2.1 Pleomorphic dermal sarcoma

Definition

Pleomorphic dermal sarcoma (PDS) is a rare tumour occurring in elderly patients, in chronically ultraviolet (UV) radiation–exposed skin. It is genetically, morphologically, and clinically related to atypical fibroxanthoma, but subcutaneous invasion and lymphovascular/perineural invasion are not features of atypical fibroxanthoma and confer an increased risk of aggressive behaviour.

ICD-O coding

8802/3 Pleomorphic dermal sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2C35 & XH7XH3 Cutaneous sarcoma & Pleomorphic dermal sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma of the skin; cutaneous sarcoma NOS; superficial malignant fibrous histiocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The primary location is the head and neck region, with a preference for the hairless scalp {34202213; 35099104}.

Clinical features

This malignancy typically manifests as a rapidly growing nodule with a diameter of 5–50 mm (median: 25 mm). Ulceration is observed in at least 50% of the lesions {35099104}.

Epidemiology

PDS occurs on severely sun-damaged skin of White patients in their seventh or eighth decade of life {22510760; 26264237}, with a male predominance {22510760}.

Etiology

Actinic damage is a mandatory precondition. The malignancy can be associated with iatrogenic immunosuppression and haematological comorbidities, including chronic lymphocytic leukaemia or paraproteinaemias {35099104}.

Pathogenesis

PDS is an undifferentiated tumour of uncertain histogenesis that does not express epithelial, melanocytic, vascular, or muscle differentiation markers. Similarly to atypical fibroxanthoma, PDS occurs in chronically UV radiation–exposed skin, frequently with or after UV radiation–dependent cutaneous epithelial malignancies including actinic keratosis or squamous cell carcinomas, sometimes on chronic radiodermatitis. Next-generation sequencing consistently showed a high mutation load, with a UV radiation signature; mutations in TP53, NOTCH family genes, and the TERT promoter; and alterations in CDKN2A and CDKN2B responsible for cell-cycle control {34202213; 29099504}. This mutation profile widely overlaps with that of cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma.

Macroscopic appearance

PDS is a reddish to yellow nodular lesion, frequently ulcerated.

Histopathology

The tumours are centred within the dermis, with an expansile nodular or plaque-like growth pattern, poor demarcation, and irregular borders. Ulceration is frequently seen (> 50% of cases). A diffusely infiltrative growth with invasion of subcutaneous adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, or fascia is common and may be relevant for local relapse risk {32583897}.

The tumour cells vary in size and shape. Most frequent are pleomorphic spindle cells with hyperchromatic or vesicular nuclei and prominent nucleoli. In addition, epithelioid, spindled, and multinucleated giant cells may be admixed. Mitotic activity is brisk and includes atypical forms. Tumour necrosis, perineural infiltration, and lymphovascular invasion may also be seen.

The next most common morphological pattern shows atypical spindle cells in a fascicular arrangement. Other less common features include myxoid, desmoplastic, or keloidal stromal change; pseudoangiomatous features; a storiform growth pattern; and admixed osteoclast-like multinucleated giant cells.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells are consistently negative for S100, SOX10, PMEL (gp100), cytokeratins, CD34, ERG, and desmin {22510760; 24080496}. SMA and CD31 may be focally positive, and aberrant melan-A expression is occasionally seen {22510760; 22050092; 24080496; 35099104}. Immunoreactivity for CD10, CD99, and PDGFRB is typically detected.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes atypical fibroxanthoma, desmoplastic melanoma, poorly differentiated carcinoma, leiomyosarcoma, and angiosarcoma. Negative staining for ERG can be useful for excluding angiosarcoma in diagnostically difficult cases.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a pleomorphic dermal-based neoplasm; invasion of the subcutis, necrosis, perineurial infiltration, or lymphovascular invasion; exclusion of epithelial, vascular, smooth muscle, and melanocytic tumours by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Staging is not relevant, except in the setting of metastasis, in which case the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system may be helpful.

Prognosis and prediction

PDS has a certain (~20%) risk of local recurrence if not completely resected. In 10% of cases, local metastases or metastases to locoregional lymph nodes occur. Distant metastases were reported in 12% of the tumours in a recent study {34766474} after locoregional relapse. Immunosuppression is a major risk factor for metastatic spread {35099104}.
