5.7.2.3 Epithelioid sarcoma

Definition

Epithelioid sarcoma (ES) is a malignant soft tissue tumour with hybrid mesenchymal and epithelial morphological and immunophenotypic features. Two subtypes are described: classic ES tends to arise in acral skin, with a palisading, pseudogranulomatous growth pattern; proximal ES occurs in deep truncal locations as nests and sheets of larger epithelioid cells.

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Classic epithelioid sarcoma; proximal epithelioid sarcoma

Localization

Classic ES arises in the distal extremities, predominantly on the volar surfaces of the hands and feet {4014539; 26645461}. Proximal-type ES arises in deep soft tissues, most often at truncal locations or in the buttock or hip {9042279; 11454997}.

Clinical features

Classic ES manifests as superficial, slow-growing, painless, firm nodules. Sporotrichoid spread is characteristic. Non-healing skin ulcers mimic other processes {33247494}. Soft tissue ES is larger and more infiltrative {9042279; 11454997}. ES may metastasize to lymph nodes and distant sites.

Epidemiology

ES represents < 1% of adult soft tissue sarcomas {19224301} and 4–8% of childhood non-rhabdomyosarcomatous soft tissue sarcomas {16353216}. Classic ES is reported twice as often as proximal ES {19141382; 18607629}. The M:F ratio is 2:1 for classic ES {26645461} and 1.6:1 for proximal-type ES {9042279; 11454997}. Classic ES occurs mainly in adolescents and young adults. Proximal-type ES affects a somewhat older population of young to middle-aged adults {26645461}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The SMARCB1 gene (also known as BAF47, INI1, and SNF5) encodes a component of the SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex present in normal cells. SWI/SNF has ATPase activity and functions to shift the position of nucleosomes modulating transcription of genes involved in cell differentiation {28945250}. Loss of nuclear SMARCB1 protein expression is characteristic of ES, but an extreme minority retain SMARCB1 (INI1) expression, instead exhibiting abnormal expression of other SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex members, such as SMARCA4 (BRG1), SMARCC1 (BAF155), or SMARCC2 (BAF170) {29309303}. Genomic analysis has revealed complex copy-number aberrations and relatively high mutation burden {26365879}. RNA sequencing shows a unique profile that does not cluster with any particular tissue type or other SWI/SNF-aberrant system {26365879; 26343384}.

Macroscopic appearance

Classic ES presents as indurated, poorly defined, dermal or subcutaneous nodules measuring a few millimetres to 50 mm. Deep-seated tumours are multinodular. The cut surface is glistening grey and punctuated by necrotic and haemorrhagic foci. Proximal ES presents as whitish nodules ranging from 10 to 200 mm, with areas of haemorrhage and necrosis {9042279; 11454997}.

Histopathology

Classic ES consists of infiltrative, pseudogranulomatous nodules of epithelioid and spindled tumour cells palisading around foci of necrobiosis {3717528}. Constituent cells are large, with deeply eosinophilic cytoplasm and atypical nuclei with vesicular chromatin and small nucleoli. Mitoses are inconspicuous. Dystrophic calcification and metaplastic bone are seen in 20% of cases {4014539}. Proximal ES is characterized by sheet-like growth of larger epithelioid cells with vesicular nuclei with nucleoli {9042279; 11454997}. Tumour necrosis is frequently seen. Rhabdoid cells occur in both forms but are more frequent in proximal ES {9042279}.

ES is high-grade by definition.

Immunohistochemistry

Both subtypes show immunoreactivity for cytokeratins and EMA {10452506}. Most express CK8/19 {10452506} but are negative for CK5/6 {12946229}. CD34 is expressed in > 50% of cases. Loss of nuclear SMARCB1 expression occurs in the vast majority of ESs {19033866; 18973917; 21357725}. ERG expression is observed in 40–67% of ESs {23774169; 24072183; 25479928}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes granulomatous processes, carcinomas, and epithelioid vascular tumours.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In challenging cases, panel sequencing may detect mutations inactivating SMARCB1 {30652038}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour presenting as a soft tissue mass (termed distal or proximal presentations); epithelioid to spindled cytomorphology with infiltrative growth; diffuse loss of SMARCB1 (INI1, BAF47); staining for EMA and keratin.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

Reported 5- and 10-year survival rates are 45–70% and 45–66%, respectively {24046109; 25663591; 28756018; 28799063}. Localized disease has an overall survival rate of 62–88%. Overall survival drops to 24% with metastasis {11454997; 24046109; 28756018}. Location in the hand correlates with good outcome {28756018}. Deep location correlates with lower overall survival {24046109; 28756018; 28799063}. Rates of local recurrence, lymph node dissemination, and metastasis for localized ES are 14–25%, 34–52%, and 33%, respectively {24046109; 28756018; 28799063}. Management relies on surgery aiming for R0 margins {32307188}. About 22–30% of cases are metastatic at diagnosis {24046109; 25663591; 28756018}. EZH2 inhibitors have been approved for ES {33035459}. Responses to checkpoint inhibitors have been described {32815894; 34692503}. Unresectable ES has a poor response to chemotherapy and short progression-free survival {33296083}.
