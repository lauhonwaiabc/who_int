5.3.2.7 Composite haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Composite haemangioendothelioma (CHE) is a locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing endothelial neoplasm composed of an admixture of components, each resembling other vascular lesions. Neuroendocrine marker expression may be present.

ICD-O coding

9136/1 Composite haemangioendothelioma

9136/1 Neuroendocrine composite haemangioendothelioma

ICD-11 coding

2F72.Y & XH8D24 Other specified neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of skin & Composite haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Neuroendocrine composite haemangioendothelioma

Localization

CHE most often occurs distally, especially on the hands and feet or the head and neck {10716148; 18190450; 26050262; 28731049; 17895759}.

Clinical features

The lesions are longstanding (up to several decades) reddish-blue nodules or plaques {10716148}. Some patients have a history of lymphoedema.

Epidemiology

CHE affects young adults, with a female predominance; infants and children are rarely affected {17895759; 10716148; 21233686}.

Etiology

Rarely, CHEs have arisen in the setting of chronic lymphoedema or radiotherapy {10716148; 18190450; 26050262; 28731049; 17895759}.

Pathogenesis

CHEs display endothelial differentiation. A subset of tumours (27% in one study) harbour YAP1::MAML2 gene fusions {32991341; 34791601}. PTBP1::MAML2 and EPC1::PCH2 gene fusions have been identified in 3 cases and 1 case of neuroendocrine CHE, respectively {28731049; 34862698}.

Macroscopic appearance

CHE presents as an infiltrative, uninodular or multinodular mass (individual nodules measure 4–300 mm; median size: 32 mm) or as an area of poorly defined swelling. Some of the lesions are associated with reddish-purple skin discolouration, suggestive of a vascular neoplasm.

Histopathology

There is a complex and variable admixture of histological patterns, including retiform, spindle cell, epithelioid, lymphangioma, haemangioma, and even well-differentiated angiosarcoma–like components {10716148}. They most often consist of areas identical to retiform haemangioendothelioma, juxtaposed to foci somewhat resembling epithelioid haemangioendothelioma {17895759}. Neuroendocrine CHE is characterized by a distinctive admixture of retiform haemangioendothelioma–like areas, epithelioid haemangioendothelioma–like areas, and a component with a striking nested appearance, as well as by expression of neuroendocrine markers (typically synaptophysin).

Immunohistochemistry

The endothelial markers CD31, CD34, FLI1, and ERG are positive. CHE with YAP1::MAML2 fusions show loss of expression of YAP1 C-terminus {34148063}. Neuroendocrine CHE is consistently positive for synaptophysin but usually negative for chromogranin {28731049}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes retiform haemangioendothelioma, epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, and angiosarcoma. CHEs showing foci resembling high-grade angiosarcoma are extremely rare, and this diagnosis should be made only after conventional angiosarcoma is rigorously excluded on clinical and morphological grounds.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: presence of at least two morphologically distinct vascular tumour elements, most often closely resembling retiform haemangioendothelioma and epithelioid haemangioendothelioma.

Note: Cases arising in pre-existing (lymphatic) vascular malformations must show at least two additional discrete endothelial tumour components.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The behaviour of CHE in general appears to be much less aggressive than that of conventional angiosarcoma {10716148; 17895759}. Multiple local recurrences over a protracted course are common. Several lesions recurred locally between 4 months and 10 years after excision of the primary mass, often with multiple recurrences. There is potential for lymph node metastasis, although it is rare {17895759; 10716148; 18190450}. Neuroendocrine CHE appears to be considerably more aggressive, with distant metastases to bone, lung, liver, or brain reported in half of the patients {28731049}.
