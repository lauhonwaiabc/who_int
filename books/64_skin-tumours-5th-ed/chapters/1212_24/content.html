1.2.1.2 Superficial basal cell carcinoma

Definition

Superficial basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is a type of BCC, often with multifocal epidermal connection, that is confined to the papillary dermis.

ICD-O coding

8091/3 Superficial basal cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C32.2 Superficial basal cell carcinoma of skin

Related terminology

Not recommended: superficial multifocal basal cell carcinoma; superficial multicentric basal cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Superficial BCC occurs most frequently on the trunk and limbs and accounts for 10–30% of BCCs.

Clinical features

Superficial BCC is characterized by erythematous, often annular, mildly scaly patches with rolled borders, ranging from 1–2 mm to > 100 mm in diameter {30741348}. Dermoscopy most often reveals short fine telangiectasia, multiple small erosions, and shiny white structures {31706938}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Lobules of basaloid cells with peripheral palisading extend from the base of the epidermis, and occasionally the adnexal epithelium, into the papillary dermis {23627639; 30741348}. Retraction of lobules from surrounding fibromyxoid stroma and a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate may be seen {11176057}. Lesions may appear multifocal but are connected in three dimensions {3631446}. Admixed regions of nodular, micronodular, and infiltrative BCC may occur.

Differential diagnosis

BerEP4/EpCAM is positive in BCC but negative in actinic keratosis {10816226}. Follicular induction resembling superficial BCC may be seen over dermatofibromas, scars, and other reactive or neoplastic conditions {25222196}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobules of basaloid cells with peripheral palisading extend from the base of the epidermis into the papillary dermis.

Staging

Prognosis and prediction

Superficial BCC is considered to have a low risk of recurrence and a negligible risk of metastasis (see section Basal cell carcinomas).
