2.4.2.1 Acral melanoma

Definition

Acral melanoma is a melanoma that occurs on the palms, soles, volar surface of fingers and toes, and nail units.

ICD-O coding

8744/3 Acral melanoma

8744/3 Desmoplastic acral melanoma

8744/3 Subungual melanoma

ICD-11 coding

2C30.3 Acral lentiginous melanoma, primary

Related terminology

Acceptable: nail apparatus melanoma; acral lentiginous melanoma.

Not recommended: plantar lentiginous melanoma; palmar-plantar-subungual-mucosal melanoma; melanotic whitlow.

Subtype(s)

Desmoplastic acral melanoma; subungual melanoma

Localization

Acral melanoma occurs on the volar surfaces of the hands, feet, or nail units {7438021}, most commonly on the sole, where weight-bearing locations such as the heel are most commonly affected {27305207; 34079182}. Subungual melanoma affects the fingernails more frequently than the toenails {33065755; 24025653; 18043047}. In Asian populations, the thumbnail is most often affected and right-side involvement is most common, whereas in White people left-side fingernails are common sites {33935456; 24067997; 30565042}.

Clinical features

Acral lentiginous melanoma (ALM) is the most common pattern of acral melanoma. It starts with an in situ component that manifests as a variably pigmented flat patch with irregular outline, progressing to invasive melanoma with a darker papule or nodule over months to years, with ulceration in advanced lesions. Amelanotic or hypopigmented acral melanoma may be misdiagnosed as poroma, callus, wart, or tinea pedis {23972510}. Subungual melanoma begins as longitudinal melanonychia {33065755; 26028523}, with progressive widening and blurring of margins. Extension of pigment into the skin beyond the nail fold or hyponychium (Hutchinson sign) is virtually diagnostic {21542738; 32314423; 30339563}. Some lesions may be amelanotic. The parallel ridge pattern seen by dermoscopy consists of brown to black, band-like pigmentation along ridges of skin markings and is highly characteristic of acral melanoma {9606325; 15492186; 21175752}. Less typically, subungual melanoma can show irregular colours, longitudinal lines on a brown background, and/or a wide pigmented band that narrows from proximal to distal {12374538; 17320240; 21175755}.

Epidemiology

The relative incidence of acral melanoma varies with ethnicity and geography. Acral melanoma represents only 2–3% of cutaneous melanomas in the USA {19380664}, but it is the most frequent subtype in Asian, Hispanic, and African populations {19380664; 27576582; 17352373}, representing > 50% of melanomas {24068331}. Subungual melanoma accounts for as many as 30% of acral melanomas {34079182; 28707317}. The incidence of acral melanoma increases with age (mean age: 63 years) {28342269; 10951133; 19380664; 16911282}. The incidence rates are similar in both sexes.

Etiology

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure and a personal/family history of melanoma play a minimal role {28373299; 16911282}. Physical trauma may be an etiological factor {34079182}. The secretory portion of eccrine sweat glands on the volar surface has been suggested to provide an anatomical niche for the melanocyte precursor cells of ALM {25818865; 25065272; 34348144}. Patients with acral melanoma have other cancers more frequently than patients with non-acral melanomas {26584878}.

Pathogenesis

Predominance in weight-bearing locations suggests a possible role for mechanical stress and shear force in acral melanomas {27305207; 28717212; 28745985}. Tumour mutation burden is lower, and structural aberrations (including multiple high-level amplifications) are more frequent, in acral melanoma than in melanomas of other pathways {16291983; 34355205; 28467829}. Oncogenic mutations of BRAF (15–21%), NRAS (15–28%), KIT (12–40%), and NF1 (11–23%) are recurrent mutations in the MAPK pathway. BRAF and NRAS mutations tend to be mutually exclusive, whereas KIT and NF1 mutations can co-occur. Amplifications are found recurrently at CCND1, CDK4, TERT, PAK1, GAB2, EP300, YAP1, and MDM2 loci {24460190; 28467829; 30657954; 34355205}. The amplifications arise early during progression {39034322} and can be found in a field of histopathologically normal melanocytes adjacent to the in situ portion {10766187}. Deletions in PTEN and CDKN2A are seen in as many as 25% of cases {28467829}. Structural rearrangements involving BRAF, NTRK3, ALK, and PRKCA are found in 7% of cases {30657954}.

ALMs show distinct molecular profiles from those of acral melanomas in skin with a low degree of cumulative sun damage (low-CSD melanomas). ALMs have a low mutation burden, frequent amplifications, deletions, and structural rearrangements {16291983; 28467829} and uncommonly have BRAF or NRAS mutations {29191620}. ALMs develop de novo from field cells with genetic amplifications {10766187; 39034322}, whereas acral superficial spreading melanoma might arise from acral naevi, because BRAF p.V600E mutations are common in both {33978681}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

ALM is the most common histopathological pattern of acral melanoma, followed by the nodular melanoma and low-CSD melanoma (superficial spreading melanoma) patterns {24067997; 27545198; 28296711}. The superficial spreading melanoma pattern and the nodular pattern resemble those occurring in low-CSD skin; see section Low-CSD melanoma (including superficial spreading melanoma). In situ ALM shows irregularly distributed, mostly basal melanocytes with enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei and prominent dendrites, and it can show confluent proliferation followed by dermal invasion. Melanoma cells in the invasive vertical growth phase of ALM are frequently spindle-shaped or mixed with round epithelioid cells. BRAF mutation is associated with round epithelioid cells, while NF1 mutations may show elongated and spindle cells with prominent dendrites in acral melanomas {29191620}. ALM may be desmoplastic, neurotropic, or syringotropic {7396539; 6343519}. Subungual melanoma begins with a subtle proliferation of melanocytes with hyperchromatic nuclei, scant cytoplasm, and elongated dendrites in the nail matrix {18043047; 19120763; 26423820}. A high melanocyte count, hyperchromatism, and irregular contours of nuclei are histological parameters for the early diagnosis of subungual melanoma in situ {33065755}. With progression, pagetoid spread, cytological atypia with mitosis, lymphocytic infiltrates, and dermal invasion become evident. Dermal invasion is more conspicuous in the hyponychium and appears later in the nail matrix {19120763; 24743223}.

Immunohistochemistry

HMB45 and melan-A can highlight prominent dendrites of intraepidermal melanoma cells. HMB45 can sometimes be only focally positive or negative in amelanotic acral melanoma {12709000; 23972510}. HMB45 and melan-A are negative in desmoplastic acral melanoma, but SOX10 is positive. SOX10 can also help in assessing the density and distribution of melanocytes within the nail matrix or epidermis {33065755}. PRAME is expressed in most acral melanomas, in particular in the in situ portion, but rarely in acral naevi {34800527; 34476825; 30045064}.

Differential diagnosis

Early ALM can sometimes be difficult to distinguish from acral naevus. Acral melanoma has angulated, larger, and more hyperchromatic nuclei, with fewer nests. Inflammation or a junctional component of entirely single melanocytes favours acral melanoma. Patient age, lesion size, and dermoscopic findings should all be taken into account {15492186; 21175752}. Heterologous osteocartilaginous differentiation is more common in acral melanoma than other types and represents a potential pitfall in misdiagnosing a primary osteocartilaginous lesion {23793189}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FISH for commonly amplified genes such as CCND1 or TERT, or other methods of copy-number analysis, can help confirm the diagnosis in difficult cases {34225728}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an atypical melanocytic proliferation in the epidermis, dermis, or nail unit; for ALM: a lentiginous proliferation of malignant melanocytes in the epidermis.

Desirable: demonstration of melanocytic lineage by immunohistochemistry (e.g. HMB45, melan-A, SOX10).

Staging

Acral melanoma is staged according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM criteria, like other cutaneous melanomas {29028110}.

Prognosis and prediction

Acral melanomas are associated with worse survival than non-acral melanomas {25505211}. Sex, ethnicity, sentinel lymph node status, and pathological stage are independent risk factors {25455840; 32208196; 30261102}. Amelanotic primaries are associated with lung metastasis, whereas heavily pigmented primaries first metastasize to regional lymph nodes {32891781; 33934532}.
