2.1.2.2 Simple lentigo and lentiginous melanocytic naevus

Definition

Simple lentigo and lentiginous melanocytic naevus are proliferations of individual melanocytes along the epidermal–dermal junction. Simple lentigo does not have nests but lentiginous melanocytic naevus does.

ICD-O coding

8742/0 Simple lentigo and lentiginous melanocytic naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.0Y & XH88L0 Other specified common acquired melanocytic naevus & Simple lentigo

2F20.2Y & XH9QC8 Other specified congenital melanocytic naevus & Lentiginous melanocytic naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: lentigo simplex (for simple lentigo).

Not recommended: jentigo; naevus incipiens.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Simple lentigo and lentiginous naevi occur on the skin and mucous membranes, most commonly on the trunk and extremities.

Clinical features

Simple lentigo is a round to oval, uniformly pigmented macule typically < 5 mm in diameter (range: 3–15 mm). Rarely, multiple simple lentigines may be seen, including in familial eruptive lentiginosis {16843122} and partial unilateral lentiginosis {8548990}. In the autosomal dominant Noonan and LEOPARD syndromes, caused by mutations in PTPN11, multiple lentigines are seen in association with skeletal and cardiovascular aberrations {19206169}. Lentiginous naevi are usually < 6 mm in diameter, symmetrical, and uniformly pigmented tan or brown, with a smooth border, and they may be macular or papular. Simple lentigines and lentiginous naevi typically show a reticulated pattern by dermoscopy.

Epidemiology

Lentigines and lentiginous naevi are predominantly seen in White populations; they are less common in populations with darker skin tones. They may first occur in childhood, accruing into mid-life and diminishing in number with advanced age {16645597}.

Etiology

Risk factors include phenotypic factors such as Fitzpatrick type I skin, sun exposure, and genetically predisposing factors {25307738; 20647408}.

Pathogenesis

The specific nature of oncogenic alterations in benign melanocytic proliferations with a lentiginous growth pattern has not been studied. Simple lentigines lack BRAF p.V600E mutations {19536147}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Simple lentigo is characterized by hyperpigmentation of basal layer epidermal keratinocytes with elongated rete ridges, and variably increased numbers of junctional melanocytes as solitary units. Lentiginous naevi additionally have nests of melanocytes, defined as a collection of three or more melanocytes. The cornified layer above the proliferation may contain melanin pigment. Junctional naevi do not have dermal melanocytes, whereas compound naevi do.

Lentiginous melanocytes may display slight nuclear enlargement, few pigmented dendrites, and (rarely) pigmented cytoplasm. Nested intraepidermal melanocytes may have inconspicuous cytoplasm or display variable degrees of cytoplasmic pigment. Nuclear atypia is absent.

Differential diagnosis

The major criteria that distinguish melanoma include size, symmetry, circumscription, ulceration, cytological atypia, confluent lentiginous proliferation, pagetoid scatter, and dermal mitoses. Dysplastic naevi tend to be larger and have additional attributes (see section Dysplastic naevus).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a pigmented macule; proliferation of melanocytes along the basal epidermis, either singly (lentigo simplex) or as nests (lentiginous naevus).

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Simple lentigo and lentiginous naevi are benign lesions with a very low risk of progression to melanoma. Individuals with an increased total number of lentiginous naevi may be at increased risk of developing melanoma {15617989}.
