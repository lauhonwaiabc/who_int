2.3.0.4 Spitz melanocytoma (atypical Spitz tumour)

Definition

Spitz melanocytoma (atypical Spitz tumour) is a melanocytic neoplasm characterized by one or more atypical features and is morphologically and genetically intermediate between Spitz naevus and melanoma.

Related terminology

Acceptable: atypical Spitz tumour.

Subtype(s)

The subtypes of Spitz melanocytoma are similar to the genomic subtypes of Spitz naevus. See #38596Table 3.08 in section Spitz naevus.

Localization

Like Spitz naevi, Spitz melanocytomas may arise anywhere on the skin {19318795}.

Clinical features

Spitz melanocytomas typically appear as papules, nodules, or plaques ≥ 5 mm in diameter, and they may display asymmetry, irregular borders, colour variegation, and/or ulceration {16446713; 2802011; 10086449} (see #35968Table 3.09).

Epidemiology

Spitz melanocytomas can occur at any age but are most common in adolescents and young adults.

Etiology

Spitz melanocytomas arise through genetic progression from a Spitz naevus.

Pathogenesis

Spitz melanocytomas have one of the initiating mutations of Spitz naevus, including fusions involving the kinase genes ALK, BRAF, MET, RET, ROS1, NTRK1, NTRK2, NTRK3, or MAP3K8, or HRAS mutation, but they also harbour additional pathogenic alterations such as inactivation of CDKN2A (see #35968Table 3.09). The full spectrum of secondary alterations that distinguish Spitz melanocytomas from Spitz naevi and Spitz melanomas remains to be characterized {31900433}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Spitz melanocytomas often arise as asymmetrical, poorly circumscribed proliferations of large epithelioid to spindled melanocytes, arranged as cellular aggregates or nodules that replace the dermis, with little or no maturation and a pushing lower border. Peripheral pagetoid scatter of single cells or small nests, consumption of the epidermis, and ulceration can be present {2802011; 10086449; 8625056}. Kamino bodies are less frequent, smaller, or absent. The neoplastic melanocytes usually have larger, more hyperchromatic nuclei than those of Spitz naevi, with large eosinophilic nucleoli {2802011; 8625056}. Large multinucleated melanocytes with bizarre nuclei and foci with necrotic melanocytes may be present {31719662}. The mitotic count may be increased, and atypical mitoses may be present.

The histopathological diagnostic criteria differ with age, with a higher tolerance for atypical features in children aged < 10 years (see #35968Table 3.09).

Immunohistochemistry

p16 expression is often absent {27569296; 23005921}, but absent p16 expression may also be seen in Spitz naevi and Spitz melanomas.

Differential diagnosis

Spitz melanocytoma is defined by one or more atypical histopathological features. There is low interobserver concordance {24618612; 10333219}, and a greater degree of atypia is associated with greater diagnostic uncertainty. Molecular analyses are helpful (see #35968Table 3.09). See also section Spitz naevus .

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of a Spitz initiating oncogene is helpful in confirming Spitz lineage. While rare instances of BRAF p.V600E have been reported in atypical Spitz tumours, these mutations typically indicate a melanocytic neoplasm of a different pathway, such as naevoid melanoma of the low cumulative sun damage (CSD) pathway or a BAP1-inactivated melanocytoma. Complete absence of p16 expression is associated with genetic inactivation of CDKN2A which, by definition, does not occur in Spitz naevi {27569296}. Other copy-number aberrations can be seen, often affecting chromosomal loci involved in rearrangements generating the kinase fusion {25602801; 26013381; 35104769}. Other copy-number aberrations, in particular those common in melanoma, or genetic alterations involving TERT raise concern for melanoma {26061100}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a larger, more deeply invasive, more cellular, and more atypical proliferation of epithelioid to spindled melanocytes than is acceptable for Spitz naevus; usually ≤ 10 mm in diameter; usually no frankly malignant features (large areas of necrosis, expansile nodules with high mitotic activity [≥ 6 mitoses/mm2], marked nuclear pleomorphism, multiple atypical mitoses, mitoses at the deep advancing front of the tumour).

Desirable: asymmetrical silhouette.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Spitz melanocytoma is considered a benign to low-risk neoplasm. Although distant metastasis and death has occasionally been reported, those cases may have been misclassified melanomas or may have represented further neoplastic progression of residual tumour cells. A scoring system has been proposed as an aid for risk stratification {10086449; 26061100}. Sentinel lymph node biopsy is not recommended, because it is positive in 40% of cases {16784980; 19123453; 19609204; 18000712; 24694641} without predicting an adverse outcome.
