2.1.1.3 Naevus spilus

Definition

Naevus spilus is a benign melanocytic naevus presenting as a hyperpigmented patch containing a variable number of darker macules or papules.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Naevus spilus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH40S8 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Naevus spilus

Related terminology

Acceptable: speckled lentiginous naevus.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Naevus spilus can occur at any anatomical site. In rare cases it can be seen in the oral cavity {28045755}.

Clinical features

Naevus spilus appears as a light-brown patch with black, brown, or reddish-brown macules or papules within it. It is usually 30–60 mm in diameter, and the speckles within it are commonly 2–3 mm in size. Approximately 80% of these lesions are present at birth or appear during early infancy. They may initially present as café-au-lait macules, with the hyperpigmented macules and papules developing later {11176689}.

In phakomatosis pigmentokeratotica (concomitant naevus sebaceus and naevus spilus), patients additionally have a separate orange-yellow pebbly plaque representing the naevus sebaceus.

Epidemiology

The prevalence of naevus spilus (0.2–2.8% depending on age) is similar to that of congenital melanocytic naevi {4004301; 17241580; 2187718}. There is no ethnic or sex predilection.

Etiology

Genetic and environmental factors may play a role in the occurrence of naevus spilus.

Genetic susceptibility

Speckled lentiginous naevus syndrome has been characterized as a distinct neurocutaneous phenotype in which ipsilateral neurological abnormalities are present in association with naevus spilus {11872407; 19706091; 15459538}.

Pathogenesis

Naevi spilus probably represent clonal populations of melanocytes resulting from a postzygotic HRAS mutation in a melanocyte precursor. The mutation is present in the background macule, with the individual darker papules having additional alterations, including amplification of the mutant HRAS allele {24751729; 24390138}. Mosaic translocations have been demonstrated in agminated Spitz naevi within a naevus spilus {32004563; 32386465}. Phakomatosis pigmentokeratotica may harbour HRAS or KRAS mutations {28523882}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The background hyperpigmented patch has elongated, hyperpigmented rete ridges with slightly increased numbers of melanocytes. The speckles show variable findings, including those of lentiginous junctional, compound or intradermal, congenital-pattern, Spitz, or blue naevi, and naevi with cytological atypia {32386465}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes café-au-lait macules, agminated melanocytic naevi, and partial unilateral lentiginosis.

Cytology

None

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a hyperpigmented patch containing multiple small darker papules or macules.

Desirable: patch area showing a mild increase in small junctional melanocytes and darker areas showing benign junctional or compound naevi.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Naevus spilus is a benign neoplasm. Patients with larger or segmental manifestations of naevus spilus may be at increased risk of developing a melanoma {25072686; 21571187; 28293687}.
