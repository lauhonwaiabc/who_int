5.1.0.3 Angiolipoma

Definition

Angiolipoma is a subcutaneous tumour composed of mature fat cells and clusters of thin-walled capillaries, often with fibrin thrombi.

ICD-O coding

8861/0 Angiolipoma

8861/0 Cellular angiolipoma

ICD-11 coding

2E80.0YZ & XH3C77 Lipoma, unspecified site & Angiolipoma, NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Cellular angiolipoma

Localization

Angiolipomas most frequently involve the extremities, followed by the trunk, but a wide range of sites may be involved, including the head and neck, breast, deep soft tissue, and organs {13716236; 28662999; 21087987; 20197228; 32972286}.

Clinical features

Patients with angiolipomas usually present with multiple small, painful subcutaneous nodules {30858718; 8436643}.

Epidemiology

Angiolipomas typically arise in children and young adults, with a male predominance. Approximately 5% of cases are familial with an autosomal dominant mode of inheritance {2706802}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The precise pathogenesis is unknown, but angiolipomas have frequent mutations in PRKD2 and PIK3CA. It is thought that altered PRKD2 in adipocytes probably stimulates angiogenesis {28139834}. PIK3CA mutations disrupt the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway. In mouse models, alterations of this signalling pathway can cause spontaneous development of lipomatous tumours {32662895; 26317218}.

Macroscopic appearance

Angiolipomas are usually encapsulated yellow to reddish nodules.

Histopathology

Angiolipomas have varying proportions of mature adipocytes and clusters of thin-walled capillaries, often with intravascular thrombi containing numerous platelets, embedded in hyalinized stroma with spindled cells. The vascular component is more prominent at the periphery of the tumour. Cellular angiolipomas are predominantly composed of the vascular component, with few fat cells {22935891}.

Differential diagnosis

Spindle cell lipoma lacks clusters of capillaries and has ropy collagen bundles. Kaposi sarcoma is composed of spindled cells with slit-like vascular spaces and is immunoreactive for KSHV/HHV8. The vessels of angiosarcoma have complex architecture, and the tumour cells have nuclear atypia. Intramuscular haemangiomas have more-variable vessels, rather than only clusters of capillaries.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tender, often multiple subcutaneous nodules; mature fat with a variable number of capillary vessels; fibrin microthrombi.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Angiolipomas are benign and typically do not recur.
