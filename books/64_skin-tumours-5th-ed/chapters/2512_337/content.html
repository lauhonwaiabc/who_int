2.5.1.2 Melanosis

Definition

Melanosis is the presence of pigmented macules on mucous membranes, characterized by hyperpigmentation of epithelial cells and with little to no increase in the number of junctional melanocytes.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

ED61.Y Other specified acquired melanotic macules or lentigines

Related terminology

Acceptable: lentiginosis; melanotic macule; constitutional melanosis; complexion-related melanosis.

Not recommended: non-proliferative melanocytic pigmentary patch; ephelis; freckle.

Subtype(s)

Congenital melanosis; acquired melanosis

Localization

Melanosis arises in non-cutaneous sites of the female and male genital tracts, distal urinary tract, anorectal region, oral cavity, and nasal and paranasal sinuses {25267379; 32785609; 23422641}. In women, all vulvar sites can be affected, with the labium minus being the most common localization {32785609; 25267379}. In men, the most common sites are the glans and prepuce {25267379}.

In the eye, conjunctival epithelial melanosis is usually bilateral, and it can be unifocal or multifocal. It is most common in the perilimbal region, but it can also occur in the bulbar conjunctiva and extend to the palpebral conjunctiva {19128387; 26556007; 21417906; 34424954} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Eye and orbit tumours [Internet; beta version ahead of print]. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2023. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 13). https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/65.]].

Clinical features

Clinically, melanosis presents as single or multiple asymptomatic pigmented macules or patches of varying sizes and colours that tend to be asymmetrical with poorly defined borders {25267379; 32785609}. Dermoscopically, inhomogeneous black or brown coloration is typical for melanosis {32785609; 12734496; 15274702}, whereas red, blue, or grey raises suspicion for melanoma.

Epidemiology

Melanosis is common in women {25267379; 32785609}, with approximately 8% of women developing melanosis in the genital region. The average age is 46 years {32785609}. Conjunctival melanosis is frequent in people with Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI, occurring with lower frequency in White populations {19128387; 34424954}.

Etiology

The etiology of melanosis is unknown.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis of melanosis is largely unknown. In women, hormonal status has been implicated {30883945; 22849791}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Melanosis is characterized by melanin pigment deposited in basilar keratinocytes and may be associated with a slight increase in junctional melanocytes {32785609}. Other possible features include elongation of the rete ridges, dendritic melanocytes without atypia at the dermoepidermal junction, and melanophages in the papillary dermis.

Immunohistochemistry

Conjunctival melanocytes are positive for melanocytic markers, including melan-A, MITF, HMB45, and SOX10.

Differential diagnosis

Melanocytic naevi have junctional nests. Melanoma in situ tends to have more melanocytes and is less well circumscribed.

In the conjunctiva, the differential diagnosis includes naevi and conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia/lesions, as well as pterygia or squamous cell carcinoma in situ with hyperpigmentation {19128387; 21417906; 26556007; 33130046}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analyses are usually not required for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dermoscopic findings consistent with a melanosis; melanin deposition in basilar epithelial cells; epithelial melanocytes without a significant increase in number or atypia.

Desirable: a slight increase of epithelial melanocytes; melan-A, MITF, HMB45, or SOX10 expression.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Melanoses are benign and do not need to be treated {32785609}. In the conjunctiva, malignant transformation into conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia/lesions or primary acquired melanosis with atypia and/or conjunctival melanoma is exceedingly rare {21417906}.
