2.8.0.2 Conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions

Definition

Conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesions (C-MILs) are primary acquired melanocytic proliferations confined to the conjunctival epithelium, associated with varying degrees of melanocytic atypia, from mild to severe, including melanoma in situ.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Low-grade conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesion

8720/2 High-grade conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial lesion

ICD-11 coding

2E63.1 Melanoma in situ of conjunctiva

Related terminology

Acceptable: primary acquired melanosis; conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia; melanoma in situ.

Not recommended: precancerous melanosis; premalignant melanosis; intraepithelial melanocytic proliferation with atypia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These lesions are generally unilateral and often multifocal. They vary in size, involving a mean of three clock-hours of the conjunctiva {27009410; 17884168; 31796811}. In decreasing frequency, C-MILs involve the bulbar, limbal, forniceal, and palpebral conjunctiva; they can also extend to the cornea and occur in the caruncle {17884168}.

Clinical features

C-MIL is usually a unilateral, irregularly shaped, pigmented macule or patch, which may wax and wane {27009410; 17884168; 31796811}. The lesions tend to be larger and less circumscribed than conjunctival naevi. Rarely, C-MIL is amelanotic {19128387; 17884168}.

Imaging

Imaging of conjunctival melanocytic lesions involves regular anterior segment photodocumentation with everted eyelids, slit-lamp microscopy, and potentially ultrasound biomicroscopy. Recent developments include anterior segment optical coherence tomography {29444155; 32926848} and in vivo reflectance confocal microscopy {28654937}.

Epidemiology

The precise incidence of C-MIL is not known, yet it occurs more frequently than invasive conjunctival melanoma, approximating the incidence of conjunctival naevi. Most cases occur in White individuals of middle or older age {17884168; 31796811}. There is no sex predilection {19128387; 17884168}.

Etiology

The predilection for the interpalpebral zone suggests that ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure may play a role {12788119}, but non-exposed conjunctiva may develop C-MIL as well.

Pathogenesis

C-MIL arises from basal conjunctival melanocytes {26556007}. TERT promoter mutations were found in 2 (8%) of 25 cases of primary acquired melanosis with atypia {25159205}. BRAF p.V600E alterations occur in approximately 40% of conjunctival melanomas, but some studies suggest that these cases arise from naevi with mutations rather than from C-MIL {28938534; 26425792}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

C-MIL is an intraepithelial proliferation of conjunctival melanocytes with varying degrees of cytological atypia, which determines the grade {21128776; 3972395; 33130046} (see #35920Table 3.10). The spectrum of cytological features ranges from small melanocytes with nuclear hyperchromasia and scant cytoplasm to severely atypical large pleomorphic epithelioid cells with ample cytoplasm and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli. Architectural growth patterns include basilar single cell proliferation, variably sized nests, pagetoid scatter of single cells, and a confluent growth pattern at the epithelial–stromal junction {21128776; 3972395; 33130046}. C-MIL may extend into conjunctival epithelial invaginations in the underlying lamina propria (pseudoglands of Henle), simulating invasive melanoma. Clinicopathological correlation may be needed to differentiate C-MIL from intraepithelial conjunctival naevi.

The updated fifth-edition WHO classification of C-MIL with histological criteria is summarized in #35920Table 3.10 . Low-grade C-MIL encompasses cases of primary acquired melanosis with mild atypia and/or conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia (C-MIN) scores of 2–4. High-grade C-MIL encompasses cases of primary acquired melanosis with moderate to severe atypia and/or C-MIN scores of 5–10. The term “conjunctival melanoma in situ” can be used for the most atypical high-grade C-MILs, characterized by a confluent proliferation of moderately to severely atypical melanocytes that are arranged along the epithelial–stromal junction or involve much or all of the conjunctival epithelium and/or have a C-MIN score of > 5 and/or histologically obvious melanoma without invasion.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic melanocytes are immunoreactive for melan-A, S100, HMB45, MITF, and SOX10. Cyclin D1 is of value in highlighting the atypical melanocytes {35356600}. The role of PRAME immunohistochemistry requires further examination {34089198; 34268800}.

Cytology

Not commonly performed

Diagnostic molecular pathology

FISH for imbalances of loci commonly altered in melanoma, such as RREB1 (6p25), MYB (6q23), and CCND1 (11q13), has been found to be of value in difficult cases {20100177; 23137666}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a melanocytic proliferation with cytological atypia, confined to the epithelium.

Desirable: architectural atypia (upward migration, confluent growth pattern, pagetoid growth, consumption of the epithelium); immunohistochemistry to confirm melanocytic phenotype in morphologically challenging cases.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system is used (pTis = conjunctival melanoma in situ).

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 13% of C-MILs progress to invasive melanoma {23222568; 19128387; 17884168}. Epithelioid cell cytomorphology with cytological atypia, cellular nesting, and pagetoid spread is associated with a higher risk of recurrence and progression {18985522; 3972395; 17255762; 21128776; 17884168} than C-MIL composed solely of hyperchromatic melanocytes with scant cytoplasm and nuclei devoid of discernible nucleoli {17255762; 17884168}.
