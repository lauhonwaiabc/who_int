4.2.4.1 Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour

Definition

Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour (IDCT) is a clonal proliferation of cells expressing dendritic cell markers (CD1a, S100) and lacking CD207 (langerin), with prominent cutaneous involvement.

ICD-O coding

9757/3 Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B31.6 & XH3ZM0 Indeterminate cell histiocytosis & Indeterminate dendritic cell tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: indeterminate cell histiocytosis; indeterminate dendritic cell histiocytosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

A papular skin eruption or a solitary large plaque to tumour over the arms or scalp are the most common clinical presentations. Extracutaneous involvement may occur in bone, spleen, lung, thyroid, parotid, and lymph node {3095403; 16115054; 17459045}.

Clinical features

IDCT is generally a skin-limited disorder presenting with single, multifocal, or generalized papules, nodules, or tumours. These lesions are usually soft, erythematous to brownish papules that may be ulcerated and tend to coalesce {16115054; 3401031; 26272726}. Infrequently, patients may present with single large lesions. Patients with extracutaneous IDCT present with symptoms related to the mass lesion.

Epidemiology

IDCT is rare, with about 100 cases reported in the literature {16115054}. The median age at diagnosis is 48 years; IDTC rarely occurs in children. The M:F ratio is 1.4:1 {2845833; 1856341; 8380541; 10102017}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. A fraction of IDCT cases may represent unusual cases of Langerhans cell histiocytosis lacking CD207 expression {16115054}. About 20% of cases are associated with haematological neoplasms such as non-Hodgkin lymphomas {21566494; 18813122} and chronic or acute myeloid neoplasms {28880462; 17459045; 28885734}. Cases associated with myeloid neoplasms may represent a cutaneous manifestation of the underlying and sometimes clonally related malignancy {22895265; 21228369; 28885734}.

Pathogenesis

The cell of origin was formerly considered to be an immature Langerhans cell or veiled cell {18813122}. However, the existence of veiled cells has never been confirmed in immunological studies.

Some cases have been reported to show mutations in BRAF (p.V600E), KRAS, or KMT2A (MLL) {21566494; 25787243; 28885734}. Cytogenetic anomalies including t(1;1), t(14;18), and trisomy 21, as well as recurrent ETV3::NCOA2 gene fusion, have been reported {10102017; 18813122; 25228298; 26438513; 29070620}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

In cutaneous lesions the epidermis is usually spared {28880462}. The dermis and/or subcutis is infiltrated by sheets of monomorphic mononuclear cells resembling Langerhans cells, with kidney-shaped and deeply grooved nuclei and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Rarely, the cells can appear spindled or show a blastic morphology {11737523; 37016977}. Extracutaneous involved sites show infiltration by similar lesional cells, which may exhibit variable degrees of nuclear atypia and mitotic activity {31955466}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells are S100-positive, CD1a-positive, and CD207 (langerin)-negative; ultrastructurally, no Birbeck granules are recognizable {18422973; 18813122}. There is often an inflammatory infiltrate at the periphery of the lesion. Eosinophils and lymphocytes may be prominent, as can be reactive CD68+, CD163+, S100+/−, CD1a− macrophages {16115054}.

Differential diagnosis

Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma lacks CD1a expression (which is positive in IDCT).

Reticulohistiocytoma, characterized by histiocytes with epithelioid morphology and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, is negative for CD1a, rarely positive for S100, and positive for macrophage markers (CD68, CD163, factor XIIIa) {31955466}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analysis of BRAF may be helpful to exclude other diagnoses.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tissue infiltration by cells morphologically reminiscent of Langerhans cells (CD1a+, S100+, CD207 [langerin]−); absence of the typical picture of multisystem Langerhans cell histiocytosis or Erdheim–Chester disease and other defined histiocytoses (e.g. ALK-positive histiocytosis).

Desirable: Ki-67 assessment.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Upon diagnosis, it is important to exclude the involvement of other organs/tissues with a whole-body imaging technique, complete blood counts, liver and kidney function tests, and neurological examination. In the presence of blood cell count anomalies and/or hepatomegaly or splenomegaly, a bone marrow biopsy is warranted, to exclude associated haematological neoplasms.

Blastic morphology is considered to be of prognostic value in IDCT, and there is evidence to suggest the existence of two clinicopathological subgroups among patients with IDCT {37016977}.

Most cases of cutaneous IDCT show complete remission after treatment or even spontaneous regression. As many as 25% of cases may show disease progression and/or malignant behaviour {28880462}; such cases often show atypical cytological features, a high proliferation index (Ki-67 index: > 20%) {28633156; 3886125; 10102017}, or association with other haematological neoplasms {3886125; 18813122}. Outcome data on extracutaneous IDCT are limited.

Targeted therapy for disseminated IDCT exhibiting BRAF p.V600E mutation has been reported to produce a good response {33324955}.
