3.1.2.8 Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma

Definition

Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma (EMPSGC) is a low-grade mucin-producing neuroendocrine neoplasm of sweat gland origin with a predilection for the eyelid and periorbital skin. It is a precursor to mucinous adenocarcinoma with neuroendocrine differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8509/3 Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH4EK4 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma

2E64.Y & XH8010 Other specified carcinoma in situ of skin & Endocrine mucin-producing sweat gland carcinoma in situ

Related terminology

Not recommended: cutaneous analogue of papillary solid carcinoma of the breast.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is a predilection for the eyelid/periorbital skin, with very rare extrafacial occurrence {24673415}.

Clinical features

Patients with EMPSGC present with a slow-growing, skin-coloured or bluish nodule, papule, or cyst. Sometimes, it may be multifocal {16160476}.

Epidemiology

The true incidence is unknown. Most cases occur in the sixth and seventh decades of life. Women are affected twice as often as men {32452870; 16160476}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

EMPSGC is a cutaneous analogue of solid papillary carcinoma of the breast {9414195; 32452870}. In some cases, it is associated with adjacent mucinous sweat gland carcinoma with neuroendocrine features, and most likely represents a precursor lesion {9414195; 16160476; 32452870}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

EMPSGC typically grows as one or more well-demarcated, expansile nodules with cystic, solid, papillary, pseudopapillary, or cribriform architecture. Tumour cells are polygonal to round, small to intermediate-sized ductal cells, about 1.5 times the size of sweat gland luminal ductal cells. They have amphophilic or bluish cytoplasm and round to oval nuclei with finely stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. Mild cytological pleomorphism can be noted in some cases. Small amounts of intracellular and extracellular mucin can be demonstrated by mucicarmine or PAS staining {9414195; 16160476; 32452870}. EMPSGC is frequently seen within dilated eccrine ducts as it spreads between the benign luminal ductal epithelium and the myoepithelial layer. At this (the earliest) stage, the tumour represents carcinoma in situ. Careful evaluation is needed so as not to mistake such a lesion for hidrocystoma. As EMPSGC grows, tumour cells begin to fill the ductal lumina, forming papillae, cribriform arrangements, and/or solid nests, which later become large expansile nodules. The nodules show variable proportions of solid, cystic, papillary, or pseudopapillary architecture. The myoepithelial rim around the larger tumour nests becomes incomplete or lost entirely, consistent with invasive carcinoma with pushing invasion {16160476; 25925290}. An infiltrating growth pattern is not seen. The presence of infiltrating tumour nests associated with pools of stromal mucin indicates progression to invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma. Mitotic activity is low, and the Ki-67 proliferation index is seldom greater than 5–10% {25219513}. Tumour necrosis is usually absent. Atypical patterns with increased size, pleomorphism, solid growth, and increased mitotic activity can be encountered. Lesions with even the smallest component of mucinous adenocarcinoma should be diagnosed as invasive mucinous carcinoma {32452870}.

Immunohistochemistry

Synaptophysin or chromogranin expression can be focal, or even absent on a small biopsy, and does not rule out the diagnosis {32452870}. INSM1, a most sensitive neuroendocrine marker {32745280}, is usually positive. All EMPSGCs strongly express ER, PR, and CK7. In contrast, expression of these receptors in apocrine carcinoma is variable. Markers such as p63 or calponin can highlight the myoepithelial layer at the carcinoma in situ stage. EMA shows stronger staining of the surface of the luminal cells. EMPSGC has also been shown to express CK8, CK18, AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, GATA3, GCDFP-15, WT1, and RCCm {27533071; 25925290; 25219513; 16160476; 31335409}.

Cytology

Not described

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: solid, papillary, and/or cystic architecture; polygonal cells with bluish cytoplasm; low mitotic activity; mild cytological pleomorphism; neuroendocrine nuclei; intracellular or extracellular mucin.

Desirable: expression of neuroendocrine markers.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system for non-melanoma eyelid carcinoma applies.

Prognosis and prediction

EMPSGC has an excellent prognosis after excision with clear margins, recurring in < 10% of cases {32452870}. The recurrence rate of EMPSGC-associated mucinous sweat gland carcinoma is about 20% {32452870}. Rarely, metastases may occur many years after surgery {33660318; 34184156; 31977506}.
