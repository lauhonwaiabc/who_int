5.8.0.3 CRTC1::TRIM11 cutaneous tumour

Definition

CRTC1::TRIM11 cutaneous tumour (CTCT) is a dermal spindled and epithelioid cell tumour with partial melanocytic differentiation and CRTC1::TRIM11 translocation.

ICD-O coding

9044/3 CRTC1::TRIM11 cutaneous tumour

ICD-11 coding

2C35 Cutaneous sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: TRIM11 melanocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CTCT shows a broad anatomical distribution, although it occurs most commonly in the extremities (upper extremity 40%, lower extremity 30%), followed by the trunk (15%), head, and mucosa (oral and nasal reported) {35993578; 29240581; 31237704}.

Clinical features

The tumours occur as a discrete, slowly growing, skin-coloured nodule. Mild pain and tingling have been noted {35993578; 29240581; 31237704}.

Epidemiology

Nearly 50 cases have been reported in the literature {29240581; 30676334; 31237704; 31276279; 33586183; 35993578; 35751643; 36177516}, without an obvious sex bias and across a broad age range (11–87 years, median: 44 years) {35993578}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Although the precise molecular function of the CRTC1::TRIM11 fusion protein has not been proved, CRTC1 (CREB-regulated transcription coactivator 1, 19p) is thought to enhance cell proliferation, differentiation, and expression of the oncogene MITF through activation of CREB1 (the cAMP response element-binding transcription factor) {14506290; 14536081; 28263292}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are circumscribed, with a pale, firm cut surface without pigmentation. The size ranges from 5 to 35 mm (mean < 10 mm) {29240581; 31237704}.

Histopathology

The morphology is highly uniform and resembles that of clear cell sarcoma. Tumours are dermal-based, well-circumscribed, unencapsulated nodules with a pushing border and no pigment. They can abut, but only very rarely involve, the epidermis. Cells are arranged in nests and streaming long and short intersecting (at times herringbone to vaguely palisaded) fascicles with intervening thin fibrous bands; they are fusiform with abundant pale cytoplasm, smooth nuclear membranes, vesicular chromatin, and prominent nucleoli. Mitoses are typical, although not conspicuous {35993578; 29240581; 31237704; 35751643}.

Immunohistochemistry

All tumours express diffuse SOX10. Diffuse S100 staining is seen in only 50% of cases; melan-A (MART1) and HMB45 are only focally expressed. Pan-TRK expression can be observed (60% diffuse). TRIM11 immunohistochemistry is typically positive in nuclei at the periphery of the nodule {35993578; 29240581; 31237704}.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis includes clear cell sarcoma and metastatic melanoma {35993578}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of CRTC1::TRIM11 fusion is necessary for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed, non-pigmented dermal tumour comprising intersecting bundles of epithelioid to fusiform cells with pale cytoplasm and vesicular nuclei; expression of SOX10, with at least partial loss of other melanocytic markers; no demonstrable EWSR1 rearrangement.

Desirable: demonstration of TRIM11 rearrangement.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

CTCT has been reported to show indolent behaviour, with rare (~10%) recurrence or metastasis following complete excision, although follow-up is limited {30676334; 36177516}.
