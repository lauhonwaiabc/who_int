3.1.2.12A NUT carcinoma

Definition

Primary cutaneous NUT carcinoma, with rearrangements of BRD3 or NSD3 with a member of the NUT family, is a provisional and rare tumour entity that is distinct from NUTM1-rearranged porocarcinoma and has some phenotypic similarities to homonymous tumours described in the thorax and in other organs.

ICD-O coding

8023/3 NUT carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2D42 & XH2855 Malignant neoplasms of ill-defined sites & Nuclear protein in testis (NUT)-associated carcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: NUT adnexal carcinoma.

Not recommended: NUT midline carcinoma; t(15-19) translocated carcinoma; BRD3::NUTM1 adnexal carcinoma; NSD3::NUTM1 adnexal carcinoma; BRD3::NUTM2B adnexal carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

NUT carcinoma of the skin has so far been described on the lower limb, shoulder, hand, and trunk {34296453; 34482332; 34469332}. However, because of the rarity of cases, there is insufficient knowledge of the anatomical distribution.

Clinical features

The tumour may arise as a cutaneous papule or nodule, which in some cases may have been present for years {34482332; 34469332}. Regional metastatic spread might be present at the time of diagnosis {34482332; 34469332}.

Epidemiology

NUT carcinoma of the skin is a very rare and probably underrecognized tumour entity.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

NUT carcinoma is characterized by the fusion of the NUTM1 gene either with BRD4 or with genes encoding interacting partners of BRD4, i.e. BRD3 {17934517} and NSD3 {24875858}. So far, only fusions involving BRD3 and NSD3 have been detected in primary cutaneous NUT carcinoma cases {34482332; 34469332}. Such fusion proteins lead to the expression of key transcription factors, such as MYC, SOX2 and p63 {26220994; 24736545}, and result in proliferation maintenance and differentiation blockage {17934517}. One case harbouring a BRD3::NUTM2B fusion has been reported {34296453}. The cell of origin of NUT carcinoma is unknown.

Macroscopic appearance

No consistent macroscopic features have been described.

Histopathology

This infiltrating neoplasm is located in the dermis and/or subcutaneous tissue, usually without apparent connection to the epidermis {34296453; 34482332; 34469332} (which may be ulcerated). Tumour cells are organized in strands, tubules, or solid aggregates {34296453; 34482332; 34469332} and display round nuclei with vesicular clear chromatin and a prominent single nucleolus. Characteristic features are small round ducts with eosinophilic cuticles and foci of abrupt keratinization. Clear cell changes and high-grade areas may be observed. Ductal structures are positive for EMA and CEA. Diffuse positivity for CK5/6, CK7, p40, and SOX10 has been reported. Positivity of p63 without p40 expression might be observed {32981612}. NUT expression is detected in tumours with NUTM1 rearrangement.

Differential diagnosis

This includes metastasis of extracutaneous NUT carcinoma {22794811} and YAP1::NUTM1-rearranged poroma and porocarcinoma, the latter displaying NUT positivity by immunohistochemistry {31145701; 33739783}.

Cytology

The features of these tumours in cytology preparations have not yet been described.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRD3::NUTM1, NSD3::NUTM1, and BRD3::NUTM2B gene fusions are detected in this setting {34296453; 34482332; 34469332}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primitive epithelial neoplasm composed of monotonous tumour cells displaying a prominent single nucleolus, with frequent formation of ducts and keratinization foci; molecular evidence of BRD3 or NSD3 gene fusion with a member of the NUT family (NUTM1 or NUTM2B); exclusion of metastasis from elsewhere.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

NUT carcinomas of the skin have metastatic potential, but they behave less aggressively than classic NUT carcinomas with BRD4::NUTM1 fusions {32328562}. No predictive factors have yet been described in cutaneous tumours.
