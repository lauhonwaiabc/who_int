3.4.1.2 Fibroadenoma and phyllodes tumour of anogenital mammary-like glands

Definition

Fibroadenoma and phyllodes tumour of anogenital mammary-like glands are biphasic fibroepithelial neoplasms composed of an epithelial glandular component and a predominant stromal component. They are microscopically identical to their mammary homologues.

ICD-O coding

9010/0 Fibroadenoma, NOS

9020/0 Phyllodes tumour, benign

ICD-11 coding

2F33.Y & XH9HE2 Benign neoplasm of other or unspecified female genital organs & Fibroadenoma, NOS

2F33.Y & XH50P7 Benign neoplasm of other or unspecified female genital organs & Phyllodes tumour, benign

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Fibroadenoma, NOS; phyllodes tumour, benign

Localization

Most cases occur in the vulva and perianal area {16096415}. Rare patients also present with mammary fibroadenoma, similar lesions in the axilla {26863057}, and bilateral anogenital tumours {197847; 13197326; 5775152}.

Clinical features

Fibroadenoma and phyllodes tumour occur as solitary nodules measuring 8–60 mm (mean size: 30 mm). In the reported cases, the age at diagnosis was 20–69 years (mean: 39 years) {18190454; 20035149}.

Epidemiology

Both tumours are very uncommon, with < 80 cases reported to date. Most patients are female {26863057; 20035149}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The tumours are thought to arise from anogenital mammary-like glands {27759697; 27655126; 8005737; 21169735}. One study identified AKT1 and MET mutations in fibroadenoma and ABL1 and TP53 mutations in low-grade phyllodes tumour {28648934}. Single cases of fibroadenoma and phyllodes tumour have been reported in patients with PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome / Cowden syndrome {34524588; 33850997}.

Macroscopic appearance

These neoplasms are circumscribed solid masses and may have small cystic spaces. In phyllodes tumours, leaf-like structures may be evident grossly. The size range from < 10 mm to 60 mm or larger {21293278; 20035149}.

Histopathology

Fibroadenoma is a well-circumscribed neoplasm composed of round or elongated, often branching and anastomosing glandular structures surrounded by a paucicellular stroma containing bland spindled or stellate cells, showing low or practically no mitotic activity. When the glandular lumina maintain round or oval configurations, the image is that of a pericanalicular growth pattern, whereas abundant stroma compressing the glandular elements into slit-like structures produces an intracanalicular pattern {21169735; 20035149}.

Phyllodes tumour shows a growth pattern with leaf-like projections into glandular lumina. The stroma is usually hypercellular, typically with periglandular condensation; cellularity commonly varies within a given neoplasm {11469353; 17318573; 16096415; 20035149}. Three categories are delineated: benign, low-grade, and high-grade; the grade is defined by stromal atypia. Most vulvar phyllodes tumours are either benign or low-grade; high-grade tumours are extremely rare {21293278; 8394655}.

Rare features in these tumours include mammary-type ductal lesions, hyperplasia of myoepithelial cells, pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia (PASH), various types of metaplasia in the glandular component and stroma, and lactation-like changes {18190454; 16096415; 20035149; 18587019}.

Immunohistochemistry

The epithelial component stains for ER, PR, and GATA3, whereas ERBB2 (HER2) is negative. Stromal cells, including those in PASH areas, are positive for vimentin and CD34 and variably positive for actins {17318573; 16096415; 12460213; 18587019; 32592993}. Vascular markers are negative in PASH {16096415}.

Differential diagnosis

Fibroadenoma and benign phyllodes tumour manifest overlapping features; in some cases, distinguishing between them can be difficult. Generally, phyllodes tumours have more-cellular stroma (with variation within a lesion), less regular outlines, and leaf-like projections. Focal connection to the epidermal surface with numerous plasma cells may resemble syringocystadenoma papilliferum. Fibroepithelial foci resembling fibroadenoma may rarely be found in other complex lesions of anogenital mammary-like glands {16625075; 19508499; 7416759}. Fibrocystic disease and other lesions with prominent ductal hyperplasia may be differential diagnoses in a limited specimen {16441411; 16625075; 23435363}. Lactation-like change (secretory hyperplasia) must not be confused with malignancy; the presence of intracytoplasmic vacuoles and intraluminal secretion is the clue to the diagnosis. PASH should be distinguished from low-grade angiosarcoma, which exhibits at least mild nuclear pleomorphism and immunopositivity for vascular markers.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: biphasic histology (for both lesions); for fibroadenoma: round or elongated, often branching and anastomosing glandular structures surrounded by usually paucicellular stroma; for phyllodes tumour: leaf-like projections and hypercellular stroma.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases are benign. Local recurrence can occur in phyllodes tumours {20035149}.
