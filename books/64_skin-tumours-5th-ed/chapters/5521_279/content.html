5.5.2.1 Atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasm

Definition

Atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasm is a primary dermal neoplasm composed of generally well-differentiated spindled cells closely resembling normal arrector pili smooth muscle but showing nuclear enlargement, hyperchromatism, and mitotic activity.

ICD-O coding

8897/1 Atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasm

ICD-11 coding

2F9C & XH1EN1 Neoplasms of unknown behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Smooth muscle tumour of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Acceptable: cutaneous leiomyosarcoma.

Not recommended: pilar leiomyosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumours most commonly involve the trunk and lower extremities {21358302; 20102404}.

Clinical features

The tumours typically manifest as small (5–30 mm), solitary, and occasionally painful nodules.

Epidemiology

Cutaneous leiomyosarcomas are rare, with an age-adjusted incidence of roughly 0.2 cases per 100 000 person-years {31729020}. They most often occur in older men, with a mean patient age of 56 years and an M:F ratio of 3:1 to 4:1 {21358302; 20102404}.

Etiology

Rare cases have been described in association with Li–Fraumeni syndrome {21358302}.

Pathogenesis

The genetic events underlying the development of atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasm are unknown. Germline FH mutation does not appear to be a predisposing factor {20618355}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasms are typically well differentiated, consisting of fascicles of relatively uniform, eosinophilic spindled cells with perinuclear vacuoles and cigar-shaped nuclei. Compared with normal pilar smooth muscle, these tumours show greater cellularity, nuclear enlargement, and hyperchromatism, and they show mitotic activity.

Marked nuclear pleomorphism and necrosis are rare; the presence of such features should raise concern for metastasis from a deeply situated tumour, which may be regarded as cutaneous leiomyosarcoma {21989966}. Atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasms typically grow in an infiltrative fashion into the surrounding dermis, and they may show limited involvement of the subcutaneous fat.

Immunohistochemically, these tumours are essentially always positive for SMA and desmin, and almost always positive for caldesmon {21358302; 20102404}. Expression of keratins may be seen in as many as 45% of cases {21358302}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a dermal-based tumour with an infiltrative growth pattern; fascicles of relatively uniform, eosinophilic spindled cells with perinuclear vacuoles and cigar-shaped nuclei; variable SMA, desmin, and caldesmon expression; limited involvement of the subcutaneous fat.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of these tumours is excellent, with no metastases documented in the largest series to date (84 cases) for tumours confined to the dermis that are completely excised {21358302}. Data are limited, but dermal tumours showing subcutaneous involvement may have a low risk of metastasis and recurrence. Grading is not of prognostic value.
