5.8.0.2 Papillary haemangioma

Definition

Papillary haemangioma is a distinctive vascular proliferation consisting of dilated vascular channels with prominent intraluminal papillary projections lined by endothelial cells, many of which contain intracytoplasmic globules.

ICD-O coding

9120/0 Papillary haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Papillary haemangioma occurs almost exclusively on the head and neck {17927585; 31592865}.

Clinical features

Papillary haemangioma appears as a solitary, longstanding, bluish-red papule as large as 11 mm in diameter.

Epidemiology

It is most common in adult men.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Papillary haemangioma is characterized by dermal, widely dilated, thin-walled vascular channels with numerous luminal papillary projections containing capillaries lined by plump endothelial cells, surrounded by pericytes, and displaying frequent intracytoplasmic eosinophilic globules. These globules, known as thanatosomes, represent dilated lysosomes containing debris and fat vacuoles. Endothelial cells are positive for CD31, CD34, and ERG and are negative for lymphatic markers.

Differential diagnosis

The lesions are histologically similar to glomeruloid haemangiomas, which differ in their typical multifocally, involvement of the trunk and extremities, and association with POEMS syndrome.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nodule consisting of ectatic dermal vascular channels with prominent papillary projections lined by endothelial cells with frequent intracytoplasmic eosinophilic globules.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Papillary haemangioma is benign, and recurrences are exceptional.
