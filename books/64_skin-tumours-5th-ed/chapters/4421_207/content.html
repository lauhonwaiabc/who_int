4.4.2.1 Severe mosquito bite allergy

Definition

Severe mosquito bite allergy is an Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)-positive NK-cell (or occasionally T-cell) lymphoproliferative disorder characterized by high fever and severe skin manifestations after mosquito bites.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Not recommended: hypersensitivity to mosquito bites.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Severe mosquito bite allergy is a skin-localized disease.

Clinical features

Severe mosquito bite allergy is characterized by a high fever and severe skin manifestations after a mosquito (or rarely other insect) bite. The bite area develops blisters, bullae, indurations, necrosis, and ulceration, leaving a scar that heals in 2–3 weeks. Transient systemic symptoms such as fever, lymphadenopathy, and hepatic dysfunction may occur, but persistence of systemic symptoms indicates systemic chronic active EBV disease. Patients have high levels of IgE, and EBV-infected large granular lymphocytes in the peripheral blood {31240305; 34334322}. Some patients also exhibit hydroa vacciniforme–like eruptions in sun-exposed areas {25234411; 31833428}.

Epidemiology

The disease is extremely rare and has mostly been reported from eastern Asia {9045900; 22096243; 25234411; 16246422; 19636274}, Mexico, and Central and South America {16083813; 30819780}. Most cases occur before the age of 20 years, with no sex predilection {11568749}.

Etiology

Genetic and environmental factors and EBV strains may play etiological roles {16791843}.

Pathogenesis

In the event of a mosquito bite, CD4-positive T cells respond to mosquito salivary secretions and induce reactivation of EBV-infected NK cells, causing inflammation {16297196; 17169531}. Somatic mutations (including DDX3X mutations) have been detected in EBV-positive cells {30664667}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

There is a variably dense infiltrate of small to large atypical lymphocytes with a perivascular distribution in the dermis and subcutaneous tissue. An angiocentric and angiodestructive growth pattern is typically present in more advanced lesions, triggering necrosis, ulceration, and bulla formation {11568749}.

Immunohistochemistry and in situ hybridization

Most cases exhibit an NK-cell immunophenotype, but some have a T-cell phenotype {22096243}. NK cells are negative for sCD3 and T-cell receptor proteins, and positive for CD3ε, CD16, CD56, and cytotoxic markers (e.g. TIA1 and granzyme B). Lymphoid cells are positive for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) by in situ hybridization {22096243}.

Differential diagnosis

Mosquito bites may occasionally cause severe erythema, swelling, and blistering in healthy individuals, but unlike in severe mosquito bite allergy, there is usually no fever or ulceration. Hydroa vacciniforme lymphoproliferative disorder is morphologically identical to severe mosquito bite allergy but is distinguished by the clinical manifestations. Patients with persistent systemic symptoms are classified as having systemic chronic active EBV disease {15655776; 11435294; 34334322}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There is not currently a clinical assay to determine NK-cell clonality. In clinical practice, clonality testing for TR genes is performed and an absence of TR gene rearrangement provides indirect support of an NK-cell origin. However, identification of a clonal expansion (e.g. with the detection of an abnormal karyotype or the presence of a mutation) is indicative of a malignant evolution {34334322}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: high fever and severe skin manifestations after mosquito bites; bite site biopsy shows lymphoid infiltrate with an NK-cell (or less commonly a T-cell) phenotype; EBER positivity.

Desirable: high circulating EBV DNA load; lack of T-cell receptor protein expression and/or clonal TR gene rearrangement in cases of NK-cell origin.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The disease is protracted and associated with an increased risk for haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, chronic active EBV disease, aggressive NK-cell leukaemia, and extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma {11568749; 16280311; 22096243}. Late-onset disease (> 9 years of age) and BZLF1 expression correlate with a poor prognosis {25234411}.
