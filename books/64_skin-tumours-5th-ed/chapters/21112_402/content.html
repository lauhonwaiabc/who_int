2.1.1.12 MITF pathway-activated melanocytic tumours

Definition

MITF pathway–activated melanocytic tumours are clear cell tumours with melanocytic differentiation and an ACT::MITF or MITF::CREM translocation.

ICD-O coding

8727/0 MITF pathway–activated melanocytic tumours

ICD-11 coding

2F20.1 Atypical melanocytic naevus

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours involve distal extremities and trunk {33264137; 33462743}.

Clinical features

They present as a solitary unpigmented nodule {33264137; 33462743}.

Epidemiology

The age distribution is wide, ranging from children to the elderly {33264137}. Tumours with an ACT::MITF translocation show a female predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

MITF fusions are the oncogenic driver. MITF is a transcription factor and member of the MiT/TFE family, which also contains TFEB, TFE3, and TFEC and acts as the master regulator of melanocytic differentiation {16899407}. The actin gene involved in the ACT::MITF translocation can be ACTB or ACTG1 {33264137}. The large C-terminal MITF fragment of the fusion protein retains its transcriptional activation domain, suggesting hyperactive MITF function {33264137}. MITF::CREM fusions are an alternative driver and may drive expression of the non-rearranged MITF allele, resulting in hyperactive MITF function {33462743}. CREM is a member of the CREB1/ATF1/CREM family of transcription factors that regulate diverse cellular processes, including transcription of MITF. The N-terminal MITF portion of the fusion protein lacks its transcriptional activation domain, whereas the transcriptional activation domain is preserved in the CREM fragment, analogous to the EWSR1::ATF1 and EWSR1::CREB1 fusions found in clear cell sarcoma.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The tumours are primarily dermal with common subcutaneous extension, and they lack an intraepidermal component. There is a distinctive splaying of the reticular dermal collagen bundles, creating a checkerboard-like pattern. The tumour cells are large, with copious optically clear or occasionally granular cytoplasm. The cytomorphological features are variable; morphology ranges from low-grade to higher-grade with nuclear enlargement, nuclear contour irregularity, and nucleolar enlargement. Necrosis, ulceration, perineural involvement, and lymphovascular involvement are not typical, and mitotic activity is inconspicuous {33264137; 34958499}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumours are positive for HMB45 or melan-A (MART1) and usually for S100. MITF is strongly positive {33264137}.

Differential diagnosis

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumour (PEComa), melanoma (either metastatic or primary dermal), and clear cell sarcoma represent the main differential diagnoses. S100 expression is typically not prominent in PEComa {18277881}. Clear cell sarcoma should have EWSR1::ATF1 or EWSR1::CREB1 fusions {8401579}, whereas cutaneous melanoma typically has MAPK pathway driver mutations.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of the ACT::MITF or MITF::CREM rearrangement.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a dermal neoplasm of clear cells expressing melanocytic differentiation; evidence of MITF translocation.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Current evidence suggests an indolent course for tumours with actin fusions, whereas the number of cases with CREM rearrangements is currently too small to assess.
