1.2.2.6 Lymphoepithelial carcinoma

Definition

Lymphoepithelial carcinoma is a poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) admixed with variable amounts of lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate.

ICD-O coding

8082/3 Lymphoepithelial carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C31.Z & XH1E40 Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma & Lymphoepithelial carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lymphoepithelial carcinoma of the skin typically involves sun-exposed skin, most often of the head and neck.

Clinical features

This rare type of SCC typically shows rapid growth, but it has no features to distinguish it from other SCC types.

Epidemiology

Lymphoepithelial carcinomas of the skin are exceedingly rare.

Etiology

The etiology is similar to that of common forms of SCC (see section Squamous cell carcinomas).

Pathogenesis

Although lymphoepithelial carcinoma of the skin resembles undifferentiated nasopharyngeal carcinoma (lymphoepithelioma) histologically, a strong association with EBV has not been demonstrated {29723365}.

Macroscopic appearance

See section Squamous cell carcinomas .

Histopathology

Lymphoepithelial carcinoma of the skin is characterized by poorly differentiated tumour cell islands surrounded and infiltrated by lymphocytes and variable numbers of plasma cells {25102913}. Accordingly, the lesions resemble undifferentiated nasopharyngeal carcinoma. Immunohistochemistry (pancytokeratin, CK5/6, or p63) confirms the presence of epithelial elements that may be masked by the lymphoid component.

Differential diagnosis

Lymphoepithelial carcinoma of the skin must be distinguished from reactive and neoplastic lymphoid infiltrates of the skin, or inflamed SCC {25102913}. Lymphadenoma has a banal epithelial component {11763398}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a poorly differentiated SCC; tumour islands surrounded and infiltrated by lymphocytes and variable numbers of plasma cells.

Staging

Tumours should be staged according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

Excision is usually sufficient, but radiotherapy is sometimes used in cases with positive margins {23133769; 31501980}.
