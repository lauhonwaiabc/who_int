3.1.2.14 Secretory carcinoma

Definition

Secretory carcinoma is a rare adnexal carcinoma that is histopathologically identical to homologous lesions in the salivary gland and breast.

ICD-O coding

8502/3 Secretory carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH44J4 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Secretory carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: primary cutaneous mammary analogue secretory carcinoma; mammary-type secretory carcinoma of the skin.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The axilla is the most common site. Rare lesions arise on the face (including the lips), trunk, groin, and limbs {25140660; 27631515; 27506949}.

Clinical features

The neoplasm presents as a nondescript solitary small papule or nodule {27631515}.

Epidemiology

Secretory carcinoma is a rare and probably underrecognized neoplasm, with < 40 cases reported. There is slight female preponderance. The reported age range is wide (22–71 years) {27763904; 27631515; 19342945; 20631609; 29205457}.

Etiology

Because of the localization in the axillae and lips, a relationship with mammary tissue (ectopic) and salivary glands (minor) has been suggested. However, the lack of direct connection to (and lack of residua of) mammary tissue or salivary glands argues against this theory {27631515}.

Pathogenesis

Similarly to homonymous neoplasms of the breast and salivary glands, cutaneous secretory carcinoma is associated with the characteristic balanced t(12;15)(p13;q25) ETV6::NTRK3 translocation {27631515}. A single case with NFIX::PKN1 fusion has been reported {31045890}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with a relatively well demarcated or poorly circumscribed nodule.

Histopathology

Tumours present as intradermal, circumscribed, unencapsulated nodules composed of bland neoplastic cells arranged in microcystic, tubular, and solid growth patterns, with characteristic abundant secretion within the microcystic and tubular spaces. Cells in the tubular areas may have a cuboidal appearance. Necrosis, perineural invasion, and lymphovascular involvement are not seen. The mitotic rate is low. Immunohistochemically, the neoplastic cells are diffusely positive for S100, mammaglobin, and STAT5A, and they are variably positive for anti-NTRK antibody {27631515}.

Differential diagnosis

Primary cutaneous secretory carcinoma appears identical to its extracutaneous counterparts; therefore, clinical correlation is essential to distinguish it from a metastasis from a breast or salivary gland primary. The presence of prominent intraluminal secretions sometimes resembling thyroid colloid may occasionally raise the possibility of a metastasis from a thyroid carcinoma, but secretory carcinoma is negative for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF1). Of note, there are thyroid gland neoplasms histologically resembling secretory carcinoma, as well as tumours with different morphology but harbouring the ETV6::NTRK3 translocation {27282352; 26808131}. Microsecretory adenocarcinoma (with MEF2C::SS18 fusion) has recently been reported in skin {35690998} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Head and neck tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 9). https://publications.iarc.who.int/629.]]. Cribriform carcinoma of the skin composed of solid areas punctuated by small round spaces arranged in a cribriform pattern may occasionally resemble a secretory carcinoma, in particular when abundant eosinophilic secretion that gives a homogeneous positive PAS reaction is evident in the lumina of the cystic and tubular spaces. However, in cribriform carcinoma, the cells in the tubular areas are not cuboidal, the glands are not distributed back-to-back, and all tumours are characterized by peculiar thin, thread-like intraluminal bridging strands resembling those seen in adenomatoid tumours of the urogenital tract {14571427; 21315957}. Adenoid cystic carcinoma displays a cribriform pattern, but the presence of true ductal structures with limited secretions, pseudolumina filled with mucin, or homogeneous basophilic and basaloid tumour cells are distinguishing features {24025525}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

ETV6::NTRK3 translocation is detected in most cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic abundant secretion within microcystic and tubular spaces.

Desirable: coexpression of S100, mammaglobin, and STAT5A; evidence of ETV6::NTRK3 fusion or ETV6 rearrangement.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

Secretory carcinoma has an apparently indolent disease course, but studies with long follow-up are limited. Lymph node or distant metastases are exceptional {33882152}.
