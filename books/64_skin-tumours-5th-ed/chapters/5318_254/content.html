5.3.1.8 Tufted haemangioma

Definition

Tufted haemangioma is a benign cutaneous vascular tumour sometimes associated with Kasabach–Merritt syndrome and now widely considered part of the kaposiform haemangioendothelioma spectrum.

ICD-O coding

9161/0 Tufted haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y & XH2EX4 Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms & Acquired tufted haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: acquired tufted angioma; angioblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These lesions have a predilection for the neck and upper trunk.

Clinical features

Tufted haemangiomas manifest as slow-growing, poorly defined macules, papules, nodules, and plaques, with rare focal regression {12383094; 20644037}. Patients with large cutaneous lesions may develop Kasabach–Merritt syndrome {10642677; 27607323}. This and other features, including genetic evidence of a homogeneous methylation pattern, support the widely held opinion that tufted haemangioma is a smaller, more superficial clinical presentation of kaposiform haemangioendothelioma {31887709}.

Epidemiology

This rare haemangioma has a strong predilection for infants and children, with no sex predilection {2644316; 20644037}. Congenital cases have been reported {30318642}, and presentation in adult life is exceptional {17186794}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

Multiple scattered, tightly packed lobules of capillaries are present throughout the dermis and (more rarely) the subcutis, in a cannonball pattern. The lumina of vascular channels are often difficult to discern, and pericytes are prominent. The endothelial cells are flat and occasionally contain intracytoplasmic hyaline structures. A distinctive feature is the presence of a crescent-like lymphatic channel around many individual tumour lobules. The stroma between tumour lobules may be desmoplastic and contain lymphatic-like channels. Focal fascicles of spindled endothelial cells, which are positive for podoplanin (recognized by D2-40), CD31, ERG, and CD34, may be seen within the capillary lobules.

Differential diagnosis

Nodular Kaposi sarcoma lacks a cannonball architecture and is positive for KSHV/HHV8.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: tufts of capillaries scattered throughout the dermis, with frequent crescent-like lymphatic vessels around tumour lobules.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Tufted haemangioma is benign, but patients with large tumours may develop Kasabach–Merritt syndrome, which may be life-threatening.
