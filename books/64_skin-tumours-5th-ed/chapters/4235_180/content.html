4.2.3.5 Histiocytic sarcoma

Definition

Histiocytic sarcoma (HS) is a malignant tumour showing morphological and immunophenotypic features of macrophages. Tumorous proliferations of acute monocytic leukaemia are excluded.

ICD-O coding

9755/3 Histiocytic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B31.1 Histiocytic sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: histiocytic lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

HS occurs in lymph nodes or extranodal sites. The skin is a common extranodal site of involvement, but primary cutaneous HS is extremely rare {23801128; 30626916}.

Clinical features

Patients present with plaques, nodules, and tumours at any site on the body {29414400}. It can be localized or disseminated.

Epidemiology

HS is rare. It occurs over a wide age range, with a slight male predominance {23801128}.

Etiology

Some cases of HS are associated with prior or metachronous lymphoma {18272816; 15050956}. A subset of cases are associated with primary mediastinal germ cell tumours, especially in young male patients {16809892}.

Pathogenesis

Some cases of HS show clonal IG gene rearrangement, particularly when associated with lymphoid neoplasms {21666687; 32929178}. This is postulated to represent the transdifferentiation process.

The most frequent genomic alterations are mutations in the MAPK pathway, including KRAS, NRAS, BRAF, MAP2K1, PTPN11, NF1, and CBL {31768065; 32929178; 33904632}. Mutations in tumour suppressors such as CDKN2A and TP53 are common {33904632}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

HS shows a diffuse infiltrate of oval to round, and sometimes spindled, non-cohesive large tumour cells with variable pleomorphism. The cytoplasm is abundant and eosinophilic, and often vacuolated. The nuclei are oval, grooved, or irregularly folded, often with distinct nucleoli. Haemophagocytosis is occasionally observed. Variable numbers of reactive cells are often admixed.

Immunohistochemistry

HS shows expression of multiple histiocytic markers, including CD163, CD68, and lysozyme. There can be heterogeneous expression of S100. Langerhans cell markers (CD1a, CD207), follicular dendritic cell markers (CD21, CD35), and myeloid cell markers (myeloperoxidase, CD13) are negative, as is ALK. Melanocytic markers (SOX10, HMB45), epithelial markers (cytokeratins), and specific mature B-cell/T-cell markers (CD20, PAX5, CD3) are also negative.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnoses include various large cell neoplasms, such as lymphoma (especially large B-cell lymphoma and anaplastic large cell lymphoma), Langerhans cell sarcoma, follicular dendritic cell sarcoma, melanoma, carcinoma, and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. A relatively non-cohesive growth pattern and an abundance of eosinophilic cytoplasm are clues to the diagnosis of HS, which has to be further supported by expression of the relevant immunohistochemical markers.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour composed of non-cohesive large cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm; variably pleomorphic neoplastic cells with reniform, grooved, or irregularly folded nuclei and distinct nucleoli; positive immunostaining for two or more histiocytic markers; negative for CD1a, CD207 (langerin), CD21, and CD35.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

HS is usually aggressive, with a limited response to therapy and a high mortality rate. Some patients with a localized neoplasm have a more favourable outcome {15316312; 15696128}.
