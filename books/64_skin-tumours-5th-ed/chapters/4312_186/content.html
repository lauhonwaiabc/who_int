4.3.1.2 Primary cutaneous follicle centre lymphoma

Definition

Primary cutaneous follicle centre lymphoma (PCFCL) is a tumour of follicle centre cells, including centrocytes and variable numbers of centroblasts, with a follicular, follicular and diffuse, or diffuse growth pattern, that generally arises in the skin of the head or trunk.

ICD-O coding

9597/3 Primary cutaneous follicle centre lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A80.3 Primary cutaneous follicle centre lymphoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Patients with PCFCL characteristically present with solitary or localized skin lesions on the head/neck or the trunk. Approximately 5% present with skin lesions on the legs, and 15% with multifocal skin lesions {15947086; 16492713; 17353548}.

Clinical features

Patients present with firm erythematous to violaceous plaques, nodules, or tumours of variable size. Tumours may be surrounded by erythematous papules and slightly infiltrated, sometimes figurate plaques, which may precede the development of tumours by months or years {3049688; 2013039; 9207472}. Rare cases featuring miliary and clustered papules or macules on the head have been reported {27380773; 21601947}. Cutaneous relapses are observed in about 30% of patients, but extracutaneous dissemination (including nodal) is uncommon (~10% of patients) {17353548; 16492713}.

Epidemiology

PCFCL makes up approximately 50% of primary cutaneous B-cell lymphomas and 10% of all primary cutaneous lymphomas. It mainly affects middle-aged adults, with an M:F ratio of approximately 1.5:1 {17353548; 16492713; 24001373}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

PCFCL is a monoclonal proliferation of germinal centre–derived B cells, which harbour clonally rearranged IG genes, with somatic hypermutation {9808579; 11886516; 18806482; 22588547}. In most studies, cases of PCFCL with disease limited to the skin on staging rarely show BCL2 rearrangements {15166666; 21704419; 24679486; 25696794}. The presence of BCL2 rearrangements at diagnosis is associated with a higher risk of later systemic spread {25696794; 33560380}. Deletion of chromosome 14q32.33, containing the oncogene AKT1 as well as the immunoglobulin heavy chain locus, has been reported {16330669}. PCFCL harbours mutations in CREBBP (in 25% of cases), KTM2D (in 21%), and BCL2 (0%) much less frequently than classic follicular lymphoma, in which these genes are frequently mutated {33045225}. PCFCL shows the gene expression profile of germinal centre–like large B-cell lymphomas, and often features amplification of the REL gene {9808579; 16330669; 15308563}. B-cell receptor genes appear to acquire N-linked glycosylation motifs similarly to classic follicular lymphoma and in contrast to primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (PCLBCL) {31042459}. Mutations in MYD88 and inactivation of CDKN2A and CDKN2B by deletion (9p21.3) or their promotor hypermethylation are not or only rarely found in PCFCL {16330669; 27189828}, in contrast to PCLBCL, leg type.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The PCFCL infiltrates may show a follicular, follicular and diffuse, or diffuse growth pattern {2013039; 16293178; 9207472}. Histological grading (which is used for extracutaneous tumours) is not applicable in PCFCL. Cases with a follicular growth pattern show nodular infiltrates throughout the entire dermis, often extending into the subcutis. Unlike in cutaneous reactive B-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation, the follicles in PCFCL are often poorly defined, show a monotonous proliferation of BCL6-positive follicle centre cells, lack tingible-body macrophages, generally have an attenuated or absent mantle zone, and show a variably high proliferation rate with a lack of polarization {10845929; 12023577}. Reactive T cells may be numerous, and a prominent stromal fibroblastic/fibrohistiocytic component is usually present. Cases with a diffuse growth pattern usually show a monotonous population of large centrocytes, some with a multilobated appearance, and variable numbers of admixed centroblasts {2013039; 3049688; 11504742; 9207472}. In rare cases, the large centrocytes may be spindle-shaped {10949453; 11531800; 28684330}. The Ki-67 proliferation index in diffuse PCFCL is generally high.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells express CD20 and CD79a but are usually immunoglobulin-negative. PCFCL consistently expresses BCL6. CD10 may be positive in cases with a follicular growth pattern but is generally negative in cases with a diffuse growth pattern {11395550; 15947086; 11821444; 17353548}. A disrupted meshwork of CD21+/CD35+ follicular dendritic cells is frequently seen in the atypical follicles but might be absent in cases with a diffuse growth pattern {21704419}. Most cases do not express BCL2 or show faint BCL2 staining (weaker than admixed T cells) {17353548; 10845929; 11298531; 15947086}, although several studies have reported BCL2 expression in 8.5–61% of PCFCL cases {11557777; 14657713; 11821444; 25696794}. Staining for IRF4 (MUM1) and FOXP1 is negative in most cases, and CD5 and CD43 are always negative {15947086; 17353548}.

Differential diagnosis

Strong expression of both BCL2 and CD10 by the neoplastic B cells should always raise suspicion of a classic (nodal) follicular lymphoma involving the skin secondarily {14674895}. PCFCL with a follicular growth pattern may be distinguished from cutaneous marginal zone B-cell lymphoma primarily on the basis of the expression of germinal centre markers (CD10 and BCL6, and if necessary, other markers such as MEF2B, LMO2, or HGAL [GCET2]) and lack of expression of MNDA and plasmacytic differentiation in PCFCL. The main differential diagnosis in cases with a diffuse growth pattern is PCLBCL, leg type. Unlike PCLBCL, PCFCL does not express IgM and does not show strong expression of IRF4 (MUM1), and it has a germinal-centre phenotype (see section Primary cutaneous diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Clonality assays, BCL2 rearrangement analysis, and targeted sequencing may be informative for challenging cases (see Pathogenesis, above).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cutaneous tumour of follicle centre cells, as defined by the following: (1) follicular and/or diffuse proliferation of centrocytes and admixed centroblasts (diffuse lymphomas comprising exclusively centroblasts/immunoblasts are excluded), (2) B cells with coexpression of germinal-centre markers (BCL6 and/or CD10 or other germinal-centre markers); no extracutaneous involvement by lymphoma.

Desirable: localization to head or trunk; evidence of B-cell monoclonality; absent or weak BCL2 expression (usually); lack of IRF4 (MUM1) expression; lack of BCL2 rearrangement (usually).

Staging

Because cases with a secondary cutaneous follicular lymphoma may have a very similar clinical presentation, staging is important to rule out a systemic disease. Cases are staged using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) / International Society for Cutaneous Lymphomas (ISCL) recommendations and the TNM staging system for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides and Sézary syndrome {17339420}. PCFCL workup includes blood tests (including LDH) {17339420} and CT/PET of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis {18567836}. Bone marrow biopsy is not routinely performed.

Prognosis and prediction

Irrespective of the clinical, histological, and immunohistochemical features and genetic aberrations, PCFCL has an excellent prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate of > 95% {11504742; 2013039; 17353548; 16492713}. PCFCL arising on the leg is reported to have a less favourable prognosis {17353548; 15947086}. In patients with localized or few scattered lesions, local radiotherapy is the preferred treatment modality {17353548; 24001373}. Cutaneous relapses, observed in about 30% of patients, do not indicate progressive disease. Systemic therapy is required only for patients with very extensive cutaneous disease, extremely thick cutaneous tumours, or extracutaneous disease.
