3.3.1.2 Sebaceous adenoma

Definition

Sebaceous adenoma is a benign neoplasm composed mostly of fully differentiated sebocytes, admixed with immature basaloid epithelial cells.

ICD-O coding

8410/0 Sebaceous adenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH1NC5 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Sebaceous adenoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Sebaceous adenoma is principally seen in the skin of the head and neck.

Clinical features

Sebaceous adenoma is a well-circumscribed yellow-tan nodule (5–15 mm) {7560356; 34023105}. Lesions associated with Muir–Torre syndrome may be multifocal {10770437} and can have a central cystic area or a papillomatous morphology resembling keratoacanthoma {10535567}. Mismatch repair (MMR)-deficient sebaceous neoplasms occur more commonly outside of the head and neck area than do MMR-stable sebaceous neoplasms {18551751; 33201015}.

Epidemiology

Sebaceous adenomas are principally seen as solitary lesions in people aged > 40 years {4129561}. They are usually located in sun-damaged skin of the head and neck area. Lesions associated with Muir–Torre syndrome may be multifocal and located at other anatomical sites {10770437; 4758274; 18551751}.

Etiology

Sebaceous adenomas may arise through defective MMR, which manifests as loss of expression of MMR gene products, including MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2; a subset of these lesions are related to Muir–Torre syndrome {19515040; 25427047; 26892655; 28323777; 21499234; 20055911}. Preserved expression of all four proteins suggests microsatellite stability, whereas loss of nuclear staining for any of the proteins indicates deficient MMR, suggests the candidate gene, and indicates the need for further testing (see section Muir–Torre syndrome). However, clinical parameters outperform MMR immunohistochemical studies for identifying patients at risk for Muir–Torre syndrome {24603434; 23212176}. Moreover, a newly described rare autosomal recessive form of Muir–Torre syndrome lacks all aberrations in the expression of MMR gene products {26892655}.

Pathogenesis

Next-generation sequencing studies of sebaceous adenomas have found frequent mutation of RAS family genes (HRAS and KRAS), TP53, CDKN2A, EGFR, and CTNNB1. Abnormalities in TP53 were most common {27311873}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Sebaceous adenomas are well-circumscribed lobular aggregates composed mostly of mature sebocytes, with a variable rim of immature basaloid cells, recapitulating normal sebaceous glands {12354803}. The mature sebocytes are characterized by an abundant vacuolated, “bubbly” cytoplasm and a central scalloped nucleus, and these sebocytes predominate (> 50% of all tumour cells) over the small monomorphic immature basaloid cells. Sebaceous adenoma may show a direct connection to the overlying epidermis, which may be covered by keratin and cellular debris. MMR-deficient sebaceous neoplasms show cystic change, intratumoural mucin, squamous metaplasia, ulceration, a lymphocytic infiltrate, and intertumoural heterogeneity more often than do MMR-stable sebaceous neoplasms {33201015}.

Immunohistochemistry

The vacuolated cytoplasm of the mature sebocytes expresses EMA and adipophilin {17593083; 20118912}.

Differential diagnosis

The principal differential diagnosis for sebaceous adenoma is sebaceous hyperplasia, in which sebaceous lobules are arranged around a central follicle that is connected to the epidermis, but sebaceous hyperplasia shows a clear association with a central hair follicle, whereas sebaceous adenoma does not. Sebaceoma is another benign nodular lesion composed of a more equal admixture of basaloid epithelial cells and small groups of vacuolated sebocytes {27836252}. Considerable morphological overlap may exist between sebaceous adenoma and sebaceoma {8599428}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Diagnostic molecular pathology is not relevant for individual tumours. Germline sequencing may be indicated in some cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed yellow-tan nodule; well-circumscribed lobular aggregates consisting mostly of mature sebocytes, with a variable rim of immature, cytologically benign basaloid cells; direct connection to the overlying epidermis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Sebaceous adenoma is a benign lesion, and simple excision is curative. However, the subset of patients with this tumour who have Muir–Torre syndrome may experience morbidity and mortality from associated visceral malignancies {26892655}.
