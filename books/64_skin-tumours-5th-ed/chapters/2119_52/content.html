2.1.1.9 Pigmented epithelioid melanocytoma

Definition

Pigmented epithelioid melanocytomas (PEMs) are melanocytic neoplasms composed of pigmented epithelioid melanocytes, characterized by combined mutational activation of the PKA (protein kinase A) and MAPK (mitogen-activated protein kinase) pathways.

ICD-O coding

8780/1 Pigmented epithelioid melanocytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F20.1 & XH4VD0 Atypical melanocytic naevus & Pigmented epithelioid melanocytoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: epithelioid blue naevus; PRKAR1A-inactivated melanocytoma.

Not recommended: animal-type melanoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PEMs arise on the extremities, head and neck, trunk, and (rarely) mucosal sites {23143456}.

Clinical features

PEMs appear as slowly growing pigmented nodules or papules {14707861}.

Epidemiology

PEMs are rare tumours. They can occur at any age, most commonly arising in the second and third decades of life {14707861}.

Etiology

PEMs can occur sporadically or in patients with Carney complex {8772778}.

Pathogenesis

Approximately two thirds of PEMs have inactivating genetic alterations in PRKAR1A {18059235; 28796000; 30475255; 35319526} in addition to one of several activating mutations of the MAPK pathway {30475255; 35319526}. PRKAR1A inactivation constitutively activates PKA and induces melanocyte proliferation, differentiation, and pigmentation {32118628; 32682887}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Most PEMs are heavily pigmented, predominantly dermal tumours, with an inverted wedge or bulbous configuration. Small junctional nests are seen in 30% of cases, often with accompanying epidermal hyperplasia. They are composed of pigmented dendritic and epithelioid melanocytes with vesicular nuclei that have prominent nucleoli, sometimes resembling Reed–Sternberg cells or fried eggs. The epithelioid cells can show substantial nuclear polymorphism and multinucleation and may have diminished pigmentation.

The term “atypical PEM” may be applied to lesions with severe histological atypia and/or genomic aberrations suggestive of progression towards melanoma.

Immunohistochemistry

Loss of expression of PRKAR1A is found in as many as 80% of cases {18059235}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes blue naevus with epithelioid cells {22892599}, blue naevus with PRKCA fusion, melanoma (metastatic or primary), Spitz naevus, and WNT-activated melanocytoma. Tumours with fusions of PRKCA or with other mutations in the Gqα pathway can have histological phenotypes resembling PEMs. As mutational activation of the Gqα pathway is pathognomonic of blue naevi, neoplasms with PRKCA fusions {26676968; 28796000; 30475255; 31437301} are not classified as PEMs but are instead classified with blue naevi and related neoplasms, unless they also have mutations in the PKA pathway {35319526}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

DNA- and RNA-based sequencing helps to establish the diagnosis of PEM by confirming the presence of PRKAR1A inactivating mutations and rule out the presence of molecular markers of progression towards melanoma such as TERT promoter mutations or CDKN2A inactivation in atypical presentations.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a proliferation of pigmented dendritic and epithelioid melanocytes admixed with melanophages.

Desirable: demonstration of PRKAR1A inactivation.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

PEMs are melanocytic tumours of low to intermediate malignant potential {19773637}. Spread to regional sentinel lymph nodes was found in 46% of cases, more commonly in cases with ulceration {14707861; 19773637}. Distant metastases are extremely rare. The prognosis is excellent, even in cases with lymph node metastases {19773637}.
