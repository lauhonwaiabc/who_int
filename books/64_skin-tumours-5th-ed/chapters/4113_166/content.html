4.1.1.3 Mast cell sarcoma

Definition

Mast cell sarcoma (MCS) is a rare and clinically aggressive form of mastocytosis characterized by the presence of a locally destructive solid tumour comprising highly atypical mast cells.

ICD-O coding

9740/3 Mast cell sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2A21.2 Mast cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant mast cell tumour; malignant mastocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Any organ and system may be involved. Bone and the gastrointestinal tract are most often affected {27602777}. Cases involving the skin and oral mucosae are exceedingly rare {23196796; 3088063; 22498828}.

Clinical features

MCS arises de novo in most cases. Transformation of a previous mast cell neoplasm is rare and should be best regarded as MCS-like progression {18055976; 22498828; 22661384}. MCS may appear concomitantly with systemic disease, often fulfilling criteria for mast cell leukaemia {32242922}. The clinical presentation varies depending on the location of the mass. As many as 30% of patients present with symptoms of mast cell activation syndrome, including flushing, fever, malaise, diarrhoea, and tachycardia. Serum tryptase levels are frequently increased {27602777}.

Epidemiology

MCS is exceedingly rare in humans. Most patients are adults, but children can also be affected. The median age of adults with MCS is 55 years (range: 1–77 years), and there is a slight female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:1.3) {27602777}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. MCS is almost exclusively sporadic. Only one familial case of MCS has been reported {23129735}.

Pathogenesis

KIT mutations are rarely demonstrated in MCS, as compared with systemic mastocytosis. KIT is wildtype in 50% of the reported cases, and the canonical KIT p.D816V mutation is found in only 21% of patients. Other KIT mutations have been described in single cases {32242922; 23196796}. The paucity of KIT p.D816V mutations suggests that MCS is molecularly distinct from systemic mastocytosis.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

MCS manifests as a localized tumour of immature and often highly atypical cells showing an infiltrative and destructive growth pattern {3088063; 10430280; 12826896}. Neoplastic mast cells are difficult to recognize unless special stains are performed. They are usually medium-sized to large pleomorphic or epithelioid cells, with oval or bilobed nuclei. Multinucleated mast cells are sometimes present. Cytoplasmic metachromatic granules can be seen on Giemsa and toluidine blue staining. Increased mitotic activity is sometimes noted, and the tumour is frequently infiltrated by eosinophils.

Mast cells with striking nuclear pleomorphism, including bilobed and multilobed forms (promastocytes), have been reported in biopsies of cutaneous childhood-onset mastocytosis and in an adult case. Such a finding does not seem to represent a malignant phenotype and should not be interpreted as cutaneous MCS {30252695; 29148588}. Progressive cytological pleomorphism and an unusually high proliferation rate have been reported in an adult with a recurrent cutaneous lesion but without evidence of systemic involvement {34152029}.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic mast cells express mast cell tryptase, and KIT (CD117) is positive in most cases. Mast cells variably express CD2, CD25, and CD30 {32242922}. Positivity for other nonspecific antigens, like CD68, CD45, CD43, and MITF, may cause differential diagnostic problems {23196796; 27602777; 32242922}. Ki-67 is variably increased, ranging from 3% to 50% {23196796}.

Differential diagnosis

MCS needs to be differentiated from sarcomas, metastatic carcinoma, melanoma, histiocytic or myeloid neoplasms, and lymphomas, particularly ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphoma {24141335; 27602777; 32242922; 23196796}.

Cytology

Cytology of the primary lesion is not usually obtained. In cases with bone marrow infiltration, the neoplastic mast cells share features with other subtypes of mastocytosis {27602777}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Whole KIT sequencing is recommended, given the low frequency of MCSs harbouring the typical KIT p.D816V mutation that confers resistance to imatinib {32242922}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a localized, infiltrative lesion composed of highly atypical mast cells expressing tryptase and KIT (CD117); criteria for systemic mastocytosis are not fulfilled.

Desirable: immunohistochemical expression of CD2 and/or CD25 and/or CD30.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of patients with MCS is dismal. Locally destructive growth and quick progression to aggressive systemic mastocytosis or mast cell leukaemia occur in most cases {3088063; 12826896; 23129735; 27602777}. The median survival time of patients with MCS is 17 months (range: 1–45 months) {27602777}. The terminal phase of MCS is often indistinguishable from mast cell leukaemia {32242922}.
