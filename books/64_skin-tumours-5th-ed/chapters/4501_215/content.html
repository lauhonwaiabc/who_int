4.5.0.1 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL) is an extranodal lymphoma of NK or cytotoxic T-cell lineage, characterized by vascular damage and destruction, prominent necrosis, a cytotoxic phenotype, and a strong association with Epstein–Barr virus (EBV).

ICD-O coding

9719/3 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A90.6 Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type

Related terminology

Acceptable: extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type.

Not recommended: angiocentric lymphoma; polymorphic reticulosis; lethal midline granuloma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The skin is the second most common site of involvement by ENKTL, after the upper aerodigestive tract. More than one cutaneous region is typically involved, most commonly the trunk and extremities {9192774; 25188863; 34060130; 19029440; 19014396}, with the lower extremities being the most common location of the skin lesions {34060130}. There may also be simultaneous or subsequent involvement of extracutaneous sites, most commonly the upper aerodigestive tract, including the nose. Cutaneous involvement may occur either as primary disease or as dissemination from nasal disease.

Clinical features

Cutaneous lesions can manifest as nodules (with or without ulceration), cellulitis or abscess-like swellings, or erythematous to purpuric patches {34060130; 24629518}. Erythematous necrotic plaques or ulcers of the nose and adjacent tissues represent secondary spread from the nasal mucosa to the skin. Approximately 40% of the cases have evidence of extracutaneous dissemination at diagnosis {34060130; 19014396; 15096371}. Systemic symptoms such as fever, malaise, and weight loss can be present. Some cases may be complicated by haemophagocytic syndrome.

Epidemiology

ENKTL occurs most commonly in Asia and Central and South America, but it also occurs (rarely) in White populations. It is more common in males, and it usually affects middle-aged adults, at a median age of 49–53 years {9192774; 25188863; 34060130}.

Etiology

The invariable association with EBV, irrespective of ethnic origin, indicates a pathogenic role of the virus {34060130; 18701406}.

Pathogenesis

TR genes are in a germline configuration in a high proportion of cases, but they may be clonally rearranged in 10–40% of cases {34060130}. Cases with clonally rearranged TR genes presumably represent neoplasms of cytotoxic T cells {34060130; 19029440; 23232851; 25554090; 22314189}.

ENKTLs show frequent 6q21-q25 deletion, as well as mutations of JAK/STAT pathway genes, epigenetic regulators, tumour suppressor genes (TP53, MGA), and RNA helicase (DDX3X), but the studies to date have been based exclusively or predominantly on nasal cases, and therefore it is unclear whether the findings also apply to primary cutaneous ENKTLs. Mutations of JAK3, reported in 0–35% of cutaneous cases, may result in cytokine-independent constitutive JAK/STAT activation {23689514; 23808814}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

The skin shows a dermal lymphoid infiltrate that is diffuse or predominantly perivascular, variably accompanied by subcutaneous involvement. Epidermotropism, interface dermatitis, or pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia can be present {34060130; 26695102}, and necrosis is common. The lymphoid cells can exhibit a broad cytological spectrum, ranging from a predominance of small, medium-sized, or large cells, to a mixture, with medium-sized cells being the most common. The medium-sized cells show round or irregular folded nuclei, granular chromatin, small nucleoli, and pale to clear cytoplasm. The large cells possess round or folded nuclei, vesicular or granular chromatin, and distinct nucleoli. Mitotic figures are readily found, even in small cell–predominant lesions.

Angiocentric growth is observed in 40–70% of cases {34060130; 23232851; 19200574}. Vascular damage with fibrinoid change and red blood cell extravasation are commonly observed {26695102}. These vascular changes are less common in lesions presenting as erythematous to purpuric patches {26695102}.

Infiltration of tumour cells into the subcutaneous layer can result in a panniculitis-like pattern {25188863; 24629518; 26695102}. The tumour cells can palisade around fat vacuoles, and fat necrosis is common {25188863; 26695102}.

The rare cases of intravascular NK/T-cell lymphoma involving skin are not classified as cutaneous ENKTL but are provisionally classified with aggressive NK-cell leukaemias.

Immunohistochemistry

The lymphoma cells are commonly positive for cytoplasmic CD3ε, CD56, and cytotoxic markers, and negative for surface CD3, CD5, CD4, CD8, TCRγδ, and TCRαβ. However, some cases can be CD56-negative or express CD8. CD43 and CD45RO are commonly positive, and CD7 is variably expressed. CD16 and CD57 are usually negative.

The cells are usually of NK-cell lineage, but a proportion of cases express TCRαβ or TCRγδ, indicating a T-cell lineage {25188863; 34060130; 25554090}. CD30 is positive in approximately 40% of cases, correlated with larger tumour size {34060130; 30597548}. In situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is positive, but LMP1 is often negative.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In situ hybridization for EBER is positive in the majority of tumour cells. TR gene rearrangement studies may be performed for selected problematic cases {25188863}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dermal and/or subcutaneous infiltration by lymphoma cells with variable morphology; NK-cell or cytotoxic T-cell phenotype; majority of lymphoma cells positive for EBER.

Desirable: angiocentric growth and necrosis.

Staging

Staging is by the TNM classification system for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides and Sézary syndrome.

Prognosis and prediction

Cutaneous ENKTL is highly aggressive, with a 5-year overall survival rate of 22% and a median overall survival of 12–15 months, although the disease may rarely pursue an indolent course {24433873; 25188863; 34060130; 24629518; 22547542; 31841860}. The prognosis is better for patients with localized disease but is particularly poor for patients with generalized skin lesions and extracutaneous involvement. Patients with skin lesions on the legs have a lower survival rate {34060130}. The distinction between NK-cell and cytotoxic T-cell lineage is not of prognostic importance {34060130; 25554090}.
