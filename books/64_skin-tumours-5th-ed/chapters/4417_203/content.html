4.4.1.7 Primary cutaneous acral CD8-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder

Definition

Primary cutaneous acral CD8-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder is a skin tumour characterized by dermal, non-epidermotropic infiltrates of clonal medium-sized CD8-positive cytotoxic lymphocytes, preferentially located at acral sites (especially the ears).

ICD-O coding

9709/3 Primary cutaneous acral CD8-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder

ICD-11 coding

2B0Y & XH7S84 Other specified primary cutaneous mature T-cell or NK-cell lymphomas and lymphoproliferative disorders & Primary cutaneous acral CD8-positive T-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: indolent CD8-positive lymphoid proliferation of the ear; primary cutaneous acral CD8-positive T-cell lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The disease shows a predilection for acral sites, especially the ears (the most common site), as well as the nose, hands, and feet {19602068; 23189944; 21083681; 24394306; 26423705; 31433793; 34988968}. Involvement of other sites (e.g. eyelids, trunk, leg) has also been reported {17012921; 23189944; 24556898; 22885550}.

Clinical features

The tumour usually manifests as an asymptomatic, solitary, reddish, non-ulcerated papule, nodule, or (less frequently) plaque, measuring as large as 40 mm, that grows slowly over weeks or months. Rarely, the tumoural lesions are multiple {23189944} or bilateral, particularly on the ears and feet {19602068; 22142195; 18043044; 24102688}.

Epidemiology

The disease is rare (< 1% of all primary cutaneous lymphomas) {31558559}. It affects adults (median age: 56 years), with an M:F ratio of 2:1 {26423705; 31433793}. Paediatric cases have not been reported.

Etiology

Except in 1 case possibly induced by persistent delayed-type hypersensitivity to gold earrings {33675733}, no infectious or other causative agents have yet been identified.

Pathogenesis

Genetic or epigenetic factors contributing to tumour formation have not yet been described.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of a dense and monotonous dermal proliferation of atypical medium-sized lymphocytes with irregular and frequently folded nuclei with fine chromatin and moderate nuclear pleomorphism {18043044}. Less commonly, a perivascular pattern is observed. Mitotic activity is low (< 1%) or absent. The epidermis is mostly spared, but focal minimal epidermotropism and focal folliculotropism may be seen {22142195; 23189944}. In one third of the cases, the infiltrate is separated from the epidermis by a grenz zone. Plasma cells, histiocytes, neutrophils, and eosinophils are absent or few. The proliferation frequently involves the underlying subcutis.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells display a CD3+, CD4−, CD8+, CD30−, βF1+, and cytotoxic phenotype, with expression of TIA1, whereas granzyme B and perforin are usually negative. Loss or weak expression of one or more T-cell markers (CD2, CD5, CD7) is found in 85% of the cases {34988968}. The tumour cells express CD99 but are negative for CD56, PD1, and CD4, except for 1 case with coexpression of CD4 and CD8 {24394306; 30552698}. CD57, CD30, TdT, and T follicular helper (TFH) cell markers are always negative {23189944}. CD68 displays a characteristic Golgi dot-like staining pattern of the tumour cells, present in the majority (> 80%) of cases, which is almost unique to this entity {25524664; 34988968}. The Ki-67 (MIB1) proliferation index is typically low (< 10%), although a few cases with a high proliferation index have been reported {21083681; 34988968}. There may be reactive B-cell aggregates or, rarely, secondary follicles. EBV is always negative.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes other dermal CD8-positive lymphoproliferations (see #33473Table 6.16).

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The neoplastic T cells show clonal rearrangements of TR genes in nearly all cases {34988968}. Specific genetic abnormalities have not yet been documented.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dense, predominantly dermal non-epidermotropic infiltrate of atypical small to medium-sized CD8-positive cytotoxic T cells; typical clinical presentation of a usually solitary nodule (at acral sites); no extracutaneous involvement at diagnosis.

Desirable: characteristic Golgi dot-like expression of CD68 by tumour cells; monoclonal rearrangement of TR genes; EBV always negative; absence of immunodeficiency.

Staging

The TNM classification system for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides is applied {17339420}. Radiological staging is not mandatory if typical clinical and histopathological findings are present in the absence of immunodeficiency {31558559; 30635287}.

Prognosis and prediction

The tumour has an excellent prognosis {26423705; 34988968}. No fatal outcome has been reported. Complete remission after surgical excision or local radiation therapy is the rule {30635287; 34988968}. Recurrence after treatment is possible and may be more frequent in younger patients, and it can occasionally occur at other cutaneous sites {28796362; 23189944; 25524664; 24394306; 30351475}. Dissemination to extracutaneous sites is exceedingly rare {28796362}.
