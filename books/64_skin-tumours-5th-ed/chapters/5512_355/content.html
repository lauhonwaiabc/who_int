5.5.1.2 EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour

Definition

EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour (EBV-SMT) is an EBV-driven benign smooth muscle neoplasm occurring in the clinical setting of immunosuppression.

ICD-O coding

8897/1 EBV-associated smooth muscle tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F9C & XH00B4 Neoplasms of unknown behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Smooth muscle tumour, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: EBV-associated angioleiomyoma; EBV-associated myopericytoma; EBV-associated leiomyosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The commonest site of involvement is the CNS, followed by the liver, lung, and subcutaneous tissues of the limbs and trunk {15844077; 16330945; 21437186; 27362573; 31700854; 22175892; 25027306}.

Clinical features

When arising in the subcutis, the tumour manifests as a skin-coloured nodule. Occasionally, there may be associated pain {22175892; 12207602; 33245561; 33627249}. Multiple tumours may evolve concurrently or sequentially at different anatomical sites {15844077; 12207602}.

Epidemiology

EBV-SMT is a rare neoplasm. It occurs in approximately 1–5% of patients with immunosuppression {25027306}. The age range varies widely, but most patients are adults. There is a slight female predominance {21437186; 31700854; 2217589; 25318810}.

Etiology

Tumorigenesis is driven by EBV2 infection in the setting of immunosuppression related to a congenital immunodeficiency disorder, posttransplant iatrogenic immunosuppression, or HIV infection {25027306; 16330945; 7990861}.

Pathogenesis

It is postulated that EBV gains entry into a progenitor smooth muscle cell of vascular origin through CD21 receptor function or interaction with an EBV-infected lymphocyte {16330945; 7990860; 19706821}. There is aberration of the mTOR/AKT pathway {19706821}. Overexpression of MYC has been reported {9890420; 22420456}. Multiple tumours represent separate clonal events {16330945}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours show circumscription and pink-tan to whitish cut surfaces. They range from 10 to 60 mm in size {31700854; 28280632; 20733553; 12207602}.

Histopathology

Irrespective of site, the majority of EBV-SMTs show circumscription and interlacing fascicles of spindled myoid cells in a background of collagenous matrix {31700854; 22175892; 33627249; 3324556}. Typically, there is an admixed population of smaller rounded cells and intratumoural lymphocytes. Some tumours show focal myxoid change. Mitotic activity is generally low. However, tumours with leiomyosarcoma-like features showing nuclear atypia and necrosis in addition to high mitotic activity have been described {22175892; 7990860; 25318810}. Some may mimic myopericytoma or angioleiomyoma {25027306; 21437186; 18199115; 22175892}. EBV-SMTs show expression of h-caldesmon, desmin, SMA, and MSA.

Important differential diagnoses include Kaposi sarcoma, leiomyoma, leiomyosarcoma, and a pseudotumour secondary to mycobacterial or fungal infection. None of these entities show nuclear reactivity by in situ hybridization for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

EBV infection of neoplastic myoid cells is usually demonstrated by in situ hybridization for EBER.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of EBV infection in the constituent myoid cells of a smooth muscle tumour arising in an immunosuppressed patient.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Although the prognosis is generally favourable, it is dependent upon immune status, irrespective of tumour morphology {16330945; 25027306; 15844077}.
