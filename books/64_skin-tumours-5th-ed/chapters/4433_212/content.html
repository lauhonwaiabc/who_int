4.4.3.3 T-prolymphocytic leukaemia

Definition

T-prolymphocytic leukaemia (T-PLL) is a clonal proliferation of small to medium-sized prolymphocytes with a mature postthymic T-cell phenotype, characterized by juxtaposition of TCL1A or MTCP1 next to a TR locus – most commonly the TRA/TRD locus.

ICD-O coding

9834/3 T-prolymphocytic leukaemia

ICD-11 coding

2A90.0 T-cell prolymphocytic leukaemia

Related terminology

Acceptable: T-cell prolymphocytic leukaemia.

Not recommended: T-cell chronic lymphocytic leukaemia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Involvement of the peripheral blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, spleen, liver, skin, and pleural fluid has been observed {17707160; 25310835}.

Clinical features

There is extreme lymphocytosis (with > 100 lymphocytes × 109/L in 75% of cases) with anaemia and thrombocytopenia due to bone marrow infiltration. Frequently, hepatosplenomegaly and generalized lymphadenopathy with only slightly enlarged lymph nodes are observed. Approximately 30% of patients present with cutaneous manifestations, including generalized eruptions, purpuric vasculitic lesions {34089197; 33837964}, oedema, nodules, and erythroderma mimicking Sézary syndrome {7888364; 25310835; 16908353}, which often involve the head and neck area {7888364; 25310835; 34089197}. The lesions show a predilection for the face and upper trunk regions. About 30% of patients may present at an asymptomatic and indolent phase, but these eventually progress to active disease with exponentially rising blood lymphocytosis. Serum immunoglobulins are normal.

Epidemiology

T-PLL predominates in the elderly, with a median age of 65 years (range: 30–94 years) and an M:F ratio of 1.33:1 {31292114}.

Etiology

A subset may arise in the context of ataxia–telangiectasia, characterized by germline mutations in ATM {8555463; 25488969} (see section Chapter 9: Genetic tumour syndromes associated with skin malignancies).

Pathogenesis

The chromosomal rearrangements inv(14)(q11q32) and t(14;14)(q11;q32), juxtaposing TCL1A and homologous MTCP1, respectively, to the TRA/TRD TR gene locus, are considered initiating events {1913594; 9614908; 1742486; 31677197}. These rearrangements result in dysregulated expression of members of the TCL1 oncoprotein family, which enhance T-cell receptor and AKT signalling to increase cell proliferation and inhibit activation-induced cell death {33301031}. Hypomorphic (reduced function) ATM resulting from mutations and/or deletions leads to impaired DNA damage repair and cooperates with TCL1 deregulation to accumulate DNA lesions, contributing to leukaemogenesis {29449575}. Activating mutations of IL2RG, JAK1, JAK3, and STAT5B leading to constitutive STAT5 signalling are highly recurrent (75%) in T-PLL {24048415; 24825865; 26917488; 31766351}. Mutations in EZH2, FBXW10, and CHEK2 further contribute to the pathogenesis through their functions in epigenetic regulation, proteasome degradation, and DNA-repair pathways, respectively {24825865; 29449575}.

Complex karyotypes are observed in 70–80% of cases. Abnormalities of chromosome 8 including idic(8)(p11), t(8:8)(p11-12;q12), and trisomy 8q are seen in 70–80% of cases {10077617}. Deletions in 12p13 {11920168} and 22q {19480937}, gains in 8q24 (MYC) {25310835}, and abnormalities of chromosomes 5p, 6, and 17 {1913594; 14580769; 19278963} have also been described.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Skin biopsy shows a predominantly non-epidermotropic, perivascular and periadnexal infiltrate of prolymphocytes in most cases. Limited epidermotropism is seen in some cases.

The cells are small to medium-sized, with basophilic cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli. A small cell pattern (~25%) and a cerebriform pattern (~5%) of the disease have been described.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells are positive for CD2, CD3, CD7, CD52, S100 {26379148}, and cytoplasmic TCL1, and negative for TdT, CD1a, CD16, and CD30 {24825865}. The expression of CD4 and CD8 varies: CD4+/CD8− (60%), CD4+/CD8+ (25%), and CD4−/CD8+ (15%).

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma, T-cell large granular lymphocytic leukaemia, T-lymphoblastic leukaemia, and other mature T-cell leukaemias.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Clonal TR gene rearrangements (of TRB or TRG) are detected, but without prominent clonotypic biases {30414304}. Rearrangements involving TCL1 (T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma 1 family) genes (TCL1A, MTCP1) and the TRA/TRD locus are detectable in 90% of cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #42080Box 6.04 .

Staging

At diagnosis, about 30% of cases are in the “inactive” stage, while approximately 70% of patients present with active disease {31292114}.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients have an early stable condition for 1–2 years, followed by rapid progression {31292114}. The median overall survival time from diagnosis is 19–20 months, and the poor prognostic factors include low haemoglobin (< 9.3 g/dL), a high serum LDH level (≥ 1668 IU/L), an increased white blood cell count (≥ 208 × 109/L), the presence of pleural effusion {28379307}, and a complex karyotype {28194886}.

Essential (major) diagnostic criteria:

 

Peripheral blood lymphocytosis > 5 × 109/L or bone marrow infiltrate with T-PLL immunophenotype

 

Evidence of T-cell monoclonality

 

Demonstration of TCL1A (14q32) or MTCP1 (Xq28) rearrangement (desirable identification of juxtaposed TR locus, most frequently TRD) or TCL1A protein expression

Minor diagnostic criteria (at least one required):

 

Abnormalities involving chromosome 11 (11q22.3, ATM)

 

Abnormalities in chromosome 8: idic(8)(p11), t(8;8), trisomy 8q

 

Abnormalities in chromosome 5, 12, 13, or 22, or complex karyotype

 

Involvement of specific sites (e.g. splenomegaly, effusions)

In 90–95% of cases, the diagnosis is established by all three major criteria being met.
