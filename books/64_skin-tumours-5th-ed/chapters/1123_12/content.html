1.1.2.3 Verruca plana

Definition

Verruca plana is an HPV-induced benign epidermal proliferation characterized by flat-topped cutaneous papules.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

1E80.1 Plane warts

Related terminology

Acceptable: plane wart; flat wart.

Subtype(s)

Epidermodysplasia verruciformis acanthoma

Localization

The lesions occur most frequently on the face, hands, and shins {22131115}. Rarely, plane warts may occur on the site of a tattoo {32282734}.

Clinical features

Patients present with several smooth-surfaced, skin-coloured to light-brown papules. Associated koebnerization can occur {22131115}. Redness or depigmentation is often indicative of regression {921896}. Dermoscopy has emerged as a useful diagnostic adjunct {33866733}.

Epidemiology

Verruca plana most commonly affects children. In adults, there is a predilection for women {22131115}.

Etiology

The most frequently implicated HPV types are HPV3, HPV5, HPV10, and HPV28 {2172338; 22131115; 23932731}. Epidermodysplasia verruciformis, an autosomal recessive genodermatosis with mutations in TMC6 (EVER1) or TMC8 (EVER2) confers susceptibility to causative HPV types {9146528}. Immunosuppression is a risk factor {22131115}. Multiple plane warts occupying > 5% of the body surface area are a potential harbinger of underlying HIV infection {19940800}. In patients infected with HIV, a sudden eruption of lesions may herald the immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome {18975443}.

Pathogenesis

See the Pathogenesis subsection of the section Verruca vulgaris section .

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

There is orthokeratosis, mild acanthosis, and variable hypergranulosis, but only minimal papillomatosis. Scattered koilocytes (some with grey-blue cytoplasm) are present in the upper third of the epidermis {22131115}. Owl’s-eye intranuclear inclusions may be found. Regression is associated with a superficial perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate, lymphocytic exocytosis, and keratinocytic apoptosis {2839432; 900963}.

Epidermodysplasia verruciformis exhibits a spectrum of features, including orthokeratosis, an irregular granular layer, and enlarged keratinocytes with abundant bluish-grey, often vacuolated cytoplasm {11023102}.

Differential diagnosis

Seborrhoeic keratoses tend to be more scattered, and apart from being histologically distinctive, they possess different dermoscopic and reflectance confocal microscopic features from plane warts {33866733; 33174647}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Routine HPV subtyping is not indicated.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mild acanthosis; hypergranulosis; koilocytosis.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Some lesions show spontaneous regression, whereas others can be persistent or recurrent despite treatment {23932731; 24938453}. In one historical study of plane warts that had regressed, 64% were found to have done so spontaneously; the other 36% underwent regression following various forms of treatment {900963}.
