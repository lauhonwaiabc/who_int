5.1.0.6 Pleomorphic liposarcoma

Definition

Pleomorphic liposarcoma is a pleomorphic, high-grade spindle cell sarcoma containing a variable number of pleomorphic lipoblasts. No areas of well-differentiated liposarcoma or other lines of differentiation are present.

ICD-O coding

8854/3 Pleomorphic liposarcoma

8854/3 Epithelioid pleomorphic liposarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B59.Y & XH25R1 Liposarcoma, other specified primary site & Pleomorphic liposarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid pleomorphic liposarcoma

Localization

Tumours most commonly involve the extremities, followed by the trunk and the head and neck region {22472959; 11979090; 15371941; 26775787; 30852046}. Primary cutaneous and subcutaneous tumours are extremely rare (< 75 cases reported, with < 15 being primary dermal) {26775787; 22472959}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a rapidly growing, nonspecific or occasionally fatty-appearing mass of the skin or subcutis.

Epidemiology

Pleomorphic liposarcoma typically occurs in adults in later life {22472959; 11979090; 15371941; 26775787}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic and molecular genetic studies have consistently identified multiple chromosomal copy-number and structural abnormalities similar to the findings in other high-grade pleomorphic sarcomas {21598240; 9591630; 29220294; 33802620}. Subcutaneous tumours have similar molecular abnormalities to deep-seated lesions. Like other pleomorphic sarcomas, pleomorphic liposarcomas show frequent loss of TP53, RB1 {18784837; 21598240}, and NF1 {20601955}. Frequent gains in 1p21, 1q21-q22, 5p13-p15, and 7q22A are also seen {20217954}.

Macroscopic appearance

Superficial pleomorphic liposarcomas are smaller than their deep-seated counterparts, which have a median size of 80–100 mm {11979090; 15371941}. Tumours may be circumscribed, but they are unencapsulated or poorly defined, infiltrative, and sometimes multinodular. The cut surface appears white to yellow, with frequent myxoid change and necrosis.

Histopathology

The tumours contain a variable number of pleomorphic lipoblasts in a background of undifferentiated, high-grade, pleomorphic spindle cell sarcoma. Lipoblasts are required for the diagnosis, but they may be abundant or few in number. Rare cases consist of sheets of pleomorphic lipoblasts, sometimes showing epithelioid (epithelioid subtype) or small cell morphology. Myxofibrosarcoma-like histology may be infrequently seen in subcutaneous tumours {11979090; 27778364}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes other pleomorphic spindle cell sarcomas that lack lipoblasts and other lipogenic tumours – mainly atypical lipomatous tumour and dedifferentiated liposarcoma (described in the WHO classification of tumours of soft tissue and bone [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]]) – as well as spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Absence of MDM2 amplification with a complex karyotype and widespread complex copy-number changes may help in the diagnosis. Specific reoccurring molecular alterations have not been reported {20217954; 21598240}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pleomorphic spindle cell sarcoma with variable numbers of pleomorphic lipoblasts; for epithelioid subtype: sheets of carcinoma-like epithelioid cells with pleomorphic lipoblasts.

Desirable: absence of MDM2 amplification.

Staging

The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM systems may be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of dermal and subcutaneous pleomorphic liposarcoma is very favourable (unlike that of its more common deep-seated counterparts). Only rare cases of metastatic disease have been reported {22472959; 11979090; 15371941; 26775787}.
