3.1.2.2 Microcystic adnexal carcinoma

Definition

Microcystic adnexal carcinoma is an infiltrative adnexal carcinoma showing follicular and sweat duct differentiation and bland cytology.

ICD-O coding

8407/3 Microcystic adnexal carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH17P2 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Microcystic adnexal carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: sclerosing sweat duct carcinoma; syringomatoid carcinoma; eccrine epithelioma; malignant syringoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumours have a preference for the central face, mainly the upper lip and nasolabial fold. Extrafacial lesions are rare {12037446}. The solid pattern of the tumour is more common on the scalp (see below) {30187501}.

Clinical features

Most cases arise as a sclerotic plaque, with an average diameter of 20 mm; less commonly, they can arise as a nodule, or a cyst-like tumour. Ulceration is unusual.

Epidemiology

There is no sex predilection. Patients are usually affected in their fifth or sixth decade of life, but the reported age range is wide. The neoplasm predominantly affects White populations {11074698}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown, but rare cases have been associated with radiation therapy.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Microcystic adnexal carcinoma is an infiltrative neoplasm that involves the dermis and often extends to underlying tissues, commonly showing horizontal stratified zonation {7093897; 4091180; 2163783}. It is composed of various proportions of cornifying cysts with compact or laminated keratinous material, usually distributed in the upper part of the lesion; solid nests, strands, and columns of small monomorphic epithelial cells in the midzone; and ductal structures composed of two layers of small cuboidal cells (empty or filled with eosinophilic material) in the lower part. Perineural invasion is common, but there is no pleomorphism or necrosis, and mitoses are exceptional. The stroma is sclerotic, hyalinized, and paucicellular, and granulomatous inflammation induced by ruptured cysts may be seen. Focal clear cell change is frequent. Less common features include other types of adnexal differentiation (e.g. follicular or sebaceous), the occurrence of metaplastic bone, and conspicuous single-file cell infiltration resembling a metastatic lobular carcinoma of the breast {2466067}. A solid pattern of the neoplasm has been described and consists of larger solid nests of cells that are poroid or associated with more abundant pink cytoplasm or clear cell change {30187501}. Exceptionally, neoplasms with areas displaying high-grade morphology are seen {32359178}. The immunoprofile is nonspecific. The neoplasm stains for various cytokeratins and p63; expression of PHLDA1 is variable. Ductal differentiation can be highlighted by staining for EMA or CEA {25873411; 1689137}. The proliferation rate is very low.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sclerotic plaque or (sometimes) a nodule or cystic tumour; an infiltrative neoplasm with a desmoplastic stroma composed of strands and nests of bland cells with variable formation of cysts and ducts and no mitotic activity.

Desirable: perineural invasion.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

The tumours are locally aggressive and capable of involving deep underlying tissue, including bone. When incompletely excised, microcystic adnexal carcinoma is associated with frequent recurrence, but regional lymph node and distant metastases are rare {11074698; 10426893; 16549917; 11298716}.
