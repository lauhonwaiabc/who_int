4.4.0.1 Reactive T-cell-rich lymphoid proliferations

Definition

Reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferations (rT-LPs) are defined by a T-cell–predominant lymphocytic infiltrate. Histologically, and in some cases also clinically, rT-LPs can resemble cutaneous T-cell lymphomas.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: cutaneous lymphoid hyperplasia.

Not recommended: pseudolymphoma; nodular T-cell pseudolymphoma; CD30-positive T-cell pseudolymphoma; atypical lymphoid hyperplasia; T-cell dyscrasia; pseudolymphomatous folliculitis; lymphomatoid drug reaction; lymphomatoid contact dermatitis; actinic reticuloid; benign atypical intravascular (CD30+) lymphoproliferation.

Subtype(s)

Nodular reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation; mycosis fungoides–like reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation; CD30-positive reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation; intravascular reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation; angiomatoid reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation; subcutaneous reactive T-cell–rich lymphoid proliferation

Localization

No data exist on the predilection sites of rT-LP.

Clinical features

Nodular rT-LP manifests with a solitary reddish non-ulcerated nodule {17178981; 28477891}. Manifestations of other rT-LP forms include papules and small nodules (e.g. CD30+ rT-LP), maculopapular eruptions, or erythroderma (e.g. in drug-induced rT-LP, formerly also referred to as lymphomatoid drug reaction; actinic reticuloid), as well as erythematous and scaly lesions (e.g. ultraviolet [UV] radiation–induced rT-LP, including cases formerly referred to as actinic reticuloid) {7986687; 9631994; 28477891; 31237707}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of rT-LP is unknown.

Etiology

Many causative factors have been identified, including drugs {29361381; 19336342; 2137071; 23328787}; arthropod bites {18496425}; viruses (HIV, herpes, molluscum contagiosum virus, orthopoxviruses, parapoxviruses) {10534634; 7695009; 31062372; 17714139}; bacteria (Borrelia spp., Treponema pallidum) {23038025; 30411381}; parasites (Leishmania) {17714139; 25617014}; vaccines {12218234; 15793512}; tattoo dyes {34276026; 29136262}; contact allergens {1021341; 34991101}; UV radiation exposure (causing cases formerly designated as actinic reticuloid); other inflammatory skin diseases; and trauma (especially in intravascular rT-LP) {25319987; 33911554; 26844617}. All cases with an identifiable cause can formally be referred to as causative factor–associated rT-LP and thus be assigned to specific disorders (e.g. T-cell–rich Borrelia infection).

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is not well understood. Various causative factors can induce T-cell activation and proliferation.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Various histopathological subtypes of rT-LP have been described {31237707; 16173980; 7986687; 9631994; 28477891}.

Nodular rT-LP is characterized by a nodular or (in incisional biopsies) diffuse dermal infiltrate of small lymphocytes with an admixture of a variable number of eosinophils, plasma cells, and B cells {31237707}. Exocytosis of lymphocytes into the interfollicular epidermis is absent or only subtle and focal, but exocytosis of lymphocytes into the hair follicles can be found {10554999; 21522048}. Occasionally, perifollicular accentuation and/or a granulomatous component can be present. In most cases the lymphocytes do not display significant nuclear atypia.

In mycosis fungoides–like rT-LP and cases simulating CD8-positive cytotoxic T-cell lymphomas, often a superficial perivascular or band-like infiltrate of small lymphocytes is found with a variable degree of interface change. Intraepidermal lymphocytes can exhibit irregular nuclear contours.

In CD30-positive rT-LP, the hallmark finding is medium-sized to (rarely) large lymphocytes, arranged scattered or in small clusters, expressing CD30 {16412214; 19336342; 18616762; 23328787}.

Intravascular rT-LP is characterized by large blastoid cells with variable expression of CD30 in and around lymphatic vessels {25319987; 33911554; 26844617}.

Rarely, rT-LP manifests with subcutaneous infiltrates of small lymphocytes in a predominantly lobular pattern (subcutaneous rT-LP), e.g. in Borrelia sp. infection {23038025}.

Angiomatoid rT-LP shows a prominent proliferation of capillaries or postcapillary venules {8030763; 21352259; 28477891; 24628800}.

Immunohistochemistry

The T cells in rT-LP express CD3, βF1 (TCRαβ), CD4, and (less commonly) CD8 {20687841; 29055067}, without loss of other T-cell antigens except for a partial loss of CD7 (usually < 30% of T cells have loss of CD7) {29361381; 30411381}. CD8 cells may predominate in the epidermis or junctional zone and CD4 cells in the dermis. CD30 can be expressed by scattered atypical-appearing lymphocytes {16412214; 19336342; 18616762}. Small to medium-sized T cells may express PD1 or BCL6 but are negative for CD10 {25384543; 21989349}. A variable number of B cells and an interstitial or perifollicular distribution of CD1a-positive and S100-positive dendritic cells can be observed {25384543; 10554999}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes various cutaneous T-cell or systemic NK/T-cell lymphomas (see #33484Table 6.12). Clinicopathological correlation is of utmost significance for the final diagnosis.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In most cases, clonality studies revealed a polyclonal result for TR genes {21501893}. Rarely, T-cell clones may be present {34049317; 18547309; 2404672}. Borrelia sp. DNA may be detected in some cases {25617014; 23038025}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an epidermotropic and/or a dermal and/or subcutaneous T-cell–rich infiltrate of predominantly small lymphocytes mimicking cutaneous T-cell lymphoma; exclusion of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma by clinicopathological correlation.

Desirable: an absence of TR gene clonality is supportive, although some cases of rT-LP may show TR gene clonality; identification of causative factors (infectious agents, allergens).

Staging

There is no staging system for rT-LP. Radiological staging is not necessary, but it should be considered in cases with an admixture of atypical medium-sized or large cells and/or multifocal lesions.

Prognosis and prediction

rT-LP is a reactive benign proliferation of T lymphocytes and has an excellent prognosis {9631994; 29136262}.

Regression without further active therapeutic intervention is commonly observed after withdrawal or avoidance of inducing factors (e.g. allergens). Antimicrobial therapy is needed for cases of infection-associated rT-LP {29136262}.
