5.3.1.11 Congenital non-progressive haemangiomas: Rapidly involuting congenital haemangioma and non-involuting congenital haemangioma

Definition

Congenital non-progressive haemangiomas are a distinct group of vascular lesions that arise in utero and are fully formed at birth. There are two clinical subtypes: rapidly involuting congenital haemangioma (RICH) and non-involuting congenital haemangioma (NICH). For cases that regress incompletely, the term “partially involuted congenital haemangioma (PICH)” has been proposed.

ICD-O coding

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Rapidly involuting congenital haemangioma (RICH); partially involuted congenital haemangioma (PICH); non-involuting congenital haemangioma (NICH)

Localization

The most common location is the head, followed by the extremities.

Clinical features

The lesions are usually fully formed at birth, typically following either a persistent clinical course (NICH) or a very rapidly regressive course through infarction (RICH) during the first 6 months of life {15018449}. Initial lesions vary in size and consist of a vascular red or bluish mass with overlying telangiectasia, occasionally associated with ulceration. Rare tumours regress incompletely (PICH) {24176519}. NICH increases in size proportionally as the patient grows, and it does not regress {11391180}. Exceptional cases show limited postnatal growth.

Epidemiology

Congenital non-progressive haemangiomas arise at birth, with no sex predilection. They are less common than infantile haemangioma.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Somatic activating mutations in GNAQ and GNA11 at Q209 have been identified in both NICH and RICH {27058448}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

RICH, PICH, and NICH vary along a histopathological spectrum, largely dependent on the degree of infarction. All are composed of variably well-circumscribed capillary lobules within the dermis and/or subcutis. The interlobular stroma is often fibrotic and may contain arteries, veins, and sometimes lymphatic channels of moderate size. Intralobular capillary endothelial cells and pericytes can be plump, but they lack the increased mitotic activity, GLUT1 positivity, and basement membrane multilamination of infantile haemangioma. Persistent lesions (NICHs and PICHs) are more likely to display arteriovenous shunts, hobnail endothelial cells, and a large vessel in the centre of the vascular lobules {11391180}. The endothelial cells of all subtypes are negative for GLUT1 {15153887}.

Differential diagnosis

The distinction from infantile haemangioma is usually straightforward, on the basis that congenital non-progressive haemangiomas arise at birth and lack endothelial positivity for GLUT1 {8774499; 9551150; 15153887; 24176519}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a congenital lesion; dermal and/or subcutaneous lobules of capillaries lined by endothelial cells that are negative for GLUT1.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Most RICHs involute spontaneously, and NICHs tend to persist. Persistent congenital non-progressive haemangiomas often require surgical or laser intervention.
