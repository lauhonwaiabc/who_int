4.4.1.4 Subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma (SPTCL) is a lymphoproliferative disorder with adipotropism that mostly involves the subcutaneous tissue but occasionally involves other sites. It is predominantly composed of CD8-positive lymphocytes expressing the TCRαβ heterodimer. Other cytotoxic lymphomas frequently involving the subcutaneous tissue, like gamma-delta and NK/T-cell lymphomas, are excluded from this category.

Localization

SPTCL primarily involves the subcutaneous tissue of any anatomical site, with a predilection for the lower extremities, followed by the upper extremities and trunk {17934071}. Rarely, other sites rich in adipose tissue (e.g. mesentery, perinodal fat, bone marrow) can be involved {32924664; 24201122}.

Clinical features

Patients present with tender, non-ulcerated nodules. A sequela of lipoatrophy is occasionally observed {30126736; 36001337}. Constitutional symptoms, including fever and malaise, are commonly observed, while approximately 20–30% of cases may evolve to haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis {27722764; 17934071}. A minority of cases present with a single nodule that can trigger similar systemic symptoms {32924664; 24201122}.

Epidemiology

SPTCL is rare. The incidence rate is approximately 1.5 new cases per 10 million person-years {30951189} in the USA {30951189}, but it is more common in Asia {24438138; 26560051; 22521781; 34235215}. There is a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:3–4). The median age at presentation is 35–42 years, with a significant number of paediatric and adolescent cases {23382035}. Autoimmune comorbidities are observed in at least 25% of cases {17934071; 27722764}; in particular, lupus erythematosus is a common comorbidity, which may cause difficulties in differentiating SPTCL from an associated lupus panniculitis {33966586}.

Etiology

Autoimmunity and immune dysregulation may play a role in many cases {27722764}. The constellation of findings may overlap with lupus erythematosus, raising the differential diagnosis of lupus panniculitis in cases presenting with lymphocyte-rich lobular infiltrates with adipotropic features {19590424}. SPTCL is not associated with EBV {17934071}.

Pathogenesis

Germline mutations of HAVCR2 altering the HAVCR2 protein (also known as TIM3), a negative membrane modulator of immune response, have been identified in 25% of patients of European descent {32005988} and 51–85% of patients of Asian descent {34535012; 30792187}. Protein misfolding abrogates membrane expression of this negative immune checkpoint, regulating peripheral tolerance {30374066}. Low levels of T regulatory (Treg) cells (FOXP3+, CCR4+) may lead to immune activation {31355940}. Various triggers have been proposed for patients without germline mutations, including viral infections and autoimmunity {30792187}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The infiltrate is typically composed of medium-sized lymphocytes with irregular and hyperchromatic nuclei, involving the subcutaneous lobules and often extending into the periadnexal fat pad. The atypical cells may display a rimming pattern around adipocytes with variable fat necrosis, karyorrhectic debris, and haemorrhage. Lipid-laden macrophages are abundant and may show haemophagocytosis. Plasma cells, granulocytes, and plasmacytoid dendritic cells are inconspicuous. Tumoural large cell transformation is typically not observed.

Immunohistochemistry

This reveals CD8-positive mature T cells with αβ heterodimer expression (BF1+) and retention of pan–T-cell markers (CD2+, CD5+, CD7+). The cells display foci of high Ki-67 proliferative activity and typically express the cytotoxic markers TIA1, perforin, and granzyme B. CD30 and EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) are negative. A small subset of reactive T cells expressing TCRγδ may be observed, but markers for Treg cells (FOXP3, CCR4) are typically low {31355940}. Clusters of plasmacytoid dendritic cells are not present in SPTCL, in contrast to lupus panniculitis {30597607}.

Differential diagnosis

This includes lupus panniculitis, gamma-delta T-cell lymphomas, and EBV-positive NK/T-cell lymphomas (see #36592Table 6.15).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Clonal rearrangements of TR genes (in particular TRB and TRG) are detected in most cases. EBV in situ hybridization is negative {17934071}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobular subcutaneous infiltrate composed of atypical lymphocytes with rimming of adipocytes and expression of TCRαβ and CD8; absence of TCRγδ expression; negative EBV.

Desirable: demonstration of clonal TR gene rearrangement.

Staging

SPTCL is staged according to the TNM classification system for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides and Sézary syndrome {17540844}.

Prognosis and prediction

SPTCL carries a good prognosis, often responding to a variety of therapies including systemic steroids and immunosuppressive drugs (e.g. ciclosporin or methotrexate). Single-agent or multiagent chemotherapies are also effective and tend to be used when immunomodulatory agents fail {30126736; 27722764}. Life-threatening haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, particularly in patients with HAVCR2 mutations (approximately 20–30% of patients), may require chemoablative therapy followed by stem cell transplantation {27722764; 32005988}.
