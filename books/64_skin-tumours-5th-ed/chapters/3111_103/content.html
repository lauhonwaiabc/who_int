3.1.1.1 Hidrocystoma/cystadenoma

Definition

Hidrocystoma/cystadenoma constitutes a spectrum of benign cystic lesions of predominantly ductal sweat gland origin, with architecture ranging from simple cystic (hidrocystoma) to more complex (cystadenoma).

ICD-O coding

8404/0 Hidrocystoma/cystadenoma

8440/0 Cystadenoma

8404/0 Apocrine hidrocystoma

8404/0 Eccrine hidrocystoma

ICD-11 coding

EK70.3 Hidrocystoma

2F3Z & XH5RJ2 Benign non-mesenchymal neoplasms of unspecified site & Cystadenoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: apocrine gland cyst; eccrine ductal cyst.

Subtype(s)

Cystadenoma; apocrine hidrocystoma; eccrine hidrocystoma

Localization

Most hidrocystomas occur on the face, with a predilection for the eyelids, but they can also affect the ears, vulva, and fingers.

Clinical features

Hidrocystomas are solitary, asymptomatic, bluish, cystic, and translucent lesions, usually located on the face. They typically measure 5–10 mm but can reach 70 mm in size. Multiple lesions (Robinson-type hidrocystomas) are rare {8548991}. Schöpf–Schulz–Passarge syndrome, a form of ectodermal dysplasia, is associated with multiple hidrocystomas at the edges of the eyelids {2947556}. Multiple hidrocystomas may also be associated with bilateral keratoconus, oesophageal papillomas, and hiatal hernias in patients with Goltz syndrome {1158014}. Occasionally, multiple facial hidrocystomas of the face have been reported in patients with Graves disease {12100710}.

Epidemiology

Hidrocystoma affects middle-aged adults of both sexes [[Requena L, Sangüeza O. Cutaneous adnexal neoplasms. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2017.]].

Etiology

Hidrocystomas usually increase in size during the summer or after exposure to heat; they decrease in size or disappear in winter.

Pathogenesis

Most hidrocystomas result from obstruction of the duct, but some may be neoplastic. Most appear to be of apocrine origin, but some may be eccrine.

Macroscopic appearance

Hidrocystomas are usually received for histology as small, thin-walled, collapsed cysts with surrounding skin or connective tissue.

Histopathology

Hidrocystomas typically present as round to oval solitary cystic structures. They are lined by columnar epithelium, often showing decapitation secretion, surrounded by myoepithelial cells and a basement membrane. Mucin may be present in the columnar cells. Most cases contain abundant mucin in the lumen, but the content is sometimes homogeneous and eosinophilic. Accumulation of secretion material can compress and flatten the epithelium. Rare hidrocystomas with papillary-like epithelial projections or more-complex architecture are regarded as cystadenoma.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemically, luminal cells are positive for EMA and CEA; the myoepithelial elements are immunoreactive for S100, SOX10, and p63. Other markers include GCDFP-15, as well as various keratins in a pattern similar to that seen in the secretory portion of the eccrine glands [[Requena L, Sangüeza O. Cutaneous adnexal neoplasms. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2017.]].

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes lacrimal gland cysts.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a unilocular or multilocular cystic lesion lined by columnar cells with or without decapitation secretion and surrounded by a layer of myoepithelial cells.

Staging

Prognosis and prediction

Hidrocystomas are benign lesions.
