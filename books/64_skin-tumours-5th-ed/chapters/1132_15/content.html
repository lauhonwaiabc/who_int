1.1.3.2 Solar lentigo

Definition

The solar lentigines of solar lentigo are ultraviolet (UV) radiation–related benign intraepidermal keratinocytic proliferations, closely related to seborrhoeic keratoses.

ICD-O coding

8052/0 Solar lentigo

ICD-11 coding

EJ20.1 & XH7B58 Actinic lentigo & Solar lentigo

Related terminology

Acceptable: actinic lentigo.

Not recommended: senile lentigo.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Chronically sun-exposed skin, particularly of the face, the forearms, and the dorsal aspects of the hands, is affected.

Clinical features

Patients with solar lentigines present with small and uniformly pigmented tan to brown macules, measuring a few millimetres in diameter. Darkly pigmented examples are also referred to as ink-spot lentigines.

Epidemiology

Solar lentigines are markers of chronic UV radiation exposure. Their incidence increases with age, and onset is typically in middle-aged to elderly adults with fair skin {15140067}.

Etiology

Chronic UV radiation exposure is the main etiological factor {15140067}.

Pathogenesis

Little is known about the pathogenesis of these keratinocytic proliferations. Like in seborrhoeic keratoses, mutations in the FGFR3 and PIK3CA genes have been detected in a number of cases {19076977}. Solar lentigines appear to lack BRAF mutations.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Solar lentigines are well-demarcated intraepidermal proliferations of bland basaloid keratinocytes, characterized by elongated rete ridges with hyperpigmentation of the basal cell layer and budding at the base. There may be fusion of rete ridges, overlying hyperkeratosis, and mildly increased numbers of junctional melanocytes {9091478}. Solar lentigines typically arise on a background on solar elastosis. Ink-spot lentigines are small and sharply circumscribed, with intense melanin pigmentation of basal cell layer keratinocytes that is most prominent at the tips of the rete ridges.

Solar lentigines may show some morphological overlap with seborrhoeic keratosis, and reliable separation is not always possible. Pigmented actinic keratoses are characterized by squamous dysplasia. Lentigo maligna is typically associated with loss of the rete ridge pattern and shows confluent growth of atypical melanocytes, best highlighted by immunohistochemistry for melan-A, SOX10, or MITF {21797920}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed intraepidermal keratinocyte proliferation with elongated rete ridges and a lack of cytological atypia or squamous dysplasia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Solar lentigines are benign.
