5.2.1.11 Nodular fasciitis

Definition

Nodular fasciitis is a self-limited benign myofibroblastic/fibroblastic neoplasm, classically occurring in the subcutis, arranged in a disorganized tissue culture–like pattern; it usually harbours USP6 rearrangement.

ICD-O coding

8828/0 Nodular fasciitis

8828/0 Intravascular fasciitis

8828/0 Cranial fasciitis

ICD-11 coding

FB51.2 & XH5LM1 Fasciitis and fibromatosis & Nodular fasciitis

Related terminology

Not recommended: pseudosarcomatous fasciitis; nodular pseudosarcomatous fasciitis; subcutaneous pseudosarcomatous fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

Intravascular fasciitis; cranial fasciitis

Localization

Nodular fasciitis can arise from the superficial fascia, from the subcutaneous septa, or (rarely) in the dermis, and it usually bulges into the subcutis. Intramuscular, intra-articular, and visceral lesions are rare {20716998; 16434899; 27686647; 8311183; 10380041}. The most common sites are the forearm, thigh, and upper arm, which account for more than half of all cases {6462780}; however, nodular fasciitis can involve any part of the body {19469872}. Well-described rare subtypes include intravascular fasciitis and cranial fasciitis {7351023; 7246849; 9923933}.

Clinical features

Nodular fasciitis presents clinically as a relatively rapidly growing subcutaneous mass that enlarges (typically over the course of 3–6 weeks, but in some cases over a period as long as 3 months) and then regresses. It is typically smaller than 20–30 mm in maximal diameter, although an exceptional 100 mm case has been reported {6279273; 31355033}. The mass may be tender or painless {6462780}.

Epidemiology

Nodular fasciitis can occur at any age but has a peak incidence in the third and fourth decades of life, with equal distribution between the sexes {5675403; 13737962}.

Etiology

Previously thought to be a reactive process, nodular fasciitis is now recognized as a translocation-driven neoplasm that consistently regresses (transient neoplasia) {21826056}.

Pathogenesis

Nodular fasciitis has been shown to harbour a translocation between chromosomes 17 and 22, t(17;22) (p13;q13), which results in MYH9::USP6 fusion and subsequent overexpression of USP6 {21826056}. USP6 encodes a deubiquitinating enzyme with roles in cell trafficking, cell signalling, protein breakdown, and inflammation. MYH9 encodes a non-muscle myosin heavy chain that has roles in cell motility, shape, adhesion, and differentiation. A study of p16, CDK4, MDM2, and p53 implicated the possible involvement of cell-cycle arrest and presumed senescence by p16 in lesional myofibroblastic cells in nodular fasciitis, resulting in its self-limited growth and regression {31933915}. In addition, USP6 expression creates a negative feedback via TNFSF10 (TRAIL) and interferon-β, resulting in apoptosis and senescence of tumour cells {34381187}.

Rearrangements in USP6 have been shown in approximately 90% of nodular fasciitis cases, and > 65% of these are MYH9::USP6 gene fusions {21826056}. These are most often detected in earlier (younger) lesions, while the detection of USP6 rearrangements in older lesions can be as low as 20% because of the negative feedback from USP6 expression in tumour cells {34381187}. Other gene promoters can be substituted for MYH9, sometimes with atypical features {28752842}: PPP6R3::USP6 and CALD1::USP6 have been reported in exceptional malignant cases {31538390; 34213575}, EIF5A::USP6 fusion has been described in a dermal nodular fasciitis of the left forearm with atypical mitosis {31880592}, and CTNNB1::USP6 fusion was seen in an intraneural nodular fasciitis {35175167}. USP6 rearrangements have also been reported in aneurysmal bone cyst, fibro-osseous tumour of the digits, and a subgroup of fibroma of tendon sheaths. Tumours in this group (including nodular fasciitis) have morphological and clinical overlap, and it has been suggested that they may represent a spectrum of manifestations of the same disease process in different locations {32635781; 30758758}.

Macroscopic appearance

Nodular fasciitis is unencapsulated, with variable circumscribed to infiltrative borders and a fibrous to myxoid and occasionally cystic cut surface.

Histopathology

Nodular fasciitis is composed of fibroblasts and/or myofibroblasts without overt cytological atypia. Mitotic activity can be frequent, but the mitoses are not atypical. Cellularity varies between and within lesions. Feathery tissue culture–like areas are typical of the lesion, but more-cellular areas can form fascicles. The stroma can be myxoid and/or collagenous, even keloidal. The vasculature is rich and fine, resembling granulation tissue. Extravasated red cells are usually seen, often in the tissue culture–like areas. Osteoclastic giant cells and chronic inflammatory cells can be present.

Immunohistochemistry

Strong expression of SMA by the neoplastic cells is characteristic.

Differential diagnosis

The histological differential diagnosis depends on the location, the proportion of collagen and myxoid stroma, and cellularity. Collagenous patterns can be mistaken for fibromatosis, collagenous fibroma (desmoplastic fibroblastoma), or granulation tissue. The differential diagnosis also includes neural tumours, nerve sheath tumours, myositis ossificans, proliferative fasciitis, proliferative myositis, and inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours. Large and/or mitotically active nodular fasciitis can be mistaken for sarcoma {11601130}.

Cytology

Tumour cells are bland and spindled with unipolar curved to bipolar processes, round to oval elongated nuclei, and occasional small nucleoli. A tissue culture–like appearance and myxoid stroma can be appreciated {30311731; 26981858}. Hypercellular early lesions and mitotically active lesions can lead to a misdiagnosis of sarcoma {31741577}, and collagenous forms may generate unsatisfactory hypocellular smears {27525591}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing is usually unnecessary in clinically and histologically typical cases. However, in challenging cases, detection of USP6 rearrangement may be useful to confirm the diagnosis {28752842; 29623468; 30139549}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland, typically cellular myofibroblastic spindle cells with a tissue culture–like pattern, variable myxoid stroma, extravasated red blood cells, and lymphocytes.

Desirable: USP6 rearrangement is useful in challenging cases, such as small biopsies or atypical presentations.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Nodular fasciitis can regress spontaneously and may very rarely recur following incomplete excision. Recurrence is rare.
