3.1.2.15 Cribriform tumour (previously carcinoma)

Definition

Cribriform tumour is an indolent adnexal neoplasm composed of epithelial cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm forming solid and cribriform structures with small lumina, devoid of a myoepithelial cell layer. Although this tumour was originally reported as a carcinoma, its malignant potential is uncertain.

ICD-O coding

8201/1 Cribriform tumour (previously carcinoma)

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH1YZ3 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Cribriform carcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: primary cutaneous cribriform carcinoma.

Not recommended: solid-cribriform carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The site of predilection is the lower extremities, which are affected in > 80% of cases. The head and neck area and other sites are rarely involved {25732813; 19751882}.

Clinical features

Cribriform tumour manifests as a solitary skin-coloured nodule of 10–30 mm in diameter, often having a firm consistency on palpation. The duration of symptoms ranges from several months to several years {19751882; 25732813} [[Kazakov DV, Michal M, Kacerovska D, et al., editors. Cutaneous adnexal tumors. Philadelphia (PA): Lippincott Williams & Wilkins; 2012.]].

Epidemiology

Cribriform tumour is a rare neoplasm. The M:F ratio is 1:2, and the reported age range is 20–77 years, with a median patient age of 47 years {25732813}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. No associated hereditary syndromes, susceptibility genes, or SNPs have been reported to date.

Pathogenesis

The histogenesis is unknown. Because of evidence of apocrine secretion in some cases, the neoplasm is considered to be apocrine.

Macroscopic appearance

There is a relatively well-demarcated unencapsulated nodule.

Histopathology

Most tumours are well circumscribed, although the borders are occasionally jagged and there may be small, satellite-like aggregates of tumour cells seemingly separated from the main lesion. The neoplasm is composed of multiple interconnected solid nests of epithelial cells with round or oval, hyperchromatic nuclei; inconspicuous or absent nucleoli; granular chromatin; and scant eosinophilic cytoplasm. In addition to the solid areas, there are also small round lumina that are either empty or filled with an eosinophilic substance that gives a homogeneously positive PAS reaction, giving rise to a cribriform pattern. The ratio of solid to cribriform areas varies both within a given neoplasm and among tumours. Common features are larger ductal spaces (sometimes with decapitation secretion); intraluminal micropapillary projections; and thin, thread-like intraluminal bridging strands. Mitotic figures, especially atypical ones, are rare. Individual necrotic cells or foci of necrosis can be evident focally. The small, satellite-like aggregates, composed of a single cell to three or four cells, usually display more-pronounced cytological atypia and nuclear hyperchromasia than the main tumour bulk, but perineural or lymphovascular invasion is not a feature. The stroma is scant and fibrotic, often containing nodular lymphoid aggregates at the periphery of the neoplasm {21315957; 19751882}.

The neoplastic cells are positive for various cytokeratins (MNF116, AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, CK5/6, and CK7) and BerEP4, and variably positive for KIT {19751882; 18085381}. Staining for CEA and EMA highlights the ductal component. CK20, GCDFP-15, CD15, CD31, GATA3, and PR are negative {29431200}. Immunoreactivity for S100 and p63 is variable {25732813; 18085381; 19751882; 16115058}. ER can be positive, as can ERBB2 {19751882; 18085381}. No myoepithelial cell component is identified by staining for α-SMA, MSA, or calponin, which is an important differential diagnostic feature {25732813; 19751882}.

Differential diagnosis

Adenoid cystic carcinoma shows a cribriform pattern but is composed of basaloid epithelial cells arranged around pseudolumina (pseudocysts) filled with mucin or eosinophilic or basophilic material. In addition to the pseudocystic structures, there are small true ducts with myoepithelial cell differentiation. These dual-type lumina are never seen in cribriform tumour; neither are the deposits of basement membrane material or perineural invasion that are typical of adenoid cystic carcinoma {24025525}. Cutaneous secretory carcinoma also shows cribriform areas but consists of back-to-back proliferations of tubules and microcysts with conspicuous intraluminal secretions. Coexpression of S100, mammaglobin, STAT5A, and pan-TRK helps to distinguish the neoplasm from cribriform tumour {27631515}. Rare cases of tubular adenoma may show overlap with cribriform tumour, but the presence of basal/myoepithelial cells in tubular adenoma distinguishes it {25732813}.

Cytology

Not reported

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nodular tumour composed of interconnected solid and cribriform nests of epithelial cells with scant eosinophilic cytoplasm, nuclear hyperchromasia, small round lumina, intraluminal micropapillary projections, and thin thread-like intraluminal bridging strands.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

Cribriform tumour is an indolent neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential, with no cases of recurrent or metastatic disease reported to date {26114606; 28802502}. A few cases have been reported to persist at the site secondary to incomplete excision {25732813}.
