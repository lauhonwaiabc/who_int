5.8.0.5 Inclusion body fibromatosis

Definition

Inclusion body fibromatosis is a benign myofibroblastic tumour characterized by eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions and the potential for local recurrence.

ICD-O coding

8814/0 Inclusion body fibromatosis
8814/0 Classic (digital) inclusion body fibromatosis
8814/0 Non-classic (extradigital) inclusion body fibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: infantile digital fibroma/fibromatosis; recurring digital fibrous tumour(s) of childhood; recurring digital fibroma(s) of childhood/infancy.

Subtype(s)

Classic (digital) inclusion body fibromatosis; non-classic (extradigital) inclusion body fibromatosis

Localization

The classic subtype occurs in the hands or feet and involves the dorsal/dorsolateral aspect of the distal or middle second, third, and fourth digits most frequently, followed by the fifth digit, while typically sparing the first digit {840560; 3892466; 18830128}. Non-classic tumours are reported to occur on the extremities, tongue, and breast {3740876; 8209857; 6496846; 7377793; 2836293}.

Clinical features

Patients with the classic subtype present with an asymptomatic, dome-shaped/polypoid cutaneous nodule, typically no larger than 20 mm {840560; 3835952; 18830128}. Synchronous and/or metachronous lesions occur, sometimes affecting multiple digits {4344937; 840560; 3835952}, but simultaneous involvement of both the fingers and the toes is rare {840560}. Patients with the non-classic subtype present with solitary masses/nodules {18830128}.

Epidemiology

The classic subtype accounts for 2% of paediatric fibroblastic tumours {1946077}. More than 80% of cases present by 3 years of age, with about 30% found at birth {3892466; 840560}. No sex predilection exists. Rare cases occur in adults {11069537; 7377793; 2836293; 8209857}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are firm to rubbery, smooth-surfaced protuberant/polypoid dermal nodules. The cut surface is fibrous and off-white to grey in colour.

Histopathology

Spindled cells have lightly eosinophilic cytoplasm and an elongated, cytologically bland nucleus, with low mitotic activity. Cells proliferate in whorls, interlacing short fascicles, or storiform arrays within a collagenous dermis, and they characteristically grow perpendicularly to the epidermis and around adnexa, occasionally infiltrating deeper tissues {4344937; 3892466}. A variable number of cells harbour a rounded, pale pink, intracytoplasmic (frequently paranuclear), 1.5–24 μm inclusion {14322942; 3892466} highlighted by trichrome (red), phosphotungstic acid–haematoxylin (dark purple), and Movat’s (pink) stains {18830128}.

Immunohistochemistry

Cells express vimentin, muscle actin(s) in a peripheral “tram-track” pattern, calponin, desmin, and CD99 {1310240; 18830128}. Inclusions mark with calponin-1 {24117680} and occasionally with caldesmon {18830128}.

Cytology

Cytology preparations yield plump spindled cells with intracytoplasmic inclusions in a collagenous background {21986179}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a dermal-based, cytologically bland, spindled cell proliferation; eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Large series of classic cases report recurrence rates between 61% and 75% {3892466; 18830128}. Lower recurrence rates are documented after initial complete (preferably wide) excision {8583050; 9493986; 17273037}. Current treatment options include function-preserving excision for symptomatic lesions and either intralesional steroid injection or clinical observation (as tumours may spontaneously regress) for asymptomatic lesions {840560; 17542881; 29780440}. Approximately 25% of non-classic tumours may recur {18830128}.
