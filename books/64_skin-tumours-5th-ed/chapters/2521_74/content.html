2.5.2.1 Mucosal melanoma

Definition

Mucosal melanomas are melanomas arising in the anogenital, oral, or sinonasal mucosa.

ICD-O coding

8720/3 Mucosal melanoma

8745/3 Desmoplastic mucosal melanoma

ICD-11 coding

2C70.1 Melanoma of vulva

2C11.2 & XH4846 Other specified malignant neoplasms of other or ill-defined digestive organs & Malignant melanoma, NOS

2C11.2 & XH5QP3 Other specified malignant neoplasms of other or ill-defined digestive organs & Mucosal lentiginous melanoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucosal lentiginous melanoma; vaginal melanoma; vulvar melanoma; vulvovaginal melanoma; genital melanoma; oral melanoma; sinonasal melanoma.

Subtype(s)

Desmoplastic mucosal melanoma

Localization

The sinonasal and oral mucosa are the most common sites of origin, followed by the anorectal mucosa and the female genital tract {9781962; 31670075}. Primaries of the pharynx and larynx, oesophagus, and lower urinary tract or cervix are less common {28325255; 25242350; 30044323}. In the oral cavity, most cases arise on the maxillary gingiva and palate {26753505}. Melanomas arising in palpebral conjunctiva have genetic alterations typical of mucosal melanoma {34359736} (see section Conjunctival melanoma).

Clinical features

Mucosal melanomas arise as irregularly pigmented macules or plaques, typically in older patients {25384702; 28107150}. Sinonasal melanomas come to clinical attention with nonspecific symptoms, including nasal obstruction, epistaxis, and sinus congestion, which simulate inflammatory conditions {12873174}.

Epidemiology

Mucosal melanomas represent approximately 1–3% of all melanomas {9869521; 24272143}. The mean age at diagnosis is 67 years {9781962}.

Etiology

Mucosal melanomas have no association with ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure {28467829; 31320640}.

Pathogenesis

Mucosal melanomas have a low mutation burden, without a UV radiation signature. Instead, they are characterized by numerous chromosomal structural variants and abundant copy-number changes, including multiple high-level amplifications {31320640; 30847022; 28467829; 23620124}. The most common somatic mutations affect BRAF, NRAS, KIT, NF1, SPRED1, SF3B1, CDKN2A, PTEN, ATRX, and TP53, with frequent amplifications of CCND1, PAK1, GAB2, TERT, CDK4, MDM2, YAP1, and CRKL, and deletions of CDKN2A, PTEN, NF1, and SPRED1 {28467829; 28720667; 35706047}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The invasive areas of the tumour are usually composed of sheets and expansive nodules of large pleomorphic and large epithelioid cells or, less commonly, spindle or smaller ovoid cells {17276948}. The nuclei often have vesicular chromatin and prominent nucleoli. The presence of an adjacent intraepithelial or superficially invasive component of atypical melanocytes supports a mucosal primary. Melanin production is variable but usually at least focally present within both melanoma cells and background macrophages.

Vulvar melanoma often shows an intraepidermal component with a confluent nested and lentiginous growth pattern and with pagetoid spread. In contrast, vaginal melanoma typically has a lentiginous growth of single atypical melanocytes in the basal epithelial layer, sometimes with nests or confluent growth. A subepithelial lymphocytic infiltrate is common. A desmoplastic component may be present {35257324}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry for S100, SOX10, MITF, HMB45, and melan-A is usually positive {12409721; 25292260}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes dysplastic naevi and special-site naevi (see section Dysplastic naevus, and section Special-site naevi (of the breast, axilla, scalp, and ear)).

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The absence of a UV radiation mutation signature and presence of characteristic clustered copy-number changes may assist in excluding metastatic cutaneous melanoma {34060071}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a malignant melanocytic neoplasm from a mucosal site.

Desirable: exclusion of a visceral metastasis from a melanoma elsewhere; reactivity for melanocytic markers (if needed).

Staging

Mucosal melanomas of the head and neck are staged using specific American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) criteria. Vulvar and vaginal melanomas are staged using the AJCC cutaneous melanoma staging system {25384702}.

Prognosis and prediction

The 5-year overall survival of mucosal melanoma ranges from 20% to 50% {24548569; 31670075; 28325255; 29748886; 28600969}. Large tumour size, advanced age and stage, and distant metastasis portend a poor prognosis {24548569; 31077407}.
