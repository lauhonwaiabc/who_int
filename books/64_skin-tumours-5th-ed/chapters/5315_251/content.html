5.3.1.5 Glomeruloid haemangioma

Definition

Glomeruloid haemangioma is a reactive vascular proliferation associated with multicentric Castleman disease and POEMS syndrome (polyradiculoneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, clonal plasma cell disorder, and skin changes).

ICD-O coding

9120/0 Glomeruloid haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y & XH9NB0 Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms & Glomeruloid haemangioma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This lesion affects the trunk and extremities.

Clinical features

The lesions are multiple erythematous papules.

Epidemiology

This rare haemangioma shows no sex predilection. Examples occurring outside the setting of multicentric Castleman disease or POEMS syndrome are likely to in fact be papillary haemangioma (see Differential diagnosis, below) {18001423; 19077091; 18805868; 17927585}. A rare association with Erdheim–Chester disease has been reported {30563806}.

Etiology

Glomeruloid haemangioma is generally considered to be a reactive process.

Pathogenesis

Raised serum levels of VEGF have been described in one study, suggesting that this may play in a role in the development of the vascular lesions {30563806}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

There is dermal proliferation of dilated vascular channels containing clusters of capillaries that resemble renal glomeruli. The endothelial cells often contain intracytoplasmic inclusions that give a positive PAS reaction; these inclusions may be immunoglobulins, enlarged lysosomes, or thanatosomes {2173428; 19614998; 8030771}.

Differential diagnosis

Papillary haemangioma has a predilection for the head and neck and manifests as a single lesion consisting of dermal dilated channels lined by endothelial cells with frequent papillary projections and lacking glomeruloid architecture {18805868}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dilated dermal vascular channels with intraluminal proliferation of small blood vessels mimicking renal glomeruli.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Glomeruloid haemangioma is benign but tends to persist, paralleling underlying systemic disease.
