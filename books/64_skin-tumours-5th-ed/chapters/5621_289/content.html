5.6.2.1 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a malignant spindle cell or epithelioid tumour often arising from a peripheral nerve, from a pre-existing benign nerve sheath tumour, or in a patient with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1). Cutaneous MPNST is less frequently associated with NF1 than deep MPNST.

ICD-O coding

9540/3 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

9542/3 Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B5E Malignant nerve sheath tumour of peripheral nerves or autonomic nervous system, primary site

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant schwannoma; neurofibrosarcoma; neurogenic sarcoma; malignant perineurioma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Localization

Most are located in the head and neck region and the extremities {19586501}.

Clinical features

The most common symptoms are localized swelling and pain, but many cases are asymptomatic {25695445}. They can occur at any age, but patients with cutaneous MPNST tend to be older than patients with deep-seated tumours. Patients with NF1 are usually younger at the time of diagnosis than patients with sporadic tumours.

Epidemiology

Cutaneous MPNST is extremely rare, with few reported cases. Only 2% of cutaneous spindle cell malignancies are MPNSTs {2729526}. Although epithelioid MPNST accounts for < 5% of MPNSTs, this subtype more often involves superficial soft tissues.

Etiology

Conventional MPNST is associated with NF1. Epithelioid MPNST is not associated with NF1 but may arise in association with a schwannoma {30864974}.

Pathogenesis

Genomic studies of deep-seated conventional MPNSTs demonstrated highly frequent and concurrent inactivating mutations in three pathways: NF1, CDKN2A/CDKN2B, and PRC2 core components (EED or SUZ12), resulting in complete loss of function {25240281; 25119042; 25305755; 30722027}. In patients with NF1, malignant transformation from pre-existing neurofibromas is often accompanied by progressive genomic alterations that inactivate neurofibromin (NF1), p16/p15 (CDKN2A/CDKN2B), and PRC2 pathways, as well as increased genomic copy-number variations {21987445; 25240281; 30722027}. About 80% of high-grade MPNSTs exhibit complete loss of PRC2 activity and H3 p.K27me3 {26645727; 25240281; 26585554}. The loss of H3 p.K27me3 has become a useful diagnostic tool in high-grade MPNST. Given the critical role of PRC2 in development and cell-lineage specification, PRC2 loss may underlie the molecular mechanisms of the histological phenotype of heterologous differentiation in MPNSTs. Given the rarity of cutaneous MPNST, its molecular pathogenesis remains unclear.

Epithelioid MPNSTs are distinct from conventional MPNSTs molecularly. SMARCB1 gene inactivation resulting in SMARCB1 loss by immunohistochemistry is observed in approximately 75% of cases {30864974}. They rarely have genetic alterations in the neurofibromin (NF1), p16/p15 (CDKN2A/CDKN2B), and PRC2 pathways {25240281; 28368924}.

Macroscopic appearance

MPNSTs are firm, poorly demarcated or well-demarcated tumours that involve the dermis and may extend into the subcutaneous tissue.

Histopathology

The classic form is composed of fascicles of spindle-shaped cells, often with a haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern, and alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas (tapestry appearance) and perivascular accentuation of cellularity. Heterologous elements, as observed in deep-seated tumours, rarely occur.

Epithelioid MPNST is composed of plump, epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, sometimes embedded in a myxoid or hyalinized matrix, and typically demonstrating a lobulated growth pattern {1746681}.

Immunohistochemistry

MPNST may be positive for S100 (< 50% of tumours), SOX10 (< 70%), and GFAP (20–30%) {23929265}. Importantly, expression of S100 and SOX10 is patchy or focal. Diffuse positivity for S100 or SOX10 is not usually compatible with the diagnosis of conventional MPNST. Complete loss of expression of H3 p.K27me3 may be helpful in the diagnosis of MPNST, with high-grade tumours (and radiation-induced MPNST) showing more-frequent loss than low-grade tumours {28776579; 26645727; 26585554; 29482987}.

Epithelioid MPNSTs show strong and diffuse expression of S100 and SOX10 but do not show positivity for melanoma markers. They retain H3 p.K27me3, and most show loss of SMARCB1 expression {30864974}. Epithelioid MPNST may also be positive for keratin.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes spindle cell melanoma, fibrosarcomatous dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, melanoma, and (myoepithelial) carcinoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In diagnostically challenging cases, DNA methylation profiling can identify a specific tumour signature of MPNST {33479225}. Other supporting molecular data include mutations in NF1 and SUZ12 or EED.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #38514Box 7.01 .

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The treatment is complete resection. The tumour may locally recur or metastasize. The 5-year survival rate is approximately 60% {25695445}, which is twice that of deep-seated tumours. There are no histological parameters that can predict behaviour {25602794}.

Essential:

Histopathologically consistent malignant spindle cell tumour in a patient with NF1a OR in a pre-existing neurofibroma

OR

Malignant spindle cell tumour associated with a peripheral nerve AND no more than focal/patchy S100/SOX10 expression AND no SS18::SSX (SSX1, SSX2, or SSX4) fusion gene present

OR

Malignant spindle cell tumour associated with a peripheral nerve AND evidence of PRC2 inactivation (molecularly or via loss of H3 p.K28me3 [K27me3] immunostaining)

OR

Tumour with features of ANNUBP in a patient with NF1, but with a mitotic count of at least 1.5–4.5 mitoses/mm2 (3–9 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.51 mm in diameter and 0.20 mm2 in area)

OR

Unresolved lesion with the methylation profile of MPNST

Desirable:

Loss of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3)

Loss of neurofibromin (NF1) expression

ANNUBP, atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential; NF1, neurofibromatosis type 1.

 

aIn the setting of NF1, a diagnosis other than conventional MPNST demands strong molecular evidence (e.g. detection of a fusion gene pathognomonic of another tumour type).
