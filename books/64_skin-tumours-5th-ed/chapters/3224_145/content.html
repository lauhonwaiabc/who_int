3.2.2.4 Trichilemmal carcinoma

Definition

Trichilemmal carcinoma is a malignant adnexal neoplasm with outer root sheath differentiation. By this definition, trichilemmal carcinoma is the malignant counterpart of trichilemmoma.

ICD-O coding

8102/3 Trichilemmal carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH9K96 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Trichilemmocarcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: tricholemmal carcinoma; trichilemmomal carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Trichilemmal carcinoma involves sun-exposed areas, most commonly the face, scalp, and neck, and less frequently the back of the hands.

Clinical features

Trichilemmal carcinoma manifests as an erythematous, tan, or flesh-coloured papule; a keratotic nodule; or an indurated plaque mimicking squamous or basal cell carcinoma. It is frequently ulcerated. Trichilemmal carcinoma may arise in patients exposed to chronic actinic damage, receiving radiotherapy treatment with long latency periods, or affected by xeroderma pigmentosum. It can also occur in solid-organ transplant recipients. Presentation with multiple tumours and occurrence in Black patients has been reported {17543793; 20881348}.

Epidemiology

Trichilemmal carcinoma has a predilection for elderly individuals; the majority of cases occur between the seventh and ninth decades of life. There is no sex predominance {14692940}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Some cases originate from pre-existing trichilemmoma.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis seems to be related to actinic damage, long-term low-dose X-irradiation, previous trauma or scar, or malignant transformation of trichilemmoma. Genetic disorders (xeroderma pigmentosum), and immunosuppression for solid-organ transplantation may play a role. Loss of the tumour suppressor gene TP53 may contribute to the development of trichilemmal carcinoma {9490283}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Trichilemmal carcinoma is characterized by a proliferation of tumoural lobules (composed of large atypical cells with clear cytoplasm) with a sharply defined border and pushing margins continuous with the epidermis or pilosebaceous structures. The tumour cells give a positive PAS reaction, and there are prominent nucleoli, frequent mitoses, and foci of trichilemmal keratinization. At the periphery of the lobules, the keratinocytes show palisading and are surrounded by a prominent connective tissue sheath. The tumour is more commonly associated with an invasive component centred on a pilosebaceous unit and may show pagetoid spread in the overlying epidermis. The tumour cells are strongly positive for p53 and focally positive for CD34 (My10), which is a marker of differentiation from the outer root sheath. They are usually negative for EMA. Trichilemmal carcinoma also expresses CK1, CK10, CK14, CK7, and CK17, suggesting that it differentiates towards the follicular infundibulum {14692940; 17091763; 8468416; 3278992} [[Rongioletti F, Margaritescu I, Smoller BR, editors. Rare malignant skin tumours. Berlin (Germany): Springer; 2015.]].

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis of trichilemmal carcinoma is clear cell squamous cell carcinoma, although these tumours are considered by some authors to be the same entity {18645304}. Squamous cell carcinoma with clear cells lacks trichilemmal keratinization and lobular proliferation. Trichilemmal carcinoma must be distinguished from other skin cancers with clear cell changes. The identification of ductal differentiation with CEA and EMA staining is useful for differentiating hidradenocarcinoma from trichilemmal carcinoma. Desmoplastic trichilemmoma may rarely exhibit an infiltrative growth pattern but lacks pleomorphism and mitoses. Malignant proliferating trichilemmal tumour, which is often confused with trichilemmal carcinoma, demonstrates extensive areas of necrosis, abrupt keratinization, minimal pleomorphism, low mitotic activity, and foci resembling a trichilemmal cyst. Sebaceous carcinoma is distinguished by the presence of multiple intracytoplasmic lipid-rich vacuoles indenting the nucleus {8468416}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cutaneous nodule on a sun-exposed, hair-bearing anatomical site; a lobular proliferation of atypical, glycogen-rich clear cells lacking mucin and palisading around a follicular unit.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

Trichilemmal carcinoma is a low-grade malignancy with a good prognosis that exceptionally can metastasize {25645796; 15232451; 27872134; 24649199}. Tumour-free margins are needed because of the potential for local invasion and recurrence, and Mohs micrographic surgery can be considered in the head and neck region for tumour eradication. Prognostic factors are limited to lymph node status and the status of surgical margins {27872134}.
