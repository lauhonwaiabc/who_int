2.4.1.1 Acral naevus

Definition

Acral naevus is a benign melanocytic neoplasm of the volar skin of hands or feet or the nail apparatus.

ICD-O coding

8744/0 Acral naevus

8744/0 Nail matrix naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH9DB2 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Acral naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: naevi of special sites; melanocytic acral naevus with intraepidermal ascent of cells.

Not recommended: atypical lentiginous naevus; acral lentiginous naevus.

Subtype(s)

Nail matrix naevus

Localization

Acral naevi occur on the palms, soles, and volar surface of the fingers and toes. They involve non–weight-bearing areas of the sole in adults and the forefoot in children {28676327; 30055202}. Nail matrix naevus (NMN) occurs on the nail matrix, and the thumbnail is the most common site {8632071; 27696528; 29107341}.

Clinical features

Acral naevi are brownish to black small macules or flat papules. Most display one of three major dermoscopic patterns: the parallel furrow pattern, the lattice-like pattern, or the fibrillar pattern {9606325; 16021115; 15492186; 21175752}. NMN manifests as longitudinal melanonychia {8632071}. Dermoscopy of NMN typically shows thick parallel lines without colour variation {27696528}, but NMNs may be darker, larger, or multicoloured, and in children they may display Hutchinson’s sign {27177439; 31850539}.

Epidemiology

Acral naevi are common, occurring in more than a third of individuals, and their incidence peaks in the third and fourth decades of life {26803347; 19389519}. They are more common in females {26803347} and in individuals with higher constitutional pigmentation and many melanocytic naevi {20956637; 26803347}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Acral naevi harbour BRAF or NRAS mutations, similarly to common acquired naevi {29191620; 33978681}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Acral naevi are relatively small and circumscribed, and they can be junctional, compound, or intradermal {32640104}. Acral skin is a “special site” where naevi may show features considered atypical in other locations; pagetoid scatter, bridging between rete, and fibroplasia are not uncommon in the centre of the lesion {28676327}. Naevus cells are arranged as single units and variably sized nests, which can be confluent {7929919; 9688763; 27870726}. There may be random cytological atypia {16446715}. Intradermal melanocytes display maturation. Nuclear atypia, confluence of melanocytes, focal pagetoid spread, and periungual involvement are common in NMN in children {29107341; 32314423}.

Melanocytic acral naevus with intraepidermal ascent of cells is a presentation of acral naevus showing pagetoid scatter of nests or single cells, which is more commonly seen in children {31373032; 28676327}.

Immunohistochemistry

Acral naevi express melanocytic markers, which can help reveal subtle proliferations in NMN.

Differential diagnosis

A confluent lentiginous intraepidermal proliferation of atypical melanocytes and a patchy inflammatory infiltrate is more common in acral melanoma than in acral naevus. Strong cyclin D1 expression supports acral melanoma {34225728}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a brown to black macule or papule of acral skin; proliferation of benign melanocytes on histology.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Acral naevi are benign, and progression to melanoma is very rare. NMN in children initially shows a rapid widening and colour variegation and may undergo spontaneous regression by adolescence {8053713; 8632071; 26806608; 31850539}.
