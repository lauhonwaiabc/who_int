5.5.1.1 Cutaneous leiomyomas

Definition

Cutaneous leiomyomas are benign dermal smooth muscle tumours derived from intrinsic dermal arrector pili smooth muscle (pilar leiomyomas) or dartoic/vulvar/areolar smooth muscle (genital leiomyomas).

ICD-O coding

8890/0 Cutaneous leiomyomas

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y & XH4CY6 Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms & Leiomyoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: piloleiomyoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The extensor surfaces of the extremities are most commonly affected, followed by the trunk and the head and neck region {11907496}. Genital leiomyomas can involve the scrotum, vulva, nipple, or areola.

Clinical features

Cutaneous leiomyomas are flesh-coloured to reddish-brown, firm, dome-shaped papules or nodules. Multiple leiomyomas are more often painful and may be distributed in linear, segmental, or zosteriform patterns {27584971}. Solitary leiomyomas are usually no larger than 20 mm {25605645}. Genital leiomyomas are usually solitary raised to pedunculated papules or nodules, usually < 20 mm but occasionally larger, and asymptomatic. Vulvar tumours may enlarge during pregnancy {25605645}.

Epidemiology

Cutaneous leiomyomas are rare. Cases involving multiple tumours typically have an earlier onset than do solitary leiomyomas {25605645}. Solitary tumours show a female predominance.

Etiology

Single tumours are sporadic and are not uncommon. Multiple pilar leiomyomas are commonly inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, with variable penetrance. Heterozygous germline loss-of-function mutations in FH, located at 1q43, predisposes individuals to hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer syndrome, which manifests as cutaneous and uterine leiomyomas and renal cell carcinoma {18366737; 21398687; 21404119; 28196407}; this syndrome is very rare {25605645}.

Pathogenesis

For sporadic tumours, the pathogenesis is unknown. For familial leiomyomas, see the Urinary and male genital tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Urinary and male genital tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2022. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 8). https://publications.iarc.who.int/610.]].

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Pilar leiomyomas are poorly circumscribed dermal nodules of intersecting, well-differentiated smooth muscle bundles composed of spindle cells with elongated, blunt-ended nuclei, perinuclear vacuoles, and eosinophilic cytoplasm {11907496; 21430885; 25605645}. Rare mitotic figures may be seen {9056647}. Enlarged, hyperchromatic cells similar to those seen in symplastic uterine leiomyomas may be encountered {23550704; 11481520; 26981742}. SMA, desmin, and caldesmon are typically expressed. Demonstration of loss of fumarate hydratase (FH) expression and enhanced expression of 2-succinocysteine (2SC) is useful in identifying possible cases of hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer syndrome {26945337; 28288038; 27097334; 25292446}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing is not required in sporadic cases. In the familial setting, although FH and 2SC immunohistochemistry identify FH-deficient neoplasms, they do not replace genetic testing, because biallelic FH mutation/inactivation can occur as a purely somatic phenomenon.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-differentiated smooth muscle closely resembling normal pilar smooth muscle; occasional mitotic figures and enlarged, hyperchromatic cells may be present; infiltration of subcutaneous fat is absent.

Desirable: expression of SMAs, desmin, and caldesmon (in most cases); absent FH expression and enhanced 2SC expression in hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell carcinoma syndrome–associated tumours.

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Pilar leiomyomas are benign. Multiple leiomyomas may be associated with aggressive type 2 papillary renal cell carcinoma in hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer syndrome, influencing disease management and prognosis.
