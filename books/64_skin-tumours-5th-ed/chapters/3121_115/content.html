3.1.2.1 Adnexal adenocarcinoma NOS

Definition

Adnexal adenocarcinoma NOS is a primary carcinoma of the skin with ductal/glandular differentiation but lacking specific histological features that would allow further classification.

ICD-O coding

8390/3 Adnexal adenocarcinoma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH89V4 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Adnexal adenocarcinoma, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: sweat gland carcinoma; sweat duct carcinoma; eccrine carcinoma; high-grade eccrine syringomatous carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Adnexal adenocarcinoma can involve any site, but it occurs most commonly in the head and neck (particularly the cheek), followed by the trunk {25873411}.

Clinical features

Patients with adnexal adenocarcinoma usually present with a solitary nodule or plaque of variable size and colour, which may be longstanding and slow-growing.

Epidemiology

Most cases occur in the elderly, with no sex predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Patients usually present with a non-ulcerated papule/nodule.

Histopathology

The lesions are asymmetrical, poorly circumscribed, and infiltrative. They often involve the full thickness of the dermis and extend into the subcutaneous and deeper tissues. Tumour cells are arranged in tubules, cords, and nests, with ductal/glandular structures devoid of an obvious myoepithelial cell layer. There may be solid areas. Tumour cells exhibit abundant eosinophilic or granular cytoplasm and pleomorphic nuclei, occasionally with numerous mitotic figures. There may be squamous differentiation. Lumina may contain homogeneous eosinophilic material, foamy histiocytes, and necrotic debris {6096426}.

The ductal structures are highlighted with EMA and CEA. p63 and p40 are at least focally expressed in adnexal adenocarcinoma (generally in > 25% of the cells). CK5/6, CK7, calretinin, and podoplanin (recognized by D2-40) may also be expressed {17518775; 24746214; 17255777; 20190734; 15618924; 14690459}. A number of these lesions express ERBB2 and thus may be amenable to targeted therapy {17214855; 28225558}.

Differential diagnosis

Microcystic adnexal carcinoma has a superficial component with keratin-filled (follicular) cysts, as well as a deeper glandular component that has less atypia than is seen in adnexal adenocarcinoma. Apocrine carcinoma shows glandular and ductal structures, with cytologically malignant cells showing decapitation secretion. Basal cell carcinoma with eccrine differentiation shows obvious basal cell carcinoma (i.e. basaloid cells with peripheral palisading, clefting, and myxoid stroma) and focal ductal differentiation. Squamoid eccrine ductal carcinoma shows malignant squamous epithelium in the superficial regions, occasionally resembling squamous cell carcinoma, while the deeper areas of the lesion display ducts expressing CEA and EMA {26796504}. Secretory carcinoma has the characteristic secretion contents in the lumen.

The primary importance of this entity is its similarity to adenocarcinomas metastatic to the skin {20190734; 18560891}. Distinguishing primary from metastatic cutaneous adenocarcinoma may present a diagnostic conundrum. Adnexal adenocarcinoma NOS may resemble skin metastasis, typically from the breast, lung, gastrointestinal tract, kidney, or ovary {17515511} [[Scolyer RA, Thompson JF, Mahar A, et al. Metastatic tumors involving the skin. In: Busam KJ, editor. Dermatopathology. 2nd ed. London (UK): Elsevier; 2015. pp. 674–88.]]. Internal malignancies tend to metastasize to the skin of the region (e.g. lung carcinomas to the skin of the trunk; gastrointestinal, genitourinary, and gynaecological carcinomas to the skin of the abdomen, in particular the umbilicus). On histology, multifocality or lymphovascular invasion favours metastasis {26602416}. For further details, including an immunohistochemical algorithm, see section Chapter 8: Metastases to the skin.

Cytology

FNAB smears show malignant appearances with pleomorphic epithelial cells in clusters, as well as glandular structures [[Devanand B, Vadiraj P. Fine needle aspiration cytology of eccrine skin adnexal tumors. J Cytol Histol. 2011;2:129. doi:10.4172/2157-7099.1000129]].

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not established

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primary infiltrative dermal epithelial neoplasm with evidence of ductal differentiation, which cannot be designated as a specific type of cutaneous sweat gland carcinoma; exclusion of metastasis from another site.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

Adnexal adenocarcinoma NOS can recur and metastasize {2439558}.
