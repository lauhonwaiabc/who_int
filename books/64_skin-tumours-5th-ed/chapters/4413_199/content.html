4.4.1.3 Primary cutaneous CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder: Lymphomatoid papulosis

Definition

Lymphomatoid papulosis (LyP) belongs to the spectrum of primary cutaneous CD30-positive lymphoproliferative disorders. It is characterized by self-healing but recurrent papulonodular skin lesions, histological features of atypical CD30-positive T cells, and an excellent prognosis.

Related terminology

Subtype(s)

Primary mucosal CD30-positive T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder; lymphomatoid papulosis A, B, C, D, and E; lymphomatoid papulosis with DUSP22-IRF4 rearrangement

Localization

LyP most frequently involves the trunk and extremities, including acral sites {10845893}. The disease may have primary mucosal involvement or, rarely, can concurrently involve mucosal and cutaneous sites {15604547; 22388754}.

Clinical features

LyP manifests with recurrent eruptions, with a waxing and waning course of papulonodular skin lesions that may ulcerate {5634442}. The number of lesions may vary from a few to more than a hundred. Occasionally, larger and longer (up to 20 mm) persistent nodular lesions can develop. Characteristically, skin lesions in different stages of evolution coexist {10845893; 21982062}. Individual skin lesions spontaneously regress within 3–12 weeks, leaving behind scars, hypopigmentation, or hyperpigmentation {5634442}. LyP may persist for several months, years, or decades {14639383; 21982062}.

Epidemiology

The annual incidence is 1.2–1.9 cases per 1 million people, with an M:F ratio of 2:1 to 3:1 {14710887; 10845893; 21982062; 14639383}. LyP most often affects adults (median age at onset: 35–45 years), but children may be affected {11251571; 19577330; 32172217; 15023774; 27138554}.

Etiology

The cause of LyP is unknown. A viral etiology has been suggested, but no association has been established {2877013; 8227605; 8393923; 11168749}. Endogenous retroviral elements were detected, but their etiological role is unclear {12584181; 31883959}.

Pathogenesis

The t(2;5)(p23;q35) translocation resulting in ALK expression is not detected in LyP {8605362}. Rearrangements of the DUSP22-IRF4 locus on chromosome 6p25.3 are found in a small subset of LyP cases {23648461; 21169992}. NPM1::TYK2 gene fusion, STAT mutations, and oncogenic fusion transcripts activating the JAK/STAT pathway have been identified {25349176; 32147503}. Atopy and staphylococcal superantigens have been pathogenetically linked to LyP {32049976}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The histological presentation of LyP is extremely broad and in part depends on the evolution of the biopsied lesion {7104214; 14710887; 15096372; 29719017}. Six subtypes (LyP A–E and LyP with DUSP22-IRF4 rearrangement) with distinct histological and/or genetic features are delineated (see #33472Table 6.14) {7104214; 10845893; 15096372; 20661014; 23026936; 23648461}. They may occur in different but concurrent lesions. A single LyP lesion may show overlapping histological features of different subtypes of LyP {15096372; 14710887; 29719017}. Other rare histological subtypes have been described, including folliculotropic, syringotropic, and granulomatous LyP {23375516; 23348211; 12760293}. Mucosal lesions show a similar spectrum of findings {15604547; 22388754}. Rare cases can show locoregional lymph node extension and have CD15 expression {22367293; 2370346}.

Immunohistochemistry

The atypical lymphoid cells express CD30, often with a Golgi dot-like pattern. They predominantly express CD4 in LyP subtypes A, B, and C; express CD8 in LyP subtypes D and E; and are often double-negative in LyP with DUSP22-IRF4 rearrangement {33987864}. Atypical cells often express TIA1 {9347791; 33987864}. Rare cases with either a CD56, TCRγδ, or T follicular helper (TFH) cell phenotype have been reported {32568843; 11531801; 17052504}. SATB1 is expressed in the vast majority of LyP cases {29510190}.

Differential diagnosis

Although the LyP subtypes do not have any prognostic or therapeutic implications, recognition of these subtypes is important to avoid misdiagnosis of other, more aggressive lymphomas (see #33472Table 6.14). Clinicopathological correlation is essential to differentiate LyP not only from these lymphomas, but also from a wide variety of infectious and inflammatory skin diseases that can contain a substantial number of CD30-positive cells {20043512; 16412214; 18616762}.

The term “borderline lesions” refers to cases in which, despite careful clinicopathological correlation, a definite distinction between primary cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphoma and LyP cannot be made at diagnosis, but evolution of the clinical presentation during follow-up discloses whether such patients have cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphoma or LyP {10845893; 14710887; 30635287; 7751878}.

Cytology

Fine-needle biopsy is not recommended to make this diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Monoclonal rearrangements of TR genes can be detected in 22–80% of LyP cases {8618007; 8288780; 12091351; 17083688}. Identical T-cell clones have been demonstrated in some cases of LyP lesions and associated lymphomas {8618007; 25666664; 1532439}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infiltrates of mostly medium-sized atypical T cells with expression of CD30 and T-cell markers; clinical presentation with waxing and waning papulonodular skin lesions; spontaneous regression within weeks to a few months; exclusion of other lymphomas by clinicopathological correlation.

Desirable: detection of a monoclonal rearrangement of TR genes may be helpful in selected cases (especially in LyP subtype A).

Staging

In patients with the typical clinical presentation and no indication of extracutaneous disease by physical examination or blood tests, there is no need for further staging {21841159; 20026848}.

Prognosis and prediction

LyP has an excellent prognosis, but there is an increased risk (15–50% of patients) of a second lymphoma (particularly mycosis fungoides or cutaneous and/or nodal anaplastic large cell lymphoma, which may precede, be associated with, or follow LyP {26518172; 10845893; 21982062; 10506709; 26668250; 14639383; 1532439; 28614957; 31715046}) and of non-haematological malignancies {31715046; 10506709}. Demonstration of clonal TR gene rearrangement and fascin expression by CD30-positive cells in LyP were suggested as risk factors for the development of LyP-associated lymphomas, pending confirmation in larger studies {21982062; 12100631}. Because of the increased risk of associated lymphomas, long-term follow-up is advised {21841159; 14710887}.
