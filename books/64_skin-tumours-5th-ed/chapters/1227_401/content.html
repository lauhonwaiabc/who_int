1.2.2.7 Squamous cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid differentiation

Definition

This is squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) showing evidence of sarcomatoid differentiation, usually recognizable as chondroblastic, osteoblastic, rhabdomyosarcomatous, or myoid differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8074/3 Squamous cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid differentiation

ICD-11 coding

2C31.Z & XH6D80 Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma & Squamous cell carcinoma, spindle cell

Related terminology

Acceptable: carcinosarcoma; metaplastic carcinoma; sarcomatoid squamous cell carcinoma.

Not recommended: pseudosarcomatous squamous cell carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SCC with sarcomatoid differentiation typically involves sun-exposed skin of the face and head, neck, chest, and upper extremities.

Clinical features

SCC with sarcomatoid differentiation typically shows rapid growth, but it has no individually characteristic features.

Epidemiology

SCC with sarcomatoid differentiation is exceedingly rare.

Etiology

The etiology is similar to that of common forms of SCC.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown, although epithelial–mesenchymal transition is postulated {25619916}. Both elements share a common clonal origin by next-generation sequencing {25031014}.

Macroscopic appearance

See section Squamous cell carcinomas .

Histopathology

SCCs with sarcomatoid differentiation typically have a component of SCC with classic morphology demonstrating keratinization, along with a sarcomatous component {27730657}. The sarcomatous component accounts for a variable proportion of the tumour, and thorough sampling may be necessary to establish the diagnosis. In non-genital sites, a background of severe solar elastosis is typical. Heterologous elements may include chondroblastic, osteoblastic, rhabdomyosarcomatous, and myoid differentiation. Elements with myoid differentiation are rarely positive for markers of epithelial differentiation.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: SCC with recognizable sarcomatoid differentiation, usually chondroblastic, osteoblastic, rhabdomyosarcomatous, or myoid.

Desirable: expression of epithelial markers.

Staging

These tumours should be staged according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

Like for other cutaneous SCCs, surgical excision is usually sufficient {27730657}.
