1.2.1.6 Basosquamous carcinoma

Definition

Basosquamous carcinoma (BSC) is a type of basal cell carcinoma (BCC) with areas of both BCC and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and may contain transition zones between the two.

ICD-O coding

8094/3 Basosquamous carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C32.Y & XH4C18 Other specified basal cell carcinoma of skin & Basosquamous carcinoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: metatypical basal cell carcinoma; basal cell carcinoma with squamous differentiation.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

BSC has a similar distribution to other high-risk BCCs and is most common on sun-exposed skin of older White men, frequently in the head and neck.

Clinical features

Patients present with a slowly evolving papule or nodule, which is frequently ulcerated. Dermoscopy shows combined features of both BCC and SCC, with unfocused arborizing vessels, white structureless areas, keratin masses, ulceration or haemorrhagic crust, white structures, blue-grey blotches, and blood spots on keratin masses. A dermoscopic pattern with criteria for both BCC and SCC raises suspicion of BSC {33116191}.

Epidemiology

BSC is a rare tumour type, estimated to account for 1.2–2.7% of all skin cancers {27340741}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Controversy over the origin of this biphenotypic carcinoma exists, and some consider it to represent a collision between BCC and SCC. More recently, it has been considered to represent a BCC in which a component undergoes genetic transformation in a higher-grade squamous carcinoma {34885255}. Immunohistochemical and histological evidence of a transition zone between a BerEP4-positive/EMA-negative basal cell component and BerEP4-negative/EMA-positive SCC component supports this hypothesis {27340741}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Lesions show histologically and immunophenotypically distinct zones of BCC intermixed with SCC, in varying proportions. There may be a zone of transition between the two growth patterns, or they may be closely intertwined. The BCC areas show the typical basaloid cell growth and are often of infiltrative histology; SCC zones show histological atypia with keratinization. Perineural invasion is seen in 5.5% of lesions {29973992}.

Differential diagnosis

Nodular BCC with squamous differentiation shows focal mature keratinization in the centre of basaloid nests, without high-grade atypia. Squamous change around an ulcer may mimic BSC.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: BCC intermixed with SCC.

Desirable: BerEP4 and EMA immunohistochemical stains are helpful to demonstrate the transition zone, or partial staining of a tumour with both basaloid and squamous morphology.

Staging

These tumours should be staged according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system, depending on site.

Prognosis and prediction

BSC is considered an aggressive carcinoma and is characterized by a higher local recurrence rate than both BCC and SCC and a relatively high metastatic potential. Recurrence after surgical excision has been reported in 12–51% of cases, with a 4% recurrence rate after Mohs micrographic surgery {33116191}. Male sex, positive margins, and lymphatic and perineural invasion predict recurrence {10717618}. The metastatic rate is similar to that of SCC (5–8.4%) and occurs most commonly in regional lymph nodes, followed by lungs {10717618}. The overall prognosis for metastatic disease is intermediate between BCC and SCC; in a study of 19 patients, the median overall survival time was 6.5 years, the median progression-free survival time was 1.1 years, and the 5-year overall survival rate was 54% {24831322}.
