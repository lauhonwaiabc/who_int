5.2.1.4 Superficial fibromatosis

Definition

Superficial fibromatoses (palmar, plantar, and penile) are nodular, locally recurrent fibroblastic/myofibroblastic proliferations.

ICD-O coding

8813/1 Superficial fibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

FB51.Y & XH75J5 Other specified fibroblastic disorder & Palmar/plantar-type fibromatosis

GB06.2 Penile fibromatosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: for palmar fibromatosis: Dupuytren disease/contracture; for plantar fibromatosis: Ledderhose disease; for penile fibromatosis: Peyronie disease.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Palmar fibromatosis affects the volar surface of the hands and the flexor aspects of the fingers. In adults, plantar fibromatosis occurs in non–weight-bearing surfaces of the foot, such as the medial plantar arch, from the navicular bone to the base of the first metatarsal {6507097}. In the paediatric population, plantar fibromatosis most commonly involves the anteromedial heel pad {11123783}. About 50% of cases of palmar fibromatosis and as many as 25% of cases of plantar fibromatosis are bilateral {6507097; 29310463}. Penile fibromatosis involves the tunica albuginea, on the dorsal aspect of the penis {32681945}.

Clinical features

Palmar fibromatosis develops gradually and manifests as small, painless coalescing nodules forming cords or bands in the subcutaneous tissue and underlying fascial plane. Plantar fibromatosis is often asymptomatic and does not cause contractures, but pain may be present after periods of standing or walking. Penile fibromatosis manifests as firm nodules or plaques, causes abnormal curvature of the penis, and may elicit pain during erection and intercourse {6507097; 32681945}.

Epidemiology

Palmar fibromatosis shows a prevalence of 1–2% in the population, affecting mainly adults, with a male predominance (M:F ratio: 3:1). Prevalence increases with age and is higher in populations of northern European descent, reaching 30% in age groups > 60 years {10760640; 19145463}. Plantar fibromatosis is less common and affects younger patients, including the paediatric population, where it shows a female predominance {8989694; 16006806}. Penile fibromatosis affects predominantly White populations of middle-aged adults, with a mean age of 55 years {32681945}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. There is no association with desmoid fibromatosis {16006806; 6507097}. Associations with microtraumas, epilepsy, diabetes mellitus, smoking, and alcohol abuse have been suggested {17950195}. The development of palmar/plantar fibromatosis in relatives of paediatric patients suggests a familial causation {16006806}.

Pathogenesis

Although the pathogenesis of superficial fibromatosis is driven at least in part by the WNT/β-catenin signalling pathway, this tumour lacks the CTNNB1 and APC mutations characteristic of desmoid fibromatosis {11455002; 30774465; 29310463}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are small, poorly defined, 5–30 mm nodules, intimately associated with a tendon or aponeurosis and extending into subcutaneous tissue. The cut surface is firm, greyish-yellow or white (depending on the collagen content), and slightly whorled.

Histopathology

Superficial fibromatoses are characterized by three phases of growth (proliferative, involutional, and late stage). The proliferative stage consists of a cellular, bland proliferation of long fascicles of fibroblasts/myofibroblasts blending into tendons and aponeuroses. Neoplastic cells are elongated cells with tapering vesicular nuclei, small inconspicuous nucleoli, and pale eosinophilic cytoplasm. Mitotic figures may be increased, especially in children. In the background, a collagenous matrix (which increases in the late stages) is seen. Rarely, multinucleated giant cells and osseous or cartilaginous metaplasia are seen.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic cells may be positive for SMA and focally for desmin. Rare cases display nuclear expression of β-catenin {11455002; 17711447; 16006806} despite the absence of CTNNB1 or APC mutations

Differential diagnosis

Hyperplastic scars may resemble fibromatosis but tend to be non-infiltrative and lack β-catenin expression. Sclerotic fibromas have “plywood”-type collagen and usually express CD34. Nodular fasciitis has a more myxoid background resembling tissue culture.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a cellular fascicular proliferation of spindle cells embedded in collagenous stroma; involvement of aponeurosis and tendons and (variably) the subcutis and dermis.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Superficial fibromatoses are benign. There is a high rate of local recurrence, which correlates with the extent of surgical intervention {30774465}.
