5.8.0.16 NTRK-rearranged spindle cell neoplasm

Definition

This is an emerging group of molecularly defined monomorphic spindle cell neoplasms with activating NTRK or other kinase fusions and displaying a wide spectrum of morphological patterns including lipofibromatosis-like growth.

ICD-O coding

9563/0 NTRK-rearranged spindle cell neoplasm

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: lipofibromatosis-like neural tumour; NTRK-positive tumour resembling peripheral nerve sheath tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours occur in the deep dermis and subcutis of the extremities, trunk, and head and neck area {27259011; 30276917; 31219820; 31994201; 32506523}.

Clinical features

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell tumours present as a palpable mass, 10–60 mm in diameter.

Epidemiology

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell tumours are predominantly encountered in the paediatric population {30585824; 31994201} but may arise at any age (range: 0–77 years, median: 19 years), with both sexes affected equally {27259011; 30276917; 32506523}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Activation of MAPK signalling occurs through the formation of chimeric proteins, often LMNA::NTRK1, TPR::NTRK1, or TPM3::NTRK1 {27259011; 30276917}. Other kinase gene fusions include NTRK2, NTRK3, RAF1, BRAF, RET, MET, ROS1, or ALK with various partners {27259011; 31994201; 32506523}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours manifest as a nodular or multinodular subcutaneous mass.

Histopathology

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell tumours have a range of morphological features. Many superficial tumours show a pattern reminiscent of lipofibromatosis, frequently referred to as lipofibromatosis-like neural tumour. In these cases, fibrous septa with increased spindled cells that have small ovoid to tapering nuclei and indistinct cytoplasm extend through adipose tissue. Mitotic activity is inconspicuous. Other tumours may be more cellular, with sheets of spindled to ovoid cells present in fascicles or loosely arranged in a myxoid background. Commonly, however, tumours have a hybrid appearance, with areas of increased cellularity devoid of entrapped adipose tissue intermingling with areas that are adipose-rich. Within this spectrum are tumours with low to high cellularity and a range of mitotic counts. Infrequent tumours may show higher-grade histological features with fascicles of malignant-appearing spindle cells with high mitotic activity. Focal marked nuclear pleomorphism or necrosis may be seen {27259011; 30276917; 30585824; 32506523}. Other histological features include lymphocytic infiltrate, thin-walled staghorn vessels, perivascular hyalinization, and stromal bands of hyalinized collagen {27259011; 30276917; 30585824; 32506523}.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumours show variable coexpression of CD34, S100, and occasionally SMA; however, expression of CD34 or S100 is not required, and tumours may have a nonspecific immunoprofile {27259011; 32506523}. There is usually diffuse pan-TRK expression in tumours with activating NTRK fusions {29683818; 29863809; 30585824}. SOX10 is typically absent and H3 p.K27me3 is retained.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular genetic testing for receptor tyrosine kinase fusions (e.g. NTRK1, NTRK2, NTRK3, RET, MET, EGFR, ROS1, ALK) or downstream effector molecule fusions (ABL1, BRAF, or RAF1) or activating point mutations is required for a conclusive diagnosis and determination of therapy {30276917; 30585824; 32506523}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a spindle cell neoplasm with a wide morphological spectrum and histological grades defined by activating receptor tyrosine kinase fusions (e.g. NTRK fusions or others); the majority of tumours are characterized by haphazardly arranged monomorphic spindle cells, infiltrative growth within fat resembling lipofibromatosis, distinctive stromal and perivascular keloid collagen, and coexpression of CD34 and S100.

Desirable: diffuse pan-TRK reactivity in tumours with NTRK fusions.

Staging

Staging is only applicable to the malignant subset. The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) or Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

NTRK-rearranged spindle cell tumours are highly infiltrative and may recur after incomplete resection. Hybrid tumours have a low rate of metastasis. The prognostic role of traditional histological grading features is still unclear {27259011; 30276917; 30585824; 32506523}.
