2.1.1.6 Meyerson naevus

Definition

Meyerson naevus is a benign naevus with superimposed spongiotic dermatitis.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Meyerson naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH8NP4 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Meyerson naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: eczematous naevus.

Not recommended: halo eczema; halo dermatitis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Meyerson naevus occurs predominantly on the trunk and proximal extremities {22699863}.

Clinical features

Erythema and scaling symmetrically surround and extend beyond the borders of the central naevus, producing an eczematous halo. The lesions may be pruritic. Multiple naevi may be involved {20128988}, and similar patterns have been described for atypical naevi and melanomas {31022058; 5580293; 8655726}.

Epidemiology

Meyerson naevus typically occurs in young adults, with an equal sex distribution {22699863}.

Etiology

The eczematous reaction is probably triggered by factors within the naevus, because excision of the naevus has been reported to lead to regression of the accompanying inflammation {2025942}.

Genetic susceptibility

Meyerson naevus may be associated with atopic dermatitis {19706090}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Meyerson naevus is an acquired, dysplastic, or congenital naevus with superimposed acanthosis, spongiosis, and parakeratosis. Eosinophils may be present.

Differential diagnosis

Halo naevi lack the changes of spongiotic dermatitis and have an infiltrate predominant in CD8-positive lymphocytes, whereas Meyerson naevi show a predominance of CD4-positive T cells {25611257}. Melanoma with associated spongiotic changes can be excluded with close examination of architectural and cytological features.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a junctional or compound naevus associated with acanthosis, spongiosis, parakeratosis, lymphocytic inflammation; absence of diagnostic features of melanoma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Meyerson naevi are benign neoplasms.
