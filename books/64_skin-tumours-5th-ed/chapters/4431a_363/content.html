4.4.3.1A Anaplastic large cell lymphoma

Definition

Systemic anaplastic large cell lymphomas (ALCLs), both ALK-positive and ALK-negative, are a group of clinically and genetically heterogeneous mature T-cell lymphomas characterized by highly pleomorphic tumour cells with uniform strong expression of CD30, which may involve the skin.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Systemic anaplastic large cell lymphoma, ALK-positive; systemic anaplastic large cell lymphoma, ALK-negative

Localization

Systemic ALCLs are typically nodal diseases. Extranodal disease including the skin and/or subcutis is seen in about 20% of cases {18385450}. From limited data, cutaneous involvement by ALK-negative systemic ALCL seems to be very rare {22630575; 21366363}. ALK-positive systemic ALCL may involve skin in about 26% of cases {11090048}.

Clinical features

The macroscopic appearance of skin localization of systemic ALCL is mostly similar to that of primary cutaneous ALCL. Skin lesions are often generalized and may manifest as papules, nodules, or (ulcerating) tumours; they may be present at diagnosis or develop during disease progression {10845893}.

Epidemiology

ALK-positive ALCL is rare, accounting for 10–15% of paediatric and adolescent cases of non-Hodgkin lymphoma and approximately 3% of adult non-Hodgkin lymphomas {27098994}. There is a male predominance (M:F ratio: 3:1).

ALK-negative ALCL represents 5.5–15% of mature T-cell neoplasms {33560375; 34327343}. Most occur in adults, with a median age of 54 years (range: 18–89 years) {33560375; 11564061; 11090048} and an M:F ratio of 1.6:1 {33560375}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

ALK-positive ALCL

The aberrant expression of ALK is a consequence of chromosomal rearrangements that fuse the 3′ portion of ALK on chromosome 2p23 and the 5′ portion of a partner gene that leads to constitutive expression of the chimeric oncoprotein. The major fusion partner in ALK-positive ALCL is NPM1, which is involved in the t(2;5)(p23;q35) translocation. The NPM1::ALK oncogenic tyrosine kinase activates numerous cellular pathways, including the PLCγ {9819383}, PI3K/AKT {11280786; 11110708}, Cdc42/Rac1 {26747246}, JNK/JUN {17416736}, RAS/ERK/MAPK, mTOR {19531656}, JAK/STAT3 {11751994}, and STAT5B {11522649} pathways, to promote cancer. NPM1::ALK controls T-cell identity by epigenetic silencing of many T-cell–associated antigens {19887607}.

ALK-negative ALCL

Activating mutations of JAK1 and STAT3 lead to constitutive activation of the JAK/STAT3 pathway {25873174}. Rearrangement of 6p25.3 (harbouring DUSP22) occur in about 30% of cases {25349176}, and these cases appear to be distinct from other ALK-negative ALCLs in that they lack STAT3 activation, resulting in a DNA hypomethylation state and enhanced immunogenicity {30093402}. Rearrangement of TP63 occurs as an inversion of 3q28 in about 5% of cases, involving the partner gene TBL1XR1 {24894770; 22855598}. Copy-number abnormalities, including loss of 17p13 (TP53) {24004669; 18275429; 15111330}, are seen in a subset.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The tumour cells show an infiltrative, diffuse growth pattern that can be associated with necrosis. Cases with classic morphology demonstrate a variable number of characteristic hallmark cells that show abundant cytoplasm and large horseshoe-shaped nuclei with multiple nucleoli. The large lymphoma cells show abundant basophilic cytoplasm with occasional vacuoles and large pleomorphic nucleoli with prominent nucleoli. However, the morphological spectrum is broad. Some may have a plasmacytoid appearance with a prominent perinuclear hof and cytoplasmic vacuoles.

Systemic ALCLs with cutaneous involvement can show mild epidermotropism and frequently have a component of small cells, like the primary cutaneous small cell pattern of ALCL {19887607}.

Immunophenotype

The neoplastic cells exhibit strong uniform expression of CD30 highlighting the cell membrane and Golgi region, and they are frequently positive for EMA, especially ALK-positive cases. Immunohistochemistry for ALK highlights the neoplastic cells and is reflective of aberrant expression of ALK secondary to ALK rearrangements. There is variable loss of T-cell–associated antigens {8916967}. CD2, CD5, and CD4 are most commonly expressed (40–70% of cases), whereas CD3 is negative in > 75% of the cases. CD43 and CD45RO are commonly observed {8916967; 17535098}. The tumour cells express cytotoxic markers, including TIA1, granzyme B, and perforin, even when they lack T-cell antigens including CD8. They typically express strong uniform CD25, clusterin, and BCL6 {10887098}. CD15 and PAX5 are almost always absent. The tumour cells may also lack CD45 and T-cell receptor molecules such as βF1 or TCRδ; in addition, occasional cases aberrantly express epithelial markers such as cytokeratins, myeloid antigens such as CD13 and CD33, NK cell markers such as CD56, and stem cell transcriptional factors including SOX2 {22885405}. EBV is negative. ALCLs with DUSP22 rearrangement, however, have a different immunoprofile, in that they frequently lack cytotoxic markers {30093402}.

Differential diagnosis

Distinguishing between skin involvement by systemic ALCL and primary cutaneous ALCL is important because of differences in their management and clinical outcome {10845893}. Cases with large, cohesive tumour cells may simulate metastatic carcinoma.

Plasmablastic lymphoma and primary effusion lymphomas (solid) may show cohesive sheets of large pleomorphic cells resembling the tumour cells of ALK-positive ALCL. Other neoplasms that express ALK, such as ALK-positive large B-cell lymphoma, inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours, histiocytic neoplasms, and carcinomas must be excluded.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

ALK-positive ALCLs express the ALK fusion protein secondary to chromosomal translocations of the ALK gene at the 2p23 locus {8122112}.

ALK-negative ALCLs are a heterogeneous group comprising several cytogenetic subtypes with prognostic implications, including those with 6p25 rearrangements at the locus involving DUSP22 {24894770} and the TP63 gene located on chromosome 3q28 {24894770}.

Most cases of ALK-positive ALCL are diagnosed on the basis of aberrant expression of ALK protein by immunohistochemistry. Demonstration of clonal T-cell populations by TR gene rearrangement may confirm the neoplastic process.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: neoplastic T cells with pleomorphic, anaplastic cytology; strong, uniform expression of CD30; presence of extracutaneous disease; ALK immunohistochemistry for status identification (ALK-positive ALCL, ALK-negative ALCL).

Desirable: expression of T-cell markers and cytotoxic markers, albeit with frequent losses; clonal TR gene rearrangement.

Staging

The Cotswold modification {2809679} of the Ann Arbor staging system is the standard anatomical staging system used for patients with systemic ALCL.

Prognosis and prediction

In patients with ALK-positive ALCL patients, long-term survival rates are about 80% {31004022} and significantly better than ALK-negative ALCL {18385450}, except for those that harbour the DUSP22 rearrangement {28522440}. There is an approximate failure rate of 30% regardless of treatment strategy. Small molecule inhibitors that target ALK may modify the prognosis {28787259}.
