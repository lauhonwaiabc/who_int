2.5.1.1 Genital naevus

Definition

Genital naevus is a benign melanocytic naevus located in the genital region.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Genital naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH5EL4 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Genital naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: genital naevocytic naevus; naevus of the genital tract; naevus of special site.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In women, the labium minus is the most affected site, followed by the labium majus, clitoris, and vulvar vestibule; in men, the glans, shaft, and prepuce are most commonly affected {25830986; 9445124}.

Clinical features

Genital naevi are uniformly pigmented, well-circumscribed, solitary macules or papules, up to 10 mm in size, that typically arise in young adults.

Epidemiology

Genital naevi account for < 5% of pigmented lesions in the genital area in women {25830986}.

Etiology

The etiology is similar to that of common naevi.

Pathogenesis

Most genital naevi have BRAF p.V600E mutations {15161700; 24842760; 27220476}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The junctional component consists of single cells and nests haphazardly distributed along the epidermal rete ridges, often oriented parallel to the epidermal surface, with focal retraction artefact and dyscohesion. The centre may have limited pagetoid scatter. The dermal component may show cytological atypia superficially, but shows maturation with increasing depth. Mitotic figures are very rare and only superficial {18162770}, with increased numbers in pregnancy. Lesions may be polypoid, have a dense lymphocytic infiltrate (halo phenomenon), or be associated with lichen sclerosus {11790170}.

Differential diagnosis

Mucosal melanoma is more common in older individuals than genital naevus, has more cytological atypia and mitoses, lacks maturation, shows patchy HMB45 immunoreactivity, and has a higher proliferation rate.

Dysplastic naevi share many features with genital naevi, in particular the architectural disorder (bridging and lamellar fibrosis) {18976416}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a pigmented genital lesion; benign melanocytic proliferation.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These are benign lesions. Melanomas associated with genital naevi are very rare {25239415}.
