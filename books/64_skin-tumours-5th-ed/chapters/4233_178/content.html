4.2.3.3 Rosai-Dorfman disease

Definition

Rosai–Dorfman disease (RDD) is a histiocytosis characterized by the nodal or extranodal (including cutaneous) accumulation of large, S100-positive histiocytes/macrophages that commonly exhibit emperipolesis.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Rosai–Dorfman disease

ICD-11 coding

EK92 Histiocytoses of uncertain malignant potential

Related terminology

Acceptable: Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease.

Not recommended: sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RDD most frequently involves lymph nodes, particularly the cervical region. Extranodal disease occurs in 43% of cases, most often involving the head and neck, skin, and CNS {2180012; 8491488; 17241598; 17325475; 26966089}. Cutaneous RDD most frequently involves the trunk, followed by the head and neck {17241598}.

Clinical features

The presentation is variable; multiple painless reddish-brown papules, nodules, or plaques are typical {25310213; 17325475}, although single nodules are common. Lymphadenopathy and cytopenias are typically absent, although the skin may be involved, in addition to other nodal or extranodal sites {2180012; 29720485}.

Epidemiology

The global incidence of RDD is unknown; the annual incidence in the USA is 100 new cases per year, and the prevalence is 1:200 000 {29720485}. RDD affects people of all ethnicities but is more common in patients of African descent. Men are 3.2 times as frequently affected as women, except in cutaneous RDD, which is more prevalent in Asian women {17241598; 17325475}. Nodal RDD usually occurs in the second and third decades of life, whereas extranodal RDD usually occurs in the fifth decade of life {2180012}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Some rare inherited conditions predispose patients to familial RDD, including germline mutations in SLC29A3 (H syndrome / Faisalabad histiocytosis) or FAS (TNFRSF6) (autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome) {20140240; 24172204; 15958855}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is incompletely understood. Approximately 50% of cases carry gain-of-function mutations in the MAPK/ERK pathway, including KRAS, NRAS, MAP2K1, ARAF, CSF1R, and (rarely) BRAF p.V600E, suggesting that at least a proportion of cases of RDD represent a neoplastic disorder {26566875; 26922062; 28597077; 28664935; 29236635; 32601132}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Cutaneous RDD typically shows sheets of dermal or subcutaneous infiltrates of distinctive large histiocytes that possess round nuclei and prominent nucleoli, surrounded by abundant pale cytoplasm with poorly defined borders. Emperipolesis, the presence of intact haematopoietic cells (typically lymphocytes, but also plasma cells, neutrophils, and erythrocytes) within the histiocyte cytoplasm, is characteristic of RDD. Cytological atypia, mitoses, and multinucleation are infrequent. The microenvironment contains small B and T cells and numerous polytypic plasma cells within medullary cords. Neutrophils and neutrophilic microabscesses may be present, and eosinophils are inconspicuous. Inconspicuous emperipolesis, spindle-shaped histiocytes, admixed foamy histiocytes, and stromal fibrosis may be present, making the diagnosis challenging {2180012}. Perivascular plasma cells are a helpful feature in the diagnosis of extranodal RDD {2180012}.

Immunohistochemistry

RDD histiocytes are immunoreactive for S100, which highlights the emperipolesis. The histiocytes are positive for OCT2 {2180012; 33177341}, frequently positive for phosphorylated ERK and cyclin D1 {28664935; 31172509}, and variably positive for histiocytic markers such as CD68 and CD163. They lack CD1a and CD207 (langerin) {28664935; 26966089; 29720485}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes juvenile xanthogranuloma (which variably expresses S100 and lacks emperipolesis), infectious diseases, IgG4-related disease, and other histiocytoses {26637774; 30573850} (see #36272Table 6.05). RDD can coexist with Langerhans cell histiocytosis or lymphoma {30323237}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

For suspected familial RDD, the workup should include SLC29A3 and FAS (TNFRSF6) germline mutations {24172204; 20140240; 15958855}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large histiocytes with round nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant pale cytoplasm, often with emperipolesis; abundant plasma cells in the background; positive immunostaining for S100, which also highlights the emperipolesis.

Desirable: in diagnostically challenging cases, expression of OCT2 and cyclin D1, in the absence of CD1a, CD207 (langerin), and ALK.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Cutaneous RDD most often resolves spontaneously. In other extranodal or nodal sites, surgical intervention is warranted if the upper airway, spinal cord, or end organs are compromised. Persistent disease or recurrence may require further treatment. In such patients, MEK inhibitors may be helpful (MAP2K1 encodes MEK1) {28664935}. Immune dysregulation and the involvement of select extranodal sites (kidney, tracheobronchial tract / lung, and liver) contribute to an adverse prognosis; disease-specific death is reported in 5–11% of patients {2180012}.
