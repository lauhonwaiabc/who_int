3.1.1.6 Spiradenoma

Definition

Spiradenoma is a (multi)nodular, solid, benign sweat gland neoplasm showing substantial morphological overlap with cylindroma.

ICD-O coding

8403/0 Spiradenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH3AM1 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Spiradenoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: eccrine spiradenoma.

Not recommended: cystic epithelioma of the sweat glands.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Spiradenomas most commonly occur on the face or upper part of the trunk.

Clinical features

Spiradenomas are rare neoplasms that usually appear in adulthood, although congenital cases have been described {2851273}. Most spiradenomas are solitary subcutaneous nodules; however, multiple lesions can occur in both syndromic and sporadic settings, sometimes in a linear distribution. Pain is a common clinical feature associated with spiradenoma {6313776}. The cause of pain is unknown, although various theories have been proposed, including the contraction of myoepithelial cells and an abnormal distribution of sensory nerves inside the neoplasm.

Epidemiology

Spiradenomas are quite rare benign tumours occurring in younger adults. There is no predilection for sex or ethnicity.

Etiology

Multiple spiradenomas, which are inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, are often associated with multiple trichoepitheliomas, cylindromas, and tumours of the parotid gland, an association known as Brooke–Spiegler syndrome {1333921}. The gene responsible for this syndrome, CYLD, is located at 16q12.1 and shows sequence mutations or loss of heterozygosity at 16q typical of this tumour suppressor gene.

Pathogenesis

Germline CYLD mutations are present in syndromic spiradenoma. Mutations in ALPK1 (p.V1092A) and, less often, in CYLD are observed in sporadic spiradenoma, and these are mutually exclusive {31101826}. MYB overexpression has been identified in spiradenoma in the absence of a MYB::NFIB fusion {31101826; 25857824; 28098399}.

Spiradenoma and cylindroma are related to oncogenic activation of the NF-κB pathway, triggered by mutually exclusive mutations of ALPK1 and CYLD {31101826}. A wide range of CYLD sequence variants affect coding and splicing, resulting in inactivation of CYLD {10835629}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Spiradenomas consist of single or several round nodules of basaloid cells with smooth borders. They are centred in the dermis and occasionally extend into subcutaneous tissue. The stroma is oedematous, with numerous dilated blood and lymph vessels. The tumour is often populated by numerous lymphocytes. Spiradenomas are composed of two types of cells: light (or clear) and dark. The clear cells tend to occupy the centre of the neoplastic nodules, and the dark cells form the peripheral layer. Ductal structures are frequently seen inside the solid areas of spiradenoma. Rarely, true adenomatous or atypical adenomatous areas may be seen {18806484}. Basement membrane material can be seen in the form of a thick basement membrane surrounding the islets of tumour cells or in the form of round deposits inside the aggregates, although such deposits are not as abundant as they are in cylindromas. Perineural growth has been reported exceptionally rarely {26260952}. Cystic degeneration can resemble tumour necrosis.

Immunohistochemistry

SOX10 is intensely expressed. Myoepithelial cells express calponin, SMA, and p63. Luminal cells express KIT (43%), EMA, CEA, and cytokeratins.

Differential diagnosis

Rarely, spiradenoma shows prominent cystic change from which immature basaloid cells emanate in a fenestrated pattern, simulating trichoblastoma. Some cases show features overlapping those of cylindroma, emphasizing the close relationship between these tumours. Spiradenomas can focally resemble adenoid cystic carcinoma {19633533}. Spiradenocarcinoma can be distinguished by the lack of dual cell differentiation and mitotic activity in low-grade spiradenocarcinoma, and the presence of a high-grade carcinoma adjacent to a benign-appearing spiradenoma {25857824}. MYB expression is lost in the malignant tumours, which is diagnostically helpful {25857824}.

Cytology

FNAB smears contain dense cell clusters, smaller loose cell aggregates, single cells, and bare nuclei. Most cells have round to oval nuclei, scant cytoplasm, and indistinct cell borders. A second population of cells with spindled nuclei, often dispersed as single cells, is present. Two cases showed the presence of pseudorosettes composed of hyaline globules of basement membrane–like material with a surrounding row of basaloid cells {35672250}. However, to avoid confusion with other basaloid neoplasms, ancillary immunohistochemistry is mandatory.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CYLD mutation analysis may be helpful, particularly if a germline genetic disorder is suspected.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: round nodules of basaloid cells admixed with lymphocytes and basement membrane material.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Spiradenoma is a benign neoplasm.
