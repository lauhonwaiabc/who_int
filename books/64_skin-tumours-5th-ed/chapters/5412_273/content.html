5.4.1.2 Myopericytoma

Definition

Myopericytoma is a benign neoplasm with perivascular myoid cell differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8824/0 Myopericytoma

8824/1 Multicentric myopericytomatosis

8824/1 Intravascular myopericytoma

ICD-11 coding

2E84.Y & XH2HE9 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumour of other specified sites & Myopericytoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Multicentric myopericytomatosis; intravascular myopericytoma

Localization

The distal extremities are the main sites of involvement, followed by the proximal extremities, head and neck (including the oral cavity), and trunk. Most lesions are dermal-based or subcutaneous, while deep lesions are rare, and visceral and intracranial involvement is very rare {16330949; 26068635}. Myointimoma of the glans penis may fall within the spectrum of these lesions.

Clinical features

Most arise as solitary painless dermal or subcutaneous nodules. The tumour may occur over a wide age range, from childhood to old age. Multifocal lesions affecting the same or different anatomical sites are uncommon {16330949}. Rare intravascular lesions may be painful {21355959}.

Epidemiology

No epidemiological data are available.

Etiology

There are no known etiological or predisposing factors. Rare myopericytoma-like lesions originating at unusual visceral or intracranial sites have been documented in association with EBV infection in patients with AIDS {19675451}.

Pathogenesis

Myopericytoma probably differentiates towards myopericytes, intermediate cells between pericytes of Zimmermann and true smooth muscle cells {16330949}. Neointimal (myointimal) cells may contribute to tumorigenesis {21955312}.

A subset of myopericytomas (15%) harbour BRAF mutations, but available data are still limited {25063326; 27776010}. Pericytoma-like neoplasms with recently identified gene fusions such as ACTB::GLI and SRF::RELA are probably unrelated to myopericytoma {15111311; 28248815}. The few cases examined for PDGFRB mutations showed contradictory data {27776010; 28505006}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with well-circumscribed superficial nodules with a firm consistency, usually measuring < 20 mm.

Histopathology

Myopericytoma is a well-circumscribed but unencapsulated nodule of cytologically bland, oval to spindled cells with concentric multilayered perivascular growth. The cytoplasm is eosinophilic, with myoid features. The nuclei are round to ovoid. The cellularity varies greatly, as does the vasculature. The presence of thin-walled communicating vessels may mimic solitary fibrous tumour, while lesions with thicker vessels result in an appearance reminiscent of angioleiomyoma. Cellular solid patterns are similar to myofibroma {16330949}. Rare subtypes include diffuse plexiform growth (myopericytomatosis), intramural growth (within vascular walls), and intravascular myopericytoma. Uncommon features include hyalinization, metaplastic bone, ischaemic necrosis, and degenerative atypia. Myopericytoma usually stains for α-SMA and h-caldesmon, and occasionally for MSA, but desmin immunoreactivity is usually absent or focal {27047931; 16330949}.

Myopericytoma was considered to form a spectrum with myofibroma, angioleiomyoma, and glomus tumour, but recent studies suggest these are rather distinct at the molecular level, albeit having significantly overlapping morphological and phenotypic features {26558691}.

The differential diagnosis includes myofibroma, angioleiomyoma, and solitary fibrous tumour.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a dermal or subcutaneous nodule with concentric perivascular growth of bland myoid cells.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Myopericytoma is benign and non-recurring, even after marginal excision.
