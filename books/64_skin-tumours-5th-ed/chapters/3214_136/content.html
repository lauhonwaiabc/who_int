3.2.1.4 Trichofolliculoma

Definition

Trichofolliculoma is a cystic lesion with panfollicular differentiation, consisting of a central dilated follicular infundibulum from which several smaller secondary hair follicles radiate into the surrounding dermis.

ICD-O coding

8101/0 Trichofolliculoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH0U05 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Trichofolliculoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most lesions occur on the face {28370423; 27608202}. Rarely, cases occur on the genitalia {12068167} and upper limbs {22640019}.

Clinical features

Patients with trichofolliculoma present with a single skin-coloured, dome-shaped papule measuring 5–7 mm. A characteristic but not constant feature is the presence of a central pore with a tuft of thin white vellus hair shafts (Pinkus sign) {27608202}. Multiple trichofolliculomas are rarely seen {22640019}.

Epidemiology

Trichofolliculoma is rare. It shows a slight male predominance and a predilection for middle-aged adults {28370423; 27608202}. Rare congenital cases may occur {27608202}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Signalling pathways involving BMPs and PYGO2 are associated with the experimental development of trichofolliculoma-like proliferations {24982158}.

Macroscopic appearance

Patients typically present with a dome-shaped papule with a central dilated pore.

Histopathology

Trichofolliculoma is a symmetrical, well-circumscribed lesion, with its larger axis perpendicular to the skin surface and a central dilated follicular infundibulum connected to the epidermis. Emanating radially from this cystic infundibulum are numerous vellus hair follicles showing differentiation towards the lower hair follicle segment, in different stages of development. The central cystic infundibulum contains infundibular keratin and occasional hair shafts.

Sebaceous glands may be associated with the hair follicles, and in some lesions they are the dominant component. Surrounding each miniature hair follicle is a well-developed perifollicular connective tissue sheath, and in the vicinity there are well-circumscribed collections of fibrocytes that represent miniature follicular papillae. Morphological variations have been reported according to the age of the lesion, because the vellus hair follicles of the periphery may undergo the phases of the hair follicular cycle {28370423}. Incidental findings described in lesions of trichofolliculoma include epidermolytic hyperkeratosis and focal acantholytic dyskeratosis {15352674}.

Immunohistochemical studies demonstrate that each component of trichofolliculoma expresses characteristic markers of the corresponding segment of the normal hair follicle {28632903}.

Differential diagnosis

Folliculocystic sebaceous hamartoma, with large, voluminous sebaceous glands rather than miniature villous hair follicles at the periphery, is considered by some authors to be a late-stage trichofolliculoma (previously called sebaceous trichofolliculoma) {9765020}, but this suggestion has recently been questioned {28370423}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a central cystic, dilated follicular infundibulum surrounded by immature villous hair follicles in different stages of development.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Trichofolliculoma is a benign lesion with recurrence only in exceptional cases {24021443; 27608202}.
