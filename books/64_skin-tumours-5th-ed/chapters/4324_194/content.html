4.3.2.4 B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma

Definition

B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma (B-ALL/LBL) is a neoplasm of precursor lymphoid cells committed to the B-cell lineage.

ICD-O coding

9811/3 B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

C83.5 Non-Hodgkin lymphoma lymphoblastic (diffuse) (lymphoma presentation)

C91.0 Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (leukaemia presentation)

Related terminology

Acceptable: B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (B-ALL); precursor B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia; B-precursor or B-lymphoblastic leukaemia of defined precursor maturational stage (pro-B, pre-pre-B, pre-B, common, etc.).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

B-lymphoblastic lymphoma (B-LBL) most commonly involves the skin, soft tissue, bone, and lymph nodes {6977384; 11075849; 11392884}. The cutaneous lesions of B-LBL typically arise on the head and neck {9200360; 24314877; 21745698; 18794461}.

Clinical features

Cutaneous involvement of B-LBL typically occurs concurrently with B-ALL and manifests as solitary or multiple rapidly growing, firm, erythematous to violaceous nodules {21745698; 24314877}. Rare cases of primary cutaneous disease have been reported {18794461}. Patients with lymphoma presentation are often aleukaemic {11075849; 11392884; 8912603}.

Epidemiology

B-LBL is a rare malignant neoplasm most often found in children and adolescents {10551409; 9200360; 1810981}; B-LBL accounts for 20% of all cases of lymphoblastic lymphoma in children {21745698}. As many as one third of cases of B-LBL may involve the skin {24589157}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The postulated normal counterpart is a haematopoietic precursor cell committed to the B lineage. Multiple molecular pathways seem to be affected for leukaemogenesis {21135757; 19100366}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Skin biopsy demonstrates a dense, monotonous dermal infiltrate with sheets of neoplastic cells sparing the upper dermis and surround appendages, dissecting collagen fibres of small to medium-sized blastic cells with a high N:C ratio, thin nuclear membranes, finely stippled chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli. The neoplastic cells vary from small blasts with scant cytoplasm, condensed nuclear chromatin, and indistinct nucleoli to medium-sized blastic cells with a high N:C ratio. The tumour cells have moderate amounts of bluish-grey cytoplasm, occasional vacuoles, thin nuclear membranes, finely stippled chromatin, and multiple variably prominent round or convoluted nucleoli. Mitoses and apoptotic debris are often present. Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are rare {21745698; 18794461}.

Immunophenotype

CD19, CD79a, CD22, TdT, CD34, CD10, CD24, and PAX5 are commonly expressed by the tumour cells. CD20 is typically negative {8338949; 21745698; 18794461}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes T-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma, acute myeloid leukaemia, chronic lymphocytic leukaemia or chronic myeloid leukaemia in blast phase, B-prolymphocytic leukaemia, mantle cell lymphoma, plasma cell leukaemia, and small blue round cell tumours (e.g. Ewing sarcoma / primitive neuroectodermal tumour). Non-malignant entities, including HIV and infectious mononucleosis, may also be considered.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic abnormalities are now the fundamental basis for subclassification of B-ALL/LBL. For details, see the Haematolymphoid tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential for B-ALL: > 20% B lymphoblasts by flow cytometry with B-cell lineage markers.

Essential for B-LBL: diffuse infiltration of the skin by a monomorphic population of blasts with a B-cell immunophenotype (CD19, CD22, cCD79a, and/or PAX5) and markers of immaturity (TdT, CD34, and/or CD10); surface immunoglobulin negative; in rare cases, CD34 and TdT expression may be absent, posing a diagnostic challenge.

Desirable: immunophenotypic profiles associated with specific recurrent genetic abnormalities; identification of specific recurrent genetic abnormalities.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Although highly aggressive, B-LBL may be curable if diagnosed early, particularly in children {21745698}.
