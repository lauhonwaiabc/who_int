5.8.0.13 Angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma

Definition

Angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma is a rare neoplasm of intermediate malignant potential and uncertain differentiation, mostly occurring in the subcutis and histopathologically characterized by nodules of ovoid and spindle cells, haemorrhagic pseudocystic spaces, and a peripheral rim of lymphoplasmacytic cells.

ICD-O coding

8836/1 Angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH9362 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiomatoid malignant fibrous histiocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours most frequently occur as dermal or subcutaneous lesions in the extremities, followed by the trunk and the head and neck. Nearly two thirds of cases occur in sites where lymph nodes are normally found {10571514}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a slow-growing, superficial, painless soft tissue mass simulating a haematoma or a haemangioma {2174650; 228836}.

Epidemiology

It accounts for 0.3% of all soft tissue tumours, with no sex predilection. A wide age range is reported, with a peak incidence in the first two decades of life {2174650; 2845834; 17724745; 27166030}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The most frequent genetic abnormality is the t(2;22)(q33;q12) translocation, resulting in a EWSR1::CREB1 fusion, followed by t(12;22)(q12;q12) translocation, leading to a EWSR1::ATF1 fusion {17724745; 18094413; 27166030; 15884099}. Rare tumours harbour a FUS::ATF1 fusion {12218455; 11063792}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, these tumours appear as multinodular and cystic masses, with a size ranging from 7 to 120 mm (median size: 20 mm) {228836; 2174650}. The cut surface shows multilocular haemorrhagic to yellowish-white areas {228836; 2174650; 25927151}.

Histopathology

Tumours are circumscribed and lobulated. They contain varying proportions of fascicles or sheets of oval and spindle-shaped cells containing bland vesicular nuclei, and pseudoangiomatous blood-filled spaces. They also have a thick fibrous pseudocapsule with haemosiderin deposition, and a pericapsular cuff of lymphoplasmacytic cells with germinal centres, simulating a metastatic lymph node. Nearly one third of cases have a completely solid appearance. Mitotic figures are few, although atypical mitotic figures may be noted. A variable number of pleomorphic cells may be present {25927151; 27166030; 24043718}. Cellular atypia and increased mitotic figures are not associated with an aggressive clinical outcome {2174650}. One pattern of angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma is characterized by small blue round cells, resembling an undifferentiated round cell sarcoma {10571514; 11012987}. A myxoid pattern has also been described {24503754; 21822206}.

Immunohistochemistry

Most tumours express SOX9 {31567275}. Nearly 50% of cases are immunoreactive for desmin. These tumours are consistently negative for MYOD1 and myogenin. EMA, CD99, and CD68 are variably immunopositive {10571514}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is invariably low {21822206}.

Cytology

Smears are cellular and composed of loosely arranged ovoid to spindled cells displaying moderate nuclear pleomorphism. Interspersed are blood products and inflammatory cells {22045622; 16007669}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of the presence of EWSR1::CREB1 or alternative gene fusions may be helpful for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinically, a small subcutaneous painless nodule; histopathologically, solid nodules of ovoid and spindled cells, pseudoangiomatoid spaces, and a peripheral rim of lymphoplasmacytic cells.

Desirable: SOX9 and desmin positivity by immunohistochemistry; demonstration of EWSR1 gene rearrangement or the EWSR1::CREB-family fusions (in selected cases).

Staging

None

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases follow an indolent course, with recurrences in nearly 15% and metastasis in up to 5% of cases, predominantly to locoregional lymph nodes and rarely to the lungs, liver, and brain {2174650; 9638550; 10571514}. Rare deaths have been reported from metastasis, although long-term survival after resection is reported in metastatic cases {228836; 2174650}. Cases with incomplete surgical clearance and those occurring in deeper locations are associated with higher rates of recurrence and metastasis {2174650}.
