2.1.1.5 Halo naevus

Definition

Halo naevus is a naevus with circumferential depigmentation clinically, often associated with a brisk lymphocytic infiltrate histologically.

ICD-O coding

8723/0 Halo naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH5971 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Halo naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: Sutton naevus; regressing naevus.

Not recommended: leukoderma acquisitum centrifugum; perinaevoid vitiligo; perinaevoid leukoderma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Halo naevi are most common on the trunk, less common on the head, and rare on the limbs.

Clinical features

Halo naevi often arise during the summer, perhaps because the halo contrasts better with tanned skin. They are most common in teenagers and young adults; can be associated with dysplastic naevi; and arise in multiplicity, beginning with the appearance of the halo around a compound naevus, followed by fading and disappearance of the naevus. The most common overall dermoscopic patterns are the uniform globular pattern and the structureless pattern {17178990}. Halo naevi can be seen in patients with melanoma, sometimes in response to treatment, or in patients with vitiligo {22508876; 32822787; 29036261}.

Epidemiology

In the general population, the incidence of halo naevi is estimated to be 1% {9692308}. The average age at presentation is 15 years. Patients with Turner syndrome have an increased incidence of halo naevi {15337976}. There is no ethnic or sex predilection.

Etiology

Halo naevi represent an immune-mediated rejection of a common naevus {34466016}.

Pathogenesis

Antigen-presenting cells and CD4-positive and CD8-positive T cells have been identified in the inflammatory infiltrates of halo naevi, implicating immunological mechanisms in the destruction of naevus cells {9344203}. Affected individuals also show activated lymphocytes in their peripheral blood {10495377}, as well as clonal expansion of T cells {9973443} and IgM antibodies {8053701}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Halo naevi are naevi with a superimposed dense lymphocytic infiltrate that partially or completely obscures the naevus cells. Melanin-laden histiocytes and mast cells may be present {7499574}. Occasional halo naevi contain a few giant cells, or there may be a frankly granulomatous response {18496423}. The clinically depigmented areas may show complete absence of junctional melanocytes histologically, and there may be a few scattered lymphocytes at the dermoepidermal interface. Hyperchromatism and mild to moderate pleomorphism are present in about 50% of halo naevi {7499574} and are considered reactive. Mitotic figures are not usually seen {1234372}.

Immunohistochemistry

SOX10 or melan-A can be useful to delineate the naevus cells {30601204}. PRAME is usually negative {33184871}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of involuting halo naevi includes other lichenoid interface reactions, such as those seen in lichenoid keratosis and melanoma with lichenoid inflammation. Findings suggestive of melanoma include the presence of a separate population of cells with an expansile pattern of growth; severe cytological atypia; and frequent mitoses, ulceration, or tumour necrosis. The halo phenomenon may occasionally involve other types of naevi, including dysplastic {10082398}, Spitz {9449488}, and congenital {8053701} naevi, as well as melanomas {17381800}. In most lesions, there is no intraepidermal melanocytic proliferation adjacent to the dermal component. If in situ or superficially invasive melanoma is present adjacent to a dermal lesion simulating halo naevus, the entire lesion is most likely melanoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a naevus with a dense lymphocytic inflammatory cell infiltrate; exclusion of a melanoma with brisk inflammation, lichenoid tissue reaction, or regression.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Halo naevi are benign lesions. Adult-onset halo naevi are associated with a 1% risk of primary cutaneous melanoma development in the year after diagnosis, a risk comparable to that of individuals with a history of atypical naevi or a personal or family history of melanoma {32822787}.
