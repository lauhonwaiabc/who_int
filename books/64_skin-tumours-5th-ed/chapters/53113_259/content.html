5.3.1.13 Verrucous venous malformation

Definition

Verrucous venous malformation is a distinctive localized vascular malformation associated with overlying prominent epidermal changes.

ICD-O coding

9142/0 Verrucous venous malformation

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y & XH23S6 Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms & Verrucous keratotic haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: verrucous haemangioma (obsolete); verrucous venulocapillary malformation; verrucous keratotic haemangioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These lesions have a predilection for the distal extremities; occasionally they occur on the trunk.

Clinical features

The lesions are slightly raised singular, grouped, or confluent red to purple plaques that become more hyperkeratotic and may ulcerate, bleed, and slowly enlarge over time. Linear lesions have been reported {10688730}. Local tissue hypertrophy is not a feature.

Epidemiology

This is a rare congenital lesion with no sex predilection.

Etiology

Not known

Pathogenesis

Verrucous venous malformation is non-familial. A somatic missense mutation in MAP3K3 has been identified in some cases {25728774}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

An epidermis with prominent acanthosis, papillomatosis, hyperkeratosis, parakeratosis, and crusting overlies an expanded papillary dermis with numerous dilated and congested capillaries and venules, often extending into the investing dermis of the skin adnexa while sparing the reticular dermis. The subcutaneous tissue displays an increased density of variably congested capillaries and venules, with occasionally admixed larger veins. Mitotic activity is rare to absent. There may be focal light endothelial positivity for GLUT1, but the full placenta-like vascular phenotype of infantile haemangioma is lacking. Small dilated lymphatic vessels also may be present but the predominant differentiation is clearly blood vascular {10886755; 6038751; 25263605}.

Differential diagnosis

The superficial dermal changes closely resemble angiokeratoma. Histological distinction relies on the identification of a deep dermal / subcutaneous component.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: plaques with verrucous epidermal hyperplasia overlying dilated vascular channels in the dermis, with an associated subcutaneous component.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Verrucous venous malformation shows persistence and slow expansion following incomplete excision.
