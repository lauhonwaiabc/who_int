3.1.2.9 Digital papillary adenocarcinoma

Definition

Digital papillary adenocarcinoma is a malignant adnexal tumour with a marked predilection for acral sites and a solid and/or papillary architecture.

ICD-O coding

8408/3 Digital papillary adenocarcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C33 & XH6FB5 Adnexal carcinoma of skin & Digital papillary adenocarcinoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: aggressive digital papillary adenocarcinoma; digital papillary carcinoma.

Not recommended: aggressive digital papillary adenoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The fingers and toes, and the adjacent skin of the palms and soles, are affected. The hands are more commonly involved (accounting for > 80% of cases) than the feet. Rare tumours have been reported on non-acral sites, particularly the scrotum {35538210}.

Clinical features

The neoplasm presents as a slow-growing, asymptomatic, deep-seated nodule in most cases, measuring an average of 17 mm (range: 4–43 mm). The reported duration of symptoms before excision is between 2 months and 15 years. The tumour can sometimes be clinically confused with a ganglion or a cyst. Ulceration is rare. Pain may occur if the neoplasm involves the underlying bone, joint, or nerves. Rarely, patients present with metastatic disease.

Epidemiology

A population-based study revealed an incidence of 0.08 cases per 1 million person-years for digital papillary adenocarcinoma {29578881}. The patient age at presentation is 14–83 years (average: 52 years), with a clear male preponderance {10843279; 23026931}.

Etiology

Oncogenic HPV42 has been detected in digital papillary adenocarcinoma {35331965; 35538210; 36213965}.

Pathogenesis

A study using transcriptome analyses suggested an involvement of crucial oncogenic driver pathways, highlighting an activation of cell-cycle and ribosomal pathways in the tumour. Significant overexpression of FGFR2 mRNA was found {30472730}. In another study, however, no significant expression of FGFR2 was found immunohistochemically {33210330}. In rare cases, a BRAF p.V600E mutation has been found {36756976}; other oncogenic mutations (i.e. of EGFR, PIK3CA, KRAS, NRAS, HRAS, and MET) have not been identified {26386519; 32568833}.

Macroscopic appearance

This is a lobular or poorly circumscribed solid-cystic unencapsulated neoplasm.

Histopathology

Historically, aggressive digital papillary adenomas and digital adenocarcinomas were both recognized, but cases originally classified as aggressive digital papillary adenoma later developed metastases {3301927}; therefore, the term “adenoma” has now been abandoned in this setting, and all such lesions are classified as digital papillary adenocarcinoma.

Digital papillary adenocarcinoma is usually a multinodular, poorly circumscribed or well-circumscribed lesion located within the dermis and often the subcutaneous tissue, without a connection to the epidermis. A mixture of solid, cystic, and papillary growth patterns is usually seen. In the areas with papillary structures, both true papillae with a supportive fibrous core and pseudopapillae lacking a core protruding into or lying free within the cystic spaces may be seen. A typical feature is the presence of fused back-to-back glands lined by cuboidal to low columnar epithelial cells, occasionally showing apocrine secretion. At the periphery of the glands, basal/myoepithelial cells can sometimes be recognized. Cytological atypia is mild to moderate in most cases; high-grade lesions are less common. The mitotic rate is extremely variable {10843279; 23026931}. In a subset of cases, more-solid areas dominate, and as many as 20% of these neoplasms are essentially solid, with areas of necrosis. Squamous metaplasia and focal clear cell change may be present. The stroma is paucicellular, hyalinized, and (rarely) myxomatous. Involvement of lymphatic vessels is uncommon {26386519; 23026931}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells are diffusely positive for pancytokeratin and CK7. Staining for EMA and CEA highlights the glands and luminal borders of the papillae. Immunostaining for S100 is variable in both luminal cells and myoepithelial cells. The myoepithelial cells stain for SMA, calponin, p63, and (focally) podoplanin (recognized by D2-40) {18214401; 23026931}. Demonstration of the HPV42 virus by in situ hybridization provides further diagnostic certainty in diagnostically challenging examples {35538210}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Mutation detection and HPV detection may be relevant in cases with metastasis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a tumour consisting of true papillae with a supportive fibrous core, fused back-to-back glands, solid lobules, and nests, with at least a focally recognizable peripheral layer of myoepithelial cells.

Desirable: HPV42 positivity; immunohistochemistry showing a myoepithelial component.

Staging

Staging follows the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification.

Prognosis and prediction

The reported rates of local recurrence and metastasis are 5–21% and 26–50%, respectively {10843279; 23026931}. Complete excision, or a partial amputation of the affected digit, considerably decreases the risk of recurrence and metastasis {23026931}. The most common site of metastasis is the lung, followed by the lymph nodes. Metastases have been reported to develop as late as 20 years after excision of the primary neoplasm {23026931}. In one series, half of the patients with metastatic disease died {10843279}. Histopathological features appear not to be predictive of metastatic potential {23026931}.
