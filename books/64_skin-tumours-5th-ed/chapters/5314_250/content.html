5.3.1.4 Hobnail haemangioma

Definition

Hobnail haemangioma is a benign vascular malformation of lymphatic lineage, with hobnail endothelial cells and a delayed presentation.

ICD-O coding

9120/0 Hobnail haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

LA90.1Z & XH8PD3 Lymphatic malformations, unspecified & Hobnail haemangioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: targetoid haemosiderotic haemangioma; superficial haemosiderotic lymphovascular malformation; targetoid haemosiderotic lymphatic malformation.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This lesion affects the trunk and extremities.

Clinical features

Most lesions arise as a small, nondescript erythematous papule. Uncommonly, the lesion is a small reddish-brown or purple papule surrounded by concentric ecchymotic haloes, hence the original name “targetoid haemosiderotic haemangioma”.

Epidemiology

This relatively rare haemangioma occurs mainly in young and middle-aged adults, with a male predilection.

Etiology

The underlying etiology of hobnail haemangioma is unclear, but it is currently considered a malformation rather than a neoplasm.

Pathogenesis

Expression of lymphatic markers and negativity for WT1 and mitotic markers supports the conclusion that these are superficial lymphatic malformations that undergo delayed perilesional bleeding and haemosiderin deposition {21798621; 21821311}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The lesions are wedge-shaped and consist of scattered, thin-walled, irregular vascular channels lined by bland endothelial cells with a hobnail appearance and focal intraluminal papillary projections. Vessels deeper in the lesion become smaller, flattened, and inconspicuous, without hobnail endothelial cells. Red blood cell extravasation, haemosiderin deposition, and perivascular lymphocytes are common. The endothelial cells are strongly positive for podoplanin (recognized by D2-40) and usually negative or weakly positive for CD34, suggesting a lymphatic lineage {15059220; 9888709; 10472756; 24601986; 26475078; 21821311}.

Differential diagnosis

Atypical vascular lesions occurring after radiotherapy may be identical, but they usually lack the wedge-shaped architecture. Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma (Dabska tumour) and retiform haemangioendothelioma have hobnail endothelial cells but have a distinctive pattern and are larger and more infiltrative.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

None

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a superficial dermal, wedge-shaped collection of irregular, thin-walled lymphatic vascular channels lined by hobnail endothelial cells.

Desirable: perilesional deposits of haemosiderin.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Hobnail haemangioma is benign, with no tendency for recurrence.
