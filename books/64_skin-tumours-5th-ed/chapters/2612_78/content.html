2.6.1.2 Congenital dermal melanocytosis

Definition

Congenital dermal melanocytosis (CDM) is an increased number of melanocytes in the dermis, usually on the lower back and buttocks.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LC10 Dermal melanocytosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: Mongolian spot.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common site is the sacral area (affected in 78.9% of cases) {23834326}.

Clinical features

CDM manifests as a bluish or darker patch {23834326}. It can be associated with inborn errors of metabolism, including GM1 gangliosidoses, mucopolysaccharidosis type I (Hurler syndrome) and type II (Hunter syndrome), mucolipidosis, Niemann–Pick disease, mannosidosis {24340274}, cleft lip/palate, supernumerary digits, benign leptomeningeal melanocytoma, lysosomal storage disease, and Sjögren–Larsson syndrome {8157782; 12873889; 17300667}. Phakomatosis pigmentovascularis is the association of extensive, aberrant, persistent CDM with vascular anomalies, such as naevus flammeus, Sturge–Weber syndrome, or Klippel–Trénaunay syndrome {17937434; 18045734}.

Epidemiology

CDMs are most common in Asian and African populations {572849; 7028357; 17172067; 23834326; 24340274; 31141213}. There is no sex predilection {6997352; 20653863}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unclear. It has been proposed that dermal melanosis arises from an arrest of migration of melanocytes, derived from neural crest cells, which become trapped in the dermis instead of reaching the epidermis {22197860}. GNAQ and GNA11 mutations have been found in cases associated with phakomatosis pigmentovascularis {26778290}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

CDMs are composed of single pigmented dendritic melanocytes sparsely dispersed in the dermis, often parallel to the epidermis {13130904}.

Immunohistochemistry

The melanocytes express melanocytic markers such as SOX10, melan-A, or HMB45.

Differential diagnosis

Naevus of Ito and naevus of Ota have similar histology but different anatomical localization. Blue naevi tend to be more cellular and have bundles of dendritic cells {22197860}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bluish patches on lumbosacral skin and other parts of the body; presence of dendritic melanocytes in the dermis, without atypical features.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

CDMs typically disappear by the age of 5–6 years. Involvement of other sites, multiple patches, size > 100 mm, and dark colour are associated with persistent CDMs {23834326}.
