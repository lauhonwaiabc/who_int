1.2.1.8 Basal cell carcinoma with adnexal differentiation

Definition

Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) with adnexal differentiation is a type of BCC with follicular, sebaceous, eccrine, or apocrine differentiation {26264868}.

ICD-O coding

8090/3 Basal cell carcinoma with adnexal differentiation

ICD-11 coding

2C32.Y & XH6S67 Other specified basal cell carcinoma of skin & Basal cell carcinoma with adnexal differentiation

Related terminology

Not recommended: basal cell carcinoma with divergent differentiation.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Infundibulocystic BCC and BCC with ductal differentiation have a predilection for periocular skin {20489535; 15564201}.

Clinical features

The clinical features are similar to those of other members of the BCC family (see section Basal cell carcinomas).

Epidemiology

See the family description (section Basal cell carcinomas).

Etiology

Multiple infundibulocystic BCCs are a feature of multiple hereditary infundibulocystic BCC syndrome and naevoid BCC syndrome {15583598; 26677003; 33063358}. BCC with sebaceous differentiation may rarely be seen in Muir–Torre syndrome {16446711}.

Pathogenesis

A small series of sporadic BCCs with matrical differentiation showed molecular abnormalities in CTNNB1, KIT, CDKN2A, TP53, SMAD4, ERBB4, and PTCH1, providing supporting evidence that these tumours may be a type of BCC {28368926}.

Macroscopic appearance

There are no distinctive macroscopic features.

Histopathology

BCC with adnexal differentiation can show different patterns, including ductal differentiation, matrical differentiation, and sebaceous differentiation, or an infundibulocystic pattern. BCC with matrical differentiation contains shadow cells, trichohyalin granules, and grey-blue keratin indicative of hair matrix / inner root sheath differentiation {1503202; 15701088}. Infundibulocystic BCC shows differentiation towards the follicular infundibulum, with anastomosing cords and nests of basaloid cells, punctuated by small infundibular cyst-like structures {8517497; 2981015; 2235986}. BCC with sebaceous differentiation shows mature sebocytes that stain for EMA or adipophilin {15249860}. BCC with ductal differentiation contains CEA-positive and EMA-positive ducts, with or without a cuticle, resembling those found in apocrine or eccrine glands (normal apocrine and eccrine ducts are indistinguishable). BCC with apocrine glandular differentiation shows decapitation secretion {16446711; 8440908; 15564201; 4031160}.

Differential diagnosis

Matrical, sebaceous, or apocrine differentiation in BCC is generally a focal finding, and a predominance of areas of conventional BCC is a clue to the diagnosis. In a limited specimen, distinguishing BCC with adnexal differentiation from true adnexal tumours can be exceedingly difficult. BCC with conspicuous matrical differentiation must be distinguished from pilomatrical carcinoma. The differential diagnosis of infundibulocystic BCC includes basaloid follicular hamartoma and trichoepithelioma. BCC with sebaceous differentiation should be differentiated from sebaceous carcinoma and sebaceoma {16446711}. BCC with ductal differentiation must be distinguished from other sweat gland carcinomas and entrapped native apocrine and eccrine ducts.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Inherited infundibulocystic BCCs are associated with mutations in SUFU, whereas sporadic tumours were additionally found to harbour alterations beyond SUFU in the hedgehog pathway, including SMO, GLI1, and GLI2 {32796174}. PTCH1 is implicated in naevoid BCC syndrome.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a papular or nodular skin tumour, with or without ulceration; typical histology of islands of basaloid cells with peripheral nuclear palisading, with additional adnexal differentiation, i.e. shadow cells for matrical differentiation, mature sebocytes for BCC with sebaceous differentiation, ducts with cuticle for BCC with ductal differentiation, or glands with apocrine secretion for BCC with apocrine glandular differentiation.

Desirable: immunohistochemistry for CEA and EMA may outline apocrine ducts; β-catenin may be used to highlight matrical differentiation; adipophilin may highlight sebaceous differentiation.

Staging

These tumours should be staged according to Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) guidance, depending on site.

Prognosis and prediction

The presence of adnexal differentiation confers no prognostic significance.
