1.1.3.4 Clear cell acanthoma

Definition

Clear cell acanthoma is a sharply demarcated intraepidermal keratinocytic proliferation with pale- to clear-staining cytoplasm.

ICD-O coding

8084/0 Clear cell acanthoma

ICD-11 coding

2F21.Y & XH13L5 Other specified benign keratinocytic acanthomas & Clear cell acanthoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: pale cell acanthoma; Degos acanthoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is a predilection for the lower limbs {24087787}.

Clinical features

Patients present with sharply delineated, solitary, erythematous or brown papules and nodules, measuring from 5 mm to a few centimetres and occasionally showing a polypoid growth {24087787}. Dermoscopy reveals a linear or serpiginous vascular pattern with a string-of-pearls appearance against a homogeneous pale or pink background {29267474}.

Epidemiology

Middle-aged to elderly adults are most commonly affected, without a marked sex bias {24087787}.

Etiology

It is unclear whether clear cell acanthoma represents a benign neoplasm or an inflammatory/reactive dermatosis. The solitary nature, persistence, and lack of regression point to a neoplastic origin, but immunohistochemistry has demonstrated similarities in keratin expression with psoriasis {17667172; 7547383; 32989899}.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Clear cell acanthomas are well circumscribed, with psoriasiform epidermal hyperplasia and elongated and fused rete ridges with thinning of the papillary plates and sparing of the adnexal structures. They are composed of keratinocytes with pale- to clear-staining cytoplasm, and there is an abrupt radial transition to adjacent normal keratinocytes. The pale keratinocytes contain PAS-positive glycogen. There is overlying parakeratosis and there are admixed intraepidermal and intracorneal neutrophils. The superficial dermis contains a mixed inflammatory infiltrate and prominent vasculature.

Squamous cell carcinoma in situ can be separated by the presence of squamous dysplasia. Psoriasis is excluded clinically.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sharply demarcated intraepidermal keratinocytic proliferation with clear cell change, lacking squamous dysplasia.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Clear cell acanthomas are benign.
