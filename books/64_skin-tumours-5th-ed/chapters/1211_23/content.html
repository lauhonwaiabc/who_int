1.2.1.1 Nodular basal cell carcinoma

Definition

Nodular basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is the most common BCC type and is defined by well-circumscribed large dermal tumour islands with peripheral palisading.

ICD-O coding

8097/3 Nodular basal cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C32.0 Nodular basal cell carcinoma of skin

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Nodular BCC is most common in the head and neck region.

Clinical features

Patients with nodular BCC present with a pink papule or nodule with frequent ulceration and occasionally pigmentation. On dermoscopy, nodular BCC shows arborizing vessels and, if pigmented, blue globules {20708470}. Cystic BCC may be translucent and resemble hidrocystoma.

Histopathology

Well-circumscribed, variably sized nodules of basaloid cells with peripheral palisading and retraction spaces are seen; disorganized nuclear arrangement with apoptosis is frequently observed in the centre of the nodules. The tumour is usually connected with the epidermis. The stroma is paucicellular; amyloid deposition or keloidal-type collagenous stroma may be seen, but not desmoplasia. Functional melanocytes are found in many BCCs and may be associated with melanin pigment in tumour nests and in stromal melanophages {27612950}.

Patterns of nodular BCC include pigmented, keratotic, cystic, and nodulocystic. Keratinizing BCC tumour nests show central mature keratinization. Nodulocystic or cystic BCC shows cystic degeneration within tumour islands. Cribriform tumour nests are seen in adenoid BCC. Less common patterns include rippled, labyrinthine, or trabecular arrays. Clear, signet-ring, granular, and giant (monster) cell types have been described {8227606; 226002; 1714249; 8468420}.

Differential diagnosis

Cases with small nodules should be differentiated from aggressive micronodular BCC. Distinction from benign and malignant trichoblastic tumours, especially trichoblastoma, can be difficult in some cases. The presence of papillary mesenchymal bodies and eosinophilic fibroblastic stroma encasing tumour nodules but retracting from the surrounding dermis favours the latter. Lesions with cystic change may simulate other adnexal tumours, particularly adenoid cystic carcinoma. BerEP4 expression is found in BCC and Merkel cell carcinoma, which may cause diagnostic difficulties. Conversely, BCC can express markers of neuroendocrine differentiation, such as chromogranin and synaptophysin in as many as 27% of cases {23292839}, but it lacks the dot-like perinuclear positivity for CK20 typical of Merkel cell carcinoma.

Ulcerated BCC may show superficial mature-appearing squamoid change immediately beneath the ulcer, raising the possibility of squamous cell carcinoma. Basosquamous carcinoma shows malignant-appearing squamous elements admixed with typical basaloid carcinoma elements {32166375}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed, variably sized nodules of basaloid cells with peripheral palisading.

Staging

Staging is according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

Prognostic and predictive factors include tumour location, tumour size, and patient immune status. After complete surgical excision, recurrence and metastasis are exceedingly rare.
