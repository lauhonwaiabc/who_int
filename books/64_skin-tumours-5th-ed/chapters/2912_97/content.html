2.9.1.2 Naevoid melanoma

Definition

Naevoid melanoma is a primary melanoma that resembles the architectural and cytological features of a common intradermal or compound melanocytic naevus.

ICD-O coding

8720/3 Naevoid melanoma

ICD-11 coding

2C30.Y & XH8681 Other specified melanoma of skin & Naevoid melanoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: minimal deviation melanoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Naevoid melanomas can affect all regions of the skin {11391094; 8314219; 10994474; 25388239}.

Clinical features

Patients with naevoid melanoma present with a papule or nodule, with varying or no pigmentation, that can resemble an intradermal or other benign naevus. The presence of atypical vascular structures and irregular globules/dots dermoscopically is a diagnostic clue {25388239}.

Epidemiology

Naevoid melanomas represent < 0.5% of melanomas {11391094} and can occur throughout life, with no sex predominance {28741688}.

Etiology

The etiology appears to be similar to that of other melanomas, both those in skin with a high degree of cumulative sun damage (CSD) and those in low-CSD skin.

Pathogenesis

There is no evidence that naevoid melanoma represents a distinct molecular type, and mutations in BRAF or NRAS are common {28741688}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Several patterns have been described. They mimic, on scanning magnification, a benign papillomatous or intradermal naevus. The neoplastic melanocytes are small to intermediate in size, with less atypia, and can show maturation with descent into the reticular dermis. Mitoses are usually present and are the main diagnostic clue {11391094; 22388759}.

Immunohistochemistry

The immunohistochemistry is similar to that of low-CSD melanoma.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnoses are common or dysplastic naevi. Atypical architectural and cytological features can be seen as a reactive phenomenon in the intraepidermal component of polypoid compound melanocytic naevi {31335410}. Increased mitoses can be seen in naevi during pregnancy {32649342}. Sclerosing naevi with pseudomelanomatous features can be difficult to distinguish on morphological grounds alone {18537860; 21238828}. Immunohistochemistry for p16, Ki-67, and PRAME can be helpful. Melanoma metastases may also have a naevoid appearance.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The diagnostic molecular pathology is similar to that of low-CSD melanoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primary cutaneous melanoma; architectural and cytological features mimicking a melanocytic naevus.

Desirable: supportive immunohistochemical staining (e.g. loss of p16 staining, PRAME positivity); supportive molecular data (e.g. evidence of copy-number variation, CDKN2A loss, TERT promoter mutation).

Staging

Naevoid melanoma is staged according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM criteria like other cutaneous melanomas {29028110}.

Prognosis and prediction

It has been suggested that naevoid melanoma may be less aggressive than other melanomas {10994474; 28741688}, but this has not been firmly established. Childhood naevoid melanoma composed of small cells with a predilection for the scalp may have a poor prognosis {8625056}.
