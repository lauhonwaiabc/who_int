2.1.1.4 Special-site naevi (of the breast, axilla, scalp, and ear)

Definition

Special-site naevi are benign melanocytic naevi occurring on the breast, axilla and other flexural sites, scalp, ear, umbilicus, and genitalia and showing atypical or unusual histopathological features that can make distinction from melanoma difficult.

ICD-O coding

8720/0 Special-site naevi (of the breast, axilla, scalp, and ear)

ICD-11 coding

2F20.1 Atypical melanocytic naevus

2F20.Y Other specific types of melanocytic naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: melanocytic naevi of special sites; naevi with site-related atypia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Special-site naevi affect the breast, axilla, and other flexural sites, as well as the scalp, ear, umbilicus, and thigh/leg. For genital or acral naevi, see the respective sections.

Clinical features

Special-site naevi are clinically innocuous, but they may show an increased size (typically > 6 mm) and irregular borders {24075550}. Most patients are not aware of any recent lesional changes {16446715}.

Epidemiology

Atypical naevi of the breast occur in both sexes {14690457}. Atypical naevi of the scalp occur more frequently in younger patients {17448189}.

Etiology

The etiology is similar to that of other melanocytic naevi. It is unclear whether special-site naevi are distinct clinicopathological entities or represent a histological variation of common acquired naevi.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis and molecular features have not been studied specifically but are likely to be similar to those of common and dysplastic naevi.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Special-site naevi have architectural features that deviate from those of ordinary acquired naevi and overlap with those of dysplastic naevi and melanoma. These features include architectural disorder with asymmetry, an irregular arrangement of the junctional nests, cytological atypia, pagetoid spread, or dermal fibroplasia {27870726; 14690457; 10847544}.

Differential diagnosis

Special-site naevi do not show increased mitotic activity or necrotic melanocytes, and the cells demonstrate maturation with descent into the deeper dermis. Dermoscopy is useful in distinguishing special-site naevi from melanoma {24171788}. However, the dermoscopic features may be misleading, because special-site naevi can show a prominent pigment network with bizarre lines, as well as large globules, mimicking features of melanoma {27313053}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analyses are usually not necessary for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clinical information mentioning the exact site of the naevus; a naevus with architectural and/or cytological atypia (see Histopathology, above) insufficient for a diagnosis of dysplastic naevus or melanoma.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Special-site melanocytic naevi are benign, and no increased melanoma risk has been demonstrated.
