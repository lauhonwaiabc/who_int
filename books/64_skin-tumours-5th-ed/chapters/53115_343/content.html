5.3.1.15 Poikilodermatous plaque-like haemangioma

Definition

Poikilodermatous plaque-like haemangioma is an acquired band-like vascular proliferation involving the superficial dermis.

ICD-O coding

9120/0 Poikilodermatous plaque-like haemangioma

ICD-11 coding

2F2Y Other specified benign cutaneous neoplasms

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Almost all lesions arise in the lower extremities {30930084; 32362008}.

Clinical features

Patients present with asymptomatic, slowly growing, erythematous or violaceous scaly plaques varying in size between 20 and 70 mm that are often described as poikilodermatous. The plaques are typically solitary, rarely multiple. Although clinically the lesions raise concern for poikilodermatous mycosis fungoides, on histology these two entities share no common features.

Epidemiology

Most of the patients are elderly men.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Not relevant

Histopathology

There is a band-like proliferation of small-calibre blood vessels lined by bland endothelium in the papillary and superficial reticular dermis, with no grenz zone and with a concomitant loss or decrease of elastic fibres in the affected area. Most cases exhibit a certain degree of hyperkeratosis or acanthosis and mild lymphocytic inflammation between the vessels. The differential diagnosis includes other vascular neoplasms, such as acquired elastotic haemangioma (smaller and with a similar pattern but with the presence of solar elastosis), microvenular haemangioma (involves the superficial and deep dermis, no absence of elastic fibres, and an absence of a plaque-like architecture), and hobnail haemangioma (wedge-shaped architecture, irregular lymphatic-like channels, lined by hobnail endothelial cells and haemosiderin deposits) {30930084}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a proliferation of numerous small blood vessels with bland endothelium in the superficial dermis; concomitant loss or attenuation of elastic fibres.

Desirable: a lower-extremity, slowly growing erythematous or violaceous plaque.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Lesions are indolent and persist despite treatment.
