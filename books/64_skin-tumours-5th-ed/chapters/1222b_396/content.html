1.2.2.2B Clear cell squamous cell carcinoma

Definition

Clear cell squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is a histologically defined type of SCC that microscopically shows abundant clear cytoplasm.

ICD-O coding

8084/3 Clear cell squamous cell carcinoma

ICD-11 coding

2C31.Z & XH9DC1 Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma & Squamous cell carcinoma, clear cell type

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The head and neck are affected most frequently. Similar tumours on the glans penis may behave aggressively {26848799}.

Clinical features

These tumours, which often arise in elderly men, have no clinical features to distinguish them from conventional SCC.

Epidemiology

This rare SCC type often occurs on sun-damaged skin of older men.

Etiology

The risk factors appear to be similar to those for conventional SCC.

Genetic susceptibility

The genetic susceptibilities beyond those associated with conventional SCC are unknown.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown. There is accumulation of glycogen in the cytoplasm of the neoplastic cells.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is similar to that of conventional SCC (see section Squamous cell carcinomas).

Histopathology

The tumours contain malignant squamoid clear epithelial cells with cytoplasmic vacuolation and focal keratinization. There is no consensus on the required proportion of clear cells for the definition of clear cell SCC, but > 25% has been suggested {19463234}. The tumours may be invasive or in situ {17302606}. Intracytoplasmic vacuoles are typically unilocular, unlike in sebaceous neoplasms. The clear material is usually PAS-positive glycogen {18645304}. Some clear cell SCCs without glycogen have been identified {6163367}, with vesiculation possibly representing a degenerative phenomenon or artefact. When a single cytoplasmic vacuole compresses the nucleus, the lesions may be designated as signet-ring cell SCC {2467552; 19751229; 9550318; 24019773}. Immunohistochemistry is similar to that of conventional SCC; BerEP4 is negative.

Differential diagnosis

There is some morphological similarity between the histological appearances of clear cell SCC, signet-ring cell SCC, and trichilemmal carcinoma. Trichilemmal carcinoma is usually deeply seated, with no connection to the epidermis, and it may show peripheral palisading, basement membrane prominence, and pilar keratinization {7528473}. Sebaceous carcinoma shows multivacuolated cytoplasm with frequent positivity for perilipin, adipophilin, AR, and BerEP4 {20118912}. Clear cell basal cell carcinoma is BerEP4-positive. The presence of ducts, highlighted by staining for CEA and EMA, helps to identify porocarcinoma, hidradenocarcinoma, and dermal duct tumours. In clear cell atypical fibroxanthoma, cytokeratin staining is negative and keratinization is absent {26956561}. Clear cell and balloon cell melanomas stain for melanocyte markers. If there is no evidence of epidermal origin, metastatic carcinomas should be considered, especially from the kidney (PAX8-positive) {21552115}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: SCC with clear cell differentiation in ≥ 25% of the neoplastic cells.

Desirable: PAS positivity.

Staging

Tumours should be staged according to the eighth edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM system.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of clear cell SCC is comparable to that of other SCC types {16630176} (see section Squamous cell carcinomas).
