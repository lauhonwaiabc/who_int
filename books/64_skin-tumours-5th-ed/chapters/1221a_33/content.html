1.2.2.1A Keratoacanthoma

Definition

Keratoacanthoma is a clinically and pathologically distinctive, self-limiting squamous tumour of infundibular-trichilemmal origin characterized by rapid growth, stabilization, and spontaneous regression.

ICD-O coding

8071/1 Keratoacanthoma

ICD-11 coding

2C31.1 Keratoacanthoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: keratoacanthoma-like squamous proliferation; squamoproliferative tumour of uncertain malignant potential, keratoacanthoma-like.

Not recommended: squamous cell carcinoma, keratoacanthoma type; eruptive squamous atypia.

See #34896Table 2.03 .

Localization

Keratoacanthoma is largely limited to hair-bearing skin. Studies from Europe and the USA showed a predilection for the face {27323251}. A large cohort study from Australia emphasized lower relative frequencies on the head and neck (7%) and the trunk (12%), with most lesions occurring on the limbs and extremities {33954799}.

Clinical features

The typical presentation is an exoendophytic nodule with a central keratinous plug {35024732}, which grows rapidly over a few weeks, followed by stabilization and typically spontaneous involution by 1 year, often leaving a deep scar. Unlike keratoacanthoma-like squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), keratoacanthomas are rarely associated with actinic keratosis; may be associated with antecedent skin trauma; and demonstrate rapid initial growth, stabilization, and spontaneous regression.

Epidemiology

Keratoacanthoma is most common in fair-skinned individuals with a history of ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure. The incidence rises with age, with most cases occurring after 60 years. A prior history of skin cancer is common.

Etiology

The development of keratoacanthoma is predominantly linked to UV radiation exposure and association with a relatively high incidence of previous skin cancer or precancer. Most lesions develop spontaneously, but many appear to be reactive to antecedent trauma (e.g. injection site, skin surgery or grafting, tattooing), infections (e.g. HPV, human polyomavirus [HPyV]), and chemical carcinogens.

Immunosuppression and hereditary defects in DNA repair (e.g. xeroderma pigmentosum and Muir–Torre syndrome) are associated with an increased incidence of keratoacanthoma {33954799; 26853179}.

Patients treated with BRAF inhibitors may develop keratoacanthomas or SCCs of keratoacanthoma type within weeks of commencing therapy {27323251}.

Pathogenesis

Keratoacanthoma may display RAS driver mutations in 10–40% of cases {3724537; 24662767; 39449378}. TGFBR1 mutations are found in keratoacanthoma of the Ferguson–Smith type {23358096; 27558455}. Keratoacanthomas display numerous genetic, molecular, and immunohistochemical differences from SCC: fewer abnormalities are found on comparative genomic hybridization, fewer large regions with loss of heterozygosity, and a lower somatic mutation burden are noted {12485441; 27283355}. NOTCH1 and NOTCH2 are less frequently mutated in keratoacanthoma than in SCC (33% vs 83–86%) {24662767; 27558455; 27283355}. HPV is infrequently found in keratoacanthoma and is therefore not significantly associated with this tumour {36468731}.

Macroscopic appearance

Well-developed lesions are nodular, dome-shaped, smooth-bordered, and centrally keratotic.

Histopathology

The histological features of keratoacanthoma and the main differential diagnosis, keratoacanthoma-like SCC, are outlined in #34901Table 2.04 . Early proliferative keratoacanthoma shows a typical orderly pattern of infundibular (central) to trichilemmal (peripheral) maturation, and it lacks features suggesting malignancy, such as irregular maturation, predominant parakeratosis, aberrant mitotic activity, cellular pleomorphism beyond the peripheral one to three cells, acantholysis, follicular mucin, deep irregular invasion, or variability in stromal/inflammatory response. Keratoacanthoma, particularly in a central facial location, may show perineural and/or venous invasion, but this is rare (0.2% of cases in large series) {20414090}. Clinicians should be aware of the requirement for clinicopathological correlation {26853179}. Late-regressing keratoacanthoma could be confused with an infundibular cyst or comedo.

Immunohistochemistry can aid in the diagnosis. Peripheral graded p53 and Ki-67 are found in keratoacanthoma, in contrast to SCC. In contrast to keratoacanthoma, a high proportion of keratoacanthoma-like SCCs show highly aberrant (usually null or near diffuse / block) staining for p53 and a high rate of aberrant Ki-67 staining beyond the peripheral one to three cell layers {37573161; 39449378; 1476915; 8304756; 8600795; 10429981; 9890376; 16781827; 7531999; 8600794; 2481408; 21883393}.

Differential diagnosis

Inverted follicular keratosis and eruptive benign verrucous keratoses may be confused with keratoacanthoma but have circumscribed borders and generally lack significant inflammatory response or atypia; when eruptive benign verrucous keratoses are multiple, they are commonly a complication of BRAF inhibitor therapy. Keratoacanthoma-like, reactive, follicular, pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia may be associated with nodular prurigo; halogenoderma; hypertrophic lichen planopilaris; chronic bacterial, mycobacterial, and fungal infections; and various neoplasms {21399447}.

The term “squamoproliferative tumour of uncertain malignant potential, keratoacanthoma-like” can be used for lesions that are diagnostically challenging where a confident diagnosis of keratoacanthoma or keratoacanthoma-like SCC cannot be rendered.

Staging

Staging is not required for typical or regressed keratoacanthoma. Lesions of uncertain malignant potential can be staged according to the system for SCC.

Prognosis and prediction

Typical keratoacanthoma or subtypes (including centrifugum marginatum and lesions in Ferguson–Smith disease) have not been proved to have metastatic potential, and most cases of purported malignant behaviour are likely misdiagnoses {26853179; 24366198; 27323251}. In reviews of cases with perineural invasion (132 cases) and/or venous invasion (11 cases), no patients had adverse outcomes on follow-up {11488708; 23785617; 27323251}.
