5.4.1.3 Angioleiomyoma

Definition

Angioleiomyoma is a benign mesenchymal tumour that typically arises in the dermis or subcutis. Tumours are composed of thick-walled vessels surrounded by well-differentiated perivascular bundles of smooth muscle. It is a member of the perivascular myoid family of tumours and is on a morphological continuum with myopericytoma.

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiomyoma; vascular leiomyoma.

Subtype(s)

Solid angioleiomyoma; venous angioleiomyoma; cavernous angioleiomyoma

Localization

Tumours have a propensity to arise in the skin and subcutis of the extremities (particularly around the knee), followed by the head and neck (including oral and sinonasal mucosal sites) {8245243; 6722737; 17270242; 28058733; 34463813; 30148476; 26047608; 30208227; 15311559}. Rare sites, including subfascial, intraosseous, and CNS, as well as others, have been described {21940181; 32058118; 34057005; 35128202; 10794558}. The most common solid subtype typically arises in the lower extremities. The less common venous subtype more often involves the head and neck region, while the rare cavernous form most often occurs in the upper extremities {6722737}.

Clinical features

The lesions are solitary, small, firm, circumscribed, and slow-growing nodules. Approximately half of angioleiomyomas are painful {8025650; 17270242; 6722737}.

Epidemiology

Angioleiomyomas affect a wide age range, with peak incidence in the fourth through sixth decades of life {15311559; 6722737}. The solid subtype is significantly more common in women, whereas the venous and cavernous subtypes demonstrate a slight male predominance. The overall M:F ratio is 0.7:1 {6722737}.

Etiology

A definitive etiology has not been established. Minor trauma and venous stasis have been proposed as potential causal factors in a subset of lesions {15311559}.

Pathogenesis

Angioleiomyomas are thought to arise from vascular smooth muscle {6722737}. Reported cytogenetic abnormalities include monosomy of chromosome 13 and loss of 6p, 21q, and 13q {20471520; 14654964}. A very small subset of angioleiomyomas reportedly have NOTCH gene fusions {23999936; 32604167}. In contradistinction to myofibromas, angioleiomyomas do not have recurrent somatic PDGFRB mutations {27776010}.

Macroscopic appearance

Angioleiomyomas are solitary, circumscribed, and relatively small (< 30 mm in diameter). Tumours may be seen emanating from an attached blood vessel wall {17270242; 6722737}.

Histopathology

All angioleiomyomas are composed of bundles of bland, well-differentiated smooth muscle surrounding blood vessels of varying calibre. Mitotic activity is inconspicuous. Three subtypes of angioleiomyoma have been described based on predominant vascular morphology: solid, venous, and cavernous.

The solid subtype is composed of compact bundles of smooth muscle surrounding compressed, slit-like vessels.

Venous-subtype tumours are made up of thick-walled vessels, with smooth muscle from blood vessel walls blending with intervascular smooth muscle bundles.

Cavernous-subtype lesions are composed of dilated vascular channels, with a proliferation of smooth muscle within intervascular spaces.

Hybrid lesions have been described. Some tumours share features with myopericytoma, with concentric whorling of less spindled to ovoid myoid cells around vessels {17270242}. Minor histological patterns include those with adipocytic metaplasia, prominent hyalinization, degenerative atypia, or calcification {34086643; 26691377; 26047608; 31565982; 8879307}.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells are diffusely positive for actins (including SMA), calponin, and h-caldesmon. Desmin expression is variable and may be very focal or completely absent in a small subset of tumours {8025650; 17270242}. Melanocytic markers such as HMB45 and melan-A are negative in tumours with adipocytic metaplasia, thus enabling distinction from angiomyolipoma {6722737; 17270242; 26047608}. Similarly to glomus tumours and myopericytomas, angioleiomyomas demonstrate diffuse immunoreactivity for the pericytic antigen RGS5 {26558691}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed tumour; bundles of cytologically bland smooth muscle cells growing concentrically around blood vessels of varying calibre.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Angioleiomyoma is a benign tumour that rarely recurs after surgical excision {6722737; 21957385}.
