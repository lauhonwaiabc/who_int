5.6.1.2 Solitary circumscribed neuroma

Definition

Solitary circumscribed neuroma (SCN) is a benign nodular proliferation of nerve fibres, typically arising as an asymptomatic papule on the face.

ICD-O coding

9570/0 Solitary circumscribed neuroma

9570/0 Plexiform solitary circumscribed neuroma

ICD-11 coding

2F24 & XH90Y8 Benign cutaneous neoplasms of neural or nerve sheath origin & Solitary circumscribed neuroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: palisaded encapsulated neuroma.

Subtype(s)

Plexiform solitary circumscribed neuroma

Localization

SCN commonly occurs on the face (90%), often near mucocutaneous junctions {2660609} or in oral mucosa {20237984}. SCN can involve any cutaneous site, including acral skin {19614730}.

Clinical features

SCN usually manifests as a small, solitary, asymptomatic, firm, skin-coloured papule. Rare examples of multiple SCNs have been reported in the absence of systemic disease {26460241}.

Epidemiology

SCN can occur at any age, most commonly in the fifth to seventh decades of life. There is no sex predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

SCN is a benign tumour with slow growth of nerve fibres, unrelated to trauma {2660609}.

Macroscopic appearance

SCN appears as a circumscribed, non-pigmented papule with a firm, pale-grey cut surface.

Histopathology

SCN is a dermal-based nodule composed of fascicles of bland spindled cells encased in a delicate, often incomplete, fibrous capsule. Artefactual clefting at the tumour–stroma interface is characteristic {4639250}. Focal palisading of nuclei within the tumour is usually present but Verocay bodies are less common {30834800}. Multilobular and plexiform growth patterns are well described {19614730; 8468415}. No cytological atypia or conspicuous mitotic activity is seen.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumoural Schwann cells are strongly and diffusely positive for S100. Tiny axons throughout the tumour can be identified by staining for NFP {1705947}. The capsular perineural cells stain for EMA, claudin-1, and GLUT1 {20237984}.

Differential diagnosis

SCN, especially the plexiform subtype, can be mistaken for other benign neural neoplasms, some of which have associations with systemic disease. Neurofibromas are hypocellular tumours with a loose, delicate matrix that are unencapsulated (except in the plexiform subtype). Schwannomas tend to have a more variable cell density (Antoni A and B areas), prominent palisading (Verocay bodies), and hyalinization of vessel walls, and they lack clefting artefact.

Cytology

FNA of SCN shows a nonspecific paucicellular pattern of bland spindle cells.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nodular, multinodular or plexiform circumscribed dermal nodule composed of fascicles of Schwann cells and axons without significant atypia or mitotic activity.

Desirable: at least partial encapsulation highlighted by perineural markers (e.g. EMA); artefactual clefting.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

SCN is a benign tumour with no metastatic potential.
