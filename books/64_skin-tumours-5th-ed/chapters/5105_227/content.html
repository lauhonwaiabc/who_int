5.1.0.5 Atypical lipomatous tumour

Definition

Atypical lipomatous tumour (ALT) is an adipocytic tumour of borderline malignancy showing at least focal nuclear atypia in both adipocytes and stromal cells, and almost always associated with amplification of MDM2 and/or CDK4.

ICD-O coding

8850/1 Atypical lipomatous tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH0RW4 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Atypical lipomatous tumour

2B5H & XH7Y61 Well-differentiated lipomatous tumour, primary site & Liposarcoma, well-differentiated

Related terminology

Acceptable: in the accessible soft tissues, “atypical lipomatous tumour” and “well-differentiated liposarcoma” are considered synonyms describing morphologically and genetically identical neoplasms; however, the term “well-differentiated liposarcoma” should not be used in superficial locations.

Not recommended: atypical lipoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cutaneous ALTs do not have a known site of predilection. In deep soft tissue, common sites include the thigh, buttock, back, and shoulder {12004355; 18043041; 11598853; 9700369}.

Clinical features

ALT manifests as a nonspecific nodule or mass in the dermis or subcutis. Occasional exophytic tumours can mimic an acrochordon {21358383}.

Epidemiology

Primary cutaneous or dermal ALT is exceedingly rare {9700369; 22472959; 16871039; 21358383}; dermal involvement much more commonly constitutes extension from a more deeply situated tumour.

Etiology

Nearly all are sporadic, but rare cases are associated with Li–Fraumeni syndrome.

Pathogenesis

ALT contains giant ring chromosomes containing amplified sequences from the 12q13-q15 region {8579124; 7515663}. These sequences contain the genes MDM2, CDK4, TSPAN31 (SAS), HMGA2 (HMGIC), and CPM, among others {19820690; 17213023; 9591630; 9892106; 8500103}.

Macroscopic appearance

ALTs usually resemble mature fat, albeit with increased fibrous tissue and less well formed lobules.

Histopathology

ALT is composed of a variable proportion of mature adipose tissue and irregular fibrous septa containing hyperchromatic stromal cells; rare lipoblasts may be seen. The number of pleomorphic cells and the degree of pleomorphism is variable. A rare finding in ALT is the presence of mature heterologous osseous or myogenic differentiation, which does not in itself imply dedifferentiation {20697254; 17197914; 8352375}.

Immunohistochemistry for MDM2 nuclear expression and FISH for MDM2 amplification are useful confirmatory tests, but FISH is more specific {16160477; 19737942; 17895748; 34523152}.

A major pitfall is represented by nuclear MDM2 positivity in histiocytes in fat necrosis. ALTs associated with Li–Fraumeni syndrome are MDM2-negative; however, they express p53.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MDM2 (and/or CDK4) amplification distinguishes ALT from benign adipose tumours {17895748; 25862836; 21117066; 34523152}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mature adipose tissue transected by irregular fibrous septa containing enlarged, hyperchromatic stromal cells.

Desirable: MDM2 and/or CDK4 nuclear expression or evidence of MDM2 and/or CDK4 gene amplification (in selected or challenging cases).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence is rare in dermal or subcutaneous ALT. Dedifferentiation is exceptional {9643489; 8944047}.
