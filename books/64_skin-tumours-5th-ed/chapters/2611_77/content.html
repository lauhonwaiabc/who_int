2.6.1.1 Naevus of Ito and naevus of Ota

Definition

Naevus of Ito and naevus of Ota are dermal melanocytoses involving the shoulder and arm (naevus of Ito) or the face (naevus of Ota).

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

LC10 Dermal melanocytosis

Related terminology

Acceptable: oculodermal melanocytosis; congenital oculodermal melanocytosis.

Not recommended: naevus fusco-caeruleus ophthalmomaxillaris.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Naevus of Ito involves the side of the neck, supraclavicular and scapular areas, or shoulder {26087431}. Naevus of Ota involves the innervation areas of the first two branches of the trigeminal nerve. It is unilateral in 90% of cases, bilateral in 10% {15096155; 26087431}.

Clinical features

Naevus of Ito and naevus of Ota are bluish patches that are present at birth in about 50% of cases or appear before adolescence {12072008; 17640235; 23729678}. Two thirds of naevus of Ota cases have ocular involvement, with scleral hyperpigmentation {25704935; 27083007}. Ipsilateral deafness, meningeal or orbital melanocytoma, and phakomatosis pigmentovascularis may be associated {12399743; 21924617; 25862111; 25933209; 24989678; 30904806; 27374914}. Spontaneous regression does not occur, and the colour of the naevi deepens with puberty.

Epidemiology

Naevus of Ito and naevus of Ota are most common in Asian populations {6018994}. Naevus of Ota is more common than naevus of Ito and has a female predominance {14458325; 27083007; 20074832}.

Etiology

They arise from postzygotic mutations leading to clonal expansion of melanocytes during development.

Pathogenesis

GNAQ and GNA11 mutations are commonly found in naevi of Ota and Ito {19078957; 26260725}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not applicable

Histopathology

Sparse populations of pigmented and dendritic melanocytes are haphazardly dispersed in the upper dermis {13130904; 17642580; 23918999; 13771275}. A junctional component and mitoses are absent.

Immunohistochemistry

The melanocytes express melanocytic markers such as SOX10, melan-A, and HMB45.

Differential diagnosis

In cellular proliferations, cellular blue naevus/melanocytoma and melanoma must be considered. Uveal melanoma and melanoma arising in blue naevus may rarely develop in naevus of Ota and show the characteristic secondary genetic alterations, including gains of chromosome 6p and losses of 6q, inactivation of BAP1, and mutations of SF3B1, EIF1AX, and TP53 {20110797; 26260725; 26701415; 28409567}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bluish patches on the face (Ota) or the neck and shoulder (Ito); iris heterochromia and/or scleral pigmentation (Ota).

Desirable: dendritic melanocytes in the dermis.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Malignant transformation is rare {26260725; 32808287}. A complication of naevus of Ota is glaucoma caused by angle abnormalities or mechanical occlusion by pigment and melanocytes {29607552}.
