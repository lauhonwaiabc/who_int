8.2.0.1 Ungual fibrokeratoma

Definition

Ungual fibrokeratoma is a benign fibrokeratotic lesion occurring in the periungual areas. Patients with tuberous sclerosis often develop multiple ungual fibrokeratomas, and in this clinical context they are known as a Koenen tumour.

ICD-O coding

8052/0 Ungual fibrokeratoma

ICD-11 coding

2F23.Y Other specified benign dermal fibrous or fibrohistiocytic neoplasms

Related terminology

Acceptable: Koenen tumour; acral angiofibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most acquired ungual fibrokeratomas are located in the periungual areas, while a minority of lesions may occur in the intraungual and subungual areas. Toenails are more frequently affected than fingernails. The most commonly affected digits are the thumb and hallux {33107051; 27852988}.

Clinical features

The clinical appearance of ungual fibrokeratoma varies, and lesions have been described as rod-shaped, dome-shaped, flat, and branching. The most common types are the dome-shaped and branching types. A longitudinal groove in the nail plate is a very common associated finding with the ungual fibrokeratoma. Other features that have been described as associated with ungual fibrokeratoma include trachyonychia, chromonychia, onycholysis, subungual hyperkeratosis, dissected nail plates, and haemorrhagic crust {33107051; 27852988}.

Epidemiology

Ungual fibrokeratoma is an unusual condition, and epidemiological studies have not been performed.

Etiology

The causes of ungual fibrokeratomas are unknown. However, a minority of cases are associated with trauma, which may be an inciting factor {33107051}.

Pathogenesis

Although a definitive pathogenesis has not been established, factor XIIIa–positive dermal dendritic cells are thought to be a factor in the development of collagen in ungual fibrokeratomas {2574200}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Ungual fibrokeratomas are composed of fibrovascular tissue. The low-power view often shows a sausage-shaped tumour that may include serum at the tip of it. Sporadic ungual fibrokeratomas have shown epidermal features including hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, focal hypergranulosis, and irregular rete ridges. Kint et al. {4008685} classified ungual fibrokeratomas into three patterns: pattern 1, a core with thick, dense, closely packed, and irregularly arranged collagen bundles; pattern 2, a core with an increase in grouped fibroblasts and reduced elastic fibres; and pattern 3, a core with a poorly cellular and oedematous structure with completely absent elastic tissue. In a study evaluating the histopathological patterns of periungual fibromas in patients with tuberous sclerosis, the ungual fibrokeratomas were divided into three histopathological patterns, depending on the ratio of the vascular component to the fibrous component. From most vascular to least vascular, these types are angiomatous, mixed, and fibrous. Generally, immunohistochemistry is not required for the evaluation of ungual fibrokeratoma. A potential pitfall in diagnosis is that cellular angiofibroma and superficial acral fibromyxoma are highlighted with CD34 and may cause diagnostic confusion with other CD34-positive entities {4008685; 21238836}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a nail plate lesion with a fibrovascular core.

Desirable: epidermal hyperplasia with hyperkeratosis; a sausage-shaped appearance at low-power view; serum present at the distal pole of the specimen.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Ungual fibrokeratoma is a benign lesion.
