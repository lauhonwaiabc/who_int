5.2.1.6 Superficial acral fibromyxoma

Definition

Superficial acral fibromyxoma is a benign fibroblastic neoplasm typically restricted to acral sites with a marked predilection for the periungual region of the digits and potential for local recurrence.

ICD-O coding

8811/0 Superficial acral fibromyxoma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH8173 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Acral fibromyxoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: digital fibromyxoma; acral fibromyxoma.

Not recommended: cellular digital fibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The great majority arise in the periungual/subungual region of the fingers and toes, with occasional cases involving the hands, feet, ankles, wrists, lower legs, and thighs {11486169; 19236516; 18537858; 18764846; 22367301; 27825811}.

Clinical features

Acral fibromyxomas are slowly growing and often painful, and they range in size from 10 to 50 mm (mean: ~20 mm) {11486169; 18537858; 22367301}. Associated nail deformities and lytic lesions of underlying bone may be seen {22367301; 18327578; 29423724}.

Epidemiology

The age range is wide, but most cases occur in middle-aged adults, with a male predilection {11486169; 22367301}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

RB1 deletions have been reported {27825811}.

Macroscopic appearance

Acral fibromyxomas are dome-shaped, polypoid, or verrucous, often with a lobulated appearance. They have a firm to soft consistency and poorly defined borders.

Histopathology

Acral fibromyxomas are lobulated dermal and subcutaneous lesions, sometimes with an epidermal collarette. Most have infiltrative borders and the underlying bone may be involved. Most are relatively hypocellular, composed of bland spindled to stellate cells in a loose growth pattern that can be vaguely fascicular to storiform. Tumour cells are embedded in a variably collagenous to myxoid stroma, and a thin-walled vasculature may be present in myxoid areas. Multinucleated cells may be present. Mitotic figures are rare {11486169; 18537858; 18764846; 22367301; 28045748}. A subset may have increased cellularity {19236516}. Cartilaginous and osseous metaplasia is rare {29360681}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumour cells are positive for CD34, CD99, and nestin and may express EMA and SMA in a minority; they are negative for MUC4 and BCL2 {11486169; 28045748; 22367301}. Loss of RB1 expression is seen in most tumours {27825811}.

Differential diagnosis

The stromal component of onychomatricoma is identical to that of acral fibromyxoma, but acral fibromyxoma lacks an epithelial component. Myxoid neurofibroma is less cellular and the tumour cells are positive for S100. Myxoid dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans is very rare on the digits and lacks the collagen component, and the tumour cells display a storiform pattern.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The tumours show deletion of RB1 {27825811}, but this is not required for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: acral location, usually periungual; a lobulated dermal and subcutaneous tumour with bland spindled to stellate cells; variably myxoid to collagenous stroma.

Desirable: CD34 positivity; frequent loss of RB1 expression by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

These lesions are benign, but recurrence occurs in as many as 22% of cases {27825811; 22367301}. Metastases have not been reported {11486169; 22367301}.
