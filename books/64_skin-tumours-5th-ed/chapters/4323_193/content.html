4.3.2.3 Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma

Definition

Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) / small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) is a B-cell lymphoma comprising monomorphic small mature B cells that frequently coexpress CD5 and CD23. A peripheral blood diagnosis of CLL requires a B-cell count of ≥ 5 × 109/L with the characteristic morphology and immunophenotype. A tissue-based diagnosis of SLL requires organ enlargement (e.g. lymphadenopathy > 15 mm) and its infiltration by the above neoplastic B cells. Although CLL and SLL represent the same disease, the name SLL is used for cases with < 5 × 109/L circulating B cells and nodal, splenic, or other extramedullary involvement.

ICD-O coding

9823/3 Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A82.0Z Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia or small lymphocytic lymphoma, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: B-cell chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CLL/SLL involving skin has no specific localization.

Clinical features

Skin involvement by CLL/SLL usually occurs in patients with known CLL; only rarely is CLL/SLL first detected in the skin {8712287; 26762898; 24064196; 16466516; 23974225; 19389520; 19318802; 30183072}. Cutaneous lesions can be single, aggregated but localized, or (rarely) generalized {8712287; 17487727; 30183072; 32889697}. The lesions are erythematous papules, plaques, nodules, or large tumours {8712287; 22257836; 19389520}. CLL/SLL is frequently associated with a second skin disease observed in the same biopsy and may involve sites of herpes simplex virus or varicella zoster virus or other infections {8712287; 23253001; 16115059; 25171429; 19389520; 17487727}, foci of trauma {19389520}, or surrounding epithelial or non-epithelial malignancies {24610800; 19318802; 30183072}. Rarely, the skin is the site of transformation of CLL to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (Richter transformation) {8712287; 20145381; 24935194}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of CLL rises with age (median age: 58–70 years), and there is a male predominance {33077870}. The skin is the most common extramedullary, extranodal site of involvement {24064196}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Family members of patients with CLL have a 5–8-fold increased risk of CLL {31395603}.

Pathogenesis

CLL cells most closely resemble antigen-experienced, memory-like B cells {33604581; 26780610}. Cytogenetic and molecular genetic abnormalities, epigenetic factors, and the tumour microenvironment influence disease evolution {26065654; 11136261; 28074063; 31467127; 21941366; 24127483; 26780610}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

CLL/SLL takes the form of a perivascular/periadnexal, nodular, diffuse, or (rarely) band-like infiltrate of small, monotonous lymphocytes with clumped chromatin in the dermis with extension to the subcutis {8712287; 9001978; 19389520}. Proliferation centres are uncommon {8712287; 16115059}. An underlying associated second skin disease possibly obscured by the CLL infiltration should be investigated {30183072}.

Immunohistochemistry

The neoplastic lymphoid cells typically express CD19, CD20, CD5, CD23, CD43, and LEF1, and are negative for CD10, cyclin D1, and SOX11 {28395058; 26762898; 24460879; 22257836}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes reactive small lymphoid infiltrates and other neoplastic infiltrates predominantly composed of small lymphoid cells.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Cytogenetics, FISH, and molecular investigations are typically undertaken on peripheral blood in patients with CLL/SLL.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: skin lesion(s); a variably dense lymphocytic infiltrate in the dermis and/or panniculus, with the classic cytomorphology of SLL; CD20 positive/weak, CD5 positive/weak, CD23 (variable); cyclin D1 negative.

Desirable: LEF1 positive; CD10 and SOX11 negative.

Staging

For CLL, the Rai and Binet systems are used [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]. The stage of SLL is determined with the Lugano modifications to the Ann Arbor system {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

Although del(13q) as a sole aberration is associated with indolent disease, TP53 deletion and/or mutation predicts resistance to chemoimmunotherapy {30442727; 29467486; 29540348}. The status of somatic hypermutation of IGHV genes is a key prognostic marker {10477712; 10477713; 12615894} (see #32195Table 6.11).
