2.6.1.3 Blue naevus

Definition

Blue naevus is a dermal melanocytic neoplasm composed of pigmented, dendritic, spindle, or ovoid cells.

ICD-O coding

8780/0 Blue naevus

8780/0 Dendritic blue naevus

8790/0 Cellular blue naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.Y & XH7QJ7 Other specific types of melanocytic naevus & Blue naevus, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: common blue naevus; dermal dendritic melanocytic naevus; naevus of Jadassohn; Tièche naevus; Jadassohn–Tièche blue naevus.

Subtype(s)

Dendritic blue naevus (DBN); cellular blue naevus (CBN)

Localization

Blue naevi can occur at any cutaneous site. DBNs most commonly involve the dorsal distal extremities and face, whereas CBNs typically involve the scalp, back, and buttocks. Blue naevi can also occur on the mucosa, nail bed, or lymph nodes {23172087}. Rare plaque-type blue naevi usually affect the scalp but can also occur elsewhere.

Clinical features

Blue naevi are bluish-grey to bluish-black macules or papules, usually < 10 mm in diameter {27199078}. CBNs usually appear as pigmented nodules ranging from a few millimetres to several centimetres in size. Rare unpigmented lesions are called amelanotic or hypomelanotic blue naevi.

Plaque-type blue naevi are usually present at birth or arise in early childhood and may enlarge during puberty. Plaque-type blue naevus is often several centimetres in diameter and is composed of a single plaque or a confluence of several small macules and/or papules. Rarely, multiple blue naevi affect a circumscribed anatomical area (agminated blue naevi) or present as eruptive, linear, or targetoid blue naevi.

Epidemiology

Blue naevi are less common than common naevi. They can be present at birth or arise at any age but most commonly manifest in adults aged < 40 years. CBNs show a female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:2).

Etiology

Although sun exposure is a well-known factor in the development of other types of melanocytic naevi, its role in the development of blue naevi is not established.

Pathogenesis

Blue naevi and related melanocytic proliferations (melanocytic neoplasms of the uvea and leptomeninges) probably arise from a distinct type of melanocyte that originates from the neural crest through the ventromedial developmental pathway {24460190}.

Blue naevi have activating mutations in the Gqα pathway, most commonly in GNAQ, GNA11, or PLCB4, or less frequently in CYSLTR2. They lack BRAF, NRAS, and NF1 mutations {19078957; 21083380}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Blue naevi are intradermal proliferations of dendritic, spindled, or ovoid melanocytes. DBNs are usually non-circumscribed dermal proliferations of bipolar and spindle-shaped melanocytes with elongated dendritic processes in a stroma with variable amounts of sclerosis and admixed melanophages {19851128; 28802492}. The cells are sometimes grouped in short fascicles. When sclerotic stromal collagen is particularly prominent, the term “sclerosing blue naevus” may be used. Cellular atypia and mitotic figures are absent. Occasionally, junctional lentiginous melanocytes and nests may be seen in wide superficial DBNs, especially those located on the back of the foot {31754815}.

CBNs are well-circumscribed intradermal nodules of lightly pigmented, fusiform to ovoid melanocytes with varying admixtures of melanophages. Bulbous, vertically oriented sheets or fascicles can extend into the subcutaneous fat. In the most common pattern, well-defined nests of cells are surrounded by collagen and numerous melanophages {26909585}. The cells are usually oriented parallel to the long axis of the nests (often with central longitudinal nuclear grooves) and lack cell crowding, pleomorphism, nuclear atypia, and hyperchromasia. Oedematous areas, myxoid stroma, or cystic change may be present (typically in the centre), but necrosis is generally absent. Mitotic activity is usually absent or low (< 1 mitosis/mm2). Perineural extension and intralymphatic tumour cells may be seen. Hypomelanotic and amelanotic blue naevi are uncommon patterns of CBN, characterized by absent or minimal melanin pigment within the melanocytes and a paucity of melanophages.

Plaque-type blue naevus is characterized by a large size and often shows deep extension into the subcutis and along fascial planes, with a combination of the features seen in DBN and CBN {28673427}. Rarely, blue naevi may involve lymph nodes; such cases are usually located in the capsule and intranodal fibrous trabeculae and have cytological characteristics identical to those of their cutaneous counterparts.

Immunohistochemistry

Melanocytes are usually positive for HMB45 antigen, SOX10, and melan-A, but may be negative for S100. BAP1 expression is retained.

Differential diagnosis

For hypopigmented DBNs, dermatofibroma, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, and scar tissue can be distinguished by immunohistochemistry. Sclerosing DBN may resemble desmoplastic melanoma or other dermal spindle cell proliferations but have negligible cytological atypia and express HMB45 antigen and melan-A. Features favouring melanoma include necrosis, large pleomorphic epithelioid cells, cell crowding, frequent mitoses (> 2 mitoses/mm2), and an infiltrative growth pattern as opposed to the generally rounded or pushing margins of CBN. In cases without overtly malignant features, confident prediction of likely biological behaviour may not always be possible; such tumours may be called atypical CBNs of uncertain malignant potential (or atypical cellular blue melanocytomas). BAP1 expression is often lost in melanoma arising in blue naevus. Detection of copy-number changes of chromosomes 3, 6, and 8 supports the diagnosis of melanoma.

Clear cell sarcoma is also positive for S100, HMB45 antigen, and melan-A, but it tends to be larger than CBN and have more nuclear atypia. It is usually composed of cells with pale or clear cytoplasm (often with scattered wreath-like giant cells) and may show necrosis. Other considerations include tumoural melanosis, haemosiderin deposition, tattoos, and blue naevus–like metastasis.

Some blue naevi have large numbers of epithelioid melanocytes (epithelioid and fusiform blue naevi), and such lesions must be distinguished from pigmented epithelioid melanocytoma {22892599}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

GNAQ, GNA11, PLCB4, or CYSLTR2 mutation favours CBN, whereas EWSR1 rearrangement or t(12;22) translocation favours clear cell sarcoma. Mutations in EIF1AX or SF3B1, and in particular biallelic inactivation of BAP1, raise suspicion for progression to blue naevus–like melanoma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for DBN: a poorly circumscribed dermal proliferation of dendritic melanocytes with fibroplasia; for CBN: dense nests and sheets of bland melanocytes, often within a DBN.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Blue naevi are benign tumours. Recurrences occasionally occur after incomplete excision and usually resemble the original tumour. Incompletely excised blue naevi with atypical features warrant complete excision.
