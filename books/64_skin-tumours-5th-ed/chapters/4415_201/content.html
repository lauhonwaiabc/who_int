4.4.1.5 Primary cutaneous gamma/delta T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Primary cutaneous gamma-delta T-cell lymphoma (PCGD-TCL) is a clonal proliferation of mature, activated γδ T cells of the Vδ1 or Vδ2 subsets, arising within the skin and subcutaneous tissues.

ICD-O coding

9726/3 Primary cutaneous gamma/delta T-cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2B0Y & XH84A5 Other specified primary cutaneous mature T-cell or NK-cell lymphomas and lymphoproliferative disorders & Primary cutaneous gamma/delta T-cell lymphoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PCGD-TCL manifests as single or generalized skin and subcutaneous lesions, occasionally with mucosal or perimucosal involvement, without a preferential site of involvement {23073324}.

Clinical features

The clinical presentation and disease course of PCGD-TCL are variable, so a definitive diagnosis may not be achieved on the initial biopsy. Some patients present with generalized scaly patches and plaques suggestive of mycosis fungoides, while others present with deep dermal and/or subcutaneous nodules or tumours {10926739; 23073324}. Ulceration and rapid evolution can be seen in either presentation. Both patterns may be present in the same patient. Haemophagocytic syndrome {17934071; 9669350} and spread to extranodal (visceral) sites cause significant morbidity. Involvement of lymph nodes or bone marrow is uncommon {19018763; 28248813; 25981001}. B symptoms, including fever, night sweats, and weight loss, occur frequently.

Imaging

Findings on CT and PET-CT commonly signal cutaneous and extracutaneous sites of involvement {17934071; 23073324; 30608186}.

Epidemiology

PCGD-TCLs comprise < 1% of primary cutaneous lymphomas. SEER Program data (2006–2015) showed a cumulative incidence of 0.40 cases per 10 million population, a median patient age of 63 years, and a male predominance {30951189}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Immune dysfunction associated with chronic antigenic stimulation may play a role in the origin of PCGD-TCL {9473239}. TR gene chain usage is non-random, suggesting common antigenic drivers {32286303}.

Pathogenesis

Studies show a heterogeneous genomic landscape, with recurrent mutations of putative driver genes within the JAK/STAT {25586472}, MAPK, MYC, and chromatin modification pathways, as well as mutations in consensus cancer genes and in tumour suppressor genes. The heterogeneity of PCGD-TCL may be related to different cells of origin with distinct roles in the immunity of their originating skin compartments: delta 1 expression (Vδ1) associates with the superficial cutaneous or mucosal epitheliotropic lymphomas, and delta 2 expression (Vδ2) with the subcutaneous lymphomas {11272897; 32286303}. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation signature mutation profiles are more common in delta 1 cases, whereas senescence-related mutagenesis is noted in subcutaneous cases. Patients with deep dermal and subcutaneous lesions show enrichment in interferon-γ and other inflammatory cytokines, which probably drives the haemophagocytic syndrome {32286303}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Malignant cells are predominantly medium-sized but can range from small to large, with elongated nuclei and coarse chromatin {33759218}. Any skin compartment (epidermis, dermis, subcutis, or any combination thereof) may be involved by PCGD-TCL, and sequential and contemporaneous biopsies may show different compartmental infiltration {12522013}. Epidermal and intraepithelial (adnexal {33759218}) infiltrates can be subtle, negligible, or exuberant and pagetoid {1827136}. Angiotropism is occasionally observed. Dermal involvement may be nodular/perivascular or diffuse. Subcutaneous extension involves lobular and septal sites, with rimming of adipocytes, apoptosis, and karyorrhexis. Additional cytotoxic tissue alteration includes vacuolar interface alteration at the dermoepidermal junction, coagulative necrosis, and angioinvasion.

Immunophenotype

Tumour cells are positive for TCRγ and TCRδ, and negative for TCRβ. A CD3+, CD2+, CD7+/−, CD5− immunophenotype {17934071} with expression of at least one cytotoxic protein, including granzyme B, perforin, and/or TIA1, is typical. The majority of cases are negative for both CD4 and CD8, although CD8 positivity may be seen. CD56 expression is present in almost half of PCGD-TCL cases {17934071}. CD30 is expressed in a minority of cases. EBV is negative.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes, in particular, gamma-delta lymphoproliferative cutaneous disorders (e.g. gamma-delta subtypes of mycosis fungoides and lymphomatoid papulosis), as well as lupus profundus.

Cytology

FNAB is not recommended in this diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of clonal TR gene rearrangement can be helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: monoclonal proliferation of CD3+, TCRγδ+ T cells in the skin or subcutis; exclusion of other lymphomas, such as lymphomatoid papulosis and/or classic mycosis fungoides; EBV negative.

Desirable: CD4−/CD8− or CD4−/CD8+ phenotype; expression of at least one cytotoxic marker (TIA1, granzyme B, perforin); no extracutaneous disease at diagnosis (may evolve later).

Staging

PCGD-TCL is staged according to the TNM classification system for primary cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides and Sézary syndrome {17540844}.

Prognosis and prediction

PCGD-TCLs are generally aggressive lymphomas, with a median survival of < 2 years. Decreased survival is described in patients with histological evidence of panniculitic rather than superficial involvement {12522013; 27879514; 32286303}. Haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, metastatic disease, and infections are associated with poor survival {23073324}. PCGD-TCL responds poorly to chemotherapies. Allogeneic transplantation may provide the best option for prolonged remission {25981001; 32643494; 31709579}.
