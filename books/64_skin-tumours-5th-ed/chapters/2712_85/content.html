2.7.1.2 Proliferative nodules in congenital melanocytic naevus

Definition

Proliferative nodules (PNs) are benign nodular proliferations of melanocytes arising in medium or giant congenital naevi (GCNs).

ICD-O coding

8762/1 Proliferative nodules in congenital melanocytic naevus

ICD-11 coding

2F20.2 & XH6AH3 Congenital melanocytic naevus & Proliferative dermal lesion in congenital naevus

Related terminology

Acceptable: expansile and cellular nodules; atypical proliferative nodules.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PNs occur in the same distribution as GCNs, particularly the trunk {25517953}.

Clinical features

PNs are pink, brown, blue, or black papules, plaques, or nodules within a congenital naevus. They are typically < 20 mm in size and can be ulcerated {25517953}. They may be present at birth or arise later and show rapid growth {27486690}.

Epidemiology

PNs arise in 3–19% of GCNs {21923752; 22671135; 1593350}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Secondary genomic aberrations such as mutations, chromosomal rearrangements, or copy-number changes in a congenital naevus lead to expansion of a new clone {25517953; 27486690; 27849631; 12368190; 26763461}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Within the dermal or subcutaneous portion of a congenital naevus there is an expansile proliferation of melanocytes with spitzoid, small blue round cell, epithelioid, or spindle morphology {25517953}. The morphological transition from the surrounding naevus cells typically is gradual with intermediate cellular morphologies, but it can be abrupt {14690460}. The mitotic count is typically < 2 mitoses/mm2, and cells lack high-grade nuclear atypia. Cases with small blue round cell morphology may have more mitoses.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunomarkers for melanocytes, such as melan-A, tyrosinase, and SOX10, are expressed in PNs. Loss of p16 or H3 p.K27me3 expression and increased labelling with Ki-67 raise concern for melanoma.

Differential diagnosis

Criteria favouring melanoma are sharply demarcated, expansive nodules; necrosis; nuclear enlargement; pleomorphism; hyperchromasia; prominent nucleoli; numerous mitotic figures; and atypical mitoses {14690460}. Both melanoma arising in a congenital naevus and PNs may express PRAME, so its usefulness is unclear.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Gains or losses of entire chromosomes are seen in PNs, whereas melanomas tend to have segmental copy-number aberrations {12368190}. Mutations or epigenetic modification of the TERT promoter favour a diagnosis of melanoma {26763461}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a benign expansile clone of melanocytes within a GCN.

Desirable: normal expression of H3 p.K27me3.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

There is currently no evidence that PNs progress to melanoma, and nodules tend to spontaneously stop growing and often regress. Local recurrences may occur, showing greater cytological atypia.
