5.2.1.3 Dermatofibroma (fibrous histiocytoma)

Definition

Dermatofibroma (fibrous histiocytoma) is a common benign papular or nodular skin lesion composed of a combination of (myo)fibroblasts with surrounding reactive dendrocytes and macrophages in between collagen bundles.

Related terminology

Acceptable: benign fibrous histiocytoma.

Not recommended: fibroma durum; subepidermal nodular fibrosis or sclerosis; sclerosing haemangioma, histiocytoma (cutis); common dermatofibroma.

Subtype(s)

Classic dermatofibroma; cellular dermatofibroma; aneurysmal dermatofibroma; atypical dermatofibroma; deep fibrous histiocytoma

Localization

The classic dermatofibroma is seen most frequently on the legs, followed by the arms and trunk. Rare sites of involvement include the hands, feet, and head and neck {2171878; 19054841; 33893133}.

Clinical features

Dermatofibroma presents as an isolated, asymptomatic, dome-shaped papule or nodule that usually measures from a few millimetres to 10–20 mm; very rarely, it reaches 100 mm (giant dermatofibroma). It is hyperpigmented, but in early growing lesions and in individuals with lightly pigmented skin it may appear erythematous. Classic dermatofibroma usually exhibits a dusky brown colour. The aneurysmal subtype may show a red cut surface, whereas tumours with abundant lipid may be tan to yellow. It is firm and, when squeezed, exhibits a central dimple (“dimple sign”). Dermatofibroma can be eroded or crusted. Occasionally, numerous dermatofibromas in a widespread distribution or in an agminated pattern can be seen. The latter has no clinical significance. Eruptive dermatofibromas can be associated with autoimmune disorders (e.g. lupus erythematosus) {16334865} or in the setting of immunosuppression (e.g. HIV, immunosuppressive drugs) {31625625}.

Epidemiology

Dermatofibroma is the most common mesenchymal neoplasm in the skin. It arises over a wide age range but is most common in the third and fourth decades of life. The classic subtype is seen most frequently in the lower extremities of younger female patients. The other subtypes have a more equal sex distribution.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Recurrent gene fusions involving PKC isoforms (PRKCA, PRKCB, and PRKCD) with various genes encoding membrane-associated proteins (e.g. LAMTOR1, PDPN, CD63, and KIRREL family genes) have been identified in a minority of cases {26121314; 24721208; 30094855; 26432191}. As a result, the catalytic domain of PKC in the chimeric protein localizes to the cell membrane {24721208}. These data provide proof of the neoplastic, clonal nature of dermatofibroma.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

The tumour grows in the reticular dermis, but superficial infiltration of the subcutaneous tissue, along with perivascular lymphocytic inflammation, is frequent. Classic dermatofibroma is polymorphous, composed of spindled to rounded neoplastic cells with surrounding reactive dermal dendrocytes and macrophages {20520936}. There is characteristic, albeit nonspecific, entrapment of collagen bundles at the periphery, forming collagen balls. A storiform growth pattern may be seen. The epidermis typically exhibits hyperplasia with elongated hyperpigmented rete ridges. Follicular and/or sebaceous gland induction and junctional melanocytic hyperplasia may be present {32767564; 19955877; 25727075}. Early lesions are more cellular, whereas older lesions appear more fibrotic with less macrophages. Numerous histological patterns have been described in the literature (fibrocollagenous, lipidized, clear cell, granular cell, halo, osteoclastic, myofibroblastic, keloidal, palisading, and atrophic, among others) {24937822; 10770432; 22576077; 10231403; 9354896}, which have no clinical significance, unlike the subtypes described below; however, one or more patterns or subtypes can overlap in the same tumour. In tumours that are significantly lipidized, the histiocytoid and giant cells contain abundant vacuolated cytoplasm {10770432}.

Cellular dermatofibroma exhibits a fascicular growth pattern, composed of relatively monotonous spindle cells with fine chromatin and pale eosinophilic cytoplasm {30741794; 31074502}. Entrapment of superficial subcutaneous tissue is common, whereas approximately 10% of cases exhibit small foci of necrosis, which is not indicative of malignancy {8017561}.

Aneurysmal dermatofibroma contains blood-filled spaces and haemosiderin occupying much of the lesion and mimicking a vascular tumour. It is polymorphous in composition, like the classic subtype, containing neoplastic spindle cells, Touton-type giant cells, lymphocytes, and macrophages {30868503; 7607620}. Overlying epidermal hyperplasia and peripheral entrapment of collagen bundles is characteristic.

Atypical dermatofibroma contains scattered atypical and pleomorphic cells. Atypical mitoses and necrosis can be seen {11756767}. The background cells can be uniform or polymorphous, like in cellular or classic dermatofibroma, respectively.

Deep fibrous histiocytoma arises entirely within the subcutaneous or deep soft tissue. It is well circumscribed, in contrast to its superficial counterparts, with a uniform cellularity and storiform growth pattern {2167613; 20804915; 18300816}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry is not particularly helpful in reaching the correct diagnosis. CD68 highlights interspersed histiocytes. Multifocal staining for SMA (more pronounced in the cellular subtype) supports myofibroblastic differentiation. Desmin and CD34 can be focally positive (in 30% and 5% of cases, respectively) {22775584}. Factor XIIIa is not a specific stain, highlighting interspersed reactive dendrocytes.

Differential diagnosis

Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans infiltrates the subcutaneous tissue in a honeycomb fashion, is diffusely positive for CD34, and harbours PDGFB/PDGFD rearrangements. Angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma is an unrelated tumour of intermediate malignancy and should not be confused with the aneurysmal subtype. Superficial pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma (PMHE), spindle cell melanoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and superficial leiomyosarcoma are all in the differential with cellular dermatofibroma. They can be distinguished easily with the help of immunohistochemical studies. In approximately half of PMHE cases, interspersed neutrophils can serve as a helpful clue. PMHE expresses keratin and vascular markers (most consistently ERG) and harbours FOSB rearrangements with various partners (most commonly WWTR1 and ACTB). FOSB immunohistochemical stain can serve as a surrogate marker. Melanoma is positive for SOX10, squamous cell carcinoma for keratin markers, and leiomyosarcoma for desmin and caldesmon. Atypical dermatofibroma can be potentially confused with atypical fibroxanthoma / pleomorphic dermal sarcoma. Background findings of classic dermatofibroma would be critical to secure the correct diagnosis.

Epithelioid fibrous histiocytoma (see section Epithelioid fibrous histiocytoma) is now considered a separate entity with characteristic ALK protein overexpression due to ALK rearrangements or, very rarely, due to alternative transcription activation mechanisms {35289445}. Nevertheless, fibrous histiocytomas with epithelioid features and ALK negativity have occasionally been found to harbour PRKCB rearrangements {31653978}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ovoid to spindled cells; peripheral entrapment of dermal collagen bundles.

Desirable: epidermal hyperplasia; follicular/sebaceous induction.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Dermatofibroma is a benign neoplasm with a rate of local, non-destructive recurrence of < 5%. The cellular, aneurysmal, and atypical subtypes have a higher rate of local recurrence (~20%) {8017561; 11756767; 7607620}. Extraordinarily rarely, dermatofibromas (mainly of the cellular subtype) with metastases to lymph nodes and/or lung have been reported {23426120}. Some data suggest that metastasizing tumours have more chromosomal aberrations than the common indolent dermatofibroma {22996372}. Deep fibrous histiocytoma was reported to metastasize in 5% of cases in a series of 69 cases, although this probably represents referral bias {18300816} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]].
