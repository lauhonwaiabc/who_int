4.3.2.1 Mantle cell lymphoma

Definition

Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) is a neoplasm of small to medium-sized monomorphic B cells typically expressing CD5, cyclin D1, and SOX11; CCND1 translocation is present in > 95% of cases, leading to aberrant expression of cyclin D1.

ICD-O coding

9673/3 Mantle cell lymphoma

9673/3 Cyclin D1–positive mantle cell lymphoma

9673/3 Cyclin D1–negative mantle cell lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A85.5 Mantle cell lymphoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: mantle zone lymphoma; centrocytic lymphoma; malignant lymphoma, lymphocytic intermediate differentiation, diffuse.

Subtype(s)

Morphological subtypes: see #38136Table 6.10 .

Molecular subtypes: cyclin D1–positive mantle cell lymphoma; cyclin D1–negative mantle cell lymphoma.

Localization

In the skin, lesions (usually multiple) involve the trunk, head and neck, and extremities {12360046; 26034867; 20010283; 24733805; 31219818}.

Clinical features

MCL involves skin at presentation or relapse, typically with widespread extracutaneous disease {12360046; 26034867; 25724714; 23928453}. There is insufficient support for the existence of MCL as a primary tumour of the skin {34343606; 27539965; 12360046; 24733805; 23612032; 19461249}. Lesions are usually nodules but may manifest as infiltrated plaques or macular lesions {12360046; 26034867; 23928453; 20010283}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 2–12% of cases of MCL involve the skin {26034867; 31219818; 25724714; 9697885}. Cutaneous involvement is more common in men and older adults (median age: > 60 years) {26034867; 23928453; 26762898}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The genetic hallmark of MCL is t(11;14)(q13;q32), present in ≥ 95% of cases {10329598; 2002419}. This translocation juxtaposes CCND1 at 11q13 to IGH on chromosome 14q32 and results in overexpression of cyclin D1 {7670110; 7753558}. Activation of the cyclin D1–dependent kinase pathway overcomes the cell-cycle suppressive effects of RB1 and p27 {8623927; 12515730}. MYC translocations and TP53 mutations are often associated with disease progression {32273477; 8605352}. The neural transcription factor SOX11 also plays an important oncogenic role in MCL {22790016; 22058203}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

MCL produces a nodular, diffuse, or perivascular and periadnexal dermal infiltrate separated from the epidermis by a grenz zone. The tumour may extend into the subcutaneous fat {23928453}. Classic MCL has small to medium-sized cells with slightly irregular nuclear contours, inconspicuous nucleoli, and scant cytoplasm. Most cases of MCL involving the skin present with a blastoid or pleomorphic cytology {26034867; 31219818}. The blastoid subtype shows medium-sized cells with dispersed chromatin, frequent mitoses, and Ki-67 > 40% {12360046; 23928453; 23612032; 19461249; 26034867}. The pleomorphic subtype has larger, pleomorphic nuclei {24733805}.

Immunohistochemistry

MCL is typically CD20+, CD5+, CD10−, BCL6−, BCL2+, CD23−, cyclin D1+, and SOX11+ {12360046; 26034867; 26762898; 11801782; 19461249}. Occasionally, MCL is CD5− {23928453; 20010283; 11801782; 12360046}, CD10+ {26034867; 23612032}, CD23+ {9697885; 9058729}, or (infrequently) cyclin D1− or SOX11− {26762898}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes other B-cell neoplasms. Importantly, plasma cell myeloma may also have CCND1 rearrangements and express cyclin D1. Since cutaneous MCL is often the blastoid or pleomorphic subtype, it may mimic diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and lymphoblastic lymphoma {26034867}.

Cytology

Not relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In the context of the appropriate morphology and immunophenotype, demonstration of uniform nuclear overexpression of cyclin D1 is sufficient to confirm the diagnosis {15166673; 16203276; 16280660}. If the morphology or the immunophenotype are not diagnostic, demonstration of CCND1 rearrangement or CCND1:IGH translocation and an extended immunohistochemistry panel is recommended. Common secondary changes include deletion and/or mutation of ATM and TP53, deletion of CDKN2A {21945515}, and abnormalities of MYC {26517511}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lymphoma cells of B-cell lineage (CD20+ and usually CD5+); cyclin D1 positivity and/or detection of CCND1 rearrangement, almost always (rare cases are negative for cyclin D1 and CCND1 rearrangement).

Desirable: positive SOX11 expression (if cyclin D1 is negative, SOX11 staining is essential); expression of monotypic light chain.

Staging

Staging is performed according to the Lugano criteria {25113753}.

Prognosis and prediction

In general, the prognosis of MCL is worse with older patient age, blastoid and pleomorphic subtypes, and high proliferation index {25724714; 9697885; 21945515; 9058729}. TP53 mutation analysis may help to define a high-risk MCL cohort {26926679; 29196411}. Detailed guidelines for Ki-67 and TP53 assessment in MCL have been established {29196411; 19669190; 30132080; 31975037}.

Subtype

Morphology

Blastoid

Cells resemble lymphoblasts (precursor cells) with round nuclei, fine chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli, and a narrow rim of cytoplasm

Pleomorphic

Cells are variable in size but mostly large, with irregular nuclear contours, prominent nucleoli, and prominent (often pale) cytoplasm

Small cell

Small cells with dense chromatin, round nuclei, and a narrow rim of cytoplasm, resembling chronic lymphocytic leukaemia cells; no paraimmunoblasts

Marginal zone–like

Medium-sized cells with abundant pale cytoplasm resembling marginal zone cells and monocytoid B cells
