3.1.1.5 Hidradenoma

Definition

Hidradenoma is a benign solid and cystic sweat gland neoplasm with ductal and glandular differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8402/0 Hidradenoma

ICD-11 coding

2F22 & XH4MV7 Benign neoplasms of epidermal appendages & Hidradenoma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: clear cell hidradenoma; solid-cystic hidradenoma; nodular hidradenoma.

Not recommended: eccrine acrospiroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The anatomical distribution is wide, with a predilection for the trunk and limbs.

Clinical features

Hidradenoma is an asymptomatic, solitary, firm nodule, usually < 20–30 mm in diameter, although it can be > 100 mm {2172332}. Occasionally, lesions may be ulcerated {2545168}.

Epidemiology

Hidradenoma can affect people of any age, both male and female {9832062}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Hidradenomas harbour a t(11;19) translocation, which results in the fusion of CRTC1 (MECT1) at 19p13.1 with MAML2 at 11q21. The CRTC1::MAML2 fusion gene is not exclusive to hidradenoma; it has also been identified in salivary gland mucoepidermoid carcinoma. This translocation leads to aberrant activation of downstream cAMP/CREB signalling genes {16670462; 17334997}. Half to 75% of the cases of hidradenoma have CRTC1::MAML2 fusion, and CRTC3::MAML2 fusions occur more rarely {28295544}.

Macroscopic appearance

See Clinical features, above.

Histopathology

Hidradenomas are well-circumscribed dermal neoplasms that may extend into the subcutaneous tissue. Most hidradenomas are solid lobular proliferations, but some contain large cystic spaces filled with homogeneous eosinophilic material. The majority of hidradenomas are composed of pale or clear cells (hence the synonym “clear cell hidradenoma”). However, other cell types, including polygonal, mucinous, squamoid, oxyphilic/oncocytic, and epidermoid cells, can also be present. The proportion of various cell types and their combinations vary from case to case. The characteristic cytoplasmic clarity is due to abundant intracellular glycogen. The nuclei are small, round, and (usually) eccentrically located. In some examples of hidradenoma, polygonal and epidermoid cells are the most prominent component. Mucinous cells usually appear in small groups and can be recognized by the presence of ample granular bluish cytoplasm. When polygonal cells with ample eosinophilic cytoplasm predominate, hidradenomas have an appearance reminiscent of oncocytoma {8806968}. Squamoid cells have abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and are separated by well-defined intercellular bridges; some may keratinize individually and appear as dyskeratotic keratinocytes. Areas of ductal and glandular differentiation are usually present, even in solid neoplasms. These structures are lined by cuboidal cells that elaborate an eosinophilic luminal cuticle. In some cases, glandular structures lined by columnar cells with evidence of secretion by decapitation in the luminal border are also seen. Myoepithelial cells are not present in ductal or glandular structures of hidradenoma. The stroma in hidradenoma is characteristically hyalinized, separating epithelial islands; in lesions of long evolution, this hyalinization may be very marked, leaving only small aggregates of neoplastic cells. Focal cytological atypia can be encountered (hidradenoma with atypical features) {19252473}.

Immunostaining for EMA and CEA highlights ductal differentiation; p40, p63, CK5/6, and AE1/AE3 are strongly and diffusely expressed, whereas S100 and SMA are usually negative {31876586}.

Differential diagnosis

Poroid hidradenoma has two cell types (poroid and cuticular), both of which are very different from the cells of hidradenoma. In poroid hidradenoma, tubular structures are only seen in the areas of cuticular cells. Like other poromas, poroid hidradenoma frequently shows areas of necrosis en masse. Trichilemmomas consist of pale or clear cells showing signs of trichilemmal differentiation; they often expand the follicular infundibulum and exhibit peripheral palisading surrounded by a thick and prominent basement membrane. In contrast, hidradenoma can connect with pre-existing infundibular structures, but the neoplastic epithelial islands form solid aggregates in the dermis and subcutis. Hidradenomas with a predominantly solid pattern must be distinguished from glomus tumour that shows SMA positivity {1380207}. Hidradenoma must also be distinguished from a cutaneous metastasis of renal carcinoma, which is characterized by an abundant blood supply, numerous atypical mitoses and areas of necrosis (of both individual neoplastic cells and groups of cells), and extracellular blood lakes. A combination of PAX8 and CAIX staining may be used to solve the differential diagnosis.

A particular diagnostic pitfall is posed by cases of histologically benign hidradenomas with lymphatic involvement or evidence of a tumour in a regional lymph node diagnosed simultaneously with the cutaneous primary {23095508; 27080562; 33464750}. Histopathologically, such tumours are well circumscribed and show a lobular and cystic architecture, ductal differentiation, and a hyalinized stroma. There is no significant cytological atypia. Mitoses are rare and necrosis is absent. The lymph nodes show an essentially identical proliferation, with more cellular and extensive cystic changes containing pink amorphous material {33464750}. Because follow-up of such cases has not revealed any adverse outcomes, it has been suggested that these tumours constitute so-called benign metastases. However, a longer follow-up and more cases are needed to prove or disprove this theory {23095508; 27080562}. Complete excision is recommended.

Cytology

FNAB shows amorphous background material with epithelial duct-like cells, foam cells, and tubular structures. There may be a biphasic cytoplasmic staining pattern (eosinophilic and clear/basophilic cells) in Pap-stained sections {19365971}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a well-circumscribed neoplasm with a lobular and solid/cystic growth pattern; histologically, a mixture of clear cells, polygonal eosinophilic cells, mucinous cells, squamoid cells, and ductal structures; the stroma is characteristically hyalinized.

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Hidradenoma is a benign neoplasm. Exceptional cases of benign-looking hidradenomas may present with lymphatic invasion or single regional lymph node deposits; limited follow-up suggests that this is a benign phenomenon {23095508}.
