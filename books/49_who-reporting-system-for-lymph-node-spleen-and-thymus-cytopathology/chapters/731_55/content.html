7.3.1 Sample reports: Suspicious for malignancy

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format will depend on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and ROSE are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as “Diagnostic summary” (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Specimen type:

Ultrasound-guided FNAB axillary node

Clinical and imaging information:

F 58 with eccentric cortical thickening of several axillary nodes and a recent diagnosis of ipsilateral breast carcinoma

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: SUSPICIOUS FOR MALIGNANCY

Diagnosis: Suspicious for metastatic carcinoma. See report below.

 

Macroscopic description:

Three air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings prepared for cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These mildly cellular smears show a dispersed lymphoid population with admixed very occasional dispersed single cells with mildly enlarged, mildly atypical nuclei and eccentric pale cytoplasm. A few intracytoplasmic vacuoles are seen. There are no tubular structures.

There is no diagnostic material in the cell block.

 

Comment: A very small number of mildly atypical epithelial cells are present. In the setting of a concurrent history of breast carcinoma, the features are suspicious for malignancy, but there are insufficient atypical cells present for a definitive diagnosis of malignancy. Repeat FNAB with or without CNB is recommended.

Sample report 2

Specimen type:

FNAB enlarged axillary node

Clinical and imaging information:

F 68 with three enlarged axillary nodes with lobulated outlines and a recent diagnosis of ipsilateral lobular carcinoma of the breast

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: SUSPICIOUS FOR MALIGNANCY

Diagnosis: Suspicious for lymphoma

 

Macroscopic description:

Four air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings prepared as cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show a dispersed, relatively monotonous population of small lymphoid cells with minimally pleomorphic round nuclei and scant, concentric cytoplasm. There are scattered background lymphoid cytoplasmic fragments. No germinal-centre tissue fragments or granulomas are seen. No cohesive tissue fragments of atypical cells are seen, and no intracytoplasmic lumina are present in the cells.

There is no diagnostic material in the cell block. No flow cytometry is available.

 

Comment: A dispersed monotonous lymphoid population is present, suspicious for lymphoma, in particular chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma. Metastatic lobular carcinoma is considered less likely. Correlation with clinical, imaging, and haematological findings is required. Repeat FNAB with flow cytometry with or without CNB is recommended.

Sample report 3

Specimen type:

FNAB cervical node

Clinical and imaging information:

F 28 with multiple enlarged cervical nodes, loss of weight, and B symptoms

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: SUSPICIOUS FOR MALIGNANCY

Diagnosis: See report below.

 

Macroscopic description:

Four air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings centrifuged for cell block and a separate pass committed to flow cytometry.

 

Microscopic description:

These mildly cellular smears show a mixed population of small lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils, and histiocytes in addition to occasional atypical cells with one or two enlarged nuclei and prominent nucleoli, resembling Hodgkin or Reed–Sternberg cells.

Very occasional atypical cells are present in the cell block, which are immunoreactive with antibodies to CD30, but the CD15 is equivocal and there is no staining for MUM1 and weak staining for CD20. The flow cytometry is negative for a monoclonal B-cell population.

 

Comment: Large atypical lymphoid cells resembling Reed–Sternberg cells are present in a polymorphous background, and the features are suspicious for Hodgkin lymphoma. There is limited material in the cell block for immunocytochemistry, which is inconclusive. Excision biopsy is recommended.
