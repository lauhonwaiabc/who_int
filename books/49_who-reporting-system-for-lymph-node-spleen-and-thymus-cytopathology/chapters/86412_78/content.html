8.6.4.12 T-cell/histiocyte-rich large B-cell lymphoma

Definition

T-cell/histiocyte–rich large B-cell lymphoma (THRLBCL) is an aggressive B-cell lymphoma with < 10% large neoplastic B cells, scattered in a diffuse background rich in T cells and histiocytes, with a virtual absence of small B cells.

Clinical features and imaging

A subset of cases show marked clinical, immunophenotypic, and molecular overlap with nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL).

Patients are typically middle-aged and present with B symptoms, splenomegaly, and/or hepatomegaly, often with an elevated serum LDH. Advanced Ann Arbor stage (III–IV) is common at diagnosis, and the disease follows a heterogeneous clinical course {11870169}. On imaging studies, in contrast to NLPHL, which is frequently localized, THRLBCL commonly manifests as a systemic disease.

PET shows a maximum standardized uptake value similar to that of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS (DLBCL-NOS), higher than that observed in NLPHL {32022621; 23773450}. Imaging studies are very helpful to select the optimal target sites for cytopathological FNAB as a first diagnostic procedure.

Histopathology

THRLBCL has a diffuse growth pattern with effacement of the lymph node architecture {11903596}. Large neoplastic B cells are singly and diffusely scattered in a dense reactive background, without confluence in aggregates or sheets. The cytopathological spectrum of the tumour B cells is broad, and they may resemble the lymphocyte-predominant (LP) cells of NLPHL, or centroblasts, immunoblasts, or Hodgkin cells (HCs) or Reed–Sternberg cells (RSCs) {11870169; 12409722; 22081105}. The background is made up of bland histiocytes and numerous small T lymphocytes. There is no granuloma formation. Small B cells, eosinophils, or plasma cells are absent to sparse, and follicular dendritic reticulum cell meshworks are absent {23686423}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

High cellularity Predominantly small lymphocytes mixed with histiocytes with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm Scattered large atypical lymphoid cells constituting < 10% of cells present, may resemble the large LP cells (“popcorn cells”) of NLPHL with lobulated nuclei, or the large centroblast or immunoblast-like cells of large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL), or HCs and RSCs characterized by large single or binucleated cells with abundant cytoplasm and large nuclei with prominent nucleoli Starry-sky macrophages, plasma cells, and numerous eosinophils are typically absent.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The diagnosis of THRLBCL is challenging and largely relies on the histopathological, architectural, and immunophenotypic features of both tumour cells and the microenvironment. Therefore, a definite diagnosis of THRLBCL cannot be made on cytopathology alone, not even with extensive flow cytometry and/or molecular analysis. A tissue sample, preferably an excision biopsy, is required together with complete immunophenotypic characterization to reliably exclude other reactive processes and lymphoproliferative disorders.

Reactive lymphoid hyperplasia is a common differential diagnosis due to the very small number of neoplastic B cells present, and other differential diagnoses include LBCL, NLPHL, classic Hodgkin lymphoma, EBV-positive DLBCL, and peripheral T-cell lymphoma. In FNAB, the presence of predominantly large neoplastic cells favours a diagnosis of LBCL, including DLBCL-NOS, and excludes a diagnosis of THRLBCL. The differentiation between THRLBCL and NLPHL is challenging, particularly for the pattern E type of NLPHL, which may show a complete histomorphological overlap with THRLBCL, including the immune microenvironment, precluding a differential diagnosis by cytopathology, even in conjunction with immunocytochemistry and flow cytometry. Given the substantial differences in therapeutic options, histopathological sampling is key (preferably excision biopsy). THRLBCL may mimic classic Hodgkin lymphoma, particularly the lymphocyte-rich, mixed cellularity, and lymphocyte-depleted subtypes, which contain abundant small lymphocytes and/or histiocytes in the background. The presence of numerous eosinophils makes a diagnosis of THRLBCL less likely and may raise the possibility of a peripheral T-cell lymphoma. Complete immunophenotyping is required for a reliable diagnosis of these differential diagnoses, and excision biopsy is required for THRLBCL.

EBV-positive DLBCL in and outside immunodeficiency settings can be cytomorphologically indistinguishable from THRLBCL. Although EBV has been described in the past in a subset of THRLBCL cases that are positive for LMP1 and EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) {12409722; 12207783}, currently these cases should be classified as EBV-positive DLBCL, and underlying immunodeficiencies should be investigated {30082493}.

The T-cell–rich background is not uncommonly misinterpreted as neoplastic, leading to a diagnosis of T-cell lymphoma, especially T-cell lymphomas with a predominantly small cell pattern, such as nodal T follicular helper cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type. Special attention should be given to the cytomorphological atypia of the T cells and aberrant phenotype by flow cytometry to diagnose a T-cell lymphoma.

Ancillary testing

Immunocytochemistry on cytospins or cell blocks using pan–B-cell markers is recommended, as is flow cytometry.

The large tumour cells are uniformly positive for pan–B-cell markers and the transcription factors CD20, CD79a, PAX5, and OCT2, as well as for CD45 and MEF2B {28851661}. These cells are also positive for the germinal-centre B-cell marker BCL6 and the activated B-cell marker MUM1/IRF4. Expression of EMA and CD30 is variable {12409722; 11903596}, CD10 is infrequent, and CD15 and EBV are absent.

The tumour microenvironment is characterized by CD68+ and CD163+ histiocytes and CD3+/CD5+ T lymphocytes that do not form rosettes around large B cells. Characteristically, there is a virtual absence of small B cells, which are typically < 5%.

Flow cytometry shows abundant T cells and a distinct small subpopulation of large neoplastic B cells expressing CD19, bright CD20 and CD40, and BCL6, and negative or dim CD10, with a paucity of reactive B cells. The range of the CD4:CD8 ratio in THRLBCL is very wide, precluding the ratio having a significant role as a diagnostic parameter {26084540; 28575178}. Because immunoglobulin expression is generally not demonstrable by flow cytometry, light chain restriction is not suitable to demonstrate a clonal population {31254446}.

Demonstration of molecular monoclonality may be supportive of the diagnosis, but because of the low percentage of tumour cells, signals may be below the detection level of commonly used assays and negative results do not exclude the diagnosis.
