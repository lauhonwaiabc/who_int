5.2.3.2 Vascular lesions

Definition

Hamartoma, littoral cell angioma, and sclerosing angiomatoid nodular transformation (SANT) are vascular tumours unique to the spleen.

Clinical features and imaging

Patients usually present with incidental imaging findings {15256634; 23675122}, or with symptoms related to mass effects or hypersplenism {15256634; 23675122; 1928554; 27374010; 26339427; 21929754; 25705528; 34333257; 33088569; 33194866}. Some patients with littoral cell angioma have concurrent epithelial or haematological malignancies {27374010; 9862523; 21929754; 25705528; 34333257; 33088569; 33194866; 7199130}. The differential diagnosis on ultrasound and CT includes cystic lesions of spleen, including acquired cysts such as hydatid cysts.

The common imaging presentation for hamartoma and SANT is a solitary, well-circumscribed mass {29528823; 15897756}. SANT may also present with a “spoke-wheel pattern”, with a relatively hypovascular centre with an enhancing rim and radiating vascularized tissue penetrating from the periphery towards the centre of the lesion {18607127}. Multiple lesions are typical for littoral cell angioma {15256634}.

Histopathology

The histopathological finding shared by these vascular lesions is the presence of slit-like, ectatic, or irregularly shaped vascular channels lined by plump or spindle endothelial cells. No or minimal cytopathological atypia or mitotic activity is found. SANT often demonstrates a multinodular growth pattern with fibrotic or fibromyxoid stroma, with or without inflammatory cells {23991745}. The vascular channels of littoral cell angioma can be cystic with papillary fronds protruding into the lumina. The neoplastic cells may show haemophagocytosis, haemosiderin, or eosinophilic hyaline globules {9862523; 7510515}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Cellular smears Variable number of large tissue fragments composed of a fibrous stroma interspersed with anastomosing vascular channels lined by plump endothelial cells Endothelial cells have ovoid to spindle nuclei, evenly distributed chromatin, indistinct nucleoli, and scant indistinct cytoplasm. Similar spindle cells may be seen singly. Bloody and mixed inflammatory background Mitotic figures, nuclear atypia, and necrosis are notably absent.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

These vascular lesions have similar cytopathological features. The differential diagnosis includes haemangiomas, angiosarcomas, and metastases. Haemangioma consists of scant tissue fragments consisting of varying-sized vascular spaces lined by endothelial cells in a haemorrhagic background {2227626}. Angiosarcomas yield cellular FNAB smears consisting of spindle or epithelioid cells singly, in small cohesive tissue fragments or large papillary tissue fragments. Individual cells exhibit mild to marked nuclear pleomorphism and frequent mitoses. Intracytoplasmic lumina and rosette-like microacinar formation may be present {16048401; 29643660}. The benign vascular lesions lack cellular atypia, necrosis, and mitoses, allowing differentiation from angiosarcoma and metastases, but the distinction can be difficult if not impossible in some cases {9143823; 18181188}. The standard management is splenectomy {21488173; 23675122}.

Ancillary testing

Immunocytochemistry highlights the endothelial origins of these entities. The endothelial cells express CD31 and factor VIII-RA. CD34 positivity is noted in both SANT and hamartoma but not in littoral cell angioma {32674844}. CD8, a T-cell lymphoid marker, is positive in SANT, hamartoma, and littoral cells of the normal spleen but not in littoral cell angioma or other splenic vascular tumours including haemangioma and angiosarcoma {32674844}. The endothelial cells of littoral cell angioma express CD21, CD68, and other histiocytic markers.
