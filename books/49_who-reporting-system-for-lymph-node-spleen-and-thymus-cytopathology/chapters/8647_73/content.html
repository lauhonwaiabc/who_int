8.6.4.7 Splenic neoplasms: Introduction

Nodules, masses, and diffuse enlargements of the spleen have many causes, including cysts, vascular lesions, inflammatory and infectious processes, primary and secondary lymphomas, and metastases. In most of the cases, imaging, clinical, and serological data are diagnostic, but there are still specific conditions or equivocal clinical presentations in which a direct evaluation of nodular or diffuse splenomegaly may be useful. FNAB of the spleen was first used for the diagnosis of leishmaniasis by Aravandinos in 1916; it was then extensively used in the 1970s and later at the Karolinska Hospital in Stockholm {1251762}. Since then, FNAB of the spleen has been reported in series and case reports to diagnose abscesses, cysts, lymphoma, and splenic involvement by haematological pathologies and metastases {36447822; 34546006; 31774255; 28746730; 21953852; 18181188; 17573764; 17115435; 14674062; 12704692; 9143823; 4821038; 1067035; 3243164; 1396029; 8191816; 8629397; 10204100}.

The fear of haemorrhage related to the FNAB and the subsequent development of other non-invasive diagnostic techniques have limited, over time, the widespread use of splenic FNAB. Nonetheless, there are clinical situations in which FNAB may contribute to the diagnosis of nodular or diffuse splenomegaly, allowing splenectomies or other invasive procedures to be avoided. Splenic FNAB should be performed under ultrasound control. When the procedure is performed by expert operators such as interventional radiologists or cytopathologists, complications are rarely reported {1251762; 8191816; 17115435}, provided the FNAB is properly performed. Contraindications include EBV infection/mononucleosis and haemorrhagic diatheses {1251762; 8191816; 17115435}.

The fifth edition of the WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours has four B-cell splenic lymphoma and leukaemia entities, whose cytopathological features are presented in this chapter along with those of splenic large B-cell lymphoma [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

A new entity, splenic B-cell lymphoma/leukaemia with prominent nucleoli (SBLPN), has replaced “hairy cell leukaemia variant” and “CD5-negative B-cell prolymphocytic leukaemia” and is a grouping of rare chronic lymphoid malignancies that require further study, including as to their specific cytopathological features. The shared feature is intermediate to large atypical lymphoid cells resembling the “hairy cells” of hairy cell leukaemia (HCL) due to poorly formed cytoplasmic projections, but featuring prominent single nucleoli reminiscent of prolymphocytes {2364167; 26614904}. The splenic red pulp is diffusely infiltrated, and an intrasinusoidal infiltration is seen in the bone marrow. This group of rare chronic lymphoid malignancies express pan–B-cell markers (CD19, CD20, and CD22), DBA.44, CD11c, CD103, and FMC7, but they are negative for HCL markers including CD25, annexin A1, TRAP, and CD123. SBLPN is more aggressive in clinical course but sensitive to cladribine combined with rituximab or bendamustine {12670464; 16781083; 20558005; 7206776; 18798068; 35178674; 11243388; 17707827}. This entity is not discussed further here, similarly to HCL, which is a mature B-cell neoplasm with distinctive clinicopathological features and BRAF p.V600E (NP_004324.2) somatic mutation in ≥ 95% of cases {28297625}. Other splenic small B-cell lymphomas usually lack BRAF mutations.

Splenic marginal zone lymphoma (SMZL) and splenic diffuse red pulp small B-cell lymphoma (SDRPL) have many clinical, cytomorphological, and immunocytochemical features that overlap and are described in this chapter. Their cytopathology is described in the next section: section Splenic B-cell lymphoma. SDRPL also overlaps with HCL and SBLPN, and assessment of a splenectomy may be required to make these distinctions. However, a CD200:CD180 mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) ratio of < 0.5 on flow cytometry favours a diagnosis of SDRPL over HCL, SMZL, or SBLPN, and bone marrow trephine shows a mainly intrasinusoidal infiltration of cells rather than the variable infiltration seen in SMZL and SBLPN or the diffuse infiltration with reticulin fibrosis of HCL {29659173; 29955146; 26614904}.
