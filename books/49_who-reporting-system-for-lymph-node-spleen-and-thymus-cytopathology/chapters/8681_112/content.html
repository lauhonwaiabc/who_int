8.6.8.1 Histiocytic sarcoma

Definition

Histiocytic sarcoma (HS) is a malignant neoplasm composed of cells showing cytomorphological and immunophenotypic features of histiocytes.

Clinical features and imaging

Acute monocytic leukaemias forming tumours are excluded from HS.

HS is a rare and aggressive neoplasm with variable age distribution, although the majority of reported cases occur in adulthood. It involves lymph nodes and extranodal sites, including the gastrointestinal tract, liver, skin, and soft tissue {29183888}. It can present de novo or arise in the setting of a pre-existing or concurrent haematological malignancy such as acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, follicular lymphoma, and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia {15050956; 18272816; 21666687}. Occasional cases associated with mediastinal germ cell tumours have been reported {28695297}. Common clinical manifestations are solitary masses together with systemic symptoms including fever, weight loss, and hepatosplenomegaly and/or lymphadenopathy. Gastrointestinal cases can cause bowel obstruction. Lytic bone lesions can be present {28695297; 15316312; 12121233; 15696128}. Despite the expression of markers of histiocytic differentiation, molecular profiling has revealed receptor gene rearrangements and translocations similar to those in the concomitant lymphoproliferative disorder, for example IGH::BCL2 rearrangement when associated with follicular lymphoma. This has led to the hypothesis of “transdifferentiation” from a common progenitor cell {18272816}. Similar molecular abnormalities have also been reported in de novo cases, not overtly associated with a haematological malignancy {19145200}.

Histopathology

There is a diffuse proliferation of large cells showing variable pleomorphism, which in lymph nodes shows a sinusoidal pattern, associated with a prominent inflammatory background [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Variable cellularity Large round to oval and occasionally spindle malignant cells are present in variable numbers, dispersed or in tissue fragments, in a background of numerous small lymphocytes, plasma cells, neutrophils, and eosinophils. Nuclei are pleomorphic, round, folded, and kidney- or donut-shaped with fine chromatin and prominent single or multiple nucleoli; giant bizarre nuclei are frequent. Multinucleated cells are frequent. Abundant, sometimes eccentric, eosinophilic (Pap stain) cytoplasm is present, containing fine vacuoles. Necrosis and mitotic figures have been described in some cases, as have haemophagocytosis, emperipolesis, and fragments of cytoplasm (“lymphoglandular bodies”).

Reference(s): {14506679; 19845032; 25993973; 23813724; 28805986}

Discussion and differential diagnosis

HS is a rare malignant neoplasm with a clinically aggressive course, often underrecognized at presentation in FNAB. The dispersed large, sometimes bizarre, cells are easily misinterpreted as poorly differentiated carcinoma, melanoma, high-grade lymphoma, high-grade sarcoma, or dendritic cell neoplasms, especially when presenting at sites where some of these are more common, such as the head and neck region. However, the integration of detailed cytopathological features with immunoprofile and molecular profile can help with reaching the correct diagnosis on FNAB, with or without CNB. Recurrent cases are more easily identifiable in FNAB. Key cytopathological features include cytophagocytosis and lymphoid cell cytoplasmic fragments (“lymphoglandular bodies”) in smears containing dispersed pleomorphic large epithelioid or spindle cells with pleomorphic nuclei {28805986}. Erythrophagocytosis in the malignant population is more easily seen in the FNAB material than in histopathology, where it can be obscured by a dense inflammatory infiltrate.

Another differential diagnosis is blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm, which has been moved in the fifth edition of the WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours to the same category (histiocytic/dendritic cell neoplasms), and the distinction between these two lesions currently relies on immunophenotypic differentiation [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]. The cytopathological features await elucidation.

Ancillary testing

Immunocytochemistry demonstrates the histiocytic/monocytic differentiation. A combination of several markers of histiocytic differentiation, including CD68, CD163, and PU.1 (a macrophage transcription factor), is recommended to mitigate the lack of specificity of classic histiocytic markers such us CD68 and lysozyme {15696128; 21666687}. CD163, CD68 (KP1 and PGM1), and PU.1 are positive in the majority of cases reported, while the expression of lysozyme with a granular cytoplasmic staining is described in one third of cases {28805986}. CD31, CD4, and CD45 are also variably expressed {15316312}. Focal weak expression of S100 can be seen. Follicular dendritic cell markers (CD21, CD23, and CD35), langerin, CD1a, and myeloid markers (CD13, CD33, and MPO) are negative, as are the AE1/AE3 cytokeratin, CD20, CD3, PAX5, CD30, and ALK lymphoid markers, melanocytic markers HMB45 and melan-A, and sarcoma markers CD34 and desmin {28805986}.

Clonal IG rearrangement has been described in a subset of HS, especially in association with lymphoid malignancies {19145200}. Translocation t(14;18) is also reported in cases associated with follicular lymphoma carrying the same structural alteration {18272816}. BRAF p.V600E mutations have been reported in a small percentage of cases {24720374}, and alterations in KMT2D/MLL2 using targeted-exome NGS have been described {28805986}. Whole-exome sequencing shows numerous alterations involving NF1, MAP2K1, PTPN11, BRAF, KRAS, NRAS, and LZTR1, as well as concomitant NF1 and PTPN11 mutations in a subset of gastrointestinal cases, suggesting the possibility of a site-specific molecular subtype of HS {31439678}.
