8.6.8.3 Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma

Definition

Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma (IDCS) is a malignant neoplasm composed of spindle to epithelioid cells showing cytomorphological and immunophenotypic features similar to those of interdigitating dendritic cells.

Clinical features and imaging

IDCS is a rare neoplasm. Most cases present in adulthood, with a median age of 58 years and a male predominance (M:F ratio: 1.65:1) {11293908; 12121233; 14987233; 29660568; 31741033}. Approximately 20% of cases are associated with other malignancies, such as chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL), NK-cell/T-cell lymphoma, and acute myelomonocytic leukaemia {16594749; 21666687; 19926586; 29043241; 26071439; 23605368}. IDCS commonly presents with an asymptomatic nodal mass involving cervical, axillary, or mediastinal lymph nodes {31741033}. About 40% of cases present at extranodal sites, with skin, liver, and lung being most frequently affected {23755890; 22938549; 11914634; 28302385; 22554504; 24396471}. Systemic symptoms including fever, night sweats, and weight loss have been described, and in rare cases generalized lymphadenopathy and hepatosplenomegaly can be present {29660568; 31741033}. Surgical resection is the most common therapeutic intervention and is associated with better outcome and survival than radiotherapy and chemotherapy. The mortality rate at 12 months is about 50%, and the prognosis does not correlate with morphological appearance or different lines of treatments [[Jaffe ES, Arber DA, Campo E, et al., editors. Hematopathology. 2nd ed. Philadelphia (PA): Elsevier; 2016.]] {31741033}.

The frequent association with lymphoid malignancies and the similarity of genetic and molecular profiles support the hypothesis of transdifferentiation {19145200; 30237263}.

Histopathology

Lymph nodes show effacement of the architecture by a proliferation of ovoid and spindle cells forming indistinct fascicles in a storiform pattern. In some cases, sheets of spindle cells are present in a diffuse fashion. Small lymphocytes and plasma cells are present in the background. Residual reactive follicles can be identified. Necrosis is generally absent, and the mitotic rate is usually low and < 5 mitoses per 10 high-power fields [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Variable cellularity Dispersed single round, ovoid, or spindle cells and/or loosely cohesive tissue fragments, which may show interconnecting cytoplasmic processes Nuclei can show folds, grooves, intranuclear cytoplasmic pseudoinclusions and deep convolutions, and single or multiple nucleoli with perinucleolar chromatin clearing (“vesicular”, in Pap stain). Occasional multinucleated cells may be seen. Abundant and eosinophilic (Pap stain) cytoplasm, with irregular, ill-defined processes and cytoplasmic blebbing in some cases Large polygonal cells with high N:C ratio in some cases Small lymphocytes in the background Tissue fragments of neoplastic cells in a whorled pattern can be seen in cell block preparations.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis, which includes other histiocytic/dendritic cell neoplasms such as histiocytic sarcoma, follicular dendritic cell sarcoma, and Langerhans cell histiocytosis, is based on appropriate immunoprofiling and ultrastructural analysis. High-grade lymphomas and melanomas should also be excluded using ancillary techniques. Due to the rarity of the disease, molecular profiling to date is limited to a few case reports.

Ancillary testing

Immunocytochemistry shows markers similar to those of interdigitating dendritic cells found in the lymph node paracortex, i.e. positive for S100 and vimentin with variable weak positivity for CD68, lysozyme, and CD45, while CD163 and PU.1 are negative. IDCSs are consistently negative for the follicular dendritic cell markers CD21, CD23, and CD35, as well as for CD1a, CD34, CD30, MPO, B-cell and T-cell markers, cytokeratins, EMA, desmin, and the melanoma marker HMB45. The Ki-67 proliferative fraction is generally 10–20%. Background small lymphocytes tend to be T cells.

Clonal immunoglobulin receptor gene rearrangement has been described in some cases, particularly in association with lymphoid malignancies, and these cases were otherwise negative for B-cell and T-cell markers {19145200}. Clonally related diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS and IDCS sharing MYC translocation have been reported {30237263}. BRAF p.V600E mutation has occasionally been identified, raising problems in the differential diagnosis with spindle cell amelanotic melanoma {27299798; 26047060; 25787243}. A recent genomic profiling study demonstrated somatic “ultrahypermutation” and tumour heterogeneity, which could help explain the lack of response to chemotherapy and targeted therapy in these neoplasms {30719225}. NF1, TP53, and ARID2 mutations have also been reported in one case using targeted NGS {27680605}.

Electron microscopy shows complex and elongated interdigitating processes, but well-formed desmosomes and Birbeck granules are absent [[Jaffe ES, Arber DA, Campo E, et al., editors. Hematopathology. 2nd ed. Philadelphia (PA): Elsevier; 2016.]].
