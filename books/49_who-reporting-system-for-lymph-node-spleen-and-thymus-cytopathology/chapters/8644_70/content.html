8.6.4.4 Splenic B-cell lymphoma

Definition

Primary splenic B-cell lymphomas (PSLs) are subtypes of non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) and include the primary splenic diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, which in the fifth edition of the WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours has been renamed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS (DLBCL-NOS), and four small B-cell lymphomas: hairy cell leukaemia (HCL), splenic marginal zone lymphoma (SMZL), splenic diffuse red pulp small B-cell lymphoma (SDRPL), and splenic B-cell lymphoma/leukaemia with prominent nucleoli (SBLPN).

Clinical features and imaging

PSLs are generally indolent, arising in adult, asymptomatic patients. Imaging and gross appearance of small cell NHL, either primary or generalized, show homogeneous enlargement without discrete solitary or multiple masses or miliary deposits. When symptomatic, patients may have abdominal pain, fever, weight loss, and elevated ESR, and hypercalcaemia and pancreatitis have been reported {29187463}. Imaging of DLBCL-NOS in the spleen generally shows one large mass or several nodules, and a diffuse splenomegaly may sometimes occur.

Histopathology

DLBCL-NOS may show a white pulp pattern or a red pulp pattern. In the white pulp pattern, lymphomatous cells primarily infiltrate into the white pulp, and the normal histological structure of the spleen is replaced by a diffuse infiltrating nodular pattern. In the red pulp pattern, lymphomatous cells infiltrate the cords of Billroth and the sinusoids, producing a diffuse pattern sparing capillaries and small blood vessels. Involvement of these blood vessels is considered intravascular DLBCL-NOS {18818942}. Microscopic and phenotypical features of DLBCL-NOS are similar to those of nodal counterparts.

Small-celled PSLs mainly involve the red pulp, with the exception of SMZL, which arises in the white pulp. SMZL and SDRPL are characterized by small to medium cells with condensed chromatin.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

As well as primary splenic lymphomas, which account for 1% of all lymphomas, the spleen is involved in approximately 50% of systemic Hodgkin lymphoma and about 30% of NHL cases, particularly DLBCL-NOS, mantle cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, and lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma. Non-invasive imaging techniques have reduced the need for diagnostic splenic biopsies, including FNAB or splenectomies. However, in specific clinical situations, FNAB combined with flow cytometry and/or immunocytochemistry can produce accurate diagnoses {33002340; 8465640; 9143823; 33403241; 21953852; 17115435; 12704692; 14579297}. Splenic FNAB is performed under ultrasound guidance, and despite concerns raised by clinicians, complications are reported only rarely, provided the procedure is properly performed by experienced operators. Generally, one or two passes are advised, and the FNAB must be rapid and gentle. Infectious mononucleosis and haemorrhagic diathesis are contraindications {33002340; 8465640; 9143823; 33403241; 21953852; 17115435; 12704692; 8191816; 14579297}. Endoscopic ultrasound–guided FNAB (EUS-FNAB) of the spleen can also be performed {8465640}. The cytopathological diagnostic criteria are basically those used for lymph node FNAB, although there are often fewer cells available for study than from FNAB of lymph nodes. ROSE can assess the adequacy and the management of residual material. The differential diagnosis includes reactive, diffuse or miliary lymphoid or granulomatous and infectious processes, and in the case of DLBCL-NOS with a single mass or large nodules, metastatic carcinoma or melanoma.

Ancillary testing

Flow cytometry is the technique of choice for small B-cell PSL, but immunocytochemistry can be used on cell blocks {26414904}. The SMZL phenotype is CD19+, CD20+, CD5−, CD10−, CD11c+, CD23−, with light chain restriction {9143823; 21953852; 17115435; 12704692}. A minority of SMZLs express CD5 or CD23, or are negative for CD11c {23325563}. SMZL is typically negative for CD25, CD103, and CD123. HCL has a characteristic immunophenotype of CD25+, CD103+, CD123+, and CD200+, and it is CD10+ in a minority of cases; other markers in HCL are similar to those in SMZL, including expression of CD11c {23347903; 31077558}. By immunocytochemistry, annexin A1 is nearly always positive in HCL, which can also express cyclin D1 {23347903; 11144927}. BRAF p.V600E mutation is highly sensitive and specific for HCL, and it can be detected by molecular methods or immunocytochemistry {28297625}. SBLPN expresses CD103, but it is typically dim for CD200 and negative for CD25, CD123, and annexin A1. Other markers of SBLPN are similar to those of SMZL, although CD11c may not be as bright as in HCL {23347903; 31077558}. By immunocytochemistry, most cases of SDRPL express cyclin D3, which is rarely positive in other B-cell lymphomas {28069605}. By flow cytometry, SDRPL expresses pan–B-cell markers and CD11c, but it is usually negative for CD103 {20220064}.

Flow cytometry can also be used for DLBCL-NOS with less effective results {12704692}. The DLBCL-NOS phenotype is similar to that of its lymph node counterpart and it is CD19+, CD20+, CD22+, and CD79a+ by either flow cytometry or immunocytochemistry, but light chain restriction is less frequently seen than in small B-cell NHL. The basic CD10, BCL2, BCL6, MUM1 panel and the corresponding algorithm may be assessed by immunocytochemistry {26414904}.
