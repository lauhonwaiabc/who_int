3.1.1.4 In situ hybridization

Introduction

In situ hybridization (ISH) is a well-established method to visualize and diagnose numerical and structural abnormalities in chromosomal DNA. It is widely used in clinical laboratories as a gold standard to identify deletions, amplifications, and rearrangements of selected genes {27479335; 33489824}. The advantages of ISH are that only a small number of tumour cells are needed for assessment and that alterations can be detected specifically within the neoplastic cells of interest. The detection of cytogenetic abnormalities in cytopathological specimens from lymphoid lesions is of enormous diagnostic and prognostic value because of the low specimen cellularity required, especially when confronted with a limited sample where other diagnostic modalities, such as flow cytometry, may be unable to provide assessment. Interphase FISH to characterize the subtype of a lymphoid proliferation is frequently used in place of classic karyotyping, where often the mitotic index may be too low to achieve satisfactory results. Although classic karyotyping is considered the gold standard due to its ability to provide a complete overview of all the chromosomal changes in a tumour cell, it is a complex and time-consuming procedure that is frequently not available outside of academic centres. The use of NGS using large panels to assess lymphoid material is in some cases replacing FISH as the diagnostic test of choice if available.

Technical requirements

Targeted evaluation of cells of interest is a prerequisite for reliable FISH results. FISH permits direct correlation with cytomorphology {27479335}. Internal validation of FISH assays is needed, and external quality assurance is recommended {29396253; 25170637; 29755669}. Using probes approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or another national regulatory body, all steps of the procedure should be followed according to the manufacturer’s specifications {28743163; 32401054}. Any deviation requires separate validation. The use of adhesive slides with electrostatic positive charge is recommended to prevent cell loss during pretreatment and DNA denaturation {29494220}. FISH analysis requires a fluorescent microscope with appropriate filters, ideally equipped with an automated stage for precise relocalization of cells of interest. Scoring of FISH signals can be done at the fluorescent microscope or based on a high-quality compressed 2D image from z-stacks at the computer screen {23689429}. Interphase FISH signals are counted on non-overlapping well-hybridized cells, with a suggested minimum scoring requirement of 100 lymphoid cells {27208569}.

Management of types of cytopathological specimens

FISH can be performed on multiple specimen types, including preparations derived from aspirated effusions, cyst fluid, solid tumours, and enlarged lymph nodes, as well as bone marrow aspirates.

Fixation

Cytospins and direct smears are particularly well suited for FISH, as the retained three dimensionality of intact nuclei allows the evaluation of the ISH signals devoid of truncation artefact typical for cut slides of cell blocks or CNBs {16645199}. Alcohol-based fixation or air-drying offers better preservation of nucleic acids for hybridization as compared with formalin fixation and paraffin embedding. Cytospins and smears offer evenly spread samples with isolated non-overlapping nuclei, easily accessible for scoring. Controlling the cell concentration and spacing allows for the application of the automated instruments programmed to rapidly digitize fluorescent signals (interphase FISH).

Controls

Running external or on-slide control specimens is not required for FISH. The presence of signals in all cell nuclei in a specimen serves as the internal control for good hybridization efficiency.

Procedures

Application of fluorescent labels for the nucleic acid probes (i.e. FISH) is the most widely used in situ method, particularly for the detection of gene translocations (with break-apart and fusion probes) or for gene signal enumeration in cases of gene loss, deletion, gain, or amplification. Chromogenic ISH (CISH) or silver-enhanced ISH (SISH) may also be applied to cytopathological specimens in selected situations {20661016}. ISH is often performed on touch preparation smears of resected lymph nodes. A multicolour DNA probe set with different fluorochromes (FISH) allows the evaluation of different chromosomal gene loci and/or centromeres at the same time {33768318}.

Diagnostic applications

Depending on the genetic aberration, ISH detects translocations and gene fusions with the application of break-apart or fusion probes, respectively. Gene amplifications, such as of ERBB2 (HER2) in breast cancer metastatic to lymph nodes, as well as duplications, deletions, insertions, and aneusomies, may also be detected by ISH. ISH may demonstrate the presence of genetic material of a pathogen, for example EBV in lymphoproliferative disease or nodally metastatic lymphoepithelial carcinoma {23725487; 32197545}, or it can be used to evaluate the light chain restriction with probes for lambda and kappa chains.

Translocations are the most common type of cytogenetic aberration in lymphomas. The resulting juxtaposition of oncogenes to potent transcription enhancers results in the production of novel hybrid proteins, leading to loss of normal cell-cycle control, prevention of apoptosis, loss of cell differentiation, and unregulated cell growth {16645199}. The detection of specific cytogenetic aberrations by ISH helps to differentiate between subtypes of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma that are composed of small to intermediate-sized cells and reactive lymphadenopathy, where there may be a high degree of cytomorphological overlap {22693682}. Diagnostic ISH-detectable cytogenetic abnormalities are found in many of the most prevalent lymphomas, such as follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma (MCL), marginal zone lymphoma (MZL) / MALT lymphoma, small lymphocytic lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma (BL) or large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) with 11q aberration, ALK-positive lymphomas, and large B-cell lymphomas with BCL2, BCL6, and MYC rearrangements {32311849}. The choice of specific FISH probes is based on the cytomorphology classified according to the WHO System, immunophenotyping, the Ki-67 proliferation index, and clinical data {20862704}.

Benign lymphadenopathy

The presence of EBV may be demonstrated by ISH for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) in infectious mononucleosis {23725487; 28592786}. The demonstration of polyclonal plasma cells by kappa and lambda light chain ISH confirms reactive plasmacytosis.

Lymph node metastases

Lymph node FNAB material is well suited for the study of the predictive markers such as an amplification of the ERBB2 (HER2) gene in the cases of metastatic breast, oesophageal, gastric, or bile duct carcinomas. The rearrangement of the ALK gene can be demonstrated in cytopathological samples of lymph nodes in metastatic lung carcinomas, and EBV infection in metastatic lymphoepithelial carcinoma can be demonstrated by EBV ISH.

Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphomas

FISH can be used for prognostic purposes in chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL), which is usually an indolent disease but may have a variable clinical course. A multicolour FISH panel comprising 11q22 (ATM), 12, 13q14.3, 13q34.3 (LAMP1), and 17p13.1 (TP53) can be used to predict the risk of the transformation to large cell lymphoma and survival in patients with CLL/SLL {10794162}. Cases with aberrations of 17p13.1, 13q34.3, and 11q22 are associated with transformation to large cell lymphomas and increased risk of death compared with cases that show normal FISH.

Follicular lymphoma

Follicular lymphoma is characterized by a reciprocal translocation t(14;18)(q32;q21) resulting in the IGH::BCL2 fusion gene, which can be demonstrated with high diagnostic accuracy by either the BCL2 break-apart or IGH::BCL2 fusion FISH probes {14681948}. With insufficient samples excluded, negative FISH results were shown in 7.5% of follicular lymphomas, compared with failure to demonstrate monoclonality in 12% of flow cytometry immunophenotyping (FCM) cases. 3q27 (BCL6) rearrangement or amplification {30156771} can also be detected by FISH. MYC rearrangement is negative in most follicular lymphomas.

Mantle cell lymphoma

The hallmark of MCL is the reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 11 and 14, t(11;14)(q13;q32) {15712276}, resulting in the IGH::CCND1 fusion, which is present in more than 95% of cases. This translocation can be demonstrated by either the CCND1 break-apart or the IGH::CCND1 fusion FISH probes, in lymph node FNAB material and in peripheral blood {15001994}. Double fusion signals are more specific than single fusion signals to diagnose MCL. A subset of MCL cases are cyclin D1–negative, with translocations in CCND2 or CCND3. A subset of blastoid MCL shows MYC rearrangement in addition to the IGH::CCND1 fusion gene {32273477}. Interphase FISH for MCL is helpful to differentiate other small cell lymphomas and useful to detect measurable residual disease (MRD) in peripheral blood.

Marginal zone lymphoma including MALT lymphoma

Nodal MZL (NMZL) is the least common form of MZL, and it shares cytopathological and immunophenotypic features with extranodal MZL (EMZL) of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) and splenic MZL. There are three translocations associated with MALT, namely t(11;18)(q21;q21), t(1;14)(p22;q32), and t(14;18)(q32;q21). These translocations occur mutually exclusively of each other and are detected in different anatomical sites {16341151}. Gastric MALT lymphomas are associated with t(11;18)(q21;q21) and less commonly with t(1;14)(p22;q32), and the presence of these translocations is associated with failure to respond to antibiotics as the sole therapeutic modality. Pulmonary MALT lymphomas are characterized by t(11;18)(q21;q21) as well as t(14;18)(q32;q21). MALT lymphomas presenting as tumours of the salivary glands, skin, and ocular adnexa are characterized by t(14;18)(q32;q21), reflecting a different pathogenesis, most likely related to autoimmune disease. No rearrangements of BCL2, BCL6, CCND1, or MYC are present in MZL in FISH.

Large B-cell lymphomas

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) / high-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 rearrangements can be diagnosed by multiple FISH performed either as a multiplex or by an individual approach {21560252; 32311849; 32909092; 33768318}. Similarly, lymphomas with both MYC and BCL6 rearrangements, now regarded as DLBCL-NOS, can be diagnosed using FISH.

Burkitt lymphoma

BLs are characterized by the translocation of MYC (8q24.2) to IGH (14q32), or less commonly to the IGK locus on 2p12 or the IGL locus on 22q11 [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]. The rearrangement of MYC can be demonstrated by the break-apart gene probe. However, MYC gene translocations are not specific for BL, and they may occur in large B-cell lymphomas with BCL6, BCL2, and MYC rearrangements. Most endemic BLs and many sporadic cases are associated with EBV infection, which can be detected in tumour cells by ISH for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER).

Anaplastic large cell lymphoma

The ALK gene encodes a tyrosine kinase receptor belonging to the insulin receptor superfamily, which is normally silent in lymphoid cells. Rearrangements of the ALK gene are diagnostic for ALK-positive anaplastic large cell lymphoma, which is a T-cell lymphoma, with the most common translocation, t(2;5)(p23.2-23.1;q35.1), involving the NPM1 gene. Rare ALK-positive LBCL is an aggressive neoplasm of monomorphic large immunoblast-like B cells with plasma cell phenotype and translocation t(2;17)(p23;q23), responsible for a CLTC::ALK fusion {28195686}.

Classic Hodgkin lymphoma

EBV-associated classic Hodgkin lymphomas may be documented by EBV ISH.
