8.1.1.4 Risk of malignancy and diagnostic management recommendations

The evaluation of lymph nodes by FNAB has been routinely used as an initial diagnostic tool for many years. However, reporting lymph node FNAB can be extremely challenging, because of the large number of pathological conditions presenting as lymphadenopathy in different clinical contexts. As mentioned in a previous section (section Risk of malignancy and diagnostic management recommendations), the ROM and the management recommendation may be different from those associated with FNAB of other organs.

The sensitivity and specificity of lymph node FNAB are variable in the literature, ranging from 75% to 99% and 87% to 100%, respectively {33830657; 27554776}. Two studies showed higher ranges when they used a uniform cytopathological reporting system {33830657; 34441249}. Malignant cases correspond to about 45% of lymph node FNAB cases, and metastases correspond to 60–90% of these malignant cases {33830657; 34441249}.

A review {39097796} of 10 recently published retrospective studies, based on the similar categories used in the Sydney System, showed the ROM to be very high, ranging from 98% to 100% {37378919; 37231736; 36917967; 35148033; 34957622; 34817104; 34661975; 34289486; 34441249; 33830657}. A recent meta-analysis based on nine of these studies found a pooled ROM of 100% {38096796}.

Ancillary testing using flow cytometry, immunocytochemistry, and molecular tests plays a key role in achieving a specific malignant diagnosis, as well as a classification and subtyping of the malignancy, and it should be used whenever feasible. In the case of metastases, the application of immunocytochemistry panels driven by clinical data and cytomorphological features enables the identification of the site of origin of tumour in most cases {34441249}, but these techniques do not significantly change the ROM for the category “Malignant”. On the other hand, when FNAB has categorized a case as “Malignant” and made a diagnosis of a lymphoma, in many centres and for many clinical trials, at least CNB and in many cases excision biopsy for lymphoma diagnosis is often demanded, although the need for excision biopsy in all cases is controversial. FNAB and ancillary testing can in many cases make the diagnosis of specific lymphomas. But whenever a diagnosis of Hodgkin lymphoma or some non-Hodgkin lymphomas, especially T-cell lymphomas, has been made or suggested by FNAB, excision biopsy of the lymph nodes is recommended to confirm or make the specific primary diagnosis. Excision is also recommended for the grading of follicular lymphomas, except in specific clinical situations or in certain anatomical locations in which CNB or excision surgery are contraindicated or not possible. This recommendation should be tailored to the type of lymphoma suspected {32454496}.
