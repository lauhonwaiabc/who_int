8.6.5.9 Peripheral T-cell lymphoma NOS

Definition

Peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) not otherwise specified (PTCL-NOS) is a heterogeneous category of nodal and extranodal mature T-cell lymphomas that cannot be assigned to a specific PTCL entity, consisting of a broad cytomorphological spectrum of medium to large lymphoid cells with variable degrees of pleomorphism.

Clinical features and imaging

PTCL-NOS does not include specific PTCL entities such as nodal T follicular helper (TFH) cell lymphomas, EBV-positive nodal T-cell and NK-cell lymphomas and anaplastic large cell lymphomas (ALCLs), or primary cutaneous T-cell lymphomas and the intestinal T-cell and NK-cell lymphomas. Some of these lymphomas are rarely subject to FNAB, and the specific diagnosis of each lymphoma relies on ancillary testing.

PTCL-NOS accounts for about 30% of PTCL and is an aggressive lymphoma generally affecting adult patients, with male predominance (M:F ratio: 2:1) and a median age at presentation of 60 years {18626005}. Presentation is usually generalized lymphadenopathy with B symptoms and advanced clinical stage, with about two thirds of the patients presenting with intermediate to high International Prognostic Index (IPI) score {28115372}. Therapeutic response is poor, and the 5-year survival rate is 20–30% {21270441}. Any organs can be involved, including liver, spleen, bone marrow, lung, skin, tonsil, and gastrointestinal tract.

Associated paraneoplastic features include hypereosinophilia, skin rash, hypercalcaemia, hypogammaglobulinaemia, haemolytic anaemia, and haemophagocytic syndrome {21270441}. Although PTCL-NOS is still a broad, heterogeneous group, two novel prognostic subtypes (PTCL-GATA3 and PTCL-TBX21) have recently been described using gene expression profiling and immunocytochemistry algorithms {31562134}. The imaging features of PTCL-NOS are nonspecific and indistinguishable from those of other lymphomas, especially in advanced stages {12533636}.

Histopathology

PTCL-NOS can cause complete or partial effacement of the lymph node architecture. In most cases the pattern is diffuse, with medium to large pleomorphic cells. Less common cases with “pleomorphic small and large cells” are also described [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]. Small lymphocytes, eosinophils, plasma cells, and histiocytes are present in the background. Haemophagocytosis can occasionally be detected. Residual atrophic follicles can be present. Clustered and scattered epithelioid histiocytes are frequently seen. Proliferations of high endothelial venules and follicular dendritic cells, which are seen characteristically in nodal TFH cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type (nTFHL-AI) (previously known as angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma [AITL]), are not prominent in PTCL-NOS {8068936}. Lymphoepithelioid lymphoma (Lennert lymphoma) represents a morphological subtype characterized by numerous, often clustered epithelioid histiocytes mimicking granulomatous inflammation and often masking the neoplastic T cells, which are usually small and mildly atypical, with irregular nuclei and mature coarse chromatin {21270441; 27016488}.

Inflammatory cell background of numerous small lymphocytes, along with eosinophils, plasma cells, mast cells, fibroblasts, histiocytes, and aggregates of epithelioid histiocytes Large tissue fragments consisting of capillaries and lymphoid cells may give an initial low-power impression of an epithelial neoplasm. Reed–Sternberg–like cells and large mononuclear Hodgkin-like cells with prominent nucleoli can be seen. Clear cells in sheets as seen in nTFHL-AI may be detected. The predominantly pleomorphic small cell pattern is rare. Epithelioid histiocytes are numerous in the lymphoepithelioid lymphoma (Lennert lymphoma) subtype, but this is also extremely rare.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The best strategy to reach a diagnosis of PTCL-NOS is careful consideration and exclusion of other specific entities of mature T-cell lymphomas {23281437}. This is based on cytomorphology but requires ancillary testing in almost all cases.

Adult T-cell lymphoma/leukaemia (ATLL) must be excluded, especially in areas where HTLV-1 is endemic. The cytomorphology and immunophenotype of ATLL often closely resemble those of PTCL-NOS; therefore, HTLV-1 serology and molecular analysis of integration of HTLV-1 viral DNA are strongly recommended.

Given the CD30 positivity in some cases of PTCL-NOS with large cell cytopathology, ALK-negative ALCL must be excluded, although the difference between the two is often hazy even with comprehensive immunocytochemistry {25224410}. However, the presence of the typical hallmark cells, with diffuse and strong CD30 positivity associated with expression of EMA and cytotoxic granules, favours ALK-negative ALCL. Gene expression profiling can also help in the differential diagnosis {23857971}.

In the Lennert lymphoma pattern, the differential diagnoses that should be ruled out include toxoplasma lymphadenitis, tuberculosis, epithelioid histiocyte-rich low-grade B-cell lymphomas, and classic Hodgkin lymphoma.

In the “mixed, intermediate, and large cell” pattern, reactive follicular hyperplasia and predominantly centrocytic follicular lymphoma are in the differential diagnosis. The mixed pattern composed of “small” but actually intermediate in size cleaved cells and large non-cleaved cells can be seen in PTCL-NOS smears, but follicular dendritic cells, usually numerous in follicular lymphoma and follicular hyperplasia, and tingible-body macrophages, seen in follicular hyperplasia, are not observed.

The cytomorphological differential diagnosis between PTCL-NOS and nTFHL-AI is often challenging, especially in the presence of characteristic “pale-cell” nests and vascular tissue fragments that can be seen in both neoplasms. nTFHL-AI, however, is more often accompanied by typical paraneoplastic symptoms, including polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia, hepatosplenomegaly, skin rash, pruritus, oedema, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (including cold agglutinin disease), and arthritis, so the integration of clinical setting with the cytomorphology and appropriate ancillary testing can suggest such a diagnosis, which must then be confirmed histopathologically. In addition, the high cellularity with mixed cell types in smears of PTCL-NOS can lead to an erroneous diagnosis of reactive hyperplasia, especially at low magnification. This highlights the additional, crucial role of flow cytometry to reach the correct diagnosis {11309782}.

Ancillary testing

Immunophenotyping by flow cytometry and/or immunocytochemistry plays a key role in the diagnosis of PTCL-NOS by identifying abnormal populations and excluding other types of PTCL. Standard flow cytometry analysis may reveal an abnormal T-cell population with atypical scatter properties and/or reduced or increased expression of one or more of CD2, CD3, CD5, and CD7, or markedly aberrant proportions of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells {30729667; 32947231}. Most PTCL-NOS cases are CD4+CD8−, although some are CD4−CD8+ {32947231; 21270441}. If a suspicious population is detected and resources are available, it should be assessed for monoclonality by flow cytometry with TRBC1, or by PCR for T-cell receptor genes {32333725}.

Commoner CD4+ PTCLs, such as ALCL, nTFHL-AI, and (in areas where HTLV-1 is endemic) ATLL, should be excluded before reaching a diagnosis of PTCL-NOS. CD30 is strongly and uniformly positive in ALCL but variable or absent in PTCL-NOS {30729667; 32947231}. Expression of at least two (or preferably three) markers of TFH cells (CD10, BCL6, CXCL13, CXCR5, PD1, ICOS, and SAP) indicates nTFHL-AI or another nodal TFH cell lymphoma, but little or no expression of these markers is typical of PTCL-NOS. CD25 is commonly expressed in ATLL, although a positive result is not specific. EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER) is usually negative in the lymphoma cells in PTCL-NOS, while positive in primary EBV-positive nodal T-cell and NK-cell lymphoma, which is usually CD8+. The latter also expresses cytotoxic granule markers such as perforin, TIA1, and granzyme B {32947231}.

In a recent study on PTCL-NOS, gene expression profiling led to reclassification of 37% of cases that had initially been diagnosed as PTCL-NOS. Diagnoses were mainly changed to nTFHL-AI or ALK-negative ALCL. The majority of the remaining PTCL- NOS cases were assigned to two subsets on the basis of expression of either GATA3 or TBX21 (T-bet) {24632715}. Gene expression profiling is not always possible on routine cytopathology samples, and an immunocytochemistry strategy has been proposed to assign PTCL-NOS cases to the GATA3 or TBX21 subsets {31562134}. However, exclusion of nTFHL-AI lymphomas and subsetting of PTCL-NOS may be challenging if the sample is limited.
