8.6.5.4 Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma

Definition

Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTL) is an aggressive mature T-cell lymphoma characterized by a proliferation of cytotoxic T-cell immunophenotype within the spleen, liver, and bone marrow.

Clinical features and imaging

HSTL is a rare disease, representing 1–2% of all peripheral T-cell lymphomas and far less than 1% of all non-Hodgkin lymphomas {18626005; 31709579}. Early studies reported that most patients were adolescents or young adults, with a median age in the fourth decade {29337025; 32756940}, but a recent study showed that 51% of patients were over 60 years of age {31709579}. Up to 25% of cases of HSTL arise in the context of immunosuppression.

Patients with HSTL often present with marked splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, and B-type symptoms, and jaundice may occur in up to 50% of patients (#33365Table 7.19) {10865963; 12907441; 19237479; 26872013}. The diagnosis is usually made on bone marrow trephine and aspirate, and because lymphadenopathy is rare, FNAB is rarely the diagnostic test. Microscopic involvement of splenic hilar lymph nodes in patients with splenomegaly is common. This neoplasm can rarely involve skin, more often at the time of relapse {27562705}. Laboratory abnormalities are common, particularly cytopenias and elevated serum levels of β2 microglobulin and LDH (#33365Table 7.19) {12907441; 26872013}. Virtually all patients have advanced-stage disease {12907441; 26872013; 33160933}.

Patients with HSTL have a poor prognosis, with a median survival time of less than 2 years {18626005; 33160933; 34819748}. Patients with HSTL of T-cell receptor alpha/beta subtype may have a poorer prognosis {11224598}. Patients respond poorly to standard chemotherapy regimens, but highly aggressive chemotherapy regimens and/or allogeneic stem cell transplantation may improve long-term survival {25234166; 34409955}.

Histopathology

The spleen is markedly enlarged in patients with HSTL, typically > 1000 g when splenectomy is performed or > 200 mm in diameter by imaging {26872013}. The cut surface is homogeneous without nodules, reflecting diffuse red pulp involvement and atrophy of the white pulp. The neoplasm involves the cords and sinuses of the splenic red pulp {10865963; 12907441; 17509984}. HSTL cells are small to medium, with irregular nuclear contours, dense or more blastoid chromatin, inconspicuous or more prominent nucleoli, and pale cytoplasm {12907441; 17509984}. The lymphoma cells do not have cytoplasmic granules in bone marrow aspirates or blood smears stained with Giemsa stains {27755009}. Occasionally, the neoplastic cells can resemble blasts, or they may exhibit substantial atypia and pleomorphism {11554170; 12907441}.

The liver is usually diffusely enlarged in patients with HSTL. The neoplasm preferentially involves the sinusoids, although some portal tract expansion can also occur. The cytopathological features are the same as those observed in the spleen.

The bone marrow is commonly involved by HSTL, and in many patients the diagnosis is established by bone marrow evaluation. The biopsy specimen shows sinusoids that are expanded by lymphoma cells {11554170; 26872013; 27755009}. The uninvolved bone marrow is often hypercellular with trilineage haematopoiesis. Aspirate smears may show mild to moderate haematopoietic dyspoiesis, but these features do not appear to have clinical importance {26997444}. Erythrophagocytosis can be present, and haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis can uncommonly develop in HSTL patients {21683978}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

T-cell large granular lymphocyte leukaemia is a clinically indolent disease, often associated with autoimmune disease, and patients present with low-level lymphocytosis and large granular lymphocytes in the blood. FNAB is performed uncommonly in patients with T-cell large granular lymphocyte leukaemia, but the cells in smears resemble those in the blood and bone marrow. Immunophenotypic analysis is helpful: T-cell large granular lymphocyte leukaemia cells are often positive for CD57, CD8, and T-cell receptor alpha/beta, and STAT3 mutations occur in about one third of cases (#33366Table 7.20).

Patients with T-cell prolymphocytic leukaemia usually present with leukocytosis, bone marrow involvement, and hepatosplenomegaly. Most often, the neoplastic cells have features of prolymphocytes with large nucleoli. T-cell prolymphocytic leukaemia cells are positive for CD2, surface CD3, CD4, CD5, TCL1, and T-cell receptor alpha/beta. The leukaemic cells lack cytotoxic molecules and are associated with inv(14)(q11q32) or less often t(14;14)(q11;q32), both abnormalities resulting in the juxtaposition of TRA with a TCL1 gene. Rarely, t(X;14)(q28;q11) occurs, in which TRA is juxtaposed with MTCP1.

Small B-cell lymphomas, including chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, and splenic marginal zone lymphoma, involve the spleen and result in splenomegaly more commonly than HSTL. Unlike HSTL, these neoplasms tend to arise in older patients, are associated with lymphadenopathy, and can be associated with substantial lymphocytosis. Flow cytometry is particularly helpful and shows B-cell lineage, distinguishing these neoplasms from HSTL (see #33280Table 2.04, p. XXX, in section Flow cytometry).

Ancillary testing

HSTL cells have a distinctive immunophenotype; flow cytometry shows positivity for CD2, cytoplasmic CD3, surface CD3 (variable), CD7 (variable), CD16 (~30–40%), CD56 (~70%), and HLA-DR, and it is usually negative for CD4, CD5 (~15% dim+), CD8 (~20% dim+), CD10, CD25, CD30, and CD57 (~15% dim+) (see #33281, in section Flow cytometry). Approximately 75% of cases are positive for T-cell receptor γ/δ, about 20% are positive for T-cell receptor α/β, and 5% lack T-cell receptors (“null”) {17509984; 29337025}. T-cell receptor variable (V) region delta expression seems to be restricted to Vδ1 chain {11272897}. HSTL cells express cytotoxic markers, usually TIA1 and granzyme M, and granzyme B in about one third of cases {26997444}. By immunocytochemistry, most cases of HSTL are positive for CD2, CD3, CD56, and TIA1, and are negative for CD4, CD5, and CD8.

HSTL carries monoclonal T-cell receptor gene rearrangements. Conventional cytogenetic analysis and/or FISH can detect isochromosome 7q in up to 70% of cases and trisomy 8 in up to 50% of cases {9264394; 29337025}. Gene mutations involving the JAK/STAT pathway (particularly STAT5B) also occur in HSTL {24947020} (#33366Table 7.20).
