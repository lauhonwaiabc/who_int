8.6.5.10 Nodal T follicular helper cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type

Definition

Nodal T follicular helper cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type (nTFHL-AI) is a neoplasm of mature T cells with T follicular helper (TFH) phenotype, characterized by systemic disease and a polymorphous lymphoid infiltrate involving lymph nodes, accompanied by prominent proliferation of high endothelial venules and follicular dendritic cells (FDCs).

Clinical features and imaging

nTFHL-AI, previously known as angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma, is a type of peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) that expresses a unique array of markers [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]] {20207847; 28082343}. It is one of a newly described group of lymphomas of TFH cell origin accounting for 15–20% of cases of PTCL, which primarily affects middle-aged and elderly patients, with a slight male predominance. nTFHL shares considerable morphological and immunophenotypic overlap with several subtypes in the fifth edition of the WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]] that await elucidation, particularly in cytopathology. nTFHL-AI usually presents at an advanced stage of disease, with a clinical course that is often highly aggressive but may wax and wane, with a median overall survival of < 3 years {18626005; 26045291}. nTFHL-AI is often accompanied by B symptoms and extranodal involvement of spleen, skin, liver, lung, gastrointestinal tract, and bone marrow, as well as polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia, skin rash, pruritus, joint swelling, pleural effusion and ascites, autoimmune disorders with circulating immune complexes, cold agglutinins with haemolytic anaemia, rheumatoid factor, paraneoplastic syndromes, and anti–smooth muscle antibodies {25401231; 33083350}. Chest CT shows interlobular septal thickening, ground-glass opacity, and thickening of the bronchovascular bundles, combined with mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy {24998374}.

Histopathology

nTFHL-AI is a nodal-based disease with partial or total effacement of the architecture and a “diffuse-mixed” pattern composed of a mixture of small to medium neoplastic T cells with clear or pale cytoplasm in a polymorphous background of expanded meshworks of FDCs, arborizing high endothelial venules, scattered reactive large B cells, small lymphocytes, plasma cells, histiocytes, foci of epithelioid granulomas, eosinophils, and fibroblasts. EBV-positive large B cells are often binucleated and can be mistaken for Hodgkin or Reed–Sternberg cells. nTFHL-AI includes three overlapping patterns: the first composed of hyperplastic germinal centres with surrounding neoplastic T cells expressing a TFH phenotype, the second of neoplastic TFH cells surrounding remnant germinal centres, and a third of diffusely effaced architecture with occasional regressed germinal centres and abundant neoplastic cells in the expanded T-zone paracortex. At relapse of nTFHL-AI after multiagent chemotherapy, secondary large B-cell lymphoma from residual EBV-positive B-cell clones may develop {11888076}.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

As a result of cytokine secretion by the neoplastic cells, patients present with a wide variety of symptoms, signs, and laboratory abnormalities. The neoplastic TFH cell proliferation may be present together with numerous B cells, and it is often but not always associated with EBV infection, which is thought to be a secondary event. It is characterized by a polymorphous lymphoid infiltrate, atypical lymphoid cells, eosinophils, hyperplasia of dendritic cells, arborizing blood vessels, and high endothelial venules.

The diagnosis of nTFHL-AI can be made by the integration of cytopathology, immunocytochemistry, and flow cytometry findings in conjunction with the cell block, due to the unique immunocytochemical features of the TFH cells and FDCs, as well as the characteristic high endothelial vessels and dendritic cell meshworks. However, in many cases, including those with features that are less than typical that are not resolved by flow cytometry or immunophenotyping, excisional biopsy is required.

The differential diagnosis includes entities described in #36055Table 7.02 {12115305}.

Ancillary testing

Diagnosis of nTFHL-AI may be more straightforward on cell block than by FNAB smears, due to retention of tissue fragments containing blood vessels and hyperplasia of the FDCs positive with CD21, CD23, and CD35. The adjacent TFH lymphoid cells show the characteristic cell surface molecules CXCR5, CD154, PD1, ICOS, CD200, and cytoplasmic SAP (SLAM-associated protein), as well as BCL6. There is a background of B cells or EBV ISH–positive B cells.

Detection of an aberrant T-cell population by flow cytometry may be an early clue to the presence of nTFHL-AI, even when there are more reactive T cells than neoplastic cells. Key aberrant features of the nTFHL-AI cells in initial flow cytometry analyses are dim or negative staining for CD3 and positive staining for BCL6 and CD10, as well as the pan–T-cell markers CD2, CD4, and CD5, although CD7 is often dim or negative {16572417; 27105079}. Bright PD1 expression is characteristic of nTFHL-AI and differentiates it from other types of PTCL {32144240}. Flow cytometry is well established for CD10 and PD1, but other TFH cell markers are usually assessed by immunocytochemistry. In nTFHL-AI, flow cytometry may be useful to evaluate B cells and plasma cells for monoclonality by light chain restriction, and the B-cell immunoblasts and plasma cells are usually polytypic; however, secondary EBV-positive B-cell proliferations can be seen in large B-cell lymphoma, classic Hodgkin lymphoma, and plasmacytoma {16572417}.

NGS analysis has identified recurrent mutations in nTFHL-AI similar to those in other T-cell neoplasms derived from TFH cells {33990228}. T-cell receptor genes show clonal rearrangements in up to 90% of cases, and clonal IG gene rearrangements are found in 20–30% of cases, with increased EBV-positive B cells. Mutations of genes encoding epigenetic modifiers include IDH2 (specific for nTFHL), TET2, DNMT3A, and GTPase RHOA mutations {30782609; 33990228}. Several genes encoding components of T-cell receptor signalling pathways may rarely be mutated (FYN, PLCG1, and CD28), and fusion genes encoding a CTLA-4::CD28 hybrid protein may result in transforming inhibitory signals into stimulatory signals for T-cell activation [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]]. Very few cases of nTFHL-AI carry t(5;9)(q33;q22), which results in ITK::SYK gene fusion, an alteration originally found in follicular PTCL {24076779; 25337257}. By conventional cytogenetic analysis, clonal aberrations are common (trisomies of chromosomes 3, 5, and 21; gain of X and loss of 6q) {30782609}.
