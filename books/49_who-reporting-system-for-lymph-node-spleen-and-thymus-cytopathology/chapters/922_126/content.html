9.2.2 Category: Benign

Definition: A specimen categorized as “Benign” demonstrates unequivocal benign cytopathological features, which may or may not be diagnostic of a specific process or benign neoplasm.

A recent review {39097796} of 10 retrospective and heterogeneous studies showed the ROM for lymph node and extranodal FNAB cases categorized as “Benign” ranging from 2% to 16% {37378919; 37231736; 36917967; 35148033; 34957622; 34817104; 34661975; 34289486; 34441249; 33830657}.

A recent meta-analysis that pooled the ROM of nine of these studies found a ROM of 5% {38096796}. These studies varied greatly in terms of the patient cohort, the use of ROSE, the use of ultrasound direction, the expertise of the operator and number of FNAB procedures performed by cytopathologists, the statistical analysis, and the use of ancillary testing. The use of ancillary techniques, particularly immunoprofiling applied to direct smears, cytospins, liquid-based cytopathology, and cell block sections with different fixatives such as methanol, alcohol, and formalin, can influence the diagnostic yield.

The “Benign” cytopathology report should always be correlated with the clinical and serological data and imaging features. Therefore, when the cytomorphological report is represented by a “Benign” category, there are two possible scenarios for the management:

Firstly, a conservative approach may be recommended if the cytopathology report correlates with the clinical and serological data and imaging findings and confirms an inflammatory or infectious process. A routine follow-up with a combination of physical examination, laboratory tests, and imaging may be applied. Repeat FNAB should also be recommended when any change occurs in the clinical presentation or imaging.

Secondly, a repeat FNAB with flow cytometry and other appropriate ancillary tests such as microbiological studies, or tissue procurement by CNB or excisional biopsy {32454496; 24695506} can be recommended if clinical or serological data or imaging findings are discordant.
