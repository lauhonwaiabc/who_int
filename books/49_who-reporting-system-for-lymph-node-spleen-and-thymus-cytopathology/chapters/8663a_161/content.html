8.6.6.3A Lymphomatoid granulomatosis

Definition

Lymphomatoid granulomatosis (LYG) is an EBV-associated angiocentric and angiodestructive B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder involving extranodal sites, composed of EBV-positive atypical large B cells usually admixed with a large number of reactive T cells, in patients lacking evidence of inborn or acquired immunodeficiency/dysregulation other than immunosenescence.

Clinical features and imaging

LYG always involves the lung, although not all patients present with respiratory symptoms – in these cases, lung involvement may only be demonstrated by imaging. Other sites of involvement include the CNS, skin, kidneys, and liver. Involvement of lymph nodes and/or bone marrow is extremely rare, and if the cytopathological features raise the possibility of LYG, it is usually in the context of a differential diagnosis {32107539}.

Histopathology

LYG is characterized by an angiocentric and angiodestructive lymphoid infiltrate affecting small to large blood vessels. Central necrosis is often present. Despite the name, well-formed granulomas are typically absent, except in the skin. Grading is based on the density of EBV-positive B cells relative to the reactive lymphocyte background, but grading is poorly reproducible {25321327; 32107539}.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Mixed lymphoid infiltrate of lymphocytes and varying numbers of plasma cells and histiocytes with a usually small number of large atypical lymphoid cells Large atypical lymphoid cells may resemble immunoblasts or less frequently Hodgkin cells, with single large nuclei, reticular chromatin, and large light-blue (Giemsa) nucleoli.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The diagnosis of LYG requires demonstration of angiocentricity of the lymphoid infiltrate. The role of FNAB is to demonstrate the mix of large atypical lymphoid cells in a background of reactive lymphocytes and raise the differential diagnosis of LYG and other EBV-positive disorders and Hodgkin lymphoma, while a specific diagnosis cannot be made on cytopathology alone. If there is a uniform population of large atypical EBV-positive B cells, EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma should be diagnosed.

Ancillary testing

The presence of EBV-positive B cells is essential, as assessed by FISH for EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER). Immunoglobulin gene rearrangement by PCR shows infrequent clonality in low-grade lesions, with increased likelihood of clonality in high-grade lesions. By immunocytochemistry, the B cells are positive for CD20 and may be positive for CD30. Most background cells are CD3-positive T cells, most of which are CD4-positive {25321327}.
