7.1.1.1 Introduction

The “Suspicious for malignancy” category is used for those cases in which the features suggest a malignancy but there is a paucity of material or the cytopathological features are not sufficiently definite to establish a malignant diagnosis. Subsequent flow cytometry, immunocytochemistry, or other molecular testing may allow for a final definitive diagnosis and change the category to “Malignant” or in some cases “Benign” or “Atypical”. This category supports the specificity, high positive predictive value (PPV), and high ROM of the “Malignant” category, while maintaining the high sensitivity for detection of malignant lesions with FNAB. This strategy facilitates risk stratification and estimation of ROM, analogous to those of the Bethesda and IAC Yokohama systems for reporting thyroid and breast FNAB cytopathology, respectively [[Ali SZ, VanderLaan PA, editors. The Bethesda system for reporting thyroid cytopathology: definitions, criteria, and explanatory notes. 3rd ed. Cham (Switzerland): Springer; 2023.]] {31112942}.

There are only rare reports in the literature using an undefined designation of “Suspicious for malignancy” or “Indeterminate cytology” (or variations thereof) in the context of lymph node FNAB, some of which particularly relate to specific site investigations, such as endobronchial ultrasound–guided transbronchial FNAB in staging lung cancers or FNAB of neck nodes {20735454; 22592650} [[Altınboğa AA, Gökhan Y. Fine-needle aspiration cytology in the diagnosis of lymph nodes: correlation with histopathological diagnosis. South Clin Ist Euras. 2019;30(1)52–9. doi:10.14744/scie.2019.14622.]]. Such studies do indicate a high PPV for their suspicious category. The percentage of cases that these studies assign as suspicious varies between 1% and 20% and is decreased with the use of ancillary techniques, in particular flow cytometry {20735454; 22592650; 11353059}. In a recent large retrospective study, the Sydney System for categorizing lymph node FNAB cases {32454496} was applied to almost 7000 cases with lymphadenopathy {33830657}. Of these, only 1.4% were categorized as suspicious for malignancy, with a calculated ROM of 88%. The results from the ancillary studies increased the ROM to 91%. Notably in this study, immunocytochemistry was performed in 6% of cases and flow cytometry in only 1%. Two other recent retrospective studies have demonstrated the applicability of the Sydney proposal for systematization and standardization of FNAB of lymph nodes, both with 100% ROM for the suspicious for malignancy category {34289486; 34441249}.
