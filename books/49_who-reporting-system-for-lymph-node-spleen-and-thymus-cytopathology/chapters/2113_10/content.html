2.1.1.3 Rapid onsite evaluation

ROSE is an essential FNAB adjunct and can be performed on FNAB of lymphadenopathies and splenic or thymic masses. The main advantage of ROSE is sample triage and optimization for cytopathology and for selection of specific ancillary techniques and appropriate fixatives in each case.

ROSE aims to ensure that samples are adequate and representative and are adequately handled for cytopathology assessment and ancillary techniques that may be required for a complete diagnostic workup and an appropriate treatment plan. Special diagnostic techniques such as immunocytochemistry, flow cytometry, FISH, PCR, and NGS using cytopathology material are standard practice where available, to support and make more specific the cytopathological diagnosis.

ROSE offers a preliminary diagnosis and provides a rapid screening between a benign, reactive, and neoplastic lesion, which enables immediate further biopsy such as CNB, saving the cost of rebooking expensive theatre or procedural spaces and their staff, as well as the time of clinicians and surgeons and patients. Potentially, specific management and therapy can be implemented quickly.

In ROSE, a wide variety of cytopathological stains can be employed. The choice depends on the infrastructure of the hospital and the availability of trained cytopathology staff. Rapid staining methods like rapid H&E, ultrafast Pap, toluidine blue, brilliant cresyl blue, and Giemsa can be used.

However, to obtain excellent sample optimization, the balance between the need to perform an immediate preliminary diagnosis on smears and on the other hand to obtain and save enough material for other techniques is an operator-dependent decision, directly related to the cytopathologist’s experience. This balance also relates to the increasing complexity of obtaining the sample and the range of ancillary tests available. Particular challenges for the ROSE operator include deep lesions in hard-to-reach locations, fibrotic lesions, and FNAB performed in the paediatric age group with a limited number of repetitions.

Endoscopic ultrasound–guided FNAB (EUS-FNAB) is a well-established method used to diagnose mediastinal lesions and infradiaphragmatic lesions. There are multiple potential targets and a broad diagnostic spectrum. Diagnosis of reactive or infectious lesions, lymphomas, carcinomas, germ cell tumours, sarcomas, and even melanomas can be made. Experienced EUS-FNAB teams, where ROSE has been shown to have less impact on sample adequacy, can select which cases where ROSE will assist, while other groups may require ROSE in most cases {31964550; 31495750}. The collected sample can be used to make direct smears, with some placed in a liquid medium (PBS, liquid-based cytopathology solution, RPMI medium) or alcohol or formaldehyde for cell block preparation. A sample for flow cytometry with appropriate preservatives such as RPMI medium should always be considered.
