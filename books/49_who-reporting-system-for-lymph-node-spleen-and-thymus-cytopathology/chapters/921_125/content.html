9.2.1 Category: Insufficient/Inadequate/Non-diagnostic

Definition: A specimen categorized as “Inadequate/Insufficient/Non-diagnostic” lacks sufficient material in quantity or quality for a reliable diagnosis.

A review {39097796} of 10 recently published studies assessing the Sydney System for lymph node reporting reports a wide range of the ROM for lymph node FNAB cases categorized as “Insufficient/Inadequate”, from 17% up to 67% {37378919; 37231736; 36917967; 35148033; 34957622; 34817104; 34661975; 34289486; 34441249; 33830657}. A recent meta-analysis of nine of these studies showed a pooled ROM of the “Insufficient” category of 34% {38096796}.

The “Insufficient/Inadequate” samples should therefore never be reported as “Negative for malignancy”. The management of these cases is dependent on the local medical resources, the cause of the inadequate sample, the clinical setting, and correlation with the imaging. Correlation with clinical and imaging findings is the first step.

In cases where there is clinical and/or imaging suspicion that the lymph node harbours a specific infective process or malignancy, further assessment is required and may include repeating the FNAB or CNB for histopathological analysis. One of the roles of ROSE is to make an adequacy assessment, and if the specimen is inadequate, to alert the cytopathologist or interventional radiologist performing the FNAB, who can then immediately repeat the FNAB and, depending on the triage of the material, provide material for special stains, microbiological studies, or cell block for immunocytochemistry or NGS. If ROSE is not performed and the material is found to be inadequate at a later time, and there are clinical and/or imaging suspicions, FNAB or CNB should be recommended. The recommendation for the choice of the planned reintervention may include a different technique, such as image guidance, ROSE, or dedicated collection of a sample for ancillary studies (including cell block or cell suspension for flow cytometry), and it will also take into account the accessibility of the lymph node to FNAB or CNB.

In cases where correlation with clinical, imaging, and laboratory tests suggests a benign process, no further action other than appropriate follow-up may be required.
