5.3.1 Sample reports: Benign

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format will depend on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and ROSE are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as “Diagnostic summary” (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Specimen type:

FNAB axillary lymphadenopathy

Clinical and imaging information:

34-year-old female, HIV-positive, with constitutional symptoms and axillary lymphadenopathy

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Axillary lymph node

Category: BENIGN

Diagnosis: Mycobacterial infection

 

Macroscopic description:

Three air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Microbiological sample provided and sent to microbiology.

 

Microscopic description:

Occasional poorly formed granulomas are present in a background of extensive necrosis and scattered lymphocytes. Multinucleated giant cells are not seen.

A Ziehl–Neelsen stain is positive for acid-fast bacilli.

 

Comment: Please correlate with microbiology results for tuberculosis PCR and culture.

Sample report 2

Specimen type:

FNAB neck lymphadenopathy

Clinical and imaging information:

63-year-old female with a dry cough, tender patches on skin, and neck lymphadenopathy

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: BENIGN

Diagnosis: Granulomatous inflammation consistent with sarcoid

 

Macroscopic description:

Two air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings sent for microbiological studies.

 

Microscopic description:

Several well-formed granulomas are present in a background of moderate numbers of lymphocytes. No necrosis is evident.

A Ziehl–Neelsen stain is negative for acid-fast bacilli.

 

Comment: Please correlate with clinical and imaging findings and microbiology results, including TB PCR and cultures.

Sample report 3

Specimen type:

FNAB neck and axillary lymphadenopathy

Clinical and imaging information:

45-year-old immune-compromised patient with fever and tender, swollen axillary mass

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: BENIGN

Diagnosis: Abscess

 

Macroscopic description:

Four air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings centrifuged for cell block. Separate specimen sent to microbiology.

 

Microscopic description:

Aggregates of fungi with acutely branching hyphae are noted in a background of numerous acute inflammatory cells, histiocytes, and background debris.

The PASD and methenamine silver stain confirm the acutely branching septate hyphae, suggestive of Aspergillus spp.

 

Comment: Correlation with fungal culture is required.

Sample report 4

Type of specimen:

FNAB left mid-cervical neck lymph node

Clinical and imaging information:

23-year-old with cervical lymphadenopathy with upper respiratory tract symptoms and fever

 

Diagnostic summary:

Site: Left mid-cervical lymph node

Category: BENIGN

Diagnosis: Reactive lymphadenopathy with follicular hyperplasia

 

Macroscopic description:

Four air-dried slides and three alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings sent to flow cytometry and separate specimen sent to microbiology.

 

Microscopic description:

These moderately cellular smears show several germinal-centre cell tissue fragments with dendritic reticulin cells and tingible-body macrophages and abundant dispersed mixed lymphoid cells including centrocytes, centroblasts, and plentiful small lymphocytes. Plasma cells and scattered neutrophils are also present. There is no necrosis, granulomas, or metastatic disease, and no features to suggest a lymphoproliferative disorder.

 

Comment: Please correlate with clinical, serological, imaging, and flow cytometry results. If lymphadenopathy persists, repeat FNAB with flow cytometry with or without CNB is recommended.

Sample report 5

Type of specimen:

FNAB axillary lymph node

Clinical and imaging information:

48-year-old female with axillary lymphadenopathy. Diffuse eczematous dermatitis on both upper arms.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: BENIGN

Diagnosis: Prominent histiocytosis suggestive of dermatopathic lymphadenopathy

 

Macroscopic description:

Four air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings sent to flow cytometry and separate specimen sent to microbiology.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show a mixed population of lymphoid cells including germinal-centre tissue fragments and predominating small lymphocytes. Numerous melanin-laden histiocytes are present singly and in small loose aggregates. No necrosis or granulomas are seen. There is no evidence of metastatic disease and no features to suggest a lymphoproliferative disorder.

 

Comment: Please correlate with clinical, serological, imaging, and flow cytometry results. If lymphadenopathy persists, repeat FNAB with flow cytometry with or without CNB is recommended.

Sample report 6

Type of specimen:

FNAB cervical lymph node

Clinical and imaging information:

Prominent cervical lymphadenopathy. 18-year-old male with mild fever, lethargy, and sore throat 2–3 weeks previously. Positive monospot test.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: BENIGN

Diagnosis: Florid immunoblastic hyperplasia in keeping with infectious mononucleosis

 

Macroscopic description:

Two air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings sent to flow cytometry and separate specimen sent to microbiology.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show a mixed population of lymphoid cells including occasional germinal-centre cell tissue fragments, with numerous, singly dispersed immunoblasts that show round to oval nuclei, clear chromatin, and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli, with variable eccentric cytoplasm. Frequent tingible-body macrophages and focal necrotic debris are seen. No granulomas or Reed–Sternberg cells are seen.

 

Comment: Please correlate with clinical, serological, imaging, and flow cytometry results. If lymphadenopathy persists, repeat FNAB with flow cytometry with or without CNB is recommended.
