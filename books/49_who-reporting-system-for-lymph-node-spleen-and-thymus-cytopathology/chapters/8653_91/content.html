8.6.5.3 Angiosarcoma

Definition

Primary angiosarcoma of the spleen is a rare, high-grade malignant mesenchymal endothelial tumour.

Clinical features and imaging

Angiosarcoma of the spleen affects adult patients of both sexes. The clinical presentation is mostly nonspecific and variable, with left-sided abdominal pain and a palpable mass, as well as systemic symptoms including thrombocytopenia and coagulopathy {32674844; 24795063}. In a third of cases, organ rupture may occur {11007038}. On imaging with ultrasound, CT, MRI, or PET, angiosarcoma of the spleen presents as a splenomegaly, usually > 1000 g, with heterogeneous signals and signs of increased vascularity, haemorrhage, and necrosis {20713317; 24525666}. Primary splenic angiosarcomas are clinically aggressive tumours with a high rate of metastatic disease to liver, bone, lung, lymph nodes, and brain.

Histopathology

The cut surface of the spleen shows a poorly delineated tumour mass with foci of necrosis and haemorrhage, which may be associated with pseudocystic spaces. Microscopically, the architecture of the spleen is destroyed by diffusely infiltrating malignant, mostly spindle tumour cells with focal vasoformation. Papillary, anastomosing, or epithelioid endothelial proliferation may be observed. Haemosiderin depositions, scarring, and necrosis are usually present.

Key diagnostic cytopathological features

Usually high cellularity Predominant cell type is spindle and highly pleomorphic. Syncytial, solid tissue fragments and tissue fragments showing spindle and poorly formed irregular small vessels Abundant and eosinophilic (Pap stain) cytoplasm, especially in epithelioid tumours Nuclei are large, with prominent nucleoli and perinucleolar clearing (“vesicular” in Pap). Plentiful mitoses, including atypical forms Haemorrhagic background, usually with haemosiderin depositions and necrosis

Discussion and differential diagnosis

Primary angiosarcomas of the spleen are rarely diagnosed by FNAB. On FNAB, they have to be differentiated from benign endothelial tumours, including haemangiomas, lymphangiomas, and littoral cell angiomas, as well as from non-endothelial malignant tumours and metastatic angiosarcoma. Distinguishing primary and metastatic angiosarcomas requires correlation with history, clinical presentation, and imaging. The spindle cell type, high degree of nuclear atypia, and focal vasoformative features are clues to the diagnosis.

Ancillary testing

Immunocytochemistry on cell blocks or on the direct smears is mandatory to confirm the diagnosis. Angiosarcomas of the spleen express endothelial markers (CD31, ERG, and variable CD34), with occasional positivity for podoplanin (D2-40) or CD8 [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Soft tissue and bone tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2020. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 3). https://publications.iarc.who.int/588.]] {27088896}. Partial expression of keratins or EMA may occasionally be observed, while melanocytic, myogenic, and lymphatic markers are negative.
