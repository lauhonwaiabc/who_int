8.6.4.8 Marginal zone lymphoma

Definition

Marginal zone lymphomas (MZLs) are mature B-cell neoplasms, typically negative for CD5 and CD10. They include extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (EMZL) of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT), splenic marginal zone lymphoma (SMZL), nodal marginal zone lymphoma (NMZL), paediatric nodal marginal zone lymphoma (pNMZL), and primary cutaneous marginal zone lymphoma (PCMZL).

Clinical features and imaging

MZLs represent 5–10% of all non-Hodgkin lymphomas. Their clinical and laboratory features are summarized in #34773Table 7.01 . EMZLs are the most common type of MZL (70%), and they can arise at almost any extranodal site, with the stomach most often involved. Other well-described sites include salivary glands, ocular adnexa, thyroid gland, lungs, breasts, liver, and elsewhere in the gastrointestinal tract. EMZL is an indolent primary extranodal B-cell lymphoma that arises from marginal zone B cells of acquired MALT and is often associated with an underlying chronic inflammatory disorder. It tends to affect adults. Etiology is associated with chronic antigenic stimulation due to infection, such as Helicobacter pylori infection in the stomach and Chlamydia psittaci infection of the ocular adnexa, or autoimmune diseases such as Sjögren syndrome of the salivary glands and Hashimoto thyroiditis. Clinical features depend on the involved site {23233614; 22287488; 34985232; 26773441}. EMZL can disseminate to regional lymph nodes and mimic NMZL {28288722; 19864232; 35139275}. PCMZL and pNMZL have been separated from other EMZLs in the fifth edition of the WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

These lymphomas share morphological and immunophenotypic features, but their clinical presentation and molecular features differ {31428970; 31912792}.

SMZL may be associated with hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, although in most cases the etiology remains unknown. Most patients are in their seventh decade and usually present with symptoms of anaemia or thrombocytopenia. Splenomegaly is detected by physical examination and/or imaging {23233614; 34985232; 21842699}.

NMZL is the least common type of MZL. The median age at presentation is 60 years. NMZLs behave in an indolent manner, although the disease is usually disseminated at presentation but not bulky. Approximately 5–10% of cases of NMZL transform into aggressive lymphoma. pNMZL has been described in children, where it tends to affect boys predominantly, with the head and neck being the most frequently involved site. This indolent lymphoma has features overlapping those of paediatric-type FL.

Histopathology

The histopathological features of all MZLs are similar. These neoplasms show a mixed neoplastic infiltrate in which small to medium immature B-lymphoid cells predominate. Plasmacytic differentiation is common, and residual reactive lymphoid follicles may be present. MZL cells can infiltrate into follicles and coalesce to replace most of the follicle in so-called follicular colonization. In EMZL, epithelium of stomach or other sites can be infiltrated by lymphoma cells forming lymphoepithelial lesions. SMZL preferentially involves the white pulp of the spleen, and splenic hilar lymph nodes are often involved {19864232} [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Haematolymphoid tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2024. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 11). https://publications.iarc.who.int/637.]].

EMZL can be seen in cytopathological specimens such as bronchial washings and brushings, bronchoalveolar lavage, and gastric brushings, as well as effusions and FNAB from salivary glands, thyroid gland, lacrimal glands, and other sites, and it may show epithelium admixed with the neoplastic lymphoid cells.

Discussion and differential diagnosis

The cytomorphology of EMZL, SMZL, and NMZL is similar and usually better seen on air-dried Giemsa-stained preparations, especially the monocytoid cells, which tend to be poorly visualized on Pap-stained slides {14579297; 31757436; 24261323; 24167069; 19864232}.

Because the cytomorphology is of a mixed pattern, the differential diagnosis includes reactive lymphoid hyperplasia. This distinction may be made even more challenging if the MZL has residual reactive germinal centres. In general, reactive lymphoid hyperplasia will have more small lymphocytes, while the predominant cells in MZL are small to medium lymphoid cells with moderate amounts of cytoplasm. In EMZL, reactive conditions such as Hashimoto thyroiditis, chronic gastritis, and Sjögren syndrome (all of which predispose to the development of EMZL), as well as lymphocytic pneumonia, must be distinguished, and the diagnosis often relies on ancillary testing and surgical biopsy.

Also included in the differential diagnosis are other small-celled clinically indolent lymphomas. FL can present with a mixed cell pattern, but the small cells will be centrocytes and the presence of plasma cells and monocytoid cells is uncommon compared with MZL. Mantle cell lymphoma has a more monomorphic appearance as compared with MZL. In chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL), small monomorphic lymphoid cells make up most of the cell population, and variable numbers of prolymphocytes and paraimmunoblasts with central nucleoli are also present. Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma is composed of a variable mixture of small lymphocytes, plasmacytoid lymphocytes, and plasma cells and does not usually have monocytoid cells or centrocyte-like cells. Peripheral T-cell lymphoma presents with a mixed cell pattern, but larger atypical lymphoid cells predominate. Surgical pathology is often required, particularly in the absence of ancillary testing.

Ancillary testing

Ancillary tests are usually required to distinguish MZL from reactive conditions, as well as from other low-grade lymphomas. MZLs are clonal B-cell neoplasms that show light chain restriction by flow cytometry. By flow cytometry and/or immunocytochemistry, MZL is typically positive for CD19, CD20, CD79a, PAX5, and IgM (and occasionally other immunoglobulin classes), and typically negative for CD5, CD10, CD23, cyclin D1, SOX11, and LEF1 {23325563; 23813646; 12766579; 35066013}. However, MZL can be positive for CD5 in 5–10% of cases. In FL, germinal-centre B-cell markers such as CD10 and BCL6 are positive, whereas in CLL/SLL the tumour cells are positive for CD5, CD23, and LEF1 and display a characteristic flow cytometric immunophenotype {29024461}.

Genetic investigations of SMZL and NMZL show mutations of NOTCH genes (e.g. NOTCH2, NOTCH1, and SPEN) and NF-κB pathway genes (e.g. TNFAIP3, BIRC3, and TRAF3), as well as KLF2 mutations that regulate both NOTCH and NF-κB signalling. Recurrent mutations include KMT2D, PTPRD, NOTCH2, KLF2, and BRAF mutations. MZLs frequently carry trisomies of chromosomes 3 and 18, and NMZL shows trisomy 12, gains of chromosomes 2p and 6p, and loss of 1p and 6q as well. In EMZL, translocations involving MALT have been described that have a tendency to involve certain organs, such as stomach t(11;18)/BIRC3::MALT1 (24%) and lungs t(11;18)/BIRC3::MALT1 (40%) {35139275; 34653238; 19864232}. No recurrent gene fusions or rearrangements are seen in PCMZL or NMZL.
