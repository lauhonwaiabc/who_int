6.1.1.3 Discussion and background

An “Atypical” categorization should always include in the report a clear description of the cytopathological features, a statement as to which features are considered atypical, and the differential diagnosis of the material. The number of cases placed in this category should be kept to a minimum, and regular audits should be carried out to identify overuse.

Every FNAB of lymph nodes, spleen, and thymus can be categorized and reported as a cytopathology result, some just as a category with a discussion of the features, possible differential diagnosis, and recommended further diagnostic procedures, and others as a specific benign or malignant diagnosis. Ancillary testing can then be applied wherever it is available, and can include everything from a special stain such as a Ziehl–Neelsen stain for mycobacteria; to a cell block using special stains, immunocytochemistry, and molecular testing; to flow cytometry; and to cytogenetics, FISH, and molecular studies using scrapings from the direct smears or liquid-based cytopathology preparations.

The cytopathology report of an “Atypical” case can be issued immediately under the category, with the statement that ancillary tests have been ordered, or the cytopathologist can wait for ancillary test results before sending out any report. The ancillary tests will in many cases change the category of the “Atypical” report to “Benign” or “Malignant”. In the setting where ancillary testing is available, the only cases left in the “Atypical” category will be those with technically limited material for both cytopathology and ancillary testing, and those that have abundant lymphoid material for cytopathological assessment with some features that have raised the differential diagnosis of a reactive proliferation, such as benign follicular hyperplasia, and lymphoma but there is no material for ancillary testing.

If ancillary testing is available, the cytopathology report, including category and specific diagnosis or differential diagnosis, and all ancillary testing results should be integrated into one final report.
