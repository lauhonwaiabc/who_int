6.3.1 Sample reports: Atypical

The sample reports provided here are based on the standardized cytopathology report in the section Diagnostic categories and report structure section of the first chapter. See that section for a discussion on the structure and components.

These are sample reports that include the components required in one possible format; the final report format will depend on local practice and local laboratory information system requirements. For brevity, demographics and ROSE are not presented here.

Each institution should select a term for the main heading of the diagnostic summary, such as “Diagnostic summary” (as used in the examples below), “Diagnosis”, or “Final diagnosis”, and use it consistently.

Sample report 1

Specimen type:

FNAB left supraclavicular lymph node

Clinical and imaging information:

45-year-old male, 25 mm palpable left supraclavicular lymph node, 3 weeks after COVID-19 vaccination on the ipsilateral upper arm

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: ATYPICAL

Diagnosis: Mixed lymphoid population with scattered large atypical cells. See report below.

 

Macroscopic description:

Three air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. No needle rinsings available for flow cytometry.

 

Microscopic description:

These highly cellular smears show a mixed lymphoid population with plentiful larger lymphoid cells including immunoblasts and scattered large atypical lymphoid cells and plentiful small lymphocytes. Occasional germinal centres are present. No granulomas, no definite Reed–Sternberg cells, and no carcinoma cells are seen.

 

Comment: No cell block and no sample for flow cytometry are available. The predominance of the larger cells is considered atypical. Repeat FNAB with flow cytometry with or without CNB is recommended.

Sample report 2

Specimen type:

FNAB axillary lymph node

Clinical and imaging information:

63-year-old female with a single enlarged axillary lymph node. Lesion in breast on mammogram and ultrasound.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: ATYPICAL

Diagnosis: See report below.

 

Macroscopic description:

Two air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings prepared as cell block.

 

Microscopic description:

There is scant material consisting of a small amount of lymphoid material on the air-dried Giemsa-stained smears. The alcohol-fixed Pap-stained smears show severe air-drying artefact, but there are two minute possible epithelial tissue fragments.

 

Comment: There is scant material to assess, with features regarded as atypical. Repeat FNAB with or without CNB under ultrasound direction is recommended.

Sample report 3

Specimen type:

Endoscopic ultrasound–guided FNAB (EUS-FNAB) retroperitoneal lymph node

Clinical and imaging information:

75-year-old male, recent history of a resection of a pancreatic tumour (outside institution). Currently, 25 mm retroperitoneal lymph node.

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: ATYPICAL

Diagnosis: See report below.

 

Macroscopic description:

Six air-dried slides and two alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings centrifuged for cell block. No sample sent for flow cytometry.

 

Microscopic description:

There is a large amount of cellular material on the slide, consisting of contaminant gastric epithelium and mucin, with a moderate amount of haemodilute lymphoid material that shows a relatively monotonous small lymphoid cell population.

 

Comment: There is no evidence of metastatic carcinoma. The lymphoid material raises the possibility of a small cell lymphoma such as chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma and is considered atypical. Correlation with clinical, imaging, and haematological findings is required. Repeat FNAB or FNAB of any other lymphadenopathy is recommended.

Sample report 4

Specimen type:

FNAB cervical node

Clinical and imaging information:

F 25 with an enlarged, tender cervical node and fever

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: ATYPICAL

Diagnosis: See report below.

 

Macroscopic description:

One air-dried slide and one alcohol-fixed slide. No needle rinsings for cell block or for flow cytometry.

 

Microscopic description:

These mildly cellular smears show a dispersed, mixed lymphoid population with plentiful immunoblasts and scattered germinal-centre tissue fragments. There are several loose aggregates of epithelioid histiocytes, suggestive of granulomas, in addition to plasma cells, occasional eosinophils, and a small number of enlarged atypical cells with two or more enlarged nuclei and prominent one to three nucleoli and eccentric cytoplasm.

 

Comment: The polymorphous cytopathological features and clinical history are more suggestive of a reactive process, in particular EBV infection, and clinical and serological studies are required to confirm a diagnosis of EBV infection. But the possibility of Hodgkin lymphoma cannot be excluded completely, there is no flow cytometry or cell block available, and the lymphoid material is considered atypical. Repeat FNAB with flow cytometry (with or without CNB) and, if clinical suspicion is high, excision biopsy should be considered.

Sample report 5

Specimen type:

Ultrasound-guided FNAB lateral cervical lymph node

Clinical and imaging information:

40-year-old male with 12 mm right cervical lymph node showing some cystic features

 

Diagnostic summary:

Category: ATYPICAL

Diagnosis: See report below.

 

Macroscopic description:

Three air-dried slides and three alcohol-fixed slides. Needle rinsings prepared for cell block and sent for flow cytometry.

 

Microscopic description:

These mildly cellular smears show a dispersed mixed lymphoid population with no definite germinal-centre tissue fragments. There are several small tissue fragments composed of keratinizing squamous cells with occasional parakeratotic cells and keratinous debris. There is no dispersal of single epithelioid cells, and no definite malignant keratinizing or non-keratinizing cells are identified.

 

Comment: Scant keratinizing squamous cells in tissue fragments are present. The differential diagnosis lies between a metastatic well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma and a branchial cleft cyst. Given the young age of the patient and the lack of cytopathological features of malignancy, branchial cleft cyst is favoured, but the features are regarded as atypical, and clinical and imaging correlation is required to exclude a neoplastic lesion. Repeat FNAB with or without a CNB should be considered.
