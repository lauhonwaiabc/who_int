3.0.0.3 Choroid plexus carcinoma

Definition

Choroid plexus carcinoma (CPC) is a malignant epithelial neoplasm of the choroid plexus that shows at least four of the following five histological features: frequent mitoses, increased cellular density, nuclear pleomorphism, blurring of the papillary pattern with poorly structured sheets of tumour cells, and necrotic areas (CNS WHO grade 3).

ICD-O coding

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most CPCs are located in the lateral ventricles {20809071}.

Clinical features

CPCs tend to block cerebrospinal fluid pathways and cause symptoms related to hydrocephalus, such as increased intracranial pressure, increased head size, nausea, and vomiting {22938081}.

Imaging

On MRI, CPCs typically appear as large intraventricular lesions with irregular enhancing margins, a heterogeneous signal on T2- and T1-weighted images, oedema in adjacent brain, hydrocephalus, and disseminated tumour {15309348}.

Epidemiology

In the SEER database, CPCs accounted for 34.4% of choroid plexus tumours {25270349}. About 80% of all CPCs occur in children.

Etiology

Most CPCs occur sporadically, but about 40% occur in the context of Li–Fraumeni syndrome with germline TP53 pathogenic sequence variants {15925506; 20308654}. It is recommended that any patient with a CPC, and their family, be offered genetic counselling and testing for TP53 germline mutations {19204208; 26014290}. CPC has also been described in Aicardi syndrome {11124640}.

Pathogenesis

CPCs are believed to derive from monociliated progenitors of plexus epithelium located in the roof plate, and they show activation of the sonic hedgehog and Notch signalling pathways known to play a crucial role in the proliferation of these cells {26999738}. About 50% of CPCs carry TP53 mutations. In > 90% of TP53-wildtype CPCs, the combination of the TP53 p.R72 variant and the MDM2 SNP309 polymorphism, which is associated with reduced TP53 activity, was observed {20308654}, implicating p53 dysfunction in virtually all CPCs. TP53 mutations in CPC are associated with increased genomic instability {20308654}, with aneuploidy demonstrable by both classic cytogenetic and genome-wide array-based approaches {25336695; 11891207; 15642401; 24478045; 25575132}. These complex chromosomal alterations are related to patient age {24478045}, with childhood CPCs showing marked hypodiploidy {25575132}. TAF12, NFYC, and RAD54L oncogenes, within chromosomal gains at 1p35.3-p32, cooperate in disease initiation and progression and suggest potential therapeutic targets {25965574}.

Macroscopic appearance

CPCs are highly vascular tumours that may appear solid, haemorrhagic, or necrotic, and that show invasive growth.

Histopathology

CPCs demonstrate frank signs of malignancy, defined as showing at least four of the following five histological features: increased cellular density; nuclear pleomorphism; blurring of the papillary pattern with poorly structured sheets of tumour cells; necrotic areas; and frequent mitoses, usually > 2.5 mitoses/mm2 (equating to > 5 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.23 mm2) {17086103; 28677107}. Diffuse brain invasion is common.

Immunophenotype

Like choroid plexus papillomas, CPCs express cytokeratins, but they are less frequently positive for S100 and transthyretin. There is usually no membranous positivity for EMA. Distinct membranous staining for the potassium channel Kir7.1 is seen in about 50% of CPCs. Nuclear accumulation of p53 has been reported in CPCs that harboured TP53 mutation {20308654}. CPCs retain nuclear positivity for SMARCB1 and SMARCA4. The median Ki-67 index is reported as 20.3% (range: 7.8–42.5%) {19543851}.

Cytology

On touch preparation, CPC cells show high nuclear variance and a high N:C ratio.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CPCs can usually be identified by histological and immunophenotypic analysis. Genome-wide chromosomal copy-number analysis shows complex chromosomal alterations and characteristic hypoploidy {25575132}, which may be helpful in the diagnostic differentiation from atypical choroid plexus papillomas. TP53 mutations can be found in about 50% of cases by sequencing. Methylome analysis has revealed three clinically distinct subgroups of choroid plexus tumours, with all CPCs clustering within the same subgroup, together with prognostically unfavourable grade 1 and 2 choroid plexus tumours {26826203}.

Staging

Patients with CPC present with metastasis at diagnosis in 21% of cases {19543851}, so investigation for the presence of metastases is recommended.

Prognosis and prediction

The 5-year progression-free and overall survival rates in patients with CPC have been reported as 38% and 62%, respectively {25662896}. The extent of surgery has a significant impact on survival {25270349}. Several studies have suggested that the presence of TP53 mutations, as identified by immunohistochemical staining, is associated with a less favourable outcome {21990040; 20308654; 25662896}.

Essential:

Demonstration of choroid plexus differentiation by histopathological and immunophenotypic features

AND

Presence of at least four of the following five histological features:

·         Increased cellular density

·         Nuclear pleomorphism

·         Blurring of the papillary pattern with poorly structured sheets of tumour cells

·         Necrotic areas

·         Frequent mitoses, usually > 2.5 mitoses/mm2 in a minimum of 2.3 mm2 (equating to > 5 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.23 mm2)

AND

Intraventricular location

Desirable:

TP53 mutation analysis

Methylation profile of choroid plexus carcinoma

In select cases: demonstration of hypoploidy by genome-wide chromosomal copy-number analysis
