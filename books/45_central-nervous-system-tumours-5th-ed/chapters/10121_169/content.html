10.1.2.1 Other low-grade B-cell lymphomas of the CNS

Definition

Other low-grade B-cell lymphomas of the CNS are those lymphomas confined to the CNS at presentation that histologically correspond to one of the types of systemic low-grade B-cell lymphomas, most commonly extranodal marginal zone lymphoma; other cases have been diagnosed as small lymphocytic lymphoma, lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma, or low-grade B-cell lymphoma NOS.

ICD-O coding

9671/3 Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma

9690/3 Follicular lymphoma

ICD-11 coding

2A85.Y Further specified mature B-cell neoplasms or lymphoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours may occur as a solitary lesion or (infrequently) as multiple discrete lesions, or as diffuse involvement of the white matter of the brain. Lesions are mostly supratentorial (affecting lobes of the cerebral cortex or basal ganglia) {22295152; 29103980; 31426757; 32318699}, but the cerebellum and spinal cord also may be affected {22295152; 22994302}. Involvement of the leptomeninges and choroid plexus has been reported {16194887; 21239044; 27521434; 29103980}.

Clinical features

Male and female patients are roughly equally affected. Patients are almost all adults, with only rare paediatric cases being reported (age range: 5–79 years; median: 49 years) {22295152; 29103980; 32318699; 31808316}. Symptoms are variable, and they are occasionally present for months or years before diagnosis {21239044; 31426757}. The most common symptoms and signs include headache, seizures, and speech impairment {22295152; 24729110; 31426757; 31808316}. The clinical differential diagnosis can include glioma, intravascular lymphoma, and demyelinating disease {29103980; 32318699}. Radiographically, lesions may be well defined or infiltrative {22295152; 22994302; 24729110}.

Epidemiology

Low-grade B-cell lymphomas of the CNS are rare, and epidemiological data are not available.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown.

Pathogenesis

Limited data are available on genetic alterations in low-grade B-cell lymphoma arising in the CNS. Clonal rearrangement of IGH and/or IGK has been reported {23947787; 27521434; 32318699}. One case with loss of heterozygosity on 6q (including the locus for TNFAIP3) and one with MALT1 rearrangement are on record {32318699}. In the few cases tested, MYD88 p.L265P was absent {32318699}, and FISH for translocations of IGH, BCL2, and CCND1 was negative {16194887; 29103980}.

Macroscopic appearance

Limited data are available on macroscopy.

Histopathology

Most cases of low-grade B-cell lymphoma of the CNS correspond to extranodal marginal zone lymphoma {22994302; 32318699}; other cases have been diagnosed as small lymphocytic lymphoma {24729110; 31426757}, lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma {23947787}, or low-grade B-cell lymphoma NOS {15725082; 22295152}.

Immunophenotype

Neoplastic cells are typically CD20+, CD3−, CD5−, CD10−, CD23−, BCL6−, BCL2+, cyclin D1−, TdT−, EBV-encoded small RNA (EBER)− {22520340; 22994302; 32318699}. When present, plasma cells (CD138+) often express monotypic light chain. The proliferation index is low (< 10%) {24729110; 31426757}.

Cytology

Cerebrospinal fluid is negative in most cases, but occasional cases have cerebrospinal fluid involved by lymphoma, with the diagnosis confirmed by flow cytometry {22520340; 27521434; 31426757}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Clonal rearrangement of IGH and/or IGK genes has been reported {23947787; 27521434; 32318699}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #22832Box 10.06 .

Staging

Extent of disease is established using lumbar puncture for cytology and flow cytometry, complete blood count with differential, bone marrow biopsy with flow cytometry, and imaging to investigate sites of disease outside the CNS.

Prognosis and prediction

Low-grade B-cell lymphomas show a less aggressive course and have a better prognosis than do primary diffuse large B-cell lymphomas of the CNS. Patients have been treated with resection, steroids, radiation, and/or chemotherapy. Most are alive and free of disease or have stable disease on follow-up {23608841; 29103980; 32318699; 31808316}. Progression outside the CNS is very rare. A small subset of patients succumb to lymphoma {32318699}.

Essential:

Biopsy showing a lymphoma confined to the CNS at presentation that histologically corresponds to one of the types of systemic low-grade B-cell lymphoma

Desirable:

Detection of B-cell clonality by PCR
