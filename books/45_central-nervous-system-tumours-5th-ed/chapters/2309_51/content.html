2.3.0.9 Spinal ependymoma, MYCN-amplified

Definition

Spinal ependymoma, MYCN-amplified, is a well-demarcated spinal glioma demonstrating pseudorosettes or ependymal rosettes and comprising uniform, densely packed small cells with round nuclei embedded in a fibrillary matrix. Practically all tumours display microvascular proliferation, necrosis, and a high mitotic count. By definition, MYCN amplification is demonstrated in tumour cells.

Localization

Tumours are localized to the spinal cord, primarily to the cervical or thoracic levels (in 78% of cases) and less frequently to lumbar levels (in 7% of cases) {11303789; 31373367; 31414211; 32641156}.

Primary tumours may be intramedullary (sometimes with an exophytic component extending into the spinal canal {31373367}) or mostly extramedullary {31414211; 32641156}. They are generally large and involve multiple spinal segments. Leptomeningeal dissemination is frequent at diagnosis, or it occurs at some point during the course of the disease {11303789; 31373367; 31414211; 32641156}.

Clinical features

Presenting symptoms depend on tumour location, but they typically include neck or back pain and progressive numbness and weakness in the extremities.

Epidemiology

MYCN-amplified spinal ependymoma is rare, with only 27 reported cases (17 in women and 10 in men; M:F ratio: 1:1.7). The median age at presentation was 31 years (range: 12–56 years) {11303789; 31373367; 31414211; 32641156}.

Etiology

No specific etiology has been identified. However, multiple schwannomas were reported in 1 patient, raising the diagnostic possibility of neurofibromatosis type 2 {11303789}. No other patient has shown any signs of neurofibromatosis type 2, and no NF2 mutation was found in the 4 analysed cases {31373367; 31414211}.

Pathogenesis

MYCN, a member of the MYC family of proto-oncogenes, encodes a transcription factor that regulates the expression of genes involved in cell growth {24589438}. How MYCN amplification contributes specifically to ependymoma development is unknown.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic features of this specific tumour type have not been described, but they are unlikely to differ markedly from those reported for other circumscribed high-grade gliomas.

Histopathology

MYCN-amplified spinal ependymomas display perivascular anucleate zones (pseudorosettes) and can have a papillary or pseudopapillary architecture {31373367; 31414211; 32641156}. Most have high-grade histopathological features, such as a high N:C ratio, plentiful mitotic activity, microvascular proliferation, and necrosis. Two of four tumours with CNS WHO grade 2 histopathological features at presentation progressed to CNS WHO grade 3 at recurrence {11303789; 31373367; 31414211}.

Immunophenotype

On immunohistochemistry, tumour cells express GFAP and show a focal cytoplasmic dot-like pattern of EMA expression {31373367; 31414211}. MYCN protein expression can be detected by immunohistochemistry {31414211; 32641156}. Cells are immunonegative for OLIG2 {31373367}. Nuclear immunoreactivity for H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) was variably present in the 4 tumours from one study {31373367}, but was retained in all tumours from another {31414211}.

Grading

Although nearly all MYCN-amplified spinal ependymomas show high-grade histopathological features and have a poor prognosis, this molecularly defined ependymal tumour has yet to be assigned a CNS WHO grade {32502305}.

Cytology

Tumour cells are generally small and have round hyperchromatic nuclei and scant cytoplasm.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

High-level MYCN amplification is present and remains stable at relapse {11303789; 31373367; 31414211; 32641156}. Additional chromosomal copy-number alterations occur with variable frequency and include loss of chromosome 10 (in 32% of cases) and focal losses on chromosome 11q (in 26% of cases). Demonstration of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) loss by immunohistochemistry requires assessment of histone H3 genes for genetic alterations, because MYCN amplification can be found in diffuse midline gliomas with H3 p.K28M (K27M) mutation {24705254}.

MYCN-amplified spinal ependymoma has a DNA methylation profile distinct from that of other ependymal tumour types, as well as from that of neuroblastoma and MYCN-amplified paediatric-type glioblastoma {31414211; 32641156}.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

MYCN-amplified spinal ependymoma is an aggressive tumour associated with poor progression-free and overall survival compared with that of other spinal ependymomas. Early metastasis and dissemination throughout the neuraxis are frequent. All patients with reported follow-up data have relapsed despite aggressive treatment {11303789; 31373367; 31414211; 32641156}.
