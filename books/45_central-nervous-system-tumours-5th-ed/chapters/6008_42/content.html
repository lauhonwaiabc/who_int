6.0.0.8 Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumour (previously paraganglioma)

Definition

Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumour is a neuroendocrine neoplasm arising from specialized neural crest cells in the cauda equina / filum terminale region.

ICD-O coding

8693/3 Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumour (previously paraganglioma)

ICD-11 coding

2A02.0Y & XH1X68 Other specified gliomas of spinal cord, cranial nerves, or other parts of the central nervous system & Paraganglioma, benign

2A02.0Y & XH0EW6 Other specified gliomas of spinal cord, cranial nerves, or other parts of the central nervous system & Paraganglioma, NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: paraganglioma of the cauda equina; cauda equina paraganglioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority of spinal paragangliomas / neuroendocrine tumours are located in the cauda equina region {15796363}. Most neuroendocrine tumours of the cauda equina are entirely intradural and are attached either to the filum terminale or (less often) to a caudal nerve root {2875784}.

Clinical features

Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours exhibit no clinical features that allow their distinction from other spinal cord tumours. The most common presenting symptoms include a history of low back pain and sciatica. Less common manifestations are numbness, paraparesis, and sphincter symptoms {15796363}. Fully developed cauda equina syndrome is uncommon, as are signs of increased intracranial pressure and papilloedema {22271522; 15796363}. Endocrinologically functional neuroendocrine tumours of the cauda equina region, which are extremely rare {15796363}, can lead to signs and symptoms of catecholamine hypersecretion such as episodic or sustained hypertension, palpitations, diaphoresis, and headache. Subarachnoid haemorrhage is another unusual presentation of cauda equina neuroendocrine tumour {17293223}. Cerebrospinal fluid protein is usually markedly increased {6831356; 2875784}.

Imaging

MRI findings are nonspecific {22271522}, and the appearance of paraganglioma is indistinguishable from that of schwannoma or ependymoma {25013343}. Tumours are sharply circumscribed and occasionally partly cystic, and they can be hypointense, isointense, or hyperintense on T1- and T2-weighted images. Gadolinium enhancement may be present or absent. A low signal intensity rim (cap sign) on T2-weighted images can be caused by subcapsular haemosiderin {8456679; 15803375; 25720695}. The hypervascular architecture can give a salt-and-pepper appearance on T2-weighted images. Serpentine flow voids along the tumour surface or spinal cord margin may also be seen at MRI {20637630; 31379984}.

Epidemiology

Neuroendocrine tumours of the cauda equina region are uncommon, but nearly 300 cases have been reported since their initial description in 1970 {31247160; 4193811}. In a series of 430 spinal tumours, < 3% were paragangliomas / neuroendocrine tumours {20594020; 21811702}, and of cauda equina region tumours, 3.4–3.8% are neuroendocrine tumours {9491294; 15803375}. Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours generally affect adults, with a peak incidence in the fourth through sixth decades of life. Patient age ranges from 9 to 75 years (mean: 46–47 years) {25720695; 31247160}, with a slight predominance in male patients (M:F ratio: 1.5:1) {15796363; 31247160}.

Etiology

Almost all neuroendocrine tumours of the cauda equina are sporadic. In contrast, as many as half of all phaeochromocytomas/paragangliomas outside the CNS in adults, and > 80% of these tumours in children, are inherited {22328163}. The role of germline mutations in the pathogenesis of paragangliomas outside the cauda equina region is discussed in section Chapter 14: Genetic tumour syndromes involving the CNS.

Pathogenesis

Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours are histogenetically and molecularly distinct from paragangliomas and phaeochromocytomas outside the CNS. They overexpress the transcription factor HOXB13 {33768604}, which is developmentally expressed in the caudal extent of the spinal cord and in the urogenital sinus. It coincides with dynamic changes associated with the formation of the secondary neural tube {8756292; 12679105}, providing circumstantial evidence of a cell of origin. Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours have not been seen in people with hereditary paraganglioma/phaeochromocytoma syndrome due to mutations in SDH subunit genes {2741965}.

Genetic profile

The molecular alterations that drive tumorigenesis in cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours are unknown. The mutations found in paragangliomas outside the cauda equina region are described in section Chapter 14: Genetic tumour syndromes involving the CNS. The genetic and epigenetic profiles (i.e. methylation, expression {24859990; 23707781}, microRNA {23660872}, and metabolomics {25625332; 25622899}) of phaeochromocytomas/paragangliomas with succinate dehydrogenase defects differ substantially from those with other genetic causes.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are oval to sausage-shaped, delicately encapsulated, soft, red-brown masses that bleed freely; in 5 series of 59 spinal tumours, size ranged from 10 mm to 112 mm in greatest dimension {25013343; 25720695; 22770767}. Capsular calcification and cystic components may be found. An occasional tumour penetrates the dura to invade bone. For tumours in the cauda equina, a macroscopically identifiable attachment may be found, either to the filum terminale or (less often) to a caudal nerve root {2875784}.

Histopathology

Cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours / paragangliomas are well-differentiated. They are composed of chief (type I) cells disposed in nests or lobules (Zellballen), surrounded by an inconspicuous, single layer of sustentacular (type II) cells. The Zellballen are surrounded by a delicate capillary network and a delicate supporting reticulin fibre network that may undergo sclerosis. The uniform round or polygonal chief cells have central, round to oval nuclei with finely stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. Degenerative nuclear pleomorphism (endocrine anaplasia) is generally mild. Cytoplasm is usually eosinophilic and finely granular; in some instances, it is amphophilic or clear. Sustentacular cells are spindle-shaped; encompassing the lobules, their long processes are often so attenuated as to be undetectable by routine light microscopy and visible only on immunostains for S100. Approximately 25% of cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours contain mature ganglion cells and a Schwann cell component (gangliocytic neuroendocrine tumours). Ependymoma-like perivascular formations are also common. Some tumours show architectural features reminiscent of carcinoid tumours, including angiomatous, adenomatous, and pseudorosette patterns {2875784}. Tumours composed predominantly of spindle cells {9279569} and melanin-containing cells (melanotic neuroendocrine tumours) {9279569} have also been described at this site, as have oncocytic neuroendocrine tumours {2422107; 12053045}. Foci of haemorrhagic necrosis may occur, and scattered mitotic figures can be seen, but neither these features nor nuclear pleomorphism is of prognostic significance {2875784}.

Immunophenotype

The neuroendocrine chief cells are immunoreactive for chromogranin A and synaptophysin {2684087; 2875784; 24476517}, and they show variable S100 immunoreactivity. Immunoreactivity for cytokeratins (especially CAM5.2, AE1/AE3, and MNF116) has been described in the majority of cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours {1280616; 10199484; 7782837; 24476517; 23912549; 31442521; 32892244}, but it has also been described in other locations {31442521}.

Furthermore, gangliocytic neuroendocrine tumours / paragangliomas containing a variable mixture of epithelioid neuroendocrine cells, Schwann-like cells, and scattered ganglion-like cells can show cytokeratin positivity in the epithelioid cells {20515359; 15892009}. Expression of serotonin (5-HT) and of various neuropeptides (somatostatin, leu-enkephalin, and met-enkephalin) has been demonstrated in neuroendocrine tumour of the cauda equina region {9279569; 2875784}.

Sustentacular cells show inconsistent S100 protein reactivity {23599157; 29239044} and occasionally express GFAP {15921911}. They often express the neural crest transcription factor SOX10 {18636017}. The value of proliferation markers in cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours has not been established.

Unlike paragangliomas of the autonomic nervous system {23599157}, cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours do not express the zinc finger transcription factor GATA3 {32892244}. They express HOXB13 (a transcription factor that is developmentally expressed in progenitor cells of the caudal spinal cord), a feature they share with myxopapillary ependymoma {33768604}.

Grading

Neuroendocrine tumours of the cauda equina correspond to CNS WHO grade 1.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The DNA methylation profiles and chromosomal copy-number profiles of cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours are distinct from those of paragangliomas arising from other locations {32892244; 32926213}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

See #17830Box 6.07 .

Staging

Not relevant

Prognosis and prediction

The vast majority of cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours are slow-growing and curable by total excision; only 4% recur after gross total removal {7782837}. Cerebrospinal fluid seeding of spinal paragangliomas has occasionally been documented {2749382; 8738400; 7782837; 16283104; 28128698}, and metastasis outside the CNS (to the bone) from cauda equina neuroendocrine tumours has been reported only once {9279569}. In contrast, 10–20% of paragangliomas outside the CNS have metastatic potential {23398849; 31599769}.
