2.3.0.6 Posterior fossa group A (PFA) ependymoma

Definition

Posterior fossa group A (PFA) ependymoma is a circumscribed posterior fossa glioma aligned with the PFA molecular group of ependymomas, demonstrating pseudorosettes or ependymal rosettes and comprising uniform small cells with round nuclei embedded in a fibrillary matrix. An ependymoma can be classified as PFA by identifying a loss of nuclear H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) expression in tumour cells or by DNA methylation profiling.

Localization

Studies correlating neuroimaging with molecular group have suggested that PFA ependymomas more frequently arise from the roof or lateral aspect of the fourth ventricle than from its floor {21840481; 29909548}.

Clinical features

The clinical features of PFA ependymomas are similar to those described for posterior fossa ependymomas in general.

Epidemiology

PFA ependymomas predominantly occur in infants and children, with a median age of 3 years {21840481; 29909548; 25965575; 27269943}. The proportion of posterior fossa ependymomas classified as PFA aligns with age: > 95% of posterior fossa ependymomas in children aged < 6 years are PFA tumours, and PFB ependymomas are rare in this age group; the proportion of posterior fossa ependymomas classified as PFA decreases to 45–50% in adolescents and 5–11% in adults {21840481; 29909548; 25965575; 27269943}. PFA ependymomas are slightly more prevalent in male patients (59–62%) than in female patients {21840481; 29909548; 25965575; 27269943}.

Etiology

Although the exact etiology of PFA ependymomas is unknown, it is hypothesized that aberrant epigenetic alterations may be central drivers. Associations with specific genetic susceptibilities have not been reported.

Pathogenesis

Cell of origin

PFA ependymomas are thought to arise from an undifferentiated glial stem or progenitor cell in the developing hindbrain {31043743}.

Molecular profile

PFA ependymomas exhibit characteristic DNA methylation patterns, including hypermethylation of CpG islands and global DNA hypomethylation {24553142; 25965575; 27881822}. PFA ependymomas show a global reduction of the repressive histone mark H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3), which impacts several pathways, including neuroglial differentiation and cell-cycle regulation {27881822; 29909548}, and is caused by overexpression of EZHIP {29909548}. EZHIP phenotypically mimics the oncohistone H3 p.K28M (K27M) by binding to the H3 p.K28 (K27) methyltransferase EZH2 and inhibiting the function of PRC2 {31086175; 31451685; 31281901; 30923826}. Although most PFA ependymomas do not carry recurrent genetic mutations, about 9% exhibit mutations in EZHIP. In addition, about 4% harbour H3 p.K28M (K27M) mutations, which are mutually exclusive with EZHIP mutations {23592488; 27539613; 29909548; 28623522}. A study that examined 675 PFA ependymomas by DNA methylation profiling identified 2 molecular subgroups and 9 molecular subtypes of PFA ependymomas {29909548}. However, the clinicopathological implications of these findings have yet to be fully evaluated.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic features of PFA ependymomas are similar to those described for posterior fossa ependymomas in general.

Histopathology

PFA ependymomas show the histopathological features described for posterior fossa ependymomas in general. High-grade features, including prominent mitotic activity and microvascular proliferation, were observed in 64% of PFA ependymomas in one study {29909548}. Clear cell, papillary, and tanycytic patterns can be focally present {31679042}.

Immunophenotype

PFA ependymomas exhibit a reduction in H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3), which can be readily assessed by immunohistochemistry {28733933; 27881822; 29909548}. Retained H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) immunoreactivity in endothelial cells can be used as an internal control for the method. In most PFA ependymomas, tumour cells show a global reduction in H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) expression, but variability in the proportion of immunonegative cells can be encountered. A cut-off value of 80% immunopositive cells has been proposed, above which an ependymoma is more likely to fall into the PFB molecular group {28733933; 27881822; 30514397; 31850684; 31324163; 30768777}.

Grading

PFA ependymomas show varying degrees of anaplasia and have been regarded as CNS WHO grade 2 or 3 on this basis. Such information should be included in an integrated diagnosis {24990071}.

Cytology

Cytological preparations generally show uniform cells with round nuclei and sparse delicate cytoplasmic processes. Nuclear pleomorphism is generally mild, but it is exaggerated in the rare giant cell phenotype {16389948}. Tumour cells can form clusters and palisades around vascular structures, reflecting the arrangement of perivascular pseudorosettes.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of loss of H3 p.K28me3 (K27me3) by immunohistochemistry, or assignment to the PFA molecular group by DNA methylation profiling, is required for a diagnosis of PFA ependymoma. Because of its prognostic significance across all posterior fossa ependymomas {22526017}, gain of chromosome 1q is often assessed in PFA ependymomas, even though molecular subtypes of PFA ependymoma with or without 1q gain can be associated with a poor outcome {29909548}.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Extent of surgical resection is associated with outcome {22526017}. PFA ependymomas have a poor prognosis compared with that of PFB ependymomas {25965575}. Gain of chromosome 1q is a reproducible adverse prognostic indicator across all posterior fossa ependymomas {11953826; 22338015; 22526017}. However, among molecular subtypes of PFA ependymoma, those with or without 1q gain can have an equally poor outcome {29909548}. The prognostic significance of an H3 p.K28 (K27) mutation in a small proportion of PFA ependymomas is unknown {27539613; 28623522}.
