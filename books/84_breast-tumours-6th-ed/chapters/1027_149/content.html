10.2.7 PALB2-related cancer predisposition syndrome (PALB2)

Definition

PALB2-related cancer predisposition syndrome is caused by germline pathogenic variants in PALB2 and is associated with increased susceptibility to breast cancer.

MIM numbering

610355 Partner and localizer of BRCA2; PALB2

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: Fanconi anaemia, complementation group N.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PALB2 germline pathogenic variants are associated with cancers of the breast and the pancreas.

Clinical features

Patients with monoallelic germline PALB2 variants have increased susceptibility to breast cancer {25099575;21285249;17200668}. In a study sequencing germline DNA from 113 927 women including 60 466 breast cancer cases and 53 461 controls, protein-truncating variants in PALB2 were shown to be strongly associated with a risk of breast cancer overall (odds ratio [OR]: 5.02; 95% CI: 3.73–6.76) {33471991}. A large-scale weighted meta-analysis involving 101 397 women with breast cancer and 312 944 women without breast cancer showed OR 4.30 (95% CI: 3.68–5.03) for breast cancer association, with 0.54% (1 in 187) and 0.14% (1 in 726) in average frequencies of pathogenic variants among studies in breast cancer cases and controls, respectively {38986768}.

Somatic mutations of PALB2 in breast cancer tissues are infrequently observed. According to the American Association for Cancer Research (AACR) Genomics Evidence Neoplasia Information Exchange (GENIE) project cohort, pathogenic variants of PALB2 were found in 289 of 18 255 breast cancer samples (1.6%) [[cBioPortal Internet. New York (NY): Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center; 2024 cited 2024 Dec 19. Available from: https://www.cbioportal.org. ]] {}.

Epidemiology

Although rare in the general population (< 1%), monoallelic germline PALB2 pathogenic variants are identified in 1–5% of familial breast cancer patients {21285249;24136930}. Unlike BRCA1 and BRCA2 pathogenic variants, PALB2 variants do not appear to be strongly associated with either Ashkenazi Jewish heritage or ethnically diverse Jewish ancestry and are not significantly detected in case series including these populations {21285249;22692731}. Fanconi anaemia has a prevalence of about 1 case per 130 000 live births in the USA, and only a subset of these cases are attributable to biallelic PALB2 variants {17200671;17200672}.

Etiology

Patients with PALB2-associated familial breast cancers inherit a monoallelic pathogenic variant in the gene. The acquisition of a second hit, whether through somatic point mutations or loss of heterozygosity, causes biallelic inactivation in the majority of cancers, leading to PALB2 loss of function {29431189}.

Pathogenesis

PALB2 is a 38 kb gene located at chromosome 16p12.2 that spans 13 exons and encodes the tumour suppressor protein PALB2, which is involved in DNA double strand–break repair by homologous recombination. One of its critical roles is the binding to and stabilization of BRCA2, permitting nuclear BRCA2 accumulation {16793542;19268590;19423707}. PALB2 also promotes the incorporation of the tumour suppressor protein RAD51 into the processed single-stranded DNA end for strand exchange in homologous recombination repair {16793542;19268590;19423707}. Impairments in PALB2 function therefore lead to defective double strand–break repair, which facilitates non-homologous end joining of DNA breaks and accumulation of mutations.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is not known to be at variance with that of sporadic breast cancer.

Histopathology

Germline protein-truncating variants in PALB2 are shown to be more significantly associated with ER-negative breast cancer than ER-positive breast cancer. The PALB2 protein-truncating variants are also shown to be associated with invasive tumours rather than in situ tumours {33471991}. In a large-scale weighted meta-analysis, PALB2 pathogenic variants are shown to be associated with triple-negative breast cancer (OR: 11.31; 95% CI: 7.77–16.47) {38986768}.

Cytology

The cytology is not known to be at variance with that of sporadic breast cancer.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PALB2 variants are most often identified by next-generation sequencing performed on patient blood samples. Increasingly, these tests use multigene panels that assess for a large number of genes affiliated with familial breast cancer risk.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: germline pathogenic variants in PALB2.

Staging

The staging is the same as for sporadic breast cancer.

Prognosis and prediction

In a prospective cohort study involving 17 900 women with breast cancer and 4702 controls, two PALB2 pathogenic variants, i.e. NM_024675.4:c.509_510del p.(Arg170fs) and NM_024675.4:c.172_175del p.(Gln60fs), were associated with worse 10-year overall survival (48.0%; 95% CI: 36.5–63.2%) compared with the control population (74.4%; 95% CI: 73.5–75.8%) {25959805}. Patients with breast cancer with PALB2 variants are at an increased risk not only for secondary breast cancers, but also for pancreatic cancers. Increased screening may therefore be warranted in this population {25099575;21285249;25356972}. Several studies suggest that PALB2 germline pathogenic variants are associated with functional BRCA deficiency, raising the possibility of therapeutic intervention with poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors {28288110;31090900}.
