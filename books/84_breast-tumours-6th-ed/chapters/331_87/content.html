3.3.1 Rare and salivary gland–type tumours: Introduction

Breast cancer subtypes discussed in this section include four types of salivary gland–like breast carcinomas (adenoid cystic carcinoma, secretory carcinoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma, and acinic cell–like carcinoma) and two types of rare invasive breast carcinomas (mucinous cystadenocarcinoma and tall cell carcinoma with reversed polarity).

Of note, in this edition, polymorphous adenocarcinoma has been removed from the salivary gland–like tumours group because there are only rare case reports of this entity in the breast. In addition, acinic cell carcinomas have been renamed acinic cell–like carcinomas because recent molecular evidence indicates that these lesions in the breast show different genetic alterations from acinic cell carcinomas of the salivary glands and appear to be more closely related to the microglandular adenosis family of lesions.

All of the tumours discussed in this section are usually triple negative, but most show favourable prognosis. On the other hand, a small subset of salivary gland–like carcinomas may have more-aggressive behaviour {39389890;37651394}. Therefore, accurate diagnosis of these tumours has important implications for prognosis and correct clinical management, especially to avoid overtreatment of low-grade tumours with indolent biology. All of these tumours have characteristic molecular alterations that may be valuable for accurate diagnosis.
