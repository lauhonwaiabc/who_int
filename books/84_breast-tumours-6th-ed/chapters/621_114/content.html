6.2.1 Haemangioma

Definition

Haemangioma is a benign proliferation of mature blood vessels.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: angioma.

Subtype(s)

Perilobular haemangioma; capillary haemangioma; venous haemangioma; cavernous haemangioma; sinusoidal haemangioma

Localization

Haemangiomas may occur in the mammary parenchyma or subcutaneous tissue.

Clinical features

In most cases, haemangiomas are non-palpable and are found on imaging (ultrasound, MRI, or mammography). Occasional palpable breast lesions have been described {17065282;16684325;31562669}. Mammary haemangiomas do not have pathognomonic imaging features. Mammography may show lobular-shaped masses with circumscribed or microlobulated margins and densities equal to that of breast parenchyma {18562711}. MRI appearance can vary, although is typically T2-hyperintense and T1-hypointense with variable enhancement {38557759}. Calcifications, including phleboliths, are occasionally seen {18562711}. Most parenchymal and non-parenchymal haemangiomas measure < 20 mm (range: 3–60 mm) {4091183;4061730;15171310;16122203}.

Epidemiology

Haemangiomas are rare benign lesions of the breast and have been reported in patients ranging in age from 18 months to 82 years {1358220;22434703;38557759}. The majority of cases that come to clinical attention are described in female patients {11218030;16122203}. The most common type of haemangioma in the breast is perilobular haemangioma, which is estimated to be present in 1.2–11% of breast specimens {868802;6687795}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopic examination reveals a circumscribed red or dark-brown spongy mass with smooth homogeneous cut surfaces and possible slit-like spaces {4051098;38222768}.

Histopathology

Breast haemangiomas are characterized by the proliferation of well-formed vessels of varying sizes. The vascular channels can be interconnecting but are most often non-anastomosing, a feature distinguishing haemangioma from low-grade angiosarcoma. The vessels are lined by endothelial cells showing neither substantial nuclear atypia nor mitotic activity. Occasional hyperchromatic nuclei may be seen, with no adverse clinical implication. The vascular channels are found within the interlobular stroma, with the notable exception of perilobular haemangiomas, in which the intralobular stroma is also involved. A larger feeding vessel may be found within the lesion or in close proximity. The lesions are more or less well circumscribed, with isolated small vessels found in the surrounding breast tissue in the periphery of the lesion. Haemorrhage with disruption of the vascular channels is typical after FNA or core needle biopsy. Thrombosis with secondary endothelial hyperplasia can occur. Microcalcifications can be found within thrombotic channels or in surrounding fibrous stroma.

Perilobular haemangiomas are incidental microscopic vascular lesions, measuring < 2 mm, located primarily in the intralobular stroma or surrounding breast tissue. They are composed of a more or less well-circumscribed conglomerate of small, thin-walled, congested capillary vessels containing erythrocytes {4091183}. Other types of haemangiomas (cavernous, capillary, venous {4051098;4061730}) can be encountered, similar to in other anatomical sites. Sinusoidal haemangiomas, a subset of cavernous haemangiomas, may be seen in the subcutis of the breast and are composed of interconnected, dilated, thin-walled vessels. Although usually lobulated and circumscribed, they may contain a focally infiltrative component, mimicking a more worrisome lesion {1746680}.

Cytology

Diagnosis of haemangiomas has not been made on cytopathology.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-formed vascular spaces lined by a single layer of benign endothelial cells; a layer of pericytes around individual vascular channels.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

In the past, the discovery of a vascular lesion with features of a haemangioma on a breast core biopsy prompted surgical excision to exclude the diagnosis of well-differentiated angiosarcoma. More-recent data suggest that, especially in cases with radiological–pathological correlation, excision may not be mandatory for benign vascular lesions of the breast identified in core needle biopsy specimens {27340752;28644513;31001867;32166889}.
