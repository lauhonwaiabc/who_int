5.2.1 Syringomatous tumour

Definition

Syringomatous tumour (SyT) is a locally infiltrative neoplasm of the nipple and areolar region composed of epithelial cells with glandular and squamous morphology.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: syringomatous tumour of the nipple.

Not recommended: syringomatous adenoma of the nipple; infiltrating syringomatous adenoma of the nipple.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SyT is located in the dermis of the nipple and areola and infiltrates around lactiferous sinuses and into the areolar smooth muscle.

Clinical features

SyT is detected as a firm palpable mass in the dermis of the nipple and subareolar region in about 68% of cases; nipple retraction and nipple discharge are common (32% and 25%, respectively), and erythema, ulceration, tenderness, and pruritus may also occur {37346881}. The tumour size ranges between 5 and 40 mm but may be larger {24713747}. Bilateral SyT is exceedingly rare {24713747;8381340}. SyT may be difficult to detect by mammogram or ultrasound because of its superficial location and presence within dense areolar smooth muscle. If seen on imaging, the tumour may have irregular, circumscribed, or indistinct borders {24648330;21092877;34926057}. When calcifications are present, they are typically coarse and limited to the nipple papilla and may be the only abnormal finding on imaging {21092877;24648330}.

Epidemiology

SyT is rare, with < 100 cases reported, which describe age ranges from 11 to 76 years (mean age: 44.4 years ± 16), with most (68%) patients being < 50 years. Only occasional cases in male patients have been described {6660349;22649338;34926057}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Although SyT has a similar appearance to syringoma, a tumour thought to arise from skin adnexal eccrine glands, no eccrine glands are present in the nipple. SyT differs from syringoma also in terms of its glandular immunoprofile, which resembles that of the glandular epithelium of large breast ducts {22355740}. Origin from the lactiferous sinuses could also account for the specific location of this tumour.

Macroscopic appearance

SyT forms a firm, poorly defined mass in the dermis of the nipple and subareolar region.

Histopathology

The tumour infiltrates into the dermis and smooth muscle of the nipple and areola and may occasionally extend focally into the underlying breast parenchyma usually with poorly circumscribed tumour borders {6660349;2919717}. SyT grows around the lactiferous sinuses but does not invade into the ducts or epidermis. Pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia of the overlying skin may occur. The surrounding stroma is either fibrous or loosely cellular and may be accentuated around the tumour cells. No lymphovascular invasion is observed; perineural invasion may be present, which should not be confused with infiltration around areolar smooth-muscle bundles. The tumour cells form small solid nests and cords that are often tadpole-shaped with comma-like tails. There are usually multiple cell layers, although single-layered glands can occur. The cells are cytologically bland with round to oval nuclei. Mitoses are rare and apoptosis may be present, but tumour necrosis is not a feature.

SyT consists of cells without nuclear atypia but with heterogeneous cell types {22355740;24382117}. The predominant cell has myoepithelial-like features, including a peripheral location in the tumour nests. These cells express p63 and high-molecular-weight cytokeratins (including CK5 and CK14) and show variable expression of other myoepithelial markers. Squamous cells may be centrally located within the tumour nests and frequently express p63 or p40. These cells express CK10. Cysts containing keratin may be present in the superficial portion of the tumour. Calcifications in keratinaceous debris may occur and are detected by mammography. Glandular spaces may be present within some tumour nests; the glandular cells have a luminal-like phenotype and express low-molecular-weight cytokeratins CK8 and CK18. A PAS-positive secretion may be present in the lumina. Tumour cells are usually negative for hormone receptors and ERBB2 (HER2), although focal weak staining for ER may be observed.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma, tubular carcinoma, nipple adenoma, and dermal adnexal tumours {26838328}. SyT closely resembles low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma, as both tumours consist of multiple cell types including glandular and squamous cells {26838282}. However, low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma arises deeper in the breast and rarely invades into the nipple; furthermore, it shows nuclear atypia and scattered mitoses, the glandular lumina may contain focal necrotic debris, and rarely an associated neoplastic spindle cell component may be present. The two tumours may be difficult to distinguish in a superficial biopsy, especially if information on whether the tumour mass involves predominantly the superficial or deep aspect of the nipple is not known. Tubular carcinoma can resemble the glandular areas of SyT, but does not display squamous differentiation, and rarely invades the dermis; the tumour cells usually are ER-positive, do not express high-molecular-weight cytokeratins or p63, and lack myoepithelial cells. Nipple adenomas occur in the same location as SyTs, but they do not infiltrate around normal structures and have a continuous myoepithelial layer. SyT closely resembles some skin adnexal tumours, such as syringoma and microcystic adnexal carcinoma. However, the location in the nipple and type of glandular differentiation observed in SyT (CK8/18-positive, akin to the glandular epithelium of large breast ducts) support its categorization as a breast-specific neoplasm {22355740}.

In a systematic review, preoperative biopsy was not diagnostic in seven of nine SyTs {37346881}.

Cytology

There are scant publications about this rare lesion, but one case report stated that bland ductal cells were present intermingled with stroma {10097731}, consistent with a benign process.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelial tumour locally infiltrative in the dermis and smooth muscle of the nipple and areolar region, composed of bland-appearing cells with glandular and squamous morphology with rare mitoses; tumour cells do not destructively invade into ducts or the epidermis.

Desirable: keratin-filled cysts may be present in a superficial location.

Staging

SyT is not classified as a malignancy and is not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

SyT is locally infiltrative, but there is no definitive evidence that it can metastasize. There is a single report of isolated tumour cells in an axillary lymph node in a patient with SyT, but it is unclear whether the illustrated cells are neoplastic or may represent displaced epithelium secondary to prior breast lesion excision {12653606}.

The optimal treatment is complete excision. Local recurrence of SyT is possible when surgical margins are positive {2919717}.
