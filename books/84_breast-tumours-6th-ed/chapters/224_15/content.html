2.2.4 Microglandular adenosis

Definition

Microglandular adenosis (MGA) is a non-lobulocentric haphazard proliferation of small round glands consisting of a single layer of epithelial cells without an accompanying myoepithelial cell layer, but surrounded entirely by basement membrane

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Classic microglandular adenosis (C-MGA); atypical microglandular adenosis (A-MGA)

Localization

MGA occurs in the breast; it is usually unifocal, and rarely multifocal {6852780} or bilateral {6859388}.

Clinical features

MGA can be diagnosed incidentally in breast biopsies performed for other reasons. It may also form a mass {21847399} or present as a mammographic density or occasionally as mammographically detected calcifications. Carcinomas arising in MGA have presented as a palpable mass {25337263}.

Epidemiology

MGA is rare with no well-defined global prevalence or incidence rates in the general population. In as many as 27% of cases it is described in association with breast carcinoma {17824796}. It has been reported in adult women over a wide age range, including young women {20846267}.

Etiology

No specific factors have been identified that are etiologically linked to MGA.

Pathogenesis

Several array comparative genomic hybridization–based analyses showed that progressive molecular changes were present in MGA and associated invasive carcinomas. The acquisition of genetic alterations, identified in the progression from MGA to the synchronous invasive carcinoma component, suggests a clonal evolution associated with p53 mutations {22486256;26806567;27713419}. Invasive carcinoma arising in the context of MGA is linked to germline alterations in homologous recombination–deficiency genes {40691172;40081883}.

Macroscopic appearance

MGA does not have a characteristic appearance on macroscopic evaluation, as it usually presents as a poorly defined nodularity or mass. A mass is evident when carcinomatous transformation is present.

Histopathology

MGA can show a variety of morphological patterns, which can be subdivided as follows.

C-MGA is composed of small, round, tubular structures that grow in a haphazard non-lobulocentric pattern {29949742;27061094}. The glands are not compressed by surrounding stroma; luminal spaces are open and often contain colloid-like, eosinophilic, PAS-positive / diastase-resistant secretions. MGA glands are uniform and similar in size to or slightly larger than normal acini. The glands are composed of a single layer of cuboidal epithelial cells with small nuclei and clear or amphophilic cytoplasm. Small, fine, intracytoplasmic granules can be present {6852780;6660348}. Myoepithelial cells are absent but a complete layer of basement membrane is present.

Immunohistochemistry for collagen IV and laminin, reticulin stain, or electron microscopy can be useful to demonstrate a continuous layer of basement membrane surrounding the glands {29949742;23584829}. The MGA cells typically express low-molecular-weight cytokeratins, S100, cathepsin D, and EGFR, and they are negative for EMA, ER and PR, and ERBB2 (HER2) overexpression by immunohistochemistry {18300793}. In one study, SOX10 was weakly expressed in the majority of MGA cases tested, but GATA3 was reduced to absent {33382535}.

A-MGA shows increasing architectural complexity, with glandular crowding. Cells are disposed in single or multiple layers and show nuclear atypia. Mitotic figures can be present. No desmoplasia or stromal reaction is seen. A-MGA shares a similar immunohistochemical profile with C-MGA. Increased Ki-67 labelling index and p53 positivity accumulation can help in differentiating C-MGA versus A-MGA {18300793}.

MGA features can persist after neoadjuvant chemotherapy in cases of MGA that are associated with triple-negative breast cancer. Post–neoadjuvant chemotherapy MGA shows cellular pleomorphism, cytoplasmic vacuolization, and variability in the size of glands {33649459}. The immunohistochemical profile of post–neoadjuvant chemotherapy MGA is similar to that of C-MGA and A-MGA; in particular, a continuous layer of basement membrane surrounds the glands.

Invasive carcinomas arising in MGA are usually grade 2 or 3 triple-negative breast carcinomas; metaplastic carcinomas are also reported {40691172;30428388;28372347;40081883;28381146}. Most tumours are EGFR-positive and S100-positive {12883237;18300793;22486256;23584829}, and devoid of myoepithelium and basement membrane. Special types of breast carcinoma, including acinic cell–like carcinoma, and adenoid cystic carcinoma can rarely arise in association with MGA {12883237;30428388}.

Cytology

Preoperative diagnosis is challenging on core needle biopsy {33649459}, and not possible on cytopathology, but the limited cases reported in the literature suggest sparse cellularity, a monotonous population of intermediate cells in minute tissue fragments with uniform round nuclei, small nucleoli, and vacuolated clear cytoplasm {8458288}, with a lack of myoepithelial nuclei and bare bipolar nuclei in the background.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

For C-MGA: an infiltrating haphazard proliferation of small, round, and uniform glands; the glands are lined by a single layer of cuboidal cells without atypia, devoid of myoepithelium, but surrounded by a continuous layer of basement membrane For A-MGA: similar to C-MGA but with increasing glandular crowding and cellular atypia.

Desirable:

Immunohistochemistry for basement membrane (positive) and myoepithelial cells (negative); S100 positivity and ER negativity is useful to distinguish MGA from tubular carcinoma; HER2 negativity; A-MGA shows a higher Ki-67 proliferative index and greater p53 expression.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

C-MGA is a benign proliferation but is frequently associated with A-MGA, and progression to carcinomas arising in MGA has been described {19047897;12883237}. On the basis of molecular and cytogenetic similarities among these lesions, some investigators have suggested that MGA is a non-obligate precursor lesion of triple-negative breast carcinoma {22486256;33382535}. Furthermore, it has been suggested that A-MGA and acinic cell–like carcinoma are part of a similar spectrum {27713419}.

Owing to the rarity of invasive breast carcinomas associated with MGA and lack of long-term follow-up studies, it is uncertain whether these tumours behave differently from those not associated with MGA when matched by histological features, grade, and stage {18300793}.
