2.5.4 Encapsulated papillary carcinoma

Definition

Encapsulated papillary carcinoma consists of neoplastic epithelial cells usually of low or intermediate nuclear grade lining fine arborizing fibrovascular stalks, typically present within a cystic space and surrounded by a fibrous capsule. There are usually no myoepithelial cells along the papillae or at the periphery of the lesion.

Related terminology

Not recommended: intracystic papillary carcinoma; encysted papillary carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most tumours are central and subareolar.

Clinical features

This lesion presents as a circumscribed round mass, with or without nipple discharge. There are no specific imaging features that differentiate encapsulated papillary carcinoma from other papillary lesions; however, encapsulated papillary carcinomas tend to be larger at presentation, with a cystic growth pattern.

Epidemiology

Encapsulated papillary carcinoma is rare and accounts for 0.5–2% of breast cancers in women {21753694} and 3.5–14% in men {39508178}, described in case series. It usually presents in postmenopausal women, mainly during the seventh decade of life {21753694;27054753}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Encapsulated papillary carcinomas of low to intermediate nuclear grade display genomic features similar to those of low-grade, ER-positive invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (NST), for example, 16q losses, 16p gains, and 1q gains {22025283}. The prevalence of PIK3CA mutations is similar to that in ER-matched and grade-matched invasive breast carcinomas NST {22025283}. PAM50 subtyping classifies the majority of cases as luminal A and a minority as luminal B {25041824}. Cases with invasive carcinomas show a higher incidence of PIK3CA mutations compared with those without invasive carcinoma, similar to those of ER-matched and grade-matched invasive carcinomas NST {22025283;33667422}. By comparison with ER-matched and grade-matched invasive breast carcinomas NST, encapsulated papillary carcinomas display downregulation of genes related to proliferation, cell assembly and organization, and cellular movement and migration, and overexpression of genes related to homeostasis and angiogenesis {25041824}.

There is no information on the genetic alterations of encapsulated papillary carcinoma of high nuclear grade.

Macroscopic appearance

Encapsulated papillary carcinoma is often observed as a friable mass within a cystic space.

Histopathology

Encapsulated papillary carcinoma usually consists of a papillary mass within a cystic space; less often, it is composed of an aggregate of closely located nodules. The tumour has a rounded, pushing border, typically surrounded by a fibrous capsule of varying thickness, and is made up of multiple delicate fibrovascular stalks covered by neoplastic epithelium. Nuclear grade is low or intermediate in the majority of cases. The epithelial cells are arranged in single or multiple layers, and they can also form micropapillary or cribriform structures that fill the gaps separating adjacent papillae. There are no myoepithelial cells along the papillae. Myoepithelial cells at the periphery of the lesion are typically absent, but can be present in ≥ 10% of cases {39209705;34265775}.

The fifth edition Breast tumours volume of this series [[WHO Classification of Tumours Editorial Board. Breast tumours. Lyon (France): International Agency for Research on Cancer; 2019. (WHO classification of tumours series, 5th ed.; vol. 2). https://publications.iarc.who.int/581. ]] indicated that tumours with a growth pattern similar to that of encapsulated papillary carcinoma but displaying high nuclear grade, increased mitotic activity, triple negativity, or ERBB2 (HER2) overexpression should be graded, staged, and managed as invasive disease. Data to support this approach are very limited. The editorial board for the sixth edition recommends that such rare lesions should still be categorized as encapsulated papillary carcinoma, but with a note or comment enumerating the unusual features. In particular, caution is advised in making a diagnosis of an invasive carcinoma based purely on high nuclear grade and biomarker (ER, PR, HER2) profile without definitive histological evidence of invasion. Evaluation of more representative tissue in excisional specimens with relevant adjunctive workup is required.

Frank invasion in the setting of encapsulated papillary carcinoma is defined as the presence of neoplastic elements that permeate beyond the fibrous capsule. The invasive focus has an irregular infiltrative appearance recognizable as conventional invasive carcinoma to facilitate distinction from entrapment of neoplastic cells within the fibrous capsule and from epithelial displacement within the biopsy site. Most often this takes the form of invasive carcinoma NST.

The neoplastic epithelial component does not express high-molecular-weight cytokeratins. Staining for multiple myoepithelial markers demonstrates the absence of myoepithelial cells. In rare instances, an incomplete myoepithelial cell layer at the periphery of the lesion can be highlighted with these markers {21084964}. Most encapsulated papillary carcinomas express ER and (usually) PR and lack HER2 overexpression and gene amplification {27054753;22025283;21084964}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is low to moderate.

The differential diagnosis includes solid papillary carcinoma in situ, papillary ductal carcinoma in situ, and a pattern of invasive lobular carcinoma mimicking papillary carcinoma {27116593}. Rare hyperplastic proliferations that show papillary cystic architecture lined by benign apocrine cells but without a myoepithelial cell layer {22339683;21263240} may also enter the differential diagnosis.

Cytology

Encapsulated papillary carcinoma shares many features with papillary ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) and solid papillary carcinoma, and produces high cellularity, both rounded and irregular cohesive 3D epithelial-cell tissue fragments of variable size which are devoid of fibrovascular cores, and papillae with slender fibrovascular stalks lined by columnar cells, and often plentiful single cells with mild atypia {18552172}. Bare bipolar myoepithelial nuclei are absent.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Staging

The biological nature of encapsulated papillary carcinoma remains undetermined. Owing to the lack of myoepithelial cells, rare examples of axillary lymph node metastases, and evidence that the fibrous capsule is reactive in nature, encapsulated papillary carcinoma is considered by some to be a self-confined indolent invasive carcinoma {37232543}. Some consider the demonstrated presence of basement membrane around the tumour, irrespective of the presence or absence of myoepithelial cells, a feature of carcinoma in situ {34265775}. Regardless, the overall prognosis for encapsulated papillary carcinoma without associated frank invasion is similar to that of ductal carcinoma in situ {16861972;15762278;21451361;19141383}.

In the absence of frank invasion beyond the fibrous capsule, encapsulated papillary carcinoma should be graded according to its nuclear grade and staged as pTis (DCIS), in order to avoid overtreatment. Hormone receptor status should be assessed as for DCIS.

In the presence of frank invasion, the Nottingham grade and tumour stage should be performed according to the morphological features and pathological size of the frankly invasive component only. Hormone receptor and HER2 status should be assessed and reported on the frankly invasive component only.

Prognosis and prediction

In the absence of infiltrating carcinoma, encapsulated papillary carcinoma has a very favourable prognosis with adequate local therapy {9781648;8056421;21753694;29731165;12383904;21084964}. In very rare instances, lymph node metastases have been reported, with the metastases revealing the same papillary features as the tumour in the breast {17478767;21753694}. The presence of associated DCIS in the adjacent breast tissue confers a higher risk of local recurrence {851947}. Complete surgical excision of the tumour with extensive sampling of the surrounding breast tissue is essential for treatment and assessment of risk for local recurrence.

Lesions with a growth pattern similar to that of encapsulated papillary carcinoma displaying high nuclear grade, increased mitotic activity, triple negativity, or HER2 overexpression have been described {25382726;30004109}. The possibility that these tumours may be pseudopapillary high-grade invasive carcinomas mimicking encapsulated papillary carcinoma needs to be excluded for proper management. Multidisciplinary review for collective management is helpful.
