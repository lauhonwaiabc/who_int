5.1 Tumours of the nipple: Introduction

The nipple and the areola, which together form the nipple–areola complex (NAC), are composed of the outlets of lactiferous ducts draining milk from breast lobules. Large lactiferous ducts widen under the areola to form lactiferous sinuses, before narrowing down at the base of the nipple to terminate at the nipple orifice {17221150}. The male breast does not have breast acini but has a nipple and lactiferous ducts.

The NAC lacks subcutaneous tissue, and the skin covering the nipple rests on a thin layer of smooth muscle–rich tissue. In 10% of normal nipples, bland epithelial cells with clear/pale cytoplasm (Toker cells) are found in the basal layer of the epidermis, and may form small monolayered glands devoid of myoepithelium. The Toker cells usually express CK7, ER, and PR, akin to breast luminal cells {18614197}. The pathological relevance of Toker cells is presented in section Mammary Paget disease. The squamous epithelium of the nipple extends into the major ducts for 1–2 mm before being abruptly replaced by the luminal/myoepithelial cell arrangement of the ducts. Areola muscle comprises radial and circular fibres with an underlying thin layer of fat including blood vessels {26855906}. Sebaceous glands are present in the NAC; eccrine glands are found in the skin of the areola, but not in the nipple.

Apart from mammary Paget disease and rare cases of invasive carcinoma arising in the nipple {13352143;29431468}, most diseases of the nipple are non-neoplastic, including abnormal developments, such as retracted or inverted nipples and various forms of acute and chronic inflammatory disorders.

Each component of the NAC can give rise to both benign and malignant tumours, including squamous cell carcinoma {28551484;25494146}, basal cell carcinoma {27571043;27363851}, melanoma {29628990}, eccrine spiradenoma {30806477}, clear cell acanthoma {30261576}, microcystic adnexal carcinoma {30059333}, hidradenoma {4298177}, haemangioma, fibroma, and leiomyoma {5581295;30663114}; angiomyxoma may rarely arise in the NAC {36607731}.

Recent advances in oncoplastic surgical techniques have led to the introduction of nipple-sparing mastectomy. Rarely, breast carcinoma may recur in the nipple after nipple-sparing mastectomy {26242363}. In a systematic review, the weighted mean rate of recurrence involving the NAC after nipple-sparing mastectomy for invasive carcinoma or ductal carcinoma in situ was 1.3% (range: 0–4.9%) and was lower in patients with tumours > 10 mm from the nipple {36208279}.

Syringomatous tumour, nipple adenoma, and mammary Paget disease of the breast are among the most common tumours of the NAC (discussed in section Syringomatous tumour; section Nipple adenoma; and section Mammary Paget disease, respectively).
