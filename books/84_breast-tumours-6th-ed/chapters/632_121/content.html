6.3.2 Desmoid fibromatosis

Definition

Desmoid fibromatosis is an infiltrative and locally aggressive but non-metastasizing fibroblastic/(myo)fibroblastic neoplasm with a propensity for local recurrence.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: desmoid tumour; aggressive fibromatosis.

Not recommended: musculoaponeurotic fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Desmoid fibromatosis may arise primarily in the breast parenchyma or in the pectoral fascia of the chest wall and extend secondarily into the breast {17896146;2920364}. Localization to the dermis or subcutis is occasionally seen {33978242}. The tumours are usually solitary, although synchronous and asynchronous bilateralism has been reported {2920364;10656738}.

Clinical features

The typical presentation is of a slowly evolving, non-tender, and palpably firm mass with poorly defined boundaries. Rare symptoms include skin retraction or dimpling, nipple discharge, and pain {3789257}. Approximately 10–15% of patients report a history of trauma, including surgery, in the region of the desmoid tumour, or recent/concomitant pregnancy {30610557}. Involvement of the axillary soft tissues should not be mistaken for lymphadenopathy. Radiographical findings of an irregularly contoured, spiculated, non-calcified mass often mimic the appearance of malignancy {24990452}.

Epidemiology

Desmoid fibromatosis accounts for < 0.2% of all breast tumours in case series {10656738}. Cases present in the third to fifth decades of life, but tumours can present at any age. The overwhelming majority of cases affect female patients {17896146;2920364;10656738}.

Etiology

Desmoid fibromatosis has a multifactorial etiology including genetic (sporadic CTNNB1 mutations, sporadic and germline APC mutations), physical (surgery, trauma), and endocrine (pregnancy) factors {36404250;28961825;17896146}.

Pathogenesis

Activating point mutations in exon 3 of CTNNB1, the gene that encodes β-catenin, have been detected in 45–75% of desmoid fibromatoses of the breast, dependent on molecular technique, which is a lower frequency than at other anatomical sites {11823972;32590455;31016642;10597266;22211293;26171757}. Inactivating mutations/deletions of the APC tumour suppressor gene range from approximately 20% to 35%, and a small subset of these are germline, arising in the setting of Gardner syndrome {10597266;32590455;11823972}. Alterations of both CTNNB1 and APC inhibit degradation of β-catenin, which in turn translocates to the nucleus where it regulates gene expression. These genetic alterations lead to a pathogenic increase in transcriptional signalling through the WNT/β-catenin pathway, which drives cell proliferation.

Macroscopic appearance

Desmoid fibromatosis forms a poorly circumscribed mass lesion ranging from < 10 mm to > 100 mm in greatest dimension {17896146;2920364;3789257}. The cut surface is tan-white to grey, with a trabeculated or whorled appearance.

Histopathology

Desmoid fibromatosis is characterized by long sweeping fascicles of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts lacking cytological atypia. The fascicles typically span an entire 10× objective field and infiltrate adjacent adipose tissue and skeletal muscle. Lymphoid aggregates are often appreciated at the advancing edge of the lesion. The tumour nuclei are slender and hypochromatic with small pinpoint nucleoli. Mitotic figures may be present, but atypical mitoses are absent. Thin-walled blood vessels appear throughout the tumour, and may be gaping or staghorn with variable perivascular oedema. Dense eosinophilic collagen, myxoid change, hyalinized areas, and zones resembling nodular fasciitis may be present. Morphological features do not always distinguish desmoid fibromatosis from phyllodes tumour or metaplastic carcinoma, which must be excluded with molecular and/or immunohistochemical studies.

With immunohistochemistry, nuclear accumulation of β-catenin is seen in 76–100% of cases {11823972;15832090;17711447}, although patterns of immunoreactivity are sometimes difficult to interpret. Nuclear accumulation of β-catenin does not reliably distinguish desmoid fibromatosis from metaplastic carcinoma or phyllodes tumour {20693983;21076461}. An absence of cytokeratin expression, established using antibodies targeting broad-spectrum, luminal, and basal cytokeratins, is essential to rule out metaplastic carcinoma, especially the fibromatosis-like subtype. Lack of p63 expression also distinguishes desmoid fibromatosis from a subset of metaplastic carcinomas. Unlike in myofibroblastoma and the stroma of phyllodes tumour, there is no expression of CD34 {33708054;28821190}. ER, PR, and ERBB2 (HER2) are generally not expressed {16254103;10656738}. Most tumours express SMA in a tram-track pattern; desmin immunoreactivity may be seen in a subset of neoplastic cells.

Cytology

Cellularity is usually low. Scant single spindle cells and tissue fragments with spindle cells embedded in a predominant pale (Pap stain) collagenous matrix are present, with epithelial tissue fragments from the infiltrating margin in some cases. A specific diagnosis can be suggested by the features, but the differential diagnosis includes other spindle cell lesions and is broad {8498134;17896146;3428067;3400396;9360049}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Given the technical difficulty of interpreting β-catenin immunohistochemistry in some cases, CTNNB1 and APC mutation analysis maybe be helpful in small biopsies when the diagnostic morphological findings are absent {21884214;22766794}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: long, sweeping fascicles of cytologically bland fibroblasts/myofibroblasts; lack of nuclear pleomorphism and atypical mitoses; lack of circumscription with infiltration of adjacent tissues; exclusion of epithelial or fibroepithelial neoplasms with spindle cell morphology.

Desirable: nuclear β-catenin expression on immunohistochemistry, or molecular evidence of inactivating APC mutation or activating CTNNB1 mutation (in challenging cases or small biopsies).

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Desmoid fibromatosis is characterized by unpredictable growth, stabilization, and regression. While a primary surgical approach with negative margins was the previous standard of care, margin status fails to consistently correlate with local recurrence rates {17896146;2920364;3789257;12663732}. Furthermore, approximately one third of patients experience spontaneous regression, and extensive surgery can be morbid {31016642;25639193}. As such, a watchful waiting approach has been advocated and adopted for asymptomatic patients {28961825;32780320;28735069;31016642;19568815}. For patients undergoing surgical resection, the NM_001904.4:c.134C>T p.(Ser45Phe) variant of CTNNB1 appears to portend a higher risk of local recurrence in sporadic desmoid fibromatosis, as does larger tumour size and younger age {18832571;23913621;28961825;25341748;21844500}.
