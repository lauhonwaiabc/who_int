7.2.1 Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue

Definition

Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (EMZL) of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) is an indolent primary extranodal B-cell lymphoma with cytological and architectural features reminiscent of Peyer patch lymphoid tissue, the prototypical MALT. EMZL typically arises from marginal zone B cells of acquired MALT and is often associated with an underlying chronic inflammatory disorder.

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma; extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma.

Not recommended: MALToma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most patients are diagnosed while the disease is localized, with a single lesion in the breast parenchyma; rare cases present as bilateral disease {19570964;34672247;30686775}. Regional dissemination to ipsilateral lymph nodes, bone marrow, and other extranodal sites at presentation or relapse is relatively rare {19570964;18636016;34672247;30686775}. Characteristic dissemination of primary breast (and ocular adnexal) EMZL to subcutaneous sites has been reported, suggesting a biological relationship between these sites {22180423}.

Clinical features

Patients may present with a mass lesion detected on physical examination {18636016}. Lesions can also be identified by mammography, ultrasonography, or PET/CT, and most cases are detected in breast cancer screening programmes {19570964;30686775;34672247}. The large majority of patients are asymptomatic. Patients who present with disseminated disease may have constitutional symptoms and/or elevated LDH and B2M levels {19570964;34672247}. The large majority of EMZLs involving the breast are primary; in rare cases, secondary breast presentation of EMZL with a primary arising in another MALT site can occur {18636016;28973700;26773441}.

Epidemiology

Primary breast EMZL is very rare and constitutes approximately 3% of EMZLs at all sites {24417667}. With increasing incidence of breast implant–associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma over the past 10 years {33234792}, EMZL now ranks between the second and third most commonly described cases of primary breast lymphoma after diffuse large B-cell lymphoma {18636016;27847267;30628128}. Breast EMZL mainly affects middle-aged and older adults, almost all women, with an age of 60–70 years {19570964;24417667}. There is a relative racial predominance of non-Hispanic White people over Hispanic White people (incidence rate ratio: 0.61) and equal representation of Black people versus non-Hispanic White people based on SEER data {24417667}.

Etiology

EMZL usually arises in acquired MALT at extranodal sites, which results from a chronic inflammatory process caused by infection, autoimmunity, or unknown factors. Patients with autoimmune diseases, such as autoimmune hypothyroidism, Sjögren syndrome, and Crohn disease, before a diagnosis of breast EMZL have been reported (16.7–36.3%) {30686775;33321727;34672247}. A relationship with specific infectious species has not been identified {38864239}. Most patients with breast EMZL have no known predisposing factors.

Similarly to EMZL presenting at other sites, cases of breast EMZL have been reported in patients with immune deficiency/dysregulation {23773403;29957733}. An etiological association to the immune deficiency/dysregulation setting can only be substantiated in those cases that are EBV associated.

Pathogenesis

The genesis of EMZL is the result of oncogenic cooperation among dysregulated immune responses and somatic genetic changes {27452667;28288707}. The mutation and translocation profiles differ according to primary extranodal sites {34021249;34494161}. Based on one series (n = 15) and various case reports, primary breast EMZL is a predominantly mutation-driven disease rather than a translocation-driven one {38864239}. Recurrent chromosomal translocations, specifically t(11;18)(q21;q21) (BIRC3::MALT1) and t(14;18) (IGH::MALT1), that are characteristic of EMZL in other sites and result in activation of NF-κB are exceedingly rare {16917511;21654070;22842723;38864239}. Very few data are available on common trisomies, such as for chromosomes 3, 12, or 18 {17222250}. The mutation load in primary breast EMZL is low with mutations of TNFAIP3, KMT2D, and SPEN being most frequent {38864239}.

Macroscopic appearance

Solid fleshy lesions, diameter generally 15–30 mm (range: < 10 mm to 200 mm) {19570964}. Radiographic findings are heterogeneous, including hypoechogenic and lobulated masses {34672247}.

Histopathology

The key microscopic feature of EMZL is a proliferation of atypical small to medium-sized lymphoid cells that efface the architecture of the affected sites, with or without site-specific lymphoepithelial lesions. In breast EMZL, well-formed lymphoepithelial lesions are less commonly found than in EMZLs arising in other sites {18636016;26026200}. The neoplastic cells comprise variable proportions of small lymphocytes with round nuclei and condensed chromatin; marginal zone cells (sometimes called centrocyte-like cells) with small to medium-sized slightly irregular nuclei, smooth chromatin, and pale cytoplasm; and monocytoid cells with abundant pale cytoplasm. Reactive follicles, sometimes with follicular colonization (infiltration and partial to complete replacement by neoplastic marginal zone cells) are found in some cases. There can be a varying component of admixed plasma cells, shown to be monotypic in one third of cases, which can form large aggregates and may show Dutcher bodies, Russell bodies, or other immunoglobulin inclusions (crystals or globules) or be accompanied by crystal-storing histiocytosis or amyloid deposition {18636016;26038772;21525745;26026200}.

Scattered centroblast- or immunoblast-like cells are common. Similarly to EMZL at other sites, large cell transformation is diagnosed when there are sheets of large cells not associated with follicular dendritic cell meshworks {26400898;27460179;31912792}.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic cells express CD20, CD79a, and PAX5; usually IgM; occasionally IgG or IgA; and exceptionally IgD. EMZLs are typically negative for CD5, CD10, BCL6, CD23, cyclin D1, and SOX11 {29957733}. Specific data on expression in breast EMZL of IRTA1 (FCRL4, CD307d) and MNDA, markers associated with marginal zone cell differentiation and frequently expressed in EMZL at other, more frequent sites, are not available. Immunoglobulin light chain restriction can aid in the distinction from reactive lymphoid proliferations. CD21 highlights expanded follicular dendritic cell meshworks in the background of the lymphoma, encompassing both reactive follicles and also colonized follicles. Cytokeratin can highlight subtle lymphoepithelial lesions.

Differential diagnosis

EMZL can be distinguished from chronic inflammatory processes, including sclerosing lymphocytic lobulitis (diabetic mastopathy), non-sclerosing lymphocytic mastopathy, chronic mastitis, and reactive follicular hyperplasia, by sheet-like or permeative infiltration of CD20-positive B cells, effacement of architecture, presence of monotypic plasma cells, and monoclonal IG gene rearrangements {39542179}. Reactive lymphocytic infiltrates may show more marked fibrosis, fewer B cells with a T-cell predominance, and a predominance of IgG-positive plasma cells in contrast to more frequent IgM-positive plasma cells in EMZL {26026200}. Depending on the etiology, prominent sclerosis and atrophic ducts (sclerosing lymphocytic lobulitis), or eosinophils, fat necrosis, and granulomas (chronic mastitis) may prevail {39542179}. IgG4-related disease typically presents at extranodal sites, infrequently including breast. Diagnostic features of IgG4-related disease include storiform stromal fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis, and increased numbers of IgG4-positive plasma cells. The criteria for the histologically assessed IgG4:IgG ratio and other clinical and laboratory features vary by anatomical site. No monotypic lymphoid cells or plasma cells and no clonal IG gene rearrangements are found in IgG4-related disease (unless there is associated lymphoma).

Other mature B-cell lymphomas (e.g. follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia / small lymphocytic lymphoma) can be excluded by a lack of CD10, BCL6, CD5, cyclin D1, and LEF1 expression. EMZL with prominent follicular colonization may mimic follicular lymphoma; the presence of an appreciable extrafollicular component of B cells, a lack of extensive expression of follicular-centre markers (particularly CD10) within and outside follicles {33283568}, and an absence of BCL2 translocation may support the former. Distinction between EMZL and lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma can be very difficult. Bone marrow and lymph node involvement at presentation, high-titre IgM paraproteinaemia, frequent Dutcher bodies, and the MYD88 NM_002468.5 c.794T>C p.(Leu265Pro) mutation (also known as MYD88 L265P) would favour the latter. Rare cases of EMZL show extensive plasma cell differentiation, mimicking plasmacytoma, but the presence of a component of CD20-positve B cells, as well as presentation in the breast parenchyma, would favour the former.

Cytology

Diagnosis by cytology is challenging, and it may be difficult to distinguish EMZL from reactive lymphoid hyperplasia. Smears are usually moderately to highly cellular, with a mixed lymphoid population in which small to medium-sized cells may predominate with a varying component of plasmacytoid cells and plasma cells. Flow cytometry demonstrating light chain restriction can be helpful. Most importantly, lymphoid proliferations should be distinguished from small cell breast cancer, especially lobular carcinoma, in FNA evaluation of breast masses. In this respect, breast EMZL with dominant plasmacellular differentiation is a notorious differential diagnostic pitfall {39542179}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of monoclonal IG gene rearrangements may help establish a diagnosis when the histopathological and immunophenotypic findings are suspicious but not diagnostic.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primary lymphoma arising in the breast parenchyma; atypical small/medium-sized lymphoid cell proliferation mimicking reactive MALT and showing architectural distortion; expression of B-lineage markers; exclusion of other small B-cell neoplasms, for example, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, small lymphocytic lymphoma, lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma, and plasmacytoma.

Desirable: demonstration of light chain restriction or clonal IG gene rearrangement; lymphoepithelial lesions; remnants of underlying inflammatory background (e.g. reactive lymphoid follicles).

Staging

Lymphoid neoplasms are staged according to the Lugano classification. The Ann Arbor classification has often been used for non-gastric EMZL {31912792;26773441}.

Prognosis and prediction

EMZL is an indolent lymphoma. Local recurrences, relapses in other MALT sites, and nodal or distant metastasis may occur, but the 5- and 10-year overall survival rates reach 90% and 80%, respectively {26773441}. Owing to its rarity, specific data on sufficiently large series of breast EMZL are lacking. Small case series suggest a 3-year progression-free survival of 68.7% and 5-year progression-free survival of 56–60%, respectively {19570964;34672247;30686775}.
