10.2.1 BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndrome (BRCA1, BRCA2)

Definition

BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndromes are inherited autosomal dominant disorders of germline pathogenic variants of BRCA1 or BRCA2 in which there is an increased risk of early-onset breast cancer, tubo-ovarian carcinoma, prostatic carcinoma, pancreatic carcinoma, and other cancer types.

MIM numbering

604370 Breast-ovarian cancer, familial, susceptibility to, 1; BROVCA1

612555 Breast-ovarian cancer, familial, susceptibility to, 2 BROVCA2

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary breast-ovarian cancer syndrome.

Subtype(s)

BRCA1-related hereditary cancer syndrome; BRCA2-related hereditary cancer syndrome

Localization

BRCA1/BRCA2-associated hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome is associated with cancers of the breast, ovary, pancreas, prostate, and potentially other sites.

Clinical features

The clinical features are dependent on the presentation of the tumour; there is no specific phenotype.

Epidemiology

In a meta-analysis of 60 studies involving 105 220 breast cancer patients, 3588 patients (3.4%) were BRCA1/BRCA2 mutation carriers. Germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 are known to confer a high lifetime risk of breast and ovarian cancers {17416853}, as well as contralateral breast cancer {23628597}. In the USA, the number of women who carry actionable BRCA1 and BRCA2 variants is estimated to be 1 in 300–500 {25198398}. More-recent large international studies have refined risk estimates. The Consortium of Investigators of Modifiers of BRCA1/2 (CIMBA) assembled data on 29 700 families with BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants on six continents and found that breast and ovarian cancer risks varied by mutation type and location {25857409}. The incidence rates of breast cancer were 46% (BRCA1) and 52% (BRCA2), and of ovarian cancer 12% (BRCA1) and 6% (BRCA2). The incidence rates of breast and ovarian cancers together were 5% (BRCA1) and 2% (BRCA2); 37% of BRCA1 and 40% of BRCA2 variant carriers were without cancer {25857409}. The consortium also observed substantial variation in mutation type and frequency by geographical region and race/ethnicity. Besides the known founder variants, other alterations of relatively high frequency were also identified in specific racial/ethnic or geographical groups. A recent meta-analysis of breast and ovarian cancers in India revealed 18 novel BRCA1 and 16 BRCA2 variants that were not previously reported or included in the Breast Cancer Information Core (BIC) or ClinVar databases [[Sharma-Oates A, Shaaban AM, Tomlinson I, et al. Heterogeneity of germline variants in high risk breast and ovarian cancer susceptibility genes in India. Precis Clin Med. 2018 Sep;1(2):75â€“87. doi:10.1093/pcmedi/pby010. ]]. Three germline pathogenic founder variants of BRCA1 and BRCA2 are found in approximately 2.5% of the Ashkenazi Jewish population {9150153}. Knowledge of the population-specific BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutation spectrum could inform efficient strategies for genetic testing and may justify more broad-based oncogenetic testing in some populations {29446198}.

BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants also increase the risk of male breast cancer, with a higher frequency of BRCA2 mutations found in such cases. In high-risk families, germline pathogenic variants in BRCA2 account for 60–76% of male breast cancers while those of BRCA1 account for 10–16% {24131976}.

Other cancers in carriers of BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations

BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline pathogenic variant carriers, besides being subject to increased lifetime risks of breast and ovarian cancers, have also been reported to have increased susceptibility to prostate, pancreas, stomach, and colon carcinomas (see #48201Table 10.02) {26132389;10433620;12237281}. A trend of increased incidence of melanoma in BRCA1 variant carriers and cervical cancer in BRCA2 variant carriers has been observed {26132389}. A recent subgroup meta-analysis reporting evaluations adjusted for sex and age demonstrated an increased risk of colorectal cancer associated with BRCA1 but not BRCA2 mutations {30380096}.

Risk modifiers in carriers of BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations

The penetrance of BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants is likely to be modified by genetic, lifestyle, and reproductive factors, as well as concurrent genetic alterations {16998497;21285145;24824314}. Such information is important for predicting the risk of breast and other cancers among carriers of BRCA1 and BRCA2 variants to improve prevention of cancer development.

Lifestyle factors

Reproductive and lifestyle factors have been reported as risk modifiers of breast cancers among individuals with BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants. A higher number of term pregnancies is associated with reduced risk of breast cancer among BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers {28970854;29254229;27880720;29673794}. Breastfeeding has been reported to be a protective factor in BRCA1 variant carriers {15265971;16261399;22564871;27880720}. However, no significant association can be found between breastfeeding and breast cancer risk in BRCA2 variant carriers {22564871}. Earlier menarche has been found to be associated with increased risk of breast cancer in BRCA2 but not BRCA1 variant carriers {28970854;29254229}. In contrast, late age at menopause shows a protective effect against breast cancer in BRCA variant carriers {27880720}. There is no significant association between the use of oral contraceptives {29254229;27880720} and breast cancer among BRCA variant carriers.

Interestingly, a recent study using consortia data from BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers showed that height and height genetic score are positively associated with risk of breast cancer, although the association with height genetic score was not statistically significant {30390716}. Observed body mass index and body mass index genetic score are inversely associated with breast cancer risk. Body mass index was found to be associated with premenopause breast cancer. These environmental and lifestyle factors are particularly important in women with a strong family history {30312457}.

Etiology

BRCA1 and BRCA2 are tumour suppressor genes coding for proteins engaged in the highly conserved homologous recombination repair pathway to accurately repair DNA breaks using the sister chromatid as a template. If not repaired, such breaks promote genomic instability and lead to the development of cancer. A recent study using whole-genome sequencing of tumours from patients with germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 and non-carriers showed that some patients have unreported dual germline pathogenic variants in an additional cancer risk gene (BRCA1/BRCA2; BRCA1/MUTYH). It also showed that 100% of tumours from BRCA1 variant carriers and 91% of tumours from BRCA2 variant carriers exhibited biallelic inactivation of the respective gene, together with somatic mutation signatures suggestive of a functional deficiency in homologous recombination {31090900}. Although BRCA1 and BRCA2 variants are spread widely over these large genes, there is an association between variants in the central part of BRCA2 (the ovarian cluster region) and an increase in the likelihood of ovarian carcinoma. BRCA1 is localized to chromosome 17q21.31 and contains 23 coding exons. It encodes a nuclear protein of 1863 amino acids. A RING finger domain at the N-terminus mediates interactions with other proteins. BRCA1 is often mutated in three domains: the RING domain, exons 11–13, and the C-terminal domain {22737296}. BRCA2 maps to chromosome 13q13.1 and consists of 27 coding exons. The BRCA2 protein includes 3418 amino acids. The BRCA1 and BRCA2 proteins share no structural homology with any other protein {21336636}. #48202Fig. 10.XXX illustrates the functional domains in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 proteins.

Pathogenesis

DNA repair is essential for cell survival, and cells have developed a number of mechanisms to ensure genomic integrity. During replication, double-strand breaks at replication forks lead to the loss of genome segments with rearrangements. Loss of the tumour suppressor function of BRCA1 and BRCA2 is probably central to the development of cancer, but it is still unclear why BRCA1/BRCA2-associated cancers are mostly restricted to the breast and/or ovary. It has been suggested that, at least for BRCA1, homologous recombination–independent functions in transcription may account for the development of breast cancers {28649985}.

BRCA gene variants and promoter methylation have been linked to risk of breast cancer and have been reported to be mutually exclusive. BRCA methylation has been reported to be more common in sporadic breast cancer {28569220} and linked to increased breast cancer risk {29693332}. A recent systematic review concluded that BRCA methylation is found in 1.3% and 1.1% of breast and ovarian carcinomas, respectively, in BRCA variant carriers {29891109}. The frequency of BRCA alterations varies considerably between CpG sites in BRCA1 and BRCA2 promotors.

Numerous genetic events, as identified by population-based genome-wide association and multiconsortial studies, have been reported to affect the real risk of breast cancers among BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers {23544013;25849179}. The spectrum of BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations with indels and missense, nonsense, and splice site mutations has been found to be similar and constitutes 67% of BRCA1 and 56% of BRCA2 variants, as well as about 33% and 45%, respectively, of variants of unknown significance (VUSs) {30293211}. In contrast, these variants were reported to constitute only about 8% of VUSs in earlier studies on BRCA1 mutation {8807330}. This observation is thought to reflect the fact that advances in mutation detection have far exceeded those in mutation interpretation {30293211}. Indeed, a variety of technical approaches have been adopted to evaluate the pathogenicity of VUSs, which need to be further clarified.

Breast and ovarian cancer risks vary with the type and location of BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations. Three breast cancer cluster regions and an ovarian cancer cluster region, found in exon 11, have been identified in BRCA1 {25849179}. Variants in the BRCA1 C-terminal domain region are associated with a higher susceptibility to breast cancers than other BRCA1 mutations {29673794}. This may be because BRCA1 C-terminal phosphoprotein recognition is crucial for the BRCA1 tumour suppression effect {22034435}. In BRCA2, multiple breast cancer cluster regions have been found, together with three ovarian cancer cluster regions. Variants conferring nonsense-mediated decay have been associated with differential breast or ovarian cancer risks and an earlier age of breast cancer diagnosis in both BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers {25849179}.

Variants at specific regions, such as those on 11q22.3 {27796716} and 19p13 {27601076}, have been found to be significantly associated with breast cancer risk among BRCA1 variant carriers. Although data regarding BRCA2 are less available, a susceptibility allele at 6p24 that is specifically and inversely associated with breast cancer risk in BRCA2 variant carriers has been reported. This variant was found to be associated with risk of breast cancer in neither BRCA1 mutation carriers nor the general population {23544012}. The signal interaction network of BRCA variants, particularly genes involved in DNA damage repair and cell-cycle regulation (e.g. ATM, ATR, TP53, RB1, and RAD51), is another factor that may also affect penetrance {24832651;29617664;30293211}.

Large-scale studies using whole-genome sequencing have identified distinct patterns of genomic alterations (called mutation signatures) across different tumour types {23945592}. An algorithm integrating six of these mutation signatures, including those reflecting homologous recombination deficiency, could identify tumours associated with BRCA1/BRCA2 germline variants as well as those with functional BRCA1/BRCA2 defects with as high as 98.7% sensitivity {28288110}, suggesting that this approach could be useful in identifying patients who may benefit from poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors.

Gene expression analysis has revealed that BRCA1 tumours are characterized by alterations in genes involved in proliferation, adhesion, angiogenesis, motility, transcription, and DNA repair, whereas BRCA2 tumours are characterized by alterations in genes involved in proliferation, adhesion, signal transduction, and extracellular matrix production.

BRCA-positive breast cancers have significantly higher numbers of tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes {30614364}, and recent studies in mouse models suggest a possible role for combined immune-checkpoint blockade {28592566}; however, the data are still preliminary.

The Enhancing Neuroimaging Genetics through Meta Analysis (ENIGMA) research consortium has recently developed a gene-specific model to refine and classify BRCA1/BRCA2 variants based on the guidelines of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics / Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) {39142283}. VUSs do not impact management, but patients with VUSs should be followed up for variant reclassification {38175972}.

BRCA-like syndromes (BRCAness)

BRCAness refers to a group of tumours in the breast and ovary with homologous recombination repair defects or homologous recombination deficiency, involving non–BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes and mimicking BRCA1/BRCA2 loss. Those tumours are sensitive to the synthetic lethality effect of PARP inhibitors {37057596}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours demonstrate features similar to those of breast cancers that are not BRCA associated, although with higher proportions of smooth circumscribed boundaries and an internal lobulated cut surface with areas of haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

Tumours arising in BRCA1 and BRCA2 pathogenic variant carriers are more likely than sporadic breast cancers to have certain histological and molecular characteristics (see #48200Table 10.03). In addition, breast cancers in BRCA1 variant carriers are associated with more-aggressive tumour characteristics than those in BRCA2 variant carriers {30186165;28796549}. Tumours in BRCA1 (vs BRCA2) variant carriers have been found to exhibit significantly higher nuclear and histological grades, with prominent lymphocytic infiltrate. They were more often of the triple-negative type, whereas BRCA2 tumours were more frequently of the ER-positive phenotype. Invasive breast carcinomas of no special type (NST), invasive breast carcinomas with medullary pattern (formerly designated medullary carcinomas), and metaplastic carcinomas have been reported in BRCA1 variant carriers {17551255;26115045}. BRCA2 tumours more frequently present with ductal carcinoma in situ alone, and they more frequently exhibit calcifications that are more easily detectable by mammography {28343309}. Lobular carcinoma in situ has been reported in BRCA2 variant carriers mostly as an incidental finding in prophylactic mastectomy specimens. Limited data suggest that younger patients with inflammatory breast cancer may be more likely to harbour a BRCA1 or BRCA2 variant {29044548}.

Because the histopathological features of cancers in BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers differ from those of cancers with no known mutation, these features have been considered useful for mutation prediction, risk prediction algorithms, and statistical modelling to assess the pathogenicity of BRCA variants of uncertain clinical significance, as well as for informing clinical strategies for screening and prophylaxis. The proportions of ER-negative breast tumours and triple-negative tumours decrease with increasing patient age at diagnosis among BRCA1 variant carriers, but they increase with age at diagnosis among BRCA2 variant carriers. In both BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers, ER-negative tumours have been found to be of higher histological grade than ER-positive tumours {22144499}. Interestingly, in addition to many cases being of basal-like phenotype (see below), a substantial portion of BRCA variant carriers have recently been found to have the luminal A subtype. These cases were mainly in BRCA2 variant carriers (35%) rather than BRCA1 variant carriers (9%). The luminal B subtype was more frequent than the luminal A subtype and was also found in a higher proportion of BRCA2 variant carriers (40% in BRCA2 vs 21% in BRCA1 variant carriers) {30342055}.

The distribution of histological types of BRCA1-associated breast cancers differs from that of sporadic breast cancers {20436502}, with the majority being invasive breast carcinoma NST; approximately 15% are classified as invasive breast carcinoma with medullary pattern (formerly designated medullary carcinoma), histological grade 3, with heavy lymphocytic infiltration and pushing margins {9701363}. Triple-negative breast cancers expressing keratins (CK5/6), referred to as the basal phenotype, are overrepresented among BRCA1 variant tumours {14519755}. Although basal cytokeratin and/or EGFR expression can be used to identify triple-negative breast cancers with basal-like phenotype, the expression of these markers alone is not sufficient to determine which women with triple-negative breast cancers are likely to harbour BRCA1 pathogenic variants {19390427}. BRCA2-associated breast cancers do not appear to exhibit a specific pathological phenotype, although lobular tumours have been found to be more likely to be BRCA2-related {22144499}.

Analysis of large pathology datasets accrued by CIMBA and the Breast Cancer Association Consortium (BCAC) showed that an ER-positive phenotype negatively predicted BRCA1 mutation status, irrespective of grade. Patient age at diagnosis is an important variable, with ER-negative grade 3 histopathology being more predictive of positive BRCA1 mutation status in women aged ≥ 50 years (likelihood ratio: 4.13; range: 3.70–4.62) than in women < 50 years (likelihood ratio: 3.16; range: 2.96–3.37); likewise for triple-negative tumour status in women aged ≥ 50 years (likelihood ratio: 4.41; range: 3.86–5.04) versus women < 50 years (likelihood ratio: 3.73; range: 3.43–4.05). These features were only modestly predictive for BRCA2 variant cases, with grade being more informative than ER status {25857409}.

BRCA2 male breast cancers are of significantly higher stage and grade than BRCA2 female breast cancers. In SEER Program data, the grade was also higher than that of sporadic male breast cancers {26857456}.

Cytology

The cytology of BRCA-associated breast cancers is the same as that of non–BRCA associated breast cancers, apart from the individual features associated with the relative frequency of BRCA1-associated cases with medullary pattern.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 or BRCA2. The latest American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) and Society of Surgical Oncology (SSO) guidance recommends germline testing of all breast cancer patients aged < 65 years and in those > 65 years based on family history, ancestry, and/or eligibility for PARP inhibitors {38175972}. All patients with recurrent breast cancer who are eligible for PARP inhibitors should be offered genetic testing regardless of family history {38175972}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: identification of a germline pathogenic variant of BRCA1 or BRCA2.

Staging

The staging of cancers in the setting of BRCA-related cancer predisposition syndrome is similar to that of non-BRCA cancers.

Prognosis and prediction

The eligibility of patients for germline BRCA1/BRCA2 testing is determined using tools, including the BRCAPRO model, the Breast and Ovarian Analysis of Disease Incidence and Carrier Estimation Algorithm (BOADICEA), and the Manchester scoring system {17574839;22711857;27749552;27514840;27514841;28513990}. Breast cancer polygenic risk scores have been developed for White women, and they may overestimate the risk in other ethnicities {34460111}. More recently, BRCA1/BRCA2 germline testing has been used to inform eligibility for PARP inhibitor therapy in primary breast cancer.

In one study, cases with BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline pathogenic variants were associated with worse overall survival and disease-specific survival than were non-mutated cases {27749552}. Several meta-analyses have supported these findings {25348513;28513990}, whereas other meta-analyses have found the results to be inconclusive {25816289;27659521}. A prospective cohort study of outcome in 2733 women who developed breast cancer at or before the age of 40 years, of whom 12% were found to be BRCA1/BRCA2 variant carriers, showed no significant difference in survival between BRCA1/BRCA2 carriers and non-carriers over about 10 years of follow-up (median: 8.2 years). Among patients with triple-negative breast cancer, BRCA1/BRCA2 variant carriers had a better overall survival than non-carriers after 2 years of follow-up but not after 5 or 10 years {29337092}.

The difference in overall survival between patients with breast cancer who are BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers could be ascribed to tumour biology. Although patients with breast cancer with BRCA1 variants may have a lower 10-year disease-free survival rate than patients with breast cancer with BRCA2 variants, chemotherapy and risk-reducing contralateral mastectomy can reduce mortality for both BRCA1 and BRCA2 variant carriers {29164974}. Cisplatin and oophorectomy have been demonstrated to be effective therapies for women with breast cancer and a BRCA1 variant {29247441}.

The term “synthetic lethality” refers to the scenario in which an individual mutation in either of two genes has no effect on cell viability but mutations in both of the genes lead to cell death {27514841}. One of the best-known examples of synthetic lethality relates to BRCA1/BRCA2 and PARP inhibitors. The BRCA1/BRCA2 genes are responsible for repairing double-strand DNA breaks, and PARP is responsible for repairing single-strand DNA breaks through base excision repair. PARP inhibitors have been found to be effective in patients with ovarian cancer associated with either germline or somatic BRCA1/BRCA2 alterations {27908594}, with greater clinical benefit seen in patients with platinum-sensitive disease {20406929}. Similar promising results have also been found for BRCA1/BRCA2-deficient breast carcinomas {34081848}. The application of mutation signatures, as described above, may be useful in identifying these patients.

Besides the possibility of surveillance strategies and chemoprevention, in the USA, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the Society of Gynecologic Oncology (SGO) guidelines recommend risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy for BRCA1/BRCA2 germline variant carriers at the age of 35–40 years or once childbearing is complete, together with prophylactic mastectomy [[National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN). Genetic/familial high-risk assessment: breast and ovarian. Plymouth Meeting (PA): NCCN; 2018 accessed 2018 Nov 4. Available from: https://www.nccn.org/professionals/physician_gls/default.aspx. ]] {20810374}. This has been found to reduce the risk of breast cancer by as much as 90%.
