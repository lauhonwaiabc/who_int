3.2.6 Invasive cribriform carcinoma

Definition

Invasive cribriform carcinoma (ICC) is a low- to intermediate-grade invasive carcinoma composed of islands of tumour cells with well-defined cribriform spaces formed of fused glandular formations.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There are no specific features.

Clinical features

No specific clinical feature distinguishes ICC from other types of breast cancer. ICC may present as a mass, but it is frequently clinically occult. On mammography, ICC most commonly appears as an irregular spiculated mass with increased density, and it may harbour microcalcifications {34325338;25789036;28618754}. ICC, especially pure ICC, may also be mammographically occult {25789036}. On ultrasound, ICC exhibits a homogeneous, hypoechoic internal echo texture. It may have an irregular shape, be partially lobulated, or have well-defined margins {34325338;25789036}. In some cases, ICC can appear benign on sonography {25789036;28618754}. Multifocality is observed in 10–20% of ICCs {8780923;6884999}. Most ICCs have been reported in female patients; male ICC is extremely rare {34325338}.

Epidemiology

While pure ICC is rare, accounting for approximately 0.2% of all invasive breast carcinomas (IBCs) {33593316}, IBCs with a partial cribriform component are about three times more frequently reported in case series {28618754}. The median age at diagnosis for pure and mixed type is 46.5 and 54 years, respectively {34325338}, with an older age at diagnosis reported in some studies {33593316;17330844}. The average age in men is approximately a decade older in the few reported cases {34325338}.

Etiology

See section Epithelial tumours of the breast: Part 2: Introduction.

Pathogenesis

ICC has genomic and transcriptomic features similar to those of tubular carcinoma (both belong to the luminal A molecular class) {32794061}, with a similar immunophenotype (consistent expression of hormone receptors and lack of ERBB2 [HER2] overexpression), and both are associated with the same family of low-grade precursor lesions {18720457}.

Macroscopic appearance

No specific macroscopic features differentiate ICC from IBC of no special type (NST). ICC usually consists of a firm/hard spiculated mass, with a mean size of 31 mm {6884999}. ICCs as large as 200 mm have been described {8780923}.

Histopathology

ICC consists of invasive epithelial islands containing well-defined, rounded spaces similar in appearance to cribriform-type (cribriform meaning sieve-like or lace-like) ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS). The islands have an ovoid or angular outline and are set within a desmoplastic stroma. They comprise multilayered epithelial cells of small to intermediate size forming secondary glandular structures lined by cuboidal to columnar cells. Apical secretions are sometimes present, and the spaces may contain eosinophilic secretions with or without calcification {6884999;7810003;3343375}. Mitotic activity is low and substantial nuclear atypia is absent. Based on these features, most ICCs are classified as grade 1 tumours (with cribriform pattern given a score of 1 for tubule formation) {20804570}. Invasive cribriform and tubular morphological patterns frequently coexist, and the tumour is classified according to the dominant pattern. Stromal osteoclast-like giant cells have been described {11480724}. Low- or intermediate-grade DCIS, usually with a cribriform architecture, is present in 80% of ICCs {6884999}.

Immunohistochemistry for myoepithelial markers may be used to distinguish ICC from cribriform DCIS. ICCs are ER-positive and HER2-negative and form part of the low-grade breast neoplasia pathway {33593316;20452298}. ICC is distinguished from adenoid cystic carcinoma of the breast by its strong ER positivity and single-cell population {34458945}. Another lesion to be distinguished from ICC is primary cutaneous cribriform carcinoma, which can show striking similarity to ICC. However, unlike ICC, these tumours do not express GATA3 {29431200}.

Cytology

ICC is an uncommon lesion, and often mixed with tubular carcinoma and IBC-NST. The specific diagnosis of ICC on FNAB is challenging. Direct smears show relatively cohesive sheets and complex tissue fragments of bland epithelial cells lacking myoepithelial cells with a cribriform architecture in some tissue fragments {24167007}. Naked bipolar nuclei are absent {11480724}. Differentiation from cribriform-type DCIS is not possible by cytopathology alone and requires core needle biopsy.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: > 90% of the tumour is composed of cribriform islands of epithelial cells with uniform small round nuclei and low mitotic activity (grade 1).

Desirable: ER-positive and HER2-negative.

Staging

Staging of ICC is similar to that of other types of IBC (see section Epithelial tumours of the breast: Part 2: Introduction).

Prognosis and prediction

Generally, ICC presents at a relatively early stage compared with IBC-NST, with 73–90% of tumours < 20 mm and approximately 80% of patients lymph node–negative at presentation {32794061;22039080;33075683}. Axillary lymph node metastases, when present, are usually of low volume with no more than three positive nodes {32794061;22039080}. As with tubular carcinoma, the outcome for patients with ICC is favourable {6884999}; the 5-year disease-free survival rate is above 97% {34325338;22039080} and both 5-year and 10-year overall survival rates are between 90% and 100% {6884999;32794061;22039080;34325338} and are better than for IBC-NST. The outcome for patients with mixed ICC is less favourable than for patients with the pure form but better than for patients with IBC-NST {6884999} of identical grade and stage {33593316;23412348}. Considering the excellent outcome of ICC, adjuvant chemotherapy and axillary lymph node sampling may not be indicated {22039080;32794061;23412348}.
