2.3.0 Adenomas: Introduction

Adenomas of the breast are benign glandular epithelial tumours secondary to a proliferation of the cells in the terminal duct lobular unit. An adenoma may be an incidental finding or detected as a discrete well-circumscribed mass on clinical examination and imaging. Adenomas contain various proportions of glandular, fibrous, and fatty tissue and are further classified depending on the predominant cell type composing the lesion and its ratio to other cell types. Fibroadenoma is the most common type of adenoma. It is composed predominantly of stromal cells admixed with glands and is part of the spectrum of fibroepithelial tumours (see section Fibroadenoma). Other adenomas of the breast are less common and include tubular adenoma, lactating adenoma, and ductal adenoma. By definition, adenomas are benign and have a negligible risk of malignant transformation.
