4.1.1 Hamartoma

Definition

A hamartoma is a well-demarcated, generally encapsulated mass composed of variable proportions of normal but disorganized breast tissue components.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: adenolipoma; chondrolipoma; myoid hamartoma.

Not recommended: fibroadenolipoma; adenolipofibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is no predilection for any breast quadrant for the development of hamartoma {12121234}.

Clinical features

Hamartomas occur almost exclusively in female patients with a mean age of 40–55 years {34403159;25141109;39911671}. Hamartomas may present as soft palpable masses {12121234;12461066} or are asymptomatic and detected by mammography {7785651;11328316}. Imaging shows circumscribed rounded masses, sometimes with intralesional heterogeneous echogenicity and posterior shadowing on ultrasonography {8996902;34403159}. Owing to their well-defined borders, hamartomas are easily enucleated. Most hamartomas are sporadic; multiple hamartomas can be seen in Cowden syndrome {9445133}.

Epidemiology

Hamartomas are described in case series to account for < 5% of benign breast tumours {8078805;25141109}, occurring predominantly in women in their fifth decade of life (ranging from 33.5 to 66.5 years) {25141109}, although they can be found at any age.

Etiology

Most hamartomas are sporadic, and the etiology is largely unclear, although weak evidence suggests hormonal influences {10337660;12121234}.

Pathogenesis

Because some hamartomas are associated with molecular changes, different perspectives exist as to whether they are neoplasms or malformations. Genetic data is limited, but aberrations involving chromosome regions 12q12-q15 and 6p21, as well as PTEN loss, have been described {9290959;7497449;8143274}.

Macroscopic appearance

Hamartomas are round or oval, measuring as much as 200 mm in diameter. The cut surface may resemble normal breast tissue, lipoma, or fibroadenoma.

Histopathology

Generally encapsulated, hamartomas are lobulated and show ducts, lobules, and adipose tissue in varying proportions and disorganized architecture. Commonly there is prominent intralobular fibrosis, and the lobules have an atrophic appearance. Adipocytes may occur singly, and they are encased by dense fibrous tissue (encapsulated fat). Hamartomas may be called adenolipomas when ducts/lobules are mixed with a prominent component of adipose tissue {1866672}, myoid hamartomas when a smooth muscle component is prominent {3972402}, and chondrolipomas when chondroid islands exist within adipose tissue {2669070}. When only limited material is available, as with core biopsy, the diagnosis may be suggested by taking into consideration the radiological and clinical findings {12461066}.

Hamartomas should be distinguished from fibroadenomas based on the following features: (1) an absence of an intracanalicular or pericanalicular growth pattern in hamartomas; (2) the presence of normal terminal duct lobular units, which are often enlarged and arranged in a haphazard manner; and (3) the stroma in hamartomas is less fibrous and lacks the diffuse CD34 staining that is often seen in fibroadenomas (CD34 expression in hamartomas is primarily observed in the intralobular stromal component of the lesion). Myoid hamartoma shows diffuse strong SMA staining that is rarely seen in fibroadenoma. The diagnosis of hamartoma in practice, however, does not usually require the use of immunohistochemistry.

Cytology

Low cellularity smears show scant lobules and small tissue fragments of ductal epithelium and minimal stroma, the normal components of breast, and the diagnosis relies on the triple test {12461066;12925988}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a mass lesion comprising components of normal breast tissue; correlation is required with clinical and imaging features.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Hamartomas rarely recur. Reported recurrences probably represent incompletely excised lesions {7785651;19624414}.
