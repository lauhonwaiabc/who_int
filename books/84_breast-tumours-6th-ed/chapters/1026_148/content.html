10.2.6 Hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome (CDH1, CTNNA1)

Definition

Hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome (HGBCS) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline pathogenic variants in CDH1 or CTNNA1 and characterized predominantly by diffuse gastric cancer and/or lobular breast cancer, occasionally with cleft lip/palate.

Related terminology

Acceptable: hereditary lobular breast cancer; hereditary diffuse gastric cancer syndrome; diffuse gastric and lobular breast cancer syndrome; diffuse gastric cancer syndrome / lobular breast cancer syndrome (DGCS/LBCS); CDH1-related tumour susceptibility syndrome; E-cadherin–related tumour susceptibility syndrome.

Subtype(s)

CDH1-related hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome; CTNNA1-related hereditary gastric and breast cancer syndrome

Localization

In HGBCS, there is no specific localization of the associated lobular breast cancer (LBC). Diffuse gastric cancer can affect all topographical regions of the stomach {34643667;32758476}.

Clinical features

Female carriers of CDH1 pathogenic variants have a 37–55% lifetime risk of developing breast carcinoma {26182300;38873722;31246251;31296550} with a predisposition for invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC). The clinical presentation of ILC is described in detail in section Invasive lobular carcinoma. A multicentre study from the European Network on Genetic Tumour Risk Syndromes recently proposed criteria for lobular breast cancer–centred CDH1 testing, as described below, in Diagnostic molecular pathology {36436516}. In this cohort study, germline pathogenic variants in CDH1 were identified in individuals not fulfilling the classic clinical criteria for HGBCS screening, suggesting that identification of these variants may provide a novel method to test women with LBC with early age at diagnosis and/or positive family history of breast cancer.

The estimated lifetime risk for diffuse gastric cancer in carriers of a CDH1 pathogenic variant is 10.3–42% for men and 6.5–33% for women in unselected cohorts {31296550;38873722;31246251}. The risk of gastric cancer in carriers of CTNNA1 truncating variants is less clear, with early estimates of 49–57% probably inflated owing to family selection bias {36038258}. The clinical picture of CTNNA1-related HGBCS differs mainly regarding the uncertain LBC risk {34425242}.

For a summary of the main features of HGBCS, see #50767Table 10.10.

Epidemiology

The frequency of a germline CDH1 variant is low (< 5%) in women with early-onset or familial breast carcinoma without a family history of gastric carcinoma {20921021;36436516;38652475}.

Etiology

HGBCS is predominantly caused by germline pathogenic variants in CDH1, located at chromosome 16q22.1 {26182300;36436516} and, much less frequently, by truncating variants in CTNNA1, located at chromosome 5q31.2. No significant mutation hotspots or evidence for genotype–phenotype correlations have been observed in CDH1. The CDH1 gene comprises 16 exons, and encodes E-cadherin, a transmembrane protein that is predominantly expressed at the basolateral membrane of epithelial cells and is involved in homophilic cell–cell adhesion and transduction of mechanical force {23127608;25925582}. Its cytoplasmic domain interacts with numerous structural and regulatory proteins, including the catenin family {23127608}. These interactions influence cell survival signalling, the microtubule network, mitotic spindle orientation {38572612}, and the organization of the cortical actin cytoskeleton {23127608}, with a profound impact on cell shape, polarity, and motility {25925582}.

Pathogenesis

Initiation of diffuse gastric cancer and LBC in families with HGBCS requires somatic inactivation of the second CDH1 allele. Several CDH1 germline variants have been reported to be associated with an increased risk of ILC. Classically, ILC in patients with CDH1 germline variants are also associated with a predisposition to diffuse gastric cancer {34643667;36436516}. However, recent studies have identified CDH1 variants with a potential pathogenic role in hereditary LBC in the absence of a family history of gastric carcinoma {23709761;38652475}. The loss of E-cadherin expression occurs via various somatic mechanisms: somatic CDH1 mutations, loss of heterozygosity, CDH1 promoter hypermethylation, and allelic imbalance {11439345;10973239;18788075;19269290}.

While CDH1 germline variants are strongly associated with hereditary ILC, germline pathogenic variants detected on somatic testing of ILC are more frequently identified in BRCA2, PALB2, and CHEK2 than in CDH1; overall, CDH1 germline variants are found in only 0.3% of ILCs {31263054}.

Macroscopic appearance

ILCs in general often present as poorly defined masses that can be difficult to delineate macroscopically. There may be a vague area of increased firmness rather than a discrete mass, and the final microscopic tumour diameter often exceeds the macroscopic size estimate.

Histopathology

CDH1 germline variants have specifically been associated with ILC {37758801}.

ILC is described in detail in section Invasive lobular carcinoma; in summary, it is characterized by diffuse infiltration by small round cells with monomorphic nuclei that invade individually or as single files and cords. There is minimal disruption of background breast architecture, and the tumour cells often surround existing structures, forming a targetoid arrangement. On occasion, the cells may have prominent intracytoplasmic mucin vacuoles or pale foamy cytoplasm imparting a histiocytoid appearance. There are subtypes with solid, alveolar, or tubulolobular growth patterns, as well as a pleomorphic subtype with marked nuclear pleomorphism that often has apocrine morphology with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Classic ILCs are almost always ER-positive and ERBB2 (HER2)-negative, although the pleomorphic subtype may be ER-negative and is HER2-positive in approximately 15–40% of cases. Loss of E-cadherin membrane staining occurs in ILC as a consequence of complete (biallelic) inactivation of CDH1, irrespective of germline versus somatic origin of the CDH1 alterations {21271559}.

Cytology

Cytologically, aspirates of ILC contain small monomorphic cells with mild atypia, often with intracytoplasmic mucin vacuoles. The cells are present singly, as loose clusters, or forming cords. Some series have shown an increased false negative rate by FNA, largely related to low tumour cellularity, although the cells are sometimes so bland that they may be mistaken for lymphocytes {18300231}. A more detailed description is provided in section Invasive lobular carcinoma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic testing for CDH1 germline variants should be considered in families or individuals fulfilling the following breast cancer–centred criteria: ≥ 2 cases of ILC in family members aged < 50 years, bilateral ILC in individuals < 70 years, ≥ 2 cases of breast cancer with 1 case of confirmed ILC, history of gastric and breast cancer with 1 confirmed case of ILC regardless of age, and isolated ILC in individuals < 55 years {36436516}. In the setting of HDGCS, the International Gastric Cancer Linkage Consortium (IGCLC) provides clinical practice guidelines {32758476}.

The criteria recommended for germline CDH1 and CTNNA1 testing in HGBCS are listed in <<50768>>Box 10.04.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: germline pathogenic variants in CDH1 or CTNNA1.

Staging

The staging is the same as for other breast carcinomas.

Prognosis and prediction

The pTNM staging system is currently the single most important factor for prognosis prediction in patients with HGBCS. Lifetime risk is higher in families with a strong history of gastric cancer {26182300;38873722}, with such cases particularly prevalent in Europe {36436516}. In the setting of HGBCS, diffuse gastric cancer is the main cause of mortality in both sexes.
