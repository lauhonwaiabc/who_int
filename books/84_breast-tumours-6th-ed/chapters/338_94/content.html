3.3.8 Tall cell carcinoma with reversed polarity

Definition

Tall cell carcinoma with reversed polarity (TCCRP) is a rare type of invasive breast carcinoma characterized by tall columnar cells with reversed nuclear polarity, arranged in solid and solid papillary patterns, most commonly associated with IDH2 p.Arg172 hotspot mutations.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Not recommended: solid papillary carcinoma with reverse polarity; breast tumour resembling the tall cell variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma; solid papillary carcinoma resembling the tall cell variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is no specific predilection for location in the breast.

Clinical features

These tumours present as a screen-detected mammographic density or palpable mass {37760817;39447334}. Ultrasonography reveals a hypoechoic mass with or without posterior shadowing {19594763;22313838;35904337}. Axillary lymph node metastases have been reported in a few patients {16501842;17172492;28418993;30308500}.

Epidemiology

Current knowledge of these entities is mainly based on single case reports and no specific epidemiological data are currently available. All of the reported patients are adult women {28418993;29785016;29603332;30227763;39447334}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

TCCRP is characterized by IDH2 (NM_002168.4) p.Arg172 hotspot mutations, which have been reported in about 84% (32 of 38) of the cases studied {28375433;29785016;29603332;30227763;27913435}. A p.Arg120 mutation of the IDH2 gene has also been described {33708566}. Hotspot mutations affecting IDH2 have been described in tumours affecting several organs, but they are extremely rare in breast carcinomas other than TCCRP {29785016;27913435}. PIK3CA missense mutations have been identified in about 68% (26 of 38) of these tumours {28375433;29785016;29603332;30227763}, although mutations affecting PI3K pathway–related genes are quite common in breast cancer. There is no difference in the frequency of PIK3CA mutations in cases with and without IDH2 mutations {28375433;29785016;29603332;30227763;27913435}. BRAF mutations and RET/PTC rearrangement, which are molecular features of papillary thyroid carcinoma, have been negative in the TCCRP cases studied {19543246}.

Macroscopic appearance

TCCRP most often presents as a well-circumscribed, unencapsulated, firm, white to grey mass {17172492;24868004;29785016}. The tumours range from 6 to 50 mm, with a median size of 15 mm {28418993;29785016;29603332;30227763}.

Histopathology

TCCRP is composed of circumscribed nests of epithelial cells, most often distributed in dense fibrous stroma. Many of the nests have delicate fibrovascular cores, imparting a solid papillary pattern. Foamy histiocytes are often present within the fibrovascular cores. True papillae and cystic structures containing colloid-like material can be observed in some cases. The neoplastic nests are surrounded by a thin rim of capillary vessels. The tumour cells are tall and columnar, with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Most cases show bland, round to ovoid nuclei, with variable presence of nuclear grooves and intranuclear cytoplasmic inclusions {12883243}. The most striking histological feature of TCCRP is the presence of nuclei at the apical rather than basal poles of the columnar epithelial cells. Mitotic figures are rare {28418993;29785016;30308500;30227763}. Although TCCRP shows some histological similarities to the tall cell subtype of papillary thyroid carcinoma, immunohistochemical and molecular studies confirm the breast origin of this tumour {28418993;29785016;30308500;30227763}.

Most TCCRPs are considered invasive as the neoplastic nests lack the typical myoepithelial layer, even if there is no visible stromal reaction {28418993}. Recently, 2 cases of TCCRP with desmoplastic stromal reaction have been described {39828059}. The presence of axillary lymph node {28418993} and distant metastases confirms the invasive nature {16501842}. One case of carcinoma in situ with papillary architecture and features of reverse polarity has been recently reported {39702261}.

Most TCCRPs have a triple-negative phenotype, and the remaining cases show weak or focal hormone receptor expression {17172492;27913435;28418993;29603332;29785016;30227763;30308500;37760817}. Almost all reported cases are ERBB2 (HER2)-negative {28418993;29785016;29603332;28375433}. The Ki-67 proliferation index is usually low (< 20%). These lesions characteristically express both low- and high-molecular-weight cytokeratins, as shown with CK7 and CK5/6 or CK14 immunostaining, respectively. (The CK5/6 or CK14 immunostaining may be of mosaic pattern, reminiscent of the pattern seen in usual-type ductal hyperplasia.) Chromogranin A and synaptophysin are consistently negative. Myoepithelial cells are not present around the tumour cell nests of TCCRP. In a study of 9 cases, all showed positive calretinin expression (with strong diffuse staining or focal staining) {29785016}. The anti-IDH2 Arg172 antibody can be of diagnostic utility in both preoperative and surgical specimens {36222504}. An origin in the breast is supported by variable staining for GCDFP-15, GATA3, and mammaglobin and SOX10, along with a lack of staining for TTF1 (NKX2-1), and thyroglobulin. Immunohistochemistry with antimitochondrial antigen shows condensation of mitochondria in the basal pole of the cells {28418993}.

Cytology

Cytopathological findings have been rarely reported {38262369}: moderately cellular smears with papillary-like fronds and small tissue fragments, consisting of columnar cells with apical nuclei, suggesting reverse polarity.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

TCCRP frequently harbours IDH2 p.Arg172 mutations {28375433;29785016;29603332;30227763;27913435}, which can also be demonstrated with immunohistochemistry {31896809;36222504}. Recently, PIK3CA hotspot mutations have been detected in about 50% of cases {31896809}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an invasive breast carcinoma with the neoplastic cell nests arranged in a predominantly solid papillary pattern, composed of columnar epithelial cells showing reversed polarity of the nuclei.

Desirable: expression of both low- and high-molecular-weight cytokeratins; a triple-negative or weakly hormone receptor–positive phenotype.

Staging

Cases should be staged according to the criteria for invasive breast carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

TCCRP has an indolent clinical course, with a favourable prognosis. The majority of patients have been disease-free during the follow-up period (range: 3–132 months) {28418993;29785016;28375433;29154377;38434689;39447334}. Only 1 case with aggressive clinical behaviour (bone metastases) has been reported {16501842}.
