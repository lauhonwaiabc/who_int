10.2.2 PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (PTEN)

Definition

PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (PTEN) is a heterogeneous group of disorders with autosomal dominant inheritance caused by germline pathogenic variants of PTEN and characterized by multiple hamartomas and a variable risk of breast, thyroid, and other cancers.

MIM numbering

158350 Cowden syndrome 1; CWS1

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: multiple hamartoma syndrome; Cowden syndrome.

Subtype(s)

Bannayan–Riley–Ruvalcaba syndrome; Proteus syndrome; Lhermitte–Duclos disease

Localization

PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome (PHTS) affects multiple organ systems derived from any of the three germ layers.

Clinical features

Cowden syndrome (CS) is characterized by mucocutaneous lesions (multiple facial trichilemmomas, acral keratoses, papillomatous papules, and mucosal lesions are considered pathognomonic), an increased cancer risk, benign hamartomatous overgrowth of tissues (including hamartomatous/juvenile gastrointestinal polyposis), and macrocephaly {24136893;28476234}. Diffuse oesophageal glycogenic acanthosis in combination with colonic polyposis may be diagnostic of CS {21437855;23238744}. Cancer risk is relatively widespread, including risk for breast, thyroid, endometrial, renal cell, and colon cancers, as well as melanoma {20301661;28476234}. PHTS also includes Bannayan–Riley–Ruvalcaba syndrome and Proteus syndrome. However, most PHTS cases correspond to CS, and these terms are often used interchangeably {19668082}. Patients with PHTS may develop vascular proliferations. The lifetime cancer risks and general screening recommendations in PHTS are detailed in #50761Table 10.05.

Epidemiology

The prevalence of CS has been estimated at about 1 case per 250 000 individuals in a European population {10234502}. Female patients with CS have a cumulative lifetime breast cancer risk (to the age of 70 years) as high as 85% and a relative risk of 25 {28476234;22252256;20565722}. Multifocal and bilateral breast cancer has been reported in 34% of patients with CS with breast cancer {20565722;28476234}. Male patients with CS are also at increased risk of breast cancer, although the exact magnitude of risk is unclear {11238682;28476234}. Benign breast lesions can be found in the majority of female patients with CS {9445133}.

Etiology

Cowden syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder with age-related penetrance and variable expression. Germline pathogenic variants in PTEN (10q23.3) are found in about 85% of CS cases, as well as in subsets of other PHTS disorders (65% in Bannayan–Riley–Ruvalcaba syndrome, 20% in Proteus syndrome, and 50% in Proteus-like syndrome) {19668082;18794875;9140396;8673088}. Individualized risk calculation may aid in the assessment of a patient’s risk of carrying a germline pathogenic variant of PTEN on the basis of clinical features {21194675}.

Germline pathogenic variants of SDH have been found in about 5% of patients with PTEN mutation–negative CS / CS-like phenotypes, and they are associated with increased frequencies of breast, thyroid, and renal cancers beyond those conferred by germline pathogenic variants of PTEN {18678321}.

Pathogenesis

PTEN, which is a virtually ubiquitously expressed tumour suppressor, is a dual-specificity lipid and protein phosphatase that regulates cell proliferation, cell migration, and apoptosis through inhibition of AKT via the PI3K/AKT pathway {23613428}. Inactivation of the second copy of the gene allows deregulation of the AKT pathway.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance of tumours is similar to that of their sporadic counterparts.

Histopathology

Benign breast pathologies occur in about 75% of female patients with CS and include ductal hyperplasia, intraductal papillomatosis, adenosis, lobular atrophy, fibroadenomas, and fibrocystic change. Many patients show features suggestive of a breast hamartoma. Malignant breast lesions are mostly invasive carcinoma of no special type (NST), often associated with ductal carcinoma in situ {9445133}. A recent report suggests that apocrine differentiation is common in breast cancers in patients with germline pathogenic variants of PTEN, and expression profiling puts these cancers in the molecular apocrine category {20712882}.

Cytology

Cytologically, individual tumours are indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

PHTS is caused by germline pathogenic variants of PTEN.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: detection of germline pathogenic variants in PTEN; presence of lesions consistent with the spectrum of PHTS.

Desirable: patients are selected for genetic testing based on the International Cowden Consortium (ICC) operational diagnostic criteria, listed in #50760Box 10.01; a more quantitative set of criteria is nomogram-based {21194675}; see also #50759Table 10.06.

Staging

The staging is as for sporadic counterparts.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with PHTS are at increased risk of several types of cancer, and interval screening is recommended (see #50761Table 10.05) {22252256;23613428;23335809}. Medical management guidelines for PHTS are available from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) (see #50600Fig. 10.XXX).
