6.3.1 Myofibroblastoma

Definition

Myofibroblastoma is a benign tumour of the mammary stroma, composed of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

Acceptable: mammary-type myofibroblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Myofibroblastoma occurs most commonly in the breast parenchyma, but it can also occur in extramammary sites (mammary-type myofibroblastoma), especially in the inguinal/groin area, vulva, perineum, and scrotum.

Clinical features

Myofibroblastoma usually presents as a slow-growing, painless, non-tender mass {18976021;26328916}. The imaging features are not specific, showing a well-circumscribed, lobulated, homogeneous, solid mass lacking microcalcifications {27936420;29420368}. Rarely, bilateral or multicentric lesions have been reported {8832563}.

Epidemiology

This unusual benign tumour has been described to have no sex predilection, with patient ages reported to range from 1 to 87 years, although most cases are seen in elderly men and postmenopausal women {18976021}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Myofibroblastoma arises from a precursor cell of the mammary stroma, capable of assuming different sizes and shapes, as well as differentiating along several mesenchymal lineages (especially lipomatous or leiomyomatous tissues) {11482575;11889594}. Genetically, myofibroblastoma shows monoallelic or biallelic deletions of 13q (13q14) including the RB1 locus, sometimes in combination with monosomy of 16q {10878550;22575260}. Deletion of 13q results in the loss of RB1 nuclear expression on immunohistochemistry {22790852}. Because spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma and cellular angiofibroma share with myofibroblastoma not only some morphological and immunohistochemical features but also the same genetic alterations, it has been speculated that these tumours belong to the spectrum of the so-called 13q/RB1 family of tumours {22575260}.

Macroscopic appearance

Myofibroblastoma presents as a well-circumscribed, unencapsulated tumour with a rubbery to gelatinous, yellow to whitish/grey cut surface. The tumours range from a few millimetres to 150 mm, with most cases not exceeding 30 mm {18976021;26328916}.

Histopathology

The tumour has circumscribed margins and usually consists of bland, oval to spindle cells with pale to eosinophilic cytoplasm, arranged haphazardly or in short intersecting fascicles with interspersed variably hyalinized collagen bundles. Areas with storiform or neural-like growth patterns can be seen focally. The nuclei, which are round to oval in shape, may exhibit small nucleoli, grooves, or pseudoinclusions. Mitoses are absent or rare. Entrapment of breast ducts or lobules is usually not seen. Unusual features include focally infiltrative margins, high cellularity, focal nuclear pleomorphism, multinucleated floret-like cells, intracytoplasmic and extracellular hyaline globules, and extensive myxoedematous stroma with blood vessels containing fibrinoid material, as well as focal smooth muscle and cartilaginous or osseous metaplasia {18976021;26328916;9199625;3037930}. High mitotic count, necrosis, and extensive infiltrating margins are not features of myofibroblastoma. Five morphological patterns have been described: lipomatous, myxoid, fibrous/collagenized, epithelioid/deciduoid, and palisading/Schwannian-like {18976021;26328916;26523539}.

The tumour cells typically coexpress desmin and CD34 (each positive in 90% of cases), along with ER, PR, and AR {18976021;26328916}. There is loss of RB1 expression in approximately 90% of cases {22790852}. α-SMA, calponin, CD10, BCL2, and CD99 are variably expressed. The tumour cells are negative for cytokeratins, EMA, S100, STAT6, ALK, and β-catenin {29940288;29413661}.

Cytology

Myofibroblastoma shows variable cellularity, plump spindle cells with poorly defined pale cytoplasm and oval bland nuclei seen singly in the clean background or in tissue fragments separated by metachromatic stroma {15176028}. Nuclear grooves, pseudoinclusions, and occasional binucleation can be seen {27458002}. There is no epithelium. Lymphocytes and mast cells are often present. The diagnosis can only be suggested, and the differential diagnosis is broad.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

13q14 deletion can be confirmed by FISH in 70–80% of cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed margins; a mesenchymal tumour without epimyoepithelial components; absent or only mild nuclear atypia or pleomorphism; low mitotic count; short interlacing fascicles.

Desirable: positive immunohistochemistry for desmin, CD34, and ER/PR/AR; loss of RB1 (demonstrated by immunohistochemistry or FISH [13q14 deletion]).

See also #48120Box 6.01.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

Myofibroblastoma is cured by excision, with no tendency to recur.

Histopathology

 

Purely mesenchymal tumour – epimyoepithelial components absent

 

Well-circumscribed margins; rarely focally infiltrative

 

Bland-looking, short to elongated spindle cells; minor component: epithelioid to (more rarely) deciduoid-like cells

 

Absent to mild nuclear atypia; nuclear pleomorphism in a minority of cases

 

Short, haphazardly intersecting fascicles; focal storiform or neural-like pattern

 

Low mitotic count

 

Atypical mitoses and necrosis are absent

 

Fibrous to myxoid stroma with interspersed thick collagen bundles

 

Intratumoural areas of mature adipose tissue can be seen

Immunohistochemistry / molecular pathology features

 

Positive markers: vimentin, desmin, CD34, ER/PR/AR

 

Variable expression of α-SMA, calponin, CD10, BCL2, CD99

 

Negative markers: cytokeratins, EMA, S100, β-catenin, STAT6, RB1, ALK1

 

FISH: 13q14 deletion (in 70–80% of cases)
