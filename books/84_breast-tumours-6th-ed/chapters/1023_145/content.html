Definition

Ataxia–telangiectasia (AT) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by pathogenic germline variants in ATM and characterized by progressive cerebellar ataxia, oculomotor apraxia, choreoathetosis, sinopulmonary infections, oculocutaneous telangiectasia, variable immune deficiency, sterility, a high risk of lymphoid malignancies, and sensitivity to ionizing radiation.

Related terminology

Acceptable: cerebello-oculocutaneous telangiectasia; immunodeficiency with ataxia–telangiectasia; Louis–Bar syndrome; Boder–Sedgwick syndrome.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Patients with AT show a high incidence of malignancy, in particular of leukaemias and lymphomas {14628072}. Heterozygous ATM variant carriers have an elevated risk of breast cancer, and there is also some evidence for an increased risk of ovarian cancer {30128536} and digestive tract cancers {23561644;22585167}.

Clinical features

AT is a pleiotropic disorder with multiple manifestations, including immunodeficiency, neurological deficits, oculocutaneous telangiectasia, and increased propensity for development of multiple cancer types {18813293}. The neurological deficits include cerebellar degeneration with progressive ataxia presenting in infancy. Patients also exhibit growth retardation, hypogonadism, and elevated levels of AFP {8555463}.

For breast cancer, heterozygous pathogenic germline variants in ATM have been associated with young age of onset {9463314;10677309;15928302;11830610;16958054;19781682;21787400;26534844;27595995}. The severe toxic effects of radiation therapy that can occur in AT patients with homozygous pathogenic variants are not observed in the heterozygous variant carriers {11710885}, but there is an elevated risk of contralateral breast cancer in heterozygous carriers of deleterious ATM variants treated with radiotherapy {20305132;28627265}.

The major characteristics of AT are generally consistent, but there is variability in the age of onset, progression, and severity of symptoms, as well as in the occurrence of cancer. In particular, it has been observed that ATM missense variants resulting in some protein expression and residual kinase activity may lead to an attenuated phenotype {22213089}. In this regard, the NM_000051.4:c.7271T>G p.(Val2424Gly) missense variant has been of interest, because rare homozygotes for this variant have shown a less severe form of AT and normal levels of ATM {9463314}. In addition, elevated rates of breast cancer have been observed in homozygote and heterozygote carriers {11830610;16958054}. Germline pathogenic variants of ATM as well as other breast cancer predisposition genes (BRCA1, BRCA2, CHEK2, and PALB2) are significantly associated with increased risk of ductal carcinoma in situ of the breast {39513960}.

Epidemiology

AT is a rare condition, with an estimated prevalence of 1 case per 40 000–100 000 live births {27884168;3788973}.

Etiology

Classic AT is a monogenic disease caused by pathogenic germline variants in ATM. Most patients with AT are compound heterozygotes for variants in ATM {9259193;15289825;29436738;27884168}.

Pathogenesis

The ATM gene extends over 150 kb of DNA and has 66 exons. The ATM transcript is 13 kb, encoding a 350 kDa protein of 3056 amino acids. The initiating codon is within exon 4 {8660985}. ATM, which is expressed in all normal tissues {7792600}, is a serine/threonine kinase that belongs to the PI3K-related kinase family, with conserved kinase and regulatory domains in the C-terminal region {19779456}. ATM has a large number of phosphorylation targets, including major human tumour suppressors such as p53 and BRCA1. In response to DNA damage, in particular double-strand DNA breaks, ATM is activated by autophosphorylation. Many of its known functions relate to the activation of cell-cycle checkpoints that impede progression of the cell cycle in cells with damaged DNA. ATM functions to slow S-phase progression via phosphorylation of components of the MRN complex and direct phosphorylation of BRCA1, FANCD2, and SMC1. Phosphorylation of CHK2, BRCA1, and RAD17 contributes to the G2/M checkpoint {15557798;12612651}. ATM is also known to contribute to DNA repair through CHK2-mediated phosphorylation of BRCA1 and regulation of chromatin dynamics, and it has been implicated in the maintenance of telomere length {15557798;12612651}.

Hundreds of unique germline variants in ATM have been reported (https://databases.lovd.nl/shared/transcripts/0000155), distributed throughout the gene, with no mutation hotspots {9259193}. ATM mutations can be broadly categorized as (1) protein-truncating, non-expressing variants; (2) in-frame deletions encoding mutant protein without kinase activity; or (3) missense variants encoding mutant protein with reduced kinase activity {22213089;25040471}. Most patients with AT carry protein-truncating, non-expressing variants. Approximately 90% of patients with AT have no detectable ATM, 10% have trace amounts, and 1% express a normal amount of protein without kinase activity. Detection of ATM and in vitro assays for radiosensitivity and ATM kinase activity may form part of diagnostic testing for AT {14653405}.

The current estimate of relative risk of breast cancer in ATM variant carriers is 2.8 (90% CI: 2.2–3.7) for protein-truncating variants {26014596}, with a higher risk (11.0; 95% CI: 1.42–85.7) for the NM_000051.4:c.7271T>G p.(Val2424Gly) variant {27595995;16958054;11830610;21787400}. This finding led to the hypothesis that carriers of ATM variants were at increased risk of breast cancer and that the risk might be higher for carriers of missense variants because of a dominant-negative effect on protein function {10607471}. However, the rarity of individual variants of ATM poses an ongoing challenge to researchers seeking to elucidate precise genotype–phenotype correlates of breast cancer incidence, features, and outcome.

There is evidence that inhibition of PARP1 is synthetically lethal with mutation or loss of ATM and that the effect is mediated through mitotic catastrophe independently of apoptosis {20739657}. It was therefore thought that ATM variant carriers with breast cancer might be candidates for treatment with poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors, similar to carriers of mutations in BRCA1 and BRCA2 {27413114}. However, recent analyses have shown that breast tumours from ATM variant carriers often harbour biallelic inactivation of ATM but lack mutation signatures of homologous recombination deficiency {29506079} and large-scale genomic instability {29665859}, suggesting that they might not be susceptible to PARP inhibitors.

Macroscopic appearance

Not clinically relevant

Histopathology

There is limited information on the histopathology of breast cancers occurring in carriers of ATM variants, and no distinctive tumour phenotype has been reproducibly identified {21787400}. One series revealed no significant morphological differences between ATM-mutated cancers and controls {17064299}. Others have reported that ATM-mutated cancers are usually luminal B {29665859} and hormone receptor–positive {29506079}, with double positivity for ER and PR. Notably, although ATM plays a similar role in DNA damage repair as BRCA1/BRCA2, no series has identified a predominance of the high-grade, lymphocyte-rich morphology typical of BRCA1/BRCA2 mutation–associated cancers among breast tumours with ATM abnormalities {17064299;29958926;29506079}. Instead, most reported cases are invasive carcinoma of no special type (NST; > 80%) {29665859} or with mixed ductal and lobular-like morphology associated with a tumour-infiltrating lymphocyte–poor stroma. Breast tumours in ATM heterozygote variant carriers are usually hormone receptor–positive, display minimal immune infiltrate, and are wildtype for TP53 {29506079}.

Cytology

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: demonstration of pathogenic homozygous or compound heterozygous germline variants of ATM.

Prognosis and prediction

AT has a poor prognosis. Many patients with AT succumb to progressive pulmonary disease or to cancer, with median age at death of 25 years {16790721}.
