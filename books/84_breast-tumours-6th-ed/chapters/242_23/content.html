2.4.2 Pleomorphic adenoma

Definition

Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is a benign tumour with variable cytomorphological and architectural manifestations of epithelial and myoepithelial cell components set within a chondromyxoid stroma

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PA of the breast usually arises in the retroareolar region {36482278}.

Clinical features

PA of the breast presents as a nodule, affecting mainly adult women {1660850;36482278}. Rare cases affect male patients {1328037;34514577}. On mammography and ultrasound examination, PA appears as a round to oval lesion with well-defined borders, simulating fibroadenoma. PA with intraductal growth can present as a cystic lesion {30240069;1660850}. Radiological diagnosis is sometimes complicated by the presence of microcalcifications, which can prompt suspicion for malignancy {25830053}.

Epidemiology

PA of the breast is very rare. Our knowledge of breast PA is mainly based on single case reports or small series {35100498;1660850}. Therefore, exact data on incidence are unknown.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

PA arising in the salivary glands shows rearrangements in PLAG1 and HMGA2 {18828159;9525740;9020842}. A study reported PLAG1 rearrangements in two of seven cases of PA of the breast (29%) including TRPS1::PLAG1 fusion in one case. No rearrangements of HMG2A were identified {34292619}. Another group identified HMGA2::WIF1 and CTNNB1::PLAG1 fusions in separate breast PAs {32550265}.

Macroscopic appearance

PA presents as a solid nodule, hard in consistency and with well-defined margins. The size is usually 10–20 mm. Longstanding nodules can reach larger sizes.

Histopathology

PA of the breast shows histological features similar to those seen in the salivary glands {1660850;34458945;36482278;25114471}. Breast PAs are lobulated tumours with circumscribed borders composed of a neoplastic proliferation of epithelial and myoepithelial cells, organized in glandular structures and strands immersed in a myxochondroid stroma. In addition, single stellate cells and strands of oval or spindled (typically myoepithelial-like) cells are usually present within the stromal matrix. The neoplastic glands are lined by an inner layer of epithelial cells and an outer layer of myoepithelial cells. Diversity of growth patterns is characteristic of PA. No cellular atypia, necrosis, or atypical mitotic figures are present. The stroma is usually myxochondroid, but cartilaginous or osseous metaplasia can be observed. PA can present as an intraductal growth or be associated with intraductal papilloma {1660850}. In some cases, PA shows a polypoid intraductal growth, resulting in a multinodular structure.

Immunohistochemical staining demonstrates the dual epithelial and myoepithelial cell components. Specifically, myoepithelial markers, including SMA, calponin, p63, CK5/6, CK14, S100, and SOX10, are usually positive in the cells composing the outer layer of the neoplastic glands and the neoplastic strands. In contrast, pure luminal cell markers, including low-molecular-weight cytokeratin and EMA, label the epithelial component.

The differential diagnosis includes tumours with mucinous and myxoid stroma (e.g. mucinous carcinoma and matrix-producing metaplastic carcinoma) and biphasic tumours such as adenomyoepithelioma. Mucinous carcinoma is easily excluded, because it is composed of neoplastic epithelial cells, lacks myoepithelium, and is immersed in a background of epithelial-type mucins. The differential diagnosis can be more difficult when matrix-producing carcinoma is considered {26096183}. PA lacks cellular atypia, atypical mitotic figures, and tumour necrosis. PA glands have a well-organized biphasic pattern, with a thin and continuous outer layer of myoepithelial cells and an inner layer of regular epithelial cells. In contrast, matrix-producing carcinoma is composed of atypical cells (with frequent atypical mitotic figures) organized in nests and aggregates, with no clearly recognizable architecture and no organized layer of myoepithelial cells around the neoplastic structures. Stromal invasion with desmoplastic response is often observed in matrix-producing metaplastic carcinoma but is not a feature of PA.

Immunohistochemistry may also aid the diagnosis: a high Ki-67 index, aberrant p53 expression, lack of ER expression, and weak or patchy expression of myoepithelial-specific markers favour matrix-producing carcinoma. Adenomyoepithelioma is typically composed of glands showing a double cell layer (epithelial and myoepithelial) and may display a chondromyxoid stroma. Unlike PA, adenomyoepithelioma shows a prominent and often multistratified myoepithelial cell layer. In addition, nuclear atypia and mitoses may be observed in adenomyoepithelioma, whereas these features are absent in PA.

Cytology

Smears are highly cellular and show cohesive tissue fragments of small basal epithelial cells, often prominent tissue fragments of fibrillary chondromyxoid or sometimes denser stroma, and myoepithelial cells which can be spindle or plasmacytoid {28994509}. At times, the epithelial cells and stromal matrix merge. There is no nuclear atypia, mitotic figures are scant, and there is no necrosis to suggest matrix-producing carcinoma of the breast {26823890}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-defined margins; glands lined by a double cell layer (epithelial and myoepithelial), with no atypia or mitoses, in a myxochondroid background.

Staging

Not clinically relevant

Prognosis and prediction

PA of the breast is benign {1660850}. Recurrences can occur in cases characterized by extensive polypoid intraductal growth that are not completely excised at the time of surgery {17593049}. Rare cases of malignant transformation in PA (carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma) of the breast have been reported, one of which was lethal {15583933;34292619}. Severe cytological atypia, increased mitotic activity, and invasion beyond the capsule suggest malignant transformation {34292619;26096183}. In view of the rare reports of malignant transformation, and the histological overlap with low- and intermediate-grade matrix-producing metaplastic carcinoma, some authors consider PA to be a tumour of uncertain malignant potential {26096183}.
