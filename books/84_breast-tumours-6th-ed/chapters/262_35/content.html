2.6.2 Atypical lobular hyperplasia

Definition

Atypical lobular hyperplasia (ALH) is a non-invasive low-grade neoplastic proliferation of small, discohesive cells, originating in the terminal duct lobular units (TDLUs), with or without pagetoid involvement of terminal ducts. Fewer than half of the acini in a TDLU are filled and expanded by the neoplastic cells.

Related terminology

Acceptable: lobular neoplasia.

Not recommended: lobular intraepithelial neoplasia.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ALH is often multicentric and bilateral {209887}.

Clinical features

ALH lacks clinical and mammographic signs but can sometimes be detected as MRI non–mass enhancement {38117388}. It is usually an incidental microscopic finding in breast biopsies performed for another lesion.

Epidemiology

The true incidence of ALH in the general population is unknown. ALH is found in women over a broad age range (mean age: ~50 years), in about 0.5–1.2% of otherwise benign breast core biopsies, and in 1.6–2.9% of reduction mammoplasty specimens {30238276;11161169;17154175;23656973;21827679;24112677;27538687;22997389;22847124}.

Etiology

Information about risk and predisposing factors of ALH is very scant. Women with high mammographic breast density are more likely to have ALH than women without dense breasts {25603785;29258587}. The presence of columnar cell lesions in a breast biopsy is associated with higher prevalence of ALH in adjacent breast tissue, suggesting that these entities may share similar risk factors {18816618;25639678}.

Pathogenesis

E-cadherin inactivation, which is a hallmark of lobular lesions, is an early event and is already present in ALH {23759937;15696125;15077190}. By mediating cell–cell adhesion and maintaining tissue integrity, E-cadherin acts as a tumour suppressor {28412244}. Impaired E-cadherin function results in the characteristic discohesive growth of ALH.

Molecular studies on ALH, although sparse, have provided evidence that ALH is a clonal neoplastic proliferation. An early study reported loss of heterozygosity at 11q13 in ALH {9042790}. Losses of chromosomal material from 7p, 16p, 16q, 17p, and 22q and gains of material at 2p and 6q have been identified in ALH by chromosomal comparative genomic hybridization and array comparative genomic hybridization {9788628;16897748}. Many of these changes are shared by associated lobular carcinoma in situ (LCIS) and/or invasive lobular carcinoma, supporting ALH as an early lesion in the lobular carcinoma pathway and supporting a non-obligate precursor role for ALH {18223478;20500230}.

A study using whole-exome sequencing identified CDH1 mutations in 81% of cases of LCIS {30185420}. It is likely that ALH has a genomic landscape similar to that of LCIS, including frequent CDH1 mutations.

Macroscopic appearance

ALH is not evident macroscopically.

Histopathology

ALH demonstrates a solid proliferation of discohesive monomorphic epithelial cells filling and expanding < 50% of the acini in a TDLU, with or without pagetoid involvement of terminal ducts {1748429}. For practical diagnostic purposes there are two possible approaches: (1) if the involved acini are larger than the uninvolved acini, they are considered expanded [[Tavassoli FA, Eusebi V. Tumors of the mammary gland. Washington, DC: American Registry of Pathology; 2009. (AFIP atlas of tumor pathology, series 4; fascicle 10). ]] { (2) if the involved acini have ≥ 8 cells across the diameter, they are considered expanded [[Simpson J, Page D. Lobular neoplasia. In: Elston CW, Ellis IO, editors. The breast. London (UK): Churchill Livingstone; 1998. (Systematic pathology series, 3rd ed.; vol. 13). ]] {12927036}. However, scientific evidence for these size criteria to define distension is lacking. ALH can also be observed in other lesions such as fibroadenomas, papillomas, sclerosing adenosis and other sclerosing lesions such as radial scar, and collagenous spherulosis. Some cases of ALH may involve ducts in a pagetoid pattern in the absence of involvement of lobular acini {1748429;3343034}. ALH may have two cell morphologies, either singly or mixed: type A cells have scant cytoplasm and uniform, round, small to slightly enlarged nuclei (1–1.5 times the size of a lymphocyte nucleus) and inconspicuous nucleoli; type B cells have more-abundant cytoplasm and larger nuclei (2 times the size of a lymphocyte nucleus), with more variability in size and shape and more-prominent nucleoli {209887;12379750}. The distinction between type A and type B cells is of no known clinical significance. Intracytoplasmic lumina, which may be large enough to impart a signet ring–cell appearance, are often present but are not specific to ALH.

Loss of membranous E-cadherin expression is the defining immunohistochemical feature of lobular lesions, including ALH {23759937;15696125}. β-Catenin, α-catenin, and p120 catenin (proteins known to complex with E-cadherin) also demonstrate loss of membranous expression; p120 catenin is aberrantly located in the cytoplasm and is also upregulated {26768029;17325485;22080244;15077190}. However, approximately 15% of lobular lesions retain E-cadherin expression but with an aberrant staining pattern, which should not be interpreted to exclude a lobular phenotype {26768029;18379416;20871222}. E-cadherin antibodies may show cross-reactivity with P-cadherin, reduced specificity, or aberrant nuclear staining depending on the targeted epitope {39025406;38641322;31231125;18580099}. Therefore, these immunohistochemical markers should be interpreted in conjunction with detailed histological analysis.

Cytology

ALH produces low cellularity with small cells with mildly atypical nuclei and eccentric cytoplasm often containing a single vacuole, scattered as single cells and loosely cohesive small sheets, but there are no reliable cytopathological criteria for distinguishing among ALH, LCIS, and invasive lobular carcinoma {2750704;8143551;12112810}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: small discohesive cells with uniform hyperchromatic nuclei (type A) to slightly larger vesicular nuclei with mild variability (type B), filling and expanding fewer than half of the acini in a TDLU.

Desirable: loss of membranous E-cadherin staining, if needed.

Staging

ALH is considered a high-risk non-malignant lesion and is not staged.

Prognosis and prediction

ALH is associated with an elevated risk for subsequent cancers of different histological types in both breasts (albeit with an ipsilateral predominance) {17154175;23117858;24480577;9149887;2986821;12531579;17319851}. The relative risk for breast cancer among patients with ALH is four to five times that expected in women without ALH. The risk associated with ductal involvement by ALH alone is lower than the risk associated with ALH involving both the lobules and the ducts {3343034}. The absolute risk of breast cancer associated with ALH is estimated to be about 1% per year and is fairly constant over time, with a cumulative incidence of 30% at 25 years {25551530}.

Younger patient age at diagnosis of ALH, histological calcifications, and less age-related lobular involution have been found to further increase the risk of breast cancer, whereas chemoprevention lowers the risk {23117858;17563394;10547399;25551530;9149887}. Some (but not all) studies have demonstrated that the risk is increased with a positive family history and multiple foci of ALH {26565738;27352219;17563394;25551530;2986821}. Models used to improve risk stratification of patients with high-risk breast lesions and to personalize breast cancer surveillance may overestimate invasive breast cancer risk in patients with atypical hyperplasia including ALH {20606088}.

The management of ALH diagnosed on core needle biopsy is controversial, owing to the wide range of reported upgrade rates (0–67%) to carcinoma (including invasive carcinoma and ductal carcinoma in situ) on excision {26350664;15241819;27806840;18517282;12604888;15767810;15105449;24370150;18660792;22268170;17460455;26564004;18619840;16985141;12591964;27538687;22997389;20637429;21128240;21861212;16891208;22847124;12033958;18306873;20431477;22706860;32861602}. However, recent studies report low (0–4%) upgrade rates at excision among incidental cases of ALH alone or lobular neoplasia (combining ALH and classic LCIS cases) when concordant radiological–pathological correlation to a different target lesion has been identified {23901123;18660792;23132235;18348299;26542585;21861212;22847124;20431477;27425222;22706860}. Therefore, patients with incidental ALH on core biopsy showing an additional benign lesion with proven radiological–pathological concordance may be safely observed. Excision should be performed if there is another lesion that by itself would warrant excision or if there is no histological correlate to the target of the biopsy. Recent European guidelines advocate the same approach {38061151}.

For the purposes of risk assessment and clinical management, ALH is considered a marker of increased risk. Best practice for the care for women with ALH emphasizes close surveillance and counselling for risk reduction.
