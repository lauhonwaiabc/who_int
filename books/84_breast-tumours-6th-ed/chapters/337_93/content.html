3.3.7 Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma

Definition

Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma (MCA) is an invasive breast carcinoma characterized by cystic structures lined by tall columnar cells with abundant intracytoplasmic mucin, resembling pancreatobiliary, ovarian, or gastrointestinal MCA.

ICD-O coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

ICD-11 coding

Classification in progress, Data not available in this Beta

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Not applicable

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a palpable mass of 8–190 mm, with a median tumour size of 30 mm {9630176;29081926;29745281}. Preoperative radiological findings can be variable and sometimes misleading {39757664}.

Epidemiology

Fewer than 40 cases of this extremely rare subtype have been reported in the English-language literature. Most of the reported cases affected Asian women, typically postmenopausal, with a median age of 61 years (range: 41–96 years) {9630176;29081926;29745281;33165376}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Studies investigating the genetic characteristics of breast MCA remain limited. PIK3CA mutation was demonstrated in a study of 3 cases with absence of hotspot mutations in KRAS, BRAF, and NRAS {37983855}. Pathogenic mutations in PIK3CA, KRAS, MAP2K4, RB1, KDR, PKHD1, TERT, and TP53 were demonstrated in 1 case {36737820}. Mutations in TP53, RB1, and BAP1 were identified in another case report {34180564}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, the tumour presents as a well-circumscribed mass with both solid and cystic components. The cystic spaces are typically filled with gelatinous material {9630176}.

Histopathology

MCA is characterized by cystic spaces lined by tall columnar cells with stratification, tufting, and papillary formations {9630176}. The neoplastic cells have basally located nuclei and contain abundant intracytoplasmic mucin {9630176}. Mucin is also present within the cystic spaces. The degree of cytological atypia is variable, even within the same tumour. The cystic structures have a rounded contour but lack myoepithelial cells at the periphery {9630176}. Ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) may be present in the adjacent mammary ducts and lobules.

MCA should be differentiated from conventional mucinous carcinoma (see section Mucinous carcinoma) and encapsulated papillary carcinoma (see section Encapsulated papillary carcinoma), both of which are typically strongly and diffusely positive for ER and PR. Encapsulated papillary carcinoma also lacks intracytoplasmic mucin. In the absence of DCIS, the possibility of metastatic MCA from another site must be excluded.

Most MCAs are negative for ER, PR, and ERBB2 (HER2) {9630176;29745281}. Rare HER2-positive cases have been reported {20338619;25119013;27721884;35224708;38837688}. Even rarer cases with ER positivity have been reported {20737039;33042347}. Some tumours express CK5/6 and EGFR {29081926;22489551;22612513}.

Breast MCA is CK7-positive but negative for CK20 and CDX2; these findings may help to distinguish it in particular from metastatic pancreatobiliary or gastrointestinal MCA, which may also be CK7-positive but usually expresses CK20 and CDX2. TRPS1 may have additional utility in this context, as it was positive in breast MCAs from a limited case series, whereas ovarian and pancreatic MCAs were negative {37983855}.

Cytology

There are few descriptions of the cytopathological findings of this rare carcinoma subtype, which report abundant extracellular mucin the same as that seen in mucinous carcinoma, but with necrotic debris, and with small tissue fragments and single columnar cells with marked nuclear atypia, coarse chromatin, and prominent nucleoli {23323116;21472867}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: invasive breast carcinoma with large cystic spaces containing mucin and lined by atypical columnar cells with intracytoplasmic mucin.

Desirable: ER-negative and PR-negative (typically); adjacent DCIS; CK7-positive, CK20-negative, and CDX2-negative

Staging

Staging is the same as for other types of invasive breast carcinoma.

Prognosis and prediction

Axillary lymph node involvement is relatively uncommon, with only 5 reported patients having lymph node metastasis at presentation {29745281;39757664}. Most reported cases had a relatively good prognosis. One case of MCA arising in the background of extensive high-grade DCIS recurred locally 8 years after lumpectomy {29745281}. Rare cases of distant metastases and related deaths have been documented; however, follow-up data remain limited {33165376;38128508}.
