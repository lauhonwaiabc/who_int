1.8.7 MYOD1-mutant spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

MYOD1-mutant spindle cell / sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma is an aggressive subtype of rhabdomyosarcoma (RMS), characterized by fascicular spindle cell and/or sclerosing morphology and MYOD1 p.(Leu122Arg) mutation.

ICD-O coding

8912/3 Spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B55.Z & XH7NM2 Rhabdomyosarcoma, primary site and Spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The head and neck and extremities constitute the most common sites of involvement, followed by the trunk and abdomen (paraspinal region, chest wall, pelvis), the viscera (liver), and the mediastinum {24272621; 24824843; 27562493; 30181563; 30604891}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a rapidly growing, painless soft tissue mass with symptoms related to local compression at the tumour site. The typical radiological finding is of a well-defined mass lesion that is heterogeneous with increased vascularity {35929754}.

Epidemiology

MYOD1-mutant spindle cell / sclerosing RMSs are rare, with fewer than 100 cases reported in the literature. They occur across a broad age range (2–94 years), with a female predominance {36980529}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Spindle cell / sclerosing RMS with MYOD1 variants demonstrates a recurrent p.(Leu122Arg) gene mutation. The myogenic differentiation gene (MYOD1) is a member of the “basic-helix-loop-helix” family of proteins, which regulate cell growth through their DNA-binding sites and transcriptional control. The p.(Leu122Arg) mutation in the DNA-binding domain of MYOD1 blocks wildtype MYOD1 function and imparts a new MYC-like binding capability to this protein, thus driving cell proliferation and blocking differentiation {1329087; 24793135; 24272621; 36980529}. These tumours show frequent association with PIK3CA variants, and may rarely harbour RAS variants, including NRAS, HRAS, and NF1 {24824843; 30181563; 26501226; 34166060}. MYOD1 alterations other than p.(Leu122Arg) are reported in RMS, but these other alterations are of uncertain significance {34782706}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are invariably circumscribed and show grey-white to tan cut surfaces. There may be areas of necrosis and cystic degeneration.

Histopathology

Spindle cell RMSs are hypercellular tumours composed of long intersecting fascicles of spindle cells and herringbone-like (fibrosarcomatous) growth pattern. Individual tumour cells contain pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and oval to fusiform or blunted hyperchromatic nuclei. Mitotic figures are often present. Sclerosing RMSs are composed of round to oval to short spindle-shaped tumour cells arranged in cords, nests, trabeculae, or microalveoli. They often show a striking pseudoangiomatous growth pattern with a prominent hyaline to pseudochondroid stroma, mimicking an osteosarcoma or an extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma. There can be a variable amount or an admixture of spindle and sclerosing components, leading to hybrid forms {24824843; 27562493; 30181563; 30604891}. Rarely, primitive undifferentiated areas comprising sheets of round cells with scant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic nuclei predominate. Lipoblast-like cells and multinucleate giant cells are rarely reported {30181563; 30604891}. Rhabdomyoblastic differentiation is infrequent but can be seen in postchemotherapy tumours {30181563}.

Immunohistochemically, the tumour cells are diffusely positive for MYOD1. Desmin (diffuse to focal) and myogenin (mostly focal) may also be positive {27562493; 30098515; 30181563; 30604891}.

Cytology

Aspirated material of sclerosing RMS shows low cellularity with tissue fragments, metachromatic stroma (Giemsa), and round to polygonal cells with round to spindle nuclei and scant to moderate blue (Giemsa) to finely vacuolated cytoplasm. Spindle cell RMS displays relatively hypercellular smears containing tissue fragments of oval to spindle cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and poorly defined cytoplasm {35929754}. Ancillary tests are required for the diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of a MYOD1 p.(Leu122Arg) variant is required for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cells arranged in fascicles and/or oval to spindle cells with a variable amount of sclerotic stroma displaying positive immunohistochemical staining for MYOD1, typically diffuse and intense; demonstration of MYOD1 p.(Leu122Arg) mutation.

Desirable: variable staining for desmin and myogenin.

Staging

RMS is staged on the basis of the site of origin using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) system for soft tissue sarcomas.

Prognosis and prediction

MYOD1-mutant spindle cell / sclerosing RMSs are associated with an aggressive clinical course, with recurrences, metastasis, and fatal outcomes despite multimodal treatment, both in children and adults {24824843; 26501226; 27261172; 27562493; 30181563; 34086347}. The 5-year event-free survival in MYOD1-mutant tumours is < 10% {34166060}.
