2.1.2 Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation

Definition

Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation (BPOP) is a benign neoplasm on the surface of bone, composed of an admixture of spindle cells, cartilage, and bone.

ICD-O coding

92120/0 Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation

ICD-11 coding

2E82.Z & XH23J5 Benign chondrogenic tumours, site unspecified & Bizarre parosteal osteochondromatous proliferation

Related terminology

Not recommended: Nora lesion.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

BPOP typically affects small bones in the hands and feet, most commonly phalanges of the hand. Non-acral long tubular bones are also affected in roughly 20% of cases {37733437}. BPOP has been rarely reported on the craniofacial bones {39951207}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

BPOP presents as a mass, with or without pain, which is present for a few months to several years {37733437}. Some examples grow rapidly.

Imaging

Mature BPOP presents on radiographs and CT as a well-defined ossified mass arising from the periosteum {16602017; 37336832}. Extension into cortex or even bone marrow is rare. The typical continuity of bone marrow and cortex in the stalk of osteochondroma is absent. If imaging is done in the early stages of the disease, a soft tissue mass with no or little calcification is seen at the same location {16602017; 37336832}. These lesions have MRI characteristics of aggressive inflammation with poorly defined high signal on fluid-sensitive sequences. They mature within a few months to the mature classic type.

Epidemiology

BPOP is rare and no well-defined prevalence or incidence rates have been published. Case reports of BPOP show a wide age range (3 months to 87 years), with a peak in the second and third decades of life. There is no sex predilection.

Etiology

The etiology of BPOP is unknown, although a minority of the patients report preceding trauma {37733437}.

Pathogenesis

Recurrent cytogenetic abnormalities of BPOP include t(1;17)(q32;q21), inv(7), and inv(6) {15343507; 24412018}. The former two cytogenetic changes correspond to the fusions between a gene coding for type I collagen, COL1A1 (17q21.33) or COL1A2 (7q21.3), and a non-coding RNA gene, MIR29B2CHG (1q32.2) or LINC-PINT (7q32.3), respectively, identified in some cases {36853784}. Truncating mutations involving COL1A1 have also been reported in a case without the above fusions {36853784}. These fusions and mutations are predicted to disrupt the involved type I collagen. These findings support the notion that BPOP is a neoplasm rather than a reactive process and that it is different from subungual exostosis, which is associated with distinct translocations {20340132}.

Macroscopic appearance

BPOP is an exophytic bony lesion with a cartilaginous surface. Most tumours measure 10–30 mm.

Histopathology

BPOP is composed of a variable mixture of cartilage, bone, and fibrous tissue. The outermost part of the lesion can be covered by cellular spindle cell proliferation overlying cartilage. The cartilage has an increased number of chondrocytes with enlarged nuclei and binucleation, and it therefore often looks atypical (bizarre). Marked hyperchromasia or atypical mitoses are not seen. The cartilage transitions to trabeculae of bone via enchondral ossification, with an irregular interface. Between the cartilage and bone, characteristic stroma can be seen, with a basophilic tinctorial quality (so-called blue bone), even after decalcification {8317609; 29657458}. The intertrabecular space is filled with hypervascular tissue without a marrow component and is populated by a non-atypical spindle cell proliferation. Corticomedullary continuity has been observed in a small subset of cases and appears to be associated with the inv(7) karyotype {36853784}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes subungual exostosis, which exclusively affects the distal phalangeal bone and lacks blue bone. Osteochondroma shows medullary continuity, cortical flaring, and fatty intertrabecular spaces, generally without cartilaginous atypia. Parosteal osteosarcoma is large and harbours intertrabecular fascicles of mildly atypical spindle cells, which are often positive for MDM2 amplification. Periosteal osteosarcoma shows characteristic radiological findings, with prominent periosteal reaction and more atypical tumour cartilage and bone. Fibro-osseous pseudotumour of digits, which may harbour a USP6 rearrangement {29787930}, shows a fasciitis-like background and disorganized bone {18164411}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing is usually unnecessary; however, detecting COL1A1/COL1A2 alterations may be helpful in challenging cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an exophytic bony growth with typically intact cortex; a disorganized cellular lesion comprising spindle cells, atypical chondrocytes, and bone; the presence of blue bone is characteristic.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

BPOP is treated with simple excision. Recurrences occur in as many as half of the cases, sometimes multiple times, but they are non-destructive and managed by re-excision. BPOP has no capacity to metastasize.
