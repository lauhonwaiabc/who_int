1.3.8 Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis

Definition

Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis is a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by painful and disfiguring deposits of hyalinized fibrous material in the dermis, subcutaneous soft tissues, gingiva, and viscera.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: hyaline fibromatosis syndrome; infantile systemic hyalinosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis is a multisystem disease that involves the skin, subcutaneous tissue, gingiva, bone, and viscera.

Clinical features

Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis typically presents in infancy but can also manifest later in childhood, with more severe cases appearing earlier {30176098; 22383261}. It encompasses a clinical spectrum ranging from juvenile hyaline fibromatosis to infantile systemic hyalinosis {20331448; 31509170; 22771664}. Common clinical manifestations include slow-growing papular and nodular skin lesions, gingival hypertrophy interfering with dentition {34976780} [[Malakuti I, Persson A, Liminga G, et al. Oral and maxillofacial manifestations in young patients with hyalin fibromatosis syndrome: A case series. Oral Maxillofac Surg Cases. 2024;10:100410. doi:10.1016/j.omsc.2024.100410.]], and joint contractures (occurring in 85–95% of cases). Other features include osteopenia, osteolytic lesions, and cutaneous manifestations. Severe systemic involvement, defined by visceral symptoms such as intractable diarrhoea (seen in 50% of cases) and recurrent infections (seen in 30% of cases), leads to failure to thrive and early mortality {30176098; 22383261; 19344977}. Infantile systemic hyalinosis is frequently associated with extreme pain upon minimal handling. A rare case with a giant subcutaneous scalp tumour reaching 20 cm has been reported {39960441}.

A grading system for juvenile hyaline fibromatosis severity has been proposed: grade 1: mild (skin and/or gingiva); grade 2: moderate (joint and/or bone involvement); grade 3: severe (internal organ involvement); grade 4: life-limiting disease.

Epidemiology

Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis is extremely rare with an unknown incidence. There is no sex predilection, and the disorder follows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis has been reported in young siblings {30971561}.

Etiology

Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis (MIM number: 228600) {38814306} is caused by loss-of-function mutations, including inactivating homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the ANTXR2 gene (CMG2) {29801470; 30176098; 17284973}. Recently, a case was reported with no mutations in the ANTXR2 gene by whole-exome sequencing, which revealed homozygous mutations in the SLC39A8 and PCNT genes. No clear association has been reported among these three genes {39960441}.

Pathogenesis

ANTXR2 encodes a transmembrane protein that binds extracellular matrix proteins laminin and collagen VI and also serves as a receptor for anthrax toxin {22215446}. In normal physiology, ANTXR2 contributes to basement membrane matrix assembly, particularly regulating collagen VI homeostasis, and endothelial cell morphogenesis {17284973; 22215446; 28604699}. In juvenile hyaline fibromatosis, loss of ANTXR2 function leads to accumulation of collagen VI within the extracellular matrix. Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis and infantile systemic hyalinosis may show distinct variants, which suggests a genotype–phenotype correlation {32029086}.

Macroscopic appearance

Juvenile hyaline fibromatosis appears as solid and homogeneous subcutaneous nodules.

Histopathology

Lesions are characterized by abundant extracellular eosinophilic, non-fibrillar hyaline-like material that obliterates normal tissue structures. The cellular component comprises plump to spindled, uniform fibroblasts, often arranged in cords mimicking vascular spaces {22383261; 15078358}. The extracellular matrix deposits are strongly PAS-positive and diastase-resistant {22383261; 15078358}.

Cytology

Rare case reports have not characterized distinctive cytology findings.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The identification of a pathogenic germline variant mutation in ANTXR2 (4q21.21) confirms the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: subcutaneous nodules and gingival hypertrophy with abundant hyaline-like material.

Desirable: confirmation by germline sequencing of ANTXR2.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Earlier onset is associated with more severe disease, systemic involvement, and poor prognosis, with a median age at death of 15 months in severe cases {30176098; 19344977; 22383261}. In milder forms, progression to wheelchair dependency may occur because of severe joint contractures. Missense mutations in exons 1–12 of ANTXR2 and mutations leading to a premature stop codon are associated with more severe disease, while missense mutations in exons 13–17 typically result in milder forms {30176098; 29801470; 22215446; 20331448}. Treatment is largely supportive, including surgical excision of subcutaneous nodules and gingivectomy for gingival hypertrophy; however, recurrences are common {30176098; 22383261}.
