1.3.22 Gardner fibroma

Definition

Gardner fibroma is a benign plaque-like neoplasm composed of coarse, haphazardly arranged collagen bundles with sparsely interspersed fibroblasts, often associated with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP).

ICD-O coding

8810/0 Gardner fibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH7GT0 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Gardner fibroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: Gardner-associated fibroma.

Not recommended: desmoid precursor lesion.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Gardner fibromas commonly arise in the superficial and deep soft tissues of the torso (usually the back or paraspinal region) and less commonly in the head/neck region, but almost any non-visceral anatomical site can be involved, including the mesentery {11342777; 17325483}.

Clinical features

These tumours typically present as painless mass-forming lesions {11342777; 17325483}.

Epidemiology

Gardner fibroma is rare and diagnosed most often in children aged < 10 years but may present in adults as well {38757718}. There is no sex predilection {11342777; 17325483}.

Etiology

Between 43% and 69% of cases are associated with FAP or APC variants {11342777; 17325483; 26840078}.

Pathogenesis

Most cases arise in patients with germline APC variants (see section Desmoid fibromatosis), although sporadic tumours occur. CTNNB1 variants have also rarely been reported {38757718; 33104413}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gardner fibroma is a poorly circumscribed, rubbery, tan-white, sometimes plaque-like mass ranging in size from < 10 mm to > 100 mm {11342777; 17325483}.

Histopathology

The dominant histopathological feature is the haphazard arrangement of coarse collagen fibres with characteristic clefts or cracks and few interspersed spindle cells, without atypia, necrosis, fascicle formation, or areas of hypercellularity {11342777; 17325483}. A helpful diagnostic feature is entrapment of adjacent adipose tissue and neurovascular bundles at the edge of the lesion. Rare tumours may show abundant intralesional adipose tissue {33104413}. By immunohistochemistry, CD34 is almost always positive, whereas SMA is negative {11342777; 17325483}. Nuclear accumulation of β-catenin is often observed, particularly in cases associated with FAP {17325483; 26840078}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Although not necessary for diagnosis, identification of germline APC variants can have important implications for surgical and long-term case management {26840078}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: plaque-like mass in the paraspinal region or trunk of young children; haphazard arrangement of coarse collagen fibres with clefts or cracks and few inconspicuous spindle cells.

Desirable: expression of CD34 and nuclear β-catenin.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Gardner fibroma is benign but is associated with concurrent or subsequent development of desmoid fibromatosis in approximately 20% of cases, and this association has been seen in the context of germline APC variants and CTNNB1 variants {17325483; 9692575; 33104413}. Conversely, an associated Gardner fibroma or an area in the desmoid tumour resembling Gardner fibroma can be identified in as many as 37% of patients with desmoid fibromatosis {25064609; 27124915}. Given the close association between Gardner fibroma and FAP, a diagnosis of Gardner fibroma should prompt screening of the family for FAP.
