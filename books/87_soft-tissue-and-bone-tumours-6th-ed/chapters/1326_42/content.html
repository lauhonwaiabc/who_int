1.3.26 Giant cell fibroblastoma

Definition

Giant cell fibroblastoma (GCF) is a locally aggressive fibroblastic neoplasm, closely related to dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP), arising primarily in children and characterized by the presence of multinucleated giant cells and pseudovascular spaces.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GCF is preferentially located in superficial soft tissues of the trunk and (more rarely) the extremities, and the head and neck region {12502925; 17349565; 2804904; 3565673}. GCF rarely involves superficial skeletal muscles {17349565}.

Clinical features

GCF is typically present as painless, single, red-blue, slowly growing, skin plaque or nodule. The lesion rarely extends beyond 50 mm {12502925; 3565673; 17349565; 2804904}.

Epidemiology

GCF is a rare neoplasm arising predominantly but not exclusively in children, with a mean age of 6 years and a male predominance {3565673; 17349565; 2804904}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

GCF and DFSP (related entities) are characterized by a rearrangement involving chromosomes 17 and 22 {8978765; 7757993; 8824728; 8988177}. In GCF (and some paediatric DFSPs), this rearrangement is related to a translocation, frequently balanced, although it is preferentially related to the formation of a ring chromosome in DFSP {16843106; 9283602; 7697628; 18069662}. This rearrangement generates a chimeric gene fusing COL1A1 (17q21.33) with PDGFB (22q13.1). The breakpoint in COL1A1 is variable (from exon 6 up to exon 9), whereas the breakpoint in PDGFB is consistently located in intron 1 {12661001; 9739023; 19890351}. In the chimeric transcript, all but exon 1 of PDGFB are placed under the control of the COL1A1 promoter, retaining the tyrosine kinase function and removing all elements negatively controlling PDGFB transcription and translation. This molecular anomaly is responsible for the activation of the PDGFB pathway driving the tumorigenesis {12550751; 12661001}. Recently, a new fusion gene involving COL3A1 has been reported {36776313}.

Macroscopic appearance

GCF is a dermo-subcutaneous, poorly defined, infiltrative, and firm lesion composed of grey-to-white fibrous tissue. The size of the lesions ranges from 6 mm to 80 mm {12502925; 17349565; 3565673}.

Histopathology

GCF is a dermal and subcutaneous tumour, rarely involving skeletal muscle. Classic GCF infiltrates the subcutaneous fat with a honeycomb pattern and spare cutaneous adnexal structures. This neoplasm is usually hypocellular, composed of loose fascicles of wavy spindle cells in a myxoid to collagenous stroma. Numerous pleomorphic mononucleated and multinucleated giant cells are also present. Tumour cells display an amphophilic cytoplasm and oval hyperchromatic nuclei. Mitoses are rare, and areas of necrosis are usually absent. Spindle and pleomorphic cells rim pseudovascular sinusoidal spaces. GCF can display myoid whorls {12502925; 17349565; 2804904; 3565673}.

Hybrid GCF-DFSP combines areas of pure GCF with areas of pure DFSP, and in areas where both morphological aspects are mixed, the transition from one tumour type to the other can be gradual or abrupt. Hybrid cases may show fibrosarcomatous transformation {12502925; 17349565}.

By immunohistochemistry, spindle cells and pleomorphic cells express CD34 {12502925; 17349565}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The identification of t(17;22) is useful in non-classic cases {26970672; 25081750; 23774173}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle cells and scattered multinucleated cells, often lining pseudovascular spaces; infiltrative margins within dermis and subcutaneous fat; hypocellular lesion with myxoid or collagenous stroma.

Desirable: COL1A1::PDGFB fusion in select cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

A local recurrence rate of 47% has been reported {2804904}. Pure GCF cases can recur as hybrid tumour with DFSP areas {17349565}. If widely excised, GCF has a very low risk of local recurrence {12502925}.
