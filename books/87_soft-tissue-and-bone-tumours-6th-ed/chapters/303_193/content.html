3.0.3 Langerhans cell histiocytosis

Definition

Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) is a neoplasm composed of a clonal expansion of myeloid dendritic cells that have a Langerhans cell phenotype and lack high-grade cytological features.

ICD-O coding

9751/1 Langerhans cell histiocytosis NOS

9751/3 Langerhans cell histiocytosis, disseminated

ICD-11 coding

2B31.2 & XH1J18 Langerhans cell histiocytosis & Langerhans cell histiocytosis NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: Langerhans cell granulomatosis; histiocytosis X; eosinophilic granuloma; Hashimoto-Pritzker; Hand–Schüller–Christian disease; Letterer–Siwe disease.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LCH can present at any site of the bone and soft tissue. Single-system LCH predominantly involves bone (skull, long bones, vertebrae, pelvis, ribs, and mandible) or skin. Unifocal lesions less commonly have isolated lymph node, skin, and lung involvement. In multisystem LCH, the skin, bone, liver, spleen, and bone marrow are preferentially involved, with the latter three considered as high-risk organ involvement. CNS and pituitary involvement is typically associated with other systemic disease presentation.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Patients with single-system LCH are usually older children or adults with a painful lytic lesion eroding the bone cortex. Solitary lesions at other sites present as masses. Patients with multifocal bone lesions may have associated soft tissue involvement. Arginine vasopressin deficiency (formerly diabetes insipidus) may follow cranial bone or CNS-parenchymal involvement and is considered as a multisystem LCH. Patients with multisystem LCH are typically young infants with fever, cytopenias, skin and bone lesions, and hepatosplenomegaly {14556926; 30157397}. Cytopenias suggest marrow involvement. Hyperbilirubinaemia suggests liver involvement {38687639}.

Imaging

The imaging characteristics vary on the basis of age and lesion location. Early lesions, particularly in the long bones, show aggressive features {21190020; 32451558; 26802000}, while marginal sclerosis suggests spontaneous healing and possibly a better prognosis {5903836}. Aggressive and indolent features may coexist. In long bones, LCH appears as poorly defined lytic foci with periosteal remodelling {32451558; 21190020}. An extraosseous mass, if present, is relatively small and eccentric {32451558}; adjacent oedema-like MRI signal is extensive relative to the size of the lesion {26802000}; both features help distinguish LCH from Ewing sarcoma. Spine involvement includes vertebra plana (severe body flattening) {23602327}. Calvarial lesions are the commonest in the parietal bone {18647937}, usually causing a well-defined lytic (punched out) lesion without a sclerotic margin {5927615}. The characteristic bevelled edge is due to unequal destruction of the inner and outer tables {1636041}. Skeletal staging can be performed with low-dose biplanar radiography and isotope bone scan. However, whole-body MRI shows skeletal tumour extent accurately {33215231} and identifies more skeletal lesions than radiographs or bone scan {16896695}. PET-avid on CT is useful for both initial staging and response {31599488; 33442960; 38687639}. However, MRI is a preferred modality for CNS disease, including assessment of the pituitary bright spot, which is lost in arginine vasopressin deficiency {38687639}.

Epidemiology

Most published population-based reports reported epidemiological estimates for children in European countries, reporting annual incidence in children of about 5 cases per 1 million population {18260117; 25586293}. A recent national registry study from the United Kingdom provided a more detailed analysis and reported a prevalence of LCH of 9.95 cases (95% CI: 9.14–10.81) per 1 million population at the end of 2019. The age-standardized incidence rate is 4.46 cases per 1 million children and 1.06 cases per 1 million adults aged ≥ 15 {28653448; 35271698; 36122574}. The M:F ratio is 1.2:1 {30157397}. The annual incidence in adults is 1–2 cases per 1 million population. The disease has a more common incidence in people of European descent and Hispanic populations. LCH can also be associated with Erdheim–Chester disease, either concomitantly or preceding, with shared molecular alterations {26966089}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The cell of origin is a haematopoietic bone marrow–derived precursor of the mononuclear phagocyte system (myeloid-derived) {20220088; 28512190; 28566492; 32750121}. MAPK pathway activation is thought to play a central role in LCH pathogenesis {30157397}. A misguided myeloid differentiation model has been proposed, in which activating MAPK mutations at a pluripotent haematopoietic, tissue-restricted, or local precursor level give rise to high-risk multisystem, multifocal low-risk, or unifocal LCH, respectively {30157397}. Others have proposed that a common marrow-resident myeloid progenitor can give rise to both low- and high-risk LCH {32750121}. MAPK mutations, especially BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E), may confer an advantage for tissue site accumulation via disrupted cell migration and apoptosis inhibition {29263218}. Rare familial cases are reported {11452942}. About 30% of sporadic cases show clonal IGH, IGK, and/or TR gene rearrangements, suggesting a shared lineage precursor or derivation from a common lymphoid/myeloid precursor even in the absence of a prior haematopoietic malignancy {20551822; 27823979}.

Macroscopic appearance

Not clinically relevant

Histopathology

Neoplastic LCH cells are oval, characterized by typical grooved nuclei with irregular membranes. Nuclear atypia is minimal. The cytoplasm is moderately abundant, slightly eosinophilic, and lacks dendritic processes. The inflammatory milieu includes eosinophils, macrophages (including multinucleated and phagocytic forms), neutrophils, and lymphocytes. Necrosis and mitotic activity are not prognostic. LCH cells predominate in early lesions. In BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) LCH, the bone marrow, CNS, liver, or relapsed lesions may show a decreased or absent population of LCH cells. However, a predominant macrophage population in a patient with known LCH should prompt molecular typing, as a lineage shift towards a monocyte/macrophage phenotype can occur {28194436; 29624648; 39794638; 40167581}. Identification of the ultrastructural Birbeck granule (i.e. tennis-racket–shaped organelle with a zipper-like appearance) has been largely replaced by CD207 (Langerin) immunohistochemistry.

Immunohistochemistry

LCH cells express CD1a, CD207 (langerin), S100, and CD68 (Golgi-dot like) {30157397}. CD45 expression is low. The BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) mutation can be identified by a variant-specific VE1 antibody, but PCR-based molecular testing should confirm mutation for treatment implications {30923995; 28219109}. Cyclin D1 and phosphorylated ERK nuclear expression serves as a surrogate for upregulation of the MAPK pathway but is not indicative of a specific mutation. The Ki-67 proliferation index is highly variable (typically < 10% by double staining), but increased proliferation is not prognostic in the absence of nuclear atypia {12121233; 29263218}.

Differential diagnosis

In bone, the histological differential diagnosis includes osteomyelitis and chondroblastoma. The radiographic differential diagnosis includes high-grade malignancy (e.g. osteosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and bone lymphoma) and osteomyelitis.

Cytology

Clusters of LCH cells are diagnostic with positive immunophenotype and correlating radiographic findings, but caution is advised in lymph nodes because paracortical reactive dendritic/Langerhans cells cannot be distinguished from clonal sinus-based LCH cells without molecular testing.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

MAPK pathway gene variants are recorded in > 85% of cases. They affect BRAF or alternatively MAP2K1 or much more rarely ARAF. ERBB3, NRAS, and KRAS variants can also occur in adults {30157397}. Fusions (i.e. BRAF fusion) are less frequent but should be considered in the absence of gene variants {31768065}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large, round to oval histiocytes with grooved to convoluted nuclei expressing Langerhans cell phenotype (CD1a+, CD207+) in the correct histological/anatomical context.

Staging

LCH staging is based on clinical, radiographic, and histological sampling. Single-system LCH is defined by the involvement of one organ/system (unifocal or multifocal), and multisystem LCH is defined by the involvement of two or more organs/systems. Risk-organ involvement (i.e. liver, spleen, or bone marrow involvement) was originally defined as having increased risk of death {38687639}. Emerging data suggest a new diagnostic staging based on the risk of treatment failure, rather than the risk of death, which includes assessment of blood and/or bone marrow testing for low-level BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) mutation at diagnosis {40167581}.

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical course is related to staging at presentation, with a ≥ 99% survival rate for unifocal disease and < 20% mortality in risk-organ multisystem LCH with prolonged therapy {23589673; 12376981; 11496348; 30157397}. Progression from initially unifocal to multisystem LCH can occur, most commonly in infants with hypothalamic pituitary involvement and BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) LCH. Patient age is a less important indicator than disease extent {12376981; 11496348}. Multisystem LCH can be complicated by a hyperinflammatory/macrophage activation syndrome {11910507}. Mutant BRAF p.(Val600Glu) precursor cells in the blood and bone marrow at diagnosis correlates with increased risk of treatment failure and increased risk of CNS LCH-neurodegenerative disease, even in the absence of typical CD1a / CD207 (Langerin) staining {24638167; 27382093; 40167581}. Rare cases following haematological malignancy (i.e. follicular lymphoma or B- or T-lymphoblastic leukaemia) are considered a transdifferentiation phenomenon and behave more aggressively in which initial low-grade LCH disease may progress to high-grade disease resembling a malignant histiocytic neoplasm (i.e. Langerhans cell sarcoma) {15925822; 19642834; 23759932}.
