1.12.28 PEComa

Definition

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumours (PEComas) are mesenchymal neoplasms composed of distinctive epithelioid cells that are often closely associated with blood vessel walls and that usually express both melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

Malignant PEComas are characterized by a variable combination of mitotic activity, necrosis, nuclear atypia, and pleomorphism.

Related terminology

Acceptable: angiomyolipoma; epithelioid angiomyolipoma; lymphangioleiomyomatosis.

Not recommended: clear cell myomelanocytic tumour; sugar tumour of the lung.

Subtype(s)

Malignant PEComa; angiomyolipoma.

Localization

PEComas show a wide anatomical distribution, with a predilection for the abdominal cavity, pelvis, retroperitoneum, uterus, and digestive tract {10976698; 16327428; 30001237; 24061520; 11756764; 15842631; 18277881; 20975340}. Sclerosing PEComas have a predilection for the retroperitoneum {18223480}.

Clinical features

PEComas of soft tissue usually present as painless masses.

Epidemiology

PEComas are rare and are more frequent in women than in men (M:F ratio: 1:5), with a wide age range and a peak in young to middle-aged adults (mean age: 45 years) {10976698; 16327428; 24061520; 18223480}. Sclerosing PEComa occurs exclusively in women {18223480}.

Etiology

Most PEComas are sporadic; a small subset is associated with tuberous sclerosis {11406657; 16327428; 18223480}.

Pathogenesis

The majority of PEComas harbour TSC2 variants and show loss of heterozygosity of 16p including the TSC2 locus {18085521}, indicating the oncogenetic relationship of PEComas and angiomyolipomas. TP53 variants have been identified in 63% of TSC2-mutated PEComas {25651471}. A small subset of PEComas harbour TFE3 gene fusions, which correlate with strong nuclear immunoreactivity for TFE3 {20871214; 26975036; 38597260}. The most common fusion partner with TFE3 is SFPQ, followed by ASPSCR1 and NONO {26274027; 19606011; 25651471}. TFE3-rearranged PEComas lack TSC2 mutations / loss of heterozygosity and are thus pathogenetically distinct {25651471; 22456611}.

Macroscopic appearance

PEComas are grossly well circumscribed, with a firm and fibrous or fleshy cut surface. Tumours show a wide size range (mean: 50–80 mm); cutaneous tumours are usually smaller than those arising at deep locations {16327428; 18277881; 18223480; 24061520; 30001237}.

Histopathology

PEComas typically show a nested architecture and are composed of epithelioid cells with abundant granular eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm and round nuclei with small nucleoli. The nests or trabeculae are typically surrounded by thin-walled capillary vessels. A small subset of tumours have predominantly spindle cell morphology {16327428; 24061520}. PEComa often shows a distinctive perivascular pattern of growth, with tumour cells radially arranged around vessels, replacing the vessel wall and approaching the endothelium. The sclerosing pattern of PEComa is composed of cords and trabeculae of epithelioid cells in a densely collagenous stroma {18223480}. The classic triphasic pattern of angiomyolipoma (i.e. a variable admixture of adipocyte-like cells, thick-walled blood vessels, and smooth muscle–like cells with granular eosinophilic cytoplasm) is rarely found outside the kidney and liver. Epithelioid angiomyolipoma is synonymous with PEComa {18852677; 20410812}. Typical PEComa may contain occasional multinucleated cells and show limited pleomorphism, but mitotic figures are usually scarce or absent. Malignant PEComas are characterized by a variable combination of mitotic activity, necrosis, nuclear atypia, and pleomorphism {11406657; 16327428; 24061520; 30001237}.

PEComas characteristically express both melanocytic markers, such as HMB45 (the most sensitive immunomarker), melan-A, and cathepsin K, and muscle markers, such as SMA, desmin, and h-caldesmon {24061520; 30001237; 23379905}. Expression of melanocytic markers is more common in epithelioid tumour cells than in spindle cells. Desmin and h-caldesmon are often less extensively positive than SMA {18223480}. Some tumours lack expression of muscle markers. Nuclear expression of S100 is generally not observed in PEComas. Around 15–20% of cases show strong nuclear reactivity for TFE3 {26975036; 16327428; 24061520; 20871214}. PEComas with TFE3 rearrangement tend to occur in younger patients, have a prominent alveolar growth pattern and epithelioid morphology, and often lack expression of smooth muscle markers {26975036; 22456611; 26274027; 25651471; 38597260}. Rare TFE3-rearranged PEComas contain melanin pigment {19065101; 26975036}.

Cytology

Aspirate smears show moderate to high cellularity, with loosely cohesive tissue fragments, some containing fine vessels, consisting of epithelioid to spindle cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, oval to elongated nuclei with variable pleomorphism and (in some cases) prominent nucleoli that stain bright purple (Giemsa) or light blue (Pap), occasional nuclear pseudoinclusions, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm {33162379}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

For cases with strong TFE3 protein expression, identification of TFE3 gene rearrangement or a corresponding fusion gene can help confirm the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid and/or spindle cells with granular eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm; nested, trabecular, or sheet-like architecture with frequent perivascular orientation; variable coexpression of melanocytic and smooth muscle markers.

Desirable: TFE3 expression or rearrangement if smooth muscle markers are negative.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Clinically malignant tumours are typically large; show marked nuclear atypia and pleomorphism, conspicuous mitoses, necrosis, and infiltrative margins; and tend to pursue an aggressive clinical course {16327428; 30001237}. Tumour size > 5 cm has also been significantly associated with recurrence {22619565}. The most common metastatic sites are the liver, lymph nodes, lungs, and bone {16327428; 24061520; 30001237}.
