1.3.10 Inclusion body fibromatosis

Definition

Inclusion body fibromatosis is a benign, sometimes multicentric, myofibroblastic neoplasm with characteristic eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue

Related terminology

Acceptable: infantile digital fibroma/fibromatosis; recurring digital fibrous tumour(s) of childhood; recurring digital fibroma(s) of childhood/infancy.

Not recommended: Reye tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumour classically involves the dorsal or dorsolateral aspect of the distal or middle portion of the second, third, and fourth digits, followed by the fifth digit, while typically sparing the first digit, hand, and foot {840560; 3892466; 18830128}. Extradigital examples are reported on the extremities, tongue, and breast {18830128}.

Clinical features

The tumour presents as an asymptomatic dome-shaped or polypoid cutaneous nodule, typically no larger than 20 mm {840560; 3835952; 18830128}. Synchronous and/or metachronous lesions (sometimes affecting multiple digits) occur, but simultaneous involvement of both fingers and toes is rare {4344937; 840560; 3835952}. Non-classic examples typically present as solitary masses or nodules {18830128}.

Epidemiology

The tumour accounts for 0.1% of registered soft tissue tumours {4344937} and 2% of paediatric fibroblastic tumours {1946077} in case series. Nearly all cases are reported before the age of 7 years {3892466; 1946077}. Most tumours are documented during the first year of life, with about 30% present at birth {3892466; 840560; 3835952}. Rare cases occur in adults {2836293; 7377793; 11069537}. An M:F ratio of 1.8:1 has been reported {1946077}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Somatic FLNA variants have been identified in 2 cases, while germline FLNA variants have been reported in patients with terminal osseous dysplasia, a condition characterized by recurrent infantile digital fibromas that notably seem to lack cytoplasmic inclusions. Accordingly, a causal relationship between FLNA variants and inclusion body fibromatosis remains to be established {26061098; 39442669; 15864382}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are firm or rubbery, poorly defined protuberant or polypoid dermal nodules. The cut surface is fibrous and off-white to grey in colour.

Histopathology

Spindle cells have lightly eosinophilic cytoplasm and an elongated, cytologically bland nucleus, and they exhibit low mitotic activity. Cells proliferate in whorls, interlacing short fascicles, or storiform arrays within a variably collagenous dermis; they characteristically grow perpendicular to the epidermis, surround adnexa, and occasionally infiltrate deeper tissues {4344937; 3892466}. Cells harbour a 1.5–24 μm, rounded, pale-pink to red intracytoplasmic inclusion, which is frequently paranuclear, indenting the nucleus {14322942; 3892466}, and is highlighted by trichrome (red), phosphotungstic acid–haematoxylin (dark purple), and Movat’s (pink) stains {18830128}. Ultrastructurally, tumour cells contain intracytoplasmic bundles of actin-rich myofilaments with dense bodies, which are in continuity with a non–membrane-bound, rounded inclusion consisting of a dense core of tightly packed myofilaments {3811906; 216266; 6253042}. By immunohistochemistry, the inclusions are commonly highlighted with actin {1310240}, calponin {24117680}, and (occasionally) caldesmon {18830128}.

Cytology

FNAB shows abundant plump spindle cells and few polygonal cells with moderate cytoplasm set in a collagenous background; scattered prominent intracytoplasmic inclusions are present {21986179}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: dermal-based, cytologically bland myofibroblastic proliferation; eosinophilic intracytoplasmic and paranuclear inclusions.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Recurrence rates between 61% and 75% have been reported in large series and reviews, usually within about 4 months of surgery {215963; 3892466; 18830128}. Lower recurrence rates are achievable with initial complete (preferably wide) excision {8583050; 9493986; 17273037}. Current treatment recommendations include function-preserving excision, application of topical treatments, or intralesional steroid or 5-fluorouracil injection for symptomatic lesions and clinical observation after diagnosis for asymptomatic tumours {27684985; 29780440; 36632366}, because lesions may spontaneously regress {17542881}. Approximately 25% of non-classic tumours recur {18830128}.
