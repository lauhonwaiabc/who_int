1.5.8 Composite haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Classic composite haemangioendothelioma (CHE) is a locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing vascular neoplasm, comprising an admixture of at least two histologically distinct vascular components, most often resembling retiform haemangioendothelioma (RHE), epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, and haemangioma. A small subset may harbour an overtly malignant angiosarcoma-like component, typically lacking pleomorphism.

ICD-O coding

9136/1 Composite haemangioendothelioma

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Composite haemangioendothelioma with neuroendocrine differentiation

Localization

CHE can occur in both superficial and deep locations. Most cases occur on the distal extremities, especially the hands and feet or the head and neck {10716148; 12028155; 17895759; 18286494; 28731049; 29233122}. Cutaneous involvement is common { 18190450; 23866898; 24713748}. Multiple other sites, including lymph nodes, mediastinum, visceral organs such as the heart, deep soft tissue, and bone, can be affected {10716148; 17895759; 18190450; 19366065; 26050262; 28731049; 32418470; 32617478; 32661479; 34032697; 34837560; 34862698; 36073116; 37559782; 37620851; 37658696; 38861371; 39636090}. Neuroendocrine CHE tends to occur in deep locations {28731049; 34862698; 38861371}.

Clinical features

Lesions are usually longstanding (for as long as several decades) and have a reddish-blue, variably nodular appearance. Several patients with CHE had a history of lymphoedema {37349756}.

Epidemiology

CHE is a rare neoplasm {28731049; 26050262}, with reported cases showing a roughly equal M:F distribution. The majority of cases have been reported in adults, with a median age of 42 years {40283372}. Rare cases may occur in childhood {12028155; 39228136}.

Etiology

Rare cases have arisen in the setting of chronic lymphoedema {10716148; 17895759; 18190450; 26050262; 28731049; 37349756}.

Pathogenesis

Recurrent YAP1 and MAML2 gene rearrangements have been identified in up to a third of both CHE and RHE {32991341; 34791601}, suggesting a shared pathogenesis, which may exist on a morphological spectrum. The most common fusion in conventional CHE is YAP1::MAML2 {32991341}, with less common variants including ACTB::MAML2 and HSPG2::FGFR1 {37658696}. In contrast, the most common fusion in CHE with neuroendocrine differentiation is PTBP1::MAML2, with other, mostly non-recurrent, fusions also being described, including EPC1::PHC2, ARID1B::MAML2, and YAP1::FOXR1 {28731049; 34862698; 37658696; 38861371}.

Macroscopic appearance

CHE presents as an infiltrative, uninodular or multinodular mass (individual nodules measure 4–300 mm; median: 32 mm) or as an area of poorly defined swelling. Some of the lesions are associated with reddish-purple skin discolouration, suggestive of a vascular neoplasm.

Histopathology

CHE is a poorly circumscribed, infiltrative lesion that is typically centred in the dermis and subcutis, although occasional cases are deep-seated or involve viscera. It comprises a complex admixture of histologically benign and malignant vascular components that vary greatly in their relative proportions {17895759}. Most often the tumours show mixed morphological features of RHE and epithelioid haemangioendothelioma (EHE), although the characteristic myxochondroid matrix of EHE is not seen. Less often, foci resembling spindle cell haemangioma, angiosarcoma, and benign vascular lesions (lymphangioma, angiomatosis, vascular malformation, and cavernous haemangioma) are present. Most cases contain 2–3 components. Vacuolated, pseudolipoblastic endothelial cells are frequently present. The angiosarcoma-like areas are usually characterized by a low-grade appearance, with complex dissecting vascular channels showing subtle endothelial atypia and relatively few mitotic figures. CHEs containing foci resembling high-grade angiosarcoma, including epithelioid type, are exceptionally rare, and this diagnosis should be made only after conventional angiosarcoma is rigorously excluded on clinical and morphological grounds {25666464; 34862698}. CHE with neuroendocrine differentiation shares similar architectural patterns with conventional CHE, often with an admixture of RHE-like and EHE-like areas, but in addition may show a striking nested appearance and overtly malignant components {28731049; 34862698}.

CHEs are consistently positive for CD31, ERG, and FLI1. About half of cases are positive for CD34 and D2-40; they are negative for CAMTA1 {10716148; 18190450; 26050262; 28731049; 17895759}. Neuroendocrine CHE is characterized by expression of neuroendocrine markers, most often synaptophysin {28731049; 34862698}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing may be useful to detect YAP1::MAML2, present in both CHE and RHE {32991341; 34791601}, and PTBP1::MAML2 {34862698; 38861371} in CHE with neuroendocrine differentiation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: at least two morphologically distinct vascular tumour elements, most often closely resembling RHE and EHE; demonstration of neuroendocrine marker expression in neuroendocrine CHE.

Desirable: detection of MAML2 or YAP1 gene fusions.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The behaviour of CHE in general appears to be much less aggressive than that of conventional angiosarcoma {10716148; 17895759}. Several lesions recurred locally between 4 months and 10 years after excision of the primary mass, often with multiple recurrences {36648783}. Median recurrence-free survival is 48 months {40283372}. Lymph node metastasis can occur {18190450; 20055844}. Neuroendocrine CHE appears to be considerably more aggressive, with distant metastases to lymph nodes, bone, lung, liver, or brain reported in half of patients {28731049; 34862698}.
