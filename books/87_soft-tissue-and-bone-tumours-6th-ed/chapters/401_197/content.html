4.0.1 Genetic tumour syndromes of soft tissue and bone: Introduction

Germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants associated with an increased risk of developing soft tissue and/or bone tumours are being identified in a growing list of genes through the application of sequencing technology such as massively parallel DNA sequencing. These tumour-predisposing genetic variants often have additional phenotypic consequences, resulting in clinically recognizable syndromes. As germline (constitutional) pathogenic variants are often inherited from one of the parents, it is important to recognize such tumour-predisposing syndromes to screen at-risk individuals in affected families for referral to genetic counselling and implementation of guideline-based surveillance for early cancer detection. Although most of these syndromes demonstrate Mendelian inheritance, patients with disorders caused by postzygotic mutations have now been recognized, and they may need distinct forms of therapy and personalized follow-up. Finally, the study of germline (constitutional) tumour-predisposing variants may shed light on the pathogenesis of sporadic tumours.

This chapter provides detailed descriptions of six syndromes associated with predisposition to the development of bone or soft tissue tumours (#52440Table 6.01). These well-characterized syndromes are included because the tumour manifests in the bone or soft tissue as part of the syndrome at high frequency and is a primary outcome of the genetic alteration underlying that syndrome, often with neoplasm features that are different from those of their sporadic counterparts. #52441Table 6.02 provides a comprehensive list of other genes associated with disorders and syndromes predisposing to soft tissue and bone tumours. Additional information about disorders associated with soft tissue or bone tumours can be found in the Genetic tumour syndromes volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series.
