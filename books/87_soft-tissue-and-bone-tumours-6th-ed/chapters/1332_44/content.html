1.3.32 Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma

Definition

Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma is an intermediate, rarely metastasizing neoplasm showing both a predilection for acral sites and variably myxoid and cellular areas with bland spindle cells, a prominent inflammatory infiltrate, and scattered large polygonal cells with macronucleoli.

ICD-O coding

8811/1 Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH2D15 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumour presents most often in the acral sites: hands, fingers, and feet, more often on dorsal sides. Rarely it presents in the proximal parts of extremities and trunk wall {9578090; 9706971; 24121178; 25886867}.

Clinical features

The tumour typically forms a raised mass on hands or feet and is typically wider than deep. It can mimic an inflammatory process. Local recurrence is common, but metastases are exceedingly rare {24121178}.

Epidemiology

The tumour is rare and predominantly occurs in young to middle-aged adults with a slight female predominance. Isolated cases have been reported in children and adolescents aged 5–17 years {9578090; 9706971; 24121178; 25886867; 23919754; 32514165}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

TGFBR3::OGA (MGEA5) fusions have been detected in 32–80% of cases. Similar fusions and histological overlap between myxoinflammatory fibroblastic tumour and pleomorphic hyaline angiectatic tumour have prompted discussion about whether these tumours are related or are part of one entity {21717526; 26980036; 29979612; 26626209}. BRAF fusions are seen in as many as 30% of cases, and VGLL3 amplification is common, especially in connection with a BRAF fusion {28692601; 31898851; 32514165; 39822017}. Rarely, other fusions have been reported: SND1::BRAF, SEC23IP::VGLL3, and TEAD1::MRTFB {35776191}. Nodular necrotizing fibroblastic tumour, which shows morphological overlap with myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma, has YAP1::MAML2 fusions {35546636}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are often lobulated and can be gelatinous, fleshy, or firm. Size varies from 15 to > 200 mm, and the average size is 30 mm.

Histopathology

The tumour contains large polygonal cells with nuclear pleomorphism, pseudoinclusions, and large eosinophilic nucleoli, potentially mimicking Hodgkin cells. Vacuolated pseudolipoblasts and mildly atypical spindle cells are often seen. There is a prominent stromal lymphoplasmacytic and neutrophilic inflammatory component. Cellularity is moderate to high in half of the cases. Mitoses are usually rare: < 5 mitoses/10 mm2 (equivalent to < 5 mitoses/50 HPF of 0.5 mm diameter and 0.2 mm2 area), and only 10% of cases have > 10 mitoses/10 mm2 (> 10 mitoses/50 HPF of 0.5 mm diameter and 0.2 mm2 area). Stroma is mostly collagenous, usually with at least focal myxoid areas. Stromal haemosiderin deposition is common, and some cases have areas reminiscent of haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumour. Half of the cases are positive for CD34, while there is less common (10–15%) positivity for desmin, EMA, and keratin cocktail {24121178; 32514165; 9706971; 9578090}.

Cytology

Diagnosis on cytology is difficult, but the tissue fragments of myxoid and collagenous matrix containing a mix of bland spindle cells, polygonal/epithelioid cells, and occasional atypical Reed–Sternberg–like cells with macronucleoli, along with inflammatory cells and haemosiderin, can provide a provisional diagnosis {33431307}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: variably myxoid and cellular areas with bland spindle cells, a prominent inflammatory infiltrate, and scattered large polygonal cells with macronucleoli.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

About half of the patients experience a local recurrence, and some have multiple recurrences. Distant metastasis is very uncommon (~1%).
