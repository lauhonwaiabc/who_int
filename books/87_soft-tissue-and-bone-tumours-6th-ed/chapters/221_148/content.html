2.2.1 Osteoma

Definition

Osteoma is a benign tumour arising on the surface of bone and composed of lamellar/cortical-type or trabecular bone. The term “bone island” is used when osteoma develops within the medullary cavity.

ICD-O coding

9180/0 Osteoma

ICD-11 coding

2E83.Z & XH4818 Benign osteogenic tumours, unspecified & Osteoma NOS

Related terminology

Osteoma

Not recommended: ivory exostosis; parosteal osteoma; torus palatinus/mandibularis.

Bone island

Not recommended: enostosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Osteomas predominantly affect bones formed by membranous ossification, which include calvarial, facial, and jaw bones {18154576}. They rarely occur outside the skull {7736390}. Intramedullary lesions generally originate in the epiphysis and metaphysis of long bones, the pelvic bones, and the vertebral bodies.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Osteomas are usually slow-growing and asymptomatic, but they can cause pain and headache, obstruction of the paranasal sinuses, and/or local swellings {18154576}.

Imaging

Osteomas are round, dense, well-defined, ivory-like lesions, which are attached to the underlying bone without cortical invasion. Multiple osteomas occur in familial adenomatous polyposis syndrome {8956591}. Bone islands are intramedullary lesions typically measuring < 20 mm, although larger lesions can occur (sometimes referred to as giant bone islands). Both osteomas and bone islands can grow slowly {9160920}. Bone islands blend with surrounding trabeculations, giving them a ragged margin on CT. Multiple bone islands in osteopoikilosis (common, located in the appendicular skeleton), or in mesopyknosis (rare, located in the axial skeleton) may mimic metastases {1624505; 26185059}. CT density measurements can be used to distinguish osteoma and bone island from sclerotic metastases, using a cut-off point of > 885 Hounsfield units {27101076}. Osteomas and bone islands have, like cortical bone, low signal intensity on all MRI sequences. Osteomas and bone islands may show (33%) mild radionuclide uptake on Tc-99m bone scintigraphy and are hypointense on T1- and T2-weighted images.

Epidemiology

Since osteomas commonly represent incidental findings, their actual prevalence is difficult to determine but has been described as 3% in the general population and reported to reach 6.4% in selected case series {25719573; 19780030}. Reported osteoma cases occur in men and women equally, whereas bone islands seem to be more common in men.

Etiology

The etiology of sporadic cases is unknown. The presence of multiple osteomas is suggestive of familial adenomatous polyposis {8956591}; familial adenomatous polyposis is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutations in the APC gene. Multiple bone islands are observed in osteopoikilosis (spotted bone disease), an autosomal dominant disorder caused by loss-of-function variants of the LEMD3 gene, with or without melorheostosis and/or dermatofibrosis (dermato-osteopoikilosis, Buschke–Ollendorff syndrome) {20732851; 24663043; 25352085; 26708699; 15489854}.

Pathogenesis

Activating hotspot variants in the CTNNB1 gene encoding β-catenin, the key mediator of canonical WNT/β-catenin signalling, are present in > 60% of sporadic craniofacial osteomas. Compact, spongious, and osteoblastoma-like tumours are equally affected; the variants result in nuclear β-catenin accumulation and a transcriptional WNT signature {34725446}. A subset of osteomas might be derived from reactive new bone formation following an inflammatory stimulus, particularly in the jaws.

Macroscopic appearance

Osteomas arise on the surface of the bone and are typically well-circumscribed tumours with a broad attachment to the underlying bone. Bone islands are intramedullary and composed of compact bone that merges with the surrounding trabecular bone. Bone islands are usually small, although rare giant bone islands do occur.

Histopathology

Osteomas are predominantly composed of mature lamellar/cortical-type bone and can be divided histologically into compact and (less frequently) spongious subtypes. In cancellous areas, a well vascularized and moderately cellular and fibrous stroma fills the marrow spaces. Osteoblasts and osteocytes are generally inconspicuous; inflammatory infiltrates are typically absent. However, in the frontoethmoid region, conspicuous osteoblastic and osteoclastic activity may be present, mimicking osteoblastoma {19792048; 25663319}.

Immunohistochemistry

Nuclear β-catenin expression is present in cases with CTNNB1 mutation.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CTNNB1 variant testing can be considered in ambiguous cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a benign bone tumour with compatible imaging; arising on the surface of bone or within the medullary cavity; composed of lamellar/cortical-type or trabecular bone.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Asymptomatic cases generally do not require treatment and follow an indolent clinical course; symptomatic cases can be managed by conservative excision.
