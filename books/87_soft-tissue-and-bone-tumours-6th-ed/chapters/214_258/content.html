2.1.4 Periosteal chondroma

Definition

Periosteal chondroma is a benign cartilaginous neoplasm that arises on the bone surface beneath the periosteum, is up to 5 cm in size, and does not invade the underlying cortex.

ICD-O coding

9221/0 Periosteal chondroma

ICD-11 coding

2E82 & XH3BC3 Benign chondrogenic tumours & Periosteal chondroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: juxtacortical chondroma; chondroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Periosteal chondroma predominantly affects the surface of the proximal humerus, femur, and short tubular bones of the hands {6822582; 7180962; 10068070; 2194723}.

Clinical features

Periosteal chondroma may present with local swelling with or without pain. The majority are solitary, but they can occur in the settings of enchondromatosis, such as Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome {22057236}. The tumours are usually < 3 cm in size and, by definition, must be < 5 cm in maximum dimension. Tumours > 5 cm should be classified as periosteal chondrosarcoma, as size appears to be the most reliable predictor of outcome {11641198}.

Imaging

Radiographically, periosteal chondroma appears as a well-demarcated metaphysial and juxtacortical radiolucent neoplasm, with a variable degree of internal mineralization, ranging from punctate to diffuse. The underlying cortex is scalloped and sclerotic, and the lateral borders of the tumour are typically buttressed {7180962; 10068070; 2194723}.

Epidemiology

No data are available on the true incidence of periosteal chondromas, as they may be asymptomatic. However, it is estimated that these lesions represent 1% of all bone tumours {4051099; 6822582; 10068070}. Most are found between the second and fourth decades of life {4051099; 6822582}, and some case series show a male predominance {4051099; 6822582; 7180962; 10068070; 2194723}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

A subset of periosteal chondromas feature mutations in one of the IDH genes, most commonly codon 132 of IDH1, similar to enchondromas {21598255; 22074484; 26170993}.

Macroscopic appearance

Periosteal chondroma appears as a surface lobular cartilaginous nodule measuring < 5 cm, usually < 3 cm, with well-demarcated margins and variable adjacent cortical sclerosis. Scalloping of the outer cortex can be noted.

Histopathology

Periosteal chondroma is composed of bland to mildly atypical chondrocytes. Binucleate figures can be seen. Endochondral calcification and ossification are frequently observed. Scalloping and/or erosion of the outer cortex of the bone is frequently seen, but the borders are usually well-defined with remodelled bone.

Differential diagnosis

Differentiation from periosteal chondrosarcoma may be challenging by light microscopy. Periosteal chondrosarcomas are usually larger (> 5 cm in greatest dimension), which may be the most useful feature. Infiltration of the cortex, including infiltration of the Haversian canals, or the medullary cavity is a feature of malignancy.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Because hotspot mutations in IDH genes have been identified in subsets of both periosteal chondromas and periosteal chondrosarcomas, detection of an IDH variant cannot be used to distinguish these two entities {21598255; 25648524; 22057236}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-defined cartilaginous tumour of less than 5 cm arising on the bone surface, covered by periosteum. Scalloping or pressure erosion of the underlying cortex with no invasion into the medullary canal or Haversian system.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Periosteal chondroma is a benign tumour and can be treated by surgical excision. Recurrences are uncommon {7180962; 2194723}.
