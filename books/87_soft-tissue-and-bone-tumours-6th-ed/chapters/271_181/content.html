2.7.1 Osteofibrous dysplasia

Definition

Osteofibrous dysplasia (OFD) and atypical OFD are benign and locally aggressive fibro-osseous tumours of long bone that typically arise in the anterior cortex of the tibia and/or fibula during childhood and adolescence.

Subtype(s)

Osteofibrous dysplasia (OFD)(benign); atypical OFD (locally aggressive)

Localization

OFD occurs in the anterior cortex of the tibia in most cases {7204433} and is a rare finding in the posterior cortex {24497800}. Fibular involvement usually accompanies ipsilateral tibial disease in 10–15% of cases {7204433; 8276381} but can be isolated; one study of 80 patients reported 77 cases in the tibia and 3 cases in the fibula, with 9 of the cases affecting both bones {8276381}. Tibial tumours tend to occur in the middle third of the bone and fibular tumours in the distal third {7204433}. OFD has also been found in the tibia and fibula bilaterally, both tibiae {7204433}, the radius {1563159}, and the ulna {11764436}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Patients may present with pain (25%), a fracture (12.5%), tibial swelling (8.8%), and deformity (6.2%) {8276381}. Tibial enlargement and anterior/anterolateral bowing were the most common presenting symptoms in young patients, two thirds of whom were aged < 5 years {7204433}; 37% of tumours were discovered incidentally in one series where the median age was 9.5 years {18300815}.

Imaging

A single lesion or multiple well-defined, predominantly intracortical, lucent foci are seen in the anterior tibial diaphysis (+/− fibula) {8276381}. Ground-glass density may be seen in large locules on radiographs {7204433}. Multiple rounded lesions with intervening sclerosis result in a soap-bubble appearance {7204433}. Longitudinal, lobulated growth may expand an elongated segment of the anterior tibial cortex, and larger lesions tend to be associated with anterior bowing {8276381}. Bone overlying the locules is thinned, sometimes appearing to be absent or covered by periosteal new bone {8276381}. There is sclerosis at the deep aspect of the tumour, and the expanded cortex may encroach on the medulla {7204433}. Fractures are usually incomplete and minimally displaced but may result in tibial/fibular pseudarthrosis {7204433}. Pseudarthroses can be congenital {16944141}. Signal characteristics on MRI are nonspecific, but there is usually heterogeneous solid enhancement and occasional cystic change {24497800; 18690429}); morphology may suggest the diagnosis. Extension of OFD into the medulla is less common than with adamantinoma and, in contrast to adamantinoma, rarely fills the medullary canal {18690429}. An extraosseous mass is extremely rare and in one series was seen only in the presence of a fracture {8276381}. Atypical OFD is radiographically indistinguishable from OFD.

Features that suggest OFD rather than adamantinoma include young age, ground-glass density {18690429}, and bowing {18300815}; a more aggressive pattern of bone destruction (cortical destruction, moth-eaten appearance) and larger tumour (greater longitudinal and transverse medullary extent) suggest adamantinoma {18690429}.

Epidemiology

OFD is a very rare tumour, accounting for approximately 0.2% of all primary bone tumours. It typically develops during the first and second decades of life, with a mean age at presentation of 13.5 years {35094574; 38539216}. OFD is rare after the age of 15 years. Patients with atypical OFD are slightly older, with a mean age at diagnosis of 17 years {33027124; 38539216}. Patients with OFD and atypical OFD are younger than patients with classic adamantinoma.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Rare familial cases have been reported {26637977}.

Pathogenesis

In OFD, atypical OFD, as well as in classic adamantinoma, trisomies of chromosomes 7, 8, and 12 can be observed {9242211; 31021853}. The genetic abnormalities are found in a minority (5–10%) of spindle-shaped cells and not in the osteoblasts or osteoclasts, indicating that a large portion of the tumour is non-neoplastic in nature; it has been hypothesized that the stromal cells showing epithelial differentiation might be the neoplastic component {18300815}. Whole-exome sequencing of 4 cases revealed a low somatic mutation burden, and chromosomal losses and gains are not as prominent as in classic adamantinoma {31021853}. Causative molecular genetic aberrations have not been identified; GNAS variants (as seen in fibrous dysplasia) are absent in OFD {23370769}. Germline variants involving MET (encoding a receptor tyrosine kinase) are seen in hereditary forms of OFD {26637977}, and mouse models show that the mutation delays fracture repair and impairs osteogenesis {39042586}.

Macroscopic appearance

OFD is a solid, white or yellow, gritty tumour. It is confined to the cortex, rarely with minimal involvement of the adjacent medullary cavity. Although the cortex may be thinned, the overlying periosteum is intact.

Histopathology

OFD is composed of an admixture of bone and fibrous tissue. The bone is composed of trabeculae of woven bone with prominent osteoblastic rimming. The intervening fibrous stroma is composed of bland-looking spindle-shaped cells embedded within an extracellular collagenous or sometimes myxoid matrix. Osteoclasts may be present. Mitoses are extremely rare. Zonal architecture is delineated with thin spicules and woven bone or even fibrous tissue predominating in the centre of the lesion, with more-abundant anastomosing and lamellar bone peripherally, the latter often blending into the surrounding host bone. Hyalinization, haemorrhage, xanthomatous change, cyst formation, and foci of giant cells are rare. Cartilage is absent.

Atypical OFD is morphologically similar to OFD and in addition contains small epithelial clusters more easily identified by keratin immunohistochemistry. These small clusters often cannot be distinguished from endothelial cells on H&E.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry for keratin shows no or only single keratin-positive cells in OFD. Small clusters (defined as three or more) of keratin-positive cells are seen in atypical OFD. Typically these individual cells express CK14, CK19, and (to a lesser extent) CK5 and CK17 {7694513}. Additionally, OFD shows positive staining for p63 and occasionally S100 {11727265}.

Differential diagnosis

OFD should be distinguished from fibrous dysplasia. The stroma in fibrous dysplasia is more cellular and collagenous than in OFD, and the woven bone in fibrous dysplasia lacks prominent osteoblastic rimming; additionally, mutations in the GNAS gene are found in most cases of fibrous dysplasia but are absent in OFD. Immunohistochemically, there are no keratin-positive epithelial cells in fibrous dysplasia, and this tumour is epicentred in the medullary cavity. Classic adamantinoma can be distinguished by the presence of easily identifiable nests and strands of epithelial cells, clearly visible on H&E.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; almost exclusively in the tibia and/or fibula, always involving the cortex; for OFD: a fibro-osseous tumour of bone in which the woven bone shows prominent osteoblastic rimming; absence of or only isolated keratin-positive cells; for atypical OFD: as for OFD, but with inconspicuous small clusters of keratin-positive cells (three cells or more).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The tumour may gradually grow during the first decade of life. Most undergo spontaneous regression after puberty. It is controversial whether OFD may progress into atypical OFD or even adamantinoma {28249983}, and it is possible that some reported cases of this progression represent sampling issues or that series reporting the absence of progression lack prolonged follow-up. In atypical OFD, local recurrence rates are about 23% {28249983}. Atypical OFD may rarely metastasize after recurrence and subsequent progression to classic adamantinoma. Most patients can be treated conservatively with close observation, patient/parent education, and no surgery. In the presence of substantial deformity or pseudoarthrosis, impending or existing pathological fracture, the desire for definitive diagnosis, or severe symptoms, surgical intervention may be warranted {29227372}.
