1.3.6 Fibrous hamartoma of infancy

Definition

Fibrous hamartoma of infancy (FHI) is a benign soft tissue neoplasm of infants and young children, characterized by an organoid, triphasic morphology composed of bundles of bland fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells, nodules of primitive mesenchyme, and mature adipose tissue.

ICD-O coding

8992/0 Fibrous hamartoma of infancy

ICD-11 coding

LC2Y Other specified hamartomata derived from dermal connective tissue

Related terminology

Not recommended: subdermal fibromatous tumour of infancy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

FHI commonly involves the axillae, trunk, upper extremities, and genital region. Other reported locations include the scalp, foot, hand, orbit, and buttock {28059097; 24525510}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a painless solitary subcutaneous mass, sometimes associated with overlying skin discolouration, oedema, hypertrichosis, or tethering {28059097}. Large lesions and multiple tumours in the same patient may occur. Associations have been reported with tuberous sclerosis and Williams–Beuren syndrome {28059097; 19319513; 17326745}. A pseudoangiomatous pattern has been described in radiological assessment {36493659}.

Epidemiology

FHI is rare, generally diagnosed by 2 years of age, with a male predominance. In a large case series, approximately 15–25% of cases were congenital {24525510}. Occurrence in older children is also documented {28059097}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Recurrent EGFR exon 20 insertion/duplication variants have been identified in these lesions {27631514}.

Macroscopic appearance

FHI is poorly circumscribed and ranges from 4 to 170 mm in size (mean: 30 mm), with a mix of adipose and grey-tan fibrous tissue {28059097}.

Histopathology

FHI demonstrates a characteristic triphasic organoid pattern with fascicles of bland fibroblastic/myofibroblastic cells, mature adipose tissue, and myxoid nodules containing primitive mesenchymal cells with rounded to stellate nuclei. About 25% of cases show areas resembling giant cell fibroblastoma [[Martos-Cabrera L, Sampedro-Ruiz R, Pérez-González YC, et al. Hamartoma fibroso de la infancia: una serie de 21 casos y revisión de la literatura. Actas Dermosifiliogr. 2021;112:520–7. Spanish.]], with hyalinized collagen zones showing slit-like spaces lined by flattened tumour cells. Mitoses are rare, and necrosis is typically absent. Scattered inflammatory cells may be seen. An infiltrative growth pattern has been observed and may contribute to local recurrence {28059097; 38631996}. A sarcomatous morphology has been reported in two tumours {28059097}. A pseudoangiomatous pattern has also been described {36493659}.

By immunohistochemistry, the fibroblastic and primitive mesenchymal areas are variably positive for SMA, while S100 is positive in the adipocytic elements. Primitive mesenchyme and areas with giant cell fibroblastoma–like features are CD34-positive. EGFR expression is typically minimal to moderate and mainly localized in the primitive mesenchyme {27631514}.

Cytology

FNAB shows bland fibroblasts within a collagenous matrix and mature adipose tissue, without atypia or mitoses {18499982}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: triphasic morphology consisting of fibroblastic/myofibroblastic bundles, primitive mesenchymal nodules, and mature adipose tissue.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most patients are cured by simple surgical excision. However, approximately 15% of cases recur locally when incompletely excised {38631996}.
