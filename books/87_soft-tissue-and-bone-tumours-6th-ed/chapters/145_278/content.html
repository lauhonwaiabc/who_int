1.4.5 Keratin-positive giant cell–rich tumour

Definition

Keratin-positive giant cell–rich tumour (KPGCT) is a mesenchymal neoplasm of uncertain lineage, composed of multinucleated giant cells, mixed inflammatory cells, histiocytes, and a small number of keratin-positive mononuclear cells, and characterized by HMGA2::NCOR2 fusions.

Related terminology

Acceptable: xanthogranulomatous epithelial tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

KPGCT shows a predilection for the subcutis and bone {32415263; 33742141; 35690644; 37170907; 37496152}. Extraosseous tumours are most common in the superficial soft tissues of the trunk, upper and lower extremities, and head and neck. Less frequently, tumours arise in deep soft tissue sites. Vocal cord, sinonasal, and cranial fossa tumours have been reported {35690644; 39162814}. Osseous lesions are most often observed in the pelvic bones, mobile spine, long bones, and cranium, but rare tumours may arise in the small bones of the hands and feet, patella, and scapula.

Clinical features

Soft tissue KPGCT typically presents as asymptomatic or (less frequently) painful, solid masses; a subset may grow rapidly {35690644}. Imaging studies of soft tissue lesions often show well-demarcated contrast-enhancing masses. Bone tumours present as lytic intramedullary tumours, with or without a sclerotic rim. Some tumours show aggressive imaging features, including the presence of a soft tissue component {35690644; 37461232}. Rarely, osseous tumours extend into adjacent synovium {37170907}.

Epidemiology

Tumours occur most commonly in young adults (median age: 25 years), although the age range is wide (10 days–87 years), with a pronounced female predominance (72%) {37496152; 40555034}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The majority of KPGCTs harbour HMGA2::NCOR2 fusions {33742141; 35690644; 37170907; 35181586}, although a single tumour with HMGA2::COL14A1 has been reported {39552601}. Tumours show increased CSF1 expression in the absence of CSF1 gene alterations {37170907; 38935896; 29668829; 36439507; 37993023} and appear to respond to CSF1R inhibitors {38935896; 29668829}, suggesting that the CSF1/CSF1R pathway is involved in the oncogenesis of KPGCT; a single and highly aggressive tumour revealed chromothripsis in addition to the HMGA2::NCOR2 fusion {38935896}. The exact pathogenesis of KPGCT, however, remains unclear.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, tumours are yellow, tan, brown, and/or maroon. Soft tissue lesions appear well-circumscribed and can be vaguely nodular {33742141; 35690644}. Bone tumours can appear well marginated {39552601}.

Histopathology

Tumours are characterized by a nodular polymorphous population of mononuclear cells, histiocytes, with or without foamy cytoplasm, mixed inflammatory cells, and giant cells of Touton and osteoclast-types. A peripheral pseudocapsule with a lymphoid cuff or aggregates is common in soft tissue lesions. The mononuclear cells may display bright eosinophilic cytoplasm or appear plasmacytoid. There is often stromal haemosiderin deposition, microhaemorrhage, and intratumoural lymphoid aggregates. The majority of tumours are giant cell rich, composed of sheets of osteoclast-type giant cells in a syncytial growth pattern. Less commonly, the xanthomatous component predominates (xanthogranulomatous epithelial tumour), and hybrid tumours may be identified. A subset of KPGCT shows areas with storiform growth and only rare giant cells. Most KPGCTs have bland cytomorphology; however, moderate atypia can be seen {37496152; 39162814; 38935896}. Infarction is common, but coagulative necrosis is infrequent. Vascular invasion is rare {33742141}.

Keratins are variably positive in the mononuclear cell population, typically highlighting dendritic-like and rounded cell morphology. Plasmacytoid cells, if present, show paranuclear dot-like pattern of keratin expression. H3.3 G34W immunohistochemistry is negative. There is retained expression of SMARCB1 (INI1).

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of HMGA2::NCOR2 fusions may be helpful in select cases; however, false negative results using some methodologies are common.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: polymorphous population of giant cells (osteoclast-type or Touton), histiocytes, and inflammatory cells; keratin-positive mononuclear cells.

Desirable: HMGA2::NCOR2 fusions in diagnostically challenging or keratin-poor cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Limited data suggest that the majority of KPGCTs behave in an indolent fashion, although local recurrences may occur {37170907; 39162814; 37461232; 38935896}. Distant metastatic spread has been identified in 2 patients, but no fatal outcome has been documented to date {37461232; 38935896}. Reliable prognostic factors are currently unknown.
