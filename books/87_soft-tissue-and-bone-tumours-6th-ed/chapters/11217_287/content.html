1.12.17 NUT-rearranged sarcoma

Definition

NUT-rearranged sarcoma is an aggressive malignant mesenchymal neoplasm characterized by a heterogeneous morphology and gene fusions involving NUTM1.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2B5Y Other specified malignant mesenchymal neoplasms

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours have a wide anatomical distribution. They most often arise in the abdomen (frequently involving the peritoneum, gastrointestinal tract, or genitourinary tract) and somatic soft tissue; however, they may also occur in the thorax, pelvis, and bone, amongst other rare locations {29356724; 29431183; 30407212; 30552129; 30723300; 33714983; 34525172; 38946048; 39921028; 39985394; 40070529; 40517816}.

Clinical features

Clinical presentation is typically dictated by the anatomical site of origin, with symptoms reflecting local mass effect or organ involvement. Metastatic disease is often present at the time of initial diagnosis.

Epidemiology

These are rare neoplasms, with fewer than 100 cases reported in the medical literature. Because of their rarity and histological overlap with other entities, these tumours are probably underdiagnosed. Tumours reported span childhood to old age but are most common among middle-aged adults (median: 48 years; range 1–67 years), with an M:F ratio of 1.1:1 {39921028; 38946048}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

NUT-rearranged sarcoma is characterized by NUTM1 gene fusions, most commonly with MXD4, MGA, and MXI1 {33714983; 38946048; 39921028; 40517816; 30552129; 37442637}. Other, rare, fusions involve BCORL1, BRD3, BRD4, MXD1, ONECUT1, and ZNF532 {29356724; 39503069; 31334571}. NUTM1 homologous gene substitutions with NUTM2A, NUTM2B, or NUTM2G have also been reported {39921028; 40517816}.

It is important to emphasize that NUTM1 rearrangement alone is insufficient for a diagnosis of NUT-rearranged sarcoma. Transcriptomic analysis and methylation profiling demonstrate that NUT-rearranged sarcoma forms a distinct molecular cluster, separate from NUT carcinoma {29431183; 30407212; 39921028; 40517816; 38946048}. Several sarcomas harbouring NUTM1/NUTM2 rearrangements instead align more closely with other established sarcoma categories. These include tumours with CIC::NUTM1 and aggressive infantile sarcomas with ATXN1/ATXN1L::NUTM2A gene fusions clusters corresponding to CIC::DUX4 sarcomas {29431183; 30407212; 35836290}. Neoplasms bearing YWHAE::NUTM2 or KDM2B::NUTM2 fusions cluster with BCOR-associated sarcomas on methylation analysis {34717763; 36853789; 37634866}; the former’s association was further independently confirmed by gene expression profiling {26945340; 32933053; 40517816}.

Within NUT-rearranged sarcomas, gene expression profiling reveals additional molecular heterogeneity. A subset harbours fusions involving members of the MAD protein family (MXD1, MXI1, MXD3, and MXD4) that encode transcriptional repressors critical for regulating cell growth, differentiation, and neoplasia {8617250; 9624006}. These proteins antagonize MYC activity, competing for MAX binding, thereby repressing MYC target gene expression {11602341}. MGA, although also encodes a MAX-binding transcriptional repressor, is not a canonical MAD protein {10601024; 11031250}. MXD4 and MXI1 fusions define a distinct molecular subgroup distinct to MGA fusions {39921028; 40517816}. Clinically, MAD-rearranged tumours typically occur in older patients, involve intra-abdominal / gastrointestinal tract locations, and often show estrogen receptor overexpression {33714983; 38946048; 39921028; 40517816}. In contrast, MGA fusions occur across a wider age range and anatomical sites and follow a comparatively less aggressive behaviour {30552129; 30723300; 37442637}. Tumours with BRD3/BRD4 fusions, while highly aggressive {29356724}, remain too rare to support meaningful clinicopathological comparison. Collectively, these findings suggest the existence of multiple, potentially clinically relevant, molecular subgroups within the taxonomy of NUT-rearranged sarcoma {39921028; 40517816}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range from small to relatively large, depending on anatomical location. The cut surface is generally tan-white-grey.

Histopathology

NUT-rearranged sarcoma exhibits significant morphological heterogeneity. Architecturally tumours may be composed of cells arranged in nests, sheets, cords, or fascicles; microcystic change may also be present. The cells may be purely round, epithelioid, rhabdoid, or spindle shaped; however, an admixture is common. Cells are small to medium in size, with variably eosinophilic to amphophilic cytoplasm. The nuclei are round-ovoid and monomorphic, with macronucleoli and rare multinucleation; mitotic activity is variable but typically brisk. The stroma is often scant, but may be focally myxoid, or collagenous; amianthoid-like fibres may be observed occasionally. Necrosis is often present. Osteoclast-like multinucleated giant cells are a rare finding.

By immunohistochemistry, the tumours often have nuclear staining for NUT protein; however, this is not always reliable, particularly in tumours with NUTM2A and NUTM2G rearrangement. Variable expression of desmin, SMA, CD34, CD99, S100, GFAP, keratins, ER, and BCOR has been reported.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show epithelioid to rhabdoid cells singly and in small tissue fragments, with eccentric nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and moderate cytoplasm {35441831; 38595053}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of NUT family gene fusions with specific partners (see Pathogenesis) is critical for diagnosis. Sarcomas with CIC::NUTM1 or ATXN1/ATXN1L::NUTM2A are classified with CIC-rearranged sarcomas.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: pure, or mixed, population of round-epithelioid-spindle cells, with monomorphic nuclei; arranged in sheets, nests, or fascicles; variable collagenous-myxoid stroma; fusion of NUT family genes with specific partners (see Pathogenesis).

Desirable: nuclear expression of NUT.

Staging

NUT-rearranged sarcoma is provisionally staged under the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM principles.

Prognosis and prediction

Most tumours are clinically aggressive, showing frequent metastatic dissemination and tumour-related mortality. Metastases predominantly involve lymph nodes, liver, lung, bone, and the abdominopelvic cavity.
