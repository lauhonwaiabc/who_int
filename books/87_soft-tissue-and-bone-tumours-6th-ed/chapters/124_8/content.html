1.2.4 Lipoblastoma and lipoblastomatosis

Definition

Lipoblastoma is a benign neoplasm of embryonal white fat, which may be a localized or diffuse tumour.

ICD-O coding

8881/0 Lipoblastoma (localized)

8881/0 Lipoblastomatosis (diffuse)

8881/0 PLAG1-rearranged spindle cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2E80.1 & XH8L55 Lipoblastoma & Lipoblastomatosis

Related terminology

Not recommended: fetal lipoma; fetal fat tumour; fetocellular lipoma; embryonal lipoma; congenital lipomatoid tumour; lipoblastic tumour of childhood.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The trunk and extremities are the most common sites {19738456; 28083971; 29035641}. Lipoblastoma may arise in the abdomen, mesentery, retroperitoneum, pelvis, inguinoscrotal or labial region, perineum, mediastinum, and head/neck (including the retropharynx and oral submucosa) {23664430; 29035641; 34400796}. Lung, liver, heart, colon, and parotid gland lipoblastomas have also been described {11370265; 30955788; 16180007; 12112785; 11666056; 26819289; 29035641}.

Clinical features

Superficial, circumscribed lipoblastoma clinically simulates lipoma or a vascular malformation, whereas lipoblastomatosis originates in deep soft tissue and is more infiltrative. Truncal tumours may infiltrate the thoracic cavity from extrathoracic locations, or into the spine, with reports of neuroforaminal or intraspinal invasion {24040598; 30723667; 27444294; 29492129; 29035641}. While both patterns recur, complete excision is often more difficult to achieve in the infiltrative/diffuse form. Lipoblastoma can compress adjacent structures and interfere with function, particularly in large abdominal tumours, mediastinal tumours, or cervical tumours {19738456; 20472310; 26214680; 27097392}. Colonic and mesenteric occurrences have been associated with intussusception and volvulus {28420321; 28034384}. Ultrasonography can detect tumours with high fluid content and vascularity in superficial tissues {29035641}. MRI reveals a nodular mass with an intensity similar to or lower than that of lipoma or subcutaneous fat on T1-weighted images {21092875; 10663508; 10398794; 29035641; 33097826}. A subset of patients with lipoblastoma have developmental delays or abnormalities, seizures, or familial lipomas; these are potentially related to larger chromosome 8q alterations that include the PLAG1 gene {19738456; 9331284; 20055849}.

Epidemiology

Lipoblastoma is rare and occurs predominantly in infancy and early childhood (75–90% of cases occur before the age of 3 years), less frequently in older children and adolescents, and rarely in adults {4353020; 19738456; 18269579; 18639685; 29035641; 34400796}. In limited case series of PLAG1-rearranged spindle cell tumours, most cases have occurred in early childhood, with 1 case in adolescence and 1 in adulthood {33300192; 33893698; 39576105}. There is a slight male predilection {19738456; 9331284; 8314236; 13583102; 29035641}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Lipoblastoma often exhibits a simple, pseudodiploid, or hyperdiploid karyotype that features a structural alteration (translocation, inversion, insertion, or ring chromosome) of 8q11-q13 leading to the rearrangement of PLAG1, a developmentally regulated zinc finger transcription factor gene, in about 60% of cases {18474299; 19738456; 11549588; 23751806; 26319758; 29897891; 32692992}. The most common numerical change is one or more extra copies of chromosome 8, with or without concurrent rearrangement of 8q11-q13 {18269579; 19264235}. PLAG1 fusion gene partners in lipoblastoma show a growing list of partners, which to date include HAS2, COL1A2, COL3A1, RAD51B, RAB2A, BOC, DDX6, KLF10, KANSL1L, CHCHD7, SRSF3, HNRNPC, PCMTD1, YWHAZ, CTDSP2, PPP2R2A, PI15, ZEB2, SDCBP, HNRNPA2B1, and EEF1A1 {10987300; 15642402; 22488564; 23890983; 24700772; 26701195; 30857637; 32692992; 33097826; 34400796; 36870688}. Similarly, tumours described as PLAG1-rearranged spindle cell tumours have demonstrated the same variety of fusion partners to PLAG1 as seen in more conventional lipoblastoma, except for the partners H3-3B and ZFHX4, which have not been described in conventional lipoblastoma {39576105; 34291746; 33893698}.

PLAG1 transcriptional upregulation leading to ectopic PLAG1 expression is the result of replacement of the PLAG1 promoter element by constitutively active promoter regions from one of the partner genes. PLAG1 rearrangements may be difficult to detect, requiring multiple types of testing. To date, lipoblastoma PLAG1 fusion partners are distinct from those reported in pleomorphic adenoma; however, RAD51B::PLAG1 and CHCHD7::PLAG1 have been identified in a subset of uterine myxoid leiomyosarcomas and PLAG1-rearranged uterine sarcomas {30489320; 38942115; 38942115}. Alterations of HMGA2, an upstream regulatory activator of PLAG1, are also described in tumours with lipoblastoma morphology, including fusions of HMGA2 with EP400 and FGD6 {32692992}.

Macroscopic appearance

Lipoblastomas are typically 20–50 mm in diameter, although they can exceed 100 mm {4353020; 19738456; 9331284; 29035641}. The soft, lobulated masses are yellow, white, or tan and may display myxoid nodules, cystic spaces, or fat nodules separated by fine white fibrous trabeculae on the cut surface. PLAG1-rearranged spindle cell tumours typically have a more fibrous gross appearance.

Histopathology

Lipoblastoma is a lobulated tumour comprising sheets of adipocytes showing a spectrum of maturation, ranging from primitive stellate or spindled mesenchymal cells to multivacuolated or small signet-ring lipoblasts through to mature adipocytes, separated by fibrovascular septa {7377463; 4353020; 19738456; 9331284}. Myxoid areas display a plexiform vascular pattern with primitive mesenchymal cells. The proportion of these cell types varies, and there is sometimes a zonal pattern of maturation, with more immature myxoid cells at the periphery (adjacent to fibrous septa) and mature adipocytes in the centre of the lobule. Myxoid regions can occasionally show pooling of matrix similar to that in myxoid liposarcoma. Mature areas of entire tumours resembling lipoma or fibrolipoma with sparse lipoblasts may be seen {19738456; 9331284; 18798558}. Tumour maturation is evidenced by reports of cases diagnosed by biopsy as lipoblastoma, in which later resection showed a fibrolipomatous appearance without lipoblasts {28111780}. Other tumours may show a predominance of a fibroblastic component with minimal lipogenic component, referred to as fibroblastic lipoblastoma {33097826}. Both mature adipocytic and fibroblastic morphologies are thought to arise more commonly in adolescents and adults {33097826; 34400796}. Other histological findings include chondroid or osseous metaplasia, extramedullary haematopoiesis, chronic inflammation, and sparse multinucleated or floret cells {19738456; 9331284; 17116489; 38470300}. Hyperchromasia and mild nuclear atypia may be observed, but mitoses are very rare and abnormal mitoses are absent {26943407}. Mast cells are common.

The adipocytes of lipoblastoma demonstrate reactivity for S100, CD56, and CD34 {23519855}. PLAG1 nuclear immunohistochemical staining can be useful, especially in less differentiated tumours, but its concordance with molecular studies is < 60% and sensitivity is about 50% {32692992; 34601996}. The primitive mesenchymal cells are often reactive for CD34 and desmin {19738456}. Recently, tumours with mixed fibroblastic and myxoid morphology have been described that contain PLAG1 fusions but lack an adipocytic component {33300192; 39576105; 33893698; 40879223; 40879377}. These tumours also show consistent expression of CD34, with variable expression of desmin and S100; PLAG1 is also overexpressed by immunohistochemistry {33300192}. Further study is needed to determine if these tumours represent a non-lipogenic/fibroblastic form of lipoblastoma or a distinct entity with features overlapping with those of lipoblastoma.

Cytology

Cytological material from lipoblastoma varies according to the cellularity and extent of the immature adipocyte, mature adipocyte, myxoid matrix, and fibrous components {28902468; 30848523; 30322346}. Variable proportions of both multivacuolated and signet-ring lipoblasts, mature adipocytes, myxoid matrix, primitive spindle mesenchymal cells with ovoid nuclei with fine chromatin and distinct nucleoli are seen in tissue fragments with a fibrovascular network. Nuclear atypia is minimal, and no mitoses or necrosis are seen.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In selected cases, the finding of PLAG1 rearrangements / copy-number gain can support the diagnosis; HMGA2 alterations are less common {26319758; 32692992}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobulated mass in early childhood; sheets of adipocytes with a spectrum of maturation (primitive stellate/spindled mesenchymal cells, multivacuolated or small signet-ring lipoblasts, mature adipocytes); fibrovascular septa.

Desirable: molecular confirmation may be useful in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Lipoblastoma is benign, with an excellent prognosis after excision {4353020; 13583102; 19738456; 18639685; 29035641; 28083971}. Recurrence (rate: 13–46%) is usually due to incomplete excision. Recurrence can occur as late as 6 years after primary resection, supporting the practice of long-term follow-up with MRI or (for superficial tumours) ultrasound {29243188}. Locally aggressive behaviour with multiple recurrences has been described, but re-excision is effective {29035641}. There is no risk of metastasis. Information regarding behavioural characteristics of PLAG1-rearranged spindle cell tumours is limited to date, but available reports describe benign behaviour with local recurrence and no metastasis {39576105; 33300192; 33893698}.
