1.3.13 Myofibroblastoma

Definition

Myofibroblastoma is a benign neoplasm composed of myofibroblastic cells and bands of hyalinized collagen, with a variable adipocytic component.

ICD-O coding

8825/0 Myofibroblastoma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH3NQ0 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Myofibroblastoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: mammary-type myofibroblastoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately half of cases occur in the inguinal/groin area, including the vulva/vagina, perineum, and scrotum; however, there is a wide anatomical distribution, including trunk/axilla, breast, extremities, abdominal cavity, retroperitoneum, and viscera {11474286; 26523539}. The majority are superficial/subcutaneous. The remainder are deep-seated; intramuscular; or in the pelvis, abdomen, or retroperitoneum {26523539}.

Clinical features

Tumours generally present as either painless masses or incidental lesions; occasional lesions are painful. Tumours may be present for > 20 years before clinical presentation {26523539}.

Epidemiology

Myofibroblastoma is rare, with few case reports and series being published. Lesions arise predominantly in adults in the fifth and sixth decades of life, but there is a wide age range (4–96 years; median: 54 years) and a male predominance (M:F ratio: 2:1) {39493459}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Loss of 13q14, including the RB1 locus, is characteristic of mammary and soft tissue myofibroblastomas, as well as spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma and cellular angiofibroma. Accordingly, some authors have speculated that these tumours may represent variations along a spectrum of genetically related lesions, the 13q/RB1 family of tumours {22575260; 26523539; 20852591; 27825811}. Spindle cell lipomas have been reported to rarely express desmin {15322874}, and conversely, classic myofibroblastoma may be positive for CD34 only, supporting this hypothesis.

Macroscopic appearance

Size ranges from < 10 mm to 220 mm (median: 66 mm). The tumours are generally well circumscribed, mobile, and rubbery to gelatinous. The colour can be variable (white/yellow/pink, grey, or tan). The cut surface may be whorled or nodular and variably fatty.

Histopathology

Tumours are unencapsulated and generally well circumscribed. They are usually composed of spindle cells with short stubby nuclei with finely dispersed chromatin, small or inconspicuous nucleoli, eosinophilic to amphophilic cytoplasm, indistinct cell borders, and sometimes elongated cytoplasmic processes. The spindle cells are arranged in variably sized short fascicles, with interspersed broad bands of hyalinized collagen or (less commonly) ropy collagen. Scattered mast cells are often present. Tumours may vary from markedly cellular to paucicellular and hyalinized and usually contain admixed mature adipocytes, which can range from rare to occasionally the dominant component. Schwannoma-like palisading, focal epithelioid or round cell morphology, or degenerative-type nuclear atypia (enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei and multinucleation) can occur {26523539}. The blood vessels are generally inconspicuous, being small and often hyalinized with a perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate.

By immunohistochemistry, myofibroblastoma typically shows diffuse coexpression of desmin and CD34 (each positive in 90% of cases). Rare cases are negative for both markers. Expression of SMA is present in one third of cases. About 90% of cases show loss of nuclear RB1 {22790852; 26523539}.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show cohesive tissue fragments of spindle cells and single spindle cells with bland elongated nuclei and a background of stroma, are not diagnostic, and raise a broad differential diagnosis of other reactive and benign neoplastic spindle cell tumours {23075890}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Loss of RB1 can be demonstrated by FISH, although this is usually not necessary to establish the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: haphazardly intersecting short fascicles of bland spindle cells; bands of hyalinized collagen; variable adipocytic component.

Desirable: expression of CD34 and/or desmin; loss of RB1 in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

All tumours reported to date have followed a benign course after marginal local excision, with exceptional local recurrences.
