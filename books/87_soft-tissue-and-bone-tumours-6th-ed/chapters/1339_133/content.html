1.3.39 Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma

Definition

Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS) is a malignant pleomorphic mesenchymal neoplasm showing no identifiable line of differentiation and no distinctive molecular aberration when analysed by presently available technology. UPS is a diagnosis of exclusion.

ICD-O coding

8805/3 Undifferentiated sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B54 & XH0947 Unclassified pleomorphic sarcoma, primary site & Malignant fibrous histiocytoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant fibrous histiocytoma; sarcoma NOS; fibrosarcoma NOS.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

UPS may be found at any location.

Clinical features

UPS has no characteristic clinical features that distinguish it from other types of sarcoma. It frequently has a rapid growth rate.

Epidemiology

UPS historically accounted for as many as 20% of all soft tissue sarcomas {39502684}. Exact incidence is difficult to determine because of changing definitions and terminology over time, but overall, it seems to gradually decrease because of the reclassification of specific entities previously included {39502684}. UPS is anatomically ubiquitous and occurs predominantly in older adults, with a median age of 60–70 years. Occurrence in children and young adults is rare. There is no sex predilection {40222653}.

Etiology

The etiology of most UPSs is unknown. However, at least 25% of radiation-associated soft tissue sarcomas are UPS {20308666; 3179948}. Pathogenic germline variants of high clinical significance have been reported in TP53, NF1, MLH1, CHEK2, and BARD1 in a subset of young adult (31%) and adult (12%) patients with UPS {40222653}. Though extremely rare, UPS can occur in patients with Lynch syndrome, especially in case of germline MSH2 mutation {40190387}.

Pathogenesis

UPS typically displays genomic complexity. Cytogenetic data revealed complex karyotypes with chromosome numbers ranging from near-haploid to hyperoctaploid {10362810; 25516889; 39149945}. Tumour mutation burden is usually low (mean: < 10 mut/MB) {40222653; 33254023; 29100075}. Structural rearrangements and copy-number shifts involving most if not all chromosomes are seen {19290004; 21085701; 29100075; 30889380}. Whole-genome sequencing has revealed that punctuated evolutionary events such as whole-genome duplications and chromothripsis accompanied by telomere dysfunction underpin the genomic complexity of UPS {30889380}. The aberrations in telomere biology are achieved either through activation of telomerase through deregulation of TERT or through the alternative-lengthening-of-telomeres pathway {29100075; 30889380}. There are no tumour-specific amplicons, but some 10–15% of cases show amplification of VGLL3 or YAP1, and 10% have amplification of CCNE1 {20842732; 29100075}. Targets of frequent deletions and disruptive rearrangements include CDKN2A and CDKN2B in 9p, PTEN in 10q, RB1 in 13q, and TP53 in 17p, each affected in 10–20% of cases {10748288; 11103795; 20884963; 21125665; 29100075; 30889380}. RB1 and TP53, as well as ATRX, are among the few genes that recurrently show pathogenetic variants at the nucleotide level {29100075}. At the transcriptomic level, UPS closely resembles high-grade myxofibrosarcoma, and it is possible that these two entities may lie on a spectrum of differentiation {29100075}. No specific and recurrent gene fusions are identified {31467233}.

Macroscopic appearance

UPS has no distinctive macroscopic features, other than the frequent presence of necrosis and haemorrhage.

Histopathology

UPS is morphologically intermediate- to high-grade and may display pleomorphic, spindle cell, and epithelioid areas, without specific defining features. They lack an identifiable line of differentiation, other than a (myo)fibroblastic appearance {18446665; 11408500}. UPS is often patternless, with frequent enlarged, irregular, bizarre, multinucleated tumour giant cells with coarse chromatin and prominent nucleoli. The amount of cytoplasm is variable, and it is pale to eosinophilic; intracytoplasmic or intranuclear inclusions can be seen. Mitoses are easily seen, often including atypical mitotic figures. Myxoid areas can be present, though extensive myxoid change should prompt a diagnosis of high-grade myxofibrosarcoma. Osteoid production as well as pleomorphic lipoblasts or rhabdomyoblasts are absent. When localized in the subcutis, especially in the head and neck area of elderly male patients, UPS should be distinguished from pleomorphic dermal sarcoma with subcutaneous involvement, which is ultraviolet (UV) radiation–related and has a better outcome {40057991}. In case of predominant spindle cell morphology, a fascicular architecture with variably amphophilic or palely eosinophilic cytoplasm can be seen with tapering nuclei with at least a mild degree of nuclear variability. However, lower-grade, uniform, and monomorphic spindle cell sarcomas should not be classified as UPS, as they seem increasingly to be reclassified as other entities.

UPS can contain a variable amount and mixed composition of immune cells including eosinophils, T cells, and macrophages. Moreover, lymphoid aggregates and/or tertiary lymphoid structures are seen in 30–65% of UPS {36313661; 40007136} and may predict response to immune checkpoint inhibitors {35618839; 31942077; 38351182; 31900276}.

Immunohistochemistry should be used to confirm lack of smooth muscle, rhabdomyoblastic, melanocytic, endothelial, and epithelial differentiation. Loss of H3K27me3 may suggest the diagnosis of high-grade malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, within the appropriate clinical context, though it can also be seen in radiation-associated UPS {32735735}.

The presence of MDM2 and CDK4 amplification may suggest the diagnosis of dedifferentiated liposarcoma and should prompt a search for a well-differentiated component. In case of its complete absence, it remains to be determined whether these tumours represent dedifferentiated liposarcoma without a well-differentiated component instead of UPS {19574885; 24525499; 12640106}.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show high cellularity and tissue fragments of spindle cells in a storiform pattern, multinucleated pleomorphic cells with marked nuclear atypia, coarse chromatin, macronucleoli, and plentiful mitoses, seen singly or in tissue fragments, along with multinucleated giant cells, haemorrhage, and necrosis. Neoplastic cells can engulf inflammatory cells. It is a diagnosis by exclusion requiring correlation with site and clinical features, cell blocks for immunohistochemistry, FISH, and next-generation sequencing {14648788}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analysis may help exclude other entities in the differential diagnosis of UPS {19290004; 15221942; 12640106; 21412072}. The presence of MDM2 and CDK4 amplification may suggest a dedifferentiated liposarcoma. Mutations in BRAF, NRAS, KIT, or NF1; a UV-induced mutation signature; and/or high tumour mutation burden support undifferentiated melanoma especially in the absence of melanocytic differentiation or history of melanoma {33428337; 36990277}. PRDM10 gene rearrangements and CADM3 expression may be seen in superficial tumours with UPS-like morphology but low mitotic activity; these should be considered superficial CD34-positive pleomorphic fibroblastic tumours {25516889}. SMARCA4 inactivation (causing loss of SMARCA4 [BRG1] expression) and thoracic location should prompt a diagnosis of SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated thoracic tumour {26343384; 30451731}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindle, pleomorphic, epithelioid morphology; absence of any morphological or immunohistochemical feature of specific lines of differentiation.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

The majority of UPSs are morphologically high-grade. The reported 5-year overall survival rate is 50–75%, with local recurrence in 20–30% and a metastatic rate of 30–40% {18584203; 35571669; 31364245}. UPS in children and young adults and UPS with superficial location have a better disease-specific and progression-free survival {20055602; 9212841; 16822084; 40222653; 40057991}. Radiation-associated UPS, those of large size, those with TERT alterations, and those in elderly patients do worse {18584203; 36313661; 31364245; 40222653}. Higher infiltration of the tumour with immune cells is associated with better outcome and predicts good response to immune checkpoint inhibition while possibly associated with poor response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy {31942077; 36313661; 40007136; 39444039; 33254023; 29100075;40601026}.
