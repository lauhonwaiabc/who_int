1.5.1.2 Intramuscular vascular anomalies

Definition

Intramuscular vascular anomalies (formerly known as intramuscular haemangioma) are a heterogeneous group of slow-flow and fast-flow vascular lesions arising within skeletal muscle, sometimes associated with overgrowth of other elements, like fibrous and adipose tissues.

ICD-O coding

9132/0

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Not recommended: intramuscular angiolipoma.

Subtype(s)

Venous malformation (VM); fibroadipose vascular anomaly (FAVA); intramuscular fast-flow vascular anomaly (IFVA); arteriovenous malformation (AVM); PTEN hamartoma of soft tissue (PHOST).

Localization

Intramuscular vascular anomalies most commonly involve the lower extremities, mainly the thigh and calf, followed by the head and neck, upper limbs, and trunk. Involvement of several muscle compartments and surrounding tissues may be seen.

Clinical features

Typical presentation is a slow-growing mass that is often painful, particularly after exercise and specially associated with lesions involving the limbs. Large tumours may rarely be associated with osteolysis. Venous malformation (VM) and fibroadipose vascular anomaly (FAVA) are slow-flow, whereas intramuscular fast-flow vascular anomaly (IFVA), arteriovenous malformation (AVM), and PTEN hamartoma of soft tissue (PHOST) are fast-flow on imaging. Characteristic phenotypic features are present in most individuals with PHOST {22446940}.

Epidemiology

These lesions are relatively rare; as many as 90% of reported cases are in older children, adolescents, and young adults, with equal sex predilection {5061701; 2013460; 5069204}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most VMs are caused by somatic variants in TEK (60%) or PIK3CA (20%) {29217067; 19079259; 26637981}, most FAVAs by variants in PIK3CA, and most IFVAs and AVMs by variants in MAP2K1 or KRAS {37099413}. Germline or somatic variants in RASA1 and germline variants in EPHB4 are found in a subgroup of AVMs from patients with capillary malformation–arteriovenous malformation syndrome {31300548}. Germline variants in PTEN are present in PHOST {22446940}. Lesions indistinguishable from PHOST have been reported in association with somatic PIK3CA variants and are referred to as soft tissue angiomatosis {31630434}.

Mutations in TEK or PIK3CA lead to upregulation of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling, which is essential for angiogenesis and vasculogenesis {27519652; 29217067; 39628347}. Upregulation of RAS/MAPK signalling occurs in most cases of fast-flow vascular anomalies {25728774; 28687708}.

Macroscopic appearance

VMs are typically well-circumscribed, spongy lesions with minimal intervening fibrofatty tissue or skeletal muscle. FAVA generally shows an infiltrative growth pattern, characterized by discrete vascular nodules embedded within abundant fibrofatty stroma, sometimes replacing the muscle compartment. IFVA tends to form well-defined masses with pale-red vascular areas and variable amounts of fat. AVMs are composed of large vascular channels and fibroadipose tissue that infiltrate skeletal muscle. PHOST lesions are poorly defined, featuring red vascular nodules, prominent veins, and a variable fibrofatty component.

Histopathology

VMs form a spongiform network of irregularly shaped venous channels of varying calibres and have sharply demarcated borders with minimal to no interposed skeletal muscle or fibrofatty tissue {12447041; 31279623} (see section Venous malformations).

FAVA lesions demonstrate extensive fibrofatty stroma containing nodular clusters of thin-walled venous channels, often budding from larger dysmorphic veins. Peripheral nerves may be surrounded by fibrous tissue and infiltrated by abnormal veins. Lymphoid aggregates are frequent, and occasional foci of metaplastic bone, dysmorphic lymphatic channels, and indeterminate vascular clusters with coiled lumina and concentric muscular layers may be present {31279623; 31181028; 37099413}.

IFVA consists of capillary-like vascular channels arranged in lobules or cords associated with large veins and arteries with thick walls and indeterminate vessels. Veins are enlarged, many having irregularly thickened walls. Arteries may show enlargement with loss of internal elastic lamina, intimal cushions, and medial muscular hyperplasia. Vessels of indeterminate type and a variable amount of adipose tissue may be present {31486960}.

AVM is poorly defined, composed of variable-sized veins, arteries, and indeterminate vessels. Arteries show loss of internal elastic lamina, and veins intimal hyperplasia {36309042} (see section Arteriovenous malformation).

PHOST is characterized by a variably fibroadipocytic and myxoid stroma, along with nodules of dysmorphic venous channels that show variable muscularization, complex lumina, and surrounding arteries. Additional features include enlarged arteries (sometimes with visible arteriovenous connections); a diffuse increase in small arteries, veins, and vessels of indeterminate type; small, discrete myxoid areas containing rapidly branching small arteries; lymphoid aggregates; occasional metaplastic bone; and clusters of indeterminate vessels with coiled, thickened walls, concentric muscular layers, and narrow lumina {22446940}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of pathogenic variants in TEK, PIK3CA, MAP2K1, KRAS, RASA1, EPHB4, or PTEN may be of help in confirming the type of malformation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

For VM: spongiform clusters of dysmorphic venous channels For FAVA: prominent fibroadipose tissue, nodules of back-to-back venous channels, and variably thick-walled veins For IFVA: nodular aggregates of small vascular channels along with variably thick-walled veins For AVM: a combination of abnormal arteries with disruption of internal elastic lamina, large thick veins, and indeterminate vessels For PHOST: abundant fibromyxoid and adipose tissue and aggregates of abnormal vessels including arteries, veins, and indeterminate vessels

Desirable:

For all subtypes: compatible imaging For VM: demonstration of somatic TEK or PIK3CA variant For FAVA: demonstration of somatic PIK3CA variant For IFVA: demonstration of somatic MAP2K1 or KRAS variant For AVM: demonstration of somatic variants in MAP2K1 or KRAS and germline or somatic variants in RASA1 or EPHB4 For PHOST: demonstration of germline or somatic PTEN variant

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Treatment depends on the type, presentation, and severity of the malformation; it often involves a combination of approaches such as surgical excision, sclerotherapy, embolization, cryotherapy, or targeted medical therapies.
