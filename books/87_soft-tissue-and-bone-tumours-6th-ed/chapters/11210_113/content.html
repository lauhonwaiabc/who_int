1.12.10 Pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour of soft parts

Definition

Pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour (PHAT) is a locally recurring, non-metastasizing neoplasm, characterized by ectatic, circumferentially hyalinized, fibrin-filled blood vessels, associated with a proliferation of mitotically inactive pleomorphic cells. Haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumour–like areas are frequently present.

ICD-O coding

8802/1 Pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH2193 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

PHAT develops most often in subcutaneous tissue of the lower extremities, mainly in the region of the ankle/foot and rarely as a deep-seated soft tissue mass in locations such as perineum, buttock, and arm {8540605; 15489645}.

Clinical features

PHAT most often presents as a longstanding subcutaneous mass. Many cases clinically mimic a vascular tumour, such as haemangioma or Kaposi sarcoma.

Epidemiology

In 93 reported cases, the described age range is broad, varying from 10 to 89 years of age (median: 54 years). A slight female predilection is seen {29392614}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Many PHATs demonstrate peripheral zones of haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumour (HFLT), and foci resembling PHAT are frequently present in HFLT, findings suggesting that PHAT and HFLT may represent different manifestations of a single entity {24705316; 15489645; 28375867}. This concept is also supported by genetic data, with TGFBR3 and/or OGA (MGEA5) rearrangements found in substantial subsets of both PHAT and HFLT {24705316; 26980036}. Tumours showing histological overlap between myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma and HFLT have been reported {28375867; 26980036; 24705316; 29979612; 21717526}, suggesting a link between these two tumour types as well, but this remains incompletely understood and controversial.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, most PHATs appear lobulated, with a cut surface that varies from whitish-tan to maroon. Cystic or myxoid areas may be seen. Tumour size ranges between 50 and 100 mm {8540605; 15489645}.

Histopathology

At low-power magnification, PHAT is most notable for clusters of variably sized, thin-walled, ectatic blood vessels scattered throughout the lesion {8540605; 15489645}. These ectatic vessels are surrounded by a thick rim of amorphous eosinophilic material, often with associated hyalinosis. Organizing thrombi with papillary endothelial hyperplasia may be present. Tumour cells are arranged in sheets or fascicles and show epithelioid to spindled morphology, intracytoplasmic haemosiderin pigment, and at least multifocal nuclear pleomorphism with nuclear pseudoinclusions. Importantly, mitotic activity is very low. A mixed inflammatory infiltrate is frequently present, and psammomatous calcifications are rarely seen. At the periphery, PHAT may show a variably myxoid proliferation of haemosiderin-laden, cytologically bland spindle cells with occasional nuclear pseudoinclusions, growing in an infiltrative fashion through adipose tissue – changes identical to those seen in HFLT (early PHAT) {15489645; 28375867}. Extremely rare PHATs showing mixed HFLT-like and classic morphology have shown morphological progression to mitotically active, pleomorphic myxoid sarcoma (myxofibrosarcoma-like) {24705316; 26841226; 17493176; 22024990}. Immunohistochemical expression of CD34 is consistently observed.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: clusters of ectatic hyalinized vessels surrounded by pleomorphic, haemosiderin-laden cells with nuclear pseudoinclusions; very low to absent mitotic activity; peripheral component of HFLT often seen.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Roughly 50% of resected PHATs recur locally. Such local recurrences are usually curable with re-excision. The natural history of the extremely rare PHATs showing morphological progression to myxoid sarcoma is not well defined.
