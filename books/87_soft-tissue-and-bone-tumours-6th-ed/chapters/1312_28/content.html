1.3.12 Desmoplastic fibroblastoma

Definition

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma is a benign, paucicellular, hypovascular neoplasm composed of bland spindle or stellate fibroblasts within abundant collagenous stroma.

ICD-O coding

8810/0 Fibroma, NOS

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH2ZF3 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin or soft tissue & Desmoplastic fibroblastoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: collagenous fibroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common sites are the upper arm, shoulder, lower limb, back, and forearm; tumours may also arise on the hands and feet {7661281; 8832562; 9670823; 27442992}.

Clinical features

The tumour typically presents as an asymptomatic, painless, slow-growing subcutaneous mass, but fascial and skeletal muscle involvement is relatively common. Invasion of bone is rare {25841856; 32547801}.

Epidemiology

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma is a rare tumour that predominantly affects adults, with a median age of 50–60 years {37666661}. M:F ratio varies from 2.5:1 to 3:1 {29271377}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Chromosomal karyotyping has revealed rearrangements involving chromosome 11q12 locus including t(2;11)(q31;q12) {15036892; 22137488; 17894801}. Microarray analysis and quantitative real-time PCR showed elevated FOSL1 mRNA expression in desmoplastic fibroblastomas with 11q12 rearrangement {22411068}. Desmoplastic fibroblastoma usually harbours FOSL1 gene rearrangements, less often FOS {36426824}. Overexpression of FOSL1 has also been confirmed by immunohistochemistry, with constant diffuse and strong nuclear positivity {27442992}. There is high concordance between gene rearrangements and immunoreactivity for FOSL1 or FOS {36426824}. Both FOSL1 and FOS belong to the FOS family and possess a proteasome degradation motif conserved within the family members. It has been speculated that disruption of the proteasome degradation motif as a result of gene rearrangement reduces degradation of FOSL1 and FOS proteins, thereby preserving their functional activity and immunoreactivity {36426824}.

Macroscopic appearance

Desmoplastic fibroblastomas most often measure 10–40 mm in greatest dimension, but examples > 100 mm and as large as 200 mm have been reported. Grossly, the lesions are unencapsulated and well-circumscribed, forming oval, fusiform, or discoid masses. The tumours have a firm, cartilage-like consistency and a homogeneous pearl-grey colour on cut sections without necrosis or haemorrhage.

Histopathology

Although well demarcated grossly, most tumours microscopically infiltrate subcutaneous fat, and approximately 25% extend into skeletal muscle {7661281; 8832562; 9670823}. Rare examples are purely intramuscular. The lesions have abundant collagenous or myxocollagenous matrix with hypovascularity. Cellularity ranges from low to moderate, and the neoplastic cells tend to be uniformly distributed within the extracellular matrix. The lesional cells are stellate, bipolar, and spindled, and they have uniform, bland nuclei with distinct small nucleoli. Mitotic figures are uncommon. Rare examples have focal intravascular growth. By immunohistochemistry, SMA may be focally positive, whereas CD34, desmin, and S100 are negative. Diffuse strong nuclear positivity for FOSL1 can distinguish desmoplastic fibroblastoma from histological mimics such as fibroma of tendon sheath {27442992}. However, in most cases, the characteristic histological appearances are sufficient for diagnosing desmoplastic fibroblastoma.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: hypocellular and hypovascular tumour with abundant collagenous stroma; bland spindled and stellate cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma is benign and rarely recurs {9670823; 36426824}.
