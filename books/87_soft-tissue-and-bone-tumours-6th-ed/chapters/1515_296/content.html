1.5.1.5 Spindle cell haemangioma

Definition

Spindle cell haemangioma (SCH) is a benign neoplasm composed of spindled and vacuolated endothelial cells and ectatic vascular spaces.

ICD-O coding

9120/0

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Not recommended: spindle cell haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

SCH is usually superficially located in the subcutis or dermis; it has a preference for the distal extremities, especially the hands, although other locations, including the oral cavity, have been described. As many as half of the cases are intravascular {28477885}.

Clinical features

SCH presents as an asymptomatic, bluish nodule, 1–2 cm in size, developing over many years {28477885}. SCH is associated with Maffucci syndrome, and can also occur in the context of lymphoedema, early-onset varicose veins, and Klippel–Trénaunay syndrome {7661276; 2071088; 23485734; 8827025}.

Epidemiology

This tumour occurs at all ages (8–78 years, median: 32 years), with no sex predilection {8827025}.

Etiology

SCH is associated with Maffucci syndrome in a subset of cases {7661276; 23485734}.

Pathogenesis

As many as 94% of sporadic lesions as well as those associated with Maffucci syndrome have IDH1 p.(Arg132Cys) (R132C) mutations and, to a lesser extent, mutations at the p.Arg172 position in IDH2 {23485734; 22057234; 28845532}. Frequent somatic alterations in 2p22.3, 2q24.3, and 14q11.2 are also found in SCH and enchondromas occurring in patients with Maffucci syndrome {25565925}.

Macroscopic appearance

SCH appears as a circumscribed haemorrhagic nodule in the dermis or subcutis.

Histopathology

The tumours are often well-circumscribed, mostly involving the dermis or subcutis. Around half of the tumours are intravascular. They consist of two components: irregular, dilated, thin-walled, congested, cavernous-like vascular channels admixed with more solid areas of spindle cells. The cavernous spaces are lined by bland and flat endothelial cells and may contain thrombi, with or without dystrophic calcification (phleboliths). The solid spindle cell areas contain bland spindle-shaped cells with pericytic and fibroblastic features, and may contain slit-like vascular spaces as well as vacuolated epithelioid endothelial cells (blister cells) that may resemble adipocytes. Spindle cells are also seen in the cavernous vessel septations. Mitotic activity and nuclear atypia are minimal. At the periphery of the lesion, a cluster of large thick- or thin-walled, malformed vessels can be seen {7496504}.

The endothelial cells within the vascular channels and the epithelioid vacuolated cells show positive immunostaining for endothelial markers. Spindle cells may show focal positivity for SMA and/or desmin {7496504}.

Cytology

FNABs are not routinely performed.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cavernous vascular spaces admixed with more solid spindle cell areas; vacuolated endothelial cells; minimal nuclear atypia; and mitotic activity.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

SCHs are benign with a slow growth rate and an excellent prognosis. They do not metastasize, although recurrence has been described after simple excision in as many as 58% of patients {8827025}, which may be because of contiguous spread along an involved vessel {2071088; 8827025}.
