1.5.7 Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma

Definition

Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma (PILA) is an uncommon, rarely metastasizing, superficial lymphatic vascular tumour, showing tufting and hobnail endothelial proliferations within lymphatic channel lumina, in a background of lymphatic vascular proliferation or lymphangioma.

ICD-O coding

9135/1 Papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma.

ICD-11 coding

2F72.Y & XH4SY7 Other specified neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of skin & papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma of unspecified site.

Related terminology

Not recommended: Dabska tumour; endovascular papillary angioendothelioma; hobnail haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

Not defined.

Localization

Most PILAs involve the proximal extremities, especially the buttock or thigh, and less commonly the distal extremities or trunk, head and neck, intra-abdominal sites, or parenchymal sites (including testis) {3444784; 8522288; 21695802; 10971050}. Intraosseous locations are rarely reported {10478659; 10197456; 24063649; 29423171}. Some arise in association with pre-existing vascular malformations / lymphatic anomalies {9302935;8806966; 5343389}, including lymphangioma circumscriptum {18095998}.

Clinical features

There is a slight female predilection (M:F ratio: ~0.7:1). PILA presents as a slow-growing asymptomatic cutaneous induration, plaque, or rarely nodule, often with unremarkable overlying skin.

Epidemiology

PILA is exceedingly rare, with < 50 reported cases {5343389; 10478659; 32151408}. It is difficult to estimate the exact incidence of this tumour, as a considerable percentage of reported cases appear instead to represent papillary endothelial hyperplasia, angiosarcoma with papillary growth, and lymphatic malformations with papillary endothelial proliferation {38497350}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are poorly defined and usually involve dermis and subcutaneous tissue {10478659}. Grossly, greyish-white to pink streaks or cystic change in otherwise normal dermis and subcutis may be present, without haemorrhage or necrosis. Tumour size ranges from 10 to > 400 mm (mean: 70 mm) {10478659}.

Histopathology

PILA is a poorly delineated dermal and/or subcutaneous proliferation of lymphatic channels showing variable intraluminal papillary tuft proliferation, with hyaline cores and intermixed lymphocytes with proteinaceous fluid. A lymphatic vascular malformation is often present nearby. The cavernous or slit-like lymphatic vascular channels are lined by columnar, matchstick-like, or hobnail endothelial cells. The lesional cells have eosinophilic cytoplasm and round uniform nuclei with indistinct nucleoli; they lack cytological atypia and mitoses {10478659}. Tumour cells express panendothelial markers, such as CD31, ERG, and less often CD34, as well as lymphatic endothelial markers, such as podoplanin, VEGFR3, and PROX1 {10478659; 22067331; 10971050}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: poorly defined lymphatic vessels with stromal and intraluminal lymphocytes and proteinaceous fluid; intralymphatic, intraluminal hobnail proliferation of polarized matchstick-like columnar endothelial cells; expression of panendothelial and lymphatic markers.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most patients have excellent prognosis after complete excision {10478659}. Lymph node metastases are extremely rare, and only a single death from disease has been reported {5343389; 9302935}.
