1.12.27 Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour

Definition

Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour (ERT) is an aggressive malignant neoplasm, mainly affecting infants and children, that consists of characteristic rounded or polygonal neoplastic cells with glassy eosinophilic hyaline-like inclusions, eccentric nuclei, and macronucleoli. Morphologically and genetically identical tumours also arise in the kidney (malignant rhabdoid tumour of kidney) and brain (termed atypical teratoid rhabdoid tumour). The majority are characterized by biallelic alterations of the SMARCB1 gene, leading to loss of expression of SMARCB1 (INI1).

ICD-O coding

8963/3 Rhabdoid tumour NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH3RF3 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Malignant rhabdoid tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: rhabdoid tumour of soft tissue; malignant rhabdoid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ERT most frequently arises in deep, axial locations such as the neck, paraspinal region, perineal region, thorax, abdominal cavity, retroperitoneum, and pelvic cavity {1712749; 8545590; 9930572; 16704491}. It is also well documented in the extremities (especially the thigh), cutaneous sites, and visceral organs such as the liver (the single most common visceral location), thymus, genitourinary tract, and gastrointestinal system {23816299; 21744471}.

Clinical features

Most ERTs present as a rapidly enlarging soft tissue mass, with associated clinical symptoms depending on the site (e.g. primary organ) of involvement. Occasional cases present with multiple cutaneous nodules {24284868}. Some ERTs, particularly in infants, present with disseminated disease without an obvious primary tumour – such cases are often associated with rhabdoid tumour predisposition {21108436}.

Epidemiology

Extrarenal rhabdoid tumour is exceedingly rare, with reports largely confined to infants and children, primarily in those aged < 3 years. Among fetal and neonatal patients, ERT is more commonly reported than tumours in the kidney or brain {32105509; 31883020; 33249395; 27082136}.

Etiology

Familial cases are typically associated with germline mutations in SMARCB1 (INI1, hSNF5, BAF47) at 22q11.23 {9892189; 9671307; 21108436}. Germline mutations or deletions in SMARCB1 (rhabdoid tumour predisposition syndrome 1) are present in approximately 13% of patients with ERT {29706634}. Patients with SMARCB1 germline alterations typically present in early childhood, and they are at increased risk for other soft tissue tumours, notably schwannomas, in later decades of life. A very small percentage of rhabdoid tumours are associated with SMARCA4 variants, which may be germline (rhabdoid tumour predisposition syndrome 2).

Pathogenesis

The vast majority of ERTs arise as a consequence of homozygous inactivation of the SMARCB1 gene in chromosome band 22q11.2, in either a somatic or germline setting. SMARCB1 is a tumour suppressor gene encoding a core protein subunit of the SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex. Approximately 98% of ERTs, rhabdoid tumours of the kidney, and atypical teratoid rhabdoid tumours show genomic alterations of both SMARCB1 copies, including coding sequence variants, partial or whole gene deletions, and copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity events that unmask a recessive allele on the remaining homologue {23816299}. ERTs have a particularly high incidence of SMARCB1 homozygous deletions {22797305}. Consistent with SMARCB1 functioning as a classic tumour suppressor gene, initiating germline mutations or deletions in SMARCB1 serve as the first hit in patients with a genetic predisposition to rhabdoid tumour development. Such patients are also at risk for rhabdoid tumours of the kidney and atypical teratoid rhabdoid tumours. Rare rhabdoid tumours with retained SMARCB1 expression may alternatively harbour mutation and/or loss of the SMARCA4 gene at 19p13.2 {20137775}. Malignant rhabdoid tumours of childhood typically show simple genomes, with SMARCB1 loss representing essentially the sole recurrent event, with generally low rates of additional coding mutations in primary tumours {22797305}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours are unencapsulated, > 50 mm, and usually soft, with a grey-tan cut surface and frequent foci of necrosis.

Histopathology

ERTs are infiltrative, hypercellular masses characterized by rhabdoid cells with large vesicular rounded to bean-shaped nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant cytoplasm. Tumour cells are arranged in sheets or in a solid trabecular pattern, often with areas of discohesion, in a variably fibromyxoid stroma. Many tumour cells have juxtanuclear eosinophilic, PAS-positive, diastase-resistant hyaline inclusions or globules. Although the cells are generally uniform with minimal to sometimes mild nuclear pleomorphism, there can be heterogeneity within and between tumours, with variability in cell size, and focal cellular spindling. Some cases show a predominant undifferentiated small round cell histology, with only a small number of rhabdoid cells {27338635; 27356182}. There is prominent mitotic activity and necrosis.

Immunohistochemically, most express epithelial markers such as keratins (often with dot-like expression) and EMA {9930572; 16704491}, with frequent but variable expression of neural markers, such as synaptophysin. Less commonly, the cells express MSA and focal S100 {9930572; 27338635}. SALL4 and glypican-3 (GPC3) expression is also frequent {24827994; 25479928; 23084579}. ERT is typically negative for CD34. Desmin expression is unusual and should prompt myogenin and MYOD1 immunohistochemistry, which are negative in ERT. Characteristically, rhabdoid tumours show loss of nuclear SMARCB1 (INI1, BAF47) expression {15489652; 21934399}.

Cytology

Cytology preparations show variable cellularity, with large cells with high N:C ratio, eccentric cytoplasm, and large nuclei with macronucleoli and fine chromatin; the cells are seen singly and in loosely cohesive tissue fragments {1322263}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: infants and young children; rhabdoid cell morphology; loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression.

Staging

There is no defined staging system for ERT.

Prognosis and prediction

Regardless of tumour site, patient outcome is dismal. Because of the rarity of ERT, there is still no defined standard of care. Multimodal management with surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation has shown only modest improvements in overall survival, with median survival of approximately one year {27639430; 30914466} and an overall 5-year survival rate of < 15% {18506766; 17828773}. Advanced tumour stage and age at diagnosis of  ≤ 12 months have been significantly associated with poorer survival {30914466; 19798737}. Patients with hepatic rhabdoid tumours have worse survival than patients with those at other extracranial and extrarenal sites {21744471; 27082136}.

There have been proposals to stratify ERT by clinical and genetic risk factors, with patients presenting with the following features – metastases, lack of gross total tumour resection, and/or SMARCB1 germline variants – belonging to a high-risk group, with recommendation for management with novel therapeutic strategies within international phase I/II trials {33249395; 39120581}.
