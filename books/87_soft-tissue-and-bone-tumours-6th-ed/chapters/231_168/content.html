2.3.1 Desmoplastic fibroma of bone

Definition

Desmoplastic fibroma of bone is an extremely rare and locally aggressive bone tumour composed of bland spindle cells set in abundant collagenous stroma. It is a diagnosis of exclusion.

ICD-O coding

8823/1

ICD-11 coding

2F7B & XH6YK5 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of bone and articular cartilage & Desmoplastic fibroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: desmoid tumour of bone; intraosseous desmoid fibromatosis.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Desmoplastic fibroma may involve any bone but has most commonly been described in the mandible, followed by the long bones (femur, radius, tibia) and pelvic bones.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Some tumours are found incidentally in asymptomatic patients; however, the majority of patients present with a longstanding history of pain or bone deformity. In approximately 10% of patients, the initial presentation is a pathological fracture.

Imaging

Radiographically, desmoplastic fibroma appears as a well-defined, lobulated, radiolucent lesion that may cause expansion of the affected bone. Intralesional trabeculation is often present. Larger lesions may destroy cortical bone and extend into the adjacent soft tissue, which is best visualized on MRI. On MRI, desmoplastic fibroma typically appears as a low signal–intensity lesion on both T1- and T2-weighted images. Increased uptake may be seen on bone scintigraphy or 18F-FDG PET.

Epidemiology

Desmoplastic fibroma accounts for < 0.1% of all primary bone tumours. The tumour mainly affects adolescents and young adults, with near-equal sex distribution {30538394; 26909301}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

Because of its high collagen content, desmoplastic fibroma is a firm, tan-white tumour with a whorled architecture. The tumour borders are well-defined and may show scalloped margins, even when extending into adjacent soft tissue.

Histopathology

Desmoplastic fibroma is generally well-demarcated. When infiltration is present, it is typically observed at the periphery of the tumour. Histologically, it is a hypocellular lesion composed of fascicles of bland, nondescript spindle cells embedded in a collagenous matrix. Mitoses are rare, and necrosis and lesional bone formation are absent. The fibroblastic spindle cells exhibit variable immunoreactivity for SMA.

Differential diagnosis

Fibrous dysplasia, central low-grade osteosarcoma, and intraosseous desmoid-type fibromatosis should be excluded.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Desmoplastic fibroma is a diagnosis of exclusion. GNAS variant testing can be used to rule out fibrous dysplasia; lack of CDK4/MDM2 amplification argues against central low-grade osteosarcoma, although only 30% of these tumours are amplified {26248895; 30159138}. Most desmoplastic fibromas do not harbour specific genetic alterations {16153468}, but APC and CTNNB1 mutations {24664543}, as well as nuclear β-catenin immunostaining have rarely been reported {16153468; 30159138; 31901437}. These cases should be classified as intraosseous desmoid fibromatosis rather than as desmoplastic fibroma.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland spindle cell proliferation in bone; collagenous background; no lesional bone formation.

Desirable: rule out GNAS mutation and MDM2 amplification.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Prognosis and prediction are difficult to assess because so few cases have been reported. Desmoplastic fibroma is a slowly progressive and locally aggressive tumour, which may recur after curettage or intralesional excision {23459513}.
