1.3.34 Infantile fibrosarcoma

Definition

Infantile fibrosarcoma (IFS) is a locally aggressive fibroblastic tumour, most frequently occurring in infancy, characterized by an ETV6::NTRK3 fusion or other alterations leading to oncogenic activation of kinase genes.

ICD-O coding

8814/3 Infantile fibrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH7BC6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, of other specified sites & Infantile fibrosarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: congenital fibrosarcoma; cellular congenital mesoblastic nephroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

IFS most commonly involves the soft tissues of the extremities, followed by the trunk and the head/neck. Tumours in intra-abdominal, retroperitoneal, or visceral sites are less frequent but do occur {974993; 21843073; 25150723; 32669612}. Analogous tumours in the kidney are designated as cellular (or occasionally mixed-type) congenital mesoblastic nephroma {9823307; 29915264}.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a painless mass or exophytic nodule; rapid growth may occur {974993}. Approximately one third of cases are congenital {974993}. Imaging, typically ultrasound in young children, demonstrates a heterogeneous enhancing mass with relatively nonspecific characteristics. Dilated vessels and haemorrhage may be present, resembling a vascular tumour or malformation; in these cases, the patients may (rarely) present with Kasabach–Merritt phenomenon {16918626; 24706181}.

Epidemiology

Infantile fibrosarcoma is rare, with an estimated annual incidence of 4.3 cases per 1 million children {32155583}. Studies of paediatric patients show that the median age is 3–4 months {974993; 19917847; 30585824}, with the majority (75–80%) of cases occurring in the first year of life and no sex predilection. Fewer than 10% of cases occur in older children and adults {28877062; 30585824; 30520818}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Oncogenesis of IFS occurs through the activation of kinase signalling, most commonly from in-frame gene fusions or (less frequently) through activating variants {29915264}. The most frequent genetic alteration is the classic ETV6::NTRK3 gene fusion resulting from the t(12;15)(p13;q25) chromosomal translocation; other fusion partners with NTRK3 may occur, including EML4::NTRK3 fusions {29099503; 30585824}. Additionally, alternative fusions involving other kinase genes (including NTRK1, NTRK2, RET, MET, RAF1, ALK, FGFR1) fused to a variety of fusion partners {30585824; 28877062; 29915264; 28510278; 31994201; 34843129} have been described. EGFR-kinase domain duplication, first reported in congenital mesoblastic nephroma (CMN), may also occur in IFS {35032896; 32490123; 35801295}. BRAF fusions, complex deletions, and point mutations have been described, all thought to result in the constitutive activation of BRAF {28877062; 29915264}, a downstream effector molecule in the MAPK pathway. Similar genetic aberrations are present in CMN, which has a substantial morphological overlap with IFS {9823307; 29099503; 29915264; 31994201}.

IFS/CMN with and without ETV6::NTRK3 fusions show the same pattern of receptor tyrosine kinase activation, and they cluster together by unsupervised hierarchical gene expression cluster analysis, which suggests a common pathogenesis {22374738}. Recent studies evaluating the methylation profile of kinase-driven spindle cell tumours to include IFS have shown variable results, and this is probably an evolving area. However, the largest study to date, which included both paediatric and adult patients, demonstrated that IFSs with the canonical ETV6::NTRK3 translocation and those with alternative fusions cluster together {38332737}. RAF1 tumours with CD34 and/or S100 expression also cluster with BRAF-altered IFS-like tumours by RNA unsupervised hierarchical cluster analysis, again suggestive of these representing related entities and/or a common pathogenesis {30276917}. See also section Kinase-altered spindle cell neoplasms.

Macroscopic appearance

The size of tumours varies, although they can be quite large relative to the patient’s size; the median size is about 50–60 mm, but tumours can range from 10 to > 150 mm. The gross appearance is typically of a poorly circumscribed tumour, infiltrating into the surrounding soft tissues. A subset of tumours may have a thin, fibrous pseudocapsule {561651; 30585824}.

Histopathology

IFS may demonstrate a spectrum of histological patterns across cases and within a given case. Most frequently, IFS is a cellular, monomorphic neoplasm composed of primitive, ovoid/round to spindled cells, arranged either in compact, haphazard sheets or in long fascicles. Often tumours demonstrate a prominent ectatic or haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern. The background stroma may range from myxoid to heavily collagenous. A subset of cases have decreased cellularity with a collagenized matrix; these tumours may resemble myofibromatosis and/or fibromatosis {22420727; 30585824; 37265193; 34843129}. Prominent chronic inflammation may also be present {974993}. Mitotic counts range widely and are without prognostic significance; no atypical mitotic forms are present {974993; 8159611; 19917847; 30585824}. Necrosis also may be present, without prognostic implications.

The IFS immunoprofile is nonspecific, with variable expression of SMA, S100, CD34, and (rarely) desmin {22420727; 30585824}. Those tumours harbouring NTRK gene rearrangements typically show expression for pan-TRK antibodies {29863809; 29683818}, whereas tumours with ALK gene rearrangements show expression for ALK antibodies {34843129}.

Cytology

Aspirates show monomorphic spindle to ovoid cells with oval, mildly pleomorphic nuclei with fine chromatin and indistinct nucleoli, arranged haphazardly or in vague fascicles in cohesive tissue fragments, with stripped nuclei in the background and (occasionally) necrosis {10439797}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Gene fusions or activating variants involving receptor tyrosine kinase encoding genes (e.g. ETV6::NTRK3) or downstream effector molecule (BRAF) fusions or activating alterations may be demonstrated by various molecular modalities {10895816; 11684968; 30585824}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a primitive ovoid/round cell or spindle cell tumour, often arranged in sheets or fascicles; thin-walled, ectatic, branching vessels and/or mixed chronic inflammation.

Desirable: ETV6::NTRK3 or other alterations of kinase genes.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

IFS is a locally aggressive, infrequently metastatic tumour with an overall favourable prognosis. With standard treatment regimens, including combinations of surgery and/or cytotoxic chemotherapy, the 10-year overall survival rate is approximately 90% {974993; 561651; 19917847}. Spontaneous tumour regression is rare. Local recurrence rates range from 25% to 40%, with incomplete resection associated with an increased risk of recurrence {19917847; 30585824}. However, given the size and location of many tumours, complete surgical excision can entail significant morbidity. The proportion of patients developing metastasis is approximately 8–15% {30585824; 19917847; 561651}. Targeted kinase inhibitor therapies are being used with increased frequency and are changing the clinical course and the treatment regimens employed in patients with IFS {32784118}.
