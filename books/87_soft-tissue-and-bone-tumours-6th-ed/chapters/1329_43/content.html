1.3.29 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour (IMT) is an intermediate, rarely metastasizing myofibroblastic neoplasm composed of plump spindle cells accompanied by an inflammatory infiltrate of plasma cells, lymphocytes, and/or eosinophils.

ICD-O coding

8825/1 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH66Z0 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Myofibroblastic tumour NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: plasma cell granuloma; inflammatory pseudotumour; inflammatory myofibrohistiocytic proliferation; omental-mesenteric myxoid hamartoma; inflammatory fibrosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma (EIMS)

Provisional: myxoid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma (MIMS)

Localization

IMT shows a wide anatomical distribution, most frequently arising in the abdominal soft tissues, including the mesentery, omentum, retroperitoneum, and pelvis, followed by the lung, mediastinum, head and neck, gastrointestinal tract, and genitourinary tract (including the bladder and uterus) {7611533; 17938159; 11381424; 34001695; 40282503}. Unusual locations include somatic soft tissues, pancreas, liver, CNS, and placenta {7611533; 17938159; 32271187}.

Clinical features

The site of origin determines symptoms such as pain, bleeding, cough, and dyspnoea {7611533; 17938159; 11381424}. As many as one third of patients have a clinical syndrome, possibly cytokine-mediated, of fever, malaise, weight loss, and laboratory abnormalities including microcytic hypochromic anaemia, thrombocytosis, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia, elevated ESR, and elevated C-reactive protein {15602716; 7611533}. Radiological imaging studies reveal a lobulated heterogeneous solid mass with or without calcification {11381424}. Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma (EIMS) is predominantly intra-abdominal and associated with a more aggressive course {21164297; 38155480}. Myxoid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma (MIMS) has been recently described and suggested to be a distinct subtype of IMT (provisional), with more aggressive clinical behaviour, predominantly occurring in the peritoneum and often forming multiple nodules {38717131}.

Epidemiology

IMT is rare and primarily affects infants, children, and young adults, although the age range extends throughout adulthood {17414097; 7611533; 17938159; 31690781}. There is a slight female predominance {35604778}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

IMTs are genetically heterogeneous. In 50–60% of cases of IMT in children and young adults, the tumours harbour clonal cytogenetic rearrangements, involving chromosome band 2p23 that fuse the 3′ kinase region of the ALK gene with various partner genes, including TPM3, TPM4, CLTC, CARS1, ATIC, SEC31A (SEC31L1), PPFIBP1, DCTN1, EML4, PRKAR1A, LMNA, TFG, FN1, HNRNPA1, TIMP3, THBS1, and others in a growing list {11485898; 13679433; 10383129; 10934142; 21430068; 28504207; 24875859; 40282503}. IMT with ALK genomic rearrangement features activation and overexpression of the ALK C-terminal kinase region, which is restricted to the neoplastic myofibroblastic component {11485898; 11406658; 11684952; 10383129; 10934142}.

ROS1 and NTRK3 gene rearrangements are each found in 5–10% of IMTs; TFG::ROS1, YWHAE::ROS1, and ETV6::NTRK3 gene fusions have been reported {24875859; 25612511; 27259007; 25723109; 26647767; 37856763}. Very rare cases have RET or PDGFRB gene rearrangements, or ALK variants {25723109; 24875859; 34551905}. Additional copy-number alterations, including amplification of ALK, as well as mutations in various genes including those of the PI3K/AKT pathway can be found {34551905; 38155480}.

EIMS is often associated with RANBP2::ALK or RRBP1::ALK fusions {21164297; 27874193}.

MIMS frequently harbours gene rearrangement of PDGFRB, PDGFRA, or JAK1 {38717131}.

Macroscopic appearance

IMT is a nodular, circumscribed, or multinodular mass with a tan, whorled, fleshy, or myxoid cut surface and variable haemorrhage, necrosis, and calcification {17414097; 7611533; 17938159}. The diameter of the lesion ranges from 10 to 200 mm or more, with a median size of 50–60 mm.

Histopathology

The morphology of IMT can be heterogeneous. Spindled fibroblastic-myofibroblastic cells and inflammatory cells form three basic histological patterns {17414097; 7611533; 17938159}. The first is a myxoid pattern, which consists of loosely arranged plump or spindled myofibroblasts in an oedematous myxoid background with abundant blood vessels and an infiltrate of plasma cells, lymphocytes, and eosinophils, mimicking granulation tissue or a reactive process. The second is a hypercellular pattern, which is characterized by a compact fascicular spindle cell proliferation with variable myxoid and collagenous stroma and inflammatory infiltrate. The third is a hypocellular fibrous pattern, which features hyalinized collagenous stroma with lower cellularity of spindle cells and a relatively sparse inflammatory infiltrate, mimicking desmoid fibromatosis. Dystrophic calcifications and osseous metaplasia are occasionally seen. One or more patterns are often seen within a single tumour. Myofibroblasts with vesicular nuclei, 1–3 small nucleoli, and eosinophilic cytoplasm are typical and sometimes show a ganglion-like appearance. Necrosis is uncommon. Mitotic activity varies but is generally low. IMT showing leiomyoma-like morphology with less inflammatory infiltrate has been reported as a potential diagnostic pitfall {35426837}.

EIMS is an aggressive IMT subtype with plump epithelioid or histiocytoid tumour cells with vesicular chromatin, prominent nucleoli, and amphophilic or eosinophilic cytoplasm, often admixed with neutrophils in an abundant myxoid stroma {18701132; 21164297}.

MIMS is histologically characterized by bland to mildly atypical spindle cells with myxoid stroma, delicate branching vessels (resembling myxoid liposarcoma), and an inflammatory infiltrate {38717131}.

By immunohistochemistry, IMT displays variable staining for SMA, MSA, calponin, and desmin. Focal keratin immunoreactivity can be seen in as many as 30% of cases. Immunoreactivity for ALK is detectable in 50–60% of cases and correlates well with the presence of ALK gene rearrangement {12218210; 11395553; 11406658; 11684952; 17938159}. Of note, the ALK immunostaining pattern varies depending on the ALK fusion partner; for example, RANBP2::ALK is associated with a nuclear membranous pattern, RRBP1::ALK with a perinuclear accentuated cytoplasmic pattern, and CLTC::ALK with a granular cytoplasmic pattern; many other ALK fusion variants show a diffuse cytoplasmic pattern (most commonly seen in IMT) {11485898; 21164297; 27874193}. Highly sensitive ALK antibody clones (5A4, D5F3) may improve detection of the ALK protein in IMT {21430068; 26647767}, although D5F3 negative cases may be positive for ALK1 and vice versa {34551905}. ROS1-rearranged IMT typically shows cytoplasmic expression of ROS1 {26647767; 25612511}. IMT with ETV6::NTRK3 shows nuclear and cytoplasmic positivity for pan-TRK {31578734}.

Distinctive ALK-rearranged spindle cell tumour with S100 and CD34 expression is separated from IMT and is included in the family of kinase-altered spindle cell neoplasms {36125853}.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show bland spindle cells with oval to occasionally coffee-bean nuclei and small distinct nucleoli, in loosely cohesive tissue fragments and singly, in a variably myxoid background with lymphocytes, plasma cells, and histiocytes. Atypical ganglion-like polygonal cells can be seen {20730898}. Mitoses are scant. EIMS shows high cellularity with epithelioid cells with round nuclei, prominent nucleoli, myxoid stroma, plentiful neutrophils, and branching vessels. Diagnosis relies on ancillary tests {27087029}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In addition to immunohistochemical detection of ALK protein, molecular assays for ALK may be used to confirm the diagnosis but are generally not required {11406658}. Exceptional situations such as inversion of ALK on the same chromosome arm may lead to a false negative FISH result {28490045}. In ALK-negative cases, immunohistochemistry for ROS1 and pan-TRK and/or molecular tests for non-ALK gene rearrangements may be useful {26647767; 24875859; 27259007; 25723109; 31578734}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: fascicles of plump spindle cells with inflammatory infiltrate and a variable collagenous or myxoid stroma.

Desirable: expression of ALK; ALK, ROS1, RET, NTRK, or other gene rearrangements, in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 25% of extrapulmonary IMTs recur, in part depending on anatomical site and resectability {19852031; 7611533; 17938159}. ALK immunoreactivity does not appear to correlate with recurrence {17414097; 15602716; 11406658}. Distant metastases are rare (< 5%) and involve the lungs, brain, liver, and bone. However, reliable prognostic indicators have not been developed for conventional IMT {17414097}. ROS1-rearranged IMTs have an excellent outcome {37856763}. Intra-abdominal EIMS as well as MIMS behave much more aggressively when compared with conventional IMT {21164297; 38155480; 38717131}. IMT with specific kinase fusions may respond to molecular targeted therapy {34551905}.
