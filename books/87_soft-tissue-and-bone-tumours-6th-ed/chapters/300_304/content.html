3.0.0 Haematopoietic and dendritic cell neoplasms of soft tissue and bone: Introduction

Tumours included in this chapter are haematopoietic neoplasms that arise in non-mucosal extranodal soft tissue or involve bone primarily, often presenting as mass-forming and/or destructive osseous lesions. They are typically derived from lymphoid, plasma cell, or histiocytic/dendritic cell lineages. Myeloid neoplasms and other medullary proliferations arising within the bone marrow compartment are excluded from this chapter. It should be noted, however, that virtually any haematolymphoid neoplasm may secondarily involve soft tissue and/or bone in the setting of locally extensive or systemic disease. Accordingly, diagnostic evaluation requires integration of morphological, immunophenotypic, and molecular findings with appropriate imaging and clinical assessment to determine the extent of involvement and to establish whether the lesion represents a primary neoplasm of soft tissue or bone. For haematolymphoid tumours not included in this chapter, the reader is referred to the fifth-edition Haematolymphoid tumours volume of the WHO Classification of Tumours series.
