1.2.12 Atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma

Definition

Atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma (ALT/WDLPS) is a locally aggressive mesenchymal neoplasm composed either entirely or partly of an adipocytic proliferation showing at least focal nuclear atypia in adipocytes and stromal cells.

ICD-O coding

8850/1 Atypical lipomatous tumour

8851/3 Liposarcoma, well-differentiated, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH0RW4 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Atypical lipomatous tumour

2B5H & XH7Y61 Well-differentiated lipomatous tumour, primary site & Liposarcoma, well-differentiated

Related terminology

Not recommended: atypical lipoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ALT/WDLPS occurs most frequently in retroperitoneum or extremities, with ALT most commonly arising in deep soft tissue of proximal extremities (thigh and buttock) and trunk (back and shoulder), while WDLPS is most frequent in retroperitoneum, with rarer occurrences in the paratesticular area and mediastinum {13890373}. Rarer sites also include the head and neck (with tumours previously termed “giant fibrovascular polyps” of oesophagus and hypopharynx, now known to essentially represent ALT/WDLPS), distal extremities, and skin {28984298; 12004355; 18043041; 11598853; 9700369}.

The term “ALT” is therefore used for lesions arising at sites for which complete surgical resection is curative, with “WDLPS” denoting lesions arising in sites such as retroperitoneum, spermatic cord, and mediastinum, which show greater potential for disease progression.

Clinical features

ALT/WDLPS usually present as deep-seated, painless masses that can slowly reach large sizes, particularly in retroperitoneum. Retroperitoneal lesions are often asymptomatic until they reach > 20 cm.

Epidemiology

ALT/WDLPS represents the largest subgroup of adipocytic malignancies, accounting for approximately 40–45% of all liposarcomas in a large case series {13890373}. They almost always arise in adults, predominantly in middle age (peak incidence in the fourth to fifth decades of life) {13890373; 16912210}. Convincing childhood examples are extremely rare {13890373; 31471922} but may be associated with Li–Fraumeni syndrome. There is an equal sex distribution, with the obvious exception of lesions of the spermatic cord {13890373; 16912210}.

Etiology

ALT/WDLPS may be associated with Li–Fraumeni syndrome, but nearly all cases are sporadic. While etiology is still largely unknown, these may develop because of disturbances of adipogenic differentiation in mesenchymal stem cells {40371227}.

Pathogenesis

WDLPS is characterized by supernumerary ring and giant marker chromosomes, typically as the sole change or concomitant with a few other numerical or structural abnormalities {15527898}. Telomeric associations are frequent and may give a false impression of complexity to WDLPS karyotypes {9595041}. Both supernumerary rings and giant markers invariably contain amplified sequences from the 12q13-q15 region, with MDM2 (12q15) being the main driver gene. Several other genes in this region, including TSPAN31, CDK4 (12q14.1), HMGA2 (12q14.3), YEATS4 {20601955; 19737942; 18214854}, CPM {19820690}, and FRS2 (12q15) {21793095}, are frequently coamplified with MDM2 {29368794; 29220294; 26643872}. A subset of WDLPS shows coamplification of the GLI1 locus with MDM2 and CDK4 {38621503}. In addition to 12q13-q15–amplified sequences, WDLPS frequently contain coamplification of at least one other non-contiguous genomic segment {9892106}, most frequently 1q21-q25. WDLPS supernumerary chromosomes also consistently contain a neocentromere {18823700; 25517748}; a rare event in tumours, this is a specific hallmark of ALT/WDLPS ring and marker chromosomes. The mechanism of ring and giant marker chromosome formation is unknown, but these might be generated by chromothripsis {25517748}. Overexpression of MDM2 protein resulting from genomic amplification inactivates p53; MDM2 targets p53 degradation towards the proteasome and inhibits p53-mediated transactivation.

Macroscopic appearance

WDLPSs are typically large, circumscribed, lobulated masses, with variable consistencies, with firm grey to gelatinous areas, depending on proportions of fibrous and myxoid components. Larger retroperitoneal tumours are more heterogeneous, often containing focal fat necrosis and punctate haemorrhages.

Histopathology

ALT/WDLPS can be subdivided morphologically into three histological patterns: adipocytic (lipoma-like), sclerosing, and inflammatory; these have not been shown to be prognostically relevant {17197914}. More than one morphological pattern in the same lesion is common, particularly within retroperitoneum. Lipoma-like ALT/WDLPS is the most frequent morphology, comprising sheets and lobules of mature-appearing adipocytes resembling normal mature fat, but with variable fibrous septation, and typically scattered nuclear atypia within fat cells or stromal spindle cells that can be seen at low magnification. Scattered hyperchromatic stromal spindle cells are typically easily identified within fibrous septa or vessel walls. Mitoses are sparse. Occasionally, fat cells assume hibernoma-like features, with at least focal sheets of brown fat–like, finely multivacuolated, clear to eosinophilic cells with no or minimal nuclear atypia {27272683; 35475774}. More rarely, there are variable numbers of lipoblasts; the presence of lipoblasts is not required for diagnosis of WDLPS. Sclerosing ALT/WDLPS is second in frequency, is most often seen in retroperitoneum or spermatic cord, and comprises scattered bizarre stromal cells with marked nuclear hyperchromasia within extensive fibrillary collagenous stroma, sometimes with multivacuolated lipoblasts; the overall cellularity is low. The fibrous component may overshadow lipogenic areas, which can therefore be easily missed in small samples. Inflammatory ALT/WDLPS, the rarest pattern, occurs most often in retroperitoneum, and histologically shows at least focal, often extensive variable lymphoid infiltration, with areas that may show lymphoid follicles with germinal centres, plasma cells, and sometimes eosinophils and neutrophils. The chronic inflammatory infiltrate may obscure both the adipocytic areas and the scattered atypical stromal spindle cells {9255251; 9158675} and may histologically mimic lymphoma. In cases with scarce adipocytic components, the presence of bizarre multinucleated stromal cells is a useful diagnostic clue. A rare finding is mature heterologous differentiation, which can be osseous or myogenic, but does not by itself indicate dedifferentiation {20697254; 17197914; 8352375}. Cytologically bland dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS) may be difficult to distinguish from non-lipogenic areas of WDLPS {17197914; 37057834; 38311185}, particularly in small biopsies or in posttreatment resections. Based on survival differences, tumours with 0–4 mitoses/10 HPF are best classified as cellular WDLPS {17197914; 37057834}, while tumours with ≥ 5 mitoses/10 HPF are best classified as DDLPS {17197914; 25581729; 18704991; 37057834}. (HPF diameter was not indicated for most of these studies, so the number of mitoses/mm2 cannot be accurately counted.) The term “low-grade DDLPS” {9060596; 9127316} is no longer recommended.

MDM2 and/or CDK4 nuclear immunopositivity is present in most cases and is typically strong and diffuse {16160477; 19737942; 17895748}. In lipoma-like ALT/WDLPS, MDM2 and CDK4 expression may prove difficult to evaluate, making FISH for MDM2 (+/− CDK4) amplification a valid alternative {27508976}. A major pitfall is represented by MDM2 nuclear positivity in histiocytes in fat necrosis. ALT/WDLPS associated with Li–Fraumeni syndrome is MDM2-negative but expresses p53.

FISH for MDM2 amplification is typically recognized as the gold standard for definitive diagnosis of WDLPS and is especially useful in problematic cases of differentiated adipocytic neoplasms lacking obvious cellular atypia, or in small samples. When attempting to differentiate lipoma from ALT/WDLPS, although criteria vary, FISH for MDM2 amplification has been recommended for recurrent lesions; deep extremity lesions > 10 cm; in patients aged > 50 years; lesions with equivocal atypia; lesions within the retroperitoneum, pelvis, or abdominal cavity; and those occurring in special clinical situations as directed by treating clinicians {26146760; 20679883; 38135062; 30709444; 38134838}.

Perinephric myxoid pseudotumours of fat are benign pseudotumoural mimics of WDLPS. These typically present as masses in perinephric adipose tissue, sometimes bilateral or multiple, and can be confused for a cyst, liposarcoma, or other solid neoplasm on imaging {19525929; 30826321; 36085375}. These usually occur in men with non-neoplastic renal disease and/or type 2 diabetes, although they can also occur in patients with renal carcinoma {19525929; 30826321; 39536981}. Histologically, these are lipomatous masses composed of mature fat, with variable myxoid degeneration, variable spindled to stellate cells, and lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate. These, however, lack atypical hyperchromatic stromal spindle cells {39536981; 30826321}. IgG4-positive plasma cells and MDM2 amplification are absent {19525929; 30826321}.

Cytology

Cytology is not clinically relevant in most cases. Aspirated material may show tissue fragments of adipocytes showing variable, often minimal or no, nuclear atypia resembling a lipoma, varying to cases showing nuclear pleomorphism and hyperchromasia, floret cells, and lipoblasts with one or more vacuoles indenting one or more nuclei. No necrosis or spindle cell component is seen. Correlation with site and imaging features and immunohistochemistry and FISH assists the diagnosis {15690614}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of MDM2 (and/or CDK4) amplification {17895748; 25862836; 21117066} distinguishes WDLPS from benign adipocytic neoplasms, all of which lack MDM2 amplification.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: for lipoma-like ALT/WDLPS: at least focal nuclear atypia in stromal and/or adipocytic cells; for sclerosing ALT/WDLPS: hyperchromatic bizarre stromal cells within fibrillary collagenous background; for inflammatory ALT/WDLPS: scattered atypical stromal cells in a chronic inflammatory background.

Desirable (in selected or challenging cases): MDM2 and/or CDK4 nuclear expression or evidence of MDM2 and/or CDK4 gene amplification.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

ALT/WDLPS is locally aggressive but shows no potential for metastasis unless it undergoes dedifferentiation. WDLPS can dedifferentiate in any site (up to 10%), but there is a much greater risk (~28%) for deep tumours, particularly within retroperitoneum {7942636; 1471725; 3826477}. In addition to metastatic capacity, DDLPS shows significantly higher rates of local recurrence.

Anatomical site is the most important prognostic factor. Tumours in surgically amenable anatomical regions do not recur after complete excision, but those in deep sites such as retroperitoneum, spermatic cord, or mediastinum tend to recur repeatedly and can eventually cause death from uncontrolled local effects or (less often) as a result of systemic spread subsequent to dedifferentiation. In retroperitoneum, multivisceral resections may increase relapse-free survival {20393803}. The ultimate risk of dedifferentiation varies according to site and lesion duration and is probably > 20% in the retroperitoneum but < 2% within limbs. Overall, 10- to 20-year mortality rates range from essentially 0% for ALT of the extremities to > 80% for retroperitoneal WDLPS. The median time to death is 6–11 years {7942636; 1471725}.
