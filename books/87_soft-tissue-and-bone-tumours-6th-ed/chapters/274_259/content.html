2.7.4 Fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma

Definition

Fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma is an exceedingly rare, locally aggressive neoplasm composed of spindle cells admixed with nodules of growth plate–like hyaline cartilage.

ICD-O coding

8990/1 Fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH2AD1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Mesenchymoma NOS

2E8A & XH7AA3 Other mixed or unspecified benign mesenchymal tumours & Mesenchymoma, benign

Related terminology

Not recommended: fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma with low-grade malignancy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma occurs in the metaphysis of long tubular bones (61%), followed by the iliac-pubic bones (18%), vertebrae (15%), and metatarsal bones and ribs (3% each) {28239886; 23692424; 8338193}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

The patients can be asymptomatic or report local pain with swelling.

Imaging

Radiologically, fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma shows features of a locally aggressive or low-grade malignant tumour. It is usually circumscribed, sometimes with a sclerotic margin, lytic with focal ring-like calcifications, and expansile; it thins and erodes the cortex and often extends into soft tissue. On MRI, the tumour has low signal on T1-weighted images and high signal on T2-weighted images and enhances with contrast {28239886}.

Epidemiology

Fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma is a very rare lesion, with < 40 reported cases in the medical literature. Reported cases affect young patients (age range: 3 months to 27 years; median: 13 years), with a slight male predominance {28239886}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The neoplasm is circumscribed, tan-white, leathery, and contains blue-grey, glistening nodules of cartilage. Erosion of the cortex and growth into the soft tissue is commonplace.

Histopathology

The tumour is hypocellular to moderately cellular and composed of spindle cells with fusiform nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm enmeshed in a collagenous stroma and arranged in intersecting fascicles. Cellular areas may resemble low-grade spindle cell sarcoma, but mitoses are limited in number. Nodules of varying sizes of benign-appearing cartilage are scattered throughout the mass. Most of the cartilage has the architecture of growth plate and undergoes enchondral ossification, forming bone trabeculae that may be rimmed by osteoblasts. A minority of the cartilage lacks features of the growth plate and is moderately cellular, with the chondrocytes arranged randomly. The fibrous and cartilage components are nearly equal in quantity; however, in some cases the spindle cell elements predominate. They can infiltrate host bone and extend into soft tissue, mimicking sarcoma. Scattered osteoclast-type giant cells are usually present.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnosis is with fibrous dysplasia containing cartilage. The cartilage in fibrous dysplasia can have a growth plate–like appearance, but in fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma this is a more prominent feature. Moreover, the spindle cells in fibrous dysplasia contain small bland nuclei, unlike those in fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma with mild hyperchromasia. Desmoplastic fibroma of bone and low-grade fibrosarcoma lack cartilaginous nodules. Unlike fibrocartilaginous mesenchymoma, dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma is characterized by a high-grade sarcomatous component. Chondromesenchymal hamartoma shows similar cartilaginous nodules, but these occur in the chest wall of infants and usually contain an aneurysmal bone cyst–like component.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The absence of mutations in GNAS, IDH1, or IDH2 and amplification of MDM2 may aid in the differential diagnosis {28239886}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a bone tumour with compatible imaging; spindle cells with mild cytological atypia and nodules of hyaline cartilage–resembling growth plate–like cartilage.

Desirable: young patient age (< 30 years); exclusion of GNAS and IDH1/IDH2 variants and of MDM2 amplification.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Surgical resection with a wide negative margin is the goal of treatment {28239886}. Local recurrences occur in a minority of cases, often after intralesional removal, and may develop many years after surgery {8338193; 23692424}. No metastatic spread or tumour-related deaths have been reported {28239886}.
