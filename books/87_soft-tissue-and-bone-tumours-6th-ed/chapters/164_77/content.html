1.6.4 Angioleiomyoma

Definition

Angioleiomyoma is a benign, usually superficial tumour composed of well-differentiated perivascular smooth muscle cells arranged around numerous vascular channels. A morphological continuum exists between angioleiomyoma and myopericytoma.

ICD-O coding

8894/0 Angioleiomyoma

ICD-11 coding

2E86.1, 2F2Y & XH7CL0 Leiomyoma of other or unspecified sites, Cutaneous angioleiomyoma & angiomyoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: angiomyoma; vascular leiomyoma.

Not recommended: angiomyolipoma (even in cases with adipocytic metaplasia).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumours occur most commonly in the subcutis or dermis of the extremities, more often in the lower than the upper limbs, followed by the head and neck and the trunk {6722737; 17270242; 30148476; 26047608}. The most common solid subtype usually arises in the lower extremities of female individuals. The venous subtype involves the head and neck region more often than solid or cavernous subtypes {6722737}. The cavernous subtype most often occurs in the upper extremities {6722737}.

Clinical features

The lesion presents as a firm, well-circumscribed, and slow-growing nodule. Approximately half of angioleiomyomas are painful {6722737; 17270242}, with a higher frequency observed in the solid subtype {6722737}.

Epidemiology

Angioleiomyoma affects a wide age range, peaking in the third through the sixth decades of life {6722737; 17270242}. The overall M:F ratio is about 2:3. The solid subtype is more than twice as prevalent in women as in men, whereas the venous and cavernous types show a slight male predominance {6722737}.

Etiology

No established etiology exists, although minor trauma and venous stasis have been proposed as potential causal factors {15311559}.

Pathogenesis

NOTCH2 gene rearrangement or PDGFRB or NOTCH3 gene mutations, often identified in other pericytic tumours including myofibromas, myopericytomas, or glomus tumours, have been detected in a subset of angioleiomyoma {23999936; 3260416; 36788105; 23999936}. Additionally, rearrangement of Xq22, including IRS4, or t(4;5)(p12;q32) resulting in CARMN::TXK fusion were reported {37889065}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are typically solitary, sharply circumscribed, and relatively small, most often measuring less than 20 mm in diameter, although lesions exceeding 30 mm are extremely rare.

Histopathology

Angioleiomyomas are composed of bundles of bland, well-differentiated smooth muscle cells and intervening variably sized blood vessels. Based on their predominant vascular morphologies, the tumours are subclassified into the three histological patterns: solid, venous, and cavernous. Solid angioleiomyoma has closely compacted smooth muscle bundles with intervening thin-walled, slit-like vascular channels. Venous angioleiomyoma is characterized by thick-walled, muscle-coated blood vessels with smooth muscle cells of the vascular walls swirling and blending with intervascular smooth muscle bundles. The rare cavernous pattern is composed of dilated vascular channels with a proliferation of smooth muscle bundles in the intervascular spaces; these bundles are difficult to distinguish from the muscular walls of the vessels. Tumours showing mixed morphologies are not uncommon. Mitotic figures are absent. Some tumours contain myopericytoma-like elements, with a concentric perivascular proliferation of less eosinophilic spindled or ovoid myoid cells {17270242}. Other minor histological variations include adipocytic metaplasia, prominent hyalinization and calcification {6722737}, and degenerative atypia {8879307}. Immunohistochemically, the tumour cells are strongly and diffusely positive for SMA and calponin, and usually diffusely positive for h-caldesmon and desmin {17270242}. HMB45 and melan-A are negative even in tumours with adipocytic metaplasia {6722737; 17270242; 26047608}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-circumscribed tumour composed of cytologically bland smooth muscle cells around variably sized vascular channels.

Desirable: immunohistochemical expression of SMA and h-caldesmon.

Staging

Not available

Prognosis and prediction

Angioleiomyoma is a benign tumour that very seldom recurs after surgical excision.
