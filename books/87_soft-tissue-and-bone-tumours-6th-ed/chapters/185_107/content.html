1.8.5 Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma is a high-grade pleomorphic sarcoma of adults with a variable component of bizarre, brightly eosinophilic polygonal, round, and spindle cells displaying skeletal muscle differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8901/3 Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B55.Z & XH5SX9 Rhabdomyosarcoma, primary site & Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

These tumours arise in the deep soft tissue, with occasional exceptions {26504053}, most often in the lower extremity but also in the chest/abdominal wall, upper extremity, abdomen/retroperitoneum, and head and neck {26504053; 11406662; 8333559}.

Clinical features

Patients present with rapidly growing, usually deep, variably painful soft tissue swellings {11406662}.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare and are most common in the sixth to seventh decades of life (mean age: ~72 years), occurring more frequently in men, with an M:F ratio of 1.8:1 {26504053}. Similar-appearing tumours in paediatric age groups are considered to represent embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcomas have complex karyotypes with numerical and unbalanced structural changes, with frequent TP53 mutations, RB1 alterations, and CDKN2A deletions {19480930}. Genome-wide surveys have identified recurrent losses of DNA (e.g. 10q23, 6q), gains (e.g. 1p23-p22, 17p, and 11q), and amplifications {12550764; 38722224; 37593416}. The genetic profiles of pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcomas mirror those of undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas {30709449; 38722224; 37593416}.

Macroscopic appearance

Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcomas are usually large, well-marginated tumours {26504053}, with pseudocapsules and whitish or fleshy cut surfaces, often with necrosis.

Histopathology

Pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcomas have a variable appearance with markedly atypical spindled, epithelioid, round, and/or pleomorphic cells and a variable component (sometimes focal) of multinucleated, polygonal, spindled, or rhabdoid cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm. Tumours typically display desmin expression of varying extent (sometimes diffuse), with MYOD1 and myogenin expression (sometimes focal). Some tumours showing the morphological features of pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma represent other tumour types with divergent rhabdomyoblastic differentiation.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows high cellularity, with large pleomorphic, epithelioid, spindle, and rhabdoid cells characterized by abundant eccentric basophilic cytoplasm and pleomorphic nuclei, in tissue fragments and as single cells, with lymphocytes and binucleated to multinucleated giant cells {32159876}. Background necrosis and atypical mitoses are present.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: high-grade sarcoma with pleomorphic, spindle, epithelioid, and/or round cells and a variable number of cells with brightly eosinophilic cytoplasm and variable expression of desmin and myogenin or MYOD1.

Staging

This tumour can be staged using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging for soft tissue sarcomas.

Prognosis and prediction

These highly aggressive sarcomas were associated with a median survival of 7.3 months in the largest study to date; about 80% of patients died of disease {26504053}. Metastases to the lungs are common {26504053; 11406662; 8333559}. s
