2.2.4 Low-grade central osteosarcoma

Definition

Low-grade central osteosarcoma (LGCOS) is a malignant bone-forming neoplasm that is histologically and biologically low-grade, originating within the intramedullary cavity.

ICD-O coding

9187/3 Low-grade central osteosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B51.Z & XH7N84 Osteosarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of unspecified sites & Low-grade central osteosarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: well-differentiated intramedullary osteosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LGCOS typically arises in the metaphysis of long bones, predominantly the femur and tibia. Jaw bones, small tubular bones, and axial bones are rarely involved {20975343; 22851905; 18425560; 38160683}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

The tumour often causes swelling or pain. The duration of symptoms tends to be longer than that of conventional osteosarcoma and can exceed 10 years (average: > 2 years) {22851905; 8542697}.

Imaging

On radiographs, LGCOS usually involves the metaphysis or metadiaphysis of long bones and can extend into the epiphysis when growth plates are closed {15175837; 34951370}. It has variable appearances and typically manifests as large intramedullary lytic and/or sclerotic mass with homogeneous, trabeculated, or cloud-like patterns of radiodensity. The tumour has poorly defined or well-defined margins and may be expansile. Homogeneous dense sclerotic patterns are uncommon. The tumour often shows at least focal areas of cortical destruction {15175837}. Soft tissue extension is commonly seen on MRI and CT and is usually associated with little periosteal reaction.

Epidemiology

LGCOS is rare, accounting for 1–2% of all osteosarcomas {38160683}. Reported cases are usually those of young adults, with a peak occurrence during the third decade of life {2306687; 34742481} and a mild female predilection {18425560; 15175837; 20601938; 34742481}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Amplification of 12q13-q15 involving MDM2 and CDK4 is well documented. However, the frequency has not been established in larger series, with reported rates varying from 29% to 57% {38433050; 26248895}. Although smaller series have reported higher rates, a significant subset of cases lack MDM2 amplification {21336260; 30001240; 31154837}.

Macroscopic appearance

LGCOS is a large, intramedullary, relatively well-circumscribed, white, rubbery-to-leathery-to-hard mass, depending on the amount of mineralized neoplastic bone. Erosion of the cortex and extension into the soft tissue are frequent.

Histopathology

LGCOS grows with an infiltrative pattern encasing pre-existing trabecular bone and percolating through Haversian systems. It consists of mildly to moderately cellular fascicles of spindle cells with fusiform nuclei that have fine chromatin, small nucleoli and tapered ends, and eosinophilic cytoplasm. The mild nuclear atypia is in the form of enlargement and coarsening of chromatin. Mitotic activity is limited, and necrosis is uncommon. The tumour cells are enmeshed in a collagenous stroma and are intimately related to linear trabeculae of neoplastic woven and sometimes focally lamellar bone, which can be deposited in parallel arrangement, resembling parosteal osteosarcoma. Tumour bone may have irregular cement lines mimicking Paget disease {14976532}, and neoplastic cartilage may be focally present {2306687}. Cortical destruction with soft tissue infiltration is common, and some tumours have little bone matrix, especially in the soft tissue component. Areas of high-grade transformation (dedifferentiation) may be distinct from or merge with the low-grade component. High-grade areas often have the appearance of conventional high-grade osteosarcoma or fibrosarcoma with hypercellularity, severe pleomorphism, and mitotic activity.

Immunohistochemistry

Positivity for MDM2 and/or CDK4 can be a surrogate for amplification of MDM2 when molecular testing is limited {20601938; 21336260} (see Diagnostic molecular pathology).

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Testing for MDM2 amplification is helpful to differentiate LGCOS from benign mimics. Because MDM2 amplification is rare in conventional osteosarcoma {22074548}, it may help separate LGCOS with high-grade transformation from conventional osteosarcoma in the appropriate histological context {21336260; 22301501}. In biopsy material lacking bone formation, the presence of MDM2 amplification can be helpful in excluding desmoplastic fibroma. Unlike fibrous dysplasia, LGCOS lacks GNAS variant {26248895}. The lack of MDM2 amplification does not exclude the diagnosis of LGCOS.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: intramedullary infiltrating bone tumour with compatible imaging; relatively uniform neoplastic spindle cells with limited cytological atypia and mitotic activity; collagenous stroma and well-formed neoplastic bony trabeculae.

Desirable: MDM2 amplification.

Staging

Staging is according to bone sarcoma protocols (see <>TNM staging of tumours of bone). See also the information on staging in section Bone tumours: Introduction.

Prognosis and prediction

LGCOS requires complete resection and invariably recurs after curettage and incomplete excision. High-grade transformation (dedifferentiation) occurs in 10–30% of cases and confers a worse prognosis {38467824}, emphasizing the importance of its recognition. Overall, LGCOS has a good prognosis with a metastatic rate of < 5% and 5-year and 10-year overall survival rates of 90% and > 80%, respectively {22851905; 8542697; 2306687; 37143718}. Most metastases develop from tumours that have undergone high-grade transformation; metastatic low-grade osteosarcoma is uncommon. It remains to be determined whether the volume of the high-grade component is prognostically significant {18425560; 26524981}. Chemotherapy is reserved for high-grade tumours; however, it has not been shown to alter patient outcome {37143718}.
