1.5.6 Retiform haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Retiform haemangioendothelioma is a locally aggressive, rarely metastasizing vascular lesion, characterized by distinctive arborizing blood vessels lined by endothelial cells with characteristic hobnail morphology.

ICD-O coding

9136/1 Retiform haemangioendothelioma

ICD-11 coding

2B56.1 & XH64U8 Retiform haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: hobnail haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumour is most commonly found in the distal extremities, particularly the lower limb. Rarely, other sites are involved, including the head and neck region, trunk, penis, vulva, pleura, and mandible {33593408}.

Clinical features

Retiform haemangioendothelioma presents as a red/bluish, usually asymptomatic, slow-growing plaque or nodule, usually < 30 mm in maximum dimension. A case with multiple lesions has been described {8989934}. Exceptional cases occur in the setting of previous radiotherapy {8291650} or pre-existing lymphoedema {8291650} (including Milroy disease {27730656}), as well as in association with a cystic lymphangioma {21915027}.

Epidemiology

Retiform haemangioendothelioma is uncommon. Since its original description in 1994, only about 50 cases have been reported {21915027; 8291650; 8764941; 9432675; 16176302; 32991341}. The age range is wide, with a described predilection for young adults and children; male and female individuals are affected equally.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

A subset of retiform haemangioendotheliomas exhibit recurrent YAP1 gene rearrangements, most commonly YAP1::MAML2 fusions {32991341}. Identical fusions have also been identified in composite haemangioendotheliomas, indicating a potential biological overlap between the two entities.

Macroscopic appearance

Macroscopic examination reveals diffuse, sometimes discoloured, induration of the dermis, with frequent involvement of the underlying subcutaneous tissue.

Histopathology

Scanning magnification reveals characteristic elongated and narrow arborizing vascular channels with a striking resemblance to normal rete testis. Although this pattern is usually readily apparent, if the vascular channels are small or collapsed, the retiform architecture may be less discernible. Monomorphic endothelial cells with prominent protuberant nuclei having a characteristic tombstone or hobnail appearance line the blood vessels. These cells have scant cytoplasm, which seems to blend with the underlying stroma. Pleomorphism is absent and mitotic figures are rare. A prominent sclerotic stroma is noted. Intravascular lymphocytic infiltrates are present in about half of the cases. Focal solid areas composed of sheets of endothelial cells are often identified. Vacuolated cells are uncommonly seen. Rarely, intravascular papillae with hyaline collagenous cores similar to those seen in papillary intralymphatic angioendothelioma are seen {21915027}. Retiform haemangioendothelioma can be one of the components of a composite haemangioendothelioma. Neoplastic cells stain for vascular markers, including CD31, CD34, and ERG. Staining for CD34 is often stronger than for other vascular markers. Tumours harbouring YAP1 rearrangements may demonstrate loss of the YAP1 C-terminal region {34148063}. Although the lymphatic marker PROX1 is positive in retiform haemangioendothelioma, other lymphatic markers, including podoplanin (D2-40) and VEGFR3, are usually (but not always) negative {19674202; 22067331; 18212541}. KSHV/HHV8 is negative.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

In diagnostically challenging cases, demonstration of a YAP1 rearrangement may be useful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: arborizing branching vascular channels; hyperchromatic endothelial cells with hobnail morphology and no or very low mitotic activity.

Desirable: YAP1 rearrangement in a subset of cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Multiple local recurrences (occurring in as many as 60% of cases), often over many years, are the rule, unless wide local excision is performed {8291650}. Very rarely, lymph node or locoregional soft tissue metastasis may occur {19172034; 8291650; 9432675}. To date, no tumour-related deaths or distant metastases have been reported.
