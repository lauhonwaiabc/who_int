1.3.25 Lipofibromatosis

Definition

Lipofibromatosis (LPF) is an intermediate, locally aggressive paediatric soft tissue neoplasm with a predilection for the hands and feet; it is composed of an admixture of mature fat, short fascicles of bland spindle cells, and lipoblast-like cells in the interface between these components.

ICD-O coding

8851/1 Lipofibromatosis

ICD-11 coding

FB51.Y & XH4QB6 Other specified fibroblastic disorders & Lipofibromatosis

Related terminology

Not recommended: infantile/juvenile fibromatosis variant (non-desmoid type).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

LPF preferentially involves the hands and feet, although it can also occur at other locations such as the trunk and the head and neck {11075850}.

Clinical features

LPF typically presents as a slow-growing, poorly demarcated subcutaneous mass, which can also involve skeletal muscle {11075850}.

Epidemiology

LPF is rare and occurs more often in boys, with a wide age range from birth to 14 years. Half of the cases are diagnosed by the age of 1 year, and as many as 20% of reported cases have been congenital {11075850; 30310176}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

LPF may represent a morphological pattern common to a variety of locally recurring paediatric tumours of the extremities, rather than to a single specific entity. The presence of FN1::EGF or FN1::TGFA fusions suggests that some cases probably represent early or non-calcified calcifying aponeurotic fibromas. Moreover, identification of fusions involving genes encoding EGFR (HER1) ligands (EGF, HBEGF, TGFA) or EGFR itself, or genes encoding other receptor tyrosine kinases (ROS1, RET, PDGFRB), suggests that activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway might be implicated in the pathogenesis of LPF {30310176}.

Cytogenetic abnormalities have been described in 1 case {18036401}, and molecular analyses have identified fusions involving EGF or other genes encoding receptor tyrosine kinases or their ligands in a subset of LPF cases {30310176}.

Macroscopic appearance

The firm, rubbery, poorly demarcated mass has a white or yellow cut surface. Most are 10–70 mm in diameter, although very large tumours have been reported involving the entire extremity {11075850; 30310176; 19461290}.

Histopathology

Lipofibromatosis is composed of mature adipose tissue traversed by cellular fascicles of spindle cells with bland, uniform, elongated nuclei. Fat usually forms a major proportion of the lesion. In newborn patients, the fat lobules may have an immature appearance with myxoid matrix. Small collections of univacuolated cells may be present in the fibroblastic component, where melanin-laden cells have occasionally been documented. Lipofibromatosis entraps subcutaneous tissue and sometimes skeletal muscle. Mitotic activity is rare and necrosis absent. Occasional recurrent lesions are indistinguishable from calcifying aponeurotic fibroma {30310176}. By immunohistochemistry, the fibroblasts show variable expression of CD34 and SMA. The spindle cells are negative for desmin, in contrast to the spindle cells in lipoblastoma. The uncommon pigmented cells express melanocytic markers {11075850; 18236073}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an admixture of mature fat, short fascicles of bland spindle cells and lipoblast-like cells; infiltrative margins; most often in the hands and feet of young children.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

LPF has a high rate of local recurrence but no metastatic potential. Congenital onset, male sex, acral location, mitotic activity in the fibroblastic component, and incomplete excision may be risk factors for recurrence {11075850}.
