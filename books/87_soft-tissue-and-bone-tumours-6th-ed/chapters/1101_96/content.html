1.10.1 Soft tissue chondroma

Definition

Soft tissue chondroma is a benign neoplasm composed of hyaline cartilage with variable myxoid change and calcification, arising in the extraosseous and extrasynovial soft tissues.

ICD-O coding

9220/0 Chondroma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E89.1 & XH0NS4 Benign tumours of uncertain differentiation, soft tissue & Chondroma NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: chondroma of soft parts; extraskeletal chondroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

 Approximately 80% of cases occur in the region of the fingers {26297064; 30033358; 76505}. The remainder of cases most commonly involve the hands, followed by the toes and feet, with origin in the trunk and the head and neck region {19349148} being uncommon. Rare examples have been described in the skin {25998426}, lip {29115679}, and dura {25250651}.

Clinical features

The tumour is solitary and presents as a painless, firm, slowly enlarging mass {26297064}. Radiographically, it is well-demarcated, is separated from bone, and has spiculated or ring-like calcifications. The cartilage is bright on T2-weighted MRI.

Epidemiology

Soft tissue chondroma is rare. Patients are usually adults aged 30–60 years (median: 34.5 years), and men are affected more frequently than women (M:F ratio: 1.5:1) {76505}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic analyses of a limited number of soft tissue chondromas reveal several different aberrations, including rearrangements of 12q13-q15 and trisomy 5, and in a subset of tumours with rearrangements of 12q13-q15, a truncated or full-length HMGA2 transcript was found {8402563; 10748295; 11793371; 14614053; 25250651}. In about 50% of the cases, particularly in those with chondroblastoma-like pattern, FN1 gene rearrangements with FGFR1 and FGFR2 as fusion partners {31273315}, as well as PDGFRA fusions, have been reported {40139500}.

Macroscopic appearance

Soft tissue chondroma is a well-circumscribed, nodular, solid mass that is greyish-bluish-white and sometimes focally myxoid. Most tumours are solid and usually < 30 mm in size.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of lobules of well-formed hyaline cartilage that are delineated by fibrous septa {76505}. The cartilage is often hypercellular, and the chondrocytes are arranged in clusters and reside within lacunar spaces or float in a flocculent myxoid matrix. The nuclei are usually small, round, and dark, but they may be large with fine or coarse chromatin and small nucleoli and exhibit mild to moderate pleomorphism. Coarse calcifications and endochondral ossification may be present. The chondrocytes in the mineralized areas undergo necrosis, and heavily calcified tumours may have an infiltrate of histiocytes that may obscure the nature of the lesion. The chondroblastoma-like pattern is hypercellular and contains chondrocytes with nuclei that may be grooved or cleaved and have moderate amounts of eosinophilic cytoplasm; the matrix contains calcifications that are often grungy and may surround individual chondrocytes, thereby mimicking chondroblastoma {11342780}. Calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate crystals may be present in a subset of cases {11342780; 40139500}. Mitotic activity is limited.

Cytology

Aspiration produces low cellularity with chondroid or myxoid or myxofibrillary stroma containing chondrocytes, single or in small groups, with round dark nuclei and mild atypia in some cases {25190983}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a soft tissue mass composed of nodules of well-delineated cartilage; the matrix is hyaline or myxoid and may calcify; chondrocytes show limited atypia and little mitotic activity.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Treatment is marginal excision, and the recurrence rate is 15–20% {76505}.
