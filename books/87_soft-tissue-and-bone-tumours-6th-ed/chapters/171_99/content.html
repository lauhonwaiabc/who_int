1.7.1 Leiomyoma

Definition

Leiomyoma is a benign smooth muscle tumour that arises in the deep somatic soft tissues including the retroperitoneum / abdominal cavity.

ICD-O coding

8890/0 Leiomyoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E86.1 Leiomyoma of other or unspecified sites

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Somatic (deep soft tissues of the extremities) and gynaecological type (pelvis, abdomen, and retroperitoneum of females), also referred to as abdominal-retroperitoneal leiomyoma.

Localization

Leiomyomas of somatic soft tissue mostly occur in extremities and arise in deep subcutis or skeletal muscle {11688572; 8179073}. Although the retroperitoneum is the most common location for the gynaecological type, it may also occur in abdominal wall, omentum, mesentery, and peritoneal surface {11688572; 11684951}. In the inguinal region, leiomyomas may arise from the round ligament {21297441}.

Clinical features

Although usually solitary, retroperitoneal tumours may be multiple {11688572}. Somatic tumours often have calcification and may be detected radiographically {8179073; 11688572; 18040645; 30187111}. Local recurrences do not occur with somatic leiomyomas {8179073; 11688572}. However, local recurrences are infrequently (< 10%) seen in gynaecological-type leiomyomas of the retroperitoneum-abdominal cavity {11688572; 11684951}.

Epidemiology

Leiomyomas of somatic soft tissue occur primarily in middle-aged adults, with no sex predilection {8179073; 11688572}. Gynaecological-type leiomyomas are much more common in women, often of perimenopausal age {11688572; 11684951}.

Etiology

The causation of somatic leiomyomas is unknown. The fact that a significant number of patients with gynaecological-type leiomyomas also had concurrent uterine leiomyomas or underwent a previous hysterectomy for uterine leiomyomas may support a theory of multifocal origin {2912878; 9669352; 11688572; 11684951}.

Pathogenesis

Not surprisingly, gynaecological-type leiomyomas carry some of the same genetic changes as uterine leiomyomas {25621995; 25875009; 28591699}. Genetic analysis of 8 leiomyomas of the retroperitoneum-abdominal cavity showed 3 tumours with 12q rearrangements, 3 with 8q rearrangements, 1 with deletion of 7q, and 1 with aberrations of chromosome bands 3q21-q23 and 11q21-q22. The target genes of the 12q and 8q aberrations were HMGA2 and PLAG1, respectively {28591699}. In other studies, mutations in exon 2 of MED12 were reported in retroperitoneal leiomyomas {23222489; 24196187}. KAT6B::KANSL1 and EWSR1::PBX3 fusion genes were found in retroperitoneal leiomyomas with t(10;17)(q22;q21) and t(9;22)(q33;q12) chromosomal translocations, respectively {25621995; 25875009}.

Macroscopic appearance

Somatic leiomyomas are circumscribed masses that are tan-grey to white in colour and often have a rubbery texture and a whorled cut surface. Degenerative mucoid-cystic change is occasionally present {8179073; 11688572}. The size ranges from < 10 to 150 mm {8179073; 11688572}. Leiomyomas of gynaecological type are reddish-tan, greyish-tan, or white circumscribed masses, often having a trabecular surface resembling that commonly seen in uterine leiomyomas {11688572; 11684951}. They range in size from 25 to 370 mm {11688572; 11684951}.

Histopathology

Somatic leiomyomas are composed of cells that closely resemble normal smooth muscle cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and uniform blunt-ended, cigar-shaped nuclei. They are arranged in orderly intersecting and perpendicular fascicles and bundles, with little to no nuclear atypia, no necrosis, and essentially no mitotic activity (< 1 mitosis/7.95 mm2, equating to < 1 mitosis/50 HPF of 0.45 mm in diameter and 0.159 mm2 in area) {8179073; 11684951}. Indeed, it is best to regard any level of mitotic activity and atypia as within the spectrum of leiomyosarcoma {11756765; 11688572}. Stromal hyalinization is seen in the majority, and calcifications are frequently present. Epithelioid features are rare.

For gynaecological-type leiomyomas, which are essentially identical to those seen in the uterus, mitotic figures also tend to be uncommon; but up to 10 mitoses/7.95 mm2 (equating to up to 10 mitoses/50 HPF of 0.45 mm in diameter and 0.159 mm2 in area) may be acceptable, provided there is neither necrosis nor significant cytological atypia. Necrosis is not found in deep leiomyoma. Most lesions are paucicellular, and degenerative or regressive changes, such as fibrosis, hyalinization, calcification, and myxoid change, are common in large tumours. Compared with somatic leiomyomas, gynaecological-type tumours tend to show a more richly vascular stroma and often greater cellularity. A wide spectrum of patterns may be found, including macrotrabecular and microtrabecular organization, hyalinization, and focal myxoid or cystic changes. Focal epithelioid features, clear cell change, nuclear palisading, metaplastic bone, and fatty differentiation are also occasionally seen {1703396}. Focal degenerative nuclear atypia should always prompt a careful search for mitoses and additional sampling, because some leiomyosarcomas may have only focal atypia and very low mitotic activity.

The tumour cells in both gynaecological-type and somatic leiomyomas are usually strongly and diffusely positive for SMA, desmin, and h-caldesmon but negative for S100. Gynaecological-type leiomyomas almost always express ER, PR, and WT1 {11688572; 11684951; 21297441}. However, leiomyomas of somatic soft tissue are negative for ER and PR {8179073; 11684951}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows usually low cellularity, with cohesive large tissue fragments of slender spindle cells with poorly defined borders and blunt-ended bland nuclei, in a clean background, and the diagnosis was confirmed by immunohistochemistry on cell blocks {16900474}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: intersecting fascicles and bundles of benign and mature smooth muscle, with essentially no mitotic activity.

Desirable (in selected cases): diffuse immunohistochemical expression of SMA and, less often, desmin and/or caldesmon.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Regardless of type, leiomyomas are benign, and patients are cured by complete excision. There is no metastatic risk, but local recurrences have been reported in some patients with gynaecological-type retroperitoneal leiomyomas {11688572; 11684951}. None of these patients with recurrences have demonstrated disease progression or metastasis with follow-up.
