1.12.29 Intimal sarcoma

Definition

Intimal sarcomas are malignant mesenchymal neoplasms arising in large-calibre blood vessels and the heart, typically demonstrating intraluminal growth, embolization, and haemodynamic obstruction.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Intimal sarcomas of the pulmonary arteries mainly involve the proximal segments, including the pulmonary trunk (80%), the right or left pulmonary arteries (50–70%), or both (40%) {8448740; 3055809}. Those that involve the heart most commonly affect the left atrium {26725181; 24625414; 28474091; 28501860}. When the right heart is involved, pulmonary arterial involvement is also often present, raising the possibility of retrograde spread. Direct infiltration or lung metastases are observed in 40% of cases, and extrathoracic spread (e.g. brain, skin, lymph nodes) occurs at presentation in 20% {8448740}. Aortic intimal sarcomas mostly arise in the abdominal aorta between the coeliac artery and the iliac bifurcation, and 30% are located in the thoracic aorta {25393249; 8448740; 11355177; 16096408}.

Clinical features

Clinical presentation is nonspecific and often related to embolic phenomena {29962886; 28912665; 21894817; 21900097; 34280751; 35588894}, with recurrent pulmonary embolism being most common. Proper diagnosis is often delayed or made after death. Imaging is nonspecific, but heterogeneous attenuation seen on CT of the intravascular material can be suggestive, as well as increased 18F-FDG uptake on PET {29962886; 28912665; 21607843; 19462198; 33646906; 38188319; 30535656; 30747369}.

Epidemiology

Intimal sarcomas are rare, with major pulmonary artery lesions being more commonly described than those arising in the aorta or other vessels {11213836; 8448740; 20685895; 18670310; 16096408}. Intracardiac intimal sarcomas are rare, although this is the most common type of heart sarcoma {24625414; 37395142}. Pulmonary and cardiac intimal sarcomas are slightly more commonly reported in women (M:F ratio: ~0.7:1), whereas aortic tumours may be more common in men {25393249}. The mean age at diagnosis in reported cases is 48 years for pulmonary, 50 years for cardiac, and 62 years for aortic intimal sarcomas {29962886; 28501860; 25393249; 8448740; 34280751}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Molecular studies on pulmonary artery intimal sarcomas have revealed a high frequency of MDM2 amplifications accompanied by coamplifications of CDK4 and PDGFRA {11213836; 11921282; 18036403; 20685895; 28474091; 31480474}. In addition, FISH analysis of 7 intimal sarcomas revealed amplification / high polysomy of PDGFRA and KIT in all cases, and amplification / high polysomy of PDGFRB and EGFR was detected in 4 of 7 and 3 of 7 cases, respectively {17471466}. In 1 case, biochemical analysis revealed the presence of activated and highly expressed PDGFRA, PDGFRB, and EGFR, whereas KIT was expressed at reference level {17471466}. Based on the amplification and activation of these receptor tyrosine kinases, it has been suggested that intimal sarcomas might be sensitive to kinase inhibitors.

Similar gene amplifications and DNA methylation signature have been demonstrated in a large proportion of the so-called undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas of the heart, suggesting a common pathogenesis with intimal sarcomas {24625414; 25216322; 34312479; 35181378}.

In a recent comprehensive genomic profiling of 41 cases {39778225}, 2 molecular subsets of intimal sarcoma were described on the basis of copy-number variation enrichment: a more common, copy-number variation–high subtype (35/41 cases) and a rare subtype showing predominant MLH1 mutations or homozygous deletions, classified as microsatellite instability-high–like (3/41 cases).

Macroscopic appearance

Intimal sarcomas are intravascular masses with intraluminal growth extending along the vessels or within the cardiac chamber. The tumour may grossly resemble thrombi or thromboemboli. Tumours can range in character from a gelatinous vascular cast to a solid mass, which may show calcification. Some aortic tumours have been associated with thinning and aneurysmal dilatation with adherent thrombus, mimicking complicated atherosclerosis {21215575; 11107072}.

Histopathology

Intimal sarcomas are malignant mesenchymal tumours, consisting of mildly to severely atypical spindle cells with varying degrees of mitotic activity, necrosis, and nuclear polymorphism. Some tumours show myxoid areas or epithelioid morphology {7590704; 16096408; 35116611; 35171325}. Prominent spindling and tumour fascicles may resemble leiomyosarcoma. Some cases may contain neoplastic cartilage or osteoid, or show focal rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation {8448740; 3055809; 38835391}. Immunohistochemically, variable positivity for SMA is found, and some tumours exhibit positivity for desmin. Rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation is accompanied by the expression of myogenin and MYOD1 {24625414; 38835391}. Nuclear expression of MDM2 is observed in at least 70% of cases {11213836; 20685895}.

Cytology

Intimal sarcomas of the mediastinal large blood vessels are accessible to EUS-FNAB {30552756; 33218362; 39824238} and show large pleomorphic spindle and epithelioid cells with variable delicate cytoplasm, poorly defined borders, ovoid to spindle pleomorphic nuclei, and often prominent nucleoli, in loosely cohesive tissue fragments and as single cells. Both immunohistochemical and molecular auxiliary studies can be performed on adequate cell block specimens.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of MDM2 and/or CDK4 gene amplification can be helpful, but not specific.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: luminal growth within a large-calibre blood vessel or cardiac chamber; spindle and variably pleomorphic appearance.

Desirable: MDM2 or CDK4 amplification in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for patients with intimal sarcomas is poor, with a mean survival time of 5–9 months in patients with aortic tumours and 13–18 months in patients with pulmonary or cardiac tumours {11213836; 8448740; 3055809; 18670310; 31536651; 24135684}. Surgical procedures, including pulmonary endarterectomy, are mostly palliative, but may improve survival {28912665; 32035299; 32918676; 34280751; 36915590; 38034856; 39015681}. In most cases, a multimodal therapeutic approach is needed, but there is no established standard of care {28501860; 28912665; 32035299}. In a multicentre systemic treatment study, activity of anthracycline-based regimens was shown; however, the median progression-free survival for advanced disease was only 7.7 months {31536651}. Gemcitabine and the multi–tyrosine kinase inhibitor pazopanib, mainly used in advanced disease and further lines in this study, had a limited antitumour effect (median progression-free survival: 3.2 and 3.7 months, respectively). In a phase Ib/II study, the MDM2 inhibitor milademetan was shown to lead to partial response in 2 of 10 patients with MDM2 amplified intimal sarcoma {37369013}.
