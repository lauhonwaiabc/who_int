1.3.20 Nuchal-type fibroma

Definition

Nuchal-type fibroma is a benign fibroblastic proliferation that commonly arises in the posterior neck in middle-aged adults.

ICD-O coding

8810/0 Nuchal-type fibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH0XH6 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Nuchal fibroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: nuchal fibroma.

Not recommended: collagenosis nuchae.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Nuchal-type fibroma is a superficial lesion that occurs in the dermis and subcutis. Although the majority arise in the posterior neck (70%), they may occur at extranuchal sites, including the upper back, shoulder, head, extremities, and buttock {31950474}.

Clinical features

Clinical presentation

Nuchal-type fibroma typically presents as a slow-growing, poorly defined, and firm subcutaneous lesion, which may be fixed or mobile and painless or mildly tender. Rare cases with multiple synchronous lesions have been reported {21950671}.

Imaging

MRI shows a dense fibrous network with minimal water content. The lesion is poorly defined and subcutaneous with low signal intensity in T1- and T2-weighted images. Limited streaks of high T1- and T2-signal fatty tissue may be interspersed {34554278}. At most, mild enhancement is observed {20799020}. Ultrasound shows a hypoechoic, poorly circumscribed lesion without intralesional vascularity on colour Doppler {38227145}.

Epidemiology

Nuchal-type fibroma is rare and has a male predominance (M:F ratio: 4–5:1); it occurs over a wide age range, with a peak incidence in the fifth decade of life. A subset of patients (44%) have diabetes mellitus {9921988; 38227145}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown, but some cases are associated with diabetes mellitus and repetitive blunt trauma {29942846; 38204868}, such as weightlifting.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is typically subcutaneous but may extend into the deep dermis or superficial skeletal muscle. It is poorly defined and unencapsulated, composed of firm, tan-to-white fibrotic tissue with occasional yellow streaks, corresponding to entrapped adipose tissue. The mean tumour size is 32 mm (range: 10–60 mm) {9921988}.

Histopathology

Nuchal-type fibroma is a poorly circumscribed, paucicellular lesion composed of bland stromal fibroblasts, often with a vaguely lobular arrangement, and dense, haphazardly arranged bundles of collagen with slit-like spaces or cracking artefact. Entrapped normal structures, including adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and nerves, may be seen. Many nuchal-type fibromas contain a localized proliferation of small nerves, in some cases similar to those in traumatic neuroma. This feature is usually absent in Gardner fibroma, which may otherwise be morphologically indistinguishable from nuchal-type fibroma. Compared with the normal tissue from the nuchal area, the tissue from nuchal-type fibroma shows similarly thick collagen fibres, but with expansion of collagenized dermis and encasement of adnexa, effacement of subcutis with entrapment of adipocytes, and (in many cases) extension into underlying skeletal muscle. By immunohistochemistry, the stromal fibroblasts are positive for CD34 and negative for β-catenin (absent nuclear staining), SMA, and desmin {11420472}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a hypocellular collagenous lesion with bland fibroblasts; entrapped adipose tissue and small nerve bundles; typically arising at the posterior aspect of neck in adults.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Nuchal-type fibroma is benign. Surgical excision is the treatment of choice. With incomplete excision, local recurrence may occur in as many as 20% of patients {9921988; 32871744}, but metastasis does not occur.
