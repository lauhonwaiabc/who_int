2.1.13 Central chondrosarcoma, grades 2 and 3

Definition

Grade 2 and grade 3 central chondrosarcomas are intramedullary malignant neoplasms that produce cartilaginous matrix and are classified as intermediate-grade (grade 2) and high-grade (grade 3), respectively.

ICD-O coding

9220/3 Chondrosarcoma, grade 2

9220/3 Chondrosarcoma, grade 3

ICD-11 coding

2B50.Z & XH0Y34 Chondrosarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of unspecified sites & Chondrosarcoma, grade 3

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None.

Localization

The localization of grade 2 and grade 3 central chondrosarcomas is similar to that of central atypical cartilaginous tumour /chondrosarcoma, grade 1 (ACT/CS1), involving bones that undergo endochondral ossification. Commonly affected locations include the long tubular bones (particularly the proximal femur, proximal humerus, and distal femur) as well as flat bones, especially the pelvis (most frequently the ilium) and ribs. Less commonly, these tumours may arise in the spine or skull base.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Patients typically present with pain and/or swelling. Occasionally the lesion is discovered following a pathological fracture. Chondrosarcomas, particularly those arising in the pelvis and (less commonly) in the ribs, may reach a considerable size before becoming symptomatic.

Imaging

Central chondrosarcomas grades 2 and 3 are usually large (11 cm on average in long bones, although there is no cut-off size value) heterogeneous lesions and show aggressive imaging features, which can coexist with ACT/CS1 characteristics found in lower-grade areas {24337911}. Radiographically, large osteolytic lesions with less extensive matrix mineralization and aggressive pattern of bone destruction (permeative or moth-eaten) can be noted, often in association with bone expansion and cortical remodelling or breakthrough {12975513}. On MRI, the aggressive features include loss of entrapped fatty marrow, abundant myxoid areas (which show an osteolytic appearance on radiographs and CT, high signal intensity on T2-weighted MRI sequence, and no contrast enhancement), bone expansion with cortical thinning, cortical thickening, cortical breakthrough, periostitis, soft-tissue extension, and perilesional inflammatory reaction in bone and soft tissues {24337911; 19547983; 32429792; 34111983}. Of note, bone expansion can also be seen in central ACTs but is more common in higher-grade lesions {24337911}. On PET/CT, the maximum standardized uptake value allows the differentiation of chondrosarcoma grades 2–3 from ACT/enchondroma with 99% specificity when it is ≥ 4.5, although lower and indeterminate maximum standardized uptake values can often be detected {28608242}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of grades 2 and 3 central chondrosarcoma increased from 0.95 cases per 1 million person-years in 1989–1996 to 1.81 cases per 1 million person-years in 2005–2013 in the Netherlands Cancer Registry {30217294}. These tumours predominantly affect adults in the third to sixth decades of life, with an equal sex distribution. Patients with central chondrosarcoma arising in the setting of enchondromatosis tend to be younger than those with primary chondrosarcoma. In patients with enchondromatosis, the overall risk of developing chondrosarcoma is approximately 40%; this risk is lower when enchondromas are confined to the hands and feet, and higher when the long bones or bones of the pelvis are also involved {22147000}.

Etiology

Patients with enchondromatosis, carrying a somatic mosaic mutation in IDH1 or IDH2 {21598255; 22057236; 22057234}, are at increased risk of progression to chondrosarcoma, depending on the anatomical localization of the tumours (see section Enchondromatosis (IDH1, IDH2)).

Pathogenesis

At least half of grades 2 and 3 central chondrosarcomas harbour IDH1 or IDH2 variants, supporting their progression from enchondroma and ACT/CS1. During progression, genomic complexity is acquired {8402563; 11793445; 11793371} with involvement of the p53 and RB1 signalling pathways; the RB1 pathway is affected in 86% of high-grade chondrosarcomas {18624751; 14991902}, including by loss of p16 and overexpression and/or amplification of CDK4 {10709095; 18624751; 30808351; 23770606}. TP53 mutation is found in 20–49% of cases, while mutations in CDKN2A (encoding p16) are infrequent {10709095; 10502322; 29581779}. CDKN2A copy-number variation occurs in about 75% of high-grade central chondrosarcomas and not in enchondroma or central ACT/CS1 {25432631}. TERT promoter mutations can be found in addition to IDH mutations, especially IDH2, in grades 2 and 3 and dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma, while they are extremely rare in ACT/CS1 {36042521}. Mutations in the COL2A1 gene are found in about 45% of central chondrosarcomas {23770606; 25024164}. The COL2A1 gene encodes the alpha chain of type II collagen fibres, which are a major component of the cartilaginous matrix. Additional recurrent mutations involve YEATS2 (12.3%), EGFR (19%), NRAS p.(Gln61Lys) and p.(Gln61His) (up to 12%), and IHH signalling (18%) {23770606; 25024164; 23714727; 29581779}.

In a subset, tumour progression involves polyploidization of an initially hyperhaploid or hypodiploid cell population {19336518; 21949816}, which is more frequently seen but not exclusive to IDH wildtype tumours {36042521}.

For central chondrosarcoma, several active signalling pathways have been identified {20535132; 27166664}, which might provide novel treatment options, such as hedgehog signalling {11140704; 15685701; 16400033; 16755518}, mTOR signalling {30766792}, SRC and AKT {19602594}, and metabolic pathways {20459575; 16007578; 29576625; 28860121}. Members of the BCL2 family seem to play an important role in the chemoresistance of chondrosarcoma {22112972; 30242253}.

Macroscopic appearance

Resection specimens reveal a translucent, often lobular, bluish-grey or white cut surface. Cystic changes can be seen, as well as areas of myxoid degeneration. Calcifications can be visible as yellowish-white chalky areas. Erosion and destruction of the cortex with soft tissue extension are frequently present. Extensive sampling is essential to rule out chondroblastic osteosarcoma and areas of dedifferentiation.

Histopathology

These tumours are cellular with an overall lobular configuration with tumour lobules permeating and entrapping the pre-existing bone trabeculae {4064409}. The neoplastic cells are embedded within the cartilaginous matrix, which can be hyaline but more commonly exhibits variable degrees of myxoid change.

In grade 2, the cellularity is higher than in ACT/CS1, and mitotic figures are present. The nuclei can still be small and condensed, and severe condensation of the nuclei may hamper the detection of mitoses. More often, the nuclei vary in size, with open chromatin and a visible nucleolus. Nuclear atypia can be present but is usually not severe. Binucleation can be seen, and necrosis may be present.

In grade 3, mitoses are more readily identified, and the cells at the periphery of the mostly myxoid, highly cellular tumour lobules are spindled and less differentiated. These spindled cells gradually merge with the fibrous cells within the fibrous septa that contain numerous small vascular channels and immune cells. Cortical destruction with extension into adjacent soft tissue is commonly observed.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumours can be positive for S100 {25378537} and are negative for brachyury {16538613}. The p.(Arg132His) mutation–specific IDH1 antibody only recognizes a small percentage of the IDH mutations in chondrosarcoma, and its usefulness is therefore limited {21598255; 22057234; 22057236}.

Grading

Tumour grading should be based on the highest-grade area. However, histological grading is subject to interobserver variability {18852676; 17908885}. Histological grading does not appear to be useful for central chondrosarcomas located in the phalanx, and they are locally aggressive but rarely metastasize {10547545; 14599838}. Grade 2 chondrosarcoma is separated from ACT/CS1 predominantly on the basis of its increased cellularity, a greater degree of nuclear atypia, hyperchromasia, nuclear size, myxoid matrix changes, and the presence of mitoses. Grade 3 chondrosarcoma is highly cellular and more pleomorphic, mitoses are more easily found, and the cells at the periphery of the mostly myxoid tumour lobules are spindled and less differentiated.

Differential diagnosis

Chondrosarcoma in the skull base should be distinguished from chordoma, including the chondroid subtype. Grade 3 chondrosarcoma can resemble chondroblastic osteosarcoma.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of mutations in the isocitrate dehydrogenase genes IDH1 and IDH2 is not needed for diagnosis in most cases, though it may be relevant to select patients amenable to targeted treatment of the mutant protein using IDH mutant inhibitors {40100120}. Molecular analysis may be helpful in distinguishing these tumours from chondroblastic osteosarcoma, which lacks IDH1/2 variants {21598255; 23598960}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging, origin in the medulla of the bone; lobulated cartilaginous tumour with entrapment of pre-existing host bone; increased cellularity, myxoid matrix, and presence of mitoses; absence of bone formation associated with the malignant cells.

Staging

Staging is according to bone sarcoma protocols (see <>TNM staging of tumours of bone). See also the information on staging in section Bone tumours: Introduction.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients are usually treated with en bloc resection to obtain negative margins {18378543}. The reported 5-year overall survival rate for grade 2 chondrosarcoma is 74–99% and for grade 3 tumours 31–77% {22649023; 19411454; 30143018; 890662; 30217294; 24706044; 26633939}. The 10-year overall survival rate for grade 2 is 58–86% and for grade 3 tumours 26–55%. Even after 10 years, deaths due to disease can still occur {24706044}. Local recurrence rates are 19% for grade 2 and 26% for grade 3 chondrosarcoma {22649023}; 10–30% of the grade 2 tumours and 32–71% of the grade 3 tumours metastasize {22649023; 30143018; 890662; 9827715}. Chondrosarcomas with axial localization have a significantly lower survival than extremity chondrosarcoma {24706044; 25687868; 30143018; 30217294}. Some recent studies have shown that intraosseous conventional chondrosarcoma (with < 1 cm soft tissue extension), irrespective of grade, appears to have a very low metastatic potential {38033413; 32141094}. It is unclear at present whether the IDH variant is associated with outcome {29581779; 22057234; 28484589; 31615936}, though its presence may select patients for targeted treatment {40100120}.
