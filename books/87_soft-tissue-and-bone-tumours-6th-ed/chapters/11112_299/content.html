1.11.12 Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (epithelioid MPNST) is a malignant tumour with Schwannian differentiation, epithelioid morphology, and frequent loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression, arising de novo or in association with a pre-existing schwannoma.

ICD-O coding

9540/3 Epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B5E & XH4V81 Malignant nerve sheath tumour of peripheral nerves or autonomic nervous system, primary site & Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, epithelioid

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Epithelioid MPNST most commonly presents as a superficial mass in the extremities and trunk, although some tumours may be deep-seated or arise in visceral sites {25602794; 28452074}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a slowly enlarging, painful or painless mass.

Epidemiology

Epithelioid MPNST is a rare tumour accounting for approximately 1% of soft tissue sarcomas. It occurs over a wide age range with a median of approximately 40 years and an equal sex distribution {25602794}.

Etiology

Epithelioid MPNST is typically not associated with neurofibromatosis type 1.

Pathogenesis

Epithelioid MPNSTs are distinct from conventional MPNSTs molecularly. Genomic inactivation of SMARCB1 resulting in SMARCB1 (INI1) loss by immunohistochemistry is observed in approximately 70% of cases {25602794; 30864974}. The SMARCB1 genomic alterations include mostly large arm-level, homozygous deletions and (less often) intragenic deletions or nonsense mutations {36088476}. Loss of SMARCB1 may also be seen in a subset (42%) of benign epithelioid schwannomas {28368924}, mostly due to heterozygous deletions {36088476}. Unlike conventional MPNST, epithelioid MPNST only uncommonly harbours genetic alterations in NF1 or CDKN2A/CDKN2B and lacks alterations in genes encoding PRC2 core components (EED and SUZ12) {25240281; 30864974}.

Macroscopic appearance

Epithelioid MPNST is generally well circumscribed with a cystic, firm, or lobular cut surface. Haemorrhage and necrosis are present in a subset {25602794}.

Histopathology

Epithelioid MPNST is composed of plump, relatively uniform, epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and round vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli. Tumours demonstrate a characteristic multilobular growth pattern and are often associated with an extracellular myxoid or hyalinized matrix {1746681; 25602794}. Although rare, epithelioid MPNST is the most common type of MPNST to arise from a pre-existing schwannoma {30864974; 34050984}.

Unlike conventional MPNSTs, epithelioid MPNSTs show strong and diffuse staining for S100 and SOX10, in the absence of melanoma markers. They retain the expression of H3K27me3, and most show loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression {30864974; 25602794; 28452074}. Epithelioid MPNST may also be positive for keratin.

Although epithelioid schwannomas and epithelioid MPNSTs can show morphological and immunohistochemical overlap, the presence of infiltrative margins, geographical necrosis, and/or significant nuclear atypia supports a diagnosis of epithelioid MPNST. However, the distinction can be challenging, especially in cases with mild nuclear atypia and absence of necrosis.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows findings similar to those of MPNST with predominant epithelioid cells {22434579; 26646298}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sarcoma with solid or nested architecture and epithelioid cytomorphology, prominent nucleoli, geographical necrosis, and often a limited degree of nuclear pleomorphism; identification of Schwann cell differentiation (S100/SOX10 diffuse positivity).

Desirable: loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression (in the majority of cases).

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

Epithelioid MPNST has the potential for malignant behaviour including local recurrence, distant metastases, and death from disease {25602794; 28452074}.
