1.3.37 Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma

Definition

Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma is a malignant fibroblastic neoplasm characterized by alternating collagenous and myxoid areas, deceptively bland spindle cells with a whorling growth pattern, and arcades of small blood vessels.

ICD-O coding

8840/3 Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH4V76 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: hyalinizing spindle cell tumour with giant rosettes.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common sites of involvement are the proximal extremities and trunk, usually subfascial in depth {21921785; 17721195; 21550274; 17721195; 38008029}. Less common locations include central body sites (abdominal cavity, retroperitoneum, mediastinum, intestines) and superficial soft tissues, with the latter being affected relatively more commonly in children {21677541; 29385413; 15644777}. Origin at other anatomical sites is rare.

Clinical features

Presentation is typically with a painless mass. In many cases, the mass has reportedly been present for > 5 years.

Epidemiology

Although relatively rare among sarcomas, the true incidence of low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma may have been underestimated before the availability of ancillary diagnostic markers and because of its propensity to mimic other soft tissue neoplasms histologically. Its incidence is 0.18 cases per million, that is, 0.6% of all soft tissue sarcomas {23818812}. There is a slight male predilection, and tumours typically arise in young adults (median: 36 years), but the overall age range is wide, and as many as 20% of cases occur in patients aged < 18 years {15644777; 21921785; 11023096; 17721195; 38008029; 39466087}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The cytogenetic hallmark of low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma is the t(7;16)(q33;p11), which is present, often as the sole change, in two thirds of cases. Another 25% show supernumerary ring chromosomes {12960807; 15139001; 15640831}. Both aberrations result in the fusion of the 5′ part of the FUS gene in 16p11 with the 3′ part of CREB3L2 in 7q33; a chimeric FUS::CREB3L2 transcript is seen in > 90% of cases {15640831; 16931951; 17721195; 21536545}. Rare cases have one of the fusion variants FUS::CREB3L1 or EWSR1::CREB3L1 {15640831; 23588368; 24896634}. Apart from recurrent microdeletions in association with the t(7;16) chromosomal breakpoints and gain of 7q in cases with ring chromosomes, no recurrent genomic imbalances or SNVs have been found in low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma {21536545; 28939748}. The chimeric FUS::CREB3L2 protein functions as an aberrant transcription factor, causing deregulated expression of CREB3L2 target genes {21536545}. Many of these target genes, such as CD24 and MUC4, are shared with sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma, with which low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma overlaps with regard to underlying gene fusions but differs with regard to additional genomic imbalances {28939748}. Rare morphologically similar tumours that lack MUC4 expression harbour fusions involving YAP1 and KMT2A; such tumours have distinct gene expression and methylation profiles {31913156; 39641785}. It is unclear whether these tumours should be classified separately.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, low-grade fibromyxoid sarcomas are well-circumscribed, fibrous, and often focally mucoid. Tumour size ranges from 10 to > 200 mm in greatest dimension (median: 50 mm) {38008029}.

Histopathology

Low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma is composed of collagenous areas and myxoid nodules. An abrupt transition between these two areas is typical. The tumour cells are bland, spindled, and sometimes plump, and they grow in short fascicles or in a whorling pattern. Mitotic activity is generally inconspicuous. Arcades of small vessels and arteriole-sized vessels with perivascular sclerosis are seen. Occasionally, the vessels have a haemangiopericytoma-like pattern {21415703}. Approximately 20% of cases contain collagen rosettes – a central core of hyalinized collagen surrounded by a cuff of epithelioid tumour cells {11023096; 9414192}. In some cases, areas of sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma are present, reflecting the overlap between these two entities {21921785; 17721195; 39466087}. Unusual features (< 5% of cases) include the presence of focal pleomorphism or nuclear atypia, hypercellularity, epithelioid or round cell features, and heterotopic ossification {21415703; 21921785; 17721195}. By immunohistochemistry, tumour cells show strong, diffuse cytoplasmic expression of MUC4, an epithelial glycoprotein, in 99% of cases {21415703}. MUC4 is highly sensitive and specific to low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma and sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma among fibroblastic and neural tumours. Expression of EMA is present in 80% of cases and focal expression of SMA in approximately 30% {21415703; 17721195; 11023096}. Tumours with fusions involving YAP1 and KMT2A lack MUC4 expression {31913156}.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show variable cellularity, with bland spindle cells showing elongated, bland to mildly atypical nuclei arranged in fascicles in loosely cohesive, variably myxoid tissue fragments, and fibrotic collagenous fragments in a background of dispersed stripped nuclei {10227597; 18637810; 19899126}. The FNAB features are not distinctive and overlap with a broad differential diagnosis of benign myxoid spindle cell neoplasms, requiring immunohistochemistry for MUC4, FISH, or next-generation sequencing for diagnosis.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

If MUC4 is negative or not available, identification of FUS::CREB3L2 (or other rare variants) offers molecular genetic support for the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: alternating fibrous and myxoid areas; bland spindle cells in a whorling or short fascicular growth pattern.

Desirable: MUC4 expression; FUS gene rearrangement in select cases.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system is applicable.

Prognosis and prediction

Although low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma shows low rates of recurrence and metastasis in the first 5–10 years after excision of the primary tumour (5% and 1–5%, respectively), rates are higher with long-term follow-up {3673943; 8333558; 21921785; 11023096; 17721195; 38008029; 39466087}. A large study with long follow-up showed recurrences, metastases, and death from disease in 64%, 45%, and 42% of patients, respectively {21921785}. Metastases occurred as long as 45 years after primary excision (median: 5 years), and the median interval to tumour-related death was 15 years {21921785}. The most common metastatic sites are the lungs and pleura. Histological features generally do not correlate with clinical behaviour {21921785; 11023096}, although tumours with areas of sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma or round cell morphology tend to pursue a more aggressive clinical course {21921785; 38008029; 39466087}, similar to that expected for sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma.
