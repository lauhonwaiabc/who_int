1.5.10 Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma is a rarely metastasizing endothelial neoplasm histologically mimicking a myoid tumour or epithelioid sarcoma.

ICD-O coding

9138/1 Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma

ICD-11 coding

2E81.0Z Neoplastic haemangioma, unspecified

Related terminology

Acceptable: epithelioid sarcoma–like haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma usually arises on the lower limbs (55% of cases); the upper limbs and trunk are less commonly affected (20% each); tumours rarely occur on the head or neck (5%) {12502927; 1371711; 21263239; 23381465; 31980959}.

Clinical features

About half of affected patients present with painless nodules and half with painful nodules. In approximately 60% of patients, this disease is multifocal, often involving multiple tissue planes {21263239}. Most patients (75%) present with cutaneous and subcutaneous nodules. About 50% of affected patients have intramuscular lesions, and 20% of patients have lytic bone lesions {12502927; 1371711; 21263239; 23381465}. Some patients have intraosseous lesions without soft tissue involvement {26872012}. By PET, the tumours in most patients are highly avid for 18F-FDG; this technique can be used to visualize clinically occult deep lesions in patients who present with cutaneous nodules {21263239}.

Epidemiology

Pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma is rare. Reported cases show a marked male predominance (M:F ratio: 3.5:1), with peak of occurrence in young adults (mean age: 30 years) {21263239; 31980959}. Only 20% of patients are aged > 40 years at presentation.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The initial genetic hallmark of pseudomyogenic haemangioendothelioma was a balanced translocation t(7;19)(q22;q13), resulting in the fusion of SERPINE1 to FOSB {21536240; 24374978}. Subsequently, an alternative ACTB::FOSB gene fusion was identified in half of the cases {30256258}. These fusions lead to upregulation of FOSB, probably by a promoter-swapping mechanism, because most of the FOSB gene is retained in the fusion (fused at exon 2). SERPINE1 (fusion occurs in non-coding exon 1) is normally highly expressed in vascular cells {24374978}, and ACTB (fused at exon 3) is a ubiquitously expressed gene {30256258}. FOSB is a member of the FOS family of transcription factors, which includes FOS, FOSB, FOSL1, and FOSL2. They encode leucine zipper proteins that can dimerize with proteins of the JUN family, thereby forming the transcription factor complex AP-1, regulating cell proliferation, differentiation, angiogenesis, and survival. Upregulation of FOSB causes activation of the AP-1 complex. Overexpression of the retained part of FOSB in endothelial cells affects angiogenesis in vitro, with the retained part acting as an active transcription factor capable of regulating its own transcription {29511030}. The clinicopathological features and behaviour do not differ between pseudomyogenic haemangioendotheliomas with SERPINE1::FOSB and ACTB::FOSB fusions, although tumours with the ACTB variant more often present as solitary lesions {30256258}. Other rare gene partners to FOSB that have been reported include WWTR1, CLTC, MED15, EGFL7, and POTEI {31243110; 38337858; 32749039; 33550637; 35582841}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, margins are poorly defined, and the cut surface is firm and grey or white. Most tumour nodules are 10–25 mm in greatest dimension; only 10% of tumours are > 30 mm in size {21263239}.

Histopathology

The tumour is composed of sheets and loose fascicles of plump spindle cells with abundant, brightly eosinophilic cytoplasm, sometimes mimicking rhabdomyoblasts {21263239}. A minor component of cells with epithelioid cytomorphology is often present, but sometimes epithelioid cells predominate {1371711}. Intraosseous lesions are often dominated by epithelioid cells with densely eosinophilic cytoplasm, associated with reactive woven bone, haemorrhage, and osteoclast-like giant cells {26872012}. The tumour cells contain vesicular nuclei with generally small nucleoli. The degree of nuclear atypia is usually mild, and mitotic activity is scarce. About 10% of tumours show notable pleomorphism or large nucleoli {21263239}. Occasional tumours may contain myxoid stroma. About 50% of cases contain prominent stromal neutrophils. The tumours show infiltrative margins.

By immunohistochemistry, these tumours show diffuse expression of keratins (with AE1/AE3 but not MNF116) and the endothelial transcription factors FLI1 and ERG {1371711; 21263239}. About 50% of cases are positive for CD31. Focal expression of SMA is observed in one third of tumours. CD34 is negative in all cases, and SMARCB1 (INI1) expression is retained {21263239}. Nuclear staining for FOSB is a consistent finding {27515856; 28009608}.

Cytology

A single case report of FNAB shows moderate cellularity with plump spindle and epithelioid cells showing abundant tapering eosinophilic cytoplasm, mild nuclear atypia with vesicular chromatin, and distinct nucleoli, arranged in small tissue fragments with intercellular fibrillary stroma {32886445}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of FOSB rearrangement can be helpful for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: plump spindle or (less commonly) epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and vesicular chromatin; fascicular or sheet-like growth pattern; expression of ERG and keratins (AE1/AE3).

Desirable: demonstration of FOSB immunoexpression or FOSB gene rearrangement (in selected cases).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately 60% of patients with this tumour type experience local recurrences (often multiple) or develop additional nodules in the same anatomical region {21263239}. The interval between excision of the primary tumour and recurrence is usually short, within 1–2 years. The relationship between margin status and recurrence has not been established. Regional lymph node and distant metastases are uncommon (< 5%), most often occurring years or decades after presentation, rarely within a short interval {21263239; 31980959}. Metastatic sites may include lungs, bones, and soft tissues. Histological features do not predict metastasis.
