1.12.2 Juxta-articular myxoma

Definition

Juxta-articular myxoma is a benign myxoid tumour morphologically similar to intramuscular/cellular myxoma, arising in the vicinity of large joints.

ICD-O coding

8840/0 Myxoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E84.Z & XH6Q84 Benign fibrogenic or myofibrogenic tumor, site unknown and Myxoma NOS

Related terminology

Acceptable: periarticular myxoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Juxta-articular myxoma usually arises adjacent to large joints, with the knee being the most commonly affected site (> 85%). Other less commonly affected sites include the hip, shoulder, elbow, and ankle {1592386}; rare locations include the temporomandibular joint {26594976} and hands and feet {15680575}.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with swelling or a mass that may be associated with tenderness or pain in the vicinity of large joints. A subset of patients have severe degenerative joint disease or a meniscal tear. The duration of symptoms ranges from weeks to years. On imaging studies, the appearance of a juxta-articular myxoma is similar to that of an intramuscular myxoma {7747183}.

Epidemiology

Juxta-articular myxoma has been described to occur over a wide age range, including rarely in children; however, they typically affect patients in the fifth and sixth decades of life, and nearly three quarters are described in men {1592386; 20157818}.

Etiology

In most patients, the etiology is unknown. A minority of patients have a history of degenerative joint disease or trauma, which may be coincidental.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours usually range in size between 20 and 60 mm, but tumours as large as 130 mm have been described. The cut surface is gelatinous and myxoid, and cystic degeneration is commonly seen {1592386}.

Histopathology

Juxta-articular myxoma is poorly circumscribed, demonstrating infiltration into the surrounding periarticular soft tissue. The tumour is generally paucicellular and composed of bland spindle-shaped cells with uniform ovoid nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, and eosinophilic cytoplasm embedded in a myxoid stroma with few blood vessels. Increased cellularity can be seen (similar to cellular myxoma); however, mitoses are scarce. In the majority of cases, cyst-like spaces lined by a delicate fibrin layer or a thicker layer composed of collagen may be seen. In a subset of cases, haemorrhage, chronic inflammation, and haemosiderin/fibrin deposition may be seen, probably because of trauma. These reactive changes may also be seen in recurrent tumours. Immunohistochemical stains are generally not useful for diagnosis; however, the lesional cells may express CD34 and/or SMA and are negative for S100. Intratumoural muciphages may be detected by histiocyte markers such as CD163.

Cytology

Aspirates show the same features of intramuscular myxoma {33168472}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: soft tissue around large joints; paucicellular, myxoid, cytologically bland tumour.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence occurs in as many as one third of patients, sometimes more than once. Malignant transformation does not occur {1592386}.
