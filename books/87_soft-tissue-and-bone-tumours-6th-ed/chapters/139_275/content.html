1.3.9 Calcifying fibrous tumour

Definition

Calcifying fibrous tumour is a benign paucicellular fibroblastic neoplasm with dense stromal collagen, chronic inflammation, and variably prominent psammomatous and/or dystrophic calcification, which often arises on serosal surfaces.

XXX

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Acceptable: calcifying fibrous pseudotumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

In soft tissues, calcifying fibrous tumour may involve subcutaneous fat or deep somatic tissue, and has been reported to occur on the trunk, neck, and extremities {8470765; 12232572}. Tumours often arise at serosal sites, specifically pleura, peritoneum, and paratesticular serosal surfaces {12232572; 15660285; 28153506; 11172201}. Peritoneal tumours may be multifocal {18022134; 22689663}.

Tumours also often involve the gastrointestinal tract (small intestine, followed by colon and stomach), and may be centred in submucosa, muscularis propria, mesenteric fat, or serosa {28153506; 20090503}. Origin in other visceral organs is rare.

Clinical features

The presenting symptoms depend on the site of origin. Soft tissue lesions usually present as a painless mass. Large tumours in the gastrointestinal tract may result in pain, obstruction, or intussusception. Tumours are often detected incidentally, either by imaging or intraoperatively.

Epidemiology

There is a wide age range and no sex predilection. Rare familial cases occur, often as multiple tumours arising in young adults {12817428}. A large case series showed a trimodal age distribution, with peaks at 0–4 years, mid-twenties, and mid-thirties, and an M:F ratio of 1:1.27 {27196478}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Tumours are usually sporadic with rare familial cases {12817428}. The molecular pathogenesis is unknown. Although a relationship with inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour has been proposed, ALK is typically not expressed by immunohistochemistry, and rearrangements of ALK, ROS1, or RET have not been identified in calcifying fibrous tumour, arguing against this hypothesis {31202195; 12232572; 11172201}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, tumours are typically well circumscribed and firm, with a white to pale tan, variably gritty cut surface. Size can range from subcentimetre lesions to tumours as large as 250 mm (average: 50 mm) {12232572}.

Histopathology

Calcifying fibrous tumour is characteristically a paucicellular circumscribed mass with abundant hyalinized collagen, within which are scattered, bland, long spindled fibroblasts. These lesional cells have pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and are often inconspicuous among the collagen. There may be clefts or cracks within the collagenous stroma. A plasma cell infiltrate is common within the lesion, and many cases also have a surrounding cuff of lymphocytes and plasma cells or lymphoid aggregates, similar to gastrointestinal-type schwannoma.

Admixed calcifications may be of dystrophic or psammomatous type and may be abundant or sparse. Around 20% do not have calcifications and may be more difficult to recognize (non-calcifying calcifying fibrous tumour). By immunohistochemistry, the spindled fibroblasts express CD34 and rarely SMA, whereas KIT, DOG1, ALK, desmin, caldesmon, STAT6, β-catenin, and S100 are negative {11504838; 12232572; 28153506; 20090503; 31355265}.

Cytology

Calcifying fibrous tumour has not been reported in the FNAB literature.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: circumscribed hypocellular densely collagenous mass; scattered bland spindled fibroblasts and a sparse lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate.

Desirable: admixed dystrophic or psammomatous calcifications.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Calcifying fibrous tumour is benign. Excision is usually curative, especially in gastrointestinal tumours. Recurrences occasionally occur and are more common in soft tissue lesions or those at sites less amenable to complete excision {8470765; 12232572}.
