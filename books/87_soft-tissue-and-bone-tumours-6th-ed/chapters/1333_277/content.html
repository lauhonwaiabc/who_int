1.3.33 Nodular necrotizing fibroblastic tumour

Definition

Nodular necrotizing fibroblastic tumour is a superficial mesenchymal neoplasm composed of epithelioid cells with a brisk inflammatory infiltrate and, in many cases, central necrosis.

ICD-O coding

XXX

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Acceptable: nodular necrotizing fibroblastic sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours tend to occur in the skin and subcutis of the limbs, trunk, or acral sites, the latter of which account for less than half of cases. No bone involvement has been reported so far.

Clinical features

They present as small superficial masses of < 50 mm (size range: 10–45 mm). One patient developed lung metastases {36344258}.

Epidemiology

Nodular necrotizing fibroblastic tumour is rare, with < 20 reported examples in the literature to date. It occurs across a wide age range (27–71 years), slightly more often in men {35546636; 36344258; 39822017; 40080864}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most reported examples harbour YAP1::MAML2 fusions and a minor subset with concomitant RAF (BRAF or RAF1) fusions {36344258; 35546636; 39822017}. These tumours show flat genomic profiles that usually lack the VGLL3 amplification associated with myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma (MIFS). Gene expression profiles overlap partly with those of MIFS in initial studies {35546636}. The only clinically malignant case described so far also harboured RBMS3::RAF1 fusion and showed VGLL3 mRNA overexpression {36344258}.

Macroscopic appearance

This tumour appears as a well-circumscribed mass centred on dermis and hypodermis.

Histopathology

Nodular necrotizing fibroblastic tumour shows a nodular or multilobular growth pattern. Tumour lobules are sometimes separated by fibrous septa showing florid lymphoplasmacytic inflammation. Some lobules show central necrosis. Within the tumour nodules, there are neoplastic cells dispersed singly and in clusters in a florid inflammatory infiltrate that variably shows histiocytes, lymphocytes, plasma cells, and neutrophils. The neoplastic cells are large, with copious, dense, amphophilic to eosinophilic cytoplasm, usually with emperipolesis of inflammatory cells. The nuclei are large and vesicular, with prominent red nucleoli. Most tumours show fewer than 3 mitoses/mm2, while the sole case reported with metastasis had a high mitotic index of > 20 mitoses/10 HPF (conversion to mitoses/mm2 is not possible, as the HPF diameter was not indicated in this study) {36344258}. Nodular necrotizing fibroblastic tumour lacks the myxoid stroma seen in MIFS. About 40% of tumours express broad-spectrum keratins. Immunohistochemistry using antibodies against the C-terminus of YAP1 is negative in neoplastic cells {35546636}, as has been reported in other tumour types harbouring fusions involving the N-terminal end of YAP1 {34148063}. Nodular necrotizing fibroblastic tumour shows morphological overlap with MIFS, although the latter lacks lobulated architecture and more frequently shows myxoid stroma and infiltrative growth.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of YAP1::MAML2 fusion can be helpful {35546636; 36344258; 39822017; 40080864}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: well-defined nodules of neoplastic cells with surrounding and/or admixed inflammation; tumour cells with copious, eosinophilic to amphophilic cytoplasm and enlarged, vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli; often central necrosis.

Desirable: evidence of YAP1::MAML2 fusion.

Staging

Not available

Prognosis and prediction

There are limited outcome data as yet. In the index series, no tumours recurred or metastasized, and one tumour spontaneously regressed, suggesting indolent behaviour {35546636}. There has been a single report of a YAP1::MAML2-fusion sarcoma giving rise to metastasis {36344258}; this tumour showed sheet-like growth of neoplastic cells, concomitant VGLL3 overexpression and RBMS3::RAF1 fusion, and > 20 mitoses/10 HPF {36344258} (see Histopathology, above), a much higher mitotic count than that in most reported cases {35546636}. More work is needed to determine whether conventional pathological parameters such as mitotic index, tumour size, or VGLL3 amplification might predict outcomes.
