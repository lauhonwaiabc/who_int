2.7.9 TFCP2-rearranged rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition

TFCP2-rearranged rhabdomyosarcoma (TFCP2-RMS) is a high-grade sarcoma with skeletal muscle differentiation, characterized by the fusion of TFCP2 to EWSR1 or FUS and a predilection for bone and the head and neck region.

Localization

TFCP2-RMS arises mostly in bone and less frequently in soft tissue {31383960; 33382123; 37422156; 38168093}. There is a striking predilection for bones in the head and neck region, including the skull bones (25%), mandible (21%), and maxilla (19%), and for infiltration into the surrounding soft tissue. Less commonly affected regions include the pelvic bone (11%), truncal and extremity soft tissues (9.5%), vertebrae (7%), and femur (2%). Individual cases have been reported in the mediastinum, peritoneum, rib, and urinary bladder {38168093; 31383960; 36031724; 30720533; 38114270; 40361368; 37422156}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a large, rapidly growing swelling, with pain and signs related to compression. Tumour size varies widely (median diameter: 60 mm) {31383960; 38114270}. Exceptional cases with preceding, slowly growing tumours over several months, initially diagnosed as benign lesions, have been observed {36350070; 38168093}. Most patients present with localized tumours at diagnosis, but metastases frequently develop subsequently, most often in lung, bones, and lymph nodes {38168093; 31383960}. The evolution is typically marked by a rapid locoregional destructive growth.

Imaging features

Imaging shows large, poorly defined, infiltrating and destructive masses, commonly centred in bone and invading soft tissue {31383960}.

Epidemiology

TFCP2-RMS typically affects young adults (median age: 34.6 years, range: 7–86 years) {31383960; 33382123; 38114270; 37422156; 40361368; 38168093}; 22% of cases arise in patients aged ≤ 18 years. There is a slight female predominance (male, 36%; female, 46%).

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

TFCP2-RMS is defined by the presence of a translocation fusing TFCP2, located at 12q13.12, to either FUS or EWSR1, resulting in the expression of the fusion proteins FUS::TFCP2 (68%) or EWSR1::TFCP2 (32%). The breakpoint occurs at the 5′ end of exon 2 of TFCP2, preserving all functional protein domains, including the DNA-binding domain {29431183; 38168093}. TFCP2-RMS is characterized by gene expression and DNA methylation profiles distinct from those of other rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes {29431183; 38168093}. The DNA methylation profiles show greater similarity with UPS than with other rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes {38168093}. UBP1, a transcription factor belonging to the same family as TFCP2, may give rise to similar tumours when fused to EWSR1 {34877752}.

TFCP2-RMS harbours highly rearranged, commonly aneuploid genomic profiles, and occasionally chromothripsis {29758589; 30477883; 38168093}, consistent with homologous repair deficiency probably promoted by the TFCP2 fusions {38168093}. Recurrent SNVs are rare, and homozygous loss of CDKN2A/MTAP is common (86%) {29758589; 29431183; 38168093}. High ALK expression is often observed, probably because of direct transcriptional activation by FUS::TFCP2 binding to an upstream region of ALK {38168093}. In 61% of cases, ALK is affected by intragenic deletions often combined with alternative splicing of exon 17, resulting in the expression of ALK protein isoforms lacking parts of the extracellular domain (ALK-ST1, ALK-ST2, ALK-ST3). These shortened ALK variants are highly oncogenic and sensitive to ALK inhibitors with varying efficiencies {38168093}. Most TFCP2-RMSs display strong overexpression of a truncated TERT variant (Δex1-2), with unknown function caused by binding of FUS::TFCP2 to the intron just before exon 3 of TERT {38168093}. Occasional concomitant alterations include MDM2 amplification, ALK fusion, and mutations of TP53 {31383960; 38114270; 36350070; 33382123; 29758589; 30477883}.

In 2 cases in the head and neck, early lesions preceding the development of an aggressive mass have been reported, consisting of spindle cells expressing desmin and myogenin/MYOD1 loosely arranged in the dermis and/or subcutaneous tissue without proliferation or destructive growth and associated with the TFCP2 fusion {38168093}.

Macroscopic appearance

TFCP2-RMS appears as a solid, white-grey tumour with necrosis and bone destruction {31383960; 40361368; 37422156}.

Histopathology

TFCP2-RMSs are usually biphasic, with spindle cell and epithelioid areas in solid sheets or fascicles, with scant accompanying stroma. Rarely, TFCP2-RMSs are composed purely of epithelioid/rhabdoid, spindle, or round cells. Tumour nuclei are large, displaying brisk mitotic activity and dotted with conspicuous nucleoli. Overall, tumour nuclei are monomorphic but may show focal hyperchromasia or even pleomorphism. Necrosis is common.

Immunohistochemistry

Virtually all cases are positive for cytokeratins (AE1/AE3) and desmin. All, by definition, express MYOD1 and/or myogenin, with MYOD1 being more sensitive and myogenin occasionally negative. ALK expression is detected in 88% of cases, and there is occasional expression of p63, CK7, SATB2, p53, CD30, CD4, S100, h-caldesmon, and SMA; SMARCB1 (INI1) expression is retained {29758589; 32504289; 32556562; 33382123; 37422156; 38168093}.

The differential diagnosis includes metastatic carcinoma because of the keratin expression and epithelioid features, particularly in older individuals {30948206; 37057803}. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation (triton tumour) may be suspected in case of scant S100 staining, especially if the tumour is purely epithelioid. Sarcomas with PATZ1 fusions can enter the differential diagnosis and often show a predominant spindle to round cell cytomorphology; they often express myogenin, MYOD1, and S100 and are associated with PATZ1 translocations {30948206; 37057803}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The diagnosis can be confirmed by molecular testing using FISH with a break-apart probe for TFCP2 or by sequencing. A unique case harbouring an alternative EWSR1::UBP1 fusion has been reported {34877752}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: an epithelioid, spindle cell, or mixed cytological appearance; rhabdomyosarcomatous phenotype (desmin and MYOD1 or myogenin expression); coexpression of AE1/AE3; TFCP2 rearrangement.

Desirable: ALK expression.

Staging

No available data

Prognosis and prediction

TFCP2-RMS is aggressive and associated with a poor outcome, often presenting at a locally advanced stage with bone and soft tissue destruction {31383960; 33382123}. Distant metastasis is present at diagnosis or follow-up in 30% of cases, usually to bone, lungs, or lymph nodes {31383960; 38114270}. The estimated median survival time is 10 months, but follow-up data are limited {31383960; 33382123; 38114270}.

Most TFCP2-RMSs recur after surgery and progress despite aggressive multimodal therapy {31383960; 32556562; 38168093}. ALK inhibitors have shown limited efficacy at advanced stages but may have some effect combined with irradiation {31470995; 31965718; 39302072; 40361368}. The UBP1 variant showed response to a platinum-based chemotherapy regimen {34877752}.
