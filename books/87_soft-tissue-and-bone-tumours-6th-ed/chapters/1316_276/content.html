1.3.16 PRRX1-rearranged fibroblastic tumour

Definition

PRRX1-rearranged fibroblastic tumour is a benign spindle cell neoplasm characterized by PRRX1 rearrangements, most often with NCOA1.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours most commonly arise in subcutaneous tissue of the trunk or proximal extremities, with a smaller subset presenting in the head and neck {31008539; 34272753; 37338620; 35583270; 39887195; 34647172; 38986510; 40159029}. Rare dermal and intramuscular cases have been reported.

Clinical features

Patients report a slow-growing mass with a preoperative duration ranging from weeks to years. Tumour size is typically < 50 mm, although reported cases reach up to 158 mm. The tumours may be painful or pigmented {35583270; 37338620}.

Epidemiology

PRRX1-rearranged fibroblastic tumours are rare with less than 30 cases in the literature, although this probably represents an underestimate {31008539; 34272753; 37338620; 35583270; 39887195; 34647172; 38986510; 40159029}. These tumours present over a wide age range with a median in the fourth decade of life {41036703}. To date, one paediatric tumour has been reported {40958368}. Female individuals are more commonly affected than male individuals, with an M:F ratio of 1.5:1 {41036703}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The vast majority of tumours are characterized by PRRX1 fusions to NCOA1. The PRRX1 breakpoint is consistently located at exon 1, while the NCOA1 breakpoint is located at exon 13 or (less often) exon 15 {31008539; 34272753; 38986510}. Less common partners of PRRX1 include NCOA2 and KMT2D {31913156; 37338620; 40159029}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, tumours present as a circumscribed, tan-white, firm to rubbery mass ranging from 20 to 160 mm (median: 40 mm).

Histopathology

Tumours present as a circumscribed, occasionally multilobular mass with elongated, irregular, and ectatic vascular spaces evident at low magnification. Closer inspection shows a predominantly paucicellular neoplasm composed of bland spindle cells arranged haphazardly or in short fascicles within a hyalinized background. Focal increased cellularity, myxoid/oedematous stromal change, or dense eosinophilic ropy or wire-like collagen are often appreciated. Tumours with KMT2D alterations appear to have especially prominent perivascular hyalinization, and a subset of superficial cases may show scattered clusters of pigmented dendritic cells {37338620; 31913156; 35583270}. The tumour cells have monomorphic ovoid nuclei with small nucleoli and scant pale cytoplasm with tapering processes. Mitotic activity is inconspicuous, at < 0.5 mitoses/mm2 (equating to < 1 mitosis/10 HPF measuring 0.5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm2 in area), and necrosis is absent.

By immunohistochemistry, the tumour cells may show patchy staining for S100 and/or SOX10 but are usually negative for SMA, desmin, CD34, STAT6, MUC4, and estrogen receptor {31008539; 34272753; 37338620; 35583270; 39887195; 34647172; 38986510; 40159029}. Pigmented cases may express melanocytic markers {35583270}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of a PRRX1 rearrangement helps differentiate this tumour from other fibroblastic neoplasms.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a paucicellular, circumscribed, bland spindle cell neoplasm; hyalinized stroma with variably wire-like and ropy collagen; prominent ectatic and irregular vascular spaces; demonstration of PRRX1 rearrangement.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Although follow-up data are limited, this tumour type appears to be benign {31008539; 34647172; 37338620}. To date, no local recurrences or distant metastases have been reported following conservative excision, with follow-up in some studies approaching 2 years.
