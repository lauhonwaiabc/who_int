1.2.8 Chondroid lipoma

Definition

Chondroid lipoma is a benign neoplasm composed of small lipoblasts of varying degrees of differentiation that are embedded within a chondroid to hyalinized matrix and intermingled with mature adipose tissue.

ICD-O coding

8862/0 Chondroid lipoma

ICD-11 coding

2E80.0Z & XH7WX8 Lipoma, unspecified & Chondroid lipoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours most commonly involve the limbs, followed by the limb girdles and trunk; occasionally, they involve the head and neck, hands and feet, and retroperitoneum. They typically involve skeletal muscle and deep subcutaneous adipose tissue that abuts muscular fascia {23672313; 8214255; 10591950}.

Clinical features

Patients present with a painless mass, usually of short duration, although it may (rarely) be several years. Enlargement, occasionally associated with intralesional haemorrhage, and pain are less common symptoms.

Imaging findings most commonly show a well-demarcated, lobulated, heterogeneous fat mass on MRI because of the variable distributions of fat and chondroid areas. A fat-ring sign due to peripheral tumour enhancement after gadolinium administration has been noted in some chondroid lipomas with detailed imaging studies {25759781}. Roentgenograms and CT may show calcifications {18259746}. However, none of these imaging modalities can reliably distinguish chondroid lipoma from other benign and malignant fatty lesions; a biopsy is necessary for unequivocal diagnosis {18259746; 29954455}.

Epidemiology

Analysis of 56 reported cases of chondroid lipoma reveals a broad age range, from 14 to 75 years, with most patients presenting in the fifth decade of life, followed by those in the fourth and third decades of life. The M:F ratio is approximately 1:2–4 {8214255; 7628841; 25759781; 18259746; 29954455; 20607705; 14722764; 10524534; 8865499; 9950168; 14722764; 36734585; 10591950; 22379550; 23672313; 28969148; 11202327; 9118432; 7573689; 8734708}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Chondroid lipoma features a recurrent t(11;16)(q13;p13) chromosomal translocation that leads to fusion of the ZFTA (C11orf95) and MRTFB (MKL2) genes {20607705; 23672313}. ZFTA appears to confer three functions on oncogenic fusion proteins: nuclear translocation, chromatin binding, and the recruitment of chromatin modifiers {33741711}. MRTFB functions as a coactivator of the transcription factor SRF that regulates a broad range of cellular processes such as organization of the cytoskeleton, cell migration, cell growth, and differentiation {16778073}.

Variant ZFTA fusion partnerships involving NCOA1 and RELA have also been described in a chondroid lipoma and a distinct liposarcoma with overlapping chondroid lipoma histology (malignant chondroid lipoma), respectively {36734585; 36201637}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours are well circumscribed and lobulated with a yellowish cut surface that is firm to rubbery; haemorrhage is variable. Tumour sizes range from 18 to 120 mm with a median of 45 mm; 90% are 20–80 mm in greatest dimension {8214255; 7628841; 25759781; 18259746; 29954455; 20607705; 14722764; 10524534; 8865499; 9950168; 14722764; 36734585; 10591950; 22379550; 23672313; 28969148; 11202327; 9118432; 7573689; 8734708}.

Histopathology

Lesions are usually encapsulated and lobulated and situated in the deep subcutis abutting muscular fascia or within skeletal muscle. Variable amounts of mature adipose tissue are admixed with chondroid-appearing areas. The latter contain rather small lipoblasts in various stages of differentiation, with small, bland, and sometimes convoluted nuclei with cytoplasmic pseudoinclusions that are indented by univacuolated to multivacuolated cytoplasmic lipid droplets. The lipoblast cytoplasm may occasionally be eosinophilic and granular with variable lipid droplets in smaller cells that have sharply defined cell borders and irregular nuclei. The surrounding matrix, which is rich in sulfated glycosaminoglycans, is usually myxoid to chondroid to hyalinized, and creates distinct cell boundaries that resemble lacunar spaces around some of the lipoblasts and imparts a chondroid appearance {8214255; 10591950; 23672313}. Calcifications {8214255} and even ossification {18259746} are seen in a minority of cases.

Immunohistochemical stains for S100 reflect the full range of lipoblastic maturation within the lesion, with mature adipose tissue staining strongly; this is weak to absent in lipoblastic cells. Keratins are rarely seen {10591950; 23672313} as is EMA {8214255}, with the former probably reflecting tonofilaments that may occasionally be seen on electron microscopy {8214255; 10591950}. Detailed ultrastructural studies have shown a full spectrum of lipoblastic differentiation from primitive embryonal fat cells to mature fat {7628841; 7573689}, as well as chondroblasts and features compatible with a chondroid matrix {7628841}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows tissue fragments of variably sized lipoblasts with one to several intracytoplasmic lipid vacuoles that indent the nuclei. These are often admixed with mature fat cells and chondroid matrix that stains pinkish blue with Giemsa stains. Other tissue fragments reveal nests and strands of small to medium-sized chondroblastic-appearing cells with sharp cell borders, eosinophilic cytoplasm on H&E stains, with variable cytoplasmic vacuoles scalloping irregular, hyperchromatic nuclei that have evenly dispersed chromatin and nuclear grooves {9950168; 10524534}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The cytological and histological features of chondroid lipoma are sufficiently characteristic to make a diagnosis in most cases. However, some cases may require identification of the characteristic ZFTA::MRTFB fusion or the recently described ZFTA::NCOA1 fusion for confirmation.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a lobulated, circumscribed mass with variable admixture of mature adipocytes and lipoblasts embedded in a myxohyaline chondroid matrix.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Chondroid lipoma is cured by conservative local excision. Local recurrence is extremely rare. An unusual and fatal case of liposarcoma harbouring a ZFTA::RELA fusion with areas identical to chondroid lipoma has been reported {36201637}.
