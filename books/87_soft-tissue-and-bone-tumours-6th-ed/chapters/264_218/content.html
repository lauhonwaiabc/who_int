2.6.4 Poorly differentiated chordoma

Definition

Poorly differentiated chordoma is a high-grade malignant neoplasm that almost exclusively arises in bones of the axial skeleton, demonstrating notochordal differentiation and loss of SMARCB1 expression.

ICD-O coding

9370/3 Chordoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5Z & XH9GHO Malignant mesenchymal neoplasm of unspecified type & Chordoma NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common site is the skull base (clivus), followed by the cervical spine, less commonly the sacrococcyx, and rarely, extra-axial sites {29483606; 29623728; 27635948; 28777153; 28812319; 29119645; 25053104; 25074874; 34482218; 31243811; 39533889; 39577663}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Patients present with complaints related to mass effect at the tumour site, such as neck pain, headache, proptosis, or numbness, the latter resulting from involvement of the thoracic spine and compression fractures {29119645; 34482218; 31243811}.

Imaging

Poorly differentiated chordoma typically appears as a midline, osteolytic bone lesion with cortical destruction and soft tissue extension. On MRI, the tumour demonstrates a diffuse T2-weighted hypointense signal intensity, sometimes with some internal T2-weighted hyperintense areas of intratumoural necrosis {31243811; 34482218; 23124635}. This imaging feature distinguishes it from the typically T2-hyperintense signal intensity of conventional chordoma (due to abundant extracellular myxoid matrix) and from the biphasic appearance of dedifferentiated chordoma. Poorly differentiated chordoma also tends to exhibit less lobulated tumour margins than conventional chordoma. Marked diffuse contrast enhancement {29483606} with loss of the honeycomb pattern and low apparent diffusion coefficient values (similar to dedifferentiated chordoma) have been described {28812319; 34482218}.

Epidemiology

Poorly differentiated chordomas are very rare tumours with fewer than 100 cases reported in the literature. The tumours typically occur in children and young adults, with an average age of 9.7 years (range: 3 months to 42 years) {29483606; 31243811; 34482218; 38173946}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most poorly differentiated chordomas harbour deletions of SMARCB1 on chromosome 22q {29119645; 27218413}, which may be heterozygous or homozygous and of variable size. These deletions can be detected with DNA sequencing {25053104; 30642912}. In a minority of tumours, SMARCB1 appears intact despite the loss of SMARCB1 protein expression {21057957}, but the mechanism by which this is brought about is presently unclear {29119645}. Unsupervised clustering analysis of DNA methylation profiles distinguishes poorly differentiated chordoma from conventional chordoma and atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumour {27067307}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumour size ranges from 15 to 120 mm with an average tumour size of 43 mm {31243811; 34482218}.

Histopathology

Poorly differentiated chordoma is a cellular tumour composed of epithelioid to rhabdoid to spindle cells arranged in cohesive sheets, poorly defined nodules, fascicles, and organoid or nesting patterns. The tumour cells contain moderate to abundant eosinophilic to vacuolated cytoplasm, vesicular chromatin, and discernible nucleoli. Mitotic figures are often present, with a variable amount of geographical tumour necrosis and interspersed inflammatory cells. Myxoid stroma is rare. Physaliphorous cells are also rare, unlike conventional chordoma {29119645; 29483606; 31243811; 34482218}.

Immunohistochemistry

The tumours consistently show diffuse positivity for keratin and brachyury, variable expression of S100, and loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) {29119645; 34482218; 39577663}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes malignant rhabdoid tumour, atypical teratoid /rhabdoid tumour, rhabdoid meningioma, and epithelioid sarcoma, the latter especially in tumours occurring in spinal/paraspinal and extra-axial sites, such as knee {29483606; 34482218; 39533889; 39577663}. Other differentials include epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, myoepithelial carcinoma, and dedifferentiated chordoma {29483606; 28937370}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular testing can be used in ambiguous cases. Most tumours show homozygous or heterozygous deletion of the SMARCB1 gene, which can be detected by FISH. In some tumours, small intragenic mutations in the SMARCB1 gene may also be present {29119645; 34482218}. A subset of tumours additionally demonstrates heterozygous deletion of EWSR1, located in close proximity to SMARCB1, representing a pitfall in FISH interpretation {29119645; 31243811}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; poorly differentiated malignant neoplasm, diffuse keratin and brachyury immunostaining, along with loss of SMARCB1 (INI1) expression; predominantly involving the axial skeleton.

Staging

Staging is as per the TNM staging system for bone sarcomas.

Prognosis and prediction

Treatment primarily consists of surgery combined with radiation therapy and chemotherapy. The prognosis is worse compared with that of conventional chordoma, with reported mortality rates of 30–40% and a median survival of less than 50 months {29483606; 29119645; 24305715; 16819322; 27067307; 34482218; 31243811}.
