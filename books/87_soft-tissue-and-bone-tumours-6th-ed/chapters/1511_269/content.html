1.5.1.1 Synovial vascular anomalies

Definition

Synovial vascular anomalies (SVAs), formerly referred to as synovial haemangiomas, comprise a heterogeneous group of vascular lesions primarily involving synovium-lined surfaces, including the intra-articular region and bursal spaces.

XXX

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Synovial venous malformations, synovial arteriovenous malformations

Localization

The most common site is the knee, followed by the elbow, wrist, and ankle. Involvement of hip or other joints is exceptionally rare {24061493; 8319952}.

Clinical features

Most patients with SVAs are symptomatic and frequently present with joint swelling, effusion, persistent pain, and limitation of movement {8319952; 34950698}. Recurrent haemarthrosis has been reported {17588975; 21088622}. Delayed diagnosis may cause osteoarthritic damage. Rarely, destructive growth may be seen {20385009}, exceptionally with extra-articular extension, bone involvement, and pathological fracture of bone {21773788; 18758797}. Synovial lesions are frequently associated with adjacent cutaneous or deep soft tissue vascular anomalies. MRI is the best radiological technique for diagnosis and for determining the extent of involvement {8614857}.

Epidemiology

SVAs are extremely rare lesions, predominantly affecting children and adolescents, with no significant sex predilection {8319952; 30980138}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

It is probable that these lesions are driven by the same somatic mutations identified in their counterparts at other anatomical sites.

Macroscopic appearance

SVAs may be either well circumscribed or diffuse. Dilated vascular structures, sometimes containing thrombi and phleboliths, are commonly observed. Some lesions may display a more solid and even nodular architecture. The overlying villous synovium often shows a characteristic mahogany-brown discolouration {8319952}.

Histopathology

The lesions display variable histological features depending on the subtype. Venous malformations are composed of spongiform, variably sized, dysmorphic venous channels, often associated with thrombosis and phleboliths {12447041}.

Arteriovenous malformations are infiltrative lesions comprising small, medium, and large arteries with loss of the internal elastic lamina, and veins of similar calibre exhibiting thickened walls and intimal hyperplasia. A diffuse or lobular proliferation of small vessels with plump endothelial cells and pericytes may also be observed {21095470}.

Villous hyperplasia of the synovium is present in some cases, particularly in chronic lesions with recurrent haemarthrosis, and may show prominent iron deposition {8319952}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: benign vascular anomalies arising in synovium-lined spaces morphologically within the spectrum of venous or arteriovenous malformation.

Desirable: compatible imaging.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

SVAs are benign lesions that can be managed surgically. Small, well-circumscribed lesions are usually amenable to complete excision via synovectomy, with minimal risk of local recurrence {30980138}. In contrast, when the lesion shows more diffuse intra-articular involvement, complete resection may be challenging. There are no published studies addressing recurrence risk or prognosis stratified by specific histological subtypes.
