1.12.4 Deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma

Definition

Deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma is a paucicellular myxoid spindle cell neoplasm of the pelviperineal region, with potential for local recurrence due to its infiltrative growth.

ICD-O coding

8841/0 Aggressive angiomyxoma

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH9HK9 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Angiomyxoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: deep angiomyxoma, aggressive angiomyxoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Deep angiomyxoma occurs in the deep soft tissue of the pelvis and perineum, especially the vulvovaginal, inguinoscrotal, or perianal regions {6684403; 3997139; 8646730; 9023551; 1471726; 7572787}.

Clinical features

This tumour presents as a deep, slowly growing mass that is typically painful, although patients may experience symptoms and discomfort related to compression of adjacent or involved anatomy. Not uncommonly, it may be mistaken for a Bartholin gland cyst or lipoma. If imaging is performed, a characteristic swirl sign on MRI is seen, which may reflect the vascular pattern in the tumour {24848813; 24852676; 39631162}.

Epidemiology

Deep angiomyxoma is a rare tumour much more common in women than men based on cases reported in the literature. Although these tumours may occur over a wide age range, the peak of occurrence is reported during the fourth to fifth decades of life {16844560; 37046258; 32430760}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic aberrations involving chromosome region 12q13-q15 are typical for aggressive angiomyxoma {18295664}. Correspondingly, rearrangements of the HMGA2 (12q14.3) locus may be identified by FISH, with associated HMGA2 transcriptional upregulation and aberrant expression confirmed by RT-PCR and immunostaining in some studies {9466569; 11550285; 16568309; 17654722}. HMGA2 is a non-histone DNA architectural factor important in transcriptional regulation that is not typically expressed in non-neoplastic adult tissues. Aberrant expression of HMGA2 protein is not specific to deep angiomyxoma and is also seen in many other mesenchymal tumours, such as lipoma, pulmonary chondroid hamartoma, and uterine leiomyoma, with similar chromosomal aberrations {11550285}. The molecular mechanism for aberrant expression appears multifactorial and tumour dependent; in some instances, deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma is due to fusion transcript production, whereas in others the gene is intact but shows deregulated expression via alterations affecting the 5′ regulatory region or the untranslated 3′ region. It is not clear how HMGA2 expression leads to tumour development, but presumably altered transcription of target genes is a potential mechanism.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours vary in size, but most are > 100 mm {38762952; 35310439; 40413777}. The border with surrounding non-neoplastic tissue is poorly demarcated. The cut surface is usually soft and gelatinous, although occasionally it may be firm; tumours may have varied coloration ranging from clear to grey or tan/pink. Small foci of haemorrhage may be noted, but no necrosis should be present.

Histopathology

Deep angiomyxoma is characterized by uniform hypocellularity, copious myxoid or fibromyxoid matrix, and medium-sized to large intratumoural vasculature uniformly present throughout the tumour. Collagen fibrils and smooth muscle (myoid) bundles may be seen in close proximity to the vessels. The tumour cells are spindled or stellate, with delicate cytoplasmic processes. Less frequently encountered histopathological features include focal hypercellularity, collagenous stroma, and neurofibroma-like morphology {34441295}. Deep angiomyxoma has a highly infiltrative growth pattern and commonly extensively involves surgical resection margins. The poorly defined border and imperceptible blending of the bland spindle cells with adjacent non-neoplastic stroma may result in challenges in margin assessment.

By immunohistochemistry, deep angiomyxomas are usually positive for desmin, typically with diffuse expression. CD34 may be positive in a minority. SMA positivity is generally limited to the myoid bundles {9023551; 15735978}. Both ER and PR are typically positive in tumours occurring in female individuals {21804399; 38762952} and may also be positive in male individuals, more typically PR {16844560}. HMGA2 is a sensitive but not entirely specific marker for deep angiomyxoma {20551826}. Positivity for CDK4 but not MDM2 has been described {15735978}; of note, CDK4 is in the same region as HMGA2 on 12q.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a poorly demarcated, highly infiltrative spindle cell lesion with uniform hypocellularity; abundant myxoid matrix; medium to thick-walled vessels evenly distributed throughout the tumour.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence may occur, although this happens much less commonly than originally thought, which is reflected by the terminology change from “aggressive” to “deep” angiomyxoma, with the latter designation chosen to reflect lesional depth. Recurrence rates vary, ranging from 9% to 50% {15735978; 30518617; 27571990; 35310439}; this wide range may reflect uncertainty regarding margin status, because the border of the tumour can be difficult to delineate. More than one recurrence is very uncommon. HMGA2 staining may be useful in determining margin status. Recurrent tumours that express hormone receptors have been successfully treated with surgery and adjuvant GnRH analogues {24402356; 30567120; 30518617; 37139188; 33912645}.
