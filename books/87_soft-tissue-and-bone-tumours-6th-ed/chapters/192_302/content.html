1.9.2 SDH-deficient gastrointestinal stromal tumour

Definition

Succinate dehydrogenase (SDH)-deficient gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) is a mesenchymal neoplasm that shows differentiation towards the interstitial cells of Cajal and is driven by dysfunction of the mitochondrial complex 2 (known as SDH deficiency).

Related terminology

Not recommended: paediatric wildtype GIST; type 2 GIST.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Almost all well-characterized SDH-deficient GISTs reported to date have arisen in the stomach {20305538; 21997692; 28324028}.

Clinical features

Multifocality, young age of onset, and lymph node metastases are clues to the diagnosis of SDH-deficient GIST, although some cases may be clinically indistinguishable from other GISTs {20305538}.

Epidemiology

SDH-deficient GISTs comprise 3% of all GISTs (5–7.5% of gastric GISTs) in unselected adult populations {20305538; 21997692; 22804613; 27011036}. They account for the overwhelming majority of GISTs diagnosed in children and the majority of GISTs diagnosed in young adulthood with a median age of 22 years and a female predominance (M:F ratio: 1:3) {20305538; 21997692; 22804613; 27011036; 21173220}.

Etiology

About 30% of SDH-deficient GISTs are associated with germline pathogenic variants in the SDHA gene, and 20% with germline pathogenic variants in SDHB, SDHC, or SDHD {23060355; 22955521; 23282968; 29239034}. The remainder, approximately 50%, are associated with postzygotic systemic hypermethylation of the SDHC promoter locus (i.e. SDHC epimutation); the molecular signature of Carney Triad – the syndromic but non-hereditary association of SDH-deficient GIST; SDH-deficient paraganglioma; and pulmonary chondroma {25540324; 24859990; 29239034; 35285003}.

Pathogenesis

The SDH complex, also known as the mitochondrial complex 2, is a key mitochondrial enzyme that links the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain {22544211; 29239034}. It comprises four protein subunits – SDHC and SDHD (the anchoring components) and SDHA and SDHB (the catalytic components). Additionally, SDHAF2 is required for flavination of the SDHA protein. If any component of the SDH complex is lost or dysfunctional, the entire SDH complex becomes unstable, and the SDHB subunit is released into the cytoplasm where it rapidly degrades, accounting for the loss of expression of SDHB protein {19576851; 22544211; 22835832; 29239034}. Dysfunction of this key enzyme causes accumulation of succinate, which acts as an oncometabolite behaving as a competitor of 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases involved in a broad spectrum of tumorigenic pathways that include hypoxic regulation and epigenetic reprogramming including MGMT promoter hypermethylation {15987702; 23550148; 31666694; 32234487; 30616628; 33116851; 36198483}.

SDH-deficient GISTs demonstrate a distinctly different gene expression profile from that of other GISTs {18483389; 33806389} and lack somatic KIT or PDGFRA mutations {20305538; 21997692}. Syndromic SDH-deficient GISTs are associated with germline SDHA mutations (30%); SDHB, SDHC, and SDHD mutations (20%); or SDHC epimutation (50%). Patients with SDH-deficient GISTs should be considered for genetic counselling/testing for SDH-deficient tumour syndromes that are characterized by SDH-deficient GIST, paraganglioma/phaeochromocytoma, SDH-deficient renal carcinoma, and pulmonary chondroma.

Macroscopic appearance

SDH-deficient GISTs present as relatively circumscribed but unencapsulated tumours centred in the muscularis propria and pushing into the gastric mucosal or serosal surface. Unlike other GISTs, SDH-deficient GISTs are commonly multifocal, demonstrate a multilobulated/plexiform growth pattern, and show frequent lymph node metastases {20305538; 21753704; 21997692; 22804613}.

Histopathology

There are several morphological, clinical, and molecular clues to the separation of SDH-deficient GISTs from other GISTs. Most cases show gastric location, multifocality, a plexiform or multinodular growth pattern, a rich capillary network, epithelioid predominance (sometimes with a nested architecture), and frequent lymph node metastases {20305538; 21753704; 29239034}.

Loss of expression of SDHB (termed SDH deficiency) occurs in all SDH-deficient GISTs regardless of which SDH subunit is inactivated and is required for diagnosis. In addition to loss of expression of SDHB, SDH-deficient GISTs associated with SDHA variants also show loss of expression of SDHA by immunohistochemistry, whereas tumours associated with SDHB, SDHC, or SDHD variants, and SDHC epimutation, demonstrate preserved expression of SDHA {23060355; 22955521; 23282968}.

SDHB and SDHA immunohistochemistry may be difficult to interpret, particularly in small biopsies. Normal positive staining is strong, cytoplasmic, and distinctly granular (mitochondrial) and should always be identified in non-neoplastic cells, which act as internal positive controls before a tumour is considered SDH deficient/negative. In some SDH-deficient tumours, interpretation may be particularly challenging because a nonspecific diffuse cytoplasmic blush may be retained. In this setting, the weak and cytoplasmically diffuse, rather than granular, expression in the neoplastic cells that contrasts with the internal positive controls is a useful clue to the diagnosis of true SDH deficiency {19576851; 29239034}.

Although SDH-deficient GISTs are not associated with KIT or PDGFRA mutations, they always show diffuse strong immunohistochemical expression of both KIT and DOG1 {20305538; 21753704; 29239034}. This can be useful in the differential diagnosis with PDGFRA-mutant GISTs, which may resemble SDH-deficient GISTs because of their frequent gastric location and epithelioid cell predominance but commonly show only weak/focal, or absent, KIT expression.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show features similar to those of GIST, although most cases show epithelioid morphology and occur in the stomach and the diagnosis can be suggested. Immunohistochemistry is required for the diagnosis, and care is warranted when interpreting SDHB immunohistochemistry on small biopsies and cell block preparations {21350101; 22351482}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

SDH-associated GIST always lacks KIT and PDGFRA mutation. SDH immunohistochemistry is equally informative as SDH molecular testing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid morphology with loss of expression of SDHB and expression of KIT and/or DOG1 by immunohistochemistry.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Unlike other GISTs, the prognosis of SDH-deficient GISTs cannot be predicted by size and mitotic rate {20305538; 22804613; 27340750; 29239034; 21997692}. That is, small, mitotically inactive tumours frequently metastasize, and lymph node spread (rare in other GISTs) is common. When metastases do occur, they may be indolent. Despite a metastatic rate of as many as 50% at 2 years and 74% at 5 years {37477937}, disease-specific survival rates of > 80% at 10 and 15 years have been reported {37477937}.

SDH-deficient GISTs do not respond to imatinib but may show some response to next-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as regorafenib {36816783; 37559050}. The presence of MGMT promoter hypermethylation has been used to justify the use of alkylating agents such as temozolomide, with promising early results {30616628; 33116851; 36198483; 35116662; 36151992; 34426440}.

The accumulation of additional molecular abnormalities, such as KRAS and TP53 mutations or chromosome 1q amplification, has been associated with worse outcomes {37477937; 27011036; 29967109}. Focal loss of KIT and DOG1 expression by immunohistochemistry has been reported very rarely and always in association with morphologically dedifferentiated aggressive tumours {29967109; 31072206; 37781202}. It is probable that reports of poor-prognosis SDH-deficient gastric sarcomatoid malignancies lacking KIT and DOG1 expression represent de-differentiated SDH-deficient GISTs {32501622}.

In view of the strongly syndromic nature of SDH-deficient neoplasia and its tendency to be multifocal and metachronous, surveillance and lifelong follow-up are indicated for recurrence, metachronous disease, and other SDH-deficient neoplasms, even in individuals in whom no germline sequence variant has been identified {29239034; 34021277}.
