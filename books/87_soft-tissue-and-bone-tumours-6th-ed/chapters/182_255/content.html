1.8.2 Inflammatory rhabdomyoblastic tumour

Definition

Inflammatory rhabdomyoblastic tumour (IRMT) is a neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential, characterized by skeletal muscle differentiation, massive lymphohistiocytic infiltrate, and near-haploidization.

ICD-O coding

xxx/1: Inflammatory rhabdomyoblastic tumour

xxx/3: Inflammatory rhabdomyoblastic tumour with malignant progression

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Not recommended: histiocyte-rich rhabdomyoblastic tumour; low-grade inflammatory myogenic tumour; inflammatory leiomyosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The tumours are located in the deep soft tissues of the extremities, trunk, neck, or retroperitoneum {33318583; 30287926; 32078043; 37871654}. The iliopsoas region is particularly favoured. Rare examples presenting as pharyngeal submucosal polyps have been reported {37871654}. The preoperative history is often long before clinical attention {33318583}.

Clinical features

The tumour typically presents as a slowly growing vaguely lobulated mass. CT may demonstrate calcifications. MRI reveals intramuscular masses with variable T1 signal and heterogeneous high T2 signal intensity, accompanied by postcontrast enhancement {36966552; 32514584; 9713368}.

Epidemiology

IRMT is very rare. The tumour occurs in young to middle-aged adults, with a median age of approximately 40–50 years, and shows a significant male predilection {30287926; 33318583; 37871654}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. The tumours uncommonly present in patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 {33318583; 37871654; 33762681}.

Pathogenesis

Most tumours harbour a distinctive near-haploid karyotype with retained biparental disomy of chromosomes 5, 20, and 22, and (less often) 18 and 21 {33318583; 37871654; 9713368; 33762681; 28884746; 23019412}. Subsequent whole-genome doubling may occur {33318583; 33762681; 28884746}. Additional retained heterozygosity in chromosomes 7 or 12 is uncommon {33318583; 37871654; 33762681}. This characteristic copy-number profile is undetected in a small subset of cases, some of which may be attributed to low tumour purity {33318583; 37871654; 33762681}. NF1 pathogenic variants are present in a fraction of cases {30287926; 32078043; 37871654; 28884746}. TP53 mutation is also recurrent {37871654; 35655404}, sometimes with a low variant allele frequency probably representing secondary acquisition. Gene expression profiling reveals the upregulation of skeletal muscle-related genes (e.g. MYOD1, MYOG, PAX7, MYF5, MYF6) rather than those involved in smooth muscle differentiation {28884746}. Malignant progression to rhabdomyosarcoma is characterized by additional copy-number gains or losses, such as frequent CDKN2A/CDKN2B deletions {37871654; 36966552; 35655404}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour shows a well-circumscribed, vaguely lobulated, tan or yellow mass, often surrounded by a fibrous pseudocapsule. Calcification or cystic changes may be present.

Histopathology

The tumour is typically well-circumscribed, often surrounded by a fibrous pseudocapsule. Small foci of invasion into adjacent tissues may be seen. Typically, there is massive lymphohistiocytic infiltration that often vastly outnumbers and may even obscure underlying myogenic tumour cells. The neoplastic cells are spindled, epithelioid, or angulated in shape, arranged singly, in small groups, or in short fascicles {33318583; 30287926}. Intersecting cellular fascicles, storiform growth, or rhabdoid cells may be present uncommonly {32078043; 37871654}. They have deeply eosinophilic, glassy cytoplasm. Differentiated rhabdomyoblasts with cross-striations are not seen. The tumour cells show variable nuclear atypia and pleomorphism, ranging from bizarre, multilobated, and hyperchromatic nuclei to relatively uniform ones. Mitotic activity is low (often 0–3 mitoses/12 mm2), disproportionate to the degree of nuclear atypia {30287926; 37871654}. Tumour coagulative necrosis is absent. The inflammatory component primarily consists of numerous histiocytes that are epithelioid or spindled, with foamy or non-lipidized cytoplasm. Touton-type giant cells may be present. Histiocytes can be mitotically active or necrotic {37871654}. Lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils, and rare neutrophils are admixed. Stromal calcifications, haemosiderin deposition, cystic changes, cholesterol deposition, peripheral lymphoid aggregates, and thick-walled, hyalinized vessels are common.

The tumour cells exhibit skeletal muscle differentiation at the immunohistochemical level. Desmin and PAX7 are most often expressed. MYOD1 is positive in > 80%, and myogenin is less sensitive {33318583; 32078043; 37871654}, with expression often limited to < 10% of tumour cells. SMA may be expressed {32078043}; however, h-caldesmon is negative or only rarely expressed in a small number of cells when using the specific h-CD clone {33318583; 37871654; 36966552; 35655404}. Abundant histiocytic infiltration is highlighted with antibodies to CD163 and CD68.

Malignant progression is characterized by a high N:C ratio, frequent mitotic figures including atypical forms, coagulative tumour necrosis, and depletion of lymphohistiocytic background {37871654; 36966552; 35655404}, while skeletal muscle differentiation is maintained. A coexisting precursor conventional IRMT component is often recognizable, at least focally, but the histological transition can be gradual {36966552}. The minimal criteria for malignant progression have not been established; however, hypercellular sheets of small uniform epithelioid cells may indicate progression {36966552}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows high cellularity with foamy histiocytes mixed with epithelioid, plasmacytoid, and occasional spindle cells with eccentric cytoplasm and atypical nuclei, present in dyscohesive tissue fragments and as single cells {36519516}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular assays are usually not required for diagnosis. In challenging cases, demonstration of characteristic near-haploidization or widespread loss of heterozygosity supports the diagnosis. MYOD1 p.(Leu122Arg) mutations and MDM2 amplifications are absent {32078043; 37871654}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: spindled or epithelioid cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm, variable degrees of nuclear atypia, and low mitotic activity; massive intratumoural lymphohistiocytic infiltration; immunohistochemical demonstration of skeletal muscle markers (desmin, myogenin, MYOD1, PAX7). High mitotic activity and coagulative necrosis indicate malignant progression.

Desirable: near-haploidization or widespread loss of heterozygosity (in selected cases).

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems can be applied for cases with malignant progression.

Prognosis and prediction

Although reported cases with adequate clinical follow-up are limited, conventional IRMT has behaved indolently after excision, with only a small risk of locoregional spread. Malignant progression portends aggressive behaviour, with metastatic disease (most commonly to the lungs) and/or death in the majority of reported cases {37871654; 36966552; 35655404}. TP53 mutation does not appear to correlate with malignant progression or prognosis {37871654}.
