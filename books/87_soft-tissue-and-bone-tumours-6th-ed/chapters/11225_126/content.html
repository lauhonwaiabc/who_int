1.12.25 Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

Definition

Desmoplastic small round cell tumour (DSRCT) is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm composed of nests of small round cells associated with stromal desmoplasia, polyphenotypic differentiation with desmin and keratin coexpression, and EWSR1::WT1 gene fusion.

ICD-O coding

8806/3 Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH5SN6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

Related terminology

Acceptable: intra-abdominal desmoplastic round cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The vast majority of patients develop tumours in the abdominal cavity, frequently in the retroperitoneum, pelvis, omentum, and mesentery, and often with multiple serosal implants. Primary extra-abdominal sites are rare and include thoracic cavity, limbs, head and neck, paratesticular region, kidney, uterus, bone, and brain {17414105; 9042290; 36172631; 33803887; 35626339; 34657504; 30522375}.

Clinical features

Clinical symptoms are usually related to the primary site of presentation, such as abdominal distention, pain, palpable mass, ascites, and organ obstruction.

Epidemiology

DSRCT is a rare tumour described in case reports to primarily affect children and young adults, and usually presents with widespread abdominal/peritoneal involvement. A striking male predominance exists among reported cases, with peak in occurrence in the third decade of life, although this tumour type has been reported in cases over a wide age range (first to fifth decades of life) {1709557; 41040185}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

DSRCT is characterized by a recurrent chromosomal translocation t(11;22)(p13;q12) {1314522; 7687454; 8374894}, resulting in the fusion of the EWSR1 gene on 22q12.2 and the Wilms tumour gene, WT1, on 11p13 {8187063; 7495283; 7862627}. The most common chimeric transcript is composed of an in-frame fusion of the first 7 exons of EWSR1, encoding the potential transcription-modulating domain, and exons 8–10 of WT1, encoding the last 3 zinc fingers of the DNA-binding domain. Rare variants including additional EWSR1 exons can also occur. Studies of the EWSR1::WT1 aberrant transcription factor have revealed deregulation of several target genes {15826834}, activation of neural gene expression and partial neural differentiation via ASCL1 {24934812}, and R-loop–dependent replication stress {39412947}. Aside from this translocation, DRSCTs are genomically quiescent with infrequent reported mutations involving TERT, ARID1A, HRAS, TP53, CDKN2A, and FGFR4, and some cases with loss of heterozygosity in 6q,16q, 11p, and 11q and gains in 1q and 5p {33928700; 33753552}.

The serosal lining of body cavities, the most common site of DSRCT, has high transient fetal expression of the WT1 gene. WT1 is expressed in tissues derived from the intermediate mesoderm, primarily those undergoing transition from mesenchyme to epithelium {1722569; 8758208}. This pattern recapitulates the epithelial differentiation noted in DSRCT.

Macroscopic appearance

The typical gross appearance consists of multiple tumour nodules studding the peritoneal surface. Often there is a dominant tumour mass accompanied by smaller satellite nodules. The cut surface is firm and greyish-white, with foci of haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

DSRCT is characterized by sharply outlined, variably sized nests and trabeculae of small round cells, embedded in a prominent desmoplastic stroma {33803887}. Less commonly, the neoplastic cells may be epithelioid, ovoid, or slightly spindled; in some instances, when the tumour trabeculae become confluent, the desmoplasia is less prominent. Central necrosis is common and cystic degeneration can also be seen. Some tumours focally exhibit tubular structures or rosettes. The tumour cells are typically uniform, with small hyperchromatic nuclei, scant cytoplasm, and indistinct cytoplasmic borders. In a subset of cases, tumour cells can show cytoplasmic eosinophilic rhabdoid inclusions. Some tumours have larger cells with greater pleomorphism. The chromatin is typically dispersed, with inconspicuous nucleoli. Mitoses are frequent and individual cell necrosis is common. The desmoplastic stroma is composed of fibroblasts or myofibroblasts embedded in a collagenous matrix {9738572}. Stromal vascularity can be prominent including complex capillary tufts to larger vessels with eccentric thickened walls. Rarely, tumours can present with multiple pseudocystic or cystic areas lined with Müllerian-like ciliated epithelium {39781660}.

Immunohistochemically, DSRCT shows a distinctive polyphenotypic profile, with reactivity for keratins, EMA, and desmin, though the extent of expression can vary {12640103}. A distinctive dot-like cytoplasmic localization is often seen with desmin and occasionally with other intermediate filaments. Myogenin and MYOD1 are consistently negative. Nuclear expression of WT1 (using antibodies to the C-terminus but not the N-terminus) is usually seen {10843285; 10989634; 35488420}. Some may express neuroendocrine markers, though not INSM1 {34698236; 32203089}.

Cytology

Cytological specimens show high cellularity with loosely cohesive tissue fragments and single round cells with a high N:C ratio, scant to moderate cytoplasm with or without vacuolization, variable nuclear moulding, granular chromatin, and variable nuclear membranes, including indented, kidney shapes. Pseudorosettes can be present {11836699}. The tissue fragments round up in spheres in fluids {37289439}. Crushed nuclei, apoptosis, and purple-stained stroma can be appreciated on smears {24639098}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of the EWSR1::WT1 gene fusion or EWSR1 rearrangement is useful for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nests of predominantly small round cells in variable desmoplastic stroma; immunopositivity for keratins and desmin.

Desirable: demonstration of EWSR1 gene rearrangement or EWSR1::WT1 fusion in selected cases.

Staging

This tumour can be staged using the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging for soft tissue sarcomas in unusual histologies and sites.

Prognosis and prediction

The overall survival remains poor, despite multimodality therapy {22162435; 15868593; 29871905}. The 5-year overall survival rate is poor, about 10–23% at most. Those with no liver or extra-abdominal disease may have a better prognosis {39992542; 37243410; 36951537; 34310472}.
