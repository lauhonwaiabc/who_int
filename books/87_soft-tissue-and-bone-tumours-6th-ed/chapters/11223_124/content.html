1.12.23 Clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue

Definition

Clear cell sarcoma (CCS) of soft tissue is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm that typically involves deep soft tissue of the distal extremities, displays a distinctive nested growth pattern and melanocytic differentiation, and harbours EWSR1::ATF1 or EWSR1::CREB1 fusion.

ICD-O coding

9044/3 Clear cell sarcoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH77N6 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Clear cell sarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant melanoma of soft parts.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

CCS occurs most commonly at deep-seated sites in the extremity, with almost 50% of cases arising in the distal lower extremity, ankle, or foot, where they can be associated with tendons or aponeuroses {6614306; 10491522; 6616410; 1463095}. CCS has also been reported in the head/neck, trunk, long bones, and viscera, including the lung and gastrointestinal tract {15810965; 18408594; 6614306; 1463095; 27112339; 34759454; 36158008; 36730915}. Lesions arising in the skin (cutaneous CCS) and oral mucosa are recognized {20087159; 37879148}. Malignant gastrointestinal neuroectodermal tumour (formerly clear cell sarcoma–like tumour of the gastrointestinal tract) represents an entity distinct from CCS and is discussed in the Digestive system tumours volume of this series.

Clinical features

Almost all patients present with a palpable mass of months to years duration. Pain and tenderness are reported in approximately one third to one half of cases {6614306; 10491522; 6616410; 1463095}. They are frequently misinterpreted as benign tumours, such as ganglion cysts {39535601}.

Epidemiology

CCS mainly affects young adults, with a peak incidence in the third and fourth decades of life {24560887; 10491522; 15098780}. There is a slight female predominance {24560887; 15098780; 1463095}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The genetic hallmark of CCS is the reciprocal translocation t(12;22)(q13;q12), present in 70–90% of cases, which fuses EWSR1 with ATF1 {2009504; 1373311; 1486556; 1486557; 9824209; 11207050; 11992546; 9875133; 22406360}. Exon 8 of EWSR1 most frequently fuses with exon 4 of ATF1, and this has been designated as the type 1 fusion {8401579; 11826187; 19561568}. In the resultant EWSR1::ATF1 chimeric fusion protein, the N-terminus of EWSR1 is linked to the bZIP domain of ATF1. Consequently, ATF1, a transcription factor normally regulated by cAMP, becomes a cAMP-independent transcriptional activator and constitutively activates the promoter for MITF, a target of the MSH pathway {16766266}. This activation is responsible for melanocytic differentiation and growth of CCS {16766266; 7753552; 8552387}. PRMT5 has been found to enhance EWSR1::ATF1-mediated gene transcription to sustain CCS cell proliferation {36041632}. EWSR1::ATF1 is shown to be sufficient for murine sarcomagenesis without additional protein-coding variants, except a modest facilitation of Mitf {34156976}. ATF1, CREB1, and CREM proteins are functionally similar and belong to the CREB family of transcription factors. Thus, a small subset of cases harbour a variant translocation, t(2;22)(q34;q12), resulting in an EWSR1::CREB1 fusion {18300804; 19561568}. Rare cases may show EWSR1::CREM fusions {30219084; 39420989; 36640048}. CCS has a distinct methylation profile from other tumours harbouring EWSR1::CREB fusions {35347249}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most CCSs measure 20–50 mm, but tumours > 150 mm have been reported {6614306; 1463095}. Gross examination reveals a circumscribed mass with a lobulated tan to greyish-white appearance, sometimes with a coarse or gritty texture {14332545}. A subset of CCSs may show melanin pigmentation, necrosis, or cystic change.

Histopathology

On low-power examination, CCS exhibits a characteristic nested or fascicular architecture with epithelioid to plump spindled cells partitioned by thin fibrous septa. Tumours typically show an infiltrative growth through dense fibrous tissue. Despite its name, CCS typically has tumour cells with palely eosinophilic to amphophilic cytoplasm and vesicular nuclei with macronucleoli, with only rare examples showing true cytoplasmic clearing. Substantial nuclear pleomorphism is generally absent, although scattered wreath-like multinucleated giant cells are relatively common. Melanin pigment is present in more than half of the cases, but it is difficult to appreciate on H&E staining. Rare cases may show junctional tumour nests at the dermoepidermal interface {23980901; 28256744}. The mitotic count of CCS is frequently low (< 3 mitoses/mm2, equating to < 5 mitoses/10 HPF of 0.17 mm2), and necrosis is identified in roughly one third of cases {6616410; 1463095; 14332545}. CCS consistently express S100, SOX10, melan-A, HMB45, and MITF {18300804; 11211309; 22693489}. CCS lacks diffuse PRAME staining {37477762; 32681554; 36200756}.

Cytology

Aspirates show features similar to those of melanoma, often with high cellularity, showing small discohesive tissue fragments and mainly dispersed cells, which are monotonous, round to polygonal cells with well-defined borders, often abundant, pale granular cytoplasm that is often eccentric, round to sometimes more pleomorphic and occasionally bizarre nuclei with a variable number of nuclear pseudoinclusions, vesicular chromatin, and prominent nucleoli {8143535; 16007650; 32356379}. Melanin may be present in melanoma cells and macrophages.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of EWSR1 gene rearrangement or EWSR1::ATF1 or EWSR1::CREB1 gene fusion may be helpful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: characteristic nested or fascicular architecture; plump spindle or ovoid cells with palely eosinophilic cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli; expression of melanocytic markers (S100, SOX10, melan-A, HMB45).

Desirable: demonstration of EWSR1::ATF1 or EWSR1::CREB1 fusion.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

CCS is an aggressive malignancy with recurrence rates approaching 40% and pulmonary or lymph node metastasis in at least 30–50% of patients {6616410; 10491522; 1463095; 18300804; 29961184; 22693489}. Metastasis often occurs more than a decade after initial diagnosis; however, the presence of initial metastasis is seen in 20% of the patients and is associated with dismal survival. Survival rates at 5, 10, and 20 years are approximately 60%, 35%, and 10%, respectively {22693489; 18300804; 1463095; 29961184; 35466567}. Male sex, tumour size > 50 mm, necrosis, incomplete surgical resection, and regional lymph node involvement are unfavourable prognostic factors {1463095; 10491522; 22693489; 6616410; 2295060; 35466567; 39535601}. Lymph node sampling is recommended {39535601}. Fusion variant and transcript type have not been found to have prognostic significance {16878328}. Deletion of the 9p21.3 region, where the CDKN2A gene is located, TERT promoter mutation, and CDKN2A loss-of-function mutation (present in 40–50% of CCS) have been associated with poor survival {34923763; 35347249}.
