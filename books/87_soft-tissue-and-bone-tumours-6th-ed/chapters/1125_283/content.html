1.12.5 Acral fibrochondromyxoid tumour

Definition

Acral fibrochondromyxoid tumour (AFCMT) is a benign mesenchymal neoplasm of uncertain differentiation arising in acral sites and composed of bland spindle or round cells arranged in a chondromyxoid stroma lobulated by fibrovascular septa.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

AFCMT occurs exclusively on acral sites and is about twice as common on the hands (particularly the fingers) as on the feet {32047233; 39181449}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Tumours present as slow-growing small soft tissue–based lesions, while rare cases have been more superficially located and involve the dermis {32047233; 39181449; 36936733; 32844242}.

Imaging findings

Plain radiographs show extrinsic cortical scalloping or mild saucerization of the underlying bone. On CT studies, AFCMT presents as a soft tissue or periosteal lesion with possible erosion of the underlying bone. MRI shows low to intermediate signal on T1-weighted images, marked hyperintense signal on T2-weighted images, and heterogeneous enhancement after intravenous contrast medium injection {32844242; 39181449}.

Epidemiology

AFCMT has been described to occur in middle-aged adults, a recent case series reporting a median age of 44 years and a slight female predominance {39181449}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

THBS1::ADGRF5 fusions were identified in 27 of 29 cases {32047233; 39181449}. THBS1 is the 5′ partner and encodes a member of the non-collagenous matrix glycoproteins of the extracellular matrix. The 3′ partner is ADGRF5, which encodes an orphan transmembrane G protein-coupled receptor. At the transcriptional level, the tumours show major upregulation of the ADGRF5 transcript, not confirmed at the protein level.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours present as well-circumscribed tan nodules.

Histopathology

AFCMT is a multinodular tumour delineated by fibrous bands with numerous small vessels. Tumour cells are spindle or round, embedded in an abundant myxoid, chondroid, or fibrous stroma. They form poorly defined groups or small clusters or rows, sometimes embedded in lacunar spaces, hinting at a cartilaginous differentiation. The neoplastic cells have scant eosinophilic cytoplasm; small round, ovoid, or reniform nuclei with occasionally longitudinal grooves reminiscent of chondroblasts; and inconspicuous nucleoli. Usually, there is no pleomorphism or mitotic activity. Cystic-like changes, foci of calcifications, or ischaemic necrosis can rarely be present. By immunohistochemistry, CD34 and ERG were positive in 94% and 97% of cases, respectively.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

THBS1::ADGRF5 fusion or rearrangement of THBS1 supports the diagnosis but is generally not required in a morphological typical case.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multinodular tumour with fibrous, myxoid, or chondroid stroma, fibrovascular septa, spindle or round bland cells; acral locations.

Desirable: CD34 and ERG expression; THBS1::ADGRF5 fusion in select cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

These tumours behave indolently, with some risk of local recurrence (~18%) if incompletely excised. No reports of malignant transformation or distant metastasis exist to date {32047233; 39181449}.
