1.3.31 Superficial CD34-positive pleomorphic fibroblastic tumour

Definition

Superficial CD34-positive pleomorphic fibroblastic tumour is an intermediate, rarely metastasizing neoplasm of the skin and subcutis, characterized by spindled cells with abundant, granular to glassy eosinophilic cytoplasm, marked nuclear pleomorphism, a low mitotic count, and diffuse CD34 expression.

ICD-O coding

8810/1 Superficial CD34-positive pleomorphic fibroblastic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site

Related terminology

Acceptable: superficial CD34-positive fibroblastic tumour.

Not recommended: PRDM10-rearranged soft tissue tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

This tumour most frequently occurs in the lower extremities, especially thigh, followed by arm, buttock, shoulder, and (rarely) vulva {23887307; 26946226; 27636918; 30004071}.

Clinical features

This tumour typically presents as a slow-growing, painless mass of superficial soft tissues. A long prebiopsy duration (> 5 years) is often noted {23887307}.

Epidemiology

Approximately 50–70 cases have been reported in the English literature. Most have occurred in middle-aged adults (median age: 35–42 years; range: 14–85 years), with a slight male predominance {23887307; 27636918; 28937375; 35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36812381}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Rearrangements of the PRDM10 gene have been found in between 37% and 77% of tested cases, most often as MED12::PRDM10 or CITED2::PRDM10 fusions, and less commonly as PRDM10::RAB30 or PRDM10::ARHGAP32 fusions {35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36812381}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are circumscribed, firm, yellow to tan soft tissue masses with a variably gelatinous appearance {23887307; 27636918; 28937375; 35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36812381}. Tumour size varies from 15 to 100 mm but is usually < 50 mm {23887307; 27636918; 28937375}.

Histopathology

The lesions grow in a relatively circumscribed, but at least partially infiltrative, fashion and are composed of highly cellular fascicles and sheets of spindled to epithelioid cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, often having a granular or glassy appearance. Lipidized tumour cells are commonly present. The neoplastic cells show moderate to marked nuclear pleomorphism, often with bizarre, hyperchromatic nuclei containing prominent nucleoli and intranuclear cytoplasmic pseudoinclusions. Despite these alarming nuclear features, mitotic activity is very low, and necrosis is rarely seen {23887307; 27636918; 28937375; 35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36812381}. A mixed inflammatory cell infiltrate is often present. There is morphological overlap with tumours reported as PRDM10-rearranged soft tissue tumours {30570551; 25516889}. By immunohistochemistry, these tumours invariably express CD34 and are positive for WT1 protein in a nuclear pattern in 75% of cases {23887307; 27636918; 28937375; 35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36812381}. Granular membranous expression of CADM3 has been reported in nearly 100% of tested cases {35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36344703}. A variable degree of immunoreactivity for keratins is seen in close to 70% of cases (most often with the AE1/AE3 clones); less often, aberrant desmin immunoreactivity is present {23887307; 27636918; 28937375; 35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36812381}.

Cytology

There are limited reports on cytopathology, which show cellular smears composed of large, pleomorphic cells with granular to glassy cytoplasm {27432164}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: superficial location; spindle cells with granular to glassy eosinophilic cytoplasm; marked nuclear pleomorphism but a low mitotic count; diffuse CD34 expression.

Desirable: nuclear WT1 expression and/or membranous CADM3 expression, often in combination with aberrant expression of keratins and less often desmin.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis for patients with this tumour is excellent, with only two reported cases with lymph node metastases, no distant metastases, a small number of non-aggressive local recurrences, and no deaths from disease. All patients with this disease have been reported to be alive and disease-free at the time of last follow-up {23887307; 27636918; 28937375; 35713642; 30570551; 34121219; 36812381}.
