1.3.36 Myxofibrosarcoma

Definition

Myxofibrosarcoma is a malignant fibroblastic neoplasm with variably myxoid stroma, nuclear pleomorphism, and a characteristic curvilinear vasculature.

ICD-O coding

8811/3 Myxofibrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B53.0 Myxofibrosarcoma, primary site

Related terminology

Not recommended: myxoid malignant fibrous histiocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Myxofibrosarcomas usually arise in the superficial soft tissue of the limbs, including the limb girdles, with the lower extremities more commonly involved than the upper extremities {8604805; 15138937;38515824; 36437410}. Involvement of the trunk, head and neck, and hands and feet are rare {29065228; 34303554; 35256287}. More than half of the cases arise in the superficial soft tissue of the subcutis/dermis, with the remainder arising in subfascial soft tissue or skeletal muscle. Presentation in the retroperitoneum is exceptional, and most tumours with myxofibrosarcoma-like appearance represent a dedifferentiated liposarcoma in this anatomical location {15832195; 23020289}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a slowly growing, painless mass.

Epidemiology

Myxofibrosarcoma is one of the most common sarcomas in elderly patients and usually presents in the sixth to eighth decades of life, with a slight male predominance. Myxofibrosarcoma may present in young adults but are rare in patients < 30 years of age {8604805; 20878245; 26759307; 37742946; 36437410}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Myxofibrosarcomas have complex karyotypes with intratumoural heterogeneity and chromosome numbers in the triploid or tetraploid range in most cases, even in low-grade neoplasms {8604805}. Increased cytogenetic alterations are seen with histological grade progression. Local recurrences may also show more complex cytogenetic aberrations in comparison with the primary tumour, correlating with histological progression that may be seen in recurrences {16415793}. Myxofibrosarcomas share overlapping genomic and transcriptomic features with undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas, although the latter more commonly exhibit whole-genome doubling and more complex genomes {39258383}.

Myxofibrosarcomas have a predominance of somatic copy-number alterations with a comparatively low tumour mutation burden compared with other pleomorphic sarcomas {20601955; 29100075; 30018380; 39258383}. There are frequent gains of chromosome 5p, where several oncogenes (TRIO, RICTOR, SKP2, and AMACR) are variably coamplified and potentially associated with higher tumour grade {22322669; 25384383; 28776571; 27577794}. Occurring almost mutually exclusively, variants in TP53 (46%) and cell cycle checkpoints, RB1 (18%), and CDKN2A/CDKN2B (16%) collectively play central pathogenetic role in approximately half of myxofibrosarcomas {30018380}. Driver regulomic pathways harbour recurrently altered targets, such as missense variants and amplifications of NTRK1 and loss-of-function variants and homozygous deletions of NF1 in the RTK/RAS/MAPK pathway and amplification of VGLL3 in the Hippo pathway {20601955; 29100075; 30018380}, as well as amplifications of TRIO and RICTOR that activate the RAC/PAK and AKT/mTOR pathways, respectively {28776571; 27577794}. PAK1 overexpression, being amplification-driven in a subset, promotes angiogenesis through STAT5B-mediated CSF2 transactivation {37564209}. The genomic alterations in AXL p.(Trp451Cys) and p.(Trp450Cys) are relatively specific to myxofibrosarcoma and can activate downstream ERK signalling {38369783}. AXL gene amplification, while observed in various sarcoma subtypes, is most common in myxofibrosarcoma. Notably, high AXL mRNA levels are also seen in cases lacking AXL genomic alterations, indicating epigenetic regulation of AXL expression in myxofibrosarcoma {38369783}.

Macroscopic appearance

Superficial tumours usually consist of multiple, variably gelatinous or firm nodules. Deep-seated tumours often form a single mass with infiltrative margins. Areas of tumour necrosis are often found in higher-grade tumours.

Histopathology

Myxofibrosarcomas have a broad histological spectrum, but all tumours share common histological findings including a multinodular growth pattern, incomplete fibrous septa, myxoid stroma, and a characteristic curvilinear vasculature composed of elongated, curvilinear thin-walled vessels. Subcutaneous tumours are highly infiltrative and often extend well beyond the clinically apparent lesion. At the low-grade end of the spectrum, myxofibrosarcoma is relatively hypocellular, composed of scattered hyperchromatic, spindled to stellate cells within a prominent myxoid stroma {8604805; 15138937}. The tumour cells are often clustered around the curvilinear vessels with or without associated chronic inflammatory cells. Vacuolated tumour cells resembling lipoblasts (pseudolipoblasts) may be present. In low-grade tumours, mitotic activity is inconspicuous. In intermediate- to higher-grade neoplasms, the tumour is more cellular, composed of fascicles and sheets of atypical spindled to pleomorphic tumour cells. Pleomorphic tumour cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm resembling myoid cells may also be present. Intermediate- and high-grade myxofibrosarcomas have increased mitotic activity, frequent atypical mitotic figures, and frequent intratumoural haemorrhage. Tumour necrosis is found in high-grade myxofibrosarcoma. Intermediate- and high-grade myxofibrosarcomas frequently have areas resembling those of low-grade myxofibrosarcoma with more prominent myxoid stroma. Epithelioid myxofibrosarcoma is composed of a sheet-like proliferation to cohesive clusters of epithelioid tumour cells in myxoid stroma. The neoplastic cells have enlarged vesicular nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm {17197925; 39610041}. Histologically it may mimic metastatic carcinoma or melanoma; in cases involving the head and neck, undifferentiated/dedifferentiated melanoma should be excluded before rendering a diagnosis of epithelioid myxofibrosarcoma {39610041}. The myxoid stroma and curvilinear vasculature are often less conspicuous than those in conventional myxofibrosarcoma. By immunohistochemistry, myxofibrosarcoma may be positive for SMA and/or CD34 {23600956}. Stains for desmin and S100 are typically negative. Epithelioid myxofibrosarcoma may show focal immunoreactivity for keratin.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show moderate to high cellularity with curvilinear vessels in abundant myxoid matrix showing small spindle cells, mild nuclear atypia, and pseudolipoblasts in low-grade neoplasms. High-grade neoplasms show plentiful spindle and pleomorphic cells, some giant cells, and atypical mitoses with scant matrix, while the epithelioid pattern shows pleomorphic epithelioid cells with often high-grade nuclei and macronucleoli in abundant myxoid matrix with curvilinear vessels. Ancillary tests can assist in the differential diagnosis from other myxoid neoplasms {33041221}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multinodular architecture with infiltrative margins; variably pleomorphic tumour cells with myxoid stroma and curvilinear vasculature; hypercellular areas in higher-grade tumours.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system is applicable.

Prognosis and prediction

Given the infiltrative nature of the neoplasm, local and often repeated recurrence, irrespective of histological grade, occurs in 30–40% of cases {8604805; 15138937}. With recurrence, tumours may undergo histological-type progression from low-grade to higher-grade tumours {8604805}. Local recurrence within 12 months increases tumour-associated mortality. Metastasis and tumour-related mortality correlate with tumour grade. Low-grade myxofibrosarcoma typically does not develop metastasis, but metastasis is seen in 20–35% of high-grade myxofibrosarcoma, most commonly pulmonary metastases. Osseous and lymph node metastasis are sometimes seen. The overall 5-year mortality rate is 25–35% {38141181; 8604805; 15138937; 36437410}. Tumour size, histological grade, and surgical margins are predictors of survival. Older age at diagnosis, non-extremity location, and metastasis are also predictive of a worse outcome {38141181}. Epithelioid myxofibrosarcoma is more aggressive than conventional myxofibrosarcoma, with an increased risk of metastasis (> 50%) {17197925; 37742946}. Wide excision combined with radiotherapy reduces local recurrence and has a positive impact on overall survival {8604805; 38141181}.
