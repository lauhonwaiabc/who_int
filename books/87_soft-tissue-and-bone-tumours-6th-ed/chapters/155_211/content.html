1.5.5 Tufted angioma and kaposiform haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Tufted angioma (TA) and kaposiform haemangioendothelioma (KHE) are related vascular tumours that typically present in childhood and are widely considered to represent histological entities within a single disease spectrum.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

TA is a superficial lesion involving the skin and subcutis {11167978; 20644037}. Bulky and more proliferative lesions within this spectrum – traditionally classified as KHE – also involve the skin and subcutis but typically extend across tissue planes into deeper soft tissues of the extremities, head and neck, trunk, axilla, and groin. Less commonly, KHE involves the retroperitoneum, mediastinum, and bone, with rare cases showing direct extension into visceral organs {23796341; 8494101; 15105642; 9420544}. Multifocal forms occur, predominantly affecting bone {23373432; 27820128; 24931465}.

Clinical features

Patients with TA typically present with a pink or red-purple cutaneous macule or an infiltrated plaque, whereas those with KHE present with a poorly defined, large, bulging, indurated purple-red tumour. Both lesions may be painful and/or associated with hypertrichosis or hyperhidrosis {11167978; 20644037; 23796341}.

Together, they account for the majority of cases of Kasabach–Merritt phenomenon (KMP). This usually arises in the context of large congenital or early infantile TA/KHE lesions and is characterized by profound thrombocytopenia due to intratumoural platelet trapping, often accompanied by petechiae, ecchymoses, and a sudden increase in tumour size {15105642}. Some lesions may show partial regression or a waxing and waning clinical course {10642677}.

Epidemiology

Most cases of TA/KHE are congenital or acquired in childhood, with a slight male predilection {11167978; 20644037; 27607323; 28727248; 28579853}. Approximately 50% are detected at birth, and 90% within the first year of life {29603128}. A case series of KHE in the USA reported an estimated prevalence of about 0.91 cases per 100 000 children {22871490}. Rare cases arise in adolescence and adulthood (without KMP) {15105642; 17186794}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Genetic alterations reported in KHE and TA are heterogeneous and include a somatic activating GNA14 c.614A>T p.(Gln205Leu) mutation in some cases {27476652}. Methylation profiling studies have revealed a distinctive shared epigenetic pattern in KHE and TA {31887709}, bolstering the consensus that TA and KHE fall within the same biological spectrum. Podoplanin expressed by spindled endothelial cells is thought to elicit platelet activation by binding its natural ligand CLC1B (a lectin receptor highly expressed on platelets, also called CLEC2) and stimulates the platelet-trapping phenomenon responsible for KMP {28992650; 19630798}.

Macroscopic appearance

TA and KHE appear as multiple grey to red infiltrative nodules within fibrous tissue.

Histopathology

Classic TA histology shows multiple small, well-circumscribed vascular tufts dispersed in a cannonball-like fashion within the dermis, sometimes extending into the subcutis. The tufts are composed of closely packed capillaries with compressed lumina, occasionally accompanied by subtle fascicles of delicate spindled endothelial cells. Dilated thin-walled lymphatic vessels focally form crescentic clefts at the tuft periphery.

Classic KHE histology includes the presence of vascular tufts equivalent to those of TA, but accompanied by larger, less defined, coalescing nodules composed of fascicles of spindled endothelial cells forming elongate slit-like lumina containing erythrocytes and curving around epithelioid, pericyte-rich nodules with central microthrombi. Other areas display more typical capillary formation. Dilated lymphatic vessels reminiscent of lymphatic malformation are frequently seen within adjacent desmoplastic stroma {29217054; 24965196}. Mitoses are rare. Residual lesions are fibrotic versions of the original {10642677}.

TA/KHE endothelial cells are strongly positive for the panendothelial markers CD31, CD34, and ERG, and the spindled forms are also positive for lymphatic markers including podoplanin, LYVE1, and PROX1. Lymphatic markers are less extensively positive in superficial TA lesions than in KHE, reflecting their lesser complement of spindled endothelial cells {20975337}. TA/KHE endothelial cells are GLUT1 negative {15105642} and WT1 positive {21697701}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cannonball-like distribution of multiple small and variably well-defined intradermal lobules of tightly packed small capillaries; dual positivity of spindled endothelial cells for panendothelial and lymphatic markers; presence of platelet-rich microthrombi.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Bulky, deep KHE results in KMP more frequently than TA {20644037}. A minority of untreated superficial examples (TA) may partially regress, leaving minor fibrous cutaneous changes, but they generally recur {16785378; 15942226}. Large, deep KHE is associated with higher morbidity and risk of mortality due to tumour infiltration/compression and KMP. KHE does not fully regress even with successful medical therapy; biopsies of fibrotic residual tissue demonstrate foci of viable tumour, and recurrence is common {10642677; 28727248}. Wide surgical excision, although rarely feasible, can be curative.

TA is classified as a benign tumour, whereas KHE is deemed borderline because of its locally aggressive behaviour and a report of 2 cases with multifocal lymph node involvement along a lymphatic chain {26055853}. No distant metastases have been reported {15105642}.
