1.3.38 Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma

Definition

Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma is a rare malignant fibroblastic neoplasm composed of distinctive epithelioid cells arranged in cords and nests and embedded in a densely sclerotic hyalinized stroma.

ICD-O coding

8840/3 Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5F.2 & XH4BT2 Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified of other specified sites & Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours are deep-seated and arise most often in the upper or lower extremities or limb girdle, followed by the trunk and the head and neck {11395547; 7661286; 20614275}. Rarely, tumours arise in the pelvis, retroperitoneum, viscera, or bone {15322876; 24921641; 25353281; 31675134}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a mass of variable duration, with one third reporting recent enlargement or pain {7661286}.

Epidemiology

Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma is rare and usually arises in middle-aged and elderly adults (mean: 45–50 years), with an equal sex distribution {7661286; 36690575}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcomas are characterized by recurrent pathognomonic chromosomal translocations, involving EWSR1 (> 80%) and less commonly FUS (5–10%) {17721195; 22388756; 25231134}. The most prevalent gene fusion (> 60%) is EWSR1::CREB3L1 {22982887; 24441665; 25123073; 25231134; 24025527}, with other infrequent EWSR1 gene partners, including CREB3L2 {25231134} and CREB3L3 {28569045; 31335288}, being described. Single cases of alternative fusions have been reported, including FUS::CREM and PAX5::CREB3L1 {28939748}. Furthermore, most hybrid sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcomas / low-grade fibromyxoid sarcomas display a FUS::CREB3L2 chimera, identical to that seen in most low-grade fibromyxoid sarcomas {25231134}. Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma and low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma also share overlap in the gene expression profiles, for example, with high expression of MUC4 and CD24 in both tumours {21536545; 28939748}. In contrast to low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, however, both pure and hybrid sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma cases typically have complex genomic profiles, including deletions/copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity at chromosome arm 11p, loss of chromosome 22, and intragenic deletions of the DMD gene {28939748}. A small subset of sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcomas lack MUC4 expression and harbour instead YAP1::KMT2A fusion {31592798; 32461620}. Of note, most cases with the latter fusion have a portion of KMT2A (exons 4/5-6, encoding the CXXC domain) inserted between exon 4/5 and exon 8/9 of YAP1, resulting in a complex YAP1::KMT2A::YAP1 fusion {32461620; 37246732}. Of interest, DNA methylation analysis of YAP1::KMT2A-positive sarcomas formed a distinct cluster, separate from both conventional sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma and low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma {39641785}. These results raise the question of whether YAP1::KMT2A-rearranged sarcomas may represent a unique sarcoma subtype.

Macroscopic appearance

Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma is usually well circumscribed, lobular or multilobulated, and involves deep musculature and fascia. Periosteal adherence is common, and there is occasionally erosion of underlying bone. The cut surface is firm and white. Areas of calcification may be present. Most tumours are < 100 mm, but occasional cases measure > 200 mm {11395547; 7661286}.

Histopathology

Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma shows infiltrative growth within the adipose tissue and skeletal muscle in most cases. A characteristic feature is the prominent hyalinized sclerotic collagenous stroma, showing variable degrees of cellularity. The lesional cells, composed of relatively bland and monomorphic epithelioid cells, are arranged in cords, nests, or occasionally sheets, often separated by parallel columns of collagen bands. The tumour cells usually have clear to pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and uniform round nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli. Although more than half of the cases may show a spindle cell component, it is usually limited in nature. Pseudoalveolar or acinar growth patterns are occasionally seen. Hypocellular areas with myxoid or fibrous stroma are common. Calcifications or chondro-osseous differentiation may be seen occasionally. Moreover, the dense hyalinized stromal component may simulate osteoid matrix deposition, especially in intraosseous examples, resulting in misdiagnosis of bone-forming tumours, for example, osteosarcoma {31675134}. Rarely, the tumours show a scarce sclerotic stroma (non-fibrosing examples) being composed of solid sheets of round to epithelioid cells {26212576}. No distinct morphological features are observed between tumours with canonical EWSR1/FUS gene rearrangements and tumours showing the YAP1::KMT2A fusions {31592798; 31913156; 39641785}.

Areas of conventional low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma may be present, resulting in the designation of hybrid tumours {25231134; 22982887}.

MUC4 expression is present in 80–90% of cases and is strong, diffuse, and cytoplasmic {22982887}. MUC4 positivity is associated with the presence of EWSR1::CREB3L1/2/3 fusions, while tumours harbouring alternative YAP1::KMT2A fusions are typically negative for this marker {31592798; 31913156; 39641785}. Expression of EMA and SMA is present in approximately 40% of cases. Keratins are typically negative, which is helpful in the differential diagnosis with carcinoma {11395547; 20614275}.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show low cellularity, with cells varying from small round blue cells to ovoid spindle cells and plasmacytoid cells in a fibrotic to myxoid stroma, with a broad differential diagnosis, including malignant melanoma and mucinous carcinoma. Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma and low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma show similar MUC4 immunohistochemistry and FUS fusions on FISH and next-generation sequencing {32624384; 22982887; 24441665}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of EWSR1::CREB3L1 or other rearrangements can help confirm the diagnosis. For cases with characteristic morphology and MUC4 expression, additional molecular studies are not required. However, in tumours lacking MUC4 staining, sequencing is recommended to demonstrate the alternative fusions involving YAP1 and KMT2A for a definitive diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: epithelioid cells arranged in cords, nests, or trabeculae and embedded in a dense sclerotic hyalinized stroma; keratin-negative.

Desirable: diffuse strong MUC4 expression (in most cases); EWSR1::CREB3L1 or other rearrangements; fusions involving YAP1 and KMT2A in MUC4-negative cases.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging is appropriate.

Prognosis and prediction

Sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma pursues a more aggressive clinical course than the related low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, with recurrences (often multiple) in about 50% of cases. Metastases are common, occurring in 40–50% of cases to the lung, pleura, bone, and brain. Poor prognostic features include large tumour size and proximal location {11395547; 7661286}. Whether sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma that arises in the context of low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma has the same prognosis has not yet been established.
