1.2.10 Hibernoma

Definition

Hibernoma is a benign adipocytic neoplasm with brown fat differentiation.

ICD-O coding

8880/0 Hibernoma

ICD-11 coding

2E80.0Z & XH1054 Lipoma, unspecified & Hibernoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Hibernomas are most often located in the thigh, trunk, chest, upper extremity, and head and neck. Myxoid and spindle cell subtypes often involve the posterior neck and shoulder {11395560}. Fewer than 10% of cases occur in intra-abdominal, retroperitoneal, or thoracic locations. Intraosseous hibernomas primarily affect the innominate or pelvic bones, followed by the spine and femur {37099409}.

Clinical features

Demographics include hibernomas that typically present in young adults (mean age: 38 years; range: 2–75 years) as small, slow-growing, painless, mobile, subcutaneous {11395560}, or deep-seated {29629919} masses. There is a clear predilection for a deep/intramuscular location {29629919}; these are slightly more common in male patients.

By radiological imaging, on MRI, soft tissue hibernomas demonstrate similar signal intensity as that of subcutaneous fat on both T1-weighted and T2-weighted imaging in 56% of cases. In 36% of cases, when compared with the subcutaneous fat, hibernomas have decreased signal intensity on T1-weighted MRI and increased signal intensity on T2-weighted or short-tau inversion recovery (STIR) sequences. On CT, soft tissue hibernomas appear similar or slightly hyperdense to subcutaneous fat. Prominent internal septations, as well as serpentine internal high- and low-flow branching vascularity, with a feeding vascular pedicle (not seen in lipoma or well-differentiated liposarcoma) can be seen on both CT and MRI. Enhancement is common and more avid in lesions with a greater percentage of brown fat. Tc-99m methylene diphosphate (MDP) bone scintigraphy demonstrates variable radiotracer uptake. 18F-FDG PET/CT reveals marked radionuclide uptake. Hibernomas show high metabolic activity because of increased vasculature and are frequently detected incidentally during 18F-FDG PET performed for staging {23385517}.

Epidemiology

In case reports and case series, hibernomas account for < 2% of benign and 1% of all adipocytic tumours, with a slight male predominance and appearing in the third and fourth decades of life {25885469; 33413245}.

Etiology

Syndromic associations are not usually reported, yet there are few reports of hibernomas arising in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 {23602588; 25309600; 28478946; 33395907}.

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetically, almost all hibernomas have breakpoints in chromosome arm 11q, with a distinctive clustering to 11q13 {10393837; 12800150; 8056446; 21078971}. FISH, SNP array, and multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification analyses have revealed complex genomic rearrangements, with translocations and interstitial deletions that affect both homologues of chromosome 11 {10393837; 12800150; 21078971}. The ultimate consequence of these rearrangements is a set of deletions that cluster to a 3 Mb region in 11q13, with a preferential localization to regions covering the tumour suppressor genes MEN1 and AIP. Somatic SNVs in MEN1 and AIP have not been detected in hibernoma {28722204}. However, rearrangement, amplification, or increased expression of HMGA2, CDK4, or MDM2 are not detected {19508721; 29629919}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, hibernomas are typically well circumscribed, vaguely lobular, and brown-yellow in appearance {11395560}.

Histopathology

Hibernomas are highly vascular, composed of large polygonal brown fat cells with finely vacuolated voluminous cytoplasm, a variable degree of cytoplasmic eosinophilia, and centrally placed, normochromatic, round nuclei with small nucleoli {33406387}. Scattered throughout are univacuolated white fat cells. Classic hibernomas contain > 70% brown fat. There are several patterns of hibernoma based on the amount of brown fat, myxoid change, and tinctorial quality of the brown fat cells (eosinophilic or pale), yet hibernoma is always benign and no malignant type exists {11395560}. Stromal myxoid change and an increased number of stromal spindled cells resembling those of spindle cell lipoma may be observed {11395560}. Nuclear atypia and mitotic activity are absent {29629919}.

By immunohistochemistry, hibernomas stain positive for S100, also express CD10 {33406387}, and are generally negative for SOX10, CD68, keratin, EMA, and nuclear MDM2 {32815035}, without amplification of MDM2 {29629919; 33406387; 32815035} or fusion of DDIT3 and with low to absent Ki-67. Hibernomas strongly express the brown-fat marker UCP1 {31114671}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows tissue fragments of uniform polygonal and oval multivacuolated brown fat cells with abundant finely granular and microvacuolated cytoplasm, central bland round nuclei, and plentiful branching capillaries {11391608}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: multivacuolated brown fat cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Hibernomas are entirely benign and typically do not recur after local excision {11395560}. There are no malignant subtypes of hibernoma {11395560; 35475774}.
