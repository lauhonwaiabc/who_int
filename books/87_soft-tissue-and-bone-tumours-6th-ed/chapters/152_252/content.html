1.5.2 Anastomosing haemangioma

Definition

Anastomosing haemangioma is a benign vascular neoplasm that is poorly marginated and composed of thin-walled anastomosing vessels lined by a monolayer of endothelial cells with protuberant nuclei.

ICD-O coding

9120/0 Haemangioma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E81.0Z Neoplastic haemangioma, unspecified

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Anastomosing haemangioma was initially reported in the male genital tract {19606014}. The most common sites are the kidney and retroperitoneal/paraspinal adipose tissue {26960722; 20534992}, but examples have been reported in the female genital tract {21846922; 28050796}, gastrointestinal tract and liver {29975248; 23887160}, adrenal gland {37553871}, soft tissue {26945338}, and skin {30666666}. Lesions termed “hepatic small vessel neoplasm” and “thrombotic haemangioma of skin” are essentially anastomosing haemangiomas {27090685; 29975248; 31633489}.

Clinical features

Because many examples are visceral, they may be incidental findings on imaging studies, although some patients with renal lesions present with haematuria {19606014}; patients with superficial examples present with palpable masses or with pain {26945338}. Colorectal lesions can manifest as polyps {23887160}, and hepatic lesions often present during the evaluation of another process {23887160; 27090685; 29975248}. Imaging studies often show a well-marginated lesion that is mildly hyperdense, with heterogeneous attenuation that is inconclusive for malignancy {26960722; 26945338}. Multifocality has been documented {29279869}. Most lesions are 1–2 cm, but larger examples (as large as 7.5 cm in soft tissue {26945338}) are known. Hepatic examples can be both infiltrative and as large as 16 cm {27090685; 29366622}.

Epidemiology

Reported cases are predominantly adults (mean age in the initial series: ~60 years {19606014}), but paediatric lesions have been reported {30369288}. There is no sex predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The vast majority of cases harbour an activating hotspot mutation in GNAQ, GNA11, or GNA14; similar mutations have been identified in hepatic small vessel neoplasms {28084343; 29574926; 29975248; 31707589}, which almost certainly represent a hepatic anastomosing haemangioma. No other molecular alterations have been identified.

Macroscopic appearance

Anastomosing haemangiomas have a haemorrhagic, mahogany-coloured, spongy appearance.

Histopathology

These lesions have a loosely lobulated architecture at low magnification and can be associated with a medium-calibre vessel. There is often focal infiltration into adjoining tissue. The neoplasms consist of anastomosing sinusoidal capillary-sized vessels with scattered hobnail endothelial cells {30958692}. There may be a lymphocytic infiltrate. Mitoses are absent or rare. Mild cytological atypia can be present, but no multilayering of endothelial cells is seen. Vascular thrombi are typical, and zones of central sclerosis with focal necrosis are common. Extramedullary haematopoiesis can be a prominent feature, and some examples have striking hyaline globules. Immunohistochemistry shows labelling with CD34, CD31, and ERG in the endothelial cells.

Cytology

FNABs are not routinely performed.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: anastomosing vessels lined by hobnail endothelial cells, often in association with fibrin thrombi, hyaline globules, and extramedullary haematopoiesis.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Anastomosing haemangiomas, even when multicentric or infiltrative, are benign.
