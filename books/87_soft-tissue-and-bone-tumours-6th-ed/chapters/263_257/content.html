2.6.3 Dedifferentiated chordoma

Definition

Dedifferentiated chordoma is a chordoma with a biphasic appearance, characterized by conventional chordoma and high-grade sarcoma, that almost exclusively arises in bones of the axial skeleton.

ICD-O coding

9372/3 Dedifferentiated chordoma

ICD-11 coding

2B5Z & XH7303 Malignant mesenchymal neoplasm of unspecified type & Dedifferentiated chordoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The locations are similar to those of conventional chordoma, predominantly in sacrococcygeal sites {32427623}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

The symptoms are similar to those of conventional chordoma, although progression may be more rapid. Pain and site-related neurological symptoms are common. Dedifferentiated chordoma may present de novo or at a location of a previously resected conventional chordoma.

Imaging

Dedifferentiated chordomas show a morphology similar to that of conventional tumours and cannot be distinguished on radiographs or CT. A bimorphic appearance may be appreciated on MRI, with the dedifferentiated component suggested by areas of tumour that are relatively hypointense on T2-weighted imaging, distinct from the typically T2-hyperintense conventional chordoma. The T2-hypointense area suspicious for dedifferentiation should be the preferred site of diagnostic biopsy {18450635}. 18F-FDG PET may be used in combination for evaluation when dedifferentiation is suspected {28184416}.

Epidemiology

Dedifferentiated chordoma is the rarest notochordal tumour type, collectively, with only fewer than 90 cases published in medical literature {18450635; 17630110; 32427623}. The reported cases affect middle-aged adults, and the case series describe a median age of 58 years (range: 15–81 years) and a slight male predominance {32427623}. Skull base tumours have been described to affect younger adults and present a female predilection {31652338; 36217885}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis for dedifferentiated chordoma is similar to that for conventional chordoma. See section Conventional chordoma.

Recurrent mutations in TP53 have been identified and appear enriched in the dedifferentiated component, implicating tumour suppressor dysregulation in chordoma dedifferentiation {32427623}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour measures < 30 to > 200 mm, with a median size of 100 mm {32427623}. The tumour presents as a large mass with a biphasic appearance. The dedifferentiated component has the gross features of a high-grade sarcoma with a solid surface, juxtaposed to a gelatinous and myxoid conventional chordoma.

Histopathology

The distinguishing characteristic is the simultaneous presence of a conventional chordoma and a high-grade spindle and/or pleomorphic sarcoma. The dedifferentiated component can account for < 5% to > 95% (median 50–60%) of the tumour {32427623}. Osteosarcomatous, chondrosarcomatous, and/or rhabdomyosarcomatous elements can be seen. The two components are usually abruptly separated, but they can be intermingled or show gradual transitions.

Immunohistochemistry

While the conventional component expresses cytokeratin and brachyury, in the dedifferentiated component, cytokeratin staining can be focal to absent, and brachyury immunoreactivity is also absent {16538613; 32427623}. Specific lineages, such as rhabdomyoblastic, should be supported by immunohistochemical expression of desmin and myogenin. Complete loss of H3K27me3 and H3K27me2 has been noted in the dedifferentiated component in a subset of skull-base cases {36217885}.

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis includes dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma, radiation-associated sarcoma, other primary bone sarcomas, conventional chordoma (with prominent spindling), and poorly differentiated chordoma {32427623}.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; conventional chordoma (histologically or by history) plus high-grade sarcoma.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system is applicable.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is poor, with a high metastatic rate and a median overall survival of 20 months after the diagnosis of dedifferentiated chordoma {32427623}. In the absence of distant metastases, surgery is the primary treatment modality. The benefits of radiotherapy or chemotherapy remain limited {31338785}.
