1.2.14 Myxoid liposarcoma

Definition

Myxoid liposarcoma (MLPS) is a malignant adipocytic neoplasm composed of uniform spindled or round cells with variable numbers of univacuolated lipoblasts, set in a myxoid stroma with a branching, plexiform capillary vasculature.

ICD-O coding

8852/3 Myxoid liposarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B59.Y & XH3EL0 Liposarcoma, other specified primary site & Myxoid liposarcoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: round cell liposarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MLPSs typically present within deep soft tissues of the extremities, most often the thigh {8608528; 23401071}. Very rarely they arise in the subcutis. Primary MLPS is exceedingly rare in the retroperitoneum {27158758; 18820664}.

Clinical features

MLPSs typically present as large, painless masses. Retroperitoneal MLPS most often represents a metastasis {27158758; 18820664}. Multifocal disease, either synchronous or metachronous, represents distant soft tissue metastases {10914725}. Surgical wide excision is the current treatment mainstay {17510918}. MLPS is extremely radiosensitive {25213588} and relatively sensitive to anthracyclines and trabectedin compared with other sarcomas {17586092; 36995731; 38232337}.

Epidemiology

MLPS accounts for approximately 20–30% of liposarcomas {11757867} and 5% of adult soft tissue sarcomas, without significant sex predilection. Although peak incidence is in the fourth to fifth decades of life {33029074; 30922351}, MLPS is the most common liposarcoma subtype in children and adolescents {21394894; 19194281}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

MLPS and its hypercellular higher-grade form harbour identical genetic abnormalities. Most are characterized by the t(12;16)(q13;p11) translocation generating FUS::DDIT3 fusion transcripts, translated into a chimeric oncoprotein that alters transcription and differentiation. DDIT3 (CHOP, GADD153) is a DNA-binding transcription factor normally induced under cell stress, which blocks adipocytic terminal differentiation {23314846; 26396188}. In the fusion, FUS provides promoter sequences and its first 3–13 exons {20581806}. Fusion protein variants consistently retain the FUS N-terminal transcriptional activation domain that is largely conserved across FET protein family members, including EWSR1 {25494299}. EWSR1 substitutes for FUS in about 3% of MLPSs, generating EWSR1::DDIT3 oncoproteins {20581806}, without proven histological or prognostic differences. The result is a block to terminal fatty differentiation {18596980}. The genomic background shows a low mutation burden, with wildtype TP53. More than 50% of cases carry TERT promoter variants {24726063}, and about 25% have variants activating PI3K/mTOR {30787173}. Recently it has been shown that FUS::DDIT3 fusion oncoprotein inhibits BAF complex-mediated remodelling of adipogenic enhancer sites via sequestration of the adipogenic transcription factor, CEBPB, from the genome, generating a BAF complex loss-of-function phenotype, independent of mutations {35390276}.

Macroscopic appearance

MLPSs are typically large (> 100 mm), well-circumscribed, multinodular intramuscular neoplasms {11751490}. The cut surface is smooth, gelatinous, and glistening. Higher-grade tumours show a firmer, fleshy tan surface. Macroscopic necrosis is uncommon. Adequate sampling to estimate the amount of hypercellularity is essential, because this is a major prognostic determinant {12173385; 10379861}.

Histopathology

MLPSs are moderately cellular, lobulated tumours, featuring patternless arrays of uniform, small spindle cells, with variable numbers of lipoblasts. Tumour cells are set in abundant myxoid stroma with a striking plexiform, arborizing, capillary network (chicken wire) {11757867}, around which neoplastic cells often cluster. Mucin pools may be present, imparting a microcystic / pulmonary oedema–like pattern. MLPS typically lacks atypia or substantial mitotic activity. Nuclear pleomorphism is extremely rare, being observed in approximately 2% of cases {37704823}. The lipoblasts are smaller than in other liposarcomas, predominantly univacuolated/bivacuolated, and may be rare or even absent. A variable percentage of mature fat may be present. Chondro-osseous metaplastic elements are rarely seen {25747531}.

Higher-grade MLPS is defined by the presence of round cell areas exceeding 5% of the tumour, usually accompanied by diminished myxoid matrix. A corded or trabecular pattern is often present. Pure higher-grade MLPS may be indistinguishable from other round cell sarcomas, requiring molecular genetic studies for diagnosis. The presence of round cell areas should be recorded. Some cases show areas with modestly increased cellularity of uncertain significance without round cell morphology {8554106}. MLPS treated with either preoperative chemotherapy or radiotherapy often show a marked decrease in cellularity, with only scattered spindle cells, extensive stromal hyalinization, and sometimes maturation into white adipose tissue {23272660}.

Since the introduction of a mouse monoclonal antibody directed against the N-terminus of DDIT3, immunohistochemistry currently plays a relevant role in the diagnosis of MLPS. Overall, DDIT3 immunohistochemistry exhibits 96% sensitivity and 98% specificity for high-grade MLPS. Strong, diffuse staining appears to be 100% specific to the extent that it may replace molecular genetic testing in most cases {33731886; 32815829; 33465826}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows variable cellularity with myxoid tissue fragments showing plexiform, branching thin capillary network, small uniform spindle cells with single prominent nucleoli, and a small number of lipoblasts. There is a spectrum to a high-grade round cell MLPS, which shows high cellularity with dispersed small round cells with high N:C ratio, mild pleomorphism, and rare lipoblasts. Correlation with imaging and both FISH and immunocytochemistry assists diagnosis {33168472; 17427222}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of the translocation/fusion transcript may be helpful in distinguishing MLPS from other myxoid sarcomas, and higher-grade MLPS from various round cell sarcomas {26970672}. FUS and EWSR1 can substitute for each other and occur in other sarcomas, whereas DDIT3 is unique to MLPS. Thus, FISH break-apart probes directed at DDIT3 are a sensitive, specific strategy {22089349}. Alternative diagnostic methods include RT-PCR with primers designed to cover all common fusion variants {20581806}, massively parallel sequencing {28817827}, and multiplexed colour-coded probe pair technologies {29104083}. However, the recent introduction of DDIT3 immunohistochemistry represents a valid alternative to molecular testing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland spindle cells within a myxoid matrix; delicately arborizing capillaries; predominantly round cell morphology with diminished myxoid stroma in higher-grade MLPS.

Desirable: DDIT3 rearrangement or protein overexpression.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence occurs in 12–25% of cases {22190864; 17510918}. Distant metastases develop in 30–60%, sometimes years from onset, and may progress slowly {10379861}. Unlike most sarcomas, MLPSs often metastasize to soft tissues and bone (particularly spine) {22052112}, in preference to lung {10379861; 12173385}. Higher-grade tumours are associated with increased risk of metastasis {8554106; 11751490; 23401071; 17510918; 10379861; 22052112}. Necrosis and TP53 and CDKN2A alterations have been associated with adverse prognosis {17510918; 11751490; 22190864; 16177957}. The prognostic significance of more limited hypercellular areas is uncertain.
