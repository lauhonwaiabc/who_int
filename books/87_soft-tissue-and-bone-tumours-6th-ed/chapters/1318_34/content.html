1.3.18 Cellular angiofibroma

Definition

Cellular angiofibroma is a benign, cellular, and richly vascularized fibroblastic neoplasm that usually arises in the superficial soft tissues of the vulvovaginal, inguinoscrotal, or paratesticular regions.

ICD-O coding

9160/0 Cellular angiofibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH4E06 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Cellular angiofibroma

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiomyofibroblastoma-like tumour (in male patients).

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Cellular angiofibromas typically arise in the superficial soft tissues of the vulvovaginal, inguinoscrotal, or paratesticular regions {32958293; 39890306; 33189965}. Rare examples have been described at other sites, including oral mucosa, anorectal region, orbit, male pelvis, prostate, and subcutaneous tissue of the chest wall {9694623; 23766844; 19644547; 25105048; 34808212; 33235884; 33016164; 32576059}.

Clinical features

Patients usually present with a slow-growing painless mass. The most frequent preoperative diagnosis is (Bartholin) cyst {20852591; 15489646; 9199640}. In male patients, the mass may be associated with hernia or hydrocoele {15489646; 9422311}.

Epidemiology

Cellular angiofibroma is a rare neoplasm arising in adults. Women and men are roughly equally affected, with a peak incidence in the fifth decade of life in women and the seventh decade of life in men {20852591; 15489646; 9422311; 9199640; 39890306}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cellular angiofibroma is characterized by partial or complete loss of chromosome 13 (including the RB1 locus) and/or chromosome 16 {17727484; 20852591; 22790852; 28193293}. Loss of 13q14, including RB1, is also characteristic of mammary and soft tissue myofibroblastomas, as well as spindle cell / pleomorphic lipoma, acral fibromyxoma, and fibroepithelial stromal polyp of the vulvovaginal region {33392798; 35332938; 28543636; 27825811; 37922949; 33802620; 32800346; 28887603}. In addition, there is significant morphological and immunohistochemical overlap between these entities, suggesting that these tumours may represent variations along a spectrum of genetically related lesions, the 13q/RB1 family of tumours {22575260; 26523539; 20852591; 27825811; 33802620; 38772617}.

Macroscopic appearance

Reported examples of cellular angiofibroma vary in size from 6 to 250 mm. Those in women are generally smaller (median: 28 mm) than those in men (median: 70 mm). The tumours appear as round, oval, or lobulated well-circumscribed nodules. The consistency of the lesion varies from soft to rubbery, and the cut surface is solid with a greyish-pink to yellowish-brown colour {15489646; 9422311}. Foci of haemorrhage or necrosis are exceptional {15489646}.

Histopathology

The tumours are generally well circumscribed. Poorly marginated, infiltrative tumours occur rarely in men. Tumours are usually moderately to highly cellular and composed of uniform, short spindle-shaped cells in an oedematous to fibrous stroma containing short bundles of delicate collagen fibres and numerous small to medium-sized thick-walled blood vessels with rounded, irregularly ectatic, or branching lumina. The spindle cell component is randomly distributed throughout the lesion, occasionally with a fascicular arrangement or nuclear palisading. Spindle cells have short, oval to fusiform nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli and scant, palely eosinophilic cytoplasm with poorly defined borders. Mitoses are generally sparse, but they can be more frequent in some cases. The stroma consists primarily of wispy collagen, with occasional short bundles of densely eosinophilic collagen fibres. Variable stromal oedema, hyalinization, or myxoid change is often seen, especially in men. Perivascular lymphoid aggregates may be present. Mast cells are frequent. Small aggregates or individual adipocytes are observed in close to 50% of cases. Degenerative changes of slight nuclear enlargement and hyperchromasia, intravascular thrombi, and cystic change can be seen {15489646; 9422311; 15469474}.

Morphological sarcomatous transformation has been described rarely, mostly in vulvar cellular angiofibromas {20305534; 20852591; 19783726; 31936598; 26760687; 40072946; 23161240}. Abrupt transition from cellular angiofibroma to a discrete sarcomatous nodule composed of multivacuolated lipoblasts has been reported, as have pleomorphic and hyperchromatic spindle cells showing morphological features of pleomorphic liposarcoma, atypical lipomatous tumour, or pleomorphic spindle cell sarcoma {20305534; 26760687}. Other rare cases show severely atypical cells scattered in conventional cellular angiofibroma {20852591}. In sarcomatous areas there are few mitoses {20305534; 20852591}; necrosis or haemorrhage is exceedingly rare. By immunohistochemistry, CD34 expression has been documented in 30–60% of tumours. Variable expression of SMA and desmin is identified in a minority of cases. ER and PR are expressed in many cases {15489646; 9422311; 15469474}. RB1 loss is frequently detected {22790852}. The differential diagnosis includes aggressive angiomyxoma, angiomyofibroblastoma, spindle cell lipoma, solitary fibrous tumour, perineurioma, and leiomyoma, as well as PLAG1-rearranged fibromyxoid soft tissue tumours that affect most commonly paediatric patients {33893698; 39120141; 33300192}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Deletion of RB1, located in chromosome band 13q14, can be detected by FISH or other molecular methods but is usually not required for diagnosis {17727484; 20852591; 28193293; 33802620}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland spindle cells; short bundles of delicate collagen fibres; and numerous small to medium-sized thick-walled vessels; variable adipocytic component.

Desirable: loss of RB1 in select cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence is infrequent {15489646; 9422311; 15469474}. To date, some rare cases with atypia or sarcomatous transformation have developed recurrence {40072946}; however, they have not shown metastasis {20305534; 20852591; 19783726; 40072946}.
