1.11.10 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) is a spindle cell neoplasm often arising from a peripheral nerve, from a pre-existing benign nerve sheath tumour, or in a patient with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1); less often, it is sporadic or radiation associated.

ICD-O coding

9540/3 Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5E & XH2XP8 Malignant nerve sheath tumour of peripheral nerves or autonomic nervous system, primary site & Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour

2B5E & XH2VV8 Malignant nerve sheath tumour of peripheral nerves or autonomic nervous system, primary site & Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour with rhabdomyoblastic differentiation

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant schwannoma; neurofibrosarcoma; neurogenic sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

MPNST most commonly arises in the trunk and extremities, followed by the head and neck area {27158754; 3082508}. When arising in a peripheral nerve, MPNST most frequently involves the sciatic nerve. Bone primary tumours are rare {35176251}.

Clinical features

The presenting symptom is an enlarging painful or painless mass that may be palpable or identified on imaging studies. When the tumour involves a nerve, the patient may present with neuropathic symptoms such as motor weakness, paraesthesia, or radicular pain. There are no specific imaging characteristics that distinguish MPNST from other sarcomas, except for possible origin from a large nerve or from neurofibromas in patients with NF1. The 18F-FDG PET imaging technique is sensitive but not specific in detecting MPNSTs in patients with NF1 {17885067}.

Epidemiology

MPNST is a rare tumour, with an incidence of 1.5 cases per 1 million person-years in the general population {24390465}, accounting for approximately 3–5% of soft tissue sarcomas, and 1.6 cases per 1000 person-years in patients with NF1 {12011145}. The tumours typically occur in patients aged 20–50 years. They are rare in children and, when present, are usually associated with NF1 {36414547}. Patients with NF1 are usually younger at the time of diagnosis than patients with sporadic tumours {36414547}.

Etiology

MPNST can be associated with NF1 or radiation therapy.

Pathogenesis

Conventional MPNSTs are characterized by complex karyotypes. Despite different clinical presentations (e.g. NF1-associated, sporadic de novo, or radiotherapy-associated), recent genomic studies have demonstrated highly frequent and concurrent inactivating mutations in NF1, CDKN2A/CDKN2B, TP53, and PRC2 core components (EED or SUZ12), resulting in complete loss-of-function {25240281; 25119042; 25305755; 30722027; 36598417}. In NF1-associated peripheral nerve sheath tumours, as benign precursor plexiform neurofibroma progresses into atypical neurofibroma / atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential and/or transforms into high-grade MPNST, it is accompanied by incremental genomic alterations that inactivate the neurofibromin (NF1)–p16/p15 (CDKN2A/CDKN2B)–PRC2 pathway, and increased copy-number alterations, respectively {21987445; 25240281; 30722027}. The majority of high-grade MPNSTs exhibit deficient PRC2 activity through loss-of-function alterations of the PRC2 core component (EED or SUZ12), leading to complete loss of H3K27me3 expression {26645727; 25240281; 26585554; 27580378}. Given the critical role of PRC2 in development and cell lineage specification, PRC2 loss may underlie the molecular mechanisms of the histological phenotype of heterologous differentiation in MPNSTs. In rare cases of MPNST with H3K27me3 loss and/or in the NF1 setting, targetable NTRK fusions have been reported {35906852; 36331594; 37782551}.

Macroscopic appearance

When arising in a nerve, MPNST appears as a fusiform enlargement of the nerve. In patients with NF1, MPNST often occurs in association with a plexiform neurofibroma. MPNSTs are usually > 50 mm at the time of diagnosis and have a tan-white, fleshy cut surface, often with areas of haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

Tumours are typically composed of fascicles of spindle-shaped cells, often with a haemangiopericytoma-like vascular pattern and alternating hypercellular and hypocellular areas (tapestry or marble-like appearance). Geographical necrosis and conspicuous mitotic figures are common. The lesional cells show mostly monomorphic, hyperchromatic, and wavy nuclei with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm, but occasional extensive pleomorphism may occur, simulating an undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. Perivascular accentuation of tumour cells may be seen; in those areas, the cells frequently appear plumper. Heterologous differentiation, such as skeletal muscle, bone, cartilage, and endothelium, is seen in about 15% of tumours. A malignant triton tumour is an MPNST with skeletal muscle differentiation, including mature rhabdomyoblasts. Glandular differentiation, with or without mucin production, is rarely seen (glandular MPNST); almost all of these tumours arise in patients with NF1. When involving a large nerve, the neoplastic cells may track along the nerve bundles.

In NF1-related MPNST arising in a pre-existent neurofibroma, the distinction between a low-grade MPNST and an atypical neurofibroma / atypical neurofibromatous neoplasm of uncertain biological potential is often problematic, especially on small biopsies. Recently, diagnostic criteria to distinguish these tumours from other types of NF1-associated nerve sheath tumours have been proposed {28551330; 39500722}. Accordingly, low-grade MPNST shows 1.5–4.5 mitoses/mm2 and no necrosis, whereas high-grade MPNST shows ≥ 5 mitoses/mm2 or 1.5–4.5 mitoses/mm2 combined with necrosis.

Immunohistochemically, subsets of MPNST are positive for S100 (< 50% of tumours), SOX10 (< 70%), and GFAP (20–30%) {23929265}. Importantly, staining for S100 and SOX10 is often patchy or focal. Diffuse staining for S100 or SOX10 is usually not compatible with the diagnosis of conventional MPNST. Complete loss of staining for H3K27me3 is a surrogate for deficient PRC2 pathway and may be helpful in the diagnosis of MPNST, with high-grade tumours and sporadic de novo and radiation-induced MPNSTs showing more frequent loss compared with low-grade or NF1-related tumours {26645727; 26585554; 27580378}. Weak/focal expression or mosaic loss of H3K27me3 lacks diagnostic relevance {27580378; 28551330}. The heterologous components (e.g. skeletal muscle differentiation, osteocartilaginous or angiosarcomatous areas) stain for appropriate differentiation markers. The glands in glandular MPNST exhibit positive staining for keratin and CEA and may also be positive for neuroendocrine markers.

In the absence of a clear association with NF1 or a pre-existing neurofibroma (i.e. sporadic or radiotherapy-associated tumours), the diagnosis can be challenging and is based on the integration of histological and immunohistochemical features suggesting Schwannian differentiation and/or loss of H3K27me3 expression.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows high cellularity, with large syncytial tissue fragments showing a vague fascicular pattern of relatively uniform cells with buckled hyperchromatic nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, and variable tapering cytoplasm, in a background of necrosis, stripped nuclei, and single spindle cells. Nuclear pleomorphism can be marked, and rhabdomyoblastic cells can be seen. Ancillary testing is required for diagnosis {22434579}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There is no routine molecular diagnostic testing used for MPNST.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a spindle cell sarcoma usually arising from a pre-existing nerve sheath tumour or in a patient with NF1; in the sporadic setting, diagnosis is most often based on the identification of Schwann cell differentiation (S100/SOX10 focal positivity) and/or loss of H3K27me3 expression.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system may be used.

Prognosis and prediction

MPNST is an aggressive tumour with a poor prognosis. Truncal location, tumour size > 5 cm, local recurrence, and high-grade morphology are all adverse prognostic factors; patients with NF1-associated MPNST appear to have a worse prognosis than patients with sporadic tumours {27158754}. Malignant triton tumours are particularly aggressive.
