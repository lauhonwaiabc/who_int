2.1.18 Dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma

Definition

Dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma is a high-grade chondrosarcoma with a bimorphic appearance, composed of a conventional cartilaginous tumour juxtaposed to a high-grade, non-cartilaginous sarcoma component.

ICD-O coding

9243/3 Dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B50.Z & XH6E77 Chondrosarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of unspecified sites & Dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

For dedifferentiated central chondrosarcoma, the most common sites are the femur (46%), pelvis (28%), and humerus (11%) {17720491}. For dedifferentiated peripheral chondrosarcoma, the typical sites of involvement follow those of conventional peripheral chondrosarcoma, i.e. pelvis, scapula, and ribs {17473135; 19734846}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Common presentations include pain and a palpable mass.

Imaging

Radiological findings often indicate an aggressive destructive tumour with a distinct bimorphic appearance comprising a conventional chondrosarcomatous or enchondromatous component and an osteolytic, permeative, destructive component {15371616}. A pathological fracture is present in 25% of patients {39363319}. The radiographic and MRI features of the chondrosarcomatous component are identical to those of the conventional chondrosarcomas of various grades. The dedifferentiated component has features of a poorly differentiated aggressive tumour with poorly defined margins, bone destruction, periosteal reaction, soft tissue extension, and marked early enhancement on (dynamic) gadolinium chelate–enhanced MRI {33588810; 40358730}. Depending on the type of dedifferentiation, osteoid may be seen on radiographs and CT. Rarely, one component predominates; the dedifferentiated component may be so large that the conventional component is not seen, or small dedifferentiated areas may likewise be missed. Peripheral dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma is rare, and the bimorphic features are similar to those of the central type {40079610}.

Epidemiology

The incidence of dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma in all chondrosarcomas is low (1.4%) {19411454}, with dedifferentiation developing in 10–15% of central chondrosarcomas {3021775; 16691621}. The median age of patients with dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma is 59 years (range: 15–89 years), and there is a slight male predominance {17720491}. Dedifferentiation of peripheral chondrosarcoma has been reported in rare cases {2702574; 19734846}, and these patients may be younger (average: 46 years; range: 22–74 years) {17473135}. Among all dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas, the majority are central, with only 4–6% being peripheral {19734846}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas harbour complex karyotypes {22674453}. Pathogenic IDH1/IDH2 variants are present in 60–80% of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas {21598255; 22674453; 28552826; 36926116; 40117529}. The finding of identical TP53 and IDH1/IDH2 variants in the conventional and dedifferentiated components indicates their shared origin {10629543; 12924447; 21598255; 37747813}. In addition to TP53, alterations in CDKN2A/CDKN2B and TERT promoter are enriched in the dedifferentiated component {22674453; 33147331; 36926116; 40117529}. These findings suggest early genetic divergence of the two components in the tumour development. Global methylation profiles appear similar between the two components of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas {36926116}.

Macroscopic appearance

The resected tumours measure from < 30 mm to > 400 mm in total size. The cartilaginous and non-cartilaginous components can be grossly evident in varying proportions. The bluish-grey, lobulated cartilage component is usually located centrally, while the fleshy, non-cartilaginous sarcoma component is typically extraosseous and/or near the site of a pathological fracture. Small areas of dedifferentiation may not be grossly evident.

Histopathology

There is an abrupt transition between the conventional hyaline cartilage and high-grade, non-cartilaginous sarcoma components {5566365}. The cartilaginous portion can range from enchondroma-like to grade 1 or grade 2 chondrosarcoma. The high-grade dedifferentiated component usually demonstrates the appearance of an undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma or osteosarcoma, or (less frequently) rhabdomyosarcoma, angiosarcoma, and/or leiomyosarcoma {28648942; 36788104}. Rare dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas with overt epithelial differentiation, including squamous, glandular, and adamantinoma-like, have been reported {24792622; 26198745; 28551382}. The amounts of conventional versus dedifferentiated components are variable, and the percentage of dedifferentiated component ranges from 1% to > 99% (median: 60–70%) {16691621; 36788104}, including cases with small dedifferentiated areas {35115636}.

Immunohistochemistry

The immunophenotype of the non-cartilaginous component follows its histological line of differentiation, and immunoreactivity for keratins and/or desmin/MYOD1 can be observed. Complete loss of H3K27me3 staining has been noted in a subset of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas, often in the chest wall, showing morphological resemblance to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour {30291346; 36788104}. p53 overexpression is observed in the dedifferentiated component in a subset of tumours. PDL1 expression has been reported in approximately 50% of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas {27312065}.

Differential diagnosis

While straightforward in cases with characteristic bimorphic microscopic features, the histological diagnosis of dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma can be challenging when only the non-cartilaginous component is sampled in a limited biopsy. Differential diagnosis thus includes high-grade osteosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, and sarcomatoid carcinoma. One can arrive at the correct diagnosis by correlating with radiological findings and molecular testing for IDH1/IDH2. Additionally, molecular testing for H3-3A (H3F3A) and immunohistochemistry for histone H3 G34W can identify giant cell tumours of bone with cartilaginous differentiation that can mimic dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma {32412716}. In dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas with predominance of non-cartilaginous elements, careful radiological review and thorough gross sampling of the resection specimens are critical to identify the low-grade cartilaginous component.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

About 60–80% of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas carry mutations in IDH1/IDH2 in both the conventional and dedifferentiated components {21598255; 22057234; 22674453; 24792622; 28552826; 33147331; 36926116}, which may be helpful for diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; high-grade non-cartilaginous sarcoma juxtaposed to conventional cartilage tumour.

Desirable: presence of IDH1/IDH2 variant.

Staging

Staging is according to bone sarcoma protocols (see <>TNM staging of tumours of bone). See also the information on staging in section Bone tumours: Introduction.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis is dismal, with frequent widespread metastases and an overall 5-year survival of 7–24% {3021775; 15523011; 17720491}. Treatment includes surgery with wide or radical resections. Chemotherapy and radiotherapy have not been shown to improve prognosis {15523011; 15661546; 17720491}. Potential treatment targets under investigation include BCL2 family members, TGFB family genes, MTAP, and PDGFRB variants {23415961; 40117529}. Poor prognostic factors include size > 8 cm, presence of pathological fracture, metastatic disease at diagnosis, pelvic location, and inadequate surgical margin or treatment without surgery {17720491; 28301537; 28420036; 30559960}. Chondrosarcoma with minimal/small amounts (≤ 2 cm) of dedifferentiation is more aggressive than conventional chondrosarcoma, highlighting the need for thorough gross sampling and microscopic evaluation {35115636}. In resected dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas, the percentage of dedifferentiation (≥ 20%) and size of dedifferentiated component (> 3 cm) each predict worse overall survival and progression-free survival {36788104}.
