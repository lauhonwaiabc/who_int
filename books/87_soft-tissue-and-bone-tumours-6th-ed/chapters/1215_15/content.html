1.2.15 Pleomorphic liposarcoma

Definition

Pleomorphic liposarcoma is a malignant mesenchymal neoplasm containing variable numbers of pleomorphic lipoblasts.

ICD-O coding

8854/3 Pleomorphic liposarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B59.Y & XH25R1 Liposarcoma, other specified primary site & Pleomorphic liposarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Most pleomorphic liposarcomas arise in the extremities (60–70%; more commonly in lower than upper limbs) followed by the trunk and head/neck {36006773}. Retroperitoneum and spermatic cord are much less frequently affected {11266523; 15371941; 11979090; 10430277}. Rarely reported sites include the mediastinum, heart, pleura, breast, scalp, colon, orbit, and bone {11757868; 11598853; 11979090; 15371941; 36006773}. Most cases arise in deep soft tissue, but about 25% develop in subcutaneous fat {11757868; 11979090; 15371941}. Superficial (purely dermal) cases are exceptionally rare, but they are the most common liposarcomas to present in the skin {9700369; 11266523; 15371941; 11979090; 32525520; 28585701; 27778364; 23651098; 22472959; 31950475}.

Clinical features

Most patients report a rapidly growing painless mass, usually with a short preoperative duration (median: 3–6 months); a subset of patients report pain, and some patients have symptoms related to tumour location {11979090; 15371941}.

Epidemiology

Pleomorphic liposarcoma is a rare subtype of liposarcoma, accounting for < 5% of all liposarcomas {30340082; 30852046}. In a case series, most cases occurred in adults in later life, with peak incidence in the seventh decade of life and a male predominance {36006773}. Paediatric cases are exceptional {19194281; 40272052; 35407546; 33738541}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The molecular profiles of pleomorphic liposarcomas more closely resemble those of other pleomorphic sarcomas than those of atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated liposarcoma, dedifferentiated liposarcoma, or myxoid liposarcoma {29100075; 12036902}. Metaphase cells show high chromosomal counts and complex structural rearrangements. This complexity is represented by unidentifiable marker chromosomes, non-clonal aberrations, polyploidy, and intercellular heterogeneity. No pathognomonic structural rearrangement has been identified. Studies using array comparative genomic hybridization have shown complex profiles with numerous chromosomal imbalances {12036902; 15702084; 11948494; 15540119; 18784837}. RB1 loss occurs in as many as 50% of cases {24274714; 30852046; 33802620}. The most frequent mutations involve TP53 and NF1 {20601955; 9829856; 18784837}. The genomic profiles of pleomorphic liposarcoma and myxofibrosarcoma are reportedly similar {20601955; 15702084}. Amplification of the 12q14-q15 region (including MDM2 and CDK4 genes) is absent {12036902; 17638873; 24274714}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most tumours are large, with a median size of 80–100 mm {11979090; 15371941}. They are well demarcated but unencapsulated, or poorly defined and infiltrative and sometimes multinodular. On sectioning, most tumours are white to yellow. Myxoid changes and foci of necrosis are often observed.

Histopathology

Most tumours are infiltrative. The presence of pleomorphic lipoblasts is definitional, but their number varies considerably between cases and between areas within the same tumour, emphasizing the importance of adequate sampling {30709449; 24118009; 36006773}. Most (> 50%) cases show tumour necrosis. In most cases, the non-lipogenic component resembles undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, composed of sheets and short fascicles of markedly pleomorphic spindled cells and occasional multinucleated tumour giant cells with some notable features: namely, the presence of extremely large tumour cells often showing clear or vacuolated cytoplasm, extracellular and occasionally intracellular eosinophilic hyaline droplets, brisk mitotic activity (including atypical forms), and often large zones of tumour necrosis {36006773}. Almost half of cases contain at least focal areas similar to intermediate- to high-grade myxofibrosarcoma–like zones associated with pleomorphic lipoblasts. This myxofibrosarcoma-like component is predominant in some cases. About one quarter of cases are epithelioid, displaying a sheet-like to trabecular proliferation of variably pleomorphic, clear to lightly eosinophilic epithelioid cells with centrally placed nuclei, an appearance reminiscent of poorly differentiated carcinoma, renal clear cell carcinoma, adrenocortical carcinoma, or melanoma {11979090; 15371941; 10430277; 24384587; 12396240; 36006773}. Heterologous osteosarcomatous or chondrosarcomatous differentiation is rarely present {36006773}.

Unlike in dedifferentiated liposarcoma with homologous lipoblastic differentiation, staining for MDM2 and CDK4 is typically negative in pleomorphic liposarcoma {20834238; 11979090; 15371941; 20588177}. The epithelioid subtype may be positive for keratins and melan-A {30852046}.

Cytology

Diagnosis by FNAB is challenging with a broad differential diagnosis {15690614}. Smears may show high cellularity with loosely cohesive tissue fragments consisting of spindle cells, epithelioid cells, and a variable number of lipoblasts, along with inflammatory cells, mitoses, and necrosis. Nuclear atypia is marked. Correlation with site, imaging, and ancillary tests on cell blocks is required {24652822}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Absence of amplification of MDM2 and CDK4 alterations can help distinguish pleomorphic liposarcoma from dedifferentiated liposarcoma (with homologous lipoblastic differentiation).

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a sarcoma with pleomorphic, spindle, or epithelioid cells; variable number of pleomorphic lipoblasts.

Desirable: absence of MDM2 amplification in selected cases.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Pleomorphic liposarcomas are aggressive sarcomas exhibiting local recurrence and metastatic rates of 30–50%, with an overall 5-year survival rate of about 60%. Metastases occur mostly in the lungs and pleura. Central location, increased tumour depth, tumour size (≥ 5 cm), tumour necrosis (≥ 1%), higher mitotic count, tumour grade (French Fédération Nationale des Centres de Lutte Contre le Cancer [FNCLCC] grade 2 vs grade 3), and epithelioid morphology have been associated with a worse prognosis {11979090; 15371941; 36006773; 39328165}.
