1.3.27 Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans

Definition

Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) is a superficial, intermediate, locally aggressive spindle cell neoplasm with a diffusely infiltrative, storiform growth pattern. Fibrosarcomatous DFSP is characterized by a fascicular growth pattern, increased mitotic activity, and metastatic potential.

ICD-O coding

8832/1 Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH4QZ8 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Dermatofibrosarcoma NOS

2B53.Y & XH5CT4 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Pigmented dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans

Related terminology

Not recommended: Bednar tumour.

Subtype(s)

Fibrosarcomatous dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans

Localization

These tumours show a predilection for the trunk and the proximal extremities, followed by the head and neck region. Involvement of the genital area, the breast, and acral sites is less common {1869103; 30014607; 29240584}.

Clinical features

DFSP presents as slowly enlarging multinodular plaques or nodular tumours. Rapid tumour growth may be associated with progression to fibrosarcomatous DFSP.

Epidemiology

Although rare (< 1 case per 100 000 person-years), DFSP is the most common cutaneous sarcoma {39904126}. The age distribution is wide and includes paediatric and congenital onset as well as presentation in the elderly. Most tumours present in young to middle-aged adulthood (30–50 years), with a slight male predominance {21429521; 12502925; 41042442}.

Etiology

Most of these tumours occur sporadically. DFSP with unique features, such as multicentricity, small size, and occurrence at early age, has been reported in children affected with adenosine deaminase–deficient severe combined immunodeficiency {22153773}.

Pathogenesis

DFSP is characterized by the presence of supernumerary ring chromosomes {12661001; 12550751} that contain the centromere of chromosome 22 and comprise interspersed sequences from chromosomes 17 and 22 {7757993}. Additional aberrations, such as trisomy 5 and trisomy 8, are also observed {12661001; 12550751}. Unbalanced t(17;22)(q21.3;q13.1) translocations are present most often in children and rarely in adults {12661001; 12550751}. Most DFSP cells harbour not only a structural rearrangement but also a gain of 17q21.3-17qter and 22q10-q31 sequences {14633610; 17124411}. Both ring and der(22)t(17;22) chromosomes contain a chimeric gene fusing COL1A1 at 17q21.33 with PDGFB at 22q13.1 {8988177}. The breakpoint in COL1A1 is variable: the chimeric gene is composed of at least the first 6 exons up to exon 49 of COL1A1 and a consistent fragment retaining all but exon 1 of the PDGFB gene. Fewer than 5% of typical DFSP cases are negative for the COL1A1::PDGFB fusion gene by routine molecular testing. Alternative fusions COL1A2::PDGFB, COL6A3::PDGFD, TNC::PDGFD, EMILIN1::PDGFD, and EMILIN2::PDGFD have been identified {26332510; 29955147; 30014607; 35034038; 39956270; 40167642; 40348059}. In some cases, the COL1A1::PDGFB rearrangement is cryptic {29955147}. The COL1A1::PDGFB fusion gene encodes a fusion protein that is proteolytically processed to normal PDGFB ligand. Because tumour cells express the PDGFRB receptor on their cell surfaces, autocrine stimulation of neoplastic cells drives tumorigenesis. This molecular pathway provides a rationale for targeted therapy with tyrosine kinase inhibitors for unresectable DFSP or metastatic fibrosarcomatous DFSP {9739023; 19635106; 17950782; 20194851; 24173542; 26221412; 26189684}. Alteration of the PDGFRB/AKT/mTOR pathway is seen in fibrosarcomatous DFSP {28711648}. Tumours with PDGFD gene fusions appear to be more common in women; they present in a slightly younger age group and more commonly affect the breast {40348059}.

Macroscopic appearance

DFSP lesions are indurated plaques containing one or multiple nodules. Multiple protuberant tumours are often seen in recurrent lesions. These poorly defined and infiltrative neoplasms have firm, greyish-white cut surfaces with occasional gelatinous areas, whereas areas of tumour necrosis are only rarely observed.

Histopathology

Classic DFSP

DFSP is a dermal-based tumour typically showing additional diffuse infiltration of subcutaneous adipose tissue, giving rise to the characteristic honeycomb appearance. The tumour is composed of cytologically uniform spindled cells containing plump or elongated wavy nuclei, arranged in storiform, whorled, or cartwheel growth patterns. Cytological atypia is minimal and mitotic activity is low. The collagenous stroma contains small blood vessels. The superficial portion of the neoplasm may be less cellular, causing considerable challenges in the differential diagnosis on small biopsies. The epidermis is uninvolved, and tumour cells encase skin appendages without destroying them. Rarely, cases of DFSP present as a subcutaneous mass with infiltration of deep soft tissues {28420485; 18645303}. Rare cases may show prominent vessels, granular cell change, prominent nuclear palisading, and Verocay body formation {22257899; 26725533}.

Some cases of DFSP contain a variable number of pigmented, dendritic melanocytic cells and are known as pigmented DFSP (Bednar tumour) {3901787}. Rarely, DFSP may contain a prominent myxoid stroma called myxoid DFSP. These tumours show a more nodular growth and contain numerous vessels with slightly fibrotic vessel walls, often resulting in a more variable architecture, which may mimic other myxoid mesenchymal neoplasms {6614310; 17721193}. In addition to a myointimal, non-neoplastic proliferation in entrapped vessels, bundles and nests of spindled myofibroblastic tumour cells are rarely observed in DFSP with myoid differentiation {8720984}. This pattern is more often seen in the fibrosarcomatous subtype {9591728}. In rare cases, DFSP may show a flat, plaque-like growth resembling benign plaque-like CD34-positive dermal fibroma (plaque-like DFSP) {20061935}.

Fibrosarcomatous DFSP

Fibrosarcomatous DFSP represents morphological progression to a more cellular fascicular pattern, with acquisition of metastatic potential. Fibrosarcomatous change occurs de novo or less commonly in local recurrences. The transition to fibrosarcomatous DFSP may be abrupt or gradual. The fibrosarcomatous component often shows a nodular, rather well-circumscribed growth and is composed of intersecting cellular spindle cell fascicles with a herringbone appearance. The neoplastic cells in fibrosarcomatous areas are characterized by increased atypia and proliferative activity {9591728; 10935653; 16625088}. Very rarely, transformation to pleomorphic sarcomatous areas has been reported {9591728; 21478726}.

Immunohistochemistry

By immunohistochemistry, tumour cells stain positively for CD34 and may show expression of EMA {12426982; 15252314}. Importantly, fibrosarcomatous DFSP can show loss of CD34 expression in about half of the cases {9591728}. Tumour cells in the myoid nodules and bundles stain strongly for SMA.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show cohesive tissue fragments of spindle cells with poorly defined or bipolar cytoplasm in a storiform pattern within a collagenous matrix. Nuclear atypia is mild to moderate with uniform chromatin and few nucleoli. Occasionally, tissue fragments of fat infiltrated by spindle cells may be seen. Correlation with site and an immunohistochemical panel including CD34 and FISH and next-generation sequencing on cell block material can make the diagnosis {15048962;15830369}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

For histologically challenging cases and for tumours composed entirely of fibrosarcomatous DFSP without a conventional component, detection of COL1A1::PDGFB can be used to confirm the diagnosis {25081750; 26970672}. However, the COL1A1::PDGFB fusion may be cryptic in about 2% of cases, and another 2% of cases harbour alternative fusions involving PDGFD {29955147; 30014607}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: storiform architecture and uniform spindle cell morphology; diffusely infiltrative growth with a honeycomb pattern in the subcutis; for fibrosarcomatous DFSP: fascicular architecture with increased mitotic activity.

Desirable: expression of CD34; COL1A1::PDGFB gene fusion or rarely alternative PDGFD rearrangements in selected cases.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM system can be applied for fibrosarcomatous DFSP.

Prognosis and prediction

DFSP is characterized by locally aggressive growth and frequent, often repeated local recurrences unless widely excised. The rate of local recurrences varies from 20% to 50% in the setting of inadequate margins {10935653; 15503305; 28056985}. Ordinary DFSP does not metastasize. Higher-grade fibrosarcomatous progression is seen in 5% of cases. Fibrosarcomatous DFSP exhibits more-aggressive behaviour than ordinary DFSP, and 10–15% of patients develop distant metastases, most often to the lungs {10870053; 21570152; 21645124; 25582537; 24755121}. Histological grading has not been shown to be prognostic in fibrosarcomatous DFSP.
