1.3.17 Angiomyofibroblastoma

Definition

Angiomyofibroblastoma is a benign superficial myofibroblastic neoplasm with a predilection for the vulva and vagina.

ICD-O coding

8826/0 Angiomyofibroblastoma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH8A47 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Angiomyofibroblastoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Virtually all cases arise in pelviperineal subcutaneous tissue, with the majority arising in the vulva. About 10–15% of cases occur in the vagina. Lesions in men occur in the scrotum or paratesticular soft tissue.

Clinical features

Most cases present as a slowly enlarging, painless, circumscribed mass. The most frequent preoperative diagnosis is Bartholin gland cyst.

Epidemiology

Angiomyofibroblastoma is uncommon, having an incidence comparable to that of deep (aggressive) angiomyxoma {36262943}. These tumours arise predominantly in female individuals, principally in adults between menarche and menopause {1314521; 9308729; 8685229; 24880711}. About 10% of patients are postmenopausal {16681729}. Convincing examples have not been described before puberty. Rare cases occur in male individuals {1314521; 8980365}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The majority of angiomyofibroblastomas harbour recurrent MTG1::CYP2E1 fusions, probably leading to aberrant expression of presumably oncogenic CYP2E1, an isoenzyme belonging to the cytochrome P450 superfamily involved in the metabolism of drugs or environmental toxicants {34385605; 36177509}. Angiomyofibroblastoma lacks HMGA2 rearrangement present in a subset of deep angiomyxomas or loss of chromosome 13q14 detectable in cellular angiofibroma and myofibroblastoma {24880711; 17654722}.

Macroscopic appearance

These lesions are well circumscribed but not encapsulated, with a tan/pink cut surface and a soft consistency. Necrosis is not seen. Most lesions measure < 50 mm in maximum diameter, although rare examples as large as 100 mm have been recognized. Some cases may be pedunculated {22371044}.

Histopathology

Angiomyofibroblastoma is generally well demarcated by a thin fibrous pseudocapsule from surrounding soft tissue. At low magnification, tumours show alternating hypocellular and hypercellular areas and prominent small to medium-sized thin-walled vessels; thick-walled, often hyalinized vessels, can be seen {1314521; 7551008; 8685229; 9308729}. The tumour cells, which are set in a variably myxoedematous to fibrous stroma and typically concentrated around the vasculature, are round to spindle-shaped, with eosinophilic cytoplasm. Binucleated and multinucleated tumour cells may occur. Some tumours show plasmacytoid or epithelioid cytomorphology, and rare examples show degenerative (ancient) nuclear hyperchromasia and atypia. Mitoses are rare or absent. About 10% of cases have a variably prominent well-differentiated adipocytic component, sometimes > 90% of the tumour {25675192; 15782077; 24880711}. In postmenopausal patients, the stroma is often less oedematous and more fibrous, and there may be hyalinization of vessel walls. Rare cases show morphological overlap with deep angiomyxoma {9023551}. A case of angiomyofibroblastoma merging imperceptibly with a high-grade sarcoma, suggesting sarcomatous transformation, has been reported {9298888}.

By immunohistochemistry, the majority of cases show strong and diffuse positive staining for desmin, whereas SMA or pan-muscle actin are variably expressed {1314521; 24880711}. Desmin staining may be reduced or absent in tumours from postmenopausal patients. Tumour cells are consistently positive for ER and PR and occasionally positive for CD34.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland round to spindle cells in a perivascular distribution; prominent small to medium vessels.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Angiomyofibroblastoma is benign. Sarcomatous transformation may rarely occur and be associated with recurrence {9298888}.
