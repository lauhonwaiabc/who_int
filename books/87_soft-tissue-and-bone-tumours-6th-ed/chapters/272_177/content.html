2.7.2 Adamantinoma of long bones

Definition

Adamantinoma is a biphasic malignant tumour characterized by a variety of morphological patterns, with an obvious epithelial component and a bland osteofibrous component.

ICD-O coding

XXX

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Classic adamantinoma; dedifferentiated adamantinoma

Localization

Adamantinoma occurs almost exclusively in the tibia (~85% of cases). Non-contiguous tumour can be seen in the ipsilateral fibula (10–15%) {2743266}, which can also be destroyed by a tibial extraosseous mass {7325273}. Adamantinoma is rare outside these locations but has been described in other long bones {15045543}, metatarsals {16783903}, the calcaneum {20307451}, ribs {7949646}, vertebrae {16883154}, and the capitate {13816680}; there have been exceptional cases in pre-tibial soft tissues without bone involvement {2743266; 3966430; 3201280}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Patients present with pain of insidious onset, and often the history is long (mean: 4 years {30332521}, as many as 50 years {2743266}). Tibial swelling, a mass, and a pathological fracture are frequent {12679847}; there is occasional anterior bowing. Prior trauma has been reported in as many as 60% of patients {3514033}. There have been rare cases of paraneoplastic hypercalcaemia {1647939} and pancreatitis {7161313}.

Imaging

Imaging shows lytic destruction of the tibia, usually located in the anterior diaphysis. The lesion involves and typically originates in the cortex {15547221}, sometimes remaining intracortical but with frequent extension to the medulla. There may be multiple small lucent foci with intervening sclerosis {2743266}, larger lobules of tumour, or a single expansile mass {15547221}, often showing sclerosis at its superior and inferior margins {2743266}. Multifocal lucencies, sclerosis, and expansion result in a soap-bubble appearance {4426036}. Periosteal new bone of variable thickness forms over expansile cortical lesions and may result in anterior tibial bowing {12679847}. Cortical breach (32%) {15547221}, an extraosseous mass (9–15%) {15547221; 12679847}, and pathological fracture (10–24%) {2743266; 10954102} may be seen. Fibular lesions show similar features. MRI can suggest the diagnosis on the basis of morphology and accurately assess the extent of the tumour {15547221}. Imaging features favouring classic adamantinoma rather than osteofibrous dysplasia (OFD) include an aggressive radiographic appearance with more extensive cortical destruction, which may be associated with a mass {2743266}, and a larger tumour (greater longitudinal and transverse medullary extent) {18690429}. There are no specific imaging features that suggest dedifferentiated adamantinoma {30043662}.

Epidemiology

Adamantinoma accounts for about 0.4% of all primary bone tumours. The mean age at presentation is 32 years (range: 1–81 years) {33027124; 7929496; 7698872; 1599019}. It is slightly more common in male individuals. Rarely, at middle and advanced age, further progression into dedifferentiated adamantinoma may occur {14657712; 20717000}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Rare cases of classic adamantinoma seem to have progressed from atypical OFD (previously OFD-like adamantinoma) {33027124}. Dedifferentiated adamantinoma arises in the setting of pre-existing classic adamantinoma {14657712}.

Classic adamantinoma has a low tumour mutation burden and is characterized by copy-number alterations, specifically affecting chromosomes 7, 8, 12, and 19 {18300815; 11227067; 31021853}. In addition, chromosomal gains were found in chromosomes 1q, 2, and 10, and losses in chromosomes 1p, 3, 5, 6, 15, and 17 {31021853}. Extra copies of one or more of these chromosomes (except for chromosome 19) have also been identified in some cases of OFD, supporting a related pathogenesis {18300815; 8156503}. The progressive complexity of the karyotypic aberrations observed in OFD, atypical OFD, and classic adamantinoma may be indicative of a multistep transformation process {18300815}. The chromatin remodelling–related gene KMT2D was recurrently altered in three of eight classic adamantinomas (38%) in one report, suggesting potential involvement of deregulated chromatin structure and integrity in adamantinoma tumorigenesis {31021853}.

Aneuploidy and TP53 variants are confined to the epithelial component {7495301}.

Macroscopic appearance

Classic adamantinoma usually presents as a cortical, well-demarcated, lobulated, whitish-grey gritty tumour with peripheral sclerosis. It may be a single lesion or occasionally multifocal. Small lesions remain intracortical. Larger tumours may show intramedullary extension and cortical breakthrough, with soft tissue invasion in a minority of cases. Macroscopically detectable cystic spaces are common, filled with straw-coloured or blood-like fluid. The tumour varies in size from 5 to 260 mm (median: 70 mm) {33027124}.

Histopathology

In classic adamantinoma, the epithelial component forms cohesive islands, which are generally clearly visible on H&E. The epithelial component may be tubular, squamous, basaloid, or spindle-shaped. The basaloid cells usually have peripheral palisading and central stellate or spindle-shaped cells {2743266}. Areas of (atypical) OFD can be present, especially at the periphery of the lesion, which may cause sampling error. By far the rarest subtype is dedifferentiated adamantinoma. In these tumours, areas of classic adamantinoma gradually merge with a diffuse growing proliferation in which the characteristic epithelial differentiation is lost; instead, pleomorphic cells are present, with a high mitotic count. Chondroid differentiation, osteoid deposition, and clear cell changes can be seen in dedifferentiated adamantinoma {14657712; 20717000}.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemically, the epithelial cells show coexpression of keratin, EMA, vimentin, p63 (diffuse), and podoplanin {21674157; 21499851}. Chain-specific keratin expression reveals a predominantly basal epithelial cell differentiation, regardless of subtype, with widespread presence of basal epithelial cell keratins CK5, CK14, and CK19 {7694513}. CK1, CK13, and CK17 are also variably present. CK8 and CK18 are virtually absent. In dedifferentiated adamantinoma, the expression of keratin can be retained {14657712; 20717000}.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnoses include atypical OFD, in which small clusters of epithelial cells can be identified using immunohistochemistry (but are generally not evident on H&E). Additionally, classic adamantinoma showing a predominantly spindle cell epithelial component must be distinguished from intraosseous synovial sarcoma, because treatment is significantly different {30206413; 35946120}. Rarely, adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma can enter the differential diagnosis, although this tumour usually occurs in the head and neck and shows FET-ETS family fusions that are absent in classic adamantinoma {11592101}. Dedifferentiated adamantinoma without a history of classic adamantinoma should be distinguished from metastatic carcinoma, in which case immunohistochemistry for p63 and podoplanin can be helpful.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a bone tumour with compatible imaging; almost exclusively of the tibia and/or fibula; always involves cortex; classic adamantinoma: biphasic appearance at H&E, with obvious epithelial elements embedded in fibro-osseous stroma; dedifferentiated adamantinoma: classic adamantinoma in which the epithelial component has progressed to high-grade sarcoma.

Staging

Staging is according to bone sarcoma protocols (see <>TNM staging of tumours of bone). See also the information on staging in section Bone tumours: Introduction.

Prognosis and prediction

Risk factors for recurrence are intralesional or marginal surgery and extracompartmental growth. In classic adamantinoma, the recurrence rate is around 24% {33027124}. Recurrence is associated with an increase in the ratio of epithelium to stroma and with more-aggressive behaviour. Additionally, male sex, female sex combined with young age, pain at presentation, short duration of symptoms, young age (< 20 years), contaminated resection margins, pathological fracture, and lack of squamous differentiation of the tumour have been associated with increased rates of recurrence or metastasis {2743266; 7929496; 7567680; 10954102; 30332521; 33027124}. Classic adamantinomas metastasize in 12–29% of patients, with comparable mortality rates {7929496; 2743266; 33027124}. The tumour spreads to the lungs, and infrequently to the lymph nodes, skeleton, liver, and brain. Dedifferentiated adamantinoma has an aggressive clinical course {14657712; 20717000}.
