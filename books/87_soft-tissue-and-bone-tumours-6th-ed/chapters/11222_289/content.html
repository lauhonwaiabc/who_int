1.12.22 FET-CREB family epithelioid mesenchymal tumours

Definition

FET-CREB family malignant epithelioid mesenchymal tumours typically involve mesothelial-lined cavities, show epithelioid morphology and epithelial marker expression, and are characterized by fusions between EWSR1 or FUS genes with members of the CREB family of transcription factors (ATF1, CREB1, CREM).

ICD-11 coding

Related terminology

Acceptable: FET-CREB epithelioid mesenchymal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The majority of cases have involved the abdominal cavity, but confirmed cases have also presented in the pleural cavity, soft tissues, kidney, head and neck, and ovary {32770123; 34049318; 35347249; 39031200; 31305268; 39249507; 39888444; 34228212; 37097347; 31305268; 38460673; 39648206; 39793930; 39102073; 39793930; 36697296; 39102073; 40242428; 39984759; 34605068}. A neoplasm described as inflammatory and nested testicular sex cord tumour, which harbours EWSR1::ATF1 gene fusions, may represent the testicular presentation of this neoplasm {36791251}, although the latter cases label more consistently for SF1.

Clinical features

Patients typically present with a painless mass intra-abdominally or at other sites. Approximately 20% of patients have presented with fever, anaemia, or other systemic inflammatory/constitutional symptoms, leading to the discovery of a tumour on imaging investigations for an infectious focus.

Epidemiology

These neoplasms are very rare. Reported cases have affected male and female individuals equally, and span a wide age range {40242428}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The neoplasms are defined by gene fusions involving a member of the FET family of RNA binding proteins (EWSR1 or FUS) and a member of the CREB transcription factor family (CREM, ATF1, or CREB1). The majority of cases, 40 out of the 54 genetically confirmed cases, have demonstrated an EWSR1::CREM fusion (26 EWSR1, 14 FUS), with the remaining harbouring either EWSR1::ATF1 fusions (11/54) or EWSR1::CREB1 fusions (3/54). Abdominal cases retain BAP1 immunoreactivity and lack CDKN2A homozygous deletion {34049318}. Gene expression profiling showed two of these neoplasms clustered separately from mesothelioma with EWSR1::ATF1, clear cell sarcoma, malignant gastrointestinal neuroectodermal tumour, and angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma {32770123}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours are typically multinodular. On cut section they are often grey, with variable cystic changes.

Histopathology

The neoplasms are composed of solid sheets, cords, or nests of monomorphic, medium-sized, epithelioid and round cells with eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm. The uniform nuclei and often pink cytoplasm may suggest epithelioid mesothelioma. Some cases have small round cell or focally spindle cell morphology. Hyaline sclerosis may separate the neoplastic cells into organoid nests or cords. The abdominal tumours are often well-circumscribed and encapsulated by fibrous tissue {32770123; 34049318}, while in other sites their border is more irregular and infiltrative. The neoplasms frequently have a prominent lymphoplasmacytic cuff similar to that of AFH, but intratumoural inflammation can also be seen. Cystic change is common {32770123; 34049318}, but tumours typically lack haemorrhage. Focal necrosis is often seen, but mitotic activity is usually low (< 4 mitoses/2 mm2). The majority of neoplasms label for epithelial markers such as broad-spectrum cytokeratin and EMA. Peritoneal cases frequently label for WT1, which, along with the occasional HEG1 expression, may suggest a possible relationship with mesothelioma {32770123; 34049318}. However, these neoplasms lack the classic clinical course or histological architecture of mesothelioma, and calretinin is almost always negative. BAP1 is also consistently retained. Aberrant staining for markers such as ALK, CD99, CD34, synaptophysin, inhibin, and MUC4 is variably present, while chromogranin A is reported only rarely {34228212; 34605068}. Importantly, S100, SOX10, HMB45, and melan-A are consistently negative (unlike in MGNET and CCS), and desmin is usually negative (unlike in AFH). Cases that label for neuroendocrine markers with or without cytokeratin staining mimic neuroendocrine neoplasms {34605068}. Cases that are MUC4 positive can be mistaken for sclerosing epithelioid fibrosarcoma {32964769}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of FET-CREB family fusions is critical for diagnosis. However, these fusions are not specific to this entity, and close clinicopathological correlation is critical.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: monomorphic epithelioid to round cell neoplasm harbouring a FET-CREB family gene fusion; keratin expression; exclusion of other established entities known to harbour FET-CREB family gene fusions.

Staging

The prognostic value of staging and the proper system to use is not clear, but in general, tumours should be separated into localized, locally advanced, or metastatic categories for further study.

Prognosis and prediction

Approximately three quarters of neoplasms have recurred or progressed, and one third of patients have either died of disease or developed systemic metastasis.
