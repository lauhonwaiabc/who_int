2.7.8 Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma of bone

Definition

Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS) of bone is a malignant pleomorphic neoplasm arising in bone with no identifiable line of differentiation and no distinctive molecular aberration; UPS of bone is a diagnosis of exclusion.

ICD-O coding

8802/3 Pleomorphic sarcoma, undifferentiated

ICD-11 coding

2B54 & XH0947 Unclassified pleomorphic sarcoma, primary site & Malignant fibrous histiocytoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant fibrous histiocytoma of bone (obsolete); pleomorphic fibrosarcoma of bone.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

UPS of bone has a predilection for long tubular bones (66%), particularly around the knee {10569265; 9028358; 32492019}. The most frequently involved bone is the femur, followed by the tibia and humerus. Among the bones of the trunk, the pelvis is most commonly affected. UPS rarely occurs in the spine.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Symptomatic patients typically report pain and occasionally swelling. Pathological fractures are common, particularly in weight-bearing long tubular bones.

Imaging

The tumours are usually centred in the metadiaphysial region of long bones and occasionally extend into the epiphysis. The radiological features are nonspecific, but the majority of cases demonstrate an aggressive osteolytic neoplasm with poorly defined margins, cortical destruction, and an associated soft tissue mass {19936744}. Periosteal reaction is an infrequent finding. Areas suggestive of cartilage and/or mineralized matrix are absent.

Epidemiology

UPS of bone is rare, representing < 2% of all primary malignant bone tumours. Male individuals are slightly more frequently affected than female individuals. There is a broad age distribution, from the second to eighth decades of life, but most patients are aged > 40 years. Only 10–15% of cases occur in patients aged < 20 years.

Etiology

The etiology of primary UPS of bone is unknown. Secondary UPS of bone, representing approximately 28% of cases, arises in association with a pre-existing bone condition or disease {9028358; 10642003; 3000204}. The majority of secondary UPSs are associated with a bone infarct, Paget disease, or prior irradiation {3006524; 19229663; 26397350}. Rare examples occur at the site of a metallic orthopaedic prosthesis or hardware {11598166; 11903581}. Approximately 35% of patients with diaphysial medullary stenosis, an extremely rare autosomal dominant bone dysplasia caused by mutations in the gene encoding MTAP, develop UPS of bone (MIM number: 112250) {10053015; 11807991; 3745248; 22464254}. This disorder is characterized by cortical growth abnormalities, including diffuse diaphysial medullary stenosis with overlying endosteal cortical thickening, metaphysial striations, and scattered infarctions {12072764}.

Pathogenesis

UPS of bone is highly aneuploid and shows complex chromosomal alterations with numerous structural aberrations and marker chromosomes, with frequent losses of multiple chromosomal segments {10748288; 21254299}. Homozygous deletions of CDKN2A, RB1, TP53, and ING1 are present in a subset of UPS {21254299}. Mutations in TP53 (~30%) and/or chromatin-remodelling genes (~40%) are most frequent {30281149}. In general, the copy-number alterations in UPS of bone overlap with those identified in UPS of soft tissue {30281149; 29100075}. Although UPS of bone may exhibit multiple somatic gene fusions associated with the characteristically extensive rearrangement of the genome, none of them were shown to be specific and recurrent {30281149}. RNA sequencing indicated that UPS has an expression profile distinct from that of its soft tissue counterpart and is characterized by overexpression of FGF23 {30281149; 38397231}.

Macroscopic appearance

The gross appearance of UPS is quite variable. The cut surface texture ranges from fibrous to soft. Areas of necrosis and haemorrhage are frequently present. Cortical destruction and a soft tissue mass are commonly present. Thorough sampling is required to rule out the presence of tumoural bone and cartilage in case of osteosarcoma and dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma.

Histopathology

UPS is morphologically intermediate- to high-grade and composed of spindle-shaped, epithelioid, and/or polygonal cells with marked pleomorphism arranged in a patternless haphazard, storiform, or fascicular growth pattern. The amount of cytoplasm is variable and usually pale to eosinophilic; intracytoplasmic or intranuclear inclusions can be seen. Variable numbers of large, bizarre multinucleated giant cells and numerous typical and atypical mitotic figures are readily identified. The tumour cells are often embedded within a collagen-rich stroma, which may be hyalinized. A background population of scattered osteoclast-like giant cells, foamy histiocytes, and inflammatory cells can be seen among the neoplastic cells. Importantly, the tumour lacks any evidence of neoplastic osteoid or cartilage. When considering the diagnosis on core needle biopsy, correlation with imaging is essential: areas suggestive of a conventional cartilaginous tumour or mineralization should be absent. Trabeculae of reactive woven bone are occasionally present, particularly at the periphery or in the setting of a pathological fracture {8625223}. When extensive myxoid change is seen, similar to that in high-grade myxofibrosarcoma of soft tissue, some pathologists favour a diagnosis of high-grade myxofibrosarcoma of bone {23001328}. In case of predominant spindle cell morphology, a fascicular architecture with variably amphophilic or palely eosinophilic cytoplasm can be seen, with tapering nuclei and at least some mild degree of nuclear variability. However, low-grade, uniform, and monomorphic spindle cell sarcomas should not be classified as UPS, as they increasingly seem to be resolving into other entities.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry should be used to confirm lack of smooth muscle, rhabdomyoblastic, melanocytic, endothelial, and epithelial differentiation. Caution is required when interpreting myogenic markers, because approximately 50% of UPSs of bone show focal positivity with a single myogenic marker {12401825; 23001328}. SMA positivity with an additional myogenic marker (desmin or h-caldesmon) supports leiomyosarcoma in an appropriate histological context. Focal cytokeratin positivity can be seen in UPS, but abundant expression should raise concern for metastatic sarcomatoid carcinoma. Several melanocytic markers are indicated in the setting of focal S100 expression in order to exclude melanoma. SATB2 immunoreactivity can be seen in as many as half of the UPS cases, a limiting factor when exploring the possibility of osteosarcoma {26644288; 35311957}. Histone H3 G34W expression is occasionally seen; these tumours most likely represent malignant giant cell tumour of bone on the basis of similar molecular and methylation profiles {28545165; 28899740; 28505000; 29241742; 40157506}.

Differential diagnosis

A variety of high-grade sarcomas (dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma, malignant giant cell tumour of bone), metastatic carcinoma, and metastatic melanoma should be excluded.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The absence of IDH1 or IDH2 variants in UPS of bone can be used in the differential diagnosis with dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma {28552826}. Similarly, the presence of an H3-3A G35 (G34) variant would suggest that the tumour represents malignant giant cell tumour of bone, on the basis of similar molecular and methylation profiles {40157506; 28545165; 28899740; 28505000}. It should be noted that in two small series reported, alternative G35 (G34) variants (that are non-G35W (G34W)) were enriched, which may limit the sensitivity of H3-3A G34W antibody and require additional molecular testing to identify these tumours {40157506; 30281149}. The presence of melanoma-compatible mutations in BRAF, NRAS, KIT, or NF1, an ultraviolet (UV) radiation–induced mutation signature, and/or a high tumour mutation burden can help recognize metastatic undifferentiated melanoma, especially in the absence of melanocytic differentiation or a previous history of melanoma {33428337; 36990277}. Broad molecular testing can also be considered to further explore the option of metastasis of sarcomatoid carcinoma, particularly when a primary tumour is not evident using sensitive imaging methods such as PET/CT.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; spindle, pleomorphic, epithelioid cells; intermediate- to high-grade morphology; absence of any morphological or immunohistochemical feature of specific differentiation; demonstrated absence of distinctive molecular aberration (IDH1/IDH2, H3-3A).

Desirable: atypical mitotic figures; necrosis.

Staging

Staging is according to bone sarcoma protocols (see <>TNM staging of tumours of bone).

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with UPS of bone are generally treated with chemotherapy and wide resection {8364866; 9577426; 20559764}. Patients with localized disease and adequate therapy have a 5-year survival rate of 50–67% {10506628; 10642003; 10569265; 35184191}. Older age, distant metastases at presentation, secondary UPS, and location in the flat bone are associated with worse prognosis {32492019; 35184191}. UPS with diffuse expression of SATB2 seem to show superior survival {35311957}. Pulmonary metastases are common and occur in approximately 35–50% of cases. Lymph node metastases are extremely rare (< 2%). Incomplete expression of myogenic markers is not thought to affect the prognosis {23001328}.
