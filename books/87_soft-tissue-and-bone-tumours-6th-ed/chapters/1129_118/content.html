1.12.9 Myoepithelioma, myoepithelial carcinoma, and mixed tumour

Definition

Soft tissue myoepithelial tumours are a heterogeneous group of neoplasms defined by myoepithelial differentiation, including myoepithelioma, myoepithelial carcinoma, and mixed tumour.

ICD-O coding

8982/0 Myoepithelioma NOS

8940/0 Mixed tumour NOS

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH3CQ8 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Myoepithelioma

Related terminology

Acceptable: malignant myoepithelial tumour.

Not recommended: parachordoma.

Subtype(s)

Myoepithelioma, mixed tumour, myoepithelial carcinoma.

Localization

Most myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue (75%) arise on the limbs and limb girdles (lower more frequently than upper); others arise on the trunk and less often the head and neck {12960802; 18043035; 20815032; 31994243}. About 15% of cases occur in the bone, mostly in the long bones, and (less commonly) in the pelvis and mandible {20815032; 23681076; 31994243}. Rarely, tumours arise in visceral organs {20815032}. Tumours may also arise primarily in the skin {14745720; 21725291; 23588365}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a palpable mass that is usually painless {8990137; 12960802; 18043035}. Tumours arise in subcutaneous tissue somewhat more often than in deep soft tissue {10389622; 12960802}.

Epidemiology

Myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue show equal distribution between the sexes and a wide age range, with a peak of occurrence in young to middle-aged adults {8990137; 10389622; 12960802; 18043035}. About 30% of reported tumours, most of which are myoepithelial carcinomas, arise in the paediatric population {18043035; 31994243; 38782103}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Half of myoepithelial tumours of soft tissue (as well as those in bone and viscera) harbour EWSR1 gene rearrangements {20815032}, with a smaller subset showing FUS gene rearrangements {31994243; 25706482}. The most common fusion partners (5–15%) include POU5F1, PBX1, PBX3, and KLF17, while rare gene partners include ZNF444, KLF15, and ATF1 {20815032; 25231231; 31994243; 22154050}. Few larger studies have suggested certain associations between genotype and phenotype. Most EWSR1::POU5F1–positive tumours present in deep soft tissues of the extremities in young patients and are composed of nests of epithelioid cells with clear cytoplasm; tumours with EWSR1::PBX1 and/or PBX3 fusions show a deceptively bland and sclerotic appearance, while tumours harbouring EWSR1::KLF17 fusions show a chordoma-like morphology {20815032; 31994243}. Cutaneous syncytial myoepitheliomas show consistent spindle cell morphology and EWSR1::PBX3 {31135487}. A small subset of myoepithelial carcinomas have homozygous deletions of SMARCB1 {24585572}. PLAG1 gene rearrangements (occasionally with LIFR as the fusion partner) occur predominantly in benign cutaneous or superficial lesions with tubuloductal differentiation (i.e. mixed tumours) {22038920; 23630011}. The presence of rearrangements of PLAG1 {15920557} and EWSR1 {25581728; 33027073} in salivary gland pleomorphic adenoma and clear cell myoepithelial carcinoma, respectively, emphasizes that soft tissue myoepithelial neoplasms are somewhat related to their salivary gland counterparts.

Macroscopic appearance

Most benign myoepithelial tumours are grossly well circumscribed and nodular, whereas malignant tumours typically have infiltrative margins. The cut surface ranges from gelatinous and glistening to firm or fleshy. Tumour size range is broad (10–200 mm in greatest dimension), with a mean size of 40–60 mm {18043035; 12960802; 10389622}.

Histopathology

Myoepithelial tumours show a wide morphological spectrum of architectural and cytological features. Most lesions show a mixture of growth patterns, including reticular, nested, trabecular, or solid features. A characteristic finding is also the variability of stromal component, ranging from prominent myxoid to fibromyxoid, fibrotic, and hyalinized backgrounds {12960802; 18043035; 20815032}.

Most cases have either epithelioid or mixed spindle and epithelioid phenotype, although rare cases with predominantly spindle cell morphology may occur. Plasmacytoid cells with hyaline cytoplasmic inclusions may be prominent {7541966; 8990137; 12960802}. Moreover, some lesions (especially in children) may show an undifferentiated round cell morphology indistinguishable from round cell sarcomas {12960802; 18043035; 31994243}. Tumour cells show mostly a uniform cytomorphology, with monomorphic round to ovoid nuclei and eosinophilic to clear cytoplasm. Overt nuclear pleomorphism is highly unusual.

Studies have suggested some associations between genotype and phenotype (see Pathogenesis). Some tumours have large epithelioid cells showing prominent cytoplasmic vacuolation (and were classified as parachordoma in the past {907973; 10478665}), whereas others may show predominantly spindled morphology, including the cutaneous syncytial subtype {23588365}. Mixed tumours show a ductal component and account for approximately 10% of all myoepithelial lesions. Osseous or cartilaginous differentiation is observed in 10–15% of cases. Malignant myoepithelial tumours, i.e. myoepithelial carcinomas, are defined on the basis of nuclear atypia, increased mitotic count, and necrosis. Histological criteria predicting risk of malignancy include marked nuclear atypia with enlarged, irregular, hyperchromatic nuclei and large, prominent nucleoli; increased mitotic activity (> 5 mitoses/10 HPF, equating to > 2.5 mitoses/mm2); and necrosis {12960802; 38782103}.

Immunohistochemistry is necessary to confirm myoepithelial differentiation, although expression of myoepithelial markers is often variable. More than 90% of tumours express broad-spectrum keratins and S100 {18043035; 12960802; 10389622}. Other frequently positive markers are EMA (~70%), GFAP (~50%), and SOX10 (~80%; lower in carcinomas) {18043035; 12960802; 25724000}. p63 is positive in a subset of tumours {12960802; 22031315}. Myogenic markers are more variable, with calponin being most frequent (90%), followed by SMA (60%) {8990137; 12960802}. PLAG1 is positive in mixed tumours, correlating with PLAG1 gene rearrangement {22038920}. Loss of expression of SMARCB1 (INI1) is observed in a small subset of myoepithelial carcinomas {18043035; 19033866; 24585572}.

Cytology

Aspirate smears are similar to their salivary gland counterparts and show variably spindle, epithelioid, and plasmacytoid cells embedded within fibrillary myxoid stroma {26644362; 25693574}. Rhabdoid morphology may be seen. Myoepithelial carcinoma shows appreciable cytological atypia {25693574}, although in many small biopsies and cytology, definitive classification as benign or malignant may not be possible.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Rearrangements of EWSR1, FUS, or PLAG1 (in mixed tumours) can be helpful for diagnosis. EWSR1 FISH alone may not be sufficient for distinguishing myoepithelial neoplasms from other tumours with EWSR1 fusions; this diagnosis may require targeted RNA sequencing.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: trabecular, reticular, nested, and/or solid growth of variably spindled or epithelioid cells with a frequent myxoid or hyalinized stroma; mixed tumours in addition show ductal differentiation; myoepithelial carcinomas show increased cytological atypia and mitotic activity; positivity for EMA/keratin and S100, SOX10, or GFAP.

Desirable (in selected cases): molecular confirmation of EWSR1 or FUS gene rearrangements is recommended especially in deep-seated, malignant soft tissue or bone lesions.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Most soft tissue myoepithelial tumours follow a benign or indolent behaviour. Histologically benign tumours have a 20% risk of recurrence, but they rarely metastasize, whereas myoepithelial carcinomas recur and metastasize in 40–50% of cases {12960802; 18043035; 38782103}. Common sites of metastasis include the lungs, lymph nodes, bone, and soft tissue {12960802; 18043035}.
