1.2.13 Dedifferentiated liposarcoma

Definition

Dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS) is a usually non-lipogenic malignant neoplasm that results from progression of an atypical lipomatous tumour (ALT) / well-differentiated liposarcoma (WDLPS).

ICD-O coding

8858/3 Dedifferentiated liposarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B59 & XH1C03 Liposarcoma, primary site & Dedifferentiated liposarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Retroperitoneum is the most common anatomical location, outnumbering somatic soft tissue by at least 10:1. Other locations include the spermatic cord, abdomen/pelvis (usually as an extension/recurrence of a retroperitoneal tumour {36581724}) and (more rarely) mediastinum {18043041}, head and neck, trunk, and extremities (typically thigh). DDLPS predominantly arises in deep locations; occurrence in subcutaneous tissue is extremely rare {24118009}. Recent molecular data suggest that the incidence of DDLPS at non-retroperitoneal sites may be underestimated {24525499}.

Clinical features

DDLPS usually presents as a large painless mass, which may be found incidentally (in particular in the retroperitoneum). Radiological imaging often shows the coexistence of lipomatous and non-lipomatous solid components.

Epidemiology

According to different case series, DDLPS is a common form of liposarcoma and accounts for most high-grade sarcomas of the retroperitoneum. The risk of dedifferentiation is higher in central sites (retroperitoneum, abdomen/pelvis) and significantly lower in the limbs {17197914; 1471725}. DDLPS affects the same patient population as ALT/WDLPS and is equally frequent in male and female individuals. DDLPS can recur as WDLPS or DDLPS {7977944; 1471725}. Different case series have observed that in the retroperitoneum, DDLPS occurs in a primary tumour in 40–65% of cases {17197914; 18704991; 37057834; 25581729}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

DDLPS falls in a biological continuum with ALT/WDLPS and is genetically characterized by copy-number gains resulting from multiple discrete amplicons scattered throughout the genome. The most consistent copy-number gains are amplification of MDM2 and CDK4 (12q14-q15), shared with ALT/WDLPS {26336885; 19737942; 39266532}. Other oncogenes from the 12q13-q21 region, including GLI1, HMGA2, and STAT6, as well as from other chromosomal regions, are variably coamplified {38621503; 18214854; 25862836; 24457460}. These amplicons are located in neochromosomes – observed cytogenetically as supernumerary ring or giant marker chromosomes with neocentromeres {25517748}. Karyotypes and genomic profiles of DDLPS are often more complex than those of ALT/WDLPS. Some genomic gains are more frequent in DDLPS than in WDLPS/ALT, such as amplification of JUN (1p32.1) {19449367}, TERT (5p15.33), CPM {19820690}, MAP3K5, and other genes from the 6q21-q24 region {29100075; 17418412; 26643872}, but there is no single genomic feature diagnostic of DDLPS. DDLPS shows ATRX deletion in 30% of cases {29100075}. In DDLPS, loss of 11q22-q24 (carrying several genes, e.g. ATM, CHEK1, PPP2R1B) is associated with genomic complexity {22241790}. The mutation burden of DDLPS is low, in contrast with its high burden of copy-number changes {26643872; 30201954}.

Macroscopic appearance

DDLPS usually arises in association with WDLPS and consists of large multinodular masses. DDLPS areas are solid, discrete, tan-grey, and often show necrosis and haemorrhage {9060596}.

Histopathology

DDLPS arises alongside or in the site of a prior ALT/WDLPS. The extent of dedifferentiation is variable. A WDLPS component is not always present or may be hard to identify. In non-lipogenic tumours from anatomical areas with a high incidence of DDLPS, such as the retroperitoneum, careful sampling and morphological examination of adjacent adipose tissue often identifies a WDLPS component. Transition between the WDLPS and DDLPS components is typically abrupt; however, it may be gradual in some cases. The majority of DDLPS are non-lipogenic, although rare cases show overt lipoblastic differentiation (see below).

DDLPS exhibits variable histopathology, which is frequently high-grade but may be deceptively bland {17197914; 37057834}. High-grade areas are morphologically most often undifferentiated spindle cell or pleomorphic sarcomas, but they may resemble specific sarcoma subtypes (myxofibrosarcoma and, more rarely, solitary fibrous tumour and inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour) {20431481; 7977944; 25075469; 1471725}. Frequently, multiple different histological patterns are observed within the same tumour. Bland areas can be mildly or moderately cellular, with ovoid or spindled cells, have minimal to moderate pleomorphism, and are set in a collagenous or myxoid matrix; more cellular spindled areas may be arranged in fascicles {17197914; 37057834}. Floret-type giant cells are often present. Cytologically bland DDLPS may be difficult to distinguish from non-lipogenic areas of WDLPS {17197914; 37057834; 38311185}, particularly in small biopsies or in posttreatment resections. Based on survival differences, tumours with 0–4 mitoses /10 HPF are best classified as cellular WDLPS {17197914; 37057834}, while tumours with ≥ 5 mitoses /10 HPF are best classified as DDLPS {17197914; 25581729; 18704991; 37057834}. In all these studies, mitoses were counted in the most mitotically active areas and typically in at least 4 sets of 10 HPF, and the highest count identified was used to classify the tumour; HPF diameter was not indicated for most of these studies. The term “low-grade DDLPS” {9060596; 9127316} is no longer recommended.

DDLPS exhibits heterologous differentiation in about 5–10% of cases {7943536}, most often myogenic or osteosarcomatous/chondrosarcomatous {29076540; 17895758} and rarely angiosarcomatous. Homologous lipoblastic differentiation, either in the form of isolated lipoblasts or as sheets of atypical pleomorphic adipocytic cells morphologically indistinguishable from pleomorphic liposarcoma, is rare {20431480; 9060596; 20588177}. Homologous lipoblastic differentiation and myxoid liposarcoma-like morphological features in DDLPS are associated with DDIT3 amplification {30420727; 40540926}. A peculiar neural-like or meningothelial-like whorling pattern of dedifferentiation has been described, which is often associated with ossification and underlying GLI1 amplification {9839165; 9706974; 38621503; 21317707}. Striking epithelioid/epithelial features, including keratin expression, are rarely described in DDLPS {36113458; 31995237; 29307627; 28719466; 38608370}.

The main role of immunohistochemistry is to exclude other tumour types and to confirm heterologous differentiation. Diffuse nuclear expression of MDM2 and/or CDK4 is almost invariably observed {9071997} and allows separation of homologous DDLPS from pleomorphic liposarcoma {17895748; 20588177}.

The differential diagnosis is broad, given the histopathological heterogeneity of DDLPS. The presence of multiple different histological patterns within the same tumour, including both bland and high-grade areas, is a strong diagnostic clue that should be confirmed with immunohistochemical or molecular detection of MDM2 and/or CDK4 amplification. In common sites, such as the retroperitoneum, scrotal/inguinal regions, abdomen/pelvis, and thigh, careful sampling and examination of adjacent adipose tissue can help identify a WDLPS component. The recently described atypical spindle cell and pleomorphic lipomatous tumours lack MDM2 amplification (which allows separation from DDLPS), are more predominant in subcutaneous tissue, and typically contain readily identifiable lipoblasts {24659226; 28877053}. STAT6 is amplified in a subset of DDLPS, resulting in STAT6 protein expression that can be detected by immunohistochemistry and may be a potential pitfall in the differential diagnosis of DDLPS and solitary fibrous tumour {24457460; 25075469}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows syncytial tissue fragments with predominantly spindle cells and stellate to atypical pleomorphic cells with microvacuolated cytoplasm; similar single cells can be seen in the background. Fragments resembling ALT/WDLPS can be present. Correlation with imaging, expert radiological interpretation, site, MDM2 immunocytochemistry, and FISH can make the diagnosis in most cases {15690614; 24227706; 32926591}. Focal areas of heterologous differentiation may be present and potentially increase the risk of misdiagnosis due to limited sampling.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of MDM2 amplification helps distinguish DDLPS from other sarcomas in the appropriate clinical context. FISH is the most commonly employed molecular diagnostic modality for DDLPS {17895748}, although multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification {25862836} and array comparative genomic hybridization {21117066} have been used historically. Identification of multiple high-amplitude copy-number gains throughout the genome, including MDM2 and other oncogenes at 12q13-q15 on targeted cancer panels, exome or whole-genome sequencing, supports the diagnosis of DDLPS.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: non-lipogenic malignant mesenchymal neoplasm associated with WDLPS or MDM2-amplified non-lipogenic sarcoma in a characteristic anatomical location.

Desirable: MDM2 amplification or protein overexpression.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

DDLPS has a significantly worse prognosis than WDLPS {17197914; 18704991; 25581729; 37057834}. The most important prognostic factor in DDLPS is anatomical location, with retroperitoneal lesions exhibiting the worst clinical behaviour {17197914}. In the retroperitoneum, median DDLPS survival is 5.5–6.5 years, but patients with French Fédération Nationale des Centres de Lutte Contre le Cancer (FNCLCC) grade 3 DDLPS (or a high mitotic rate of > 20 mitoses/10 HPF) have only a 2–3 year median survival {18704991; 25581729; 37057834}. Distant metastases are observed in 15–30% of DDLPS {9060596; 17197914; 25581729; 37057834}. The frequency of local recurrence depends on anatomical location and duration of follow-up; almost all retroperitoneal DDLPS will recur locally if patients are followed up for 10–20 years {36581724}. The extent of dedifferentiated areas in WDLPS/DDLPS does not seem to predict outcome {9060596; 18704991}. Some patients with DDLPS have a prolonged clinical course, with survivals of 10–20 years {37057834; 17197914; 36581724}. Multivisceral resections appear to increase relapse-free survival {19047283; 20393803}.

Grading according to the FNCLCC criteria provides prognostic information on DDLPS, which is worse for grade 3 than grade 2 DDLPS (by definition, DDLPS cannot be grade 1) {18704991; 25581729}. Myogenic (in particular rhabdomyoblastic) differentiation in DDLPS appears to be associated with worse outcome {25581729}, in contrast with previous observations {17895758}.
