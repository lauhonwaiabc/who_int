1.12.14 Superficial FET-ETS family neurocristic tumour

Definition

Superficial FET-ETS family neurocristic tumour is an indolent superficial neoplasm that shares similar fusion events with Ewing sarcoma.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours involve the skin and subcutaneous tissue of the trunk or extremities {38866368; 39522640; 34132666; 24735198}.

Clinical features

Superficial FET-ETS family neurocristic tumours occur as small isolated, painless lesions and have sometimes been present years before coming to clinical attention.

Epidemiology

Superficial FET-ETS family neurocristic tumour is a rare entity, with cases described to occur in young adults and without a known sex predilection. To date, all reported cases have exclusively involved skin and subcutaneous tissues with a preference for the trunk or extremities {38866368; 39522640; 34132666; 24735198}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Tumours may harbour fusions between any of the FET (FUS, EWSR1) and ETS (FLI1, ERG, ETV1, ETV5, etc.) gene family members. Methylation studies have shown them to cluster distinctly from conventional Ewing sarcoma and adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcomas with a possible closer relationship to benign peripheral nerve sheath tumours {38866368; 39522640}.

Macroscopic appearance

Insufficient data

Histopathology

Superficial FET-ETS family neurocristic tumour typically consists of solid nodules, small nests, single-file arrays, and trabeculae of small, bland, round to ovoid cells with inapparent nucleoli. Thick fibrous bands, entrapped adipocytes, and frequent intratumoural haemorrhage may be seen. A subset of cases may predominantly show features of small round cell tumours closely mimicking conventional Ewing sarcoma. The mitotic count is variable, ranging from 0.5–1 up to 5 mitoses/ mm2 (equivalent to 1–2 –10 mitoses/10 HPF). Tumour cell necrosis or significant cytological atypia is usually not seen. While these tumours occasionally demonstrate strong membranous CD99 expression and other Ewing sarcoma–associated markers such as NKX2.2 and FLI1, they consistently show strong and diffuse expression of both SOX10 and S100. These tumours also frequently express CD56 and synaptophysin but lack expression of other neuroendocrine or melanocytic markers.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of FET (FUS, EWSR1) and ETS (FLI1, ERG, ETV1, ETV5, etc.) gene fusions can be helpful to confirm the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cutaneous or subcutaneous location; nests, cords, and trabeculae of small, bland, round to ovoid cells; lack of significant cytological atypia or tumour necrosis; strong and diffuse expression of SOX10, S100, and CD99.

Desirable: fusions between FET and ETS family members.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

All reported cases to date have behaved in an indolent fashion without evidence of local recurrence or metastasis after surgical removal {38866368; 39522640; 34132666; 24735198}.
