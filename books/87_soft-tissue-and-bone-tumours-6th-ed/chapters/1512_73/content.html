1.5.12 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma

Definition

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma is a malignant vascular neoplasm composed of epithelioid endothelial cells within a distinctive myxohyaline stroma; almost all cases are characterized by the presence of a WWTR1::CAMTA1 gene fusion.

ICD-O coding

9133/3 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2B5Y & XH9GF8 Other specified malignant mesenchymal neoplasms & Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma

Related terminology

Not recommended: malignant epithelioid haemangioendothelioma.

Subtype(s)

None

YAP1::TFE3-fused haemangioendothelioma, previously considered a subtype of epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, is now considered a distinct entity (see section YAP1::TFE3 haemangioendothelioma).

Localization

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma most commonly involves somatic soft tissue, lung, liver, and bone, but any site or organ can be affected. Skin and soft tissue lesions are usually solitary {7093931; 9855348; 9130982}, whereas visceral and bone lesions are often multifocal at presentation {6088383; 10091730; 6282146; 2430475; 8898834}. Within soft tissues, tumours are more often deep rather than superficial {9130982}. The skeleton can be the only organ involved (9–14% of all cases) or a component of multiorgan (liver, lung, soft tissue) disease {21546438; 33729740; 39348982; 37444414; 37625241; 36157226}. Any bone can be affected; 50–60% of cases arise in long tubular bones, especially in lower extremities, followed by pelvis, ribs, and spine; 38–64% are multifocal within a single bone or involve separate bones; however, they tend to cluster in an anatomical region {8898834; 11236956; 21904630; 37625241}.

Clinical features

Presenting symptoms depend on the anatomical site of involvement. In soft tissues and bone, epithelioid haemangioendothelioma usually presents as a painful mass. Those arising from large vessels can result in other symptoms of vascular occlusion, such as oedema and thrombophlebitis.

Radiographically, the skeletal lesions manifest as lytic lesions with well-circumscribed or poorly circumscribed margins. They may be expansile and erode the cortex {38237880; 35707348}. Permeation of the cortex with extension into the soft tissue is uncommon.

Epidemiology

Reported epithelioid haemangioendothelioma cases show a wide age distribution, but are usually described to arise in adults and are rare in children (mean age: 51 years) {18551749; 37625241; 31537895}. The sexes are equally affected.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma is characterized by a WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion in > 95% of cases {21584898; 11342784; 21885404}. The WWTR1 (TAZ) protein is a transcriptional coactivator and effector of the Hippo pathway, by which it is physiologically phosphorylated and inactivated {25961935}. The WWTR1::CAMTA1 gene fusion results in dysregulation of this pathway and nuclear translocation of WWTR1 (TAZ). Here, it acts as a neomorphic coactivator of the TEA domain (TEAD) family of transcription factors involved in cell survival and repair {25961935; 39348982}. A small number of cases have been shown to have variant WWTR1 fusions, including WWTR1::MAML2 and WWTR1::ACTL6A (this group appears to show a predilection for cardiac location) {32170768}. A recent case harbouring a WWTR1::TFE3 fusion exhibited hybrid morphological features between WWTR1::CAMTA1 and YAP1::TFE3 fusion–positive haemangioendotheliomas {38380774}.

The monoclonal origin of multifocal epithelioid haemangioendothelioma has been established using WWTR1::CAMTA1 breakpoint analysis. This indicates that synchronous/multifocal lesions arise from locoregional or distant metastatic spread from a single clone, rather than multiple independent primaries {22429593}. In later stage disease, epithelioid haemangioendotheliomas can develop secondary genetic alterations, including loss of CDKN2A {36598859}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours in visceral or deep soft tissue locations are usually larger than superficial ones, and they may measure > 100 mm. The cut surface is typically white and firm. For soft tissue tumours that arise from a small to medium-sized vein (as many as 50%) {7093931; 9130982}, origin from a vessel can be grossly identifiable, usually as a firm, tan mass circumscribed by the vessel wall.

Histopathology

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma presents as a solid mass that lacks a lobular architecture. Angiocentric tumours expand the vessel wall, obliterate the lumen, and spread centrifugally into surrounding tissue, where they elicit a sclerotic response. Infiltrative growth into adjacent skeletal muscle or fat may be seen. The tumour is characterized by cords and nests of epithelioid cells in a myxohyaline stroma. Tumour cells have moderate amounts of eosinophilic cytoplasm and round nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli. Intracytoplasmic vacuoles are sometimes present and may contain erythrocytes (blister cells or signet-ring cells). Well-formed blood vessels are not present. In most cases, there is only minimal atypia, and mitotic count is very low. The extracellular matrix is variably myxoid and hyaline, which may resemble cartilaginous matrix and obscure the tumour cells in some cases. Cystic degeneration, haemorrhage, sclerosis, and metaplastic ossification may be present {9130982; 8067508}.

A smaller subset of epithelioid haemangioendotheliomas have atypical histological features, including nuclear pleomorphism, increased mitotic activity, solid sheet-like growth, and coagulative necrosis {18551749; 9130982; 31537895; 33729740}. In some cases, these tumours may resemble epithelioid angiosarcoma; the presence of areas of conventional epithelioid haemangioendothelioma or nuclear CAMTA1 expression helps support the diagnosis.

The tumour cells typically express endothelial markers ERG and CD31, while CD34 and D2-40 are less often expressed {24986479; 25353289}. Keratins are expressed in about 40% of cases, but expression of EMA is unusual {9130982; 11014572}. SMA expression is present in approximately 50% of cases {9130982}. Diffuse strong nuclear expression of CAMTA1 is seen in most cases, which is a surrogate for the WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion protein {25879300; 26414223}. Tumours with WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion can show variable TFE3 expression, but usually not strong or diffuse positivity {26414223; 24986479}; in contrast, TFE3-fused haemangioendotheliomas are not CAMTA1-positive {34381186}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows small tissue fragments and predominantly single epithelioid cells with round, mildly pleomorphic nuclei and eccentric, often abundant cytoplasm containing vacuoles, which rarely may contain red cells, and some spindle cells in a bloody background with haemosiderophages. The nuclei can have crinkled raisinoid margins and small nucleoli, and often have grooves and intranuclear inclusions. Mitoses and necrosis are not seen {22031312; 25195738}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of the WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion transcript helps distinguish epithelioid haemangioendothelioma from potential morphological mimics {21885404; 21584898}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cords or nests of epithelioid cells with cytoplasmic vacuolization within a myxochondroid or hyaline stroma; absence of vasoformation, showing immunoreactivity for endothelial markers.

Desirable: CAMTA1 expression by immunohistochemistry; demonstration of WWTR1::CAMTA1 fusion.

Staging

Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma of somatic soft tissue and bone should be staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging systems. For tumours occurring within the abdominal and thoracic cavities, TNM staging is not applicable, but it is recommended that the size of the largest lesion is recorded if possible.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis differs depending on anatomical site. Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma arising in lung or bone has a worse prognosis than do those arising in the soft tissues {30340559; 25353289; 10091730; 21546438; 36157226}. Cutaneous epithelioid haemangioendothelioma has an excellent prognosis {9130982; 9855348}. Involvement of two or more bones is associated with a worse prognosis (74% 10-year overall survival rate), regardless of the number of organs involved {21546438; 24643837}.

Using SEER Program data and a predictive nomogram, a 5-year overall survival rate of 55.6% was estimated in a study of 215 patients with epithelioid haemangioendothelioma {36157226}. NetSarc data from 267 patients with epithelioid haemangioendothelioma, with a median follow-up duration of 20 months, predicted a 10-year overall survival rate of 62% and progression-free survival rate of 21% {37625241}.

As many as 40% of patients have metastatic disease at presentation, but even in this setting, many patients follow a prolonged course relative to other sarcoma types {37625241}. The most common sites of metastases are liver and lung.

Histological features, such as severe cytological atypia, increased mitoses, and necrosis, have been associated with overall survival in some studies {31537895; 33729740}, but not in others, as even tumours with bland morphological features can metastasize {18551749; 9130982}. Risk stratification methods have been proposed {18551749; 25353289; 31537895; 33729740}.
