1.11.3 Perineurioma

Definition

Perineuriomas of soft tissue are nearly always benign peripheral nerve sheath tumours composed entirely of perineurial cells.

ICD-O coding

XXX

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

Soft tissue perineurioma; sclerosing perineurioma; intraneural perineurioma; mucosal perineurioma; malignant perineurioma (perineurial malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour).

Localization

Soft tissue perineuriomas most commonly arise on the lower limbs, followed by the upper limbs and trunk {15958848}. The head and neck region, visceral organs, and central body cavity sites are rarely affected. Sclerosing perineuriomas are commonly found on the fingers and palms {9414186} and rarely at other sites {27845943}.

Clinical features

Perineuriomas of soft tissue usually present as painless masses. Subcutaneous tissue is involved more often than deep soft tissue. About 10% of cases are limited to the dermis {15958848; 11012740}.

Epidemiology

Perineuriomas of soft tissue are rare {1497116; 7821894; 9042282; 15370128; 15958848}. These tumours show a slight female predilection and occur over a wide age range, with a peak in middle-aged adults {15958848}. Children are rarely affected. Sclerosing perineuriomas show a male predilection and usually affect young adults {9414186; 12836021}.

Etiology

Soft tissue perineuriomas are nearly always sporadic. Very rare cases have been reported in patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 or NF2-related schwannomatosis (neurofibromatosis type 2) {17122521; 17696922; 24294391}.

Pathogenesis

Soft tissue perineuriomas share pathogenetic mechanisms with other benign nerve sheath tumours. Deletion of 22q12 and mutations in NF2 (encoding the tumour suppressor merlin [NF2]) are the most frequently reported genetic alterations {9042282; 30303818; 11290539}, like in schwannomas and meningiomas. Deletion of 17q11 (including NF1) is also a recurrent event in soft tissue perineurioma. Alterations in 10q24 are recurrent events in sclerosing perineuriomas {16096405}. Malignant perineurial tumours have been less well characterized: loss of chromosome 13q and small deletions of chromosomes 3, 6, and 9 have been reported in 2 cases of low-grade malignant perineurial tumour {18844938; 30303818}.

Macroscopic appearance

Soft tissue perineuriomas are well circumscribed but unencapsulated. The cut surface is usually firm or rubbery and yellow, tan, or white. A small subset of tumours show a gelatinous appearance. Size ranges from < 10 to 200 mm, although most tumours measure between 15 and 80 mm. The mean size of superficial tumours is 30 mm, compared with 70 mm for deep-seated tumours {15958848}.

Histopathology

Soft tissue perineuriomas typically show a predominantly storiform or whorled growth pattern. Other distinctive architectural features include long fascicles with tumour cells arranged in a lamellar fashion and perivascular growth. The tumour cells are usually slender spindle cells with wavy or tapering nuclei, indistinct nucleoli, and characteristic delicate bipolar cytoplasmic processes. Some perineuriomas contain shorter, ovoid tumour cells. The stroma is usually collagenous; about 20% of cases contain at least focally myxoid matrix, which is occasionally abundant {15958848}. Mitotic activity is typically scarce or absent. Occasional soft tissue perineuriomas show degenerative nuclear atypia, including pleomorphic and multinucleated cells, some with nuclear pseudoinclusions {15958848}. Plexiform architecture has rarely been reported {10936896; 16133356}. Sclerosing perineuriomas are composed of cords of small epithelioid to spindle cells in a dense collagenous stroma {9414186}. Reticular perineuriomas are composed of interconnected, elongated spindle cells with a lacy or reticular architecture {11257623}. Rare perineuriomas contain cells with intracytoplasmic vacuoles, mimicking lipoblasts {27395366}. The very rare malignant perineuriomas (perineurial malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours) show cytoarchitectural features similar to those of benign soft tissue perineuriomas, in addition to hypercellularity, nuclear atypia and hyperchromasia, and a high mitotic count {9808129}.

Intraneural perineuriomas show circumferential perineurial whorls around the nerve fibres on cross-sections, referred to as pseudo–onion bulbs. Mucosal perineuriomas show swirling growth of delicate spindle cells expanding the lamina propria.

By immunohistochemistry, perineuriomas consistently express EMA, which ranges from weak and focal to strong and diffuse {15958848}. Claudin-1 and GLUT1 are also often positive {12459629; 12836021}. CD34 is expressed in about 60% of soft tissue perineuriomas {15958848}, whereas S100, SOX10, and GFAP are negative. Sclerosing perineuriomas may show limited staining for keratins in addition to EMA {9414186}.

Cytology

Aspirated material shows hypocellularity, with small tissue fragments of spindle cells with bland, slender nuclei, bipolar cytoplasmic processes, and scant myxoid to collagenous matrix {27159449}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: storiform, whorled, and lamellar architecture; elongated, slender spindle cells with bipolar cytoplasmic processes; expression of EMA.

Desirable: variable claudin-1, GLUT1, and CD34.

Staging

Rare malignant perineurioma can be staged with the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) / American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) system, although its prognostic value has not been established.

Prognosis and prediction

Perineuriomas of soft tissue (and sclerosing subtype), including those with degenerative nuclear atypia, are benign and only rarely recur locally {9414186; 11257623; 15958848}. Malignant perineuriomas may sometimes metastasize but appear to behave less aggressively than conventional malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours {9808129}.
