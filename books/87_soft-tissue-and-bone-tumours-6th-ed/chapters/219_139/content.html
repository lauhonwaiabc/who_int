2.1.9 Osteochondromyxoma

Definition

Osteochondromyxoma is an extremely rare, benign, sometimes locally aggressive, cartilage- and bone matrix–producing tumour with a myxoid spindle cell component that arises in the medullary cavity and typically affects patients with Carney complex.

ICD-O coding

9211/0 Osteochondromyxoma

ICD-11 coding

2E82.Z & XH6KR3 Benign chondrogenic tumours, site unspecified & Osteochondromyxoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: Carney bone tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Tumours usually develop in the nasal region and the diaphysis of the tibia and radius; infrequently they have been reported in the rib, chest wall, and spine {28008382}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Patients present with a painless mass, usually found on screening for skeletal involvement in cases of Carney complex. Carney complex is a rare, autosomal dominant syndrome characterized by multiple endocrine neoplasms and lentigines {28008382}. Tumours occurring in non-syndromic individuals are uncommon {39130925; 11176065}.

Imaging

Osteochondromyxoma is usually solitary but can be multicentric within individual and multiple bones. Tumours have variable radiographic features and are circumscribed or poorly defined, lytic with scattered calcifications, and expansile; they erode the cortex and can elicit a periosteal reaction {25590407; 23315333; 11176065; 31929968}. MRI reveals a heterogeneous mass, and the cartilage and myxoid components are bright on T2-weighted images {28008382}.

Epidemiology

The tumour is extremely rare and affects 1% of patients with Carney complex {4010501; 3945116; 28008382}. Affected patients have been reported across a wide age range, but most are within the first two years of life, and the tumour can be congenital {11176065}.

Etiology

Osteochondromyxoma is part of the Carney complex, a genetic syndrome in which most patients have inactivating mutations in PRKAR1A, the gene encoding the type IA regulatory subunit of PKA {29939654}.

Pathogenesis

Karyotypes are normal {11176065}. Carney complex is genetically heterogeneous, with two genetic loci: PRKAR1A (CNC1) on chromosome 17 and CNC2 on chromosome 2. Most often, inactivating mutations are found in the tumour suppressor gene PRKAR1A on chromosome 17 {29939654}. PRKAR1A encodes the type IA regulatory subunit of PKA, an important regulator of cAMP signalling.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumour is circumscribed, has a lobulated architecture, and is composed of white-yellow, gelatinous, cartilaginous, and haemorrhagic tissue. The tumour can erode the cortex and extend into soft tissue {28008382}.

Histopathology

The tumours are morphologically heterogeneous and consist of myxoid spindle cell elements, hyaline cartilage, bone, and fibrous tissue that vary in proportions. The tumours are circumscribed, have a lobular architecture, and are hypocellular to moderately cellular. The myxoid regions contain cords and strands of polyhedral, spindle, and stellate cells that have round to oval to fusiform nuclei with fine chromatin and eosinophilic cytoplasm. There are occasional mitotic figures, but no cytological atypia. These zones blend with areas of hyaline cartilage that contain uniform chondrocytes of varying degrees of cellularity. Some of the cartilage is associated with enchondral ossification. Hyaline fibrous nodules and septa may be present, which can become ossified.

Immunohistochemistry

Neoplastic chondrocytes can be S100-positive. There is loss of staining for PRKAR1A.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no reports of molecular abnormalities; however it is hypothesized that many tumours have variant of PRKAR1A.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; variably cellular myxoid, hyaline cartilage, and bone-forming tumour; patients have other signs and symptoms of Carney complex.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Complete excision is curative; however, local recurrence is common with incomplete resection {11176065; 28008382; 36723686}. No metastases have been reported.
