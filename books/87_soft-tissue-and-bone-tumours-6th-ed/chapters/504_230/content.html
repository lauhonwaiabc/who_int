5.0.4 CIC-rearranged sarcoma

Definition

CIC-rearranged sarcoma is an undifferentiated round cell sarcoma defined by CIC-related gene fusions, most often CIC::DUX4.

ICD-O coding

9367/3 CIC-rearranged sarcoma.

ICD-11 coding

2B5F Sarcoma, not elsewhere classified, primary site.

Related terminology

Acceptable: CIC::DUX4 sarcoma.

Not recommended: Ewing-like sarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None.

Localization

Most CIC sarcomas occur in the deep soft tissues of the limbs or trunk, and less commonly in the head and neck, retroperitoneum, or pelvis {21813156; 28346326}. About 10% of cases have a visceral presentation, including kidney, gastrointestinal tract, and brain {28346326; 26685084; 26919435; 29901569}. Some arise in superficial soft tissues, and primary osseous involvement is rare (< 5%) {28346326; 27079694}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

CIC sarcoma presents as a mass with or without pain. Some of the patients (16–50%) present with symptoms of visceral involvement or of metastatic disease {28346326; 26685084}.

Imaging

Imaging characteristics have been described in case reports and case series only. On MRI, CIC-rearranged sarcomas showed heterogeneous contrast enhancement, with areas of internal necrosis in all cases and haemorrhagic areas in half of the cases. Flow voids may be seen internally, and perilesional oedema is common (80%) {32840647; 36542130}.

18F-FDG PET/CT is used for staging distant metastases. The primary tumour tends to show a high mean standardized uptake value of 13.2 (range: 8.5–18.1). No matrix mineralization is seen on CT in CIC-rearranged sarcoma.

Epidemiology

Cases published in medical literature report a wide age range at presentation (14–53 years) {40722508}, extending from children to elderly adults {28346326; 26685084}; however, reports show a striking predilection for young adults (median age: 25–35 years), and < 25% of cases present in the paediatric age group {28346326; 26685084}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

A CIC::DUX4 fusion is present in 95% of cases, resulting from either a t(4;19)(q35;q13) or a t(10;19)(q26;q13) {16717057; 22072439}. Less common partner genes include AXL, CITED1, FOXO4, LEUTX, NUTM1, NUTM2A, and SYK {36788092; 30407212; 28188754; 25007147; 26735859}. CIC encodes a high-mobility group (HMG) box transcriptional repressor. Most CIC breakpoints are located within CIC exon 20 {28233365}. The DUX4 gene, encoding a double homeobox transcription factor, is located within the D4Z4 macrosatellite repeat region in the subtelomeric regions of chromosomes 4 and 10 (4q35 or 10q26.3). DUX4 is normally expressed in germ cells, but it is epigenetically silenced in somatic differentiated tissues. The predicted CIC::DUX4 chimeric protein retains the HMG box, with a large part of the N-terminal DUX4 being lost. In a subset of cases, the CIC::DUX4 fusion leads to a stop codon right after the breakpoint; thus, the resulting chimeric protein lacks any DUX4 sequence, suggesting that a truncated CIC protein may be sufficient to trigger oncogenesis, particularly its C1 domain {28233365; 28493604; 39530749}. Trisomy 8 and MYC amplification are some of the other common genetic changes {24947144}. Concurrent CIC mutations have been described in the context of tumours with CIC::LEUTX fusion {26735859}. The gene expression profile of CIC sarcoma is distinct from that of Ewing sarcoma {24723486}. The CIC::DUX4 fusion markedly enhances the CIC transcriptional activity, upregulating its targets, including CCND2, MUC5AC, and PEA3 family genes (e.g. ETV1, ETV4, and ETV5) {24723486; 16717057; 28404587}. CIC::NUTM1 fusion–positive tumours cluster together with cases with CIC::DUX4 fusions by gene expression profiling, separate from other BRD3/BRD4 or MAD1L1::NUTM1 fusions {29431183; 30407212}. DNA-based methylation profiling can differentiate CIC-rearranged sarcomas from other round cell soft tissue sarcomas {29572501; 32155762}. ATXN1/ATXN1L::NUTM2A gene fusions exhibit methylation patterns concordant with CIC::DUX4 sarcomas {35836290}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are generally large, well-circumscribed, white or tan soft masses as large as 140 mm in size, with frequent haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

CIC-rearranged sarcomas have similar morphological features regardless of the fusion partner {30407212; 28188754; 25007147}. Tumours are composed of diffuse sheets of undifferentiated round cells, displaying at least in part a lobulated growth pattern, delineated by fibrotic stroma. A minor component of spindle, rhabdoid, or epithelioid cells may be seen in many cases. The tumour cells have relatively uniform cytomorphology but often reveal a mild degree of nuclear pleomorphism, with vesicular chromatin and prominent nucleoli. The cytoplasm is lightly eosinophilic, with occasional clearing. Necrosis is common and mitotic activity is brisk. In one third of the cases, focal myxoid stromal changes are present, in which tumour cells exhibit reticular or pseudoacinar arrangements {22072439; 28346326; 26685084; 24947144}.

Immunohistochemistry

By immunohistochemistry, CIC sarcomas often (87%) express CD99, mostly in a patchy cytoplasmic and/or membranous pattern and less commonly in a diffuse membranous pattern (20%) {28346326}. Nuclear staining for DUX4 (97%), ETV4 (85%), and WT1 (83%) is seen in the majority of cases {27443513; 27562494; 24723486; 39381843; 40298239; 39921488}. However, the specificity of these markers is variable, with CD99 being highly nonspecific, while WT1 (81%–90%), ETV4 (95%), and DUX4 (100%) show higher specificity. The N-terminal WT1 antibody has slightly higher sensitivity (95%) compared with the C-terminal antibody (75%) {39921488}. NKX2-2 is typically negative {26685084}. Sarcomas with CIC::NUTM1 fusions express NUT protein {26919435; 30407212}. Calretinin and ERG can be positive {26685084; 24947144; 24723486}. Keratin, S100, and myogenic markers are rarely expressed.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show high cellularity, with syncytial tissue fragments containing fine vessels and cells with poorly defined cytoplasm, and dispersed single round cells with scant to moderate eccentric cytoplasm (often vacuolated) and round to oval, mildly pleomorphic nuclei with evenly dispersed fine chromatin and often prominent nucleoli. Mitotic figures, necrosis, a myxoid matrix, and a striped proteinaceous background are variably present. The differential diagnosis includes Ewing sarcoma {33399252}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

CIC gene rearrangements can be detected by a variety of techniques {28233365}; however, none of these methods are highly sensitive {22072439; 28233365; 28493604; 39921488}. CIC break-apart FISH can be false negative in 14–43% of the cases, while targeted RNA next-generation sequencing fusion analysis also carries a risk of false negatives, which may be partly mitigated through manual data review {39921488; 28233365}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: predominant round cell phenotype; mild nuclear pleomorphism; epithelioid and/or spindle cell components; variably myxoid stroma; CIC gene rearrangement or surrogate markers (ETV family transcription factors).

Staging

CIC-rearranged sarcoma is staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM principles.

Prognosis and prediction

Most tumours follow a highly aggressive course with frequent metastases, most commonly to the lung. The estimated 5-year overall survival rate is 17–43%, significantly worse than that of Ewing sarcoma {28346326; 26685084}. The chemotherapy response to Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma regimens has been dismal {37212486; 35730520}.
