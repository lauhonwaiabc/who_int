1.12.18 PATZ1-rearranged sarcoma

Definition

PATZ1-rearranged sarcomas display round and spindle cell morphology, co-express myoepithelial and skeletal muscle markers, and are characterized by fusions involving EWSR1 and PATZ1.

ICD-O coding

None.

ICD-11 coding

None

Related terminology

Acceptable: EWSR1::PATZ1 sarcoma; round cell sarcoma with EWSR1::PATZ1.

Subtype(s)

None.

Localization

Most cases occur in the soft tissues of the thoracodorsal abdominal region, but they can occasionally involve the extremities and head and neck, including the tongue {10949935; 29431183; 30379650; 31189996; 32134119; 32362012; 33012788; 33459994; 33077252; 38043789; 38645952}. Visceral examples are exceptionally rare with only a single documented primary pulmonary case {33012788}. Primary osseous tumours have not been reported.

Clinical features

The most common presentation is that of a mass of variable duration associated with pain {30379650; 31189996; 32134119; 38645952}. Less commonly, fatigue and weight loss have also been reported {32134119}. Some cases present with widespread metastases at the time of initial diagnosis {33012788}.

Epidemiology

PATZ1-rearranged sarcomas are rare and have been described to affect predominantly middle-aged adults, but patients of any age may be affected (median age: 47.5 years, range 1–81 years). There is a male predilection in reported cases (M:F ratio: 1.8:1) {33012788; 31189996; 38278606; 38043789}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

PATZ1 encodes a zinc finger protein with a Cys2-His2 motif involved in repression of transcription {29186807;10949935}. PATZ1 resides in close proximity (2 Mb distance) to EWSR1 on chromosome 22. The EWSR1::PATZ1 fusion event is most likely the result of a submicroscopic intrachromosomal paracentric inversion (as the genes are normally transcribed in opposite directions), although more complex structural alterations cannot be fully excluded {10949935; 30379650; 31189996}. An in-frame fusion between exon 8 or 9 of EWSR1 and exon 1 of PATZ1 results in removal of the putative transcriptional repressor domain and the AT-hook domain at the N-terminus of PATZ1 and converts a transcriptional repressor into a transcription activator {31189996}. Less commonly, exon 7 of EWSR1 is involved {38043789}. By transcriptomic profiling, EWSR1::PATZ1 sarcomas appear distinct from other round cell sarcomas such as Ewing sarcomas, EWSR1/FUS::NFATC2, CIC-rearranged, and BCOR-altered sarcomas {29431183}. A recent DNA methylation analysis showed that they clustered in the vicinity of, but distinct from, CNS cases with identical fusions {40413488}. Although one study reported loss/deletion of CDKN2A/CDKN2B present only in clinically aggressive EWSR1::PATZ1 sarcomas (with concurrent MDM2 amplification in 1 case) {31189996}, more recent studies have found these alterations to be both present and absent in clinically aggressive tumours as well as present in indolent tumours {33012788; 38043789}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are variably circumscribed, solid and cystic, tan-yellow to grey-white masses measuring from 7 mm to 180 mm in greatest dimension {33012788; 38278606; 38043789}.

Histopathology

PATZ1-rearranged sarcomas are typically well circumscribed, with a well-formed fibrous capsule and peripheral lymphoid aggregates. Small, mushroom-like protrusions through the capsule into surrounding soft tissues are often seen. Diffusely infiltrative tumours are less common {38043789}. Other characteristic low-power features include thick fibrous septa dividing the tumour into irregular lobules, and numerous microcysts filled with eosinophilic secretions {30379650; 3118996; 33012788; 38043789}. A prominent thick-walled, hyalinized, and sometimes branching vasculature is present {33012788; 38043789}. The tumours are composed of variable proportions of round, spindled, and epithelioid cells arranged in reticular, trabecular, fascicular, and vaguely whorled patterns, within a fibrous to fibromyxoid background {30379650; 3118996; 33012788; 38043789}. In most cases, the neoplastic cells are small, uniform, and bland, with round nuclei, evenly dispersed chromatin, and small or inapparent nucleoli. Occasionally, the tumours show in part high-grade round cell features {10949935; 3118996; 33012788}. Mitotic activity is usually low, and necrosis is uncommon {3118996; 33012788; 38043789}. Rarely, overt rhabdomyoblastic differentiation, mature fat, or pseudorosettes are present {3118996; 33012788; 38043789}.

Immunohistochemically, PATZ1-rearranged sarcomas are polyphenotypic, with coexpression of both skeletal muscle–associated markers (e.g. desmin, myogenin, MYOD1, PAX7) and myoepithelial markers (e.g. keratins, SOX10, S100, and GFAP) {30379650; 33012788; 38043789}.

Recently, immunohistochemistry for PATZ1 has been developed as a surrogate for gene fusions {42270011}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of the EWSR1::PATZ1 fusion can be helpful for diagnosis {10949935; 29431183; 31189996; 38043789}. The sensitivity of an EWSR1 FISH break-apart probe may be compromised by the short genomic distance between PATZ1 and EWSR1 {31189996}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: monomorphic round to spindled cells in a fibromyxoid background with a thick fibrous capsule and hyalinized blood vessels; coexpression of skeletal muscle and myoepithelial markers; PATZ1 rearrangement.

Staging

PATZ1-rearranged sarcoma can be staged using the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems.

Prognosis and prediction

Initial data suggested that PATZ1-rearranged sarcomas were clinically aggressive, with metastases to lungs, liver, pleura, and pericardium in 9 of 11 (82%) patients with clinical follow-up {10949935; 29431183; 30379650; 30379650; 33012788}. More recently, larger series have found more favourable outcome, with metastases only in 3 of 16 (19%) patients, all of whom presented with metastases at diagnosis {38043789}. Patients with PATZ1-rearranged sarcomas may fall into two distinct groups: (1) those with metastases either at presentation or very shortly thereafter and (2) those in whom metastatic disease either occurs significantly later in the course of the disease or perhaps not at all {38043789}. There is no clear association between pathological variables (such as nuclear grade, mitotic activity, and necrosis) and outcome {33012788; 38043789}.
