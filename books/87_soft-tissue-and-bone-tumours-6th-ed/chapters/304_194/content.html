3.0.4 Erdheim–Chester disease

Definition

Erdheim–Chester disease (ECD) is a neoplastic systemic condition characterized by the accumulation of mature clonal histiocytes, frequently exhibiting xanthogranuloma histology, and associated with varying degrees of inflammation and fibrosis.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Erdheim–Chester disease

ICD-11 coding

2B31.Y & XH1VJ3 Other specified histiocytic or dendritic cell neoplasms & Erdheim–Chester disease

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

ECD is a multisystem disease. Long bones are involved in > 90% of cases with bilateral symmetric involvement. Retroperitoneal (58%), periaortic (46%), and perinephric (> 50%) sites are also frequent. Neurological or pituitary involvement occurs in 20–50% of cases. Infiltration of lungs, heart, skin (xanthelasma), sinus of the face, or any other organ or soft tissue may also occur, but the spleen, lymph nodes, and liver are generally spared {32187362; 29396850; 33901419}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

ECD may be asymptomatic; however, most patients have general, inflammatory, or site-related symptoms, most of which are probably related to inflammation and fibrosis associated with the histiocytic infiltration of the tissues and organs {32187362}. Bone pain (37–50%), exophthalmos (25%), xanthelasma (19–26%), and arginine vasopressin deficiency (formerly diabetes insipidus; 28%) are frequently reported. CNS disease presents with either tumour-like or neurodegenerative-type symptoms. Perivascular involvement of the basilar arteries can lead to stroke. Pulmonary, renal, endocrine, or cardiac dysfunction may also be responsible for clinical manifestation.

Imaging

The characteristic symmetrical osteosclerosis of the leg bones is best visualized by PET, with bilateral uptake of 18F-FDG. Bone lesions may be visualized on CT or MRI, but they are often missed on plain X-rays. CT will reveal bilateral perinephric infiltration, responsible for the hairy-kidney sign, along with perivascular infiltrations around the soft tissues of the abdominal or thoracic aorta. Detection of mass-like infiltration of the right atrium by MRI (33%) is highly suggestive of ECD, often with BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (also known as BRAF V600E) correlation {29396850}. CT of the chest may show pleural and septal thickening, as well as serosal effusions. Imaging also reveals symmetric fibrosis in sinuses of the face. Infiltration of other organs is less specific. It is also noteworthy that 10–20% of cases of ECD are associated with other forms of histiocytosis, such as Langerhans cell histiocytosis {24894769; 26966089} and Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease {31123032}.

Epidemiology

ECD is a rare but probably underdiagnosed disease. No data are available on the exact prevalence. Incidence was estimated to be 0.35 cases per 1 million adults per year in France and Italy during the period 2018–2020, and 0.9 cases per 10 million population in the USA in 2021. The median age at diagnosis is 55 years, with an M:F ratio of 3:1. Cases in children aged < 15 years are very rare and overlap with systemic juvenile xanthogranuloma {37490651; 37871575}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Most cases have an activating kinase variant, notably of the MAPK pathway. This variant may also be present within bone marrow progenitors. Some cases may have an associated myeloproliferative or myelodysplastic neoplasm or be associated with clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential {28679734; 24754681}. Some cases of ECD may result from tissue accumulation of histiocytes derived from mutated bone marrow progenitors {28566492}.

Macroscopic appearance

Most of the samples obtained for diagnosis correspond to biopsies of perirenal fat, retroperitoneum, bone, or skin. They are usually firm and sclerotic.

Histopathology

Lesional tissue comprises an infiltration of foamy, lipid-laden, and/or small mononuclear histiocytes associated with variable proportions of Touton giant cells, small lymphocytes, plasma cells, and neutrophils, which is histologically indistinguishable from that of xanthogranuloma. Fibrosis is often present and sometimes predominant, leading to misinterpretation of the lesion as a reactive fibroinflammatory process. However, fibrosis is not needed for diagnosis. Admixed Langerhans cell histiocytosis or Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease may be present in the same biopsy.

Immunohistochemistry

The immunohistochemical profile is identical to that of xanthogranuloma, with ECD histiocytes positive for CD163, CD68, and CD14 and negative for CD1a and CD207. Distinction is made with clinical/radiographic correlation. Lesional histiocytes are typically positive for factor XIIIa and fascin, and less commonly for S100. Expression of cyclin D1 and phosphorylated ERK is frequent in the neoplastic ECD histiocytes. Although strong diffuse cytoplasmic overexpression of mutant-specific VE1 monoclonal antibody may signal a BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) mutation, it must be confirmed by sensitive molecular methods (i.e. PCR) before targeted therapy is started, as there can be high false-positive and false-negative results. Expression of ALK1 or pan-TRK in lesional histiocytes should suggest ALK-positive histiocytosis or (emerging) NTRK1-rearranged histiocytosis, respectively {34727172; 40315374}.

Differential diagnosis

Disseminated xanthogranulomas with the relevant mutation of the MAPK pathway are now considered part of the same syndrome {26966089}. The differential diagnosis mainly includes xanthogranuloma, ALK-positive histiocytosis {34727172}, NTRK1-rearranged histiocytosis (emerging) {40315374}, as well as some chronic inflammatory processes.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

A BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) mutation is present in 50–60% of cases of ECD {22879539}. This mutation can be missed, because the mutant allele frequency is < 5% in a quarter of cases {30262559}. Patients without BRAF p.(Val600Glu) frequently have other mutations (also activating the MAPK cell signalling pathway) on ARAF, NRAS, KRAS, or MAP2K1 {26566875}. PIK3CA may also be mutated {25150293}. Other molecular fusions are found with regular frequency.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: appropriate clinical and radiological context with xanthogranuloma histology; histology is mandatory but not specific.

Desirable: collection of bland foamy clonal histiocytes with fibrosis; demonstration of a variant activating the MAPK pathway.

Staging

Not clinically relevant, as by definition, ECD is a systemic disease.

Prognosis and prediction

CNS and cardiovascular involvement are associated with a worse prognosis {29396850}. Widespread disease has worse outcomes compared with limited disease {40437172}. BRAF mutations alone do not have a prognostic value, but mutation status may be associated with distinct ECD phenotypes. In all, targeted therapies appear to improve prognosis {39787607; 40053897}, with greater effect suggested in widespread disease compared with more limited disease {40437172}.
