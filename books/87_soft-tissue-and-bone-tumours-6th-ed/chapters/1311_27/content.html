1.3.11 Fibroma of tendon sheath

Definition

Fibroma of tendon sheath (FTS) is a benign fibroblastic/myofibroblastic spindle cell neoplasm, usually attached to a tendon sheath.

ICD-O coding

8813/0 Fibroma of tendon sheath

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH0WB3 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Fibroma of tendon sheath.

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

FTS typically arises on the flexor tendons of the fingers, most commonly affecting the thumb, index, and middle fingers. Intra-articular involvement, particularly of the knee, elbow, or wrist, is rare {22028571; 21340629; 19644650; 91424}.

Clinical features

The tumour presents as a firm, small (≤ 30 mm), painless, slow-growing nodule.

Epidemiology

FTS is rare and typically occurs in patients aged 20–50 years {2729499; 23212985; 32349463}. Childhood presentation {33172434} and an M:F ratio of 1.5:1 to 3:1 have been reported {32349463}. A female predilection has been observed for plantar tumours {27247872}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

At the molecular level, many examples of FTS are characterized by rearrangements of the USP6 locus or by USP6 fusion genes involving a variety of partners, including PKM, RCC1, ASPN, COL1A1, COL3A1, MYH9, TNC, MIR22HG, CTNNB1, SPARC, CAP1, EMP1, CYTOR [LINC00152], NR1D1, and RAB1A, among others {27125357; 32661296; 34088995; 34520696; 36973197}. USP6 fusion genes were initially thought to be more common in the cellular patterns of FTS, which shows significant morphological overlap with nodular fasciitis, another lesion characterized by USP6 rearrangements. However, these fusions have also been identified in many cases of FTS with classic histology. Given that FTS, nodular fasciitis, and two non–USP6-associated tumours (myofibroma and desmoplastic fibroblastoma) may exhibit overlapping morphological features, the true prevalence of USP6 abnormalities in FTS remains uncertain. In this context, it is also plausible that many lesions historically diagnosed as cellular fibroma of tendon sheath may, in fact, represent tenosynovial nodular fasciitis {16434899; 27125357; 34088995; 40010985}.

Macroscopic appearance

FTS is well circumscribed with a fibrous cut surface.

Histopathology

FTS is well circumscribed and lobulated, composed of bland spindle cells embedded in a collagenous stroma. The cellularity is generally low but can be variable, often increasing towards the periphery of the tumour. Characteristic slit-like, thin-walled vessels are typically present and tend to be more conspicuous at the edges. Degenerative changes, including myxoid or cystic alterations, chondroid or osseous metaplasia, and occasional pleomorphic cells, may be observed. The morphological features of FTS can overlap with those of nodular fasciitis, myofibroma, and desmoplastic fibroma, making the differential diagnosis challenging {91424}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The identification of USP6 fusion genes or locus rearrangement may be useful.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a paucicellular or moderately cellular collagen-rich lesion with slit-like vessels.

Desirable: occurs most often on the finger tendons.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

FTS may recur locally in 5–10% of cases.
