1.4.3 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Definition

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour is an intermediate, rarely metastasizing, superficial neoplasm showing plexiform architecture and biphasic morphology, composed of nodules of histiocytoid cells and bundles of myofibroblastic spindle cells.

ICD-O coding

8835/1 Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH4GL1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is a strong predilection for the upper limbs followed by the lower limbs. The trunk and the head and neck area are less often affected {17870015; 2847569; 1723956; 36071257}.

Clinical features

The tumours present as slow-growing, poorly defined plaques and nodules, typically measuring 10–30 mm in diameter {2847569}.

Epidemiology

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour is rare. The age range is wide, but children and young adults are typically affected, with a median age of 14.5–20 years {2847569; 10366148; 36071257; 17870015; 1723956}. A slight female predilection has been shown in most studies {2847569; 10366148; 36071257}, while others revealed an equal distribution {17870015; 1723956}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Although cytogenetic abnormalities have been reported in 2 cases {9973942; 21261704}, recurrent genetic events are yet to be identified.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours form a nondescript mass at the dermal–subcutaneous junction, usually < 30 mm in size. By imaging, tumours are generally localized in the subcutaneous soft tissue {30145610}.

Histopathology

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumours show an infiltrative architecture with overall plexiform outlines. The tumours are composed of small nodules of histiocytoid cells and osteoclast-like giant cells, surrounded by fascicles of spindle cells in varying proportions. There may be haemorrhage, haemosiderin deposition, and a chronic inflammatory infiltrate. Mitotic activity is generally low and necrosis is absent. Nuclear atypia and lymphovascular invasion can rarely be seen. Additional rare findings include myxoid or hyalinized stromal changes, metaplastic bone formation, and tumour extension into underlying skeletal muscle tissue. By immunohistochemistry, the spindle cells express SMA {22441367}. Within the plexiform nodules, CD163 and CD11c highlight many histiocytes; CSF1 chromogenic in situ hybridization shows positivity in interspersed round cells {36071257}.

Cytology

There are very few reports of this rare neoplasm in the cytopathology literature.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: plexiform architecture; involvement of dermis and/or subcutaneous adipose tissue; biphasic composition of nodules of histiocytoid cells and osteoclast-like giant cells, interspersed with fascicles of myofibroblastic spindle cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Plexiform fibrohistiocytic tumour is associated with a risk of local recurrence ranging from 12.5% to 37.5% and rare lymph node metastasis in approximately 6% {17870015; 2847569; 1723956; 10366148; 36071257}. Distant metastasis to the lung is exceptional {9130995; 10366148}.
