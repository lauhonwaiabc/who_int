3.0.2 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS, involving bone

Definition

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) NOS is a neoplasm of large B lymphocytes with a diffuse pattern of growth, lacking features of other specific types of LBCL. DLBCL of bone refers to disease that is localized exclusively or predominantly to osseous sites.

ICD-O coding

9680/3 Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2A81 Diffuse large B-cell lymphomas NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

DLBCL of bone most commonly involves the femur, followed by the vertebrae, pelvic bones, and the tibia {19675454; 28817403}. The metadiaphysial region of long bones is commonly involved. Most patients have a solitary bone lesion, but 10–40% of patients can have multiple lesions involving a solitary bone or multiple bones concurrently.

Three localization patterns are recognized: 1) primary DLBCL of bone is a single site of bone involvement with or without regional lymphadenopathy; 2) polyostotic DLBCL of bone is defined as multiple sites of involvement of one bone or multiple bones; and 3) disseminated DLBCL of bone is defined as bone disease associated with other extranodal sites of DLBCL {19675454; 34478526}.

Clinical features

The most common symptom is pain associated with the affected bone {12960812; 23591933; 28817403}. Swelling at the site of involvement occurs in about 25% of patients, and about 20% of patients have an associated bone fracture {12960812; 23591933}. Systemic symptoms such as fever, weight loss, and night sweats are uncommon.

Imaging

Localization patterns are determined on imaging studies {40402930}. Single-site and polyostotic involvement are usually localized to the appendicular skeleton, whereas disseminated bone lymphoma is mainly found in the axial skeleton. Bone lymphoma is frequently osteolytic, but mixed sclerotic-lytic and purely sclerotic or near-normal radiographic appearances may be seen. Periosteal reaction, fracture, and/or sequestra may be identified {40402930}. Signal intensities on MRI are not specific. On CT and MRI, soft tissue extension with a relatively preserved cortex is a hallmark shared with other small blue round cell tumours {30699452}.

Epidemiology

Primary bone lymphomas represent < 1% of all lymphomas and about 7% of malignant bone tumours {12960812; 19675454; 28817403}. Patients have a median age in the fifth decade of life, and the M:F ratio is 1.15–1.53:1 {23591933; 34478526; 39470477}. Systemic lymphomas may involve the skeletal system secondarily in as many as 20% of patients and can involve one or more bones, with DLBCL being the most common systemic lymphoma to involve bones {28797508}.

Etiology

Primary DLBCLs of bone are typically sporadic and arise de novo. In rare patients, the lymphoma arises on a background of longstanding osteomyelitis {3779614} or Paget disease of bone {3779614}. Patients who are HIV positive rarely develop primary DLBCL of bone {11953618}.

Pathogenesis

Using gene expression profiling, DLBCL NOS can be divided into germinal-centre B-cell (GCB) and activated B-cell cell-of-origin subtypes; gene variant profiling has shown overlapping but preferential variant profiles associated with each subtype {28985567; 29666115}. More granular molecular subgroups of DLBCL NOS with different biological and prognostic significance have been defined {29713087; 32289277} but are not currently in use for therapeutic decision-making. Immunohistochemical analysis and gene expression profiling has shown that most cases of primary DLBCLs of bone are of GCB type, particularly EZB type, and have a profile similar to normal GCB centrocytes {28817403; 34478526}. The mutation landscape of primary bone DLBCL differs from systemic DLBCL NOS involving bone. Primary DLBCL of bone has a higher frequency of mutations in the GCB associated genes B2M, EZH2, IRF8, BCL6, KLHL6, and TNFSF14 {34478526; 40301298}. The tumour microenvironment (TME) of primary DLBCL of bone is generally richer in immune cells than that of systemic DLBCL involving bone. Cells characteristic of an immune-rich TME include CD4+ T cells, NK cells, dendritic cells, mast cells, myofibroblasts, and vascular endothelial cells {40301298}.

Macroscopic appearance

In most patients, the diagnosis of DLBCL of bone is established by biopsy alone, thereby initiating chemotherapy and radiotherapy. In rare patients who undergo resection, the neoplasm in the bone is grey-white and fleshy and frequently has areas of necrosis.

Histopathology

Morphological features

DLBCL is characterized by a diffuse growth pattern and is composed of large lymphoid cells replacing the bone and bone marrow. The neoplastic cells are large with round or irregular nuclei and variably prominent nucleoli {10602434; 12960812; 19675454; 28797508}. The tumour cell nuclei are often cleaved, multilobated, or occasionally pleomorphic. The cytoplasm of the lymphoma cells is moderate and may be amphophilic. The neoplasm can be associated with prominent fibrosis; in some cases the lymphoma cells are spindle-shaped, imparting a sarcomatoid appearance. Crush artefact is common; this histological finding should raise the possibility of a lymphoma. Typically the tumour cells are accompanied by reactive lymphocytes and histiocytes. Bone trabeculae show reactive changes. In some cases, crush artefact can completely obscure the neoplastic cells necessitating re-biopsy.

Immunophenotype

The cells of DLBCL are positive for pan B-cell markers including CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a, and PAX5 {10602434; 19675454; 28817403}. A subset of cases are positive for surface or cytoplasmic immunoglobulin. Ki-67 staining reveals brisk proliferation, with a median Ki-67 proliferation index of 70–80%. GCB-type tumours are more often positive for CD10, BCL6 (moderate to strong), and LMO2. ABC/non-GCB–type tumours are usually positive for IRF4/MUM1 and FOXP1. BCL2 is often positive, but this is usually not associated with BCL2 translocations. MYC may be expressed in highly proliferative neoplasms, but usually not uniformly or brightly unless MYC is rearranged. The neoplastic cells are usually negative for CD5. There is no association with EBV.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Assessment of IG genes for clonality can be helpful for diagnosis. FISH analysis is helpful to detect rearrangements of MYC, BCL2, and BCL6 present in approximately 10%, 15%, and 20% of cases, respectively {18426731; 19675454}. Detection of simultaneous MYC and BCL2 rearrangements or MYC and BCL6 rearrangements is unusual in primary DLBCL of bone and therefore suggestive of systemic DLBCL involving bones {19675454; 28817403}. EBV infection is rare in primary bone DLBCL {28817403}, and therefore colorimetric in situ hybridization for EBV-encoded RNA (EBER) helps exclude systemic EBV-positive DLBCL.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffuse proliferation of large neoplastic lymphoid cells of B-cell lineage; exclusion of secondary bone involvement by other specific types of large B-cell lymphoma.

Desirable: cell-of-origin subtype; assessment for MYC, BCL2, or BCL6 rearrangements.

Staging

Patients are staged according to the Lugano classification, which has been adopted by the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) TNM classification {25113753}. Patients with primary DLBCL of bone usually present with stage I or II disease. Patients with systemic DLBCL involving bones have advanced stage disease.

Prognosis and prediction

Patients with primary DLBCL of bone are typically treated with combination chemotherapy, often with radiation therapy, and the overall prognosis is generally good, with overall survival of 80–90% {19734480; 23591933; 28797508; 28817403; 34478526}. Patients younger than 60 years of age have a better prognosis. Primary bone DLBCL with an immune cell–rich TME has a better prognosis than neoplasms with immune cell–poor TME {40301298}.
