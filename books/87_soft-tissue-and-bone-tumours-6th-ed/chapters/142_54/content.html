1.4.2 Chondroid synoviocytic neoplasm

Definition

Chondroid synoviocytic neoplasm has a predilection for the temporomandibular joint region; is composed of varying amounts of large, eosinophilic synoviocytes, osteoclastic giant cells, hyaline cartilage, and calcified matrix; and frequently harbours FN1 rearrangements.

ICD-O coding

XXX

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Acceptable: chondroid tenosynovial giant cell tumour; calcified chondroid mesenchymal neoplasm.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Close to 80% of reported cases have involved the temporomandibular joint and/or temporal bone. Precise localization to the joint or adjacent bone may not be possible in extensive tumours. A minority of cases have been reported in other anatomical locations, including the hip, knee, thumb, and spine {39181450; 21087985;33727696}.

Clinical features

The tumours most often occur in middle-aged adults (median: 50 years), without a sex predilection. In the temporomandibular joint region, they usually present as expansile, locally aggressive masses. In other anatomical locations, they typically appear as a nonspecific soft tissue mass. Radiographically, they appear as lytic or expansile masses, often centred in the bone rather than in the temporomandibular joint itself. Calcified matrix is often present on CT; MRI with gadolinium contrast typically shows avid enhancement {39181450}.

Epidemiology

Chondroid synoviocytic tumours are rare, with only 53 reported examples so far, including some reported as calcified chondroid mesenchymal neoplasm. No strong sex predilection is observed, and patients range in age from 17 to 75 years (mean: 50 years {2326660; 21087985; 17334342; 23303171; 24571839; 27888510; 31771368; 33727696; 37102574}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Chondroid synoviocytic tumours most often harbour fusions of the FN1 gene with a variety of different partners, including FGFR2 (most common), TEK, FGFR1, PRG4, PLAG1, MERTK, NTRK1, BMPR2, AGFG1, and MALAT1 {39181450; 37102574; 33727696; 39016330}. Other recurrent fusions include PDGFRA::USP8; individual cases have been shown to harbour TIMP3::ZCCHC7 and CSF1::COL6A3 fusions {39181450; 37102574; 33727696; 39016330; 37642440}. Although CSF1 mRNA can be demonstrated in the neoplastic cells, expression levels of CSF1 and CSF1R are generally low {39181450}.

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours appear as friable, variably calcified masses, sometimes with visible cartilage or bone.

Histopathology

Chondroid synoviocytic neoplasms have a multinodular architecture and can in part resemble conventional tenosynovial giant cell tumours, with an admixture of synoviocytes (large, eosinophilic, mononuclear cells containing haemosiderin pigment), small histiocytes, foamy macrophages, siderophages, and osteoclast-like giant cells. The synoviocytes are often prominent and form highly cellular nodules and sheets. Additionally, lobules of (myxo-)hyaline cartilage are evident, which can resemble soft tissue chondroma or synovial chondromatosis. Sometimes, pericellular (chondroblastoma-like) calcifications or crystalline calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate deposits can be seen within the lesional cartilage and, if extensive, mimic tumoural pseudogout {39181450; 21087985}. Mitotic activity is usually low, and higher-grade atypia is absent. The synoviocytes within and outside the chondroid matrix express clusterin and are often positive for CSF1 mRNA by chromogenic in situ hybridization.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

The fusions can be identified with FISH with break-apart FN1 probes and more accurately by next-generation sequencing to identify the tumour-defining 3′ gene partner involved.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nodules of large, eosinophilic, moderately pleomorphic synoviocytes, surrounded by calcified matrix, and cartilage, with sparse histiocytes, osteoclasts, and foamy macrophages.

Desirable: clusterin expression in select cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Despite potentially worrisome features (e.g. high cellularity, synoviocytic overgrowth, nuclear atypia, and mitotic activity), chondroid synoviocytic neoplasms are at most locally aggressive and may recur locally or persist when incompletely resected. To date, metastatic disease has not been reported {39181450; 21087985; 39016330}.
