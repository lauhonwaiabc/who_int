1.12.11 Haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumour

Definition

Haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumour (HFLT) is a locally aggressive neoplasm typically arising on acral sites and composed of admixed adipocytes and haemosiderin-laden spindle cells and macrophages.

ICD-O coding

8811/1 Haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumour

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH9526 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & Haemosiderotic fibrolipomatous tumour

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The foot is the most affected site, especially the dorsum and the ankle, followed by the calf, hand, thigh, and cheek {16487368; 11106076}.

Clinical features

HFLT presents as a slowly growing, sometimes painful subcutaneous (or occasionally deeper) soft tissue mass {16487368; 11106076}. MRI shows an infiltrative fat-containing mass; special MRI sequences can demonstrate the heavy haemosiderin deposition {20127213; 25527465}. Rare lesions with secondary malignant transformation present as rapid recent growth of a longstanding swelling {34389899}.

Epidemiology

HFLT is rare. It is most commonly reported in the fifth and sixth decades of life with a female predominance, but can present at any age, including in children {16487368; 24705316}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic analysis has shown near-diploid karyotypes with balanced or unbalanced t(1;10)(p31-p22;q24-q25), loss of material from 3p, and amplification of 3p12-p11 {18406878; 19199331; 21717526}. The t(1;10) does not result in a fusion transcript but always targets TGFBR3 in 1p22, demonstrates breakpoints in or near OGA (formerly MGEA5) in 10q24, and leads to transcriptional upregulation of neighbouring FGF8 and NPM3 {19199331}. The t(1;10) has been found in as many as 85% of HFLTs {21717526}. The amplicon in 3p contains VGLL3 and CHMP2B, which show increased expression {19199331}. Similar karyotypes, rearrangement of TGFBR3 and OGA, and amplification of VGLL3 have also been observed in pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour of soft parts {21733556; 26980036} and some cases of myxoinflammatory fibroblastic sarcoma {19199331; 21717526}, suggesting a pathogenetic link between these entities {29979612; 24705316; 26980036}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumour size averages 77 mm but can be as large as 190 mm {16487368; 11106076}. The gross pattern varies on the basis of the amount of fat versus spindle cells and the amount of haemosiderin. Most cases present as poorly circumscribed, dark yellowish-brown, and friable masses, with occasional haemorrhage.

Histopathology

HFLT consists of lobules of mature fat admixed in variable proportions with fascicles of bland slender fibroblastic spindle cells containing haemosiderin, haemosiderin-laden macrophages, and osteoclast-like giant cells {16487368; 11106076}. In some areas, the spindle cells are distributed more loosely in smaller numbers and often extend between individual adipocytes and around vessels {11106076; 16487368}. Occasional larger cells with atypical nuclei are sometimes present. Mitotic activity is low and necrosis is absent. Nuclear pseudoinclusions and large, ectatic, partially thrombosed vessels with perivascular hyalinization are sometimes present, leading to morphological overlap with pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour {16487368; 11106076}. Areas resembling HFLT may be observed at the edge of pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour {15489645}. Examples with paucity of haemosiderin associated with myxoid features have been reported {39252432}. Exceptionally, cases of HFLT may recur as a myxoid sarcoma {26980036; 24025526; 21717526}. The spindled cells of HFLT are usually positive for CD34 and calponin {16487368; 11106076}.

Cytology

Aspirates typically show a variably cellular mixture of mature adipose tissue and bland, monotonous spindle cells, some of which contain haemosiderin, along with haemosiderin-laden macrophages {26841226}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mature adipose tissue admixed with bland haemosiderin-laden spindle cells and macrophages; acral locations.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The local recurrence rate is 30–50%, especially if the tumour is incompletely excised {11106076; 20127213}. The recurrence rate can be reduced by complete surgical resection. Metastases have not been reported in pure HFLT, but rare tumours with transformation to frank sarcoma may result in distant metastasis.
