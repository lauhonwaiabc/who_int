5.0.2 NFATC2-rearranged sarcoma

Definition

NFATC2-rearranged sarcoma is a round and spindle cell sarcoma with fibrohyaline stroma, associated with EWSR1/FUS::NFATC2 fusions.

ICD-O coding

XXX

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Not recommended: Ewing-like sarcoma; round cell sarcoma with EWSR1::non-ETS fusion; round cell sarcoma with EWSR1/FUS::NFATC2.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

EWSR1::NFATC2 sarcomas are predominantly located in bones, with a 2.1:1 ratio of bone to soft tissue locations {31078563}. The tumours involve primarily the metaphysis or diaphysis of long bones, with a predilection for the femur, tibia, humerus, radius, and fibula. Soft tissue cases involve extremities, head and neck, and chest wall {17175374; 29626598; 24993903}. FUS::NFATC2 tumours have been reported exclusively in long bones so far {29431183; 31049020; 31078563}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

EWSR1::NFATC2 and FUS::NFATC2 sarcomas manifest as painful, locally destructive bone lesions that typically invade surrounding soft tissue or (less commonly) are well circumscribed or confined to the soft tissue {29626598; 31078563}. A subset of patients experienced symptoms for years before diagnosis {29626598; 31078563}.

Imaging

These tumours occur mostly in the diaphysis of long bones presenting as geographical osteolytic lesions showing expansion, cortical destruction, or aggressive periostitis {36542130}. In addition, primary origin on the bone surface / periosteal origin with cortical erosion or saucerization has been described {34192825}. Longstanding indolent behaviour of tumours originating from the surface of bone and secondary extension through the cortex into the medulla are possible {31078563}.

Epidemiology

These tumours are rare compared with Ewing sarcomas, and no population-based epidemiological data are available. In medical literature, described cases show a male predominance (M:F ratio: 2.6:1) in adults and report a wide age range (0–78 years; median age: 38.5 years) {29661713; 31049020; 30994538; 40693581}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

At the cytogenetic level, the translocation event involves EWSR1 (22q12.2) or FUS (16p11.2) fused to NFATC2 (20q13.2). The translocation is typically associated with amplification of the involved regions by forming a derivative (20;22) ring chromosome {17175374; 19318479}. The EWSR1::NFATC2 fusion induces activation of the NFATC2 transcription factor consequent to the loss of the N-terminal regulatory domain, leading to the relocation in the nucleus of the chimeric transcription factor. Expression and methylome profiling have shown that EWSR1::NFATC2 and FUS::NFATC2 sarcomas are distinct from other round cell sarcomas {29431183; 30895378; 25010205; 29416716; 32327700}. In contrast to EWSR1::NFATC2, the FUS::NFATC2 fusion gene has not demonstrated amplification. Clustering analyses also suggest that EWSR1::NFATC2 and FUS::NFATC2 sarcomas display differences {29431183}, and FUS rearrangements seem to correlate with more aggressive behaviour and more complex genomic profiles with codeletions of tumour suppressor genes {32327700}. A single case associated with a FUS::NFATC4 fusion has been reported showing overlapping features with NFATC2 tumours {37813649}.

Macroscopic appearance

Gross examination of primary bone EWSR1::NFATC2 sarcoma reveals a solid, often lobulated mass with a grey-white, firm or fleshy cut surface with variable haemorrhage and necrosis. Most of these sarcomas are poorly defined and are locally destructive or infiltrate adjacent tissues; however, a few primary soft tissue and bone lesions have been described as being well circumscribed or confined to the intramedullary cavity, respectively {30994538; 29626598; 31078563}.

Histopathology

NFATC2 sarcomas are heterogeneous tumours, composed of small to medium-sized round and/or spindled cells with limited eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm, predominantly arranged in cords, small nests, trabeculae, and pseudoacinar structures in a fibrohyaline or myxohyaline stromal background. Less commonly, matrix-poor sheets of cells are encountered focally or diffusely {24215322; 30714449; 30994538}. The cellular morphology varies from one case to another and within the tumour, including small round monotonous nuclei to markedly pleomorphic nuclei, dense hyperchromatic or vesicular chromatin, and small or prominent nucleoli {24215322; 30994538; 30714449}. Tumour necrosis and mitotic activity are variable.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumour cells diffusely express CD99 in half of cases, often displaying a paranuclear dot-like pattern; PAX7 and NKX2-2 may be positive {29920735; 28643791; 32327700}. NKX3-1 is often expressed with variable results between laboratories {31972596; 33443863; 32327700; 38321863}. Focal dot-like staining for keratins can also be seen.

Differential diagnosis

Differential diagnoses include myoepitheliomas and ossifying fibromyxoid tumours, as well as osteosarcoma and osteoblastoma in the bones {29626598; 24993903; 30714449; 23001328; 25231439; 24758504; 24215322}.

Cytology

There is a single published case in which the FNAB showed atypical small to intermediate cells with a high N:C ratio, pale cytoplasm with granules, and mildly pleomorphic round nuclei with single nucleoli, embedded in myxohyaline granules. Stripped nuclei and occasional eosinophils were present in the background {38483612}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Genetic confirmation of NFATC2 rearrangement is required for the diagnosis. A distinctive unbalanced pattern of translocation is seen by break-apart FISH in > 90% of cases, with gain of the 5′ probe {31078563; 38254207; 36555836}. NFATC2 fusions are not specific, since identical fusions – without amplification of the involved translocation partners – are found in simple bone cysts {33316098; 32991339; 34081036; 36555836} and vascular malformations/haemangiomas {33316098; 32991339; 34081036; 36555836; 34310785}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: round to spindle cells, arranged in cords, nests, and trabeculae, with fibrohyaline stroma; presence of the fusion (either EWSR1 break-apart FISH with gain/amplification of the 5′ region or identification of the NFATC2 rearrangement).

Staging

The tumours are staged according to the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems.

Prognosis and prediction

The clinical course may include local recurrences and/or metastatic disease. Disease control was achieved in 11 of the 14 patients with EWSR1::NFATC2 sarcoma managed with surgical resection, with a median follow-up of 45 months. Lung, skin, and bone metastases have been reported and may occur as long as 10.5 years after the initial diagnosis; 6 patients died of disease, with follow-up ranging from 4 to 94 months (3 cases with EWSR1::NFATC2 fusion and 3 cases with FUS::NFATC2 fusion) {30994538}. Little to no histological response has been observed in patients treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy {31078563}. Temporary tumour control has been reported in isolated cases with pazopanib {37736255}. Outcome data of FUS::NFATC2 sarcomas are limited but seem to correlate with poorer outcomes in the few reported cases {32327700; 31049020}.
