1.2.11 Atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumour

Definition

Atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumour is a benign adipocytic neoplasm characterized by a variable admixture of atypical spindle cells, adipocytes, lipoblasts, and pleomorphic multinucleated cells, within a myxoid or collagenous matrix.

ICD-O coding

8857/0 Atypical spindle cell/ pleomorphic lipomatous tumour

ICD-11 coding

2E80 & XH4E98 Benign lipomatous neoplasm & Spindle cell lipoma

Related terminology

Not recommended: spindle cell liposarcoma; fibrosarcoma-like lipomatous neoplasm; dysplastic/anisometric lipoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumours arise more often in the subcutis than in deep soft tissues, and anatomical distribution is wide {27879515; 28877053; 24659226; 39335118; 32068239; 39434553; 33782225; 40405626; 30198097; 32068239; 38278611; 34958506; 30709444}. The atypical spindle cell lipomatous tumour (ASLT) subtype more frequently arises in the distal extremities (hands and feet) in contrast to the atypical pleomorphic lipomatous tumour (APLT) subtype {20228779; 27879515; 28877053; 33782225; 39335118; 31950475;40405626}. Intradermal or visceral locations are rare {27879515; 31265741; 32352578; 34790420}.

Clinical features

The tumour manifests as a persistent or enlarging soft tissue mass, nodule, or swelling {27879515; 28877053; 39335118}.

Epidemiology

Atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumour occurs predominantly in middle-aged adults, with a peak incidence in the sixth decade of life, but it can affect patients of any age (cases have been described in patients aged 6–89 years). The large majority of patients are > 30 years old. There is a slight male predominance {27879515; 28877053; 30198097; 32068239; 33782225; 40405626; 31501988}.

Etiology

While the majority of cases appear to be sporadic, rare examples have been reported in the context of Li–Fraumeni syndrome {34725851}.

Pathogenesis

Deletions or losses of 13q14, including RB1 and its flanking genes RCBTB2, DLEU1, and ITM2B, are typical findings {27879515; 28877053; 24659226; 30198097; 30370805; 20228779; 40405626; 33802620}. The APLT subtype frequently harbours TP53 (17p) alterations (mutations/deletions) in contrast to the ASLT subtype {40405626; 34725851}. Subsequent genetic aberrations lead to chromosomal instability–driven sarcomatous transformation in rare cases {38417627}. In addition, monosomy 7 has been reported {27879515; 18617058}.

Macroscopic appearance

Atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumours are unencapsulated, show a nodular or multinodular architecture, and demonstrate poorly defined margins. Sarcomatous transformation contains solid, nonfatty components. Tumour size is variable (range: 5–280 mm; median: 50–85 mm) {27879515; 28877053; 32068239}.

Histopathology

Atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumours have a lobulated or nodular growth pattern, often with areas of infiltrative growth {28877053; 33782225}. Histologically, two major subtypes can be distinguished: the ASLT {27879515; 24659226; 40405626} and the APLT {33782225; 28877053; 40405626}.

ASLT is characterized by variable proportions of well-differentiated adipocytes, atypical spindle cells, lipoblasts, and a collagenous and/or myxoid background with ropy collagen and mast cells {27879515; 24659226; 40405626; 31131655}. Cellularity varies widely, from low-cellularity tumours with mildly atypical spindle cells and abundant extracellular matrix to highly cellular tumours with mild to moderate atypia, lipoblasts, and scant extracellular matrix {24659226; 27879515; 23887155}. Mild to moderate cytonuclear atypia of the spindle cell component, characterized by enlarged hyperchromatic and irregular nuclei, is a consistent and essential diagnostic feature, although it may be focal. The adipocytic component consists predominantly of mature adipocytes and has often striking variation in cell size and shape with focal binucleation or multinucleation (dysplastic/anisometric lipoma–like morphological features) {30211727; 32631117; 30001242; 32439322; 32362169}. Foci of fat necrosis may be seen. Lipoblasts range from small, univacuolated or bivacuolated forms to large, multivacuolated cells.

APLT is distinguished by the presence of more bizarre, pleomorphic (multinucleated), and hyperchromatic cells throughout the lesion, together with multinucleated floret-like cells and/or pleomorphic lipoblasts. APLT demonstrates higher cellularity than typically seen in pleomorphic lipomas {28877053; 33782225; 40405626}. However, APLT and ASLT can show morphological overlap. Mitotic activity is low (generally < 1 per mm2), with rare atypical mitotic figures {28877053; 30084357; 29317234; 40405626}. Tumour necrosis is absent. Unusual morphological findings include hibernoma-like areas, pleomorphic hyalinizing angiectatic tumour-like features, and heterologous (metaplastic) differentiation, including smooth muscle, cartilaginous, and/or osseous elements {27879515; 30370805; 39533756; 35491657}. Extremely rare cases with unequivocal sarcomatous transformation (showing leiomyosarcoma-like, pleomorphic liposarcoma-like, or undifferentiated sarcoma-like sarcomatous components) have been described {35779129; 38417627}.

Variable expression of CD34, S100, and desmin is typical {27879515; 28877053}. Focal expression of MDM2 or CDK4 is rarely seen {27879515; 24659226; 20228779}. Loss of nuclear RB1 expression is observed in about 50–70% of cases {27879515; 28877053; 30198097; 24659226; 40405626}. Recent studies have demonstrated a good overall correlation between p53 immunohistochemistry (loss of expression or overexpression) and TP53 alterations, implying that p53 immunohistochemistry could be used to support the discrimination between ASLT and APLT {40405626}. Distinguishing between ASLT and APLT may become more clinically relevant if further observations confirm that APLTs have the potential to transform or dedifferentiate, although current data remain inconclusive and additional studies are needed.

Cytology

There are few reports; aspirations may show cells like those seen histopathologically, including mature mildly pleomorphic adipocytes in tissue fragments with a variable number of spindle cells with enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei and lipoblasts in myxoid to collagenous stroma. Additionally, atypical pleomorphic and multinucleated cells showing hyperchromasia are seen in the pleomorphic lipomatous tumour pattern {15637670; 36856555}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular studies have shown absence of MDM2 or CDK4 amplification, contrasting with atypical lipomatous tumour {28877053; 40405626}. Deletions or losses of 13q14, including RB1, have been identified in most cases {27879515; 28877053; 24659226; 30198097; 30370805; 20228779; 40405626; 33802620}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mature adipocytes in variable proportions showing variation in size and shape; atypical spindle cells with enlarged hyperchromatic and irregular nuclei; lipoblasts within a myxoid to collagenous matrix; pleomorphic multinucleated hyperchromatic cells in APLT.

Desirable: demonstration of lack of MDM2 amplification.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Atypical spindle cell / pleomorphic lipomatous tumours have a low rate of local recurrence (4–15%) for incompletely removed lesions, with no metastasis. Most patients will have an excellent prognosis if the lesion is completely excised {27879515; 28877053; 30198097; 33782225; 40405626}. The cases with sarcomatous transformation have not developed recurrence or metastasis so far {35779129; 38417627}.
