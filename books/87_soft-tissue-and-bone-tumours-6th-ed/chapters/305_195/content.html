3.0.5 Rosai–Dorfman disease

Definition

Rosai–Dorfman disease (RDD) is a histiocytosis characterized by nodal or extranodal accumulation of large, S100-positive histiocytes/macrophages that commonly exhibit emperipolesis.

ICD-O coding

9749/3 Rosai–Dorfman disease

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Acceptable: Rosai–Dorfman–Destombes disease.

Not recommended: sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

RDD most frequently involves lymph nodes, particularly in the cervical region. Extranodal disease occurs in 43% of cases, most often involving the head and neck, skin, and the CNS {2180012; 8491488; 17241598; 17325475; 26966089}. RDD of soft tissue may present as unifocal or multifocal lesions, most often involving subcutaneous adipose tissue in the extremities, trunk, head and neck, and retroperitoneum {2180012; 17241598; 17325475; 34965285; 20850691}. Bone involvement is found in 5–10% of cases, most often affecting craniofacial bones and long bones of the appendicular skeleton, particularly the proximal tibia, followed by the spine, pelvis, and tarsal bones. RDD may rarely involve the sacrum, patella, ribs, sternum, clavicle, scapula, and carpal and phalangeal bones. Bone lesions are most often multifocal, intramedullary lytic lesions centred in the metaphysis {2180012; 34965285; 20679880; 18246343}. Simultaneous involvement of nodal or extranodal sites is common in bone and soft tissue RDD {2180012; 34965285}.

Clinical features

The typical presentation is that of painless, slow-growing lymphadenopathy or extranodal soft tissue mass, with accompanying B symptoms in one third of patients. In cases of extranodal RDD, symptoms related to involved extranodal sites may be seen {2180012; 29720485}. Pain and swelling are the most common symptoms in RDD of bone {2180012; 34965285; 20679880}. See #56903Table 5.01.

Epidemiology

Some studies have estimated the overall prevalence at around 1 per 200 000 people in the USA, with a higher prevalence in children {29720485}. The global incidence of RDD is unknown, but children and young adults are most affected {23863552; 36960255}. While all ethnic groups are susceptible, the disease is more common in patients of African descent. A recent meta-analysis of 149 cases from the African continent reported that most occurred in children < 10 years of age, with a median age at diagnosis of 26 years {37790150}. Men are 3.2 times as frequently affected as women, except with cutaneous RDD, which is more prevalent in Asian women {17241598; 17325475; 37790150; 23863552}, and bone and soft tissue RDD, which shows a slight female predominance {34965285}. Nodal RDD usually arises in the second and third decades of life, whereas extranodal RDD, including bone and soft tissue RDD, arises in the fifth decade of life {2180012; 23863552}.

Etiology

The etiology is unknown. Rare inherited disorders predispose to familial RDD (see #56903Table 5.01), including disorders associated with germline variants in the nucleoside transporter gene SLC29A3 (H syndrome / Faisalabad histiocytosis), and FAS deficiency with heterozygous germline variants in FAS (TNFRSF6) (autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome) {20140240; 24172204; 15958855}. These variants are not known to be associated with sporadic RDD.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is incompletely understood. Approximately 50% of cases carry gain-of-function mutations in MAPK/ERK pathway genes, including KRAS, NRAS, MAP2K1, ARAF, CSF1R, suggesting that at least a proportion of cases of RDD represent a neoplastic disorder {26566875; 26922062; 28597077; 28664935; 29236635; 32601132}. A rare case of primary intraosseous RDD with BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) has been reported {39667566}.

Macroscopic appearance

The macroscopic appearance is that of solitary or multiple mass lesions in soft tissue or intramedullary lytic lesions in bone.

Histopathology

Extranodal RDD exhibits histiocytic clusters and a lymphoplasmacytic background, which impart an alternating light and dark pattern at low magnification, resembling the architectural features of nodal RDD despite the absence of sinuses. The distinctive large histiocytes of RDD possess round nuclei and prominent nucleoli, surrounded by abundant pale cytoplasm with poorly defined borders. Emperipolesis – the presence of intact haematopoietic cells (typically lymphocytes, but also plasma cells, neutrophils, and erythrocytes) within the histiocyte cytoplasm – is characteristic of RDD. Cytological atypia, mitoses, and multinucleation are infrequent. The microenvironment contains small B and T cells and numerous polytypic plasma cells. Neutrophils and neutrophilic microabscesses may be present, and eosinophils are inconspicuous. Fibrosis may be prominent. Inconspicuous emperipolesis, admixed foamy histiocytes, and storiform fibrosis may be present, especially in RDD involving soft tissue, making the diagnosis challenging. Perivascular plasma cells are a helpful feature in the diagnosis of extranodal RDD {2180012; 34781270; 29720485; 26966089; 34965285}.

Immunohistochemistry

RDD histiocytes are immunoreactive for S100, which also highlights emperipolesis. They are positive for OCT2 {2180012; 33177341; 37167084}, frequently positive for phosphorylated ERK and cyclin D1 {28664935; 31172509}, and variably positive for histiocytic markers such as CD68, CD163, and PU.1. They lack CD1a and CD207 (langerin) {28664935; 26966089; 29720485; 36239684}.

Cytology

Large histiocytes with emperipolesis associated with plasma cells and lymphocytes are characteristic. S100 positivity is confirmatory {22438629; 31942098; 27673407}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

For suspected familial RDD, the workup should include SLC29A3 and FAS (TNFRSF6) germline variant analysis {24172204; 20140240; 15958855}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: large histiocytes with round nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and abundant pale cytoplasm, often with emperipolesis; histiocytes are positive for S100, which also highlights the emperipolesis; abundant plasma cells in the background.

Desirable: expression of OCT2 and cyclin D1 in the absence of CD1a, CD207 (langerin), and ALK.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The disease often resolves spontaneously. Surgical intervention is warranted if the upper airway, spinal cord, or end organs are compromised. Persistent disease or recurrence may require further treatment. In such patients, MAPK/ERK pathway–targeting MEK inhibitors may be helpful {28664935}. Immune dysregulation and involvement of select extranodal sites (kidney, tracheobronchial tract / lung, and liver) contribute to an adverse prognosis; disease-specific death is reported in 5–11% of patients {2180012}.
