1.12.13 GLI1-altered mesenchymal tumours

Definition

GLI1-altered mesenchymal tumours have variable clinical behaviour, often a nested epithelioid morphology, and a rich capillary network; they are driven by fusions or amplification of GLI1.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2F7C & XH52J1 Neoplasms of uncertain behaviour of connective or other soft tissue & GLI1-altered epithelioid soft tissue tumour.

Related terminology

Acceptable: nested glomoid neoplasm; GLI1-altered epithelioid soft tissue neoplasm.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

GLI1-altered mesenchymal tumours most commonly occur in the head and neck region, especially the tongue, and the gastrointestinal tract, particularly the stomach {15111311; 26980027; 31934916; 34779913; 35933574; 38934567}. GLI1-amplified tumours show a predilection for the digits {38934567}. GLI1-altered mesenchymal tumours rarely occur in skin and subcutis {36316249}, bone {31567194; 37992966}, and genitourinary and gynaecological tracts {34907995; 39688667; 36222065}.

Clinical features

Presenting symptoms depend on the primary site. Tumours in the skin, soft tissue, and head and neck tend to present as solitary painless masses, and oral cavity lesions sometimes ulcerate the overlying mucosa. Gastrointestinal tract tumours can present with abdominal pain, melaena, or small bowel obstruction {35968961}.

Epidemiology

GLI1-altered mesenchymal tumours are rare neoplasms, with fewer than 200 reported examples. They have been described across a wide age range, from infants to elderly adults, with a peak in occurrence during the fourth and fifth decades of life {38934567; 39442668} and equal prevalence described in male and female patients {38934567; 39442668}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

GLI1-altered mesenchymal neoplasms are driven by GLI1 fusions or amplifications. The GLI1 protein is an effector of the sonic hedgehog signalling pathway {11001584}.

The most common GLI1 fusion partners are ACTB, MALAT1, PTCH1 {29309307; 31567194; 36395474; 37992966; 38934567}, and (less commonly) TXNIP {36395474; 39442668} and FOXO4 {34907995; 39688667}. The latter fusion appears to occur with predilection in the kidney.

Similar gene fusions have been reported in other related or distinct entities, such as ACTB::GLI1 in pericytoma {15111311}, MALAT1::GLI1 in plexiform fibromyxoma {27101025} and gastroblastoma {28731043}, and GLI1 complex structural variants in enteric tumours with biphasic epithelial and mesenchymal components {35968961; 37489101}.

GLI1-amplified tumours typically show coamplification of the nearby genes on 12q13-15, including DDIT3, MDM2, CDK4, and STAT6 {31189998; 38934567}. These alterations result in protein overexpression of these genes by immunohistochemistry {36693363; 37940743}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range from < 10 mm to > 200 mm. They grossly show red to tan cut surfaces with evident intratumoural septa and, sometimes, haemorrhage or microcystic spaces.

Histopathology

GLI1-altered mesenchymal tumours are typically composed of large solid lobules separated by irregularly thick fibrous septa. Most show nests of epithelioid cells with pale to clear eosinophilic cytoplasm and uniform, ovoid nuclei, separated by delicate branching capillary channels, a morphological pattern described under the rubric of nested glomoid neoplasm {29309307; 31189998; 36395474}. Less commonly, the tumour cells are more spindled, with storiform or whorled architecture. Other recurrent histological features include multinucleated tumour cells, microcystic change, myxoid stroma, and sieve-like and reticular growth. Commonly, there is protrusion of lobules of tumour cells into dilated vascular spaces {31934916; 38934567}.

GLI1-amplified tumours show a wider morphological spectrum than GLI1-rearranged tumours. Most appear lobulated and nested, but some are overtly malignant epithelioid, spindle cell, or round cell sarcomas {38934567}. Some GLI1-amplified sarcomas exhibit marked cytological atypia, an unusual feature in GLI1-rearranged tumours. Dedifferentiated liposarcoma with GLI1-coamplification can show regions morphologically identical to GLI1-altered mesenchymal neoplasms {38621503}, but an associated component of conventional liposarcoma excludes the diagnosis of GLI1-altered mesenchymal tumour.

Immunohistochemistry for GLI1 shows > 90% sensitivity and as much as 98% specificity for tumours driven by GLI1 alterations {36693363; 37940743}. GLI1-amplified tumours typically show expression of MDM2, CDK4, and (less commonly) DDIT3 and STAT6, because of coamplification of genes at the 12q13-q15 locus {31189998; 36693363; 37940743; 38934567}. Most tumours express CD56, while 20–30% express S100 and broad-spectrum keratins {36395474; 38934567}.

Cytology

Aspirates show high cellularity with monotonous epithelioid-to-plasmacytoid cells {35514197}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

GLI1 fusions can be confirmed by either RNA sequencing or GLI1 FISH (including using commercial DDIT3 probes located just 3′ to GLI1) {35933574}. GLI1 gene amplification can be detected by FISH using probes for the nearby coamplified genes, such as DDIT3, CDK4, and MDM2 {34779913; 36395474}. Immunohistochemistry for GLI1 is a sensitive and specific surrogate for both GLI1 amplification and fusions {36693363}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: lobular architecture, spindle cell or nested epithelioid growth, prominent admixed branching capillary channels; GLI1 rearrangement, amplification, or protein overexpression.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

GLI1-amplified tumours overall behave more aggressively than GLI1-rearranged tumours and account for most patient deaths {38934567; 39442668}. Tumours with GLI1 fusions can be relatively indolent even in the metastatic setting, with some patients experiencing protracted clinical courses of decades {31931637; 37992966}. Survival analyses to date show that tumour size (≥ 50 mm), mitotic index (≥ 4 mitoses/10 HPF equivalent to ≥ 2 mitoses/mm2), and necrosis were risk factors for metastasis {38934567}. The most common site of metastasis is the lung, followed by bone, lymph nodes, liver, and brain {38934567}.
