2.7.6 Lipoma and hibernoma of bone

Definition

Lipoma and hibernoma of bone are benign neoplasms composed of white adipocytes (lipoma) or brown adipocytes (hibernoma) that arise within or on the surface of bone.

8880/0 Hibernoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Lipomas commonly arise in the calcaneus and metaphysis of long tubular bones, especially the femur, tibia, and humerus, and infrequently in the skull, pelvis, vertebrae, sacrum, mandible, maxilla, and ribs; approximately 70% involve the lower limb {19386471; 30268544; 12652336; 39532321; 38310971; 37088329; 36436422; 36368613; 33561532; 32205303}. Parosteal lipoma develops on the diaphysis of long tubular bones, especially the femur, radius, humerus, and tibia {29128312; 40255418; 34797330}. Hibernoma often affects the axial skeleton (spine, pelvis) {21207023; 31247161; 37099409; 34779887}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Lipoma may be asymptomatic (30%) or produce aching pain (70%) {21207023}. Parosteal lipoma may be painful and can produce a palpable mass {29128312}. Hibernoma is usually asymptomatic and detected during evaluation for an unrelated disorder {21207023}.

Imaging

Intraosseous lipomas occur mainly (71%) in the lower extremity (calcaneus) and exhibit low density on plain radiographs, high signal on T1-weighted images, and low signal on fluid-sensitive fat-suppressed images {12652336}. Calcifications in the well-defined margin and within the lesion are seen as high density on radiographs and CT, and they show signal void on MRI. Fat necrosis and cystic degeneration occur more frequently in the calcaneus and have high signal on fluid-sensitive sequences. As lipomas in the calcaneus often have cystic changes and solitary cysts in the calcaneus are thought to develop fatty changes when maturing, the two can be impossible to differentiate radiologically {34459937}. Parosteal lipomas display the same fatty characteristics on imaging, but the lesions are superficial to and in continuity with the periosteum {37083978}. Bone formation can be seen in the centre of the lesion at the surface of cortical bone. Underlying cortical bone and bone marrow are not involved. Hibernoma occurs mainly in the axial skeleton but has been reported in the appendicular skeleton. It is characterized by a combination of imaging features: high uptake of 18F-FDG on PET/CT, uptake on bone scintigraphy, sclerotic poorly defined appearance on CT, low signal on T1-weighted images (small areas of high signal intensity representing macroscopic fat may be seen), slightly increased heterogeneous signal on fluid-sensitive MRI sequences, and mild enhancement on gadolinium chelate–enhanced MRI {24705581; 37099409}.

Epidemiology

Lipoma is rare and accounts for < 0.1% of primary bone tumours. The patient age ranges from the second to eighth decades of life (mean age: 50 years), but the peak age at discovery is usually in the fourth to fifth decades of life. Male individuals are reported to be more frequently affected than female individuals (M:F ratio: ~1.3:1) {29854348}. Parosteal lipoma accounts for 15% of bone lipomas and develops during the fifth to sixth decades of life. Hibernoma is a very rare tumour that accounts for 1.6% of all adipocytic tumours. Reported cases show a mean age of diagnosis of 38 years (range 40–80 years,) and a female predominance {21207023; 37099409; 34033341}.

Etiology

Unknown. It has been hypothesized that at least a subset of intraosseous lipomas actually represent fatty replacements of simple bone cysts {33005976}.

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is largely unknown. Unlike hibernomas of soft tissue, chromosomal microarray analysis of intraosseous hibernomas has not shown significant copy-number variations across the genome or on 11q, harbouring AIP and MEN1 {37099409; 21078971}.

Macroscopic appearance

Intramedullary lipoma is well defined, soft but sometimes gritty, and yellow; tumours containing brown adipocytes are yellowish-brown in colour. Tumours are usually 30–50 mm in size; exceptional cases are > 100 mm {29854348}. The bone surrounding the tumour is often sclerotic. Parosteal lipoma is 40–100 mm in greatest dimension, well defined, soft, and yellow. Some cases contain gritty spicules of bone or firm nodules of cartilage in the base or scattered throughout the mass.

Histopathology

Lipoma is composed of lobules of mature-appearing adipocytes {12652336; 29854348}. Delicate trabeculae of woven and lamellar bone may be present throughout the tumour. The adipocytes have a single, large, clear cytoplasmic vacuole that displaces the crescent-shaped nucleus to the periphery. Some tumours may demonstrate necrosis with foamy macrophages, fibrosis, and dystrophic calcifications. Cytological atypia is absent. Parosteal lipoma is a well-defined mass of white fat. Its base, adjacent to the cortex, may have bone trabeculae or hypocellular hyaline cartilage, which undergoes endochondral ossification. Hibernoma is composed of large polygonal cells with distinct cell membranes, finely vacuolated and eosinophilic cytoplasm, and central or paracentral small bland nuclei with prominent scalloping. Growth around trabecular bone is observed, and cells may be admixed with haematopoietic elements {21207023; 24705581; 37099409}.

Immunohistochemistry

Adipocytes are positive for S100 and adipophilin, and negative for CD68, CD163, keratin AE1/AE3, brachyury, SOX10, ERG, and CD1a. Hibernomas strongly express CD10 and the brown-fat marker UCP1 {37099409; 21078971; 31114671; 33406387}.

Differential diagnosis

Bone marrow infarction with dystrophic calcification is distinguished from lipoma by the presence of pre-existing necrotic bone throughout the lesion and the lack of a discrete mass on imaging studies.

The histological differential diagnosis of hibernoma includes metastatic carcinoma, Erdheim–Chester disease, intraosseous lipoma with fat necrosis, and benign notochordal cell tumour {37099409}.

Cytology

Cytology shows adipocytes with vacuolated cytoplasm; there is no cytological atypia.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bone tumour with compatible imaging; lipoma of bone: aggregates of mature-appearing adipocytes; parosteal lipoma: aggregates of mature-appearing adipocytes, presenting as a bone tumour on the surface of the bone; hibernoma of bone: aggregates of brown fat cells.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of lipoma and hibernoma of bone is excellent {29128312; 37099409}. Symptomatic intraosseous tumours are curetted, and parosteal lipoma is marginally excised. Recurrence rates are extremely low.
