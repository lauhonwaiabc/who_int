1.2.6 Angiolipoma

Definition

Angiolipoma is a benign subcutaneous neoplasm consisting of mature adipocytes intermingled with clusters of small, thin-walled vessels that often contain fibrin thrombi.

ICD-O coding

8861/0 Angiolipoma NOS

ICD-11 coding

2E80.0Y & XH3C77 Other specified lipoma and Angiolipoma NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Angiolipomas most commonly involve the extremities, with approximately 50% arising in the forearm, followed by the trunk {35668116}. Rare intraosseous angiolipomas have been described {36368613; 37363475}. Intramuscular haemangiomas and the so-called angiolipoma of parenchymal organs or of the CNS are different lesions that have larger vessels and should not be confused with angiolipoma {29614355; 7356383}.

Clinical features

Angiolipomas most frequently present as multiple, small, tender/painful subcutaneous nodules {7026412}.

Epidemiology

Angiolipomas are common, representing 5–17% of all lipomas {25621058}, and affect a wide age range, most commonly from the second to sixth decades of life. There is a male predominance {7026412; 35668116}. An increased familial incidence has been described in approximately 5% of cases, with an autosomal dominant mode of inheritance {17511936}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Angiolipomas typically have a normal karyotype with only rare cases showing rearrangement of chromosome 13 {9130991; 7515663}. The majority (80%) have low frequency PRKD2 variants. PRKD proteins play a role in angiogenesis {28139834}.

Macroscopic appearance

Angiolipomas appear as well-circumscribed yellowish to reddish nodules.

Histopathology

Angiolipomas are composed of mature adipocytes and clusters of branching, capillary-sized vessels embedded in a collagenous stroma. The vessels often contain fibrin thrombi. The vascularity is more prominent at the periphery of the tumour {7026412}. The proportion of adipocytes and vessels is variable, and some lesions are almost completely composed of vascular channels (cellular angiolipoma) {2294783}.

Cytology

Aspirations show the same findings of lipoma, with tissue fragments of adipocytes containing a single large, clear cytoplasmic vacuole with a compressed rim of cytoplasm and a small nucleus with an inconspicuous nucleolus. In addition, small vessels are increased in number at least focally and show fibrin thrombi, best seen in cell block material {12920746}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: mature adipose tissue; variable clusters of capillaries; often with fibrin thrombi.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Angiolipomas are benign and show no tendency for local recurrence.
