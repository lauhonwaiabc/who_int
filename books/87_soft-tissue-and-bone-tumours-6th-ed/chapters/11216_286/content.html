1.12.16 Pseudoendocrine sarcoma

Definition

Pseudoendocrine sarcoma is a malignant neoplasm of uncertain lineage, predominantly arising in paravertebral soft tissues, morphologically resembling a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumour (NET), but lacking expression of keratins and neuroendocrine markers, and harbouring CTNNB1 variants.

ICD-O coding

None

ICD-11 coding

2B5Y Other specified malignant mesenchymal neoplasms

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Pseudoendocrine sarcoma shows a strong predilection for paravertebral soft tissue {34081037; 40513989}, although rarely it can occur in other soft tissue sites such as the buttock and thigh.

Clinical features

Patients tend to present with enlarging masses evolving over months to years, some of which are painful {34081037; 40513989}.

Epidemiology

Pseudoendocrine sarcoma is very rare, with about 40 reported examples in the literature to date {34081037; 35297787; 36564514; 37953374; 39093425; 39362706; 40513989}. Tumours tend to occur in older adults, with a peak described in the sixth to seventh decade of life {34081037; 40513989}, and with a slight male predominance {34081037; 40513989}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

To date, all sequenced examples of pseudoendocrine sarcoma have harboured CTNNB1 variants, most commonly in codons 33 and 37 {40513989}. These variants disrupt the N-terminal domain of the protein, encompassing the GSK3B phosphorylation sites and/or the ubiquitination motif, leading to activation of the WNT/β-catenin signalling pathway {15829978; 34081037; 35297787; 36564514; 37953374; 39093425; 39362706; 40513989}. Some tumours have also harboured non-recurrent copy-number alterations {34081037; 40513989}.

Methylation profiling analysis has shown epigenetic overlap with solid pseudopapillary neoplasm of the pancreas {40513989}. However, gene expression profiling demonstrates significant differences between these two CTNNB1-mutated tumours {40513989}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumours range from 20 mm to 190 mm. They grossly show lobular, tan/white cut surfaces, and they sometimes erode adjacent vertebral bones.

Histopathology

Pseudoendocrine sarcoma shows lobular architecture and can infiltrate and efface adjacent non-neoplastic structures including vertebral bone. Within larger lobules, nested and trabecular patterns are present. The neoplastic cells have pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and monomorphic round nuclei with speckled chromatin. Mitotic activity is usually low, although rare examples exhibit more than 20 mitoses/2 mm2 {34081037}. Necrosis is typically absent. Additional recurrent findings include pseudoglandular architecture, extracellular hyaline globules, thin hyaline cords or septa, psammomatous calcifications, aggregates of foamy macrophages, and interstitial haemorrhage. Rare examples have shown myxoid stroma, microcystic or pseudopapillary architecture, lumen-like structures and rosettes, cytoplasmic clearing, and bone metaplasia {15829978; 40513989}.

Immunohistochemistry demonstrates nuclear β-catenin expression in almost all cases, consistent with underlying activating CTNNB1 mutations. Rarely, variant-positive cases show only diffuse membranous β-catenin expression. About 50% of cases express S100, which can be diffuse, and 35% express desmin, usually to a more limited extent. Keratins and specific neuroendocrine markers INSM1, synaptophysin, and chromogranin are all uniformly negative; however, the nonspecific neuroendocrine marker CD56 is commonly expressed. GLUT1 is also frequently positive, although data are limited. A small subset express CD34, CD99, brachyury, and AR.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of a CTNNB1 alteration may be helpful in select cases.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nested and trabecular architecture; uniform epithelioid cells with round nuclei and speckled chromatin; nuclear β-catenin (most cases); negative keratins and neuroendocrine markers.

Desirable: demonstration of a CTNNB1 alteration in select cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Among tumours with > 2 years of follow-up, about 50% have recurred locally, and recurrences can be locally destructive {34081037; 40513989}. About 20% of tumours have given rise to distant metastases, most commonly to lung and rarely to regional lymph nodes and liver {34081037; 40513989}. While one highly mitotic tumour gave rise to lung metastases {34081037}, some tumours that metastasized showed low mitotic indices {34081037; 40513989}. One tumour showed a 60% reduction in tumour size in response to radiation therapy, while other reported examples have not responded to radiotherapy or chemotherapy; however, available data are limited {34081037; 40513989}. To date, there are no documented deaths due to disease.
