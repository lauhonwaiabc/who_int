1.12.3 OGT-rearranged mesenchymal neoplasm

Definition

OGT-rearranged mesenchymal neoplasms are benign mesenchymal neoplasms with a predilection for acral locations, composed of epithelioid and spindle cells arranged in cords and nests within a variably hyalinized or myxoid stroma, and with OGT gene fusions. This lesion was formerly known as OGT-rearranged acral mesenchymal neoplasm.

Related terminology

Acceptable: myoepithelioma-like hyalinizing epithelioid tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

OGT-rearranged mesenchymal neoplasms have a predilection for acral locations {31567281; 32649321; 33534222; 36063135}. A minority of cases arise in more proximal parts of extremities or limb girdles {33455033; 33506328; 38816310}.

Clinical features

Most cases present as a slow-growing subcutaneous painless mass. MRI tends to show low to intermediate T1-weighted signals and high T2-weighted signals with heterogeneous, intermediate to hyperintense contrast enhancement {31567281; 36593918}.

Epidemiology

Only 9 molecularly confirmed cases have been reported, with a female predilection (M:F ratio: 1:2). All cases affect adults, with a wide age range {33534222}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

All tumours harbour a fusion involving the OGT gene, which encodes O-linked N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) transferase. The fusion partners reported include transcription factor or coactivator genes FOXO1 (3′), FOXO3 (3′), FOXO4 (5′ or 3′), and CITED1 (3′). The tumorigenic mechanism is unknown. The chimeric protein products are predicted to include nearly full lengths of both fusion partners, suggesting that the tumorigenesis might be the result of excessive O-GlcNAcylation of the transcription factors/coactivator (the fusion partners) facilitated by the genetic fusion {31567281; 33534222}.

Macroscopic appearance

OGT-rearranged mesenchymal neoplasms are well-defined and may appear lobulated. They are often white to tan and soft or elastic, sometimes with a glistening cut surface. Most tumours measure 10–50 mm.

Histopathology

The tumours are well-circumscribed and usually lobulated, with alternating moderately cellular and hypocellular areas. The more cellular areas can have somewhat myxoid stroma, while the hypocellular areas tend to be hyalinized or fibromyxoid, sometimes imparting a two-toned appearance. In most cases, the tumour cells are predominantly epithelioid or plump and ovoid, with a minor component of spindle cells. They are sometimes arranged in cords, nests, reticular structures, or perivascular concentric whorls. A subset of cases with OGT::FOXO1 or OGT::FOXO3 fusion have vacuolated or signet ring–like cells. The tumour cells have a small to moderate amount of eosinophilic cytoplasm, relatively uniform nuclei, delicate chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli. Mild to moderate nuclear pleomorphism is observed in few cases. Mitotic activity is very low, and tumour necrosis is absent. Most tumours harbour scattered capillaries, small, branching curvilinear vessels or larger thick-walled vessels, quite often with perivascular hyalinization focally.

Immunohistochemistry

All cases are focally to diffusely positive for CD34, and some are also positive for EMA (usually focally). A small subset of cases show varying immunoreactivity to keratins (AE1/AE3) and/or ERG. Otherwise, they have been negative for a variety of endothelial, myoepithelial, nerve sheath, myogenic, or perineurial markers, while SMARCB1 (INI1) is retained.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular detection of OGT fusions is required for diagnostic confirmation. Fusions between OGT and FOXO4 or CITED1 are probably the results of intrachromosomal rearrangements, and therefore can fail to be detected with a dual-colour break-apart FISH study {33534222; 33455033}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: cords or nests of epithelioid cells within a hyalinized or fibromyxoid stroma; OGT gene rearrangement.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

OGT-rearranged mesenchymal neoplasms are benign tumours, with no postexcision recurrence or metastasis reported so far despite the limited follow-up data.
