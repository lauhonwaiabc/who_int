1.3.19 Angiofibroma of soft tissue

Definition

Angiofibroma of soft tissue is a benign fibroblastic neoplasm composed of uniform spindle cells with abundant fibromyxoid stroma and a prominent network of branching, thin-walled blood vessels.

ICD-O coding

9160/0 Angiofibroma

ICD-11 coding

EE6Y & XH1JJ2 Other specified fibromatous disorders of skin and soft tissue & Angiofibroma NOS

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Angiofibroma of soft tissue typically arises in the lower extremity, sometimes involving or adjacent to the knee. Unusual anatomical locations include the upper extremity, back, abdominal wall, pelvic cavity, and breast. The tumours are often subcutaneous but may be intramuscular and deep {22301504; 26845637; 28639284; 36876749; 36860189}. EWSR1::GFI1B fusion-positive tumours may have a predilection for the neck region {37680034; 40095263}.

Clinical features

The lesion presents most often as a slow-growing painless mass {22301504; 26845637; 28639284}. Preoperative duration may be long {22301504}. Most lesions are sharply demarcated; however, infiltration into adjacent structures may be detected on imaging {22301504; 28639284}.

Epidemiology

Angiofibroma of soft tissue is rare and affects predominantly middle-aged adults, with a peak incidence in the sixth decade of life, but individuals of any age may be affected {22301504; 26845637; 28639284}. There is a slight female predominance {22301504; 26845637; 28639284; 36876749; 36860189}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Soft tissue angiofibromas have near-diploid karyotypes with a recurrent t(5;8)(p15;q13), which results in the fusion of AHRR in chromosome band 5p15 and NCOA2 in 8q13 {22337624}. The AHRR::NCOA2 chimera found in 60–80% of the tumours and AHRR::NCOA3 chimera reported in two tumours include the basic helix-loop-helix and PAS domains of AHRR and the two transcriptional activation domains of NCOA2 or NCOA3 {22337624; 26845637; 28639284; 36860189}. The AHRR::NCOA2/AHRR::NCOA3 chimera is expected to upregulate the AHR target genes, such as CYP1A1. EWSR1::GFI1B fusions have been reported in 5 cases {37680034; 40095263}.

Macroscopic appearance

Tumour diameter of published cases ranges from 12 to 120 mm. Most lesions are well-demarcated, nodular or multinodular solid tumours of variable size, with a white to yellow, often glistening, cut surface. Cystic or haemorrhagic areas may be present {22301504; 26845637; 28639284; 36860189}.

Histopathology

Angiofibroma of soft tissue is composed of uniform bland spindle cells in a variably myxoid to collagenous stroma with a prominent vascular network. The lesions are mostly well circumscribed. The architecture is vaguely lobulated, with alternating myxoid and collagenous areas and regional variation in cellularity. The neoplastic spindle cells have inconspicuous palely eosinophilic cytoplasm and short ovoid or tapering nuclei, with irregular nuclear contours, fine chromatin, and indistinct nucleoli. Cytological atypia and nuclear hyperchromasia are generally absent; occasional cases show degenerative nuclear atypia. The prominent vascular network is composed of innumerable small thin-walled branching blood vessels evenly distributed throughout the lesion. Less prominent medium-sized or large blood vessels with variably thick walls are also usually present. Common additional features include perivascular collagen deposition and marked hyalinization or fibrinoid necrosis of medium-sized vessel walls. Degenerative changes may be focally present, including haemorrhage or aggregates of foamy histiocytes. Multinucleated tumour cells can be prominent, and nuclear palisading may be observed. A variably dense inflammatory infiltrate comprising mainly small lymphocytes is usually present, sometimes in a perivascular distribution. By immunohistochemistry, the neoplastic cells are typically positive for CYP1A1 and ER, and variably express desmin and EMA. Immunohistochemistry for desmin sometimes highlights the dendritic processes of the tumour cells. CD163-positive cells are usually diffusely distributed throughout the lesion {22301504; 28639284; 26845637; 36876749; 36860189}.

Cytology

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Detection of AHRR or NCOA2 rearrangement or AHRR::NCOA2 gene fusion (or other rare variant fusion) supports the diagnosis, but it is generally not required.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: bland and uniform short spindle cells; variably myxoid or collagenous stroma; prominent small, thin-walled branching blood vessels.

Desirable: AHRR or NCOA2 gene rearrangements in select cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Angiofibroma of soft tissue pursues a benign clinical course, with rare local recurrences and no evident metastatic potential {22301504; 26845637; 28639284}.
