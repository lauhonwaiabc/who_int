1.12.7 Angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma

Definition

Angiomatoid fibrous histiocytoma (AFH) is an intermediate, rarely metastasizing neoplasm of uncertain lineage, most commonly arising in the subcutis and characterized by varying proportions of epithelioid, ovoid, or spindle cells arranged in a nodular and often syncytial growth pattern, with haemorrhagic pseudovascular spaces, and (frequently) a peripheral fibrous pseudocapsule with a prominent lymphoplasmacytic cuff.

Related terminology

Not recommended: angiomatoid malignant fibrous histiocytoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

AFH most commonly arises in the subcutis or deep dermis of the extremities, followed by the trunk and the head and neck. Approximately two thirds arise in areas of normal lymph node distribution, such as the antecubital and popliteal fossae or the inguinal, axillary, and supraclavicular areas {10571514}. Rare cases occur in the female genital tract, bone, brain, retroperitoneum, and mediastinum {21822206; 20090505; 18300800}.

Clinical features

Typically, patients present with a superficial, painless, slow-growing, and variably fluctuant soft tissue mass. Occasional patients have an associated paraneoplastic syndrome of anaemia, fever, and weight loss, related to tumour cytokine production {7813103}. Ultrasound and MRI may show a nonspecific, predominantly solid mass. Demonstration of intralesional blood-filled cystic spaces with fluid-fluid levels and an enhancing fibrous pseudocapsule with haemosiderin deposition may suggest the diagnosis of AFH {26162466}.

Epidemiology

AFH is a rare soft tissue neoplasm, accounting for 0.3% of all soft tissue tumours {23372863}. There is no significant sex predilection described. A wide age range is reported, from birth {2845834} to the ninth decade of life, with a peak in cases occurring during the first two decades of life {2174650; 17724745; 27166030}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

More than 90% of AFH harbour t(2;22)(q33;q12), resulting in an EWSR1::CREB1 fusion gene {17724745; 27166030; 18094413}. Rare cases have an alternative t(12;22)(q13;q12), creating an EWSR1::ATF1 fusion {17188428}. Even less commonly seen is t(12;16)(q13;p11), resulting in FUS::ATF1 {12218455} or EWSR1::CREM {31305268}. The first two fusion genes are also seen in clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue, but unlike in clear cell sarcoma, there is no downstream activation of the microphthalmia transcription pathway in AFH {17188428}. FET-CREB family fusions have also been seen in some intracranial myxoid mesenchymal tumours {28281318; 28009602} and malignant epithelioid and round cell neoplasms, with a predilection for intra-abdominal, head and neck, thoracic, and female genital tract sites {32770123; 39249507; 39031200; 37097347}.

Macroscopic appearance

These tumours appear grossly as small, multinodular, firm to cystic masses, with a wide size range (7–120 mm, median: 20 mm) {228836; 2174650}. The cut surface of the lesion commonly shows multilocular blood-filled spaces, simulating a haematoma or intranodal haemorrhage, with solid areas that are greyish-yellow to white {228836; 2174650; 25927151}.

Histopathology

These tumours are usually well circumscribed and lobulated, with several morphological components. These include (1) sheets or fascicles of spindled, epithelioid, or histiocytic-like cells arranged in nodules, containing a moderate amount of eosinophilic cytoplasm and bland vesicular nuclei; (2) non-endothelial–lined and blood-filled pseudoangiomatous spaces surrounded by tumour cells; (3) a thick, fibrous pseudocapsule with haemosiderin deposition; and (4) a pericapsular lymphoplasmacytic cell cuff with germinal centres. Nearly one third of tumours lack pseudoangiomatous spaces and are solid, simulating a lymph node. Mitotic figures are usually few, although atypical mitotic figures may be present. Focal necrosis and pleomorphic cells, including multinucleated giant cells, may be observed {25755773}. The most common morphological pattern is the spindle cell type, followed by the pleomorphic cell type {25927151; 27166030; 24043718}. Cellular atypia and an increased number of mitotic figures are not associated with aggressive behaviour {2174650}. Some tumours contain myxoid stroma {24503754; 24043718; 27166030}. A rare subtype of AFH is characterized by CD99-positive small blue round cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and scant eosinophilic cytoplasm, mimicking an undifferentiated round cell sarcoma {10571514; 11012987}.

Immunohistochemistry

Most are focally or diffusely immunoreactive for desmin and SOX9. Membranous CD99 staining is common. Tumour cells are consistently negative for MYOD1, myogenin, keratins, S100, and CD34. Pan-TRK, EMA, ALK, TLE1, synaptophysin, and CD68 are variably positive {10571514; 11012987; 31567275; 29877921; 32819737; 38407279; 38053271}.

Cytology

Cytology smears show moderate to high cellularity, with loosely cohesive tissue fragments of varying size, some containing vessels that give them a whorled or ball-like structure, with ovoid to spindle histiocytoid cells with mild to moderate nuclear pleomorphism including folding of the nucleus, small nucleoli, and moderate to abundant vacuolated cytoplasm. A variable number of haemosiderophages, lymphocytes, and plasma cells are in the background {22045622}. The differential diagnosis is broad and assisted by immunohistochemistry for ALK, FISH, and next-generation sequencing.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Demonstration of EWSR1::CREB1, EWSR1::ATF1, or FUS::ATF1 fusion is supportive of the diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: nodules of epithelioid to ovoid cells arranged in syncytial-like sheets; peripheral rims or infiltrates of lymphoplasmacytic cells, including germinal centres.

Desirable: pseudoangiomatoid spaces; molecular studies confirming EWSR1 or FUS gene rearrangements in selected cases.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

The local recurrence rate is 10–15% {2174650; 10571514}. Metastases, mainly to locoregional lymph nodes, have been documented in 1–5% of patients. Distant metastases to the liver, lungs, and brain may occur but are rare, and disease-related death is exceptional {28114920}. Histological and molecular features have not been correlated with prognosis, although local recurrence is associated with incomplete surgical excision {2174650; 21822206}.
