4.0.9 Paget disease of bone (PFN1, SQSTM1, TNFRSF11A, TNFRSF11B, ZNF687)

Definition

Paget disease of bone is a disorder characterized by abnormal osteoclastic and osteoblastic activity leading to the formation of bone with disorganized architecture.

Localization

Most commonly affected sites include the pelvis, followed by lumbar spine, long bones, and skull {20896492; 17410356; 3276082}. In long bones, the disease typically begins at one end at the articular surface and extends gradually to the opposing end. Paget disease of bone can be polyostotic or monostotic, the latter particularly seen in the axial skeleton {21606236}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Paget disease of bone can be incidentally discovered on imaging or through elevated serum total alkaline phosphatase. When symptomatic, Paget disease of bone can lead to musculoskeletal pain, less commonly bone deformities, pathological fractures, and neurological symptoms from skull or spine involvement such as deafness or spinal stenosis {25160936}. Serum total alkaline phosphatase can be used for first-line biochemical screening to evaluate for the presence of metabolically active disease {30803025}.

Imaging

On radiographs, classic features include osteolysis, cortical thickening, trabecular coarsening, bone enlargement, and sclerosis; there is a predominance of osteolysis in the early lytic phase and of sclerosis in the later phases. Characteristic findings include the blade-of-glass sign (V-shaped or flamed bone resorption), osteoporosis circumscripta (defined lytic lesions of the skull), cotton-wool appearance of osteosclerosis, picture-frame vertebrae (vertebral squaring and enlargement with cortical thickening), and banana fractures (insufficiency fractures with linear cortical radiolucent areas) {21524738; 21606236}. CT and MRI can be used to evaluate for disease complications, including fractures, neural compression, and development of secondary sarcomas. Radionuclide bone scintigraphy can be used to assess for metabolically active disease {8176486; 15050890; 30803025}.

Epidemiology

Paget disease of bone is uncommon, with a prevalence that increases with age and ranges from < 1% to > 8% of the population, being highest in the United Kingdom, Spain, and Italy, with wide geographical and locoregional differences {23643679}. Recent reduction in its incidence has been described {23643679; 33742666; 37683713}. Patients are commonly aged 50 years or older {23643679; 37683713; 32115673; 39906256}. An M:F ratio of 1.5:1 was described in a multicentre study in Europe {16995808}, but no significant differences were observed in a systematic review in China {32115673}.

Etiology

Interactions between genetic predisposition and environmental exposure have been implicated in the development of Paget disease of bone {21195346; 26284446}. A family history of Paget disease of bone has been noted in 15–40% of patients {2068956; 7610939; 37263846}; the majority of cases are inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion with incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity. Variants in SQSTM1, which encodes p62, mostly affect the highly conserved ubiquitin-binding domain and are present in 20–40% of familial and < 10% of sporadic cases {11992264; 12374763}. Autosomal dominant inheritance and recessive inheritance, respectively, have been shown for variants in TNFRSF11A and TNFRSF11B, and susceptibility genes include CSF1, OPTN, VCP, DCSTAMP, and FKBP5 {20436471; 26284446; 28524179}. Variants in ZNF687 or PFN1 have been detected in patients with severe Paget disease of bone associated with giant cell tumour of bone {26849110; 33599011}.

Pathogenesis

Paget disease of bone is caused by dysregulation of osteoclastogenesis, leading to aberrant RANKL signalling, excessive bone remodelling, and formation of deformed, weakened bone {22543925}. Evidence in mice suggests that some Paget disease of bone–associated variants in SQSTM1 cause osteoclast activation through altered interaction with CYLD protein, which suppresses osteoclast differentiation by deubiquitinating TRAF6, a critical regulator of RANKL-mediated NF-κB signalling {18382763}. The ubiquitinated TRAF6 protein plays a central role in activating signalling downstream of the RANK receptor. Normally, the CYLD protein inhibits RANK signalling by deubiquitinating TRAF6, an activity dependent on the ubiquitin-binding domain of p62 (a protein encoded by SQSTM1), which is required to recruit CYLD to the RANK-TRAF6 protein complex. Disruption of this domain of p62 impairs binding to CYLD, thereby increasing TRAF6 ubiquitination, enhancing RANK signalling, and activating osteoclasts {21878516}. In summary, ubiquitin-binding domain mutations in p62 probably cause osteoclast activation by preventing CYLD from providing its normal inhibitory effect on RANK signalling.

Macroscopic appearance

The cortical and medullary trabeculae are coarse and thickened.

Histopathology

Paget disease of bone is characterized by three phases {2942548; 18767930}. In the initial lytic phase, osteoclastic activity predominates, characterized by Howship lacunae and numerous osteoclasts that can harbour basophilic cytoplasmic inclusions and appear larger with more nuclei than their normal counterparts. In the mixed lytic and sclerotic phase, both osteoclastic and osteoblastic activities are conspicuous, leading to deposition of lamellar bone in a mosaic pattern with noticeable basophilic cement lines. In the final sclerotic phase, bone is thickened, and marrow is replaced by fibrovascular tissue; osteoclasts and osteoblasts are inconspicuous. Giant cell tumours arising in the setting of Paget disease of bone do not stain for p.(Gly34Trp) {29757500; 37310481}.

Prognosis and prediction

Bisphosphonates are the main treatment for symptomatic patients with bone pain {30803025}, while surgical indications include fracture management, joint arthroplasty for associated osteoarthritis, spinal decompression, and osteotomies to correct deformity {26410646}. Malignant transformation has been noted in < 1% of patients with Paget disease of bone {6941703; 14038496; 18767930}. Pagetoid sarcomas show a predilection for older men, follow the distribution of Paget disease of bone, are predominantly high-grade osteosarcomas, and impart poor outcomes {3857854; 6577936; 6941703; 16131876; 17550323; 19064007}.
