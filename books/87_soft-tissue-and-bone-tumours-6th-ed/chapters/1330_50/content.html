1.3.30 Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

Definition

Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma (LGMS) is an intermediate, rarely metastasizing spindle cell neoplasm with mild nuclear variability and a diffusely infiltrative growth pattern.

ICD-O coding

8825/3 Myofibroblastic sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B53.Y & XH2668 Other specified fibroblastic or myofibroblastic tumour, primary site & Low-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma

Related terminology

Acceptable: myofibrosarcoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

There is a wide anatomical distribution. Tumours most frequently arise in deep soft tissue sites, particularly in the head and neck (especially tongue, oral cavity, and jawbones) and extremities, whereas the skin and gastrointestinal tract are rarely affected {9777985; 11176071; 17513510; 27377169; 27377169}. Tumours arise predominantly in subcutaneous and deeper soft tissues; dermal presentation is very uncommon {11510507}. Rare cases involve salivary gland and nasal cavity / paranasal sinuses {10555016; 11493344}.

Clinical features

Most patients report a painless swelling or an enlarging mass. Pain or related symptoms are rarer. Radiologically, these lesions have a destructive growth pattern.

Epidemiology

LGMS is rare and occurs predominantly in adults (most frequently aged in their thirties to forties), with roughly equal sex incidence {38002641}. Children are rarely affected (range: 9–75 years) {9777985; 11176071; 8597827; 15220822; 40374967; 35791232; 33401376}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

This is unknown. Alterations in 12p11 and 12q13-q22 chromosomal regions with multiple ring chromosomes are reported, although they are not specific findings and are noted in other low-grade sarcomas {12547157}. A small subset of soft tissue atypical myofibroblastic tumours show USP6 rearrangements and might be currently interpreted as either LGMS or atypical/malignant nodular fasciitis {38651320}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, this is typically a firm mass with pale, fibrous cut surfaces and poorly defined margins {9777985}; a small number are well-circumscribed with pushing margins {11176071}.

Histopathology

Histologically, LGMSs are characterized by a diffusely infiltrative growth pattern; in deep neoplasms, cells often grow between individual skeletal muscle fibres. Most comprise cellular sweeping fascicles or more storiform distributions of spindle cells with elongated to somewhat wavy fusiform nuclei or plumper ovoid vesicular nuclei, with small single nucleoli and poorly defined, palely eosinophilic cytoplasm. The cytoplasm is sparser and less brightly eosinophilic than that of smooth muscle neoplasms. More rarely, tumours are hypocellular, with more prominent, sometimes hyalinized, collagenous stroma. Importantly, neoplastic cells show at least focal mild to moderate nuclear atypia with enlarged, hyperchromatic irregular nuclei (even if in some cases this is scattered), in contrast to non-malignant mimics such as fibromatosis or nodular fasciitis, which lack atypia. Mitotic activity is present, while necrosis is relatively rare. Numerous thin-walled capillaries may be present, and there can be rare osteoclast-like giant cells {11176071}. Tumours may progress to morphologically higher-grade myofibroblastic sarcomas {11176071}. High-grade myofibroblastic sarcoma, with widespread severe pleomorphism and extensive mitotic activity/necrosis, is not a specific entity; it encompasses part of the spectrum of undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma.

Immunohistochemically, the cells express SMA (with subplasmalemmal tram-track accentuation) and calponin, less frequently focal desmin, and occasionally focal CD34. H-caldesmon, smooth muscle myosin, myogenin, and S100 are negative {37190242}. A smaller subset shows nuclear β-catenin {17711447; 37540486}.

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

There are no specific genetic or molecular markers.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: diffusely infiltrative growth; cellular fascicles of spindle cells with pale eosinophilic cytoplasm; nuclear atypia.

Desirable: variable expression of SMA and/or desmin.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

LGMS often recurs locally in about one third or more cases, especially when incompletely excised {9777985; 11176071; 8597827; 15220822}. Metastases are rare, most often occurring after prolonged intervals {9777985}. A 5-year overall survival rate of 71.6% is reported for patients in the USA {27377169}, with older age significantly associated with worse survival {27377169}.
