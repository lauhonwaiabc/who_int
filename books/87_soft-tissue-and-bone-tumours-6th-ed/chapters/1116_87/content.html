1.11.6 Solitary circumscribed neuroma

Definition

Solitary circumscribed neuroma (SCN) is a benign, often cutaneous, circumscribed nodular proliferation of nerve fibres with Schwann cells predominating over axons, showing a strong predilection for the face.

ICD-O coding

9570/0

ICD-11 coding

2F24 & XH90Y8 benign cutaneous neoplasms of neural or nerve sheath origin & Solitary circumscribed neuroma

Related terminology

Acceptable: palisaded encapsulated neuroma; palisaded and encapsulated neuroma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Approximately 90% of SCNs occur on the face, particularly the cheek, nose, forehead, lips, eyelids, and chin, often in proximity to mucocutaneous junctions {2660609; 2660609; 30834800}. The oral mucosa is the second most common site involved {20237984}, with most involving the masticatory mucosa (palate and gingiva; 76%) followed by the labial mucosae, tongue, and buccal mucosae {20237984}. Multiple lesions involving acral sites have been reported {38246881; 34310726}. Other sites include the conjunctiva {32785439} and the glans penis {33423423}.

Clinical features

SCN usually presents as a slow-growing, firm, painless, solitary, circumscribed dermal-based nodule. Rare examples of multiple SCNs have been reported, including multiple symmetrical acral SCNs {34310726} and presentation as cobblestone plaques over the palms and oral cavity {38246881}. Multiple cutaneous SCNs have also been reported in a patient with NF2-related schwannomatosis (neurofibromatosis type 2) {36128789}, although SCNs have not been thought to be syndrome associated {2660609}.

Epidemiology

SCN accounts for 4–5% of peripheral nerve neoplasms {2660609}. It is most common in the fifth to seventh decades of life but can occur in patients of any age. There is no sex predilection.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The pathogenesis is unknown. Although SCN has been regarded as a reactive hyperplastic process similar to traumatic neuroma, it is not usually associated with trauma or histological features suggestive of trauma {20237984}. There is no evidence to support a forme fruste of a syndromic disorder, such as multiple endocrine neoplasia {2660609}.

Macroscopic appearance

SCNs are flesh-coloured papules that appear as a circumscribed dermal-based nodule with a firm, pale-grey cut surface. The tumours range in size from 2 mm to 12 mm, with most measuring ≤ 5 mm {2660609}.

Histopathology

SCN is a well-demarcated, dermal-based proliferation, composed of bland, spindle-shaped Schwann cells with wavy nuclei in a background of hyalinized collagen. Its growth pattern is commonly lobular {30834800; 20237984}, with occasional multinodular or plexiform growth {19614730}. The lesional cells are typically arranged in well-developed microfascicles, with variable nuclear palisading being characteristically present {2660609}. A thin perineurial-derived capsule may surround the lesion, but it is often incomplete at the superficial aspect, where the tumour merges with the papillary dermis {30834800; 20237984}. A parent nerve may be seen at the base of the tumour, with its perineurium merging with the perineurial-derived tumour capsule {2660609; 20237984}. Artefactual clefting is a frequent finding, and extension into the subcutis may be present on occasion {2660609; 4639250}. No cytological atypia is present, and mitoses are inconspicuous, although rare cases may show degenerative nuclear changes or focal epithelioid cytology. The overlying epidermis is usually normal or attenuated {30834800}.

The spindle cells are strongly and diffusely positive for S100 and negative for GFAP {20237984}. NFP highlights intratumoural axons {1705947}, with an axon to Schwann cell ratio of < 1:2 {20237984}. Perineurial cells in the capsule stain for EMA, claudin-1, and GLUT1.

Cytology

There are no reports of this rare neoplasm in the cytopathology literature.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: a circumscribed cutaneous lesion of Schwann cell–rich fascicles of nerve fibres with or without partial encapsulation, focal palisading, and artefactual clefting; S100 positivity with NFP-positive axons.

Desirable: tumour capsule is highlighted by perineurial markers (EMA, GLUT1).

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

SCN is benign, and it generally does not recur after complete excision. Only one recurrence (palatal-gingival SCN) has been documented {20237984}, and no metastases have been reported.
