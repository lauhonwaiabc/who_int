5.0.1 Ewing sarcoma

Definition

Ewing sarcoma is a small round cell sarcoma showing gene fusions involving one member of the FET family of genes (usually EWSR1) and a member of the ETS family of transcription factors.

ICD-O coding

9364/3 Ewing sarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B52.3 Ewing sarcoma of soft tissue

2B52.Y Ewing sarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of other specified sites

Related terminology

Not recommended: Askin tumour; primitive neuroectodermal tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Ewing sarcoma arises in the diaphysis and metadiaphysial part of long bones, pelvis, and ribs, although any bone can be affected. Extraskeletal Ewing sarcoma occurs in approximately 25–30% of patients {29977059; 33747211} and has a wide anatomical distribution, including viscera.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Ewing sarcoma often presents with locoregional pain and a palpable mass, sometimes associated with pathological fracture and fever (particularly with advanced and/or metastatic disease).

Imaging

For Ewing sarcomas occurring in bone, radiographs typically demonstrate poorly defined osteolytic-permeative lesions with an aggressive periosteal reaction (onion-skin or hair-on-end appearance; sometimes Codman angles). MRI is the modality of choice for assessing local tumour extent, while whole-body MRI, 18F-FDG PET/CT, or PET/MRI are used to evaluate extrapulmonary metastatic disease {31016339; 34500044}. An unenhanced CT of the chest is performed to assess lung metastases. MRI of Ewing sarcoma in bone demonstrates a large soft tissue mass compared with the bony involvement, and tumours tend to show strong diffusion restriction. The viability of the tumour can be characterized by monitoring changes in apparent diffusion coefficient over time during neoadjuvant chemotherapy {35229206}.

In Ewing sarcomas of the soft tissue, MRI features are nonspecific. Staging of patients with extraskeletal Ewing sarcoma is recommended by whole-body MRI and a chest CT {39030374}. Bone marrow biopsies are the standard of care for assessment of bone marrow involvement in Ewing sarcoma. However, as an alternative, imaging techniques could be used, including whole-body 18F-FDG PET/CT, whole-body MRI, or whole-body PET/MRI. This is already common practice in many sarcoma centres {36502618}.

Epidemiology

Ewing sarcoma is a common malignant primary bone tumour in children, with the USA reporting one of the highest annual incidences worldwide, of approximately 3 cases per 1 million children {32644609} and young adults, and an M:F ratio of 1.4:1 {8697401}. Nearly half of the patients are aged < 20 years, and the peak incidence occurs during the second decade of life {30538569; 34281296}. Cases in patients aged > 30 years are less common, and these tumours more often arise in the soft tissue. The rarity of Ewing sarcoma among individuals of African ancestry compared with that among individuals of European ancestry is probably due to genetic rather than environmental or lifestyle factors {21212061}.

Etiology

The majority of cases are sporadic, but germline mutations have been detected {26580448}.

Pathogenesis

All cases of Ewing sarcoma are associated with structural rearrangements that generate FET-ETS family fusion genes {25010205}. The most common Ewing sarcoma translocation (present in ~85% of cases) is t(11;22)(q24;q12), which results in the EWSR1::FLI1 fusion transcript and protein {29977059; 19841938; 10623711; 28797503; 10561219}. The second most common translocation is t(21;22)(q22;q12), which results in EWSR1::ERG in about 10% of cases {29977059; 10623711; 28797503; 10561219}. The remaining cases have alternative translocations that join EWSR1, FUS, or (rarely) TAF15 (which form the FET family) to other ETS family members {35527322}. Although all cases of Ewing sarcoma harbour FET-ETS, the fusion is not entirely specific because it is also seen in a subset of acute leukaemia, superficial FET-ETS family neurocristic tumour, and a tumour type known as adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma {37567448; 38866368; 39522640; 32925408}.

Additional mutations may occur in STAG2 (15–22%), CDKN2A (12%), TP53 (7%) {29977059; 25010205; 25186949; 25223734}, ERF (7%), and FGFR1 (~3%) {35952322}. FET-ETS family fusion genes encode chimeric transcription factors that serve as master regulators to activate and repress thousands of genes. Expression of these aberrant transcription factors, presumably in the correct cellular and developmental context, is required for the development of Ewing sarcoma. Proteins bind both GGAA microsatellites and canonical ETS binding sites in the genome and recruit chromatin regulators. Binding of the fusion proteins to GGAA microsatellites leads to a transition from a closed to an open chromatin state, establishing de novo enhancers that activate genes. Conversely, binding to canonical ETS binding sites displaces wildtype ETS factors and represses gene expression. Together, these events establish an oncogenic gene expression programme that underlies transformation and subsequent tumour initiation.

Macroscopic appearance

The cut surface of untreated Ewing sarcoma specimens is greyish-white and soft, and it frequently includes areas of haemorrhage and necrosis.

Histopathology

Classic Ewing sarcoma is composed of uniform small round cells with round nuclei containing finely stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli, scant clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm, and indistinct cytoplasmic membranes {29977059; 19841938; 21181413}. Occasionally, the tumour cells are larger, with prominent nucleoli and irregular contours (large cell or atypical Ewing sarcoma) {29977059; 19841938; 21181413}. A higher degree of neuroectodermal differentiation (poorly defined rosetted groups of as many as 10 cells oriented towards a central space and/or with a consistent immunophenotype) can be present (historically termed primitive neuroectodermal tumour) {29977059; 19841938; 21181413}. Uncommon patterns include pseudoalveolar, clear cell, mild spindling, and sclerotic forms {35952322; 19841938}. After induction chemotherapy, Ewing sarcoma cells show a variable degree of necrosis and are replaced by loose connective tissue.

Immunohistochemistry

CD99, a cell-surface glycoprotein, is a relevant diagnostic marker for Ewing sarcoma {29977059; 19841938; 21181413; 22446943; 26847175; 29661713; 16006796; 9989842; 26034869}. Strong, diffuse membranous expression of CD99 is evident in about 95% of Ewing sarcomas {29977059; 19841938; 21181413; 22446943; 26847175; 29661713; 16006796}. NKX2-2 {22446943; 26847175; 29661713} and PAX7 {28643791; 29920735; 30014288} have a higher specificity than CD99. Keratin expression is present in approximately 25% of cases {19841938; 16006796; 9989842; 26034869}. Some cases express neuroendocrine antigens and/or focal S100 {9646033; 33610950; 22534242; 12883251}.

Tumours with nests of basaloid cells within a fibromyxoid or hyalinized stroma exhibiting features of squamous differentiation (such as keratin expression, positivity for squamous markers (p63/p40), and occasional keratin pearl formation) and presenting in the head and neck are considered a different entity referred to as adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma {37567448}.

Cytology

Cytology aspirates show high cellularity with small discohesive tissue fragments, which may show pseudorosettes, and predominantly single cells with smaller light cells and dark cells, the latter having smudged darker chromatin. Nuclei are round to ovoid, with pale chromatin and indistinct or small nucleoli, and may show moulding, while in some cases the nuclei may be enlarged with grooves, irregular membranes, and prominent nucleoli. Scant to moderate cytoplasm may contain glycogen vacuoles (Giemsa). Background of stripped nuclei and debris may appear striped (tigroid) {22180234; 31995889}.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Identification of EWSR1 (or rarely FUS) rearrangements or specific gene fusions is recommended.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: small round cell morphology; CD99 diffuse strong membranous expression.

Desirable: NKX2-2 expression; EWSR1 (or rarely FUS) rearrangements or specific gene fusions.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

The prognosis of Ewing sarcoma has improved considerably with current multimodal therapy, resulting in 65–70% cure rate for localized disease {39806614; 33919988; 26304893; 27482030}. However, metastatic and early-relapsing tumours have a poor prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate of < 30% {39806614; 33919988; 26304893; 27482030}. The presence of metastases appears to be the main prognostic factor. Other negative prognostic factors include the anatomical location of the tumour, such as the pelvis {39806614; 33919988; 26304893; 27482030}. Complete pathological response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a favourable prognostic factor {26304893; 27482030; 36305090}. Reported negative prognosticators include 1q gain, 16q loss, and STAG2 loss/mutations {36221002; 21822310; 12087464}, which are not in widespread clinical use and require validation.
