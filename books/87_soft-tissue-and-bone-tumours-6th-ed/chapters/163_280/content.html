1.6.3 SRF-rearranged myoid neoplasm

Definition

SRF-rearranged myoid neoplasm is a usually benign neoplasm with myoid or immature smooth muscle differentiation and defined by SRF fusion.

ICD-O coding

To be added

ICD-11 coding

XXX

Related terminology

Acceptable: myoid neoplasm of somatic soft tissue.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

The most common location is in the deep soft tissues (skeletal muscle), with fewer cases centred within the subcutis. The tumours frequently involve the soft tissue of the trunk, head and neck, and extremities {28248815; 33021523; 36457254; 39864664}. Rare examples have been reported in the visceral organs {28248815}.

Clinical features

Most patients present with a painless mass of variable duration.

Epidemiology

SRF-rearranged myoid neoplasm is rare. There is a wide age range at presentation (infancy to 63 years); however, most cases occur in children and young adults. Overall, there is an equal sex distribution, although sex variability may be noted depending on the SRF fusion partners {28248815; 31478943}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

The SRF gene encodes for serum response factor, a transcription factor that regulates multiple genes involved in cardiac, skeletal, and smooth muscle development, including a number of smooth muscle–specific genes {9473329; 27478561}. Most tumours harbour similar SRF breakpoints (exon 4), resulting in SRF mRNA upregulation at the transcriptional level. The projected fusion protein retains the MADS box domain and the DNA-binding potential of wildtype SRF.

The most common gene partners include RELA, ICA1L, CITED1/2, and NCOA2 {28248815; 31478943; 36457254; 39864664}, with rare alternative partners including MKL2, C3orf62, ARGFXP1, and NFKBIE {33021523; 37715727; 39864664}.

Of note, SRF fusions have also been reported in a rare subset of well-differentiated spindle cell rhabdomyosarcoma in very young children {23463663; 32187044}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, the tumours have a homogeneous tan-white, nodular appearance, being well demarcated but unencapsulated {31478943}. On sectioning, the tumours have a rubbery firm texture, with variable yellow calcified areas. Most tumours measure less than 50 mm in greatest dimension, but occasional cases are as large as > 150 mm {31478943; 33021523}.

Histopathology

SRF-rearranged myoid neoplasms closely resemble smooth muscle, with uniform ovoid to spindle cells arranged in short fascicles, files or solid growth. The lesional cells show fibrillary cytoplasm and bland nuclei. Although most cases show a low mitotic activity, a small subset may show > 10 mitoses/2 mm2 (approximating to 10 HPF and focal necrosis). In some cases, features resembling myofibroma may be seen, such as a biphasic growth, with nests of more primitive-appearing round cells surrounded by fascicles of myoid spindled cells, as well as tumour protruding into dilated vessels {28248815}. Areas of calcifications and variable collagenous stroma are often present {39864664}.

Tumours show a partial or complete smooth muscle phenotype, with most cases showing SMA, desmin, and h-caldesmon positivity {33021523}. However, a variable immunoprofile has been noted depending on the fusion type, such as SMA and desmin coexpression seen in most SRF::RELA fusions {28248815}, while cases with SRF::ICA1L are mostly desmin negative {31478943}. In addition, tumours across the molecular spectrum have been reported to show variable cytokeratin expression {31478943; 33021523}. They are negative for skeletal muscle markers (myogenin and MYOD1).

Cytology

Insufficient data

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular confirmation by either targeted RNA sequencing panel or by FISH for SRF gene break-apart is recommended for a definitive diagnosis.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: monomorphic ovoid to spindle cells with fibrillary cytoplasm, arranged in short fascicles, showing a myoid or immature smooth muscle differentiation, reflected by expression of one or multiple smooth muscle markers; detection of SRF gene fusion.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) or American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging is appropriate for malignant tumours.

Prognosis and prediction

Most cases follow a benign course, with only 1 patient to date having developed lung metastases. The latter case harboured SRF::ICA1L fusion and showed a large tumour size of 19 cm and high mitotic activity {31478943}.
