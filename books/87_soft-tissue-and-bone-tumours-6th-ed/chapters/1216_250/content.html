1.2.16 Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma

Definition

Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma is a malignant adipocytic neoplasm composed of myxoid liposarcoma-like areas admixed with areas resembling pleomorphic liposarcoma.

ICD-O coding

8859/3 Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma

ICD-11 coding

2B59.Y & XH3EL0 & XH25R1 Liposarcoma, other specified primary site & Myxoid liposarcoma & Pleomorphic liposarcoma

Related terminology

None

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma shows a strong predilection for mesothelial-lined cavities, particularly the mediastinum {3361919; 18521256; 19194281; 22895273; 34168281; 36514176; 35672466; 38450446}. Other reported locations include the abdominopelvic cavity, head and neck, chest wall, trunk, and extremities {19194281; 24970901; 26647907; 28160093; 31559875; 31129458; 34517205; 36518639; 36343027; 35672466; 38232999; 38311898; 38123348}.

Clinical features

Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma generally manifests as a large, deep-seated soft tissue mass that is difficult to surgically resect {19194281; 34168281; 35672466}. Radiologically, it often presents as a large, heterogeneously enhancing soft tissue mass that compresses and invades surrounding anatomical organs and structures. MRI may demonstrate internal fat attenuation signals {35672466}. Rarely, myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma may present with massive intra-abdominal disease (sarcomatosis) {38232999}.

Epidemiology

According to different case reports and case series, the disease occurs predominantly in paediatric and young adult patients (median age: 23 years) but can be seen across a wide age range (5–68 years). There is no sex predilection {3361919; 19194281; 28160093; 22895273; 34168281; 35672466}.

Etiology

Unknown

Pathogenesis

Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcomas are characterized by genome-wide loss of heterozygosity, manifesting as a near-haploid or pseudohyperdiploid karyotype {26647907; 35672466; 38123348}. Copy-number profiling also demonstrates non-recurrent chromosomal arm–level amplifications and deletions {24970901; 34168281; 35672466}. TP53 germline (Li–Fraumeni syndrome) and somatic mutations, either hotspot missense mutations or loss-of-function mutations, are highly recurrent in these tumours {28160093; 31559875; 31129458; 35672466; 36514176; 38123348; 38232999}. RB1 inactivating mutations have been reported in a subset of cases {26647907; 34168281; 35672466; 38123348}.

Macroscopic appearance

Grossly, the tumours are large, unencapsulated, and multilobulated with a gelatinous to solid tan-yellow cut surface. The tumour often invades or is adherent to adjacent anatomical structures, such as the chest wall, pleura, and pericardium {35672466}.

Histopathology

Histologically, myxoid pleomorphic liposarcomas are composed of extensively myxoid, relatively hypocellular zones somewhat resembling myxoid liposarcoma, juxtaposed to much more cellular foci containing a large number of pleomorphic lipoblasts, as seen in conventional pleomorphic liposarcoma. Although some features (e.g. myxoid matrix, pseudocystic change, elaborate capillary vasculature, small lipoblasts) may mimic true myxoid liposarcoma, a higher degree of spindling, nuclear irregularity, and hyperchromatism are present {19194281; 34168281; 35672466}. Adjacent hypercellular areas are less myxoid and resemble conventional pleomorphic liposarcoma, with severe cytological atypia, bizarre-appearing pleomorphic lipoblasts, increased mitotic activity, and necrosis {19194281; 34168281; 35672466}. Foci of non-lipogenic spindle cell sarcoma are rarely present {35672466}. Immunohistochemistry does not play a role in the diagnosis of myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Widespread loss of heterozygosity may be detected with SNP arrays (e.g. OncoScan) and specific next-generation sequencing bioinformatics pipelines (e.g. fraction and allele-specific copy-number estimates from tumour:normal sequencing [FACETS]) {26647907; 27270079}. In contrast, conventional pleomorphic liposarcomas with myxoid change or myxofibrosarcoma-like features show multiple chromosomal gains and losses, without near-haploidy or pseudohyperdiploidy. Absent FUS/EWSR1::DDIT3 fusions and MDM2 amplification help exclude myxoid liposarcoma and myxoid well-differentiated/dedifferentiated liposarcoma, respectively. Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma and conventional pleomorphic liposarcoma have overlapping methylation profiles {34168281}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: children and young adults; location within serosal-lined cavities (especially mediastinum); tumour with extensively myxoid, relatively hypocellular foci resembling myxoid liposarcoma, juxtaposed to conventional pleomorphic liposarcoma–like areas.

Staging

The Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM systems can be applied.

Prognosis and prediction

Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma is an extremely aggressive tumour type with a high local recurrence rate, at least in part due to the considerable challenge in achieving negative surgical margins. Of the reported cases with follow-up, as many as 50% developed distant metastases to lungs, pleura, bone, and soft tissue sites. The median overall survival is 36 months, with a 5-year overall survival rate of < 20% {19194281; 34168281; 35672466}.
