2.1.14 Secondary peripheral chondrosarcoma, grades 2 and 3

Definition

Grades 2 and 3 secondary peripheral chondrosarcomas are intermediate-grade to high-grade malignant cartilaginous matrix-producing neoplasms arising from the surface of the bone in a pre-existing osteochondroma.

ICD-O coding

9220/3 Chondrosarcoma, grade 2

9220/3 Chondrosarcoma, grade 3

ICD-11 coding

2B50.Z & XH6LT5 Chondrosarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of unspecified sites & Chondrosarcoma, grade 2

2B50.Z & XH0Y34 Chondrosarcoma of bone and articular cartilage of unspecified sites & Chondrosarcoma, grade 3

Related terminology

Not recommended: chondrosarcoma secondary to osteochondroma.

Subtype(s)

None.

Localization

These tumours most commonly arise in osteochondromas located in the pelvis, trunk, and proximal femur {12782876}.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms

Enlargement or the onset of pain in a longstanding mass, particularly after puberty, should raise suspicion of malignant transformation {12782876}. Neurological symptoms and restricted joint motion may also be present.

Imaging

Secondary peripheral chondrosarcomas grades 2 and 3 show similar imaging features as the more common secondary peripheral atypical cartilaginous tumours/chondrosarcomas grade 1 (ACT/CS1s) {20392983}. However, the former have more often a diffuse pattern of cap thickening (> 75% of the circumference) and a larger cap (median thickness of 56 mm in secondary peripheral chondrosarcoma grade 2 or higher vs 39 mm in ACT/CS1) {34714386}. It should be noted that preoperative biopsy also does not reliably predict the final histological grade of a peripheral cartilaginous tumour {38901841}.

Epidemiology

Grade 2 or 3 secondary peripheral chondrosarcomas account for 9–48% of chondrosarcomas arising in osteochondromas {12782876; 35193636; 31453886; 38901841; 38351015} and occur predominantly in patients aged between 20 and 40 years, with a slight male predominance {30591865; 35193636; 39893587}.

Etiology

Patients with multiple osteochondromas syndrome are at increased risk of developing secondary peripheral chondrosarcoma within an osteochondroma {7702095; 9272707; 8027127; 30591865}. See section Multiple osteochondromas (EXT1, EXT2).

Pathogenesis

Whereas in secondary peripheral ACT/CS1, EXT-mutant cells and EXT-wildtype cells coexist, in high-grade secondary peripheral chondrosarcoma, the EXT-wildtype cells predominate {21804604}. Similar to grades 2 and 3 central chondrosarcoma, these chondrosarcomas display chromosomal instability and a complex karyotype that increases with increasing histological grade {10502322; 19336518}, probably caused by alterations in the p53 and RB1 pathways {19336518; 25644707; 23770606}. Variants in IDH1 or IDH2 are absent {21598255; 22057234}.

Macroscopic appearance

These lesions appear as translucent, lobulated, bluish-white tumours, often exhibiting areas of myxoid change and cystic degeneration. The stalk of the pre-existing osteochondroma may still be identifiable, and the cartilaginous cap typically exceeds 2–3 cm in thickness.

Histopathology

Grades 2 and 3 secondary peripheral chondrosarcomas are lobulated, cartilaginous matrix–producing tumours that are morphologically indistinguishable from their central counterparts. Foci of endochondral ossification may be present, some of which correspond to pre-existing osteochondroma. Nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic figures are typically readily identifiable. Spindle cell changes may be observed at the periphery of the neoplastic lobules. Additional features may include coarse and irregular calcifications, binucleated cells, cystic change, and areas of necrosis.

Grading

Criteria for histological grading are identical to those for grades 2 and 3 central chondrosarcoma {890662}.

Differential diagnosis

There is no role for biopsy in distinguishing secondary peripheral chondrosarcoma grades 2 or 3 from secondary peripheral ACT/CS1 {38901841}. Periosteal osteosarcoma and periosteal chondrosarcoma should also be considered in the differential diagnosis. Peripheral chondrosarcoma is distinguished from central chondrosarcoma by its location on the bone surface.

Cytology

Not clinically relevant

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Molecular analysis is not needed for diagnosis. IDH1/IDH2 variants are absent {21598255; 22057234}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: surface bone tumour with compatible imaging; chondrosarcoma arising in a pre-existing osteochondroma or with evidence of the stalk of a precursor osteochondroma; cartilage cap exceeding 2–3 cm; mitoses and nuclear pleomorphism.

Staging

Staging is according to bone sarcoma protocols (see <>TNM staging of tumours of bone). See also the information on staging in section Bone tumours: Introduction.

Prognosis and prediction

Local recurrence and/or distant metastasis occur in 36% of grade 2 and 50% of grade 3 sporadic peripheral chondrosarcomas {35193636}. The 5- and 10-year overall survival rates for these tumours are 87% and 74%. respectively, compared with 97% and 96% for secondary peripheral ACT/CS1; therefore, histological grade is the best predictor of outcome in peripheral chondrosarcoma {35193636; 38351015}. Resection with negative margins is also associated with a low local recurrence rate; in one study, no patients with a surgical margin ≥ 1 mm had local recurrence, metastasis, or disease-related death, irrespective of the grade of the tumour {31453886}. Local recurrences may occur, typically because of incomplete excision. Increased histological grade in the recurrence specimen is seen in approximately 10% of sporadic secondary peripheral chondrosarcoma {35193636}. Most patients who die of their disease have experienced local recurrence {12782876}.
