1.11.9 Hybrid nerve sheath tumour

Definition

Hybrid nerve sheath tumours are benign mesenchymal neoplasms with peripheral nerve sheath differentiation that combine features of two conventional entities – typically schwannoma, and perineurioma or neurofibroma.

Localization

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Tumours have a wide anatomical distribution in somatic soft tissues and do not usually arise in association with a nerve. They most frequently occur in the extremities (lower more often than upper), trunk, and head and neck {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}. Most arise in the subcutis; however, they may rarely involve fascia and/or skeletal muscle (other locations are possible, including dermis, submucosa, bone, and viscera) {19623031; 22832113; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Tumours have a wide anatomical distribution in somatic soft tissues and often arise in association with a nerve {1846409; 22446939; 26709712}. They most frequently occur in the extremities (lower more often than upper), trunk, head and neck, and spine {9639114; 22446939; 26709712}. They may be superficial or deep and rarely involve viscera {9639114; 22446939; 26709712; 35256555}.

Others

Neurofibroma-perineurioma {15692946; 18486904; 22446939; 24751814} and granular cell tumour–perineurioma {18724242; 40159563} are exceptionally uncommon, which makes drawing meaningful inferences challenging; they are not discussed further.

Clinical features

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Patients typically present with a superficial mass. Most are asymptomatic or mildly tender {19623031; 22832113; 33337633}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Patients typically present with a mass. Neural involvement may be associated with neurological symptoms such as pain, paraesthesia, and paresis {32017710; 35224252; 36570791; 40255822}. Orbital tumours may cause proptosis {20683694; 37061243}. Multiple masses may occur, particularly in the context of an underlying syndrome (i.e. SMARCB1- and LZTR1- related schwannomatosis (schwannomatosis), NF2-related schwannomatosis [neurofibromatosis type 2], neurofibromatosis type 1) {22446939; 26709712}.

Epidemiology

Schwannoma-perineurioma

These are rare neoplasms, with < 200 cases reported in the medical literature. The true incidence is probably underestimated due to underreporting and misclassification. Tumours occur from childhood to old age but are most common among young adults {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}. Most series suggest a slight female predilection {19623031; 22832113; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

These are rare neoplasms, with < 150 reported cases in the medical literature – most in the form of case reports. Tumours occur from childhood to old age but are most common among young adults {9639114; 22446939}. Overall, there appears to be a slight male predilection {9639114; 27765635}.

Etiology

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Tumours appear to arise sporadically {19623031; 33649458}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

A subset of tumours arise sporadically; however, they are frequently reported in the context of NF2-related schwannomatosis and SMARCB1- and LZTR1- related schwannomatosis, and (less often) neurofibromatosis type 1 and a yet-to-be-defined tumour syndrome {1846409; 22446939; 26709712; 27765635; 31881415}.

Pathogenesis

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Approximately 50% of cases are characterized by CHD7::VGLL3 gene fusions, with other VGLL3 partners possible (e.g. CHD9, MAMLD1, and SS18) {33649458; 35256555; 39713960; 40484320}. Other fusions reported include TEAD1 fusions with either NCOA2 or TOX partners, CHD9::ZFHX3, DST::BRAF, and SQSTM1::CDX1; additional, as-yet-uncharacterized fusions probably exist {33649458; 35256555; 39713960; 40484320}. Methylation profiling suggests that these tumours cluster separately from not only schwannoma and neurofibroma but also the recently recognized intraparenchymal CNS schwannoma, with which there is molecular pleiotropy {39713960}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Despite a strong syndromic association, few defining genetic events have emerged. Monosomy 22 is frequently observed, and tumours tend to have copy-number profiles similar to those of schwannomas {26857854; 27765635; 32017710}. There are various reports of mutations involving chromosome 22q genes (e.g. LZTR1 {39487562}, NF2 {36570791}, and SMARCB1 {27765635}), in addition to other locations (e.g. KMT2A {40255822}, and ERBB2 {32017710; 40094384}). Rare non-recurrent gene fusions have also been reported {35256555; 40484320}; however, their relationship to the subset of schwannomas harbouring the SH3PXD2A::HTRA1 fusion remains to be determined {27723760; 34213706; 38219951}. Methylation profiling shows that these tumours often cluster with schwannomas {26857854}; it also reveals heterogeneity within this group, suggesting the existence of distinct molecular subgroups {32017710}.

Macroscopic appearance

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Tumours average approximately 30 mm (range: 5–175 mm) {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}. They are typically circumscribed and firm, with a yellow-tan-white cut surface {19623031; 22832113; 33337633; 35256555}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Tumours average approximately 35 mm (range: 3–170 mm) {9639114; 26709712}. They are typically circumscribed, nodular to multilobulated, and soft to firm and elastic in consistency; with a yellow-white-grey-tan cut surface {9639114; 16286996; 22446939}.

Histopathology

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Tumours are generally lobulated – rarely plexiform – and composed of vague fascicles of spindle cells with variable cellularity; focal storiform, whorled, and/or microcystic patterns may be observed {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}. The low-power appearance of these tumours typically resembles that of a perineurioma. The cytoplasm is eosinophilic, often with delicate fibrillar processes {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}. The nuclei are ovoid and monomorphic, with rare pseudonuclear inclusions and palisading; mitotic activity is typically inconspicuous {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}. Interspersed cells with more-abundant cytoplasm and enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei may be observed. The stroma may be focally myxoid or collagenous, along with a nondescript thin-walled vasculature. A mild chronic inflammatory infiltrate may be present. Necrosis has not been reported {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}.

Immunohistochemically, tumours are predominantly positive for S100 and SOX10; a second population of long slender cells, often imperceptible by H&E staining alone, is variably highlighted by CD34, EMA, claudin-1, and GLUT1 {19623031; 22832113; 28526004; 33337633; 33649458; 35256555}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Tumours are generally lobulated – rarely plexiform – and composed of two distinct morphologies: a component resembling schwannoma and a component resembling neurofibroma {9639114; 22446939; 26709712}. The schwannoma component is generally nodular and composed of Antoni A regions, although some authors include Antoni B regions {22446939; 26709712}; it is circumscribed and cellular, with a fascicular-whorled pattern. The cytoplasm is eosinophilic with indistinct borders. The nuclei are ovoid, elongated, and hyperchromatic, with variable palisading; mitotic activity is minimal to absent {26709712}. There is scant stroma, and occasional vessels with hyalinized walls may be present {9639114}. The neurofibroma component is hypocellular and composed of spindle cells with a loose fascicular pattern. The cytoplasm is eosinophilic with delicate tapering processes. The nuclei are elongated and wavy {22446939}. The stroma is myxoid, with abundant collagen and a capillary-like vasculature {9639114; 22446939}. Degenerative atypia may be present {9639114; 26709712}. Peripheral lymphoid aggregates may be noted. Necrosis has not been reported.

By immunohistochemistry, the schwannoma component is diffusely positive for S100 and SOX10. The neurofibroma component is variably positive for S100 but consistently positive for CD34 {9639114; 22446939; 26709712}. The presence of immunoreactivity for CD34 has been suggested by some authors as support for a hybrid tumour; however, a similar pattern of staining may also be observed in Antoni B regions of schwannoma, limiting its diagnostic efficacy {39487562}. Likewise, entrapped axons can often be identified with neurofilament staining {9639114; 26709712}; however, this can also be observed in schwannoma {17721192}. EMA is negative within tumours but may highlight a perineurial layer {22446939; 26709712}.

Unlike for schwannoma-perineurioma, which has reproducible histopathological and molecular findings, there remains a lack of consensus on the diagnostic criteria for schwannoma-neurofibroma {39487562; 40094384} and on whether it represents a distinct entity. Some have suggested that these neoplasms represent proliferations of Schwann cells in a background of neurofibroma (e.g. neurofibroma with Schwann cell nodules, onion bulb–like changes, Schwann cell–rich neurofibroma) {8442415} [[Antonescu CR, Scheithauer BW, Woodruff JM. Tumours of the peripheral nervous system. Silver Spring (MD): American Registry of Pathology; 2013. (AFIP atlas of tumor pathology, series 4; fascicle 19).]]. Conversely, others have questioned whether the neurofibroma component might correspond to Antoni B regions in a schwannoma (e.g. myxoid schwannoma {10792492}). Currently, it remains conceivable that these tumours represent a heterogeneous group, unified by phenotypic convergence despite underlying biological distinctions.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Tumours with a prototypical morphology and immunophenotype do not require molecular testing. Demonstration of VGLL3 rearrangement, or related fusions, may provide diagnostic support in rare, challenging cases.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Essential: bland spindle cells assuming various potential architectural patterns; immunohistochemistry highlighting a population of cells with Schwann cell differentiation (e.g. S100 and/or SOX10), admixed with a population of cells with perineurial differentiation (e.g. CD34, EMA).

Desirable: VGLL3 rearrangement, or related gene fusions (in unusual and/or diagnostically challenging cases).

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Essential: juxtaposition of components morphologically and immunophenotypically typical of both schwannoma and neurofibroma.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Schwannoma-perineurioma

Tumours are considered benign, with a risk of local recurrence following incomplete excision {19623031}.

Schwannoma-neurofibroma

Tumours are considered benign, with very rare local recurrence {9639114}.
