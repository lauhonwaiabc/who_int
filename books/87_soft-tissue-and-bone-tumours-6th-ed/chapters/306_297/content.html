3.0.6 Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma

Definition

Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma is a malignant neoplasm showing morphological and immunophenotypic characteristics of follicular dendritic cells, which are stroma-derived cells found in germinal centres.

Related terminology

Acceptable: follicular dendritic cell tumour.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Follicular dendritic cell sarcomas occur more often in extranodal sites (~60% of cases) than in lymph nodes (~30%) and rarely as concomitant nodal and extranodal disease {23755890}. The most common extranodal locations are the abdominal cavity, including the digestive tract, retroperitoneum, and upper aerodigestive tract {9010103; 20503450; 32905993; 32991350}. Bone involvement is extremely rare.

Clinical features

Most patients with extranodal disease present with a slow-growing, painless mass {9010103; 23755890; 26910224}. Patients with abdominal tumours may present with abdominal pain; rare systemic symptoms can include fatigue, fever, and night sweats. Around 5–10% of cases are associated with hyaline-vascular Castleman disease. Various autoimmune diseases may also occur, including paraneoplastic autoimmune multiorgan syndrome and myasthenia gravis {30222603}.

Epidemiology

Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma is an extremely rare neoplasm, which accounts for only 0.4% of all soft tissue sarcomas. Reported cases span a wide age range, with a median age of 50 years at diagnosis and no sex predilection {8712294; 9010103; 12121233; 23755890; 34837090}.

Etiology

Unknown. As many as 10% of cases are associated with hyaline-vascular Castleman disease, which may show atypical (dysplastic) follicular dendritic cells outside of follicles, possibly a precursor to sarcoma {8172326; 9351567; 11422494; 27206572; 28695297}.

Pathogenesis

Cytogenetic studies have shown a complex karyotype {23422480}. Genomic studies show recurrent alterations in NF-κB pathway genes (BIRC3, NFKBIA, TRAF3, SOCS3, CYLD, and TNFAIP3) in 40–60% of cases and in tumour suppressor genes (CDKN2A, RB1, and TP53) in 20–30% of cases, as well as focal copy-number gains of CD274 (PDL1) and PDCD1LG2 (PDL2) in around 20% of cases {26564005; 28621320; 33904632}. IG rearrangements or BRAF p.(Val600Glu) (V600E) mutation have been reported in a small subset of cases {19145200; 24720374}. Transcriptomic analysis has shown highly specific expression of FDCSP and SRGN and upregulation of PD1, PDL1, and PDL2 {28130401; 28145886}.

Macroscopic appearance

Extranodal follicular dendritic cell sarcomas are usually well-circumscribed, with a solid, often fleshy cut surface, with haemorrhage and necrosis in large tumours. Tumour size ranges from 10 to 150 mm, with the largest tumours reported in the abdomen and mediastinum {8712294; 32905993}.

Histopathology

The tumours are composed of spindled, ovoid, or epithelioid cells with vesicular chromatin, variably prominent nucleoli, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm with indistinct cell borders, imparting a syncytial appearance, admixed with small lymphocytes, arranged in a whorled, storiform, fascicular, or sheet-like growth pattern. Occasional binucleated cells and nuclear pseudoinclusions are common findings. Some tumours show marked nuclear atypia, pleomorphism, and a high mitotic rate.

By immunohistochemistry, tumour cells express antigens characteristic of normal follicular dendritic cells, including CD21, CD23, CD35, clusterin, CXCL13, podoplanin (recognized by D2-40), SSTR2, FDCSP, and SRGN; CXCL13, CD21, and CD35 are the most specific {12121233; 15252304; 17951199; 18792075; 28145886; 30585825}. EMA and S100 are positive in as many as 40% and 10% of cases, respectively. Given their mesenchymal derivation, tumour cells are typically negative for markers of myeloid/monocyte lineage including CD4, CD45, CD68, and CD163.

Cytology

Aspirate smears are hypercellular, with large ovoid or spindle cells with irregular nuclear membranes, fine chromatin, prominent nucleoli, moderate amounts of cytoplasm, and indistinct cell borders, arranged singly and in syncytial groups against a background of small mature lymphocytes {29275417; 33493367}. Binucleated and multinucleated cells may be seen.

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Not clinically relevant

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential: ovoid, epithelioid, or spindled cells with a syncytial appearance arranged in a whorled, fascicular, or sheet-like pattern; small lymphocytes admixed with tumour cells; expression of follicular dendritic cell markers.

Staging

Not applicable

Prognosis and prediction

Extranodal follicular dendritic cell sarcomas are associated with a 30–40% risk of local recurrence and a 20–30% risk of metastasis; most common metastatic sites include lymph nodes, lung, liver, and bone {9010103; 20503450; 23755890; 25917851}. Poor prognostic factors include intra-abdominal location, large tumour size (≥ 5 cm), a high mitotic rate, and necrosis {9010103; 20503450; 23755890}.
