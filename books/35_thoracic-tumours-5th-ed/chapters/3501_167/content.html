3.5.0.1 Haematolymphoid tumours of the heart: Introduction

Overview

Tumours of the heart are rare, and haematolymphoid tumours make up only about 1% of neoplasms at this site {14565636; 15811617}. Primary lymphomas of the heart are rare and account for only about 0.5% of all extranodal lymphomas {14565636; 27140394}. By definition, primary cardiac lymphomas show involvement of the heart and/or pericardium only, with no or minimal extracardiac disease {10784641}. In contrast, secondary involvement of the heart by disseminated lymphoma is not uncommon, occurring in approximately 20–25% of lymphoma patients {14565636; 27265603}.

Primary cardiac lymphomas occur in immunocompetent and, disproportionately, immunodeficient patients (especially HIV-positive patients and transplant recipients) {8980266; 1394052}. The clinical presentation is variable, depending on the exact tumour location, but imaging evidence of a cardiac mass or an unexplained pericardial effusion are clues to possible lymphoma or other tumours {14565636}. Other, less specific presentations include cardiac failure, tamponade, and arrhythmias {8980266; 20922788}. Cytological assessment of pericardial fluid can be diagnostic in a large proportion of cases, but a histological biopsy may be required for diagnosis {9338475}. Because of their critical location and potential delays in diagnosis, the prognosis of primary cardiac lymphomas is generally poor, with chemotherapy being the mainstay of treatment {8980266}.

Classification of primary cardiac lymphoma

Primary cardiac lymphomas are non-Hodgkin lymphomas, most commonly diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, which accounts for ≥ 80% of cases {14565636; 20922788}. Other types of B-cell and T-cell lymphomas, such as Burkitt lymphoma, have also been reported but are extremely rare {20922788}.

Although rare, fibrin-associated diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is a unique type of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma that can occur in the heart, in addition to other sites of chronic fibrin deposition {20062008; 31863183}. This is newly listed as a distinct entity in the fifth edition of the WHO classification of thoracic tumours.

Primary effusion lymphoma is another rare but distinct lymphoma type that can occur in the pericardial cavity. This entity is covered in the section section Primary effusion lymphoma .
