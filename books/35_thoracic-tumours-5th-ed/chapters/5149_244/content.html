5.1.4.9 Undifferentiated carcinoma of the thymus

Definition

Thymic undifferentiated carcinoma is a primary carcinoma of the thymus lacking morphological or immunohistochemical differentiation other than epithelial differentiation. This is a diagnosis of exclusion.

ICD-O coding

8020/3 Carcinoma, undifferentiated, NOS

ICD-11 coding

2C27.0 & XH1YY4 Carcinoma of thymus & Carcinoma, undifferentiated, NOS

Related terminology

Not recommended: anaplastic carcinoma.

Subtype(s)

None

Localization

Undifferentiated carcinoma of the thymus is localized in the anterior (prevascular) mediastinum.

Clinical features

Symptoms are related to local tumour growth or metastases {22055398}. Some cases are asymptomatic.

Epidemiology

Only 2.5% of non-neuroendocrine thymic carcinomas (18 of 706) are of undifferentiated type {25524678}. The largest reported series included 7 cases {1991250}. The patients are adults, with no sex predominance and a median age of 54 years {15049903; 15767803; 23319214; 22055398}. One case has been reported in a 2-year-old girl {29411634}.

Etiology

The rare coexistence of undifferentiated carcinoma and thymoma {23319214} suggests a possible derivation from pre-existing more differentiated tumours.

Pathogenesis

Unknown

Macroscopic appearance

The tumours are often infiltrative and large {22055398}.

Histopathology

Tumour cells are large and polygonal, with nuclear atypia, mitotic activity, and necrosis {1991250; 23319214; 22055398}. Squamous, glandular, or sarcomatoid features should be absent. Rarely, there can be admixed bland-looking myoid cells. Tumour cells are positive for pancytokeratins {15049903; 23319214; 22055398}. Unlike in poorly differentiated thymic squamous cell carcinoma, there is no expression of CK5/6, p40/p63, or CD5 {23319214}. Expression of KIT (CD117) and PAX8 is reported in 60% and 40% of cases, respectively {16002927; 23319214; 22055398}. Malignant germ cell tumour markers (e.g. OCT3/4 and SALL4) and neuroendocrine markers are negative.

Differential diagnosis

The main differential diagnoses are poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma, poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), sarcomatoid carcinoma, NUT carcinoma, malignant germ cell tumours, other malignancies (e.g. melanoma, large cell lymphoma, or SMARCA4-deficient neoplasms {30451731}), invasion from a large cell or pleomorphic carcinoma of the lung, and metastatic undifferentiated carcinoma. Cases associated with prominent inflammatory reaction mimicking Castleman disease {15767803} are probably related to micronodular carcinoma with lymphoid hyperplasia {22388764}.

When immunohistochemistry or molecular studies are not available or appropriate, the diagnosis may be rendered provisionally.

Cytology

Not available

Diagnostic molecular pathology

Immunohistochemical and/or molecular studies for NUT and SMARCA4 genes are helpful for the exclusion of other poorly differentiated tumours {22790861; 30362654; 30451731}.

Essential and desirable diagnostic criteria

Essential:

  • Primary malignant thymic tumour showing only epithelial differentiation and not conforming to any other defined entity
  • Immunohistochemistry and molecular studies required to exclude other diagnoses

Staging

Thymic undifferentiated carcinoma should be staged according to the TNM system [[Brierley JD, Gospodarowicz MK, Wittekind C, editors. TNM classification of malignant tumours. 8th ed. Oxford (UK): Wiley-Blackwell; 2017.]] {25396314} and, optionally, the Masaoka–Koga system {21847052}.

Prognosis and prediction

Undifferentiated carcinoma has a poor prognosis {15049903; 15767803; 23319214; 22055398}. In the International Thymic Malignancy Interest Group (ITMIG) database, the survival and recurrence rates are similar to those for other types of thymic carcinoma {25524678}.
